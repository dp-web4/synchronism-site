## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The headline question pulled me in and the honesty is disarming, but the "beginner" path turns into specialist vocabulary by step 3, and I never got a plain-English answer to "so what does this thing actually say?"

### Journey

I clicked this from a Reddit comment. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is exactly the kind of thing I'd click. Good hook. Then immediately under it:

> C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is (log? the natural one? I remember a button on a calculator). I don't know what ρ or γ are. So about four seconds in, the site has shown me a thing I can't read. I didn't bounce, because the subhead — *"An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything"* — made me curious. That's an unusual thing for a physics site to say about itself.

Then the scoreboard hit me: **0 confirmed predictions. 6 refutations executed. 0 of 9 validated claims surviving audit.** My honest first reaction was confusion, not admiration. Why is this site telling me, on the front page, that it doesn't work? Is this a joke? A prank site? It took me a while — really until the Honest Assessment page — to get that this is the *point*: they're showing their work including the parts that failed. Once I got that, I liked it a lot. But nothing on the landing page told me *why I should keep reading a theory that lost*. There's a line about "the honest map of where it fails is the product" but it's buried under the numbers.

I clicked **Beginner Path** → **Why Synchronism?** (Step 1). This was the best page for me. The idea landed: one number, computed from how crowded a thing is, that tells you whether its parts act independently or as a unit. Crowd vs. marching band. I got that.

**First Encounter** (Step 2) was next and it's laid out as parts A through G, about 10 minutes. Parts A and B were fine — "presence" is just how dense things are in your neighborhood, and the MRH is "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," with the line *"A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy."* That's a great sentence. I understood it instantly.

Part C lost me: **γ = 2/√N_corr**. A single electron gives γ = 2, a big everyday object gives γ ≈ 10⁻¹². I can *read* that but I have no feel for it. What does it mean for gamma to be 2 versus a trillionth? Then by Part E we're on MOND and the acceleration constant a₀, and by G we're on melting points being off by 53% and a superconductor prediction of 607 K versus a real 93 K.

I clicked into **The Core Idea** (Step 3 of 6 on the *beginner* path). The dimmer-switch metaphor is good — better than the marching band, honestly. But the same page has "audited-negative," "reparametrization," and "Hill coefficients" on it. Step 3 of the beginner path should not contain a phrase like "6 of 6 badges have been demoted." I don't know what a badge is yet, or what demoting one means.

I detoured to the **Glossary** to look up tanh, half expecting it not to be there. It was: *"A mathematical function that smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1."* That's a perfectly good definition and it fixed my problem — but I had to go hunting for it 20 minutes after the site first showed me the symbol. MOND, MRH, SPARC, RAR, BTFR are all in there too. 90 terms. This glossary is a strong asset that's doing nothing for me on the pages where I actually need it.

Then the **Tools** page, which I liked — every card says what kind of thing it is and what level, so I knew not to click the "Advanced" one.

Finally I read **Research Philosophy**, and this was the biggest surprise of the visit: the research was done by AI agents arguing with each other — 3,308 sessions of it — and the page says outright that *"no record of an outside physicist reviewing these claims exists."* It also explains why two AIs disagreeing isn't as good as it sounds: they were trained on the same stuff, so they "jointly miss what the literature missed." I respect that enormously and I also think it changes the whole meaning of the site. That should not be page eleven of my visit.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — half intuitive. The strangers-in-a-plaza vs. marching-band framing is the best explanation on the whole site, and dragging γ to see the S-curve go from a gentle ramp to a sharp cliff genuinely taught me something. But then the page tells me it "does not model any real material" and the density axis "has no units." So I'm sliding a control on an abstract curve with nothing anchoring it to the world. I kept wanting a button that said "now show me water freezing." The preset labels (γ = 0.49 "SPARC fit," γ = ½ "equivalent to MOND's simple function," γ = 2 "framework's registered value") are all internal references — none of them mean anything to me. I also noticed the default is γ = 0.49, which the site elsewhere describes as the value that makes the whole thing just be MOND. That seems like a loaded default to hand a beginner without comment.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — intuitive, and the best-taught page I found. Eight different ways of measuring consciousness all land at C ≈ 0.50 (mean 0.499, spread 0.012). My gut said "wow, eight independent methods agreeing!" and the page immediately and gently told me why that's exactly wrong: they all inherit the same assumption, so the agreement is "circular, not empirical." Getting my naive reaction and then correcting it is *good teaching*. The badge saying "Speculative — no calibration exists" and the admission that this is "the single least supported thing here" made me trust the rest of the site more, not less.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked, but mostly over my head. I could see the shape of the problem: dots (real galaxies) sit above the gray line (what visible matter alone predicts), and that gap is the dark matter mystery. I got that much, and the axis note "1 kpc ≈ 3,260 light-years" was a genuinely thoughtful touch — more of that, please. Everything after that (violet vs. amber vs. green, "quadrature branch," "max C on this disk: 0.001") lost me.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the equation has 0 confirmed predictions and the galaxy result just turns into MOND, what is the site *for* now? Is it "here's a live experiment in doing science honestly," or is it "we still think there's something here"? I genuinely couldn't tell, and that's the first thing I'd want answered.
2. What is a "reparametrization"? It shows up as a content-type tag on a tool card and as a verdict on claims, and I gathered it's bad-ish — "you rewrote something known in new symbols" — but nobody told me.
3. What does it *mean* physically for a system to be "coherent"? The site warns physicists it doesn't mean the quantum thing they're thinking of, which is helpful for them, but I'm left with a metaphor (marching band) and never a real-world example I could point at.
4. The superconductor prediction was 607 K and the real answer is 93 K. Does that mean the equation is broken, or just badly tuned? Is there a version of this that could still be right?
5. Who is "dp"? The research philosophy page names a human reviewer who maintains the badge taxonomy, with no other context. Is this one person's project? A group?
6. If the AI agents can't catch errors that are "systematic across the entire training corpus," how would anyone ever know if that happened here?

### Verdict

I came for "one equation explains everything" and stayed for something more interesting: a site that publishes its own failures on the front page and then explains, patiently, why its most impressive-looking result is actually its weakest. The consciousness demo and the research philosophy page are the two best things here, and both of them are essentially teaching me how to not be fooled — which is a better thing to learn than any physics I'd have gotten from the original pitch. But the beginner path doesn't hold. It starts in plain English and by step 3 is using vocabulary the site never defined for me, and the front page shows me an equation I can't read next to a scoreboard of losses I can't interpret. I'd come back if someone linked me a specific page. I would not have found my own way from the landing page to the good stuff without twenty minutes of stubbornness that most people won't spend.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The *content* discipline here is better than most commercial docs I audit — there is a canonical taxonomy, a deprecation notice, and a migration statement — but the *structural* layer hasn't kept up: breadcrumbs describe a site hierarchy that doesn't exist, three separate status vocabularies are rendered identically, and nav labels, page titles, and URLs disagree three ways.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set I'd been handed cold: land, map the nav, walk the stated learning path, then check whether the controlled vocabulary holds up across pages.

**The navigation map.** Eight flat top-level items: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. Flat is fine for a site this size. But every interior page carries a breadcrumb with a *middle* segment — `Home / Start Here / Glossary`, `Home / Methodology / Research Philosophy`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap` — and those middle segments are not links. They have no href and no destination. So the site is telling me it has sections called "Methodology" and "Predictions & Experiments" that appear nowhere in the navigation and that I cannot click. Meanwhile "Start Here" appears in *both* systems with two different meanings: in the nav it's a page (`/why-synchronism`), in the breadcrumb it's an unclickable section containing Glossary, Key Claims, First Encounter, The Core Idea, What Synchronism Is Not, and Honest Assessment. One label, two referents, and the reader has no way to see the section structure whole.

**The learning paths.** `/learning-paths` offers seven entry points across two grouping systems and then tells me, to its credit, that "the two groupings are not equivalent" — difficulty paths reach 10 pages the topic tracks omit, topic tracks reach 14 the difficulty paths exclude. I appreciate the honesty, but as a reader I now know that *no path on this page covers the site* and I'm given no map of what's left over. There's also no default. Seven doors, no "if you're new, take this one."

This shows up downstream: `/core-idea` carries two step indicators at once — "Beginner Path — Step 3 of 6" *and* "Philosophy Track — Step 1 of 7." Nothing explains why a page has two positions or which one I'm supposed to be following. If I entered via Beginner, the Philosophy Track counter is noise; if I entered via Philosophy, it's the reverse. A single "you are here" that reflects the path I actually chose would fix it.

**Walking the Beginner path.** Step 2, First Encounter, is where the terminology discipline first slips. Parts A–G introduce ρ, MRH, C, γ, N_corr, and a₀ — and every one of those is defined at the point of use, which is good practice. But **MOND** and **SPARC** are used without expansion on this page, and the landing page's CTA is literally "Jump to First Encounter (Step 2)" — so the site's own primary call to action drops readers onto a page where two acronyms arrive cold. Both are in the glossary with clear definitions. They just aren't expanded where a path-following reader meets them.

Step 3, The Core Idea, actually handles specialist vocabulary well: "audited-negative," "reparametrization," "failed," "speculative," and "untested" are all hyperlinked to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, and "Hill coefficient," "null-class," and ρcrit are defined inline. This is the pattern the rest of the site should copy.

**The badge taxonomy.** I went to the canonical reference and it's genuinely well built: nine badges across two explicitly-named families (MRH-relationship: active-mrh, parallel-paths, sidelined, superseded, audited-negative; descriptive: untested, failed, speculative, reparametrization), plus two deprecated badges — validated and strongly-supported — with a stated reason and an explicit "existing usages being migrated incrementally." Naming your deprecated terms and saying you're mid-migration is something most doc sets never do.

Then I checked how it holds across pages, and found **three parallel status vocabularies**, only one of which is documented. `/tier-1-existing` lays them out itself: validation badges, "operational states" (Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Artifact Lesson), and "lifecycle verbs" (Underpowered as registered, Demoted to Tier-2, Closed). That's a sensible three-axis model — but the canonical reference on Honest Assessment defines *only* the first axis. And `/tier-1-existing` lists **"Kill Criterion Triggered"** among its validation badges, which is not one of the nine. So a reader who learns the taxonomy from the canonical page will meet at least five badge-shaped labels elsewhere that the canonical page doesn't contain.

The sharpest instance is on `/interactive-tools`. Every card carries a type tag and a level tag. The level values across the seven cards are: Beginner, Advanced, and **Artifact Lesson**. "Artifact Lesson" is not a difficulty — it's an operational state from the *other* vocabulary, sitting in the difficulty field. (Intermediate, which the learning paths use, appears on no card at all.) Meanwhile the *type* field holds Model Explainer, Reparametrization, and Speculative — mixing a content-format label with two validation badges. Two of the three tag vocabularies on this page are contaminated by values from elsewhere, and the cards are styled so all of them look like peers.

**Naming.** Collecting the mismatches:

| Nav label | URL | H1 |
|---|---|---|
| Test Roadmap | `/test-catalog` | Test Roadmap |
| Tools | `/interactive-tools` | Interactive Tools |
| Start Here | `/why-synchronism` | Why Synchronism? |
| (footer) For Researchers | `/for-researchers` | For Researchers: What's Citable |
| (card) Equation Anatomy | `/equation-walkthrough` | Equation Anatomy |

Each is individually trivial. Together they mean no URL on this site is guessable from its label, and prose on the Test Roadmap page refers to "the test catalog" — so both names are live in the copy, not just the routing.

**The page that isn't in the nav.** `/for-researchers` packages five framework-independent negative results — the local-density no-go, the dim-4 LIV naturalness gap, the CHSH substrate check, density-keyed unidentifiability, the globular-cluster exclusion window — as citable output, with archive paths. The landing page says "the autopsy is the product." This is the page where the autopsy is packaged for someone to actually use, and it's reachable only from the footer. That's the single clearest IA misalignment I found: the site's stated value proposition and its navigation point at different pages.

**Revision metadata in body prose.** Several pages carry dated provenance inline: "(roots added to this lead 2026-08-10, split made explicit 2026-09-05 when three personas found 2 / 3–4 / 6 on different pages; the subtraction was already published on Honest Assessment and had never reached the scoreboard)"; "corrected 2026-07-17"; "added 2026-08-09"; "The Verdict (Updated July 2026)." As a maintainer I love this. As a reader it interrupts the sentence — that first one is a 40-word parenthetical about the site's own editorial history embedded in an explanation of the physics. This belongs in a per-page revision block, not mid-sentence.

**Credit where due.** The 90-term glossary is a real asset: alphabetical, with a documented sort rule for Greek symbols, badge terms clustered, and — importantly — hover definitions wired site-wide. Every term I probed was there, including the deprecated badges marked as deprecated. The one caveat is that hover-only definition is invisible on touch devices and in print, so the glossary's reach is narrower than it looks.

### Tools Tested

- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — worked — structurally excellent, pedagogically thin. It's a six-step wizard with Previous/Next, isolating one term of C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) per step, starting at "Start with density." As information design this is exactly right: progressive disclosure, one concept per screen, a running view of the whole equation. Two problems. First, the definitions are written for someone who already knows the vocabulary — "ln: natural logarithm — grows slowly. ln(1000) ≈ 6.9" tells me a fact about a thing I may never have met. The glossary's own tanh entry is more accessible than the tool's. Second, and more interesting: the tool carries an "Honesty note" flagging γ as **Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems**. So the site's flagship equation-explainer, tagged Beginner, walks a reader through a parameter that its own audit says is backwards. That's defensible — arguably it's the whole point of this site — but the note reads as a footnote on a component rather than as a framing for the walkthrough. If step 4 of 6 is explaining a term that's been audited negative, the step itself should say so, not just the page.

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — and this is the best-documented failure I've seen on a live site. The badge is "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems." The explanation is concrete and checkable: "γ = 2/√Ncorr assigns the *sharpest* coherence transition to the least-correlated system (ideal gas) and the *flattest* to the most-correlated (BCS superconductor) — the opposite of real condensed-matter transitions." Then: "This tool is preserved for exploration; treat its outputs as 'what the inverted formula predicts' rather than as physical estimates." Crucially, **that warning sits above the calculator**, not below it and not in a collapsed section. Default Ncorr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, with seven presets and a linear/log toggle. The framing — "not a physical estimate, but a live demonstration of the formula's central defect — watch the most-correlated systems get the *flattest* curves, which is backwards" — turns the tool into an instrument for seeing the bug. From a documentation standpoint this is a model for how to keep a deprecated artifact online: state the defect, state it first, say what the output means instead, and give the reader a way to observe it. My only complaints are the unexpanded BEC/BCS acronyms and the fact that the card linking here labels this failure state in the *difficulty* field.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What are the three breadcrumb sections — Start Here, Methodology, Predictions & Experiments — and where can I see what's in each? They're the only evidence of a real site hierarchy and they're unclickable.
2. Is "Artifact Lesson" a difficulty level, an operational state, or a content type? It appears in the Level field on tool cards and as an operational state on /tier-1-existing. The site uses it as both.
3. Is "Kill Criterion Triggered" a validation badge? /tier-1-existing lists it as one; the canonical reference doesn't define it.
4. Which learning path is the default, and which pages does no path reach? The page tells me the groupings are unequal but not what falls through the gap.
5. When a page appears in two paths, which step counter is authoritative for a reader who arrived from one of them?
6. Is the migration off the deprecated "validated"/"supported" badges tracked anywhere a reader can see? The site says it's incremental — a visible count of remaining instances would make the claim checkable, and would fit this site's culture better than most.
7. Who maintains the badge taxonomy, in role terms? "A human (dp)" is a name without a function. "Reviewed by the site's badge steward" would tell me what kind of judgment was applied.

### Verdict

The vocabulary on this site is more carefully specified than the structure that carries it — there's a canonical badge taxonomy with two named families, an explicit deprecation list, and a stated migration in progress, which is better governance than most product documentation achieves. What's failing is the layer above: breadcrumbs promising sections that don't exist, three status vocabularies rendered with identical styling and only one of them documented centrally, and five places where the nav label, the URL, and the H1 are three different strings. The single highest-value fix is the tool cards — "Artifact Lesson" sitting in the Level column next to "Beginner" is a controlled-vocabulary collision that makes every card label ambiguous, and it's a small change. The second is putting `/for-researchers` in the navigation: the site says the autopsy is the product, and the page that packages the autopsy for citation is currently findable only by scrolling to the footer.

First surprise: the tanh∘ln identity is stated correctly and it's the right thing to state. `tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]` — I verified it by hand, it's exact, and `/core-idea` draws the correct conclusion: "the tanh∘ln decomposition is notation, not mechanism." Most frameworks built on a sigmoid would never volunteer that their sigmoid is an ordinary Hill function in disguise. Then it goes further and kills its own Landau borrowing — "C(ρ) is analytic on its whole domain... so it has *no critical point*... critical exponents (β, ν) are undefined by construction." That is the correct call and it's the one I was going to make. A framework that names its order parameter and then admits it has no critical point has given up the single biggest source of unearned authority in this genre.

Second surprise: at γ = 1/2, `C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2)`. I checked it: 2γ = 1 gives exactly x/(x+2), and MOND's simple interpolating function μ(y) = y/(1+y) at y = x/2 gives x/(x+2). Identical. And the SPARC fit lands on γ ≈ 0.489. So the data drags the free parameter to within 2% of the value where the whole coherence function *becomes* the MOND interpolating function, and the site says so on the page. ΔBIC = +184 against the γ = 2 pin. This is the cleanest self-administered reparametrization diagnosis I have seen from anyone, human or otherwise.

Third: the local-density no-go. I derived it independently before reading their version. For a system with V² = GM/R, density at the knee is ρ = V²/(GR²); imposing the knee at g = V²/R = a₀ gives R = V²/a₀ and therefore ρ_crit = a₀²/(GV²) ∝ **V⁻²**. The framework asserts ρ_crit ∝ V⁺². Their statement of it is correct, and it is framework-independent — it kills any algebraic modified-gravity multiplier keyed on local volumetric density, not just this one. That is a real, citable, transferable negative result and it is the best thing on the site.

Then I checked the parameters, and this is where I started finding things the audit has not connected.

I ran the constants: a₀ = cH₀/2π with H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², 13% below Milgrom — matches their number. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 119 M☉/pc² — matches. And `/parameter-derivations` correctly refuses to count Σ₀ as a second derivation: "this is the a₀ row propagated through a linear relation." Correct. Both are badged Reparametrization. No complaint.

γ = 2/√N_corr: they badge it Audited-Negative and give the right reason — "the CLT's 1/√N scaling governs iid variables. But N_corr is by construction the count of correlated degrees of freedom — exactly the regime where the iid hypothesis fails." Correct. But nobody has said what the *right* form would be, and it's sitting there: γ is a Hill index (n = 2γ, their own identification). Hill cooperativity **increases** with the number of cooperating units — hemoglobin has n ≈ 2.8 for 4 sites. So a sharpness parameter should scale as γ ∝ +√N_corr or ∝ N_corr, not 2/√N_corr. The 1/√N form is seductive because it is the correct scaling for a *fluctuation amplitude* — and a sharpness index is what you get when fluctuations are **small**, so 1/√N belongs in the denominator of γ, not in γ itself. Diagnosing the sign as inverted is right; diagnosing it as a fluctuation-vs-cooperativity slot error tells you what the repaired ansatz looks like.

And then I hit B_max = 1/Ω_m, and I think the site has under-read its own result.

### The boost ceiling is a cosmological constant wearing a local hat

`/galaxy-plotter` states it plainly: the division wiring has "C floored at Ω_m," so boost can never exceed 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17. `/parameter-derivations` badges it Audited-Negative and calls it "the one load-bearing parameter, refuted"; `/falsifiability` says it is "asserted, not derived." All true. Here is what none of those pages says:

**1. It is dimensionally legal but physically incoherent.** Ω_m is the ratio of the *cosmic mean* matter density to the critical density *at z = 0*. C(ρ) is a local function of local density inside a disc. There is no route from `tanh(γ·ln(1+ρ/ρ_crit))` to Ω_m; the floor is bolted on. `/parameter-derivations` mentions an "08-26 derivation-from-disc-geometry." Disc geometry cannot know Ω_m. If the number follows from geometry, writing it as 1/Ω_m is numerology; if it is Ω_m, it cannot follow from geometry. ### Journey

I came in expecting to spend twenty minutes confirming that an LLM had rediscovered MOND and written it in new symbols. #### The galaxy sector's equation is a constant, and TEST-09/TEST-10 were decided before execution

This is my main result and it is assembled entirely from the site's own numbers.

`/galaxy-plotter` states, for DDO 154, **max C on the disc = 0.001**, and describes the floored-division wiring as *"g_obs = g_bar/C with C floored at Ω_m"*. Ω_m = 0.315, and 1/0.315 = 3.175 — the ceiling.

Put those together. The floor is 0.315. The computed C never exceeds 0.001. The floor therefore binds at **every radius, on every disc**, by a factor of ~300. Under the floored wiring, the output of tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) is discarded at every single point where the framework is evaluated against rotation curves. What the galaxy sector actually applies is not a coherence equation. It is the constant 3.17.

I checked the arithmetic is self-consistent rather than an artifact of one dwarf. `/parameter-derivations` gives the knee at 6.5×10² M☉/pc³ against a SPARC median midplane density of 2.6×10⁻² — a ratio of 2.5×10⁴, so ρ/ρ_crit ≈ 4×10⁻⁵ and C ≈ γ·4×10⁻⁵. For DDO 154 (V_flat ≈ 47 km/s, ρ_crit = 0.029·V² ≈ 64 M☉/pc³) the quoted C = 0.001 at γ = 2 implies a midplane density of ~0.03 M☉/pc³, which is an entirely reasonable dwarf value. The numbers hang together. The floor binds everywhere.

Two consequences the site does not draw:

**TEST-09 (BTFR slope) could not have gone any other way.** A constant boost gives V_obs = √B · V_bar. A constant multiplicative rescaling of velocity shifts the *intercept* of the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation and leaves the *slope* exactly unchanged. So the framework predicts the Newtonian-baryonic BTFR slope, not MOND's 4. The test "selected MOND at 3.3σ" — but the framework's slope prediction was fixed by algebra, not by data. It has no slope freedom to lose.

**TEST-10 (dwarf DM fractions) likewise.** A constant ceiling gives f_DM = 1 − 1/B = 1 − 1/3.17 = 0.685 for *every* galaxy, with zero scatter. Observed f_DM runs from ~0.2 to 0.93. A one-line comparison of a delta function against a broad distribution refutes this without any fitting at all.

This matters beyond bookkeeping. The site treats TEST-09/TEST-10 as its two *discriminating empirical* results — the ones where data chose MOND over the framework. They are not empirical. They are the observable shadow of the floor binding, which is a statement about the knee calibration, which `/parameter-derivations` already flags as sitting 2.5×10⁴ above the densities in question. One root, derivable on paper, counted twice on the ledger and dressed as external adjudication. This is the same defect the site's own audit catches so well on the positive side — inflating a count by listing consequences of a single fact as independent items — appearing on the negative side, where nobody is auditing.

#### TEST-02 (wide binaries) is the only live discriminator on the site, and it has been shelved by a rule the surviving tests also fail

`/tier-1-existing` marks TEST-02 "unrun — self-eliminating," with this reasoning: if Gaia confirms a MOND-scale anomaly, the framework dies and MOND lives; if Gaia confirms Newton, the framework merely reproduces standard physics. *"No measurement selects Synchronism over the standard alternatives."*

Three problems.

**First, the exclusion rule is applied inconsistently.** TEST-09 and TEST-10 also could not select the framework — their registered outcomes were "framework survives" or "MOND selected," and they returned the latter. TEST-25 is explicitly *"not Synchronism-specific."* TEST-26 (below) cannot select the framework either. If "must have an outcome that selects Synchronism" is the inclusion criterion, almost nothing on the ledger passes it; if it isn't, TEST-02 should be run. A framework sitting at 0-for-26 should be *hunting* free tests that can kill it, not declining them for lack of upside.

**Second — and this is the substantive point — the wide-binary test is where density-keyed and acceleration-keyed laws are supposed to come apart.** Wide binaries in the solar neighbourhood sit at internal accelerations far below a₀ (MOND predicts a boost) while sitting in a *high* ambient density (a density-keyed law predicts none). The site's own numbers make the contrast explicit: density-keyed predicts a 0.05–0.4% velocity deviation; MOND EFE-free predicts ~18%. That is a factor of ~50 between the two hypotheses on the same objects. **That is the only genuinely different-from-MOND prediction I found anywhere on this site**, and it is the same physics as the globular-cluster fork the site *does* treat as its live result — GCs and wide binaries are both density/acceleration decoupling tests. One is the framework's one surviving open question; the other is filed unrun. They should be the same entry.

**Third, the "~80× below systematics" figure is computed against a knee the framework doesn't use.** The site states the 0.05–0.4% signal requires ρ_crit ∈ [3.8×10⁻⁵, 3.2×10⁻⁴ M☉/pc³], and in the same breath calls that window *"disjoint from every other sector's calibration."* It then says that **at the published calibration the solar-neighbourhood boost is ~3.5×10⁴ in g, excluded by the Oort limit.** So: at the framework's own parameters, wide binaries do not deliver a marginal signal — they deliver a four-order-of-magnitude exclusion against a measurement that has existed since 1932. The test is not underpowered. It is passed, and failed, and filed as unrun, because the power calculation was performed against a re-tuned parameter chosen to make the signal small.

I checked whether this exclusion propagates. It does not. `/parameter-derivations` — the canonical page for ρ_crit, the page that tabulates every sector's knee — does not mention the Oort limit or the solar-neighbourhood density at all. The single sharpest zero-cost exclusion on the site exists in one sentence inside a card marked "unrun."

I should say plainly that this cuts *for* the framework's stated purpose and against its scoreboard: this is a seventh refutation, and a cleaner one than most of the six.

#### The γ–A degeneracy is exact and derivable, not merely empirical

`/parameter-derivations` reports the 2026-09-03 sensitivity scan finding γ and A "unidentifiable," with Fisher correlation **+1.000000**. A correlation that is 1 to six decimals is not a strong numerical degeneracy; it is an analytic one, and it takes three lines to see:

With ρ ≪ ρ_crit (which the site establishes: ρ/ρ_crit ≈ 4×10⁻⁵), ln(1 + x) → x, and for small argument tanh(u) → u. So

  C ≈ γ · ρ/(A·V²) = (γ/A) · ρ/V²

γ and A enter **only** through the ratio γ/A. That is why moving γ by 40× changes χ²/N by a factor of 1.07–2.7 (A absorbs it), and it is why the Fisher matrix is exactly singular rather than nearly so. Reporting this as a numerical finding understates it and hides the useful part: the one combination the data *does* constrain is γ/A, and any future fit should be parametrised in it. It also means the galaxy sector never operates on the sigmoid at all — it lives entirely in the linear foot of tanh, so "phase transition," "S-curve," and "knee" are doing no work in the one sector with data.

#### TEST-26 is the only prospective test, and it is both already-adjudicated and non-selecting

`/test-catalog` lists TEST-26 (DESI DR3 dark-energy crossing, prospective) as the framework's one forward-looking claim: the framework's dark-energy sector cannot produce a w = −1 crossing.

DESI DR2 (2025) already reports the w0waCDM best fit crossing w = −1 near z ≈ 0.5, at 2.8–4.2σ depending on the dataset combination. Registering a prediction against DR3 when DR2 has already delivered the signal is not prospective registration in any sense a referee would accept — the outcome is knowable now.

And the claim doesn't select the framework in the other branch either. "Cannot produce phantom crossing" is generic to canonical single-field quintessence and to ΛCDM; a null result on crossing would be consistent with most of the model space. So TEST-26 fails the exact criterion used to shelve TEST-02, and it is nonetheless the site's flagship prospective entry. The asymmetry is worth naming: it can kill the framework, it cannot support it, and its adjudicating data is largely already public.

#### TEST-04a's escape route closed in 2025, and the site should say so

The framework predicts σ₈ ≈ 0.76 against DESI's 0.841 ± 0.034, quoted as 2.4σ on σ₈ and ~1.5σ on the registered fσ₈ statistic, and the site correctly calls the registration underpowered.

What the site misses is a favourable-then-unfavourable development it should own. σ₈ ≈ 0.76 sits squarely in the neighbourhood weak lensing preferred through ~2023 (KiDS-1000 S₈ = 0.759, DES Y3 S₈ = 0.776). Until recently, a defender could argue the framework predicted the *lensing* value and that the tension was with the CMB-anchored side. **KiDS-Legacy (2025) removes that defence**: S₈ = 0.81 (+0.016/−0.021), 0.73σ from Planck, fully consistent, with no residual S₈ tension in either second- or third-order shear. The low-σ₈ refuge is gone.

So the honest update runs in the direction the site would normally be quick to take: the σ₈ result is *more* adverse than when it was registered, not less. That this isn't noted is the first place I've seen this site fail to update against itself.

#### The refutation ledger is audited to a lower standard than the claim ledger

`/tier-1-existing` states, of TEST-25, that the result *"is not Synchronism-specific — the RAR-preferred interpolating function family is excluded by both MOND and Synchronism equally,"* and that the ~17.95σ figure exceeds the published Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024) ~8.7σ because this computation fixes γ and profiles a₀ only, rather than marginalising.

Both admissions are creditable and both undercut the ledger entry. TEST-25 is a replication of a published constraint on MOND, applied to a submodel of MOND. On the positive side of the ledger, the site has a word for exactly this and demotes claims for it: **reparametrization**. A replication counted as one of "6 refutations from 5 independent roots" is the negative-side analogue, and nothing in the badge taxonomy catches it. Combined with the TEST-09/TEST-10 point above, my estimate is that the six refutations reduce to roughly **three genuinely independent, framework-specific ones**, plus a replication and a pair of algebraic consequences of the floor.

The site's own framing — "0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations" — is presented as maximal honesty. It is actually a slightly flattering number in the one direction nobody thought to check: it makes the framework look more *tested* than it is.

#### On A2ACW

The protocol page is more honest about its own instrument than most methods sections I referee. It states that same-corpus self-play cannot distinguish novelty from rederivation, that no human-referee baseline arm exists, that the health metrics have no empirical validation, and — the important one — that the benchmark arms used *famous* historical cases rather than post-cutoff modest novelty.

That last limitation is fatal in a specific way the page doesn't spell out. Famous historical discoveries are in the training corpus. An LLM auditor "recognising" special relativity is retrieving a verdict, not exercising discrimination. The benchmark therefore measures recall and reports it as sensitivity, which means the 0-of-9 audit outcome is uncalibrated in *both* directions — the site's "true survival rate ≤ 0.34 at 95% CI" (correct rule-of-three arithmetic on 0/9, good) quantifies sampling error but not instrument bias.

The fix is cheaper than the "external oracles (formal verifiers, physics simulations)" the page proposes, and the template exists in an adjacent literature. The wide-binary community hit a structurally identical problem — two groups, same Gaia data, opposite conclusions, no way to adjudicate estimators — and the 2026 response was synthetic universes of known ground truth with competing estimator families run blind against them (arXiv:2608.24556). The A2ACW analogue is a corpus of **planted** claims: known reparametrizations dressed in novel notation, and genuine post-cutoff results, mixed and audited blind. That yields an ROC for the auditor. Without one, "0 of 9 survived" is a number without units — and the site is unusually well placed to notice that, since it applies exactly that standard to its own physics claims.

#### So what is actually new?

One thing, and the site under-claims it. Not the equation, not the ceiling, not any prediction — the **general no-go for density-keyed modifications**, which `/for-researchers` lists but files fifth among equals.

Stated sharply: any modification keyed on *local* density must be strong where density is low and weak where it is high. The solar wind at Cassini's heliocentric distance (n ≈ 0.05 cm⁻³) is *less* dense than the mean ISM (n ≈ 1 cm⁻³), so a density-keyed enhancement is larger at the site of the tightest null result than in the galactic disc where it is needed. Acceleration-keyed laws avoid this automatically — solar-system accelerations are enormous, so μ → 1 for free. That is the one-sentence reason MOND keys on acceleration and nobody keys on density, and I am not aware of it being written down crisply anywhere. The V⁻² vs V⁺² exponent argument the site uses is the same content in a less legible form.

The honest caveat, which strengthens rather than weakens it: the verdict depends on a **smoothing length the framework never specifies**. Smooth over 1 AU and the Sun's mass dominates, density is enormous, the boost switches off, Cassini passes. Smooth over a parsec and you get the local stellar density and it fails. `/honest-assessment` mentions the unspecified smoothing length in passing as a nuisance; it is in fact the parameter that decides TEST-25, which means the 17.95σ is conditional on an unmade choice. That is the same disease as the three-way wiring ambiguity — an underdetermined coupling — and the two should be presented as one structural fact: *the framework does not specify how its scalar couples to dynamics, so every executed test scores a choice rather than the theory.*

Write the no-go up with the smoothing-length condition made explicit and it is a short, citable paper that constrains a class of proposals, independent of anything Synchronism claims. That is a better product than the scoreboard it is currently filed behind.

### Tools Tested

- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — worked — **scientifically the most disciplined tool on the site.** Correlations of γ = 2/√N_corr against 23 curated properties, topping out at r = +0.982 (sound velocity) down to 0.000 (Hall coefficient). An r of 0.982 on a physics site is normally a red flag, and here it is immediately defused by the correct control, stated inline: *a plain 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z, fit to the same targets, matches these correlations to |Δr| ≤ 0.07* — sometimes beating γ. That is precisely the null a referee would demand, and it establishes that the γ column carries no information beyond Z. The tool also names its own collinearity (elasticity links five of the properties; Wiedemann–Franz links the conductivities), admits the 23 rows were hand-picked from 1,703, and states that *how γ was assigned to each material remains undocumented*. Three honest caveats stacked on a headline number is the right ratio. Two gaps: users can only filter and sort, not vary anything (so it's a table, not really an explorer), and the full r distribution over all 1,703 is withheld — which is the one number that would quantify the curation bias the page confesses to.

- **Crossover Regime Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — **correct physics, delivered as a demonstration of its own error.** The slider walks γ from 0 to 4 across three regimes, placing real systems. The pedagogical payload is right and well-aimed: the formula assigns BCS superconductors γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, i.e. the *flattest* C(ρ) curve in the set, when BCS and BEC transitions are among the sharpest in nature. Building a tool whose purpose is to let a reader watch the sign inversion happen is a genuinely good idea and I have not seen it done elsewhere. Two things I'd sharpen. First, BCS at 6×10⁻⁴ is noted as "three orders of magnitude off-scale" on a 0–4 axis — the most important data point in the demonstration cannot be displayed on the axis that demonstrates it; a log-γ toggle would fix that. Second, the caveat that ideal gases and galaxies both land at γ = 2.0 through a *counting artifact* is correct and important, but it undersells the scope: N_corr is asserted, not counted, for **all 17 scales**. That means γ is nowhere a measured quantity, which means the entire axis is a labelling exercise — a stronger statement than "these two coincide by accident," and the tool has the standing to make it.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — used as an instrument rather than an explainer, and it is the source of my main result. The three wirings (quadrature ≈ 1×, floored division ≈ 3×, unfloored ≈ 1000× for DDO 154, which needs ~10×) span three orders of magnitude, and the legend reports "max C on this disc: 0.001" alongside a floor at Ω_m. Those two numbers together are the whole galaxy sector: the floor binds by ~300× everywhere, so the floored branch is a constant 3.17 and the equation is inert. The tool displays every number needed to see this and does not draw the conclusion. Credit where due: the amber curve's *"Illustration only — hand-tuned to fit, NOT computed from the theory"* is disclosure I would accept in a published figure caption, which is not something I say often.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Does the floor bind at every radius on every SPARC disc, or only on dwarfs like DDO 154? If everywhere, the galaxy sector has no density dependence at all and TEST-09/TEST-10 are corollaries of one fact. This is a ten-line check against the plotter's own code and it changes the ledger.
2. What smoothing length does the density-keyed branch use? The answer alone decides Cassini — 1 AU passes, 1 pc fails. Until it is specified, what does 17.95σ constrain?
3. Why was TEST-02 shelved for being non-selecting when TEST-09, TEST-10, TEST-25 and TEST-26 are all equally non-selecting? Is the operative rule about selection, or about expected signal size, or is it post-hoc?
4. Wide binaries and globular clusters are the same test — low acceleration at high ambient density. Why is one the framework's sole live fork and the other unrun?
5. Under the ρ ≪ ρ_crit limit the model reduces to C ≈ (γ/A)·ρ/V². Has anyone fitted γ/A as a single parameter? That is the identifiable model, and nobody appears to have run it.
6. TEST-26 aside, is there any registered test whose adjudicating data does not already exist? If not, "prospective" is being used to mean "unexamined," which is a different thing.
7. Given KiDS-Legacy 2025, does the site's σ₈ verdict change? More pointedly: what is the mechanism by which external literature updates reach this site's ledger at all? Every correction I saw was internally generated.

### Verdict

This is the most rigorously self-critical framework page I have read, and its self-criticism has a systematic blind spot: it audits claims and does not audit refutations. Two of the six headline refutations are algebraic consequences of the floor binding — a constant boost cannot alter a BTFR slope and predicts a delta function in f_DM — and were therefore decided before any data was touched; one more is a replication of a published MOND constraint, which the site itself says holds against MOND equally. Meanwhile the framework's single genuinely different-from-MOND prediction, the ~50× contrast with MOND on wide binaries, is shelved as "self-eliminating" using an inclusion rule that none of the surviving tests satisfies — and a four-order-of-magnitude Oort-limit exclusion sits inside that same unrun card, never reaching the parameter page or the ledger.

What is actually new here is not a prediction and not the equation. It is the no-go: a modification keyed on local density is strongest where density is lowest, so it turns on in the solar system exactly where it must turn off — which is the reason MOND keys on acceleration, stated for the first time as a constraint on a class rather than a property of one model. That result is worth about four pages and it constrains proposals that have nothing to do with Synchronism. It is currently filed fifth on a page reachable only from the footer, behind a scoreboard that counts algebra as evidence.

My honest assessment of the honest assessment: the ledger is off in both directions and the errors point the same way — toward looking more *tested* than the work is. Fixing that would cost the site two refutations, gain it one, and produce the only citable thing it has.
