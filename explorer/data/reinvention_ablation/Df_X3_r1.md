**Short answer:** I found no published model with this exact form, so it looks unpublished. It does sit in a family of known ideas, and I'd flag it as an ad hoc variant of them.

**What my two searches found**
- I found no paper where inertial mass depends on orbital period through m_eff = m[1+κ ln(1+P/τ)]⁻¹ with τ tied to 2π/H₀. The one Hubble-related hit, [Local cosmological effects of the order of H in the orbital motion of a binary system (arXiv:1208.1523)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.1523), is about cosmological expansion perturbing orbits. It does not modify inertia.
- The closest relatives are modified-inertia proposals that tie inertia to a cosmological scale:
  - McCulloch's quantised inertia (Unruh radiation cut off by the Hubble horizon). It was applied to wide binaries in [Testing Newton/GR, MoND and quantised inertia on wide binaries (arXiv:1908.01434; Astrophys. Space Sci. 2019)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01434).
  - Milgrom's modified-inertia formulation of MOND. I only saw this described in the search summary, so I haven't verified a specific citation for it.
  
  Those models depend on acceleration, not on orbital period. The period dependence and the logarithmic form are not in either.
- On the data side, the wide-binary anomaly claims are contested. The papers are Chae ([arXiv:2305.04613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04613)) and Hernandez et al. ([arXiv:2304.07322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07322)), who report an anomaly, and Banik et al. 2024 ([arXiv:2311.03436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03436), uncertain ID, not seen in my results), who report none.

**Physics concerns (my own assessment)**
- A mass that depends on the orbital period is not a local property of a particle. It would violate the equivalence principle and Lorentz invariance unless it is built into a specific nonlocal framework. The same particle in two different orbits would have two different inertias.
- For P ≳ τ, m_eff falls as ln(P/τ) grows. If the gravitational mass stays fixed, the velocity boost is only logarithmic, which is very different from the MOND flat-rotation-curve scaling. With κ = 0.31 it would be hard to match the roughly 20% boost claimed by Chae, and it would also need to avoid predicting changes at Solar-System periods.
- τ ≈ 9×10⁴ yr is 10⁻⁶ of the Hubble time scale 2π/H₀. The factor 10⁻⁶ looks fitted rather than derived.
- The m_eff formula is only a dressing of Newtonian dynamics. It has no action or conservation law behind it.

**Verdict:** I believe this is not published in this form. I'd treat it as a phenomenological variant of Milgrom-type modified inertia and McCulloch's Hubble-scale inertia. I only ran two searches, so a broader search could still turn up a closer match.
