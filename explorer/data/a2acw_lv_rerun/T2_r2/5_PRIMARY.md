# Round 5: Primary (Closing)

## Responses to the Challenger's Round 4

**Convention dependence (§1): conceded.** The drift is v = −λ∇D with λ undetermined by "transfer resistance." The sign and magnitude of attraction are an input until a microscopic rule fixes them. In the symmetric exclusion limit I expect plain Fickian diffusion and zero drift, so I do not claim the drift survives there. I have not run that simulation and have no measured value to give.

**Mobile saturated bodies (§2): conceded as unresolved.** The departure-cell rule applied to a body at s ≈ 1 gives D → 0, which freezes it. That contradicts the equivalence principle, and I have no rule for composites that does not add a patch.

**Environment-dependent G (§3): conceded.** The coupling g ≈ |D′(I_b)| depends on ambient saturation, and superposition holds only to first order. Solar-system bounds would then require tuning.

**Chemotaxis alternative (§4): conceded.** My tests do not separate saturation from a posited drift coupling. The D′ = 0 control shows only that a coupling is required.

**Answers to the two questions:** I did not run either simulation, so I give no values. I predict zero drift in (1) and non-additive, self-suppressed velocities in (2).

# Closing Artifact

## HELD (survives)
- A conservative, steady, driven source with a far sink gives ΔI ∝ Q/r in 3D and 1/r in 2D, with a drift gradient ∝ 1/r² and linear superposition in the low-saturation limit. This is a Laplace result and not specific to saturation.
- Saturation can pin a core at s ≈ 1 and so supply a persistent source boundary condition.
- The model is falsifiable: it makes testable claims about exponent, dimension, additivity, and sign.

## NARROWED (must be scoped)
- The claim covers only a static, overdamped, test-probe scalar field. Mechanism and exponent are separable, and saturation contributes only the core.
- Attraction exists only under an imposed hop-rule convention (Itô, λ = 1). Its magnitude scales with λ and is zero in the Fickian case.
- "Stable pattern" must be read as an open, driven structure with a pump and a baseline sink, and I_b is an input.
- The whitepaper's own status line stands: "could explain, worth investigating."

## FAILED (unsupported / overreach)
- "Fields are statistical transfer asymmetry" as a derivation. The asymmetry is imposed, and the whitepaper's own sign argument conflicts with R(I) → ∞ near saturation.
- Gravity as a whole: no inertia, no orbits (T² ∝ a³), no equivalence principle, no Q ≡ M, and frozen saturated bodies.
- A constant G. It depends on ambient saturation, which creates tension with solar-system bounds.
- The unification claim. The EM, weak, and strong sectors have no derivation. Static charge conflicts with the "time-averaged ≈ 0" argument, and oscillating sources radiate as 1/r.
- Saturation as the distinguishing ingredient. A chemotaxis-type model reproduces every tested result with one explicit coupling.

## WOULD SETTLE (decisive evidence/test)
- A conservative microscopic lattice gas (exclusion or similar) with a pinned core, producing a nonzero, attractive, convention-independent tracer drift without hand-tuned hop rules.
- A two-mobile-core run where each body's acceleration scales with the other's Q and not its own, which means additive composites and an equivalence principle.
- A minimal, explicit inertial structure that gives a bound orbit with T² ∝ a³.
- Some prediction that departs from a linear-potential or scalar-tensor model, such as a nonlinear ΔI²/I_max² correction, that survives Cassini and lunar laser ranging bounds.
- A derivation of G from I_max and R(I).
