## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (7 loaded, 1 dead link)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial

### Journey
I clicked this from Reddit expecting either crackpot energy or a TED-talk. Got neither — pleasantly. The landing page opens with *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* and immediately undercuts the grandiosity with *"An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything."* That made me trust it. The marching-band-vs-wandering-crowd picture for "coherence" clicked instantly. There's a scary-looking equation right there — `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` — but I didn't bounce, because the band analogy sat right under it.

The page offered me a "No physics background? → Beginner Path" door, so I took it. **It 404'd.** I went to `/beginner` like the label implied and got a "Not Found." That's the exact reader the site says it wants, hitting a wall on step one. I recovered by finding `/first-encounter`, which IS the real beginner tour — a "7-step, 10-minute, no prerequisites" walkthrough. It was good! Marching bands, crystals, gases. But partway in there's an "Expert note" warning that the framework's own formula *contradicts* observed physics for superconductors. As a casual reader that landed weird — I haven't even finished learning the idea and the page is already telling me it's broken for one of its own examples. Honest, yes, but deflating in the *beginner* tour specifically.

`/core-idea` defined coherence fine (0 = sparse/independent, 1 = dense/collective — got it), but the density term ρ ("presence") was defined as *"compatible structural elements within a system's relevancy boundary,"* which told me nothing. Compatible how? Relevancy boundary of what? Then a sentence about *"1/√Ncorr central-limit-theorem scaling for correlated ensembles"* fully lost me.

I tested two interactive tools (below). The Coherence Explorer was actually the highlight — dragging sliders and watching the S-curve squish is the most "I get it" moment on the whole site. The equation walkthrough only seemed to show me Step 1 of 6 when I fetched it, so I couldn't tell if it pays off.

Finally I clicked the dark-matter stuff (the thing I actually came for from YouTube). Both `/honest-assessment` and `/galaxy-rotation` are admirably blunt — they straight-up say "zero confirmed predictions" and that the dark-matter idea has "the wrong sign" vs the Bullet Cluster. But the prose around those admissions is wall-to-wall ΔBIC / MOND / RAR / fσ₈ with no translation, so a casual reader gets the *bad-news headline* but not the *why*.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (all loaded; one tool exists at two parallel URLs)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structure), partial (physics — by design of persona)
- **Would return?**: yes (to audit a v2)
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is fundamentally sound — real breadcrumbs, a real glossary, honest epistemic labeling — but the controlled-vocabulary and wayfinding layers have drifted: badges aren't a closed set, the same tool lives at two URLs, the learning paths have no in-page progression, and the one term most likely to confuse readers is warned about in some places and not others.

### Journey
I audited this the way I'd audit any docs set: walk the global nav, follow the declared learning paths, spot-check terminology consistency across pages, and verify that labels point where they claim to. The skeleton is better than most research sites. Every content page I hit carried a clean three-level breadcrumb (`Home / Getting Started / The Core Idea`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1: Existing Data`) and the section grouping is logical. The glossary is genuinely good: 30+ terms, alphabetized (Greek sorted after Latin), heavily cross-linked, and — critically — it expands the acronyms the rest of the site leans on (MOND, RAR, SPARC, DESI, ΔBIC, fσ₈, N_corr, ρ_crit). That's a real reference layer, not decoration.

The cracks are in the connective tissue, and they're the kind a tech writer is paid to catch.

**Controlled vocabulary for badges has lost its boundary.** The Honest Assessment page hosts a "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" section defining exactly 9 badges in two families (MRH-relationship: active-mrh / parallel-paths / sidelined / superseded / audited-negative; descriptive: untested / failed / speculative / reparametrization) plus 2 deprecated. Good — single source of truth. But the Tier 1 page presents a *different* badge set to the reader, including `MOND-shared`, `Kill Criterion Triggered`, and `Withdrawn`, formatted and introduced exactly like badges. And the canonical page itself uses **at least eight more** label-shaped strings in its result summaries — `Kill Criterion Triggered`, `Withdrawn`, `Self-Eliminating-or-Tie`, `Sign Correction`, `Null-Class`, `MOND-shared`, `89% Boundary-Consistent`, `Template Bias Caveat` — none of them in the canonical list. The canonical page even hedges that these are "not badges" but "operational markers." So the reader meets the *same visual object* (`MOND-shared`) called a badge on one page and "not a badge" on another. Either fold these into the canonical taxonomy or visually distinguish operational markers from badges (different shape/color) and say so once.

**The learning paths don't actually guide.** `/learning-paths` declares an ordered Beginner sequence (Why → First Encounter → Core Idea → What It Is Not → Honest Assessment → Glossary). But when you land on a step — I checked `/why-synchronism` (step 1) and `/core-idea` (step 3) — there is **no "Step N of 6" indicator and no next/previous control**. The path exists only on the index page; once you're walking it, you're flying blind and have to backtrack to `/learning-paths` to find the next stop. For a site whose whole pitch is "follow this path," that's the highest-leverage IA fix here: add a path progress strip + Prev/Next to every page that belongs to a path.

**The front door contradicts its own sequence.** The landing page advertises the beginner path as Step 1 = Why Synchronism, Step 2 = First Encounter — but the primary hero button, "Start Here," jumps straight to `/first-encounter` (Step 2), skipping the step the same screen labels Step 1. Meanwhile "No physics background?" and "Beginner Path →" both go to `/learning-paths`. So three beginner CTAs split across two destinations, and the most prominent one lands you mid-path. (Note: I could not reproduce Pass 1's `/beginner` 404 — every beginner CTA I resolved this pass points to `/learning-paths` or `/first-encounter`, both live. Possibly fixed, possibly a Pass-1 fetch artifact; either way the *sequencing* inconsistency stands.)

**The coherence-inversion warning is applied unevenly.** This is the single most confusing term on the site (Synchronism "coherence" is *anti*-correlated with quantum coherence — superconductors score low). The glossary handles it well with a "⚠ Physicist terminology note." But `/core-idea` and `/why-synchronism` — both earlier in the beginner path than the glossary — define coherence with zero inversion warning. A reader meets the counterintuitive term, builds the wrong mental model, and only gets corrected several steps later (if they reach the glossary at all). Push a one-line inversion note to *first* definition, not last.

**Two URLs, one tool.** The landing page links the plotter as `/galaxy-curve-plotter`; the Tools index links it as `/galaxy-plotter`. Both load and render the same tool. Duplicate routes mean split inbound links, ambiguous canonical URL, and a maintenance trap (fix one, miss the other). Pick one, 301 the other.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, coherence-function, equation-walkthrough, for-researchers, honest-assessment, galaxy-rotation, gamma-calculator, galaxy-curve-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of what the framework *is* and where it stands)

- **Overall impression**: This is the most epistemically honest "unified framework" site I've ever read — it has done my skeptical work for me on nearly every claim — but its one genuinely novel channel (discrete-substrate LIV) is also the one place the honesty inverts: it overstates how protected-from-refutation it is.

### Journey
A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project," so I braced for the usual grand-unified-theory pattern: a sigmoid, a hand-wave to mean-field theory, a MOND "derivation," and a wall of confirmation. I went straight to the math.

**The central equation.** `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`, with `γ = 2/√N_corr`. My first instinct was the obvious objection: tanh invokes the Ising mean-field result `m = tanh(βJzm)`, but that's a *self-consistent* equation — m appears on both sides, the fixed point is what gives you spontaneous symmetry breaking and the whole critical apparatus. `C(ρ)` is a bare forward map: ρ goes in, C comes out, no feedback. So calling this "mean-field/Landau" would be a category error. I went to `/coherence-function` ready to ding them for it — and they'd already said it: *"Synchronism's C(ρ) is not the Ising equation … no feedback loop,"* tanh is *"motivated by the Ising analogy, not derived from it … a phenomenological choice from the compander family."* They even concede *"Landau-universality critical exponents (β, ν, α, δ, η) are off by ~2× … the Landau analogy is motivational, not an accurate prediction."* That is exactly right, and it's the correct diagnosis: this is a compander (μ-law/Hill/Naka–Rushton lineage), not an order parameter. `ρcrit` is a saturation knee — they note `C(ρcrit, γ=2) ≈ 0.88`, not 0.5, which confirms it: a true critical point would sit at the symmetric midpoint. There's no privileged status for tanh over logistic/erf/arctan/Hill here, and the page says so. I cannot improve on their self-critique.

**γ = 2/√N_corr.** This is where I expected the hidden CLT smuggle — "fluctuations scale as 1/√N, therefore…". The `/gamma-calculator` disavows it outright: *"The 1/√N scaling is a dimensional ansatz inspired by fluctuation theory — not a consequence of the CLT."* Better, it flags the structural defect I'd have flagged myself: γ is a **sharpness/rate** (bigger γ ⇒ steeper transition), while 1/√N is a **fluctuation width** (bigger N ⇒ narrower) — these scale in *opposite* senses. So the map assigns the **sharpest** transition (γ=2) to the **least** correlated system (N_corr=1, "ideal gas") and the **flattest** (γ→0) to the **most** correlated (N_corr=10⁶, "BEC"). That's physically backwards: BCS/BEC condensates have the *sharpest* onsets in nature. The tool states this inversion explicitly. I'm genuinely impressed — most frameworks would hide this; here it's caveat #2 in the calculator UI.

**The MOND "unification."** `/galaxy-rotation` + `/galaxy-curve-plotter`. The RAR transition-shape test is the only non-degenerate galaxy discriminator, and it's been run on real SPARC: γ=2 pinned is refuted at **ΔBIC = +184** (conservative ≈ 33) vs McGaugh; free-γ converges to **γ ≈ 0.49 with RMS identical to McGaugh to four digits**. So when it fits, it *is* MOND, and it does so with an *extra* free parameter per galaxy — strictly worse by parsimony. a₀ is fitted (`cH₀/2π`, the 40-year coincidence), not derived. The plotter says the quiet part out loud: *"all three theories draw nearly the same curve here, so a good fit proves nothing."* This is a textbook reparametrization and the site labels it as one.

**The external-data failures are stated with the actual numbers.** DESI TEST-04a: predicted fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418 (12% suppression), DESI LRG1 gives fσ₈/fid = 1.16 ± 0.13 — the suppression has the **wrong sign**, σ₈ tension **2.4σ**, kill criterion (>0.46) triggered. Bullet Cluster: dark-matter mechanism predicts the wrong sign on self-interaction. Clusters: ρcrit would need to be 10⁴–10⁶× smaller than the galaxy value — universality dead. These match the literature and the framework's own internal ledger. `/honest-assessment` closes with "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, 0 tests discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" after 3,308 sessions. As an audit, this is exemplary.

**Where the honesty breaks: the LIV channel.** `/for-researchers` offers exactly one genuinely-novel, non-MOND, non-Zurek claim: a discrete-substrate Lorentz-violation signature, declared *"structurally untestable … protected from refutation and blocked from confirmation"* via three "symmetry-protection locks." Here I stop nodding. Two problems:

1. **Lock 1 is an assumption wearing a theorem's clothes.** *"The substrate dispersion is even in k (lattice reflection symmetry), so the leading correction is quadratic (n=2) — no linear (n=1) term."* A discrete substrate does **not** have to be reflection-symmetric; plenty of discretizations break parity, and the moment they do, the n=1 term returns — and n=1 is *already* refuted (LHAASO/Fermi push E_QG,1 above the Planck scale). So "symmetry forbids the refutable channel" is really "we choose the substrate symmetry that happens to forbid the refutable channel." That's not protection, it's a selection.

2. **The preferred-frame exposure is downplayed as an opening rather than named as the sharpest refutation risk.** A discrete substrate *picks out a rest frame* — that's a preferred frame, full stop. Preferred-frame physics shows up as **dimension-4 SME coefficients (c_μν)**, which are bounded at the 10⁻¹⁵–10⁻¹⁹ level by Michelson–Morley / Hughes–Drever / clock-comparison experiments, and as sidereal/annual anisotropy. The page mentions a *"sidereal/annual modulation"* channel only to call it *"uncomputed,"* and frames the preferred-frame structure as a *potential opening to testability*. That has the sign backwards: an isotropic n=2 dispersion lock does nothing to shield the **anisotropic** c_μν sector, which is the framework's *most* exposed flank, not a future opportunity. The page never mentions SME / c_μν at all. So the one place the site claims a unique signature is also the one place it claims **more protection from refutation than it has earned** — ironic on a site this scrupulous everywhere else. (No superseded/correction banner appeared on the channel in my fetch, suggesting a research-core finding hasn't propagated to this front page.)

### Verdict

Fix that one overclaim and the site's honesty is, as far as I can check, complete.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, for-researchers, key-claims, parameter-derivations, research-philosophy, tier-1-existing, measurement-without-observers, interactive-tools, phase-boundary-visualizer, consciousness-demo)

- **Overall impression**: The site's headline negative result — *zero predictions that discriminate from MOND+EFE or Zurek, by execution* — is, as far as I can verify against the current literature, correct and honestly arrived at; the only places it fails are where it claims *more* than the ledger grants (the LIV "symmetry-protection") and where a refuted value hasn't propagated to an interactive tool.

### Journey
I review for PRD and JCAP and I came to do one thing: find a prediction that differs from MOND+ΛCDM at galaxy/cosmic scale or from Zurek einselection in the quantum sector, and check it against 2024–2025 data. I went straight past the pedagogy to `/for-researchers`, `/key-claims`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/tier-1-existing`, and `/measurement-without-observers`.

**The galaxy/cosmic sector is closed, and closed correctly.** `/tier-1-existing` states the conclusion without flinching: *"Net discriminating galaxy tests vs MOND: 0, by execution."* I checked the two tests I'd most expect to be oversold:

- **DESI (TEST-04a).** The page reports fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418 predicted (12% suppression), DESI DR1 (arXiv:2411.12021) gives fσ₈/fid = 1.16 ± 0.13 → enhancement, kill criterion (>0.46) triggered, ~2σ disfavored, and — crucially — it cites EFTofLSS (Cabass, Simonović et al. 2024–25) explaining the same DR1 number *within* ΛCDM at 1–2σ. That is exactly the current state of the field. The suppression mechanism predicts the wrong sign; there is no rescue. (Minor currency note: DESI **DR2** RSD/BAO landed in 2025; the page still cites DR1. The verdict is unchanged — DR2 if anything tightens the ΛCDM-consistency — but a referee would flag the stale citation.)
- **Wide binaries (TEST-02).** Chae (MOND-like ~1.4× boost) vs Banik (Newtonian, excludes it) correctly characterized as *unresolved on the same Gaia DR3 data with different cuts*. And the kill shot is stated: the C(ρ) prediction (0.05–0.4% deviation) sits ~80× **below** Gaia DR3 wide-binary systematics, so TEST-02 cannot adjudicate regardless of who wins Chae-vs-Banik.
