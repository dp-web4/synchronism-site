## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, start-here, learning-paths, first-encounter, tools, equation-walkthrough, coherence-explorer, gamma-calculator, key-claims, honest-assessment, glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understood the *idea* (dense stuff acts together, sparse stuff acts alone, one curve for both) and I respect how openly the site says it didn't work — but I never found the part where someone tells me why I should care anyway.

### Journey

I clicked the Reddit link and landed on **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** Good headline. I like a question. Then directly underneath it:

> C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

That's the second thing on the page. I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is. I know ρ is "rho" only because I've seen it in a Veritasium video. There *is* a little parameter key below it (C = coherence, γ = sharpness, tanh = "S-curve saturation"), which helps, and there's a plain-English restatement — "Coherence is a smooth S-curve from sparse/independent to dense/collective" — which is the sentence I actually needed. But it's *below* the equation. My eyes bounced off the symbols before I got there.

Scrolling further I hit the numbers: **0 prospective predictions confirmed. 4 refutations. 0 of 6 "validated" claims surviving audit.** Then: 3,308 A2ACW sessions, 1,703 chemical phenomena, 14,760 galaxies. And section labels reading Reparametrization, Failed, Speculative.

I want to be clear that I *liked* this, in the way you like a person who admits they were wrong. But as a first-time reader it put me in a weird spot 40 seconds in: the site had already told me the idea doesn't work, and hadn't yet told me why it's still worth my next ten minutes. (Also — "A2ACW" is never spelled out anywhere I could see on that page. I still don't know what it stands for.)

I clicked **Start Here** → it's step 1 of a 6-step Beginner Path, ~30 min. Then **First Encounter** (step 2, promised as seven ~90-second sections). This is where the site finally worked for me:

> "a marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence" vs. "a crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence"

That's it. That's the whole idea, and I got it instantly. Same with the MRH bit — "a single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy — it only responds to what's immediately around it." I actually said "oh, okay" out loud. **This analogy is three clicks deep and the equation is zero clicks deep. That's backwards.**

Feeling brave, I went to **Tools → Equation Anatomy**. Step 1 was great. The dimmer-switch line for tanh — "input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1" — is the best explanation of a math function I've read in ages. The logarithm one too: "a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger."

And then, in the middle of the thing that was successfully teaching me the equation, this:

> "the ln is cosmetic here — tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) is *exactly* [(1+x)^(2γ)−1]/[(1+x)^(2γ)+1], a Hill-type rational function."

I lost about 90 seconds of confidence there. Two problems for someone like me. First, it's a *bigger, uglier* equation dropped in to explain the smaller one. Second, and this is the part that actually nags: if the ln is cosmetic, **why is it on the homepage?** The one equation the whole site is named around apparently has a decorative part in it. I don't know what to do with that.

I played with two tools (below), then went to **Key Claims** hoping for the payoff. Three claims. Badges: Speculative, Speculative, Failed. The consciousness one is "unfalsifiable as stated," the quantum one is "untestable as stated," and dark matter is "Failed." So the three headline ideas are two we-can't-check-this and one we-checked-and-no.

Last stop, **Honest Assessment**. Mostly over my head — BTFR, ΔBIC, fσ₈, RAR, a₀, Ωm, 3.3 sigma, ΔBIC=+184 — I skimmed hard. But it has a section called *"What a non-physicist should take away,"* and inside it is the single clearest sentence on the entire website:

> "The one equation fits galaxy rotation — but only as well as MOND, an existing 40-year-old idea, and never better."

**That's the site.** In one sentence, no symbols, and I understood both the achievement and the disappointment. It's on the last page I visited, inside a subsection, below about a dozen headings of statistics. I would move it to the homepage.

The concrete failures were also weirdly satisfying to read: superconductor predicted at 607K, actual 93K. Melting points off by 53% on average. 0 of 36 fractal boundaries confirmed. That's honest in a way I don't see much, and it made me trust the site *more*, not less.

I checked the **Glossary** on the way out — 72 terms, and tanh and ln are genuinely in there with plain definitions. Great. Nobody told me that when I was staring at the equation on the homepage.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — Dragging γ down and watching the curve flatten is genuinely satisfying, and the log/linear toggle is a nice touch. Two things stopped it short of clicking for me: the density axis has no units or landmarks (1.00 of *what*?), and at the defaults the curve is already flat at ~1.0 across most of the visible width, so the "transition" the whole framework is about is happening off to the left of where my attention was. - **γ Calculator** — worked — intuitive to use, confusing to interpret — The presets are the best onboarding on the whole site: ideal gas 1, liquid water 4, galaxy 17, ferromagnet 100, BEC a million, superconductor ten million. Concrete things, real numbers, immediate feel for the scale. Then the page tells you the formula is **inverted** — superconductors and BECs, the most perfectly-coordinated matter that exists, come out with the *flattest* curves, which is backwards from reality — and that the tool is kept up as a demonstration of the flaw. I appreciate the confession. But I'd already spent five minutes building a mental model out of an equation the site knows is wrong, because the Tools page card gave me no warning. Compare the Phase Boundary Visualizer, whose card says "catch a refuted formula being wrong at every stop" before you click. That's the right way; γ Calculator should do the same.
- **Equation Anatomy** — worked — good until it wasn't — Steps 1's dimmer-switch and 1,000×-is-only-7-bigger explanations are excellent popular-science writing. The Hill-function aside undoes some of that goodwill for this audience.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What does A2ACW stand for? It's a headline number on the front page (3,308 sessions) and I never found it spelled out.
2. If "the ln is cosmetic," why is it in the equation on the homepage — is the famous one-equation actually two different equations depending on who's reading?
3. The site says the framework matches MOND but never beats it. So is the claim "we found a new reason MOND works," or "we accidentally rediscovered MOND"? Those feel like very different stories and I couldn't tell which one I was being told.
4. If the γ formula is inverted and the phase boundary formula is refuted, is the C(ρ) equation on the homepage still standing, or is it also known to be wrong? I couldn't work out which parts are still alive.
5. What is ρcrit in real units? Is it the density of anything I could point at?
6. Given 0 confirmed predictions and 4 refutations — what's the thing that would make this all worth it if it went right? Nobody ever told me what winning looks like.

### Verdict

The idea underneath this is genuinely graspable — marching band vs. wandering crowd, one curve from atoms to galaxies — and when the site explains it in words it explains it beautifully. But the words are buried and the symbols are on the front door, so most people arriving from Reddit will bounce before they reach the marching band. The radical honesty is the site's best feature and I mean that sincerely; I trusted it more with every failure it showed me. It just never paired that honesty with a reason to stay, so I finished the visit admiring the integrity of a project I still can't explain to a friend.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set: check the nav contract, check the taxonomy contract, check first-use definitions, then walk the cross-references and see what breaks.

**The nav contract.** Top navigation is flat — nine items, no dropdowns: Synchronism, Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. Three of those nine have a label that matches neither their URL nor their page title:

| Nav label | URL | H1 on arrival |
|---|---|---|
| Start Here | `/why-synchronism` | "Start Here: Why Synchronism?" |
| Test Roadmap | `/test-catalog` | "Test Roadmap" |
| Tools | `/interactive-tools` | "Interactive Tools" |

Individually trivial. Collectively it means nobody can guess a URL, and anyone citing a page in an issue or a paper has to copy-paste rather than type. It also predicts the next problem.

**The breadcrumbs describe a different site.** Every page I opened carries a three-part breadcrumb, and the middle segment is one of four category names: *Getting Started*, *Core Theory*, *Methodology*, *Predictions & Experiments*. **None of those four appear in the top navigation.** So the site has two mutually invisible taxonomies: a flat nine-item nav for wayfinding, and a four-category hierarchy that only exists in breadcrumbs.

Worse, the two disagree about where things live. Honest Assessment and Learning Paths are *top-level nav items*, but their breadcrumbs file them under `Home / Getting Started / …` — i.e. as children of a parent that has no page in the nav. Key Claims is filed under Getting Started too, and isn't in the nav at all. A breadcrumb's job is to answer "where am I in the structure?" These answer with a structure the reader has no other way to see.

I then tested whether the category segments are real. `/methodology` resolves — to the **Research Philosophy** page. That's a category that turns out to *be* one of its own children. `/research-philosophy` breadcrumbs as `Home / Methodology / Research Philosophy`, so following the parent crumb (if it's linked) lands you back where you started.

**Duplicate URLs.** Same pattern in two more places. `/start-here` and `/why-synchronism` both serve the Start Here page; `/test-catalog` and `/test-roadmap` both serve the Test Roadmap page. I fetch rendered text, so I can't tell a 301 redirect from a second render of the same component — if these are redirects, they're fine and this is a non-issue. What is *not* fine either way: the nav points at `/test-catalog` while body copy on `/tier-1-existing` links to `/test-roadmap` for the same destination ("The testing hierarchy (Tiers 1–4) is defined on the Test Roadmap"). Internal links to one canonical page shouldn't use two addresses.

**The taxonomy contract — this is the big one.** `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` is, honestly, the best-written page on the site from a docs perspective. It defines two badge families and explains why both exist:

- MRH-relationship (inventory status): Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative
- Descriptive (empirical relationship): Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed
- Deprecated, retained for back-compat: Validated, Strongly Supported

And it states the formatting rule precisely: *the color plus the leading words are the formal status; anything after a dash is free-text finding descriptor, not a new badge type.* That is exactly the right rule. It's how you keep a controlled vocabulary controlled.

Then I opened `/tier-1-existing`, the page where that vocabulary actually has to do work, and counted the status strings on twelve rows:

> Untested · Self-Eliminating-or-Tie · Never Run As Registered · Withdrawn · Disfavored on σ₈ (post-hoc) · Failed (Kill Criterion Fired) · Underpowered As Registered · Demoted to Tier-2 · Reclassified (Reparametrization) · Failed, Kill Criterion Fired · Failed, Ceiling Exceeded · Failed, Empty Intersection · Closed by Execution

Two of thirteen lead with a taxonomy badge. The rest lead with vocabulary that appears nowhere in the definitions: *Withdrawn*, *Demoted*, *Closed by Execution*, *Never Run As Registered*, *Self-Eliminating-or-Tie*, *Underpowered As Registered*. `/for-researchers` adds three more — *Registered Null*, *Structurally Unreachable*, *Naturalness Gap*. The tool cards on `/interactive-tools` add *Model Explainer* and *Artifact Lesson*.

I want to be precise about what's wrong, because the content itself is fine. "TEST-05 — Failed (Kill Criterion Fired)" is *already* the correct shape: badge first, descriptor after. "Never Run As Registered" and "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie" are not badges; they're findings that lost their badge. The fix is mechanical, not editorial — prefix each with the badge it belongs to (`Untested — never run as registered`, `Untested — self-eliminating-or-tie by construction`, `Superseded — demoted to Tier-2`) and the taxonomy holds without changing a single verdict. Right now a reader who studies the badge legend, as instructed, arrives at the tests page and finds the legend doesn't apply.

Related: `/key-claims` uses badges but doesn't explain them or link a legend inline — it references the taxonomy only in body prose. Any page that displays badges should link the legend from the first badge.

**First-use definitions.** Mixed, with a clear pattern: the *tutorial* pages are excellent and the *reference* pages are not.

`/mrh` is a model of how to do it — "The Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH) is a term proposed by Dennis Palatov, inspired by Markov blankets…", expansion before the parenthetical, formal definition in a blockquote right after, prev/next path links at the top. `/research-philosophy` does the same for A2ACW.

The reference pages don't. On `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`, and `/for-researchers` I counted these used with no expansion on first use: **BTFR, RAR, SPARC, DESI, ΛCDM, EFE, LIV, SME, AeST, BAO, GRB, LHAASO, NFW, CDM, IIT, DD**. The glossary has entries for most of them, which is the right safety net but not a substitute — an expansion costs four words inline and saves a round trip. `/for-researchers` is the page most likely to be read by someone arriving cold from a citation, and it's the densest of the three.

One correction to Pass 1, since it affects what gets fixed: **A2ACW *is* expanded on the landing page** — "~3,300 A2ACW (AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop) sessions", in the methodology note, which sits *above* the Research Activity stats block. Pass 1 reported it was never spelled out; that's not right. The real gap is narrower and elsewhere: the **glossary entry for A2ACW never expands the letters**. It reads "Protocol where AI agents stress-test each other's claims…" — a good definition of the thing, but a reader who bounces to the glossary precisely to decode the acronym doesn't get the decode. Also, the same count appears as "~3,300" in the note and "3,308" in the stat tile a screen later; harmless, but pick one.

**Hover-only definitions.** The glossary says "Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline." I can't verify the markup through rendered text, so I'm flagging the pattern rather than a confirmed defect: if inline definitions are hover-only, they're unavailable on touch devices and to keyboard navigation. The standard fix is to make the highlighted term focusable and dismissible, and to have it also work on tap/click.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Are `/start-here`, `/test-roadmap` and `/methodology` redirects or duplicate renders? If duplicates, which URL is canonical, and is anything (active-nav highlighting, analytics, sitemap) splitting across the pair?
2. Are the MRH-relationship badges (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative) actually applied anywhere in content? I saw the descriptive family used throughout and the MRH family only in the definitions and once on `/for-researchers`. If a family is defined but unused, either it's aspirational or I couldn't find where it's used — both are worth knowing.
3. Are any deprecated "Validated" / "Strongly Supported" badges still live on pages? The taxonomy says migration is incremental; nothing tells a reader which pages haven't been migrated yet.
4. ### Verdict

The writing quality here is well above average for a research site — caveats sit next to the artifacts they qualify, the badge taxonomy is thoughtfully designed down to the color-plus-leading-word rule, and tutorial pages like `/mrh` define terms exactly where they should. The problem is that the site's two organizing systems — the breadcrumb hierarchy and the badge vocabulary — are both documented and both unenforced, so the reference pages drift out of the structure the tutorial pages promise. Neither fix requires changing a single claim: reconcile the breadcrumb categories with the nav, and prefix the Tier 1 status strings with the badges that already exist.

**First: the numbers check out.** I want to lead with this because it is unusual. I recomputed every statistic I could:

| Claim | My check | Verdict |
|---|---|---|

The Hill identity is right too: tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ}−1]/[(1+x)^{2γ}+1], and dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x) is the correct derivative. I found no arithmetic errors on this site. Given how many framework sites I've seen where the σ's are decorative, that's a real credit.

**Second: the site's self-criticism is systematically one step short of its own math.** Four cases, all of which I could settle with a pencil and no new data.

**(a) The γ ansatz is refuted by the equation cited to motivate it — no condensed matter required.** `/coherence` says tanh was "motivated by its appearance in the Ising mean-field equation m = tanh(βJzm)." In that equation the prefactor is βJz, where z is the coordination number — the count of correlated neighbours. Identify γ with βJz and the framework's own motivation demands **γ ∝ N_corr**. The framework asserts γ = 2/√N_corr. Worse: mean-field order requires βJz > 1, and 2/√N_corr < 1 for every N_corr > 4 — so under the borrowed equation, *every system the framework calls strongly correlated would have no ordered phase at all*.

`/phase-boundary-visualizer` and `/gamma-calculator` both flag the inversion, but both frame it empirically ("BCS and BECs have the sharpest transitions in nature; the formula gives them the flattest"). That framing invites the reply "maybe γ isn't sharpness." The internal version admits no such reply: the ansatz contradicts the single equation the functional form was borrowed from, with zero empirical input. That is the stronger statement and it isn't on the site.

**(b) B_max = 1/Ω_m is a dimensional category error, not merely "asserted."** `/parameter-derivations` grades it "Speculative — Asserted, Not Derived," which is correct but too kind. Ω_m = ρ_m/ρ_crit,cosmo is matter relative to the *closure* density. A bound on how much a galaxy's baryonic gravity can be boosted has no business referencing closure density — a galaxy is a 10⁵–10⁶ overdensity. The only cosmological ratio with the right referent is Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 0.315/0.0493 = **6.4**, giving f_DM,max = 1 − 1/6.4 = **0.843**.

This matters because `/tier-1-existing` reports TEST-10 as **FAILED — ceiling exceeded**, citing median f_DM = 0.755 against a 0.685 cap. Under the only dimensionally defensible ceiling, **0.755 < 0.843 and the median passes.** `/parameter-derivations` does say the interpretation is "convention-dependent, affecting reported exclusion percentages" — but that caveat appears on exactly one page, and the two pages a researcher actually cites (`/tier-1-existing`, `/honest-assessment`) both present TEST-10 as a clean kill. If the kill survives, it must survive on a different statistic than the median — say the upper tail — and the ledger should say which.

**(c) TEST-11's "+18σ Cassini squeeze" is a rediscovery, and the prior art is uncited.** Free-fit γ = 0.489 ⇒ Hill exponent 2γ = 0.978 ≈ 1 ⇒ C = x/(x+2). That is MOND's **simple μ function**. Its Newtonian return is a *power law*, ~2(1+x)^{−2γ}. I ran the envelope: at Saturn, g_⊙ = GM/r² = 6.5×10⁻⁵ m/s², so x = g/a₀ ≈ 5.4×10⁵, and the fractional deviation from Newton is ≈ 2·x^{−0.978} ≈ **5×10⁻⁶**, i.e. an anomalous acceleration ~3×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² against a Cassini-ranging bound orders of magnitude tighter. Excluded by ~4 decades — direction and severity match the site's 18σ.

But this is *why McGaugh's ν = 1/(1−e^{−√y}) uses an exponential return in the first place. The solar-system exclusion of power-law-return interpolating functions has been standard MOND literature for two decades.* `/test-catalog` frames TEST-11 as "an internal-consistency squeeze" and cites nobody. Given that `/for-researchers` already records the local-density no-go being scope-corrected after a screening-literature counterexample surfaced, this is the same failure class recurring one artifact later.

**(d) A refuted formula is still load-bearing in the quantum sector.** `/measurement-without-observers` gives the collapse mechanism as:

> N_corr ↑ → γ = 2/√N_corr ↓ → MRH shrinks → correlations exceed MRH

That is the identical formula `/phase-boundary-visualizer` badges Audited-Negative / refuted, used here with no such badge — the only caveat on the page is about calibration. And the sign matters directionally: correct the inversion so γ rises with N_corr and the chain runs *backwards* — adding correlated particles would widen the MRH and suppress decoherence. The measurement story doesn't degrade under the audit, it reverses.

**Third: the "fits galaxy rotation as well as MOND" claim needs a qualifier.** Pass 1 picked out `/honest-assessment`'s line — "fits galaxy rotation, but only as well as MOND, and never better" — as the site's clearest sentence. From a referee's chair it's the site's most *generous* sentence, on two counts that `/galaxy-rotation` itself supplies:

1. That page states plainly that "**no algebraic chain from C(ρ) to g_obs exists anywhere in the framework**," and that the SPARC fits use **McGaugh's ν function**, with Synchronism contributing only a scatter hypothesis. The equation that fits the galaxies is MOND's.
2. When C(ρ) *is* used as the interpolating function, the version with the framework's own γ prescription is refuted at ΔBIC = +184. The version that ties requires γ = 0.489 — the value at which the compander *is* a known MOND function — and `/gamma-calculator` states its presets are "back-fits, not measurements."

So the honest form is narrower: *the equation ties MOND at exactly the one parameter value where it becomes a MOND function, and that value was read off the same data.* Every ingredient is already on the site; they're just on three different pages.

**Fourth: two tool pages disagree about galaxies.** `/gamma-calculator` presets "Galaxy — SPARC best fit: N_corr = 17, γ = 0.485." `/phase-boundary-visualizer` marks "Galaxies / ideal gas: γ = 2.0 (N_corr = 1)." **Finding 1 — the framework has exactly one live discriminating prediction, it is runnable today at zero cost, and the published data already excludes it. It is not in the ledger.**

This is the most important thing I found and I want to be precise about it.

`/key-claims` presents a₀ = cH₀/2π as Claim 3's principal quantitative evidence ("within 13%"). `/parameter-derivations` grades it *Reparametrization — dimensional analysis*. `/open-questions` states there are no epoch predictions. Those three positions are not jointly stable, because the identity is written in H₀, and H is not a constant.

There is a fork here that the site never states:

- **Branch A — the relation is dynamical** (a₀ tracks the expansion rate, as any "coherence transition set by the cosmic horizon" story requires). Then a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π. At z = 1, H/H₀ = 1.76; at z = 2, 2.97; at z = 2.5, 3.68. In the deep-MOND regime V⁴ = G M_b a₀, so the BTFR zero point must shift by Δlog M_b = −log₁₀(a₀(z)/a₀(0)) = **0.25 dex at z=1, 0.47 dex at z=2, 0.57 dex at z=2.5**. Equivalently, discs at cosmic noon sitting at g(R½) ≈ (3–11)·a₀(0) would instead sit at g ≈ (0.8–3)·a₀(z), i.e. deep in the modified regime, and should show *large* phantom-mass fractions.
- **Branch B — the relation is a present-epoch numerical coincidence.** Then it is not a derivation of anything, and Claim 3's only surviving quantitative success is numerology.

Branch A is *engageable*, and the literature leans against it. Milgrom's own analysis of the z ≈ 1–2.5 kinematics (arXiv:1703.06110) shows *constant*-a₀ MOND reproduces them, and Lelli et al. (2023, arXiv:2302.00030) fit a z = 2.24 disc with the unchanged local a₀ where a₀ ∝ H(z) would demand ≈3.4×. Against that, the BTFR-normalization route I reached for first is contested rather than clean: Milgrom (2017) argues the mass–asymptotic-speed relation needs an asymptotic V∞ that high-z rotation curves do not reach, so a 0.25–0.57 dex zero-point shift is not straightforwardly readable off these data, and the high-z systematics budget (M/L, pressure-support corrections, inclination, beam smearing) is large enough that I would not assert exclusion without writing it down. I also can't cite Genzel et al. (2017)'s low f_DM values as current — that group revised them upward in Nestor Shachar et al. (2023). **So: leans against, plausibly decidable on the RAR axis rather than the BTFR axis, not excluded on anything I can verify from this site plus a literature skim.**

Three consequences, in descending order of how confident I am:

1. **The gap is real regardless of which way the test lands.** `/test-roadmap` claims "all zero-cost recommendations have been executed or closed as of July 2026," and `/open-questions` states there are no epoch predictions at all. Meanwhile `/key-claims` leans on a₀ = cH₀/2π as its principal quantitative evidence. A framework cannot cite H₀ as evidence and simultaneously have nothing to say about H(z). Whatever the verdict, the *fork* belongs on `/parameter-derivations`, and "all $0 work closed" is wrong while it isn't stated.
2. **This probably does not discriminate against MOND**, and I initially thought it did. Milgrom proposes a₀ ~ cH/2π himself, with the same 2π — so an epoch-dependent a₀ is prior art in exactly the way the parent relation is, and running it would test a shared assumption rather than separate the two frameworks. The site's "0 of 24 discriminate" verdict survives this; I withdraw the under-claim reading.
3. **The right observable is probably not the one I reached for.** If this is revisited, the RAR axis — directly fitting a₀ from high-z rotation curves, which others have already done — is stronger than the BTFR zero point and doesn't depend on reaching V∞.

Fair caveat on the whole finding: an epoch-dependent a₀ has been raised before (it is implicit in every horizon-coincidence account of a₀, and there is a small a₀(z) literature including scale-invariant-vacuum treatments). It is not novel to Synchronism. The defensible version of my complaint is narrow and I want it recorded that way: **the site is silent on the epoch consequence of its own headline relation, and that silence is currently presented as completeness.**

**Finding 2 — TEST-04a is adjudicated against the wrong DESI paper, and DESI's own modified-gravity analysis is uncited.**

The framework's registered prediction is a ~12% suppression of fσ₈ at z = 0.51. The site adjudicates this against a full-shape fσ₈ point, calls it "underpowered as registered," then reaches for a σ₈ comparison and calls it "disfavored 2.4σ" while correctly flagging that σ₈ is a GR-conditioned statistic and therefore circular for a modified-gravity claim.

Both moves are avoidable, because DESI published the purpose-built statistic. **arXiv:2411.12026, "Modified Gravity Constraints from the Full Shape Modeling of Clustering Measurements from DESI 2024"**, reports μ₀ = 0.05 ± 0.22 in ΛCDM and Σ₀ = 0.008 ± 0.045, with binned 2-, 4- and 8-parameter MG parameterizations all consistent with GR. A ~12% growth-rate suppression at z ≈ 0.5 maps onto μ₀ of order −0.4 to −0.5; against that posterior it is roughly a **2σ** disfavor — a real constraint, not circular, and stronger than "underpowered." (The framework should do the mapping properly through the growth ODE rather than by eye; my point is that the statistic exists and is uncited.) The complementary number, growth index γ = 0.580 ± 0.110 from DR1 ShapeFit + peculiar velocities, tells the same story.

This paper appears nowhere on `/tier-1-existing`, `/test-catalog`, or `/open-questions`. For a framework whose one cosmological prediction is growth suppression, not citing DESI's growth-suppression paper is the single clearest literature-placement failure on the site.

That is a mild *preference* for suppressed growth, in the framework's direction. It doesn't rescue anything (the framework has no field equation to source suppression from, as `/honest-assessment` says), but it is the one place the direction isn't disfavored, and the site's ledger doesn't know it exists.
- DESI DR2's evolving-dark-energy result is the largest live anomaly in the field. A framework claiming quantum-to-cosmic scope that says nothing about w₀wₐ should say so explicitly in the gaps list, alongside "no lensing" and "no field equation."

**Finding 4 — TEST-10's ceiling was decidable a priori, and against the wrong quantity either way.**

Abundance matching (Behroozi et al., Moster et al.) has the stellar-to-halo mass ratio peaking near 0.02 and falling by more than an order of magnitude toward dwarf scales, so halo-scale f_DM ≳ 0.95 is the *generic* case, not the exception. Any framework asserting f_DM ≤ 68.5% universally was contradicted by a decade-old, model-independent result before anyone opened SPARC.

That matters for how the ledger counts. TEST-10 is presented as a $0 execution win — a kill fired on real data. It was actually available with no data at all. Counting it as an executed refutation alongside TEST-09 inflates the apparent yield of the testing program, and TEST-09's BTFR kill has the same ancestry (the n → 2 deep-limit slope follows from the bounded boost, which follows from the same undefended ceiling). Two of the four headline refutations descend from one asserted constant.

**Finding 5 — the A2ACW null is the only publishable artifact here, and it is currently under-powered and mis-scoped.**

`/for-researchers` and `/research-philosophy` report: 3,308 sessions → ~47 internally-consistent survivors (1.42% yield) → 6 sent to external audit → 0 survived; and a retrospective in which 6 canonical discoveries (Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking, Noether) were all false-flagged as reparametrizations, summarized as Youden's J = 0.

I think this is genuinely the interesting object on the site — a dated, adversarial, publicly-audited yield measurement for machine-generated theory. I would referee it toward publication. But not in this form:

1. **N = 6 cannot support "J = 0" as a measurement.** Zero hits in six trials bounds sensitivity at ≤ 39% (95%, one-sided: 1 − 0.05^(1/6) = 0.393). The honest statement is "sensitivity is bounded below ~40%; the design has essentially no resolution," not "sensitivity is zero." The site is unusually good at catching this kind of false precision elsewhere — it drops the σ on the eight consciousness estimates, and Pass 3 flagged the four-significant-figure 18σ — so this is an inconsistent application of its own standard.
2. **The control arm tests the wrong thing.** Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking, Noether are the most corpus-resident results in physics. A detector whose demotion criterion is "resembles known physics" *must* fire on them; that is not a measurement of the detector, it is a restatement of its definition. The site half-sees this — it ran the temporal-asymmetry variant (0/6) and registered a cross-vendor corpus control as pending — but the framing still treats the retrospective as an evaluation. The design that would work is post-cutoff genuine discoveries, where in-corpus and genuinely-novel come apart.
3. **The program-level null has no denominator.** "0 of 6 survived expert audit" is a rate with no reference rate. To claim A2ACW yields no discoveries you need the identical pipeline run over a corpus with a known non-zero discovery rate. Without it, the null is scoped to "this framework, this protocol, this corpus" — which is still worth publishing, but as a case study in a metascience or AI-evaluation venue, not as a general finding about adversarial AI collaboration. The site's own phrasing ("the contribution is the controlled null result") over-claims the word *controlled*: the control is registered, not run.

I'd also note the audit tests the *auditor*, not the *generator*. Whether A2ACW produces discoveries and whether A2ACW recognizes them are separate questions with separate error rates, and the site's headline number conflates them.

**Finding 6 — one kill criterion can only fire against the framework.**

`/key-claims`, Claim 1: "Design a noise environment where the synchronization model predicts resync outperforms isolation, but standard decoherence theory predicts it doesn't… If isolation wins uniformly, the synchronization ontology adds nothing."

Standard open-systems theory already predicts that active resynchronization (spin echo, CPMG, Uhrig sequences, all cited on the page) beats passive isolation in any non-Markovian bath. So the "resync wins" branch confirms textbook physics, and only the "isolation wins" branch bears on the claim. That is a one-sided criterion: it can refute, never support. The site has precise vocabulary for this — it labels TEST-02 *self-eliminating-or-tie* — and simply hasn't applied it in the quantum sector. Same class as Pass 3's observation that a refuted γ formula is still load-bearing on `/measurement-without-observers`: the empirical sectors have been audited hard and the quantum sector has not.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — **numerically exact, physically empty** — I checked every displayed value against the closed form. C = 0.5 requires tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = ½ ⇒ x = e^(artanh(½)/γ) − 1 = e^0.274653 − 1 = **0.31607**; the tool shows 0.316·ρcrit. C(ρcrit) at γ=2 is (2⁴−1)/(2⁴+1) = 15/17 = **0.88235** ✓; C(10ρcrit) = (11⁴−1)/(11⁴+1) = 0.999863 → 0.9999 ✓; C(100ρcrit) = 1 − 2/(101⁴+1) ✓. The implementation is clean and the caveat that ρcrit is a saturation knee rather than a midpoint is correct and well-stated. But the tool exposes a function, not a physical claim: ρ has no units and no system is placed on the axis, so nothing on screen can be checked against the world. The fix is also the site's most persuasive available demonstration — put real densities on the axis and the user discovers, unaided, that no galactic disc comes within three orders of magnitude of the knee. That is the mass-cancellation no-go, made visible, using the tool that already exists.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — **the most informative page on the site, and it doesn't know it** — This tool answers Pass 3's open question directly: the violet "Synchronism" curve is the compander at γ = 2 (not McGaugh's ν relabelled), it is computed honestly, and it *fails visibly* — the page reports max C ≈ 0.001 for DDO 154, so the curve sits on the baryon line and the model contributes essentially nothing. A framework site that ships a working plot of its own model producing zero effect is doing something I have not seen elsewhere, and the caveat placement (V_flat "taken from existing SPARC/MOND fits — not predicted") is exactly right. Three defects, in ascending order of seriousness. (1) The amber "hand-tuned stand-in" shares axes with computed curves — Pass 2 flagged this and I agree it will travel without its caption. (2) The construction v = √(v_b² + [V_flat·C]²) takes the observed flat velocity as an input, so no version of this plot could ever have predicted an amplitude; the page should say so. (3) The green "MOND" curve uses ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) — the simple-μ form whose power-law Newtonian return is precisely what TEST-11 excludes at 18σ. The site is comparing itself against a MOND variant it has already killed on another page. ### Unanswered Questions

1. Is a₀ = cH₀/2π meant dynamically (a₀ tracks H(z)) or as a present-epoch coincidence? Everything about how Claim 3 uses it implies the former; the site says nothing either way; and the former is engageable against existing high-z kinematics, which lean against it. What is the high-z systematics floor, and does the signal clear it?
2. Was the free-γ SPARC fit ever run with the Cassini bound as a prior, or with the interpolating-function return exponent as the fitted quantity? The ~1σ likelihood gap between free-γ and McGaugh means SPARC cannot separate them — all the discriminating power is in the solar-system tail. That reframes TEST-11 from "empty intersection" to "the data that matters is not galactic," which is a much more useful statement.
3. What is the framework's predicted μ₀ (and Σ₀)? Without those two numbers it cannot be placed on the same axes as every other modified-gravity proposal, and every cosmological adjudication on the site will keep reaching for GR-conditioned proxies.
4. `/test-catalog` references a "scale-universal tanh-log QUMOND realization" for TEST-11. QUMOND has a Lagrangian; `/honest-assessment` says the framework has none. Which is it, and if a QUMOND embedding exists, why doesn't it supply the missing lensing and growth predictions?
5. Has any A2ACW candidate ever been evaluated by someone with no access to the framework's vocabulary? Every audit described is internal to the corpus that generated the claims.
6. Given 0/6 survival and a 1.42% internal yield, what is the pre-registered stopping rule for the program? At some point "keep auditing" stops being research and becomes a maintenance schedule for a null.

### Verdict

There is nothing new in the physics, and the site says so more clearly than most authors would — the galaxy sector is a strict submodel of MOND that ties at exactly the parameter value where it *becomes* MOND, and every parameter traces to a 1970s–2010s result. What the site does not yet see is that its own honesty has a blind spot with a specific shape: it audits assertions relentlessly and audits its *literature position* not at all, so it re-derives standard constraints and calls them internal squeezes, adjudicates a growth prediction without citing the field's growth paper, and leaves its one genuinely epoch-dependent claim unregistered and untested while announcing that all zero-cost work is closed. Two concrete recommendations: state the a₀ epoch fork and stop claiming the zero-cost queue is closed while it is unstated (I initially read this as an unregistered MOND discriminator and withdrew that — Milgrom proposes a₀ ~ cH/2π himself, so it tests a shared assumption, and the BTFR route I reached for is contested at high z; the *silence* is the defect, not a hidden refutation), and re-scope the A2ACW null as a controlled case study with a real control arm and honest N=6 bounds — that null, and not the physics, is the thing I would sign off on.
