# Topic: Wide Binary Density-Dependence: Deriving the Predicted Slope

## Question

What functional form does Synchronism predict for the wide-binary anomaly's dependence on local stellar density? What is the predicted slope d(anomaly)/d(log ρ_*), its normalization, the density range over which it applies, and how does it compare to what Hernandez et al. (2024), Pittordis & Sutherland (2023), and the Banik group actually measured?

## Context

Visitor Pass 4 (2026-04-30) identified this as the single highest-leverage missing piece in the entire framework. TEST-02 (Wide Binary Density Dependence) is the only Tier-1 test that genuinely discriminates Synchronism from standard MOND — MOND predicts the wide-binary anomaly independent of local stellar density, while Synchronism's environmental coherence ansatz implies the anomaly should scale with ρ_*. But the site only says "the wide-binary anomaly scales with local stellar density" without specifying the predicted slope, normalization, or density range.

Without those, any observed correlation can be claimed as confirmation and any null result can be dismissed as out of scope. That is not a falsifiable prediction — it is a qualitative narrative with a number attached to make it look like one.

The `/wide-binary-test` URL also 404s as of 2026-04-30, which means there is no dedicated test page and the prediction is currently buried inside `/tier-1-existing`.

## Why It Matters

Without a quantitative slope prediction, TEST-02 is not falsifiable. It is a vague qualitative claim masquerading as a Tier-1 prediction. If the archive contains a derivation of the slope — even an order-of-magnitude estimate from the coherence equation — it should be surfaced on the site immediately with a proper test page. If no derivation exists, the explorer should attempt one from first principles using the framework's RAR scatter ansatz, which is already named on `/galaxy-rotation` as "Synchronism's specific contribution."

The Gaia DR3 data exists. Hernandez et al. and Pittordis & Sutherland have already measured the anomaly as a function of various parameters. The question is whether the Synchronism prediction is specific enough to be compared to those measurements, or whether it can be fit to anything the data shows.

- Synchronism research archive: search "wide binary", "wide-binary", "stellar density slope", "TEST-02" in `Research/sessions/` — look for any derivation attempt, not just references to the anomaly

- If no derivation is found: start from C(ρ) and ask what it implies for the effective gravitational coupling at separations of 0.1–10 kAU as a function of local stellar density
