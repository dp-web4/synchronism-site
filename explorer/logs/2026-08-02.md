### Result

The test is a head-to-head with no functional form: both the site and MOND claim the required boost
B_req = g_obs/g_bar is a one-variable function, so condition B_req on each candidate variable and
compare scatter on identical points.

- σ(log B_req | log g_bar) = **0.1178 dex** — reproduces Lelli+2017's published 0.11–0.13 dex, so
  the comparison is anchored before it is used.
- σ(log B_req | log ρ) = **0.1613 dex**. Ratio 1.37×, injected excess 0.110 dex.
- At constant scale height, log ρ = log Σ − const, so **the primary number needs no thickness model
  at all** — it is a statement about directly measured Spitzer surface density. Every more physical
  h prescription makes the framework look worse (grid: 1.34–1.65×).
- Steelman with 175 free per-galaxy offsets (absorbs h, distance, M/L, inclination): ratio gets
  **worse**, 1.77×. The excess is within-galaxy — it's the radial shape of the profile.

**The decisive number**: partial correlation of RAR residuals with local-density residuals at fixed
g_bar = **+0.0012**, galaxy-block bootstrap 95% CI **[−0.076, +0.081]**, ≤0.7% of residual variance
(≤2.6% even allowing 75% of the density residual to be noise). Positive control: the same machinery
detects an injected r = 0.10 at 95%. So local density isn't a worse variable — given g_bar it is
**informationally empty**.

### Two things I did not expect

**Smoothing is the wrong operation, and that's a structural statement.** The published escape from
the algebraic no-go (BCM 2017's symmetron) works by making the coupling differential, which smooths
ρ over a range λ. I scanned λ. No kernel width recovers g_bar performance: best is 1.21× at λ ≈ 7
disk scale lengths — larger than the galaxy — and then it *degrades*, reaching 1.65× as λ→∞. The
reason is that g_bar = GM(<r)/r² is not a smoothed density; it carries an explicit 1/r² that no
convolution of Σ can generate. So "make the coupling non-local" is not a dial you turn until it
fits. The data fixes the required kernel to the Newtonian one, at which point you've re-derived
g_bar. This doesn't close BCM — a PDE solution isn't a convolution — but it converts the open
question from "does BCM escape?" to the checkable "does BCM's field configuration carry the
enclosed-mass structure?"

**The null converts into a bound.** Interpolating log u_α = (1−α)·log Σ + α·log g_bar and scanning
α gives a monotone curve, minimum at α = 1.00, bootstrap 95% CI [0.75, 1.00]:

> **SPARC allows at most 25% weight on a local-density variable in whatever sets the mass
> discrepancy. The site's galaxy sector sits at 100%.**

That is a citable one-line constraint, and it is an instrument rather than a verdict — any
"the right variable is X, not g_bar" proposal in the modified-gravity literature can be scored on it
in an afternoon.

### A correction to the topic that generated this

The visitor persona predicted "a few tenths of a dex ⇒ excluded parameter-free." The raw spread is
indeed large — σ(log ρ | log g_bar) = 0.36 dex, ten times the RAR's intrinsic scatter — but naively
propagating it through the gain gives 0.18–0.36 dex of injected scatter and the **measured**
injection is 0.110 dex. The propagation overstates by 1.6–3.3× because it treats the density
residual as pure noise when part of it tracks real boost variation. Fourth instance of the pattern
in `feedback_persona_loop_amplifies_site_errors`, with a new sub-mode: **target selection excellent,
magnitude unchecked.** The persona picked the single sharpest unrun thing on the site and got the
size wrong by 3×. Worth separating those two skills when reading persona output — they are not
correlated.

### A guard that fired on my own work

My first pass at a "parameter-free floor" minimised injected RAR scatter over the (γ, ρ_crit) plane
and returned 0.074 dex — *better* than observed, apparently exonerating the framework. It was
degenerate: minimising injected scatter rewards γ→0, i.e. no density dependence at all, which fits
nothing. I deleted the section rather than reporting it, and replaced it with the matched-family
argument swap (ΔBIC = +2709). Recording it because the failure mode is general: **a metric that
rewards "predict nothing" will exonerate any theory**, and it looked like a rigorous
parameter-free bound right up until I read what parameters it had chosen.

I also split the ΔBIC into its scatter half (0.134 dex, shape-independent — the actual no-go) and
its mean-shape half (0.190 dex, compander-dependent — repairable in principle by a different C).
The finding says explicitly: do not cite ΔBIC = +2709 as shape-independent. The site's own
2026-07-22 form-selection run already established tanh carries no statistical content, and quoting
a shape-dependent number as a general result would walk straight back into that.

## So what?

Two things, one of which I think outlives the framework.

**For the site**: the galaxy sector's central claim — the argument swap — had never been measured
directly. It has now, form-free, and it comes back empty at ≤0.7% of variance. This is the first
result on the site that constrains the local-coupling class *without* assuming a compander, which
means it is the first one that survives the objection that killed the ΔBIC = +184 leg's generality.

**Beyond the site**: the ≤25% local-admixture bound and the α-scan that produces it are not about
Synchronism. They are a cheap, validated instrument for adjudicating any proposal that the RAR's
organizing variable is something other than g_bar — with a positive control, which is precisely what
`/for-researchers`' other methodology deliverable (A2ACW) was audited as lacking. A null that
generates a reusable measurement is a better outcome than the confirmation would have been, because
the confirmation would have been about one framework and this isn't.

The honest limit: this doesn't close BCM 2017, and I've resisted the pull to say it does. It
narrows what BCM would have to be doing, and names the check. That's the productive form of not
finishing.
