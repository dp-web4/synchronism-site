## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The hook is genuinely great and the honesty is refreshing, but the beginner path throws me off a cliff at step 3 and never tells me why I should care that it failed.

### Journey

Clicked from Reddit. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is exactly the kind of thing that makes me click. Good. Then directly under it:

> C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is doing there. I have three Greek letters in one line. My eyes slid right off it. What saved me was the marching band line right after — *"a marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence."* That I got instantly, and it's the best sentence on the site. If the analogy came *before* the equation instead of after, I'd have arrived at the math already wanting to read it.

Also above the fold: "Prospective predictions confirmed: **0**" and "6 refutations." I actually respected that a lot — it's the opposite of every crackpot physics site — but I also thought "wait, so the answer to the headline is no?" Nobody tells me why to keep reading at that moment. I only found the answer nine pages later on /honest-assessment ("the honest map of what failed is the actual product"), which is a great line that's stranded in the wrong place.

Hit **Beginner Path**. Step 1 (/why-synchronism) was the best page I read. Plain problem statement — physics is split into small-stuff rules and big-stuff rules and they don't talk — plus the crowd/band analogy again, plus a straight list of what worked and what didn't. "Predicted 607K for YBCO; actual is 93K" is a failure I can understand without knowing what YBCO is.

Buried in that page: **"3,308 autonomous AI research sessions with no human intervention."** I stopped and reread that. That is the single most interesting fact on this entire website and it's sitting there in smaller type than the equation. A site that says "an AI ran three thousand physics research sessions by itself and here's the honest scorecard" is a story I'd send to friends. "One equation explains everything" is a story I've seen a hundred times and mostly from cranks.

Step 2 (/first-encounter) introduced "presence (ρ)" and then said presence is... density. I kept waiting for the part where presence turns out to be more than density and it didn't come on that page. Renaming a thing I already understand into a word I don't made me trust the site slightly less, not more. The MRH idea ("an atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy") landed fine — that's just "only nearby stuff matters," and the analogy sells it.

Step 3 (/core-idea) is where I bounced, and I want to be specific because this is a *beginner* path. It opens beautifully — tanh is "just an S-shaped curve, flat then steep then flat" and the whole thing is "a dimmer switch." Perfect. I was in. Then within the same page I hit: an "exact identity" with fractions and exponents; "no singularity, no free-energy functional"; "critical exponents (β, ν) are undefined by construction"; "abandoning criticality means abandoning universality classes entirely"; and the phrase "decorative vocabulary, not physics."

I could not tell you whether that section is the authors admitting a mistake, defending a choice, or something else. I'm honestly not sure whether "the equation is secretly a Hill function" is bad news or a neat discovery. It reads like two different documents spliced together — a friendly explainer and a technical audit memo — with no signal about which parts I'm allowed to skip. On a beginner path, step 3 of 6, that's fatal. A normal Reddit visitor closes the tab here.

I skipped to **Tools**, which is where the site actually works.

Then I clicked **Consciousness Threshold Demo** because "consciousness" is the most clickable word on the site. The page is an elaborate explanation of why its own headline number doesn't mean anything — eight approaches converge on 0.50, but they all share the same assumptions, and 0.50 is the middle of a 0-to-1 range so of course things land there. I appreciate that they say it. I also felt a little baited: the title promises a result and the page delivers a debunking of itself. Maybe rename it to what it actually is.

Ended on /glossary (72 terms, alphabetical, genuinely useful) and /honest-assessment, which I'd rank as the second-best page on the site.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (/coherence-explorer) — worked — intuitive — Best thing on the site for me. Two sliders, a curve that moves, and the log/linear toggle actually taught me something: the "S-curve" only looks like an S in log scale. Ten seconds of dragging γ taught me more than the equation did. Only gap is that the numeric readouts are unlabeled — I don't know what a "good" C value is.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — worked — mostly intuitive — Also excellent. Pick a galaxy, see the gray "what gravity predicts from visible stuff" line drop away while the blue observed dots stay flat. *That gap is the dark matter puzzle* — I have watched multiple videos about this and this chart explained it faster than any of them. Then the site plots its own violet prediction and shows it failing to close the gap, which is a genuinely unusual and admirable thing to put on your own website. Confusing bit: the amber "drawn to fit, NOT computed" curve is still on the plot, and a fake curve sitting next to real ones took me a while to sort out.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (/consciousness-demo) — worked — confusing by design — Eight hoverable methods converging on 0.499. The convergence looks impressive until the page explains that 0.50 is just the middle of the range so everything lands there. Effective as a lesson, mismatched with its title.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If presence is density, why is it called presence? What does the framework get from the new word?
2. The site's "coherence" is backwards from the coherence in every quantum video I've seen. Which one is the misnomer, and does the framework's version connect to the quantum one at all, or do they just share a word?
3. Is the "the equation is actually a Hill function" discovery bad news for the framework or a neat simplification? /core-idea states it as fact but never tells me how to feel about it, and the emotional register of that section is impossible to read.
4. With 0 confirmed predictions and 6 refutations — is the framework dead? Are people still working on it? What would it take to revive it? I couldn't find a straight answer.
5. Why does γ default to 0.49 in the tool when the equation is supposed to give γ = 2/√N_corr, and other pages use γ = 2?
6. Who ran the 3,308 AI research sessions and how? That's the story I actually want to read and there's no page for it.

### Verdict

The site is honest in a way I've never seen a physics site be honest, and two of its interactive tools taught me real things about dark matter and S-curves in under a minute each. But the beginner path is mislabeled — steps 1 and 2 are genuinely beginner, step 3 is a technical audit memo wearing a beginner page's clothes, and that's where a Reddit visitor closes the tab. The deeper problem is that the site's hook ("one equation for everything") and its actual product (a rigorously documented failure, produced by thousands of autonomous AI sessions) are two different and mismatched stories — and the second one is far more interesting than the first. Lead with the real story and I'd come back.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is unusually disciplined — real breadcrumbs, a real glossary, a real status taxonomy — but the taxonomy exists in four separate copies that have already drifted apart, and the site's single most important symbol (γ) carries two incompatible one-line definitions depending on which page you land on.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs site before a release: nav inventory first, then the reference layer (glossary, taxonomy), then a sample of content pages to see whether the reference layer is actually being honored.

**Nav and breadcrumbs — genuinely good.** Eight top-level items, each with a descriptive subtitle ("Glossary — 72 terms defined", "Test Roadmap — 24 proposed experiments by tier"). Every page I opened had a working breadcrumb in a consistent `Home / Section / Page` shape, with four sections in play: Getting Started, Core Theory, Methodology, Interactive Tools. Prev/next pagers on the path pages are correctly labeled with destination titles, not bare arrows. This is above average for a site of this size and I want to say so before the complaints start.

**The reference layer is where it comes apart.** The validation badges are the site's core reader-facing convention — every claim carries one — so I went looking for the one place they're defined. I found four:

1. `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, self-labeled **"Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)"**
2. `/research-philosophy` § "Validation Badge Taxonomy" — a full second copy
3. `/glossary` — a third copy, clustered as a badge group
4. `/test-roadmap` § "Validation Badge Guide" — a fourth, abbreviated copy

They have already diverged. `Failed` is defined as *"Prediction contradicted by data (with specific error)"* on the canonical page and *"Prediction tested and wrong. Kept visible as permanent record."* on `/research-philosophy`. Those aren't paraphrases of each other — one specifies an artifact requirement (a specific error figure), the other specifies a retention policy. A writer applying the badge gets different instructions depending on which copy they read.

The routing makes this worse rather than better. The top nav's own description of Research Philosophy is *"Validation taxonomy, kill criteria, methodology."* So a reader who wants the taxonomy will click exactly the right-looking link and land on the **non-canonical** copy. Meanwhile the canonical copy is filed under **Getting Started**, on a page called Honest Assessment — the one section where a reader looking for a controlled vocabulary would never think to look.

**Badge vocabulary leaks.** Beyond the two documented families (MRH-relationship: Active-MRH / Parallel-Paths / Sidelined / Superseded / Audited-Negative; Descriptive: Untested / Speculative / Reparametrization / Failed), I collected status words in the wild that appear in no taxonomy: **Withdrawn** (landing page "Scientific Outcomes: Withdrawn: 1"; honest-assessment "Withdrawn claims: 1 (TEST-04, BAO modulation)"), **New Ontology** (`/key-claims`), **Post-diction** (defined only on `/research-philosophy`, not in the canonical list), and the five "Speculative sub-types" (Motivated Ansatz, Dimensional Analysis, Freeman's Law Re-expressed, Jeans Criterion, 5% Agreement / 3% Error) — which also appear only in the non-canonical copy. `/mrh` carries "Speculative — Theoretical Framework"; `/gamma-parameter` carries "Speculative — Motivated Ansatz — Not Derived"; `/coherence-function` carries "Speculative — Phenomenological Ansatz". Three different qualifier suffixes on the same base badge, none of them enumerated anywhere.

`/tools` adds a *third* vocabulary on top. Each tool card carries a chip row mixing three orthogonal dimensions with no visual distinction between them: difficulty (Beginner / Advanced), tool type (**Model Explainer**, **Artifact Lesson**), and validation status (Reparametrization, Speculative). "Artifact Lesson" and "Model Explainer" are not badges, but they sit in the same chip position as ones that are. I initially read "Artifact Lesson" as a validation verdict.

**The γ problem is the one I'd escalate.** γ appears on the landing page, in the headline equation, in the glossary, on three theory pages and in two tools. Here is every definition I collected, verbatim:

| Page | Definition of γ |
|---|---|
| Landing | "sharpness of transition" |
| `/coherence-function` | "coupling strength" |
| `/gamma-parameter` | "Universal coupling strength" — "how efficiently compatible presence within an MRH converts into coherent state transitions" |
| `/gamma-calculator` | "the steepness of the coherence S-curve — how abruptly a system switches from 'independent individuals' to 'acting as one'" |
| `/equation-walkthrough` | "2/√Ncorr — controls sigmoid sharpness" |

Two of these say γ is a *coupling* quantity; three say it is a *steepness* quantity. I'm not qualified to say whether those are the same thing physically — but I can say that the site never states they're the same thing, never picks one, and that the seam between them is precisely where the site's own most-discussed error lives. `/gamma-calculator` and `/phase-boundary-visualizer` both prominently warn that γ = 2/√Ncorr *"assigns the sharpest coherence transition to the least-correlated system... the opposite of real condensed-matter transitions."* That warning is legible **only** under the steepness reading. A reader who arrived via `/coherence-function` holding "coupling strength" in their head cannot parse why the warning is a warning. Fixing the physics is out of my scope; picking one gloss and propagating it is not, and it's cheap.

**"Presence" has the same shape of problem, one severity lower.** `/first-encounter`: *"presence is density — how much stuff is packed into a given space."* `/coherence-function`: *"compatible structural elements within the system's MRH. Physical density is one form of presence."* `/equation-walkthrough`: *"the density of compatible elements within the system's relevancy boundary."* The third is a reasonable merge of the first two, so the concept is recoverable — but the beginner page's flat "presence is density" is contradicted, not refined, by the theory page's "density is *one form of* presence," and no page marks the transition. Pass 1 read this as a pointless rename; from the doc side it's worse than that, because the payoff for the new word *is* stated on `/coherence-function` and never reaches the page where the word is introduced.

**MRH surface forms.** I counted six: MRH, Markov Relevancy Horizon, MRH boundary, relevancy horizon, relevance horizon, relevancy boundary. The `/mrh` page is excellent — it expands the acronym, and it actually explains *why* "Markov" (Markov blankets from probabilistic graphical models, extended to a dynamic scale-dependent boundary), which is more than most sites do for a borrowed term. But that same page has an "Open Gap" section floating "Relevancy Horizon" / "Relevance Horizon" as possible renames — while `/equation-walkthrough` is already shipping "relevancy boundary" in body copy as if the decision were made. An unresolved naming decision has leaked into published prose.

**Acronym-on-first-use is inconsistent, and the fallback is fragile.** `/first-encounter` and `/key-claims` do it correctly — `/key-claims` expands QM, MRH, CRT, PRL, DD, IIT, MOND, ΛCDM, RAR, SPARC, BEC/BCS, ENAQT all on first use, which is genuinely careful work. But `/coherence-function` — step 1 of the Intermediate path, i.e. the first page many readers hit after the beginner track — uses MRH, BCS, BEC, SPARC, MOND, RAR, BTFR and expands **none** of them. The site's stated strategy is *"Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline."* Hover is a supplement, not an expansion policy: it doesn't survive printing, copy-paste into a doc, touch devices, or screen readers. The glossary is strong enough that the fix is small — expand on first use per page, keep hover as the convenience layer.

**Counts that don't reconcile.** `/test-roadmap` says *"24 specific, falsifiable experiments organized into four tiers"* and the tier table sums correctly (10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24). But Tier 1 is listed as **10** while the IDs run **TEST-01 through TEST-11** — eleven identifiers. I assume the gap is TEST-04, which `/honest-assessment` records as withdrawn, and/or the TEST-03s / TEST-04a variants. Nothing on the roadmap page says any of that, so a reader who counts gets a discrepancy with no resolution path. Separately: only Tier 1 has IDs at all. The site cross-links tests by anchor (`/tier-1-existing#TEST-04a`), so 14 of the 24 experiments are currently uncitable.

**One rendering bug.** On `/key-claims`, the sentence pointing at the taxonomy renders as: *"Status labels (Speculativeuntestable as stated — ontological reframeNew Ontology, etc.)"* — badge label and tooltip text concatenated with no separator or spacing. Same pattern in the Claim 1 badge: "Speculative" and "(untestable as stated — ontological reframe)" run together.

**Tools.** `/tools` is titled "Interactive Tools" (and is the breadcrumb section name), but **Equation Anatomy** has no controls — it's a static six-step walkthrough with Next buttons. It's a good page; it just isn't interactive, and it's filed under a section whose name promises otherwise. Also: the tool is titled "Equation Anatomy" but lives at `/equation-walkthrough`. Title/URL mismatch, which hurts shared links and search. Naming across the seven tools uses five different nouns — Explorer, Plotter, Calculator, Visualizer, Demo — with no evident rule.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — intuitive — Clean single-input tool: Ncorr field, linear/log scale toggle, seven presets (ideal gas → BCS superconductor), live γ output, regime classification, S-curve, and a reference table. The best-designed tool I opened. Two doc notes: the default is Ncorr = 4 ("liquid water") with no stated reason for that default, and the tool's own caveat — that the formula gives the sharpest transition to the *least*-correlated system — contradicts the "coupling strength" gloss a reader would arrive with from `/coherence-function`. The caveat is well-written; it just assumes a definition the site doesn't consistently supply.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — clear, unusually well-framed — γ slider from 0 to 4 with regime cards and a "reality check" line at each stop. The intro is the best piece of instructional writing on the site: *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose... Your task is to catch the formula being wrong at every stop, not to learn its placements."* That's a stated user task in the second sentence, which is exactly what most docs pages fail to do. It also discloses that an earlier version "kept reporting that the animation out-taught the caveat" — self-documenting a usability finding, which I've rarely seen a site do. One structural note: BCS sits at γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ but the axis starts at 0, so it's rendered as an off-edge direction indicator; a log axis option (the γ Calculator already has one) would put it on the chart.
- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — worked — clear but mislabeled — Static six-step walkthrough, "Step 1 of 6" indicator, Next buttons, onward links to the γ Calculator and Parameter Derivations. Good content, not an interactive tool.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which of the four badge taxonomy copies is authoritative in practice? `/honest-assessment` self-declares canonical, but the nav routes taxonomy-seekers to `/research-philosophy`, and the two already disagree on `Failed`.
2. Is γ a coupling quantity or a steepness quantity? If both, what's the relationship, and which one should a reader hold while reading the sign-inversion caveat?
3. Are "Withdrawn", "New Ontology", "Post-diction", and the "Speculative — X" qualifier suffixes intended as badges, or as prose? If badges, why aren't they in the list?
4. Is "relevancy boundary" an approved synonym for MRH, or leakage from an unresolved rename?
5. Tier 1 is 10 tests but has 11 IDs — which ID is not a test, and where is that stated?
6. Will Tiers 2–4 get IDs? Without them, 14 of the 24 headline experiments can't be linked or cited.
7. Is the hover-tooltip glossary the intended acronym policy site-wide, or a stopgap? It determines whether the missing first-use expansions are a bug or a design choice.

### Verdict

Structurally this is a better-built site than most funded documentation projects — real breadcrumbs, real prev/next, a 72-term glossary with inline lookup, a status taxonomy applied consistently enough that I could audit it, and at least one tool page that opens by telling the user what their task is. The failures are all the same failure: **the reference layer is duplicated instead of referenced.** Four badge taxonomies, five glosses of γ, three definitions of presence, six surface forms of MRH — each copy was correct when written and they have drifted since, which is exactly what copies do. Consolidating to one source per concept and linking to it would close most of this log without a single new page.

The escalation is γ. It is the symbol in the headline equation, it drives two of the seven tools, and the site's most prominent self-criticism is only comprehensible under one of the two definitions currently in circulation. That is a terminology defect that undermines the site's best content, and it's fixable in an afternoon.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 18
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The math on this site is *correct* — I checked the identities and the tool readouts and they hold — but the site consistently stops one algebraic step short of its own sharpest conclusions, and two of its six refutations are the same underived assumption counted twice.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project," so I skipped the onboarding and went straight to `/parameter-derivations`, `/coherence-function`, `/gamma-parameter`, `/galaxy-rotation` and `/tier-1-existing`. Then I checked the tools against the equations, and finally read `/honest-assessment` and `/for-researchers` to see whether the scoreboard matched what I'd found.

**First, credit where it's due, because I came in expecting to write a demolition.** `/parameter-derivations` closes with *"zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations"* and then actually lists them: γ's CLT argument invokes iid scaling for explicitly correlated degrees of freedom (self-contradictory, correctly identified); A = 4π/(β_J²GR₀²) is off by 600× from its own stated formula; a₀ = cH₀/2π is attributed to Milgrom 1983 and shared with McCulloch, Verlinde and Smolin; Σ₀ is flagged as *"the a₀ row propagated through a linear relation, not a second derivation"*; 1/φ is badged **Fitted-Then-Named**. I have refereed papers that would not survive this page. The 6/6 demotion of previously-"Validated" badges to Reparametrization is the single most credible thing here.

**The core identity is right, and undersold.** `/core-idea` states

> tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^2γ − 1] / [(1+x)^2γ + 1]

which is just e^{γln(1+x)} = (1+x)^γ substituted into tanh. Fine. But write y = 1 + ρ/ρ_crit and n = 2γ and you get C = (yⁿ − 1)/(yⁿ + 1) = 2·[yⁿ/(1+yⁿ)] − 1 — an affine rescaling of a **Hill function with K = 1**. Now set n = 1, i.e. γ = 1/2:

> C = x/(x + 2) = u/(1 + u), with u ≡ ρ/(2ρ_crit)

That is *exactly* MOND's simple interpolating function, with ρ_crit → 2ρ_crit. And `/galaxy-rotation` reports that when γ is freed on the 2,807-point SPARC RAR, the fit converges to **γ ≈ 0.489**, i.e. n = 2γ = 0.978 — within 2% of the simple-μ exponent, with ΔBIC = +7.1 (pure parameter penalty) and RMS identical to McGaugh's baseline.

So the site has the identity on one page and the fit on another and **never multiplies them together**. The conclusion currently written is the weak one ("the added parameter bought no fit improvement"). The available conclusion is much stronger and is an *exact algebraic statement, not a fit quality*: released from its pinned value, the free parameter walks to the point where the compander **is** MOND's simple μ-function. That's not "no improvement," that's "the data selects the parent theory's functional form to within 2%." One sentence, no new computation, on `/galaxy-rotation` and `/mond-unification`.

**The concavity result is also stronger than stated.** `/coherence-function` gives dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x), "maximal at x = 0 and decreases monotonically." Both factors decrease, so C is **strictly concave on ρ > 0 with no inflection point at all**. The "S-curve" exists only against ln ρ — it is a property of the axis, not the system. Consequences:
- ρ_crit is not a critical density, not a midpoint, and not an inflection. The Coherence Explorer is honest about this (C = 0.5 lands at 2.07 ρ_crit at γ = 0.49) and says so out loud. Good.
- `/core-idea` says "critical exponents (β, ν) are undefined by construction." Correct. But `/coherence-function` says critical exponents came out **"2× off"** and attributes it to *"scope limitations, not function failure."* Those two statements cannot both be true — you cannot be 2× off from a quantity that is undefined by construction. Someone extracted a β and a ν from a function with no critical point, and neither page says how. That's a live contradiction between two adjacent theory pages, and the honest resolution favors `/core-idea`.
- The site is still wearing criticality vocabulary the math doesn't support: a tool literally named **Phase Boundary Visualizer**, "regimes," "transition sharpness." The compander framing has reached `/core-idea` but not the tool names.

**γ = 2/√N_corr: the diagnosis is right, the name is missing, and the repair is already dead.** `/gamma-parameter` correctly calls the CLT invocation self-contradictory and correctly identifies the sign problem. What it doesn't say is that this is the **Ginzburg criterion run backwards**: a larger correlated volume makes a system *more* mean-field-like and its transition *sharper*, so any sensible mapping has γ growing with N_corr, roughly √N_corr. Naming Ginzburg would make the diagnosis rigorous instead of hand-wavy, and it points straight at the obvious repair.

Then the repair should be checked, and it fails — using only numbers already on the site. The site's own anchor is γ(N_corr = 1) = 2. The site's only empirically fitted γ is 0.49, from SPARC. Under the current law that implies **N_corr = (2/0.49)² ≈ 17** for a galaxy — seventeen correlated degrees of freedom in 10¹⁰ stars. Under the sign-flipped law with the same anchor, γ < 2 is **impossible for any N_corr ≥ 1**, so the galaxy fit becomes unreachable. The sign flip is not free. `/gamma-parameter` gives the N = 1 and N = 10²⁴ endpoints and never mentions 0.49; `/coherence-explorer` *defaults* to 0.49 and never mentions N_corr. The two pages are one multiplication apart from a result that closes the ladder in either direction, and neither performs it.

**The a₀(z) row is live, and the data arrived in April.**

Three pages make three incompatible statements about the same quantity:

- `/key-claims`: a₀ "emerging from the coherence transition, **not as a fundamental constant**."
- `/mond-unification`: Synchronism treats a₀ as "an emergent scale" versus MOND's "fundamental constant" — presented as *the* distinguishing feature.

**The fork is not real.** Branch (B) — "a₀ happens to equal cH₀/2π today, no statement about H(z)" — is only available if a₀ is a fundamental constant that coincidentally matches a cosmological quantity. That is the MOND position, and it is precisely the position `/key-claims` and `/mond-unification` say the framework rejects. If a₀ *emerges* from a coherence transition tied to the expansion rate, then a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π is forced, not chosen. The framework's own ontology selects branch (A). Writing this as an open fork lets the framework keep an escape hatch its own text has already closed.

And branch (A) now has data. **Ciocan et al. 2026, "MUSE-DARK III: The evolution of the radial acceleration relation at intermediate redshifts" (A&A 709, L16; arXiv:2604.22613)** fit the RAR in 79 star-forming galaxies over 0.33 < z < 1.44 from MUSE HUDF, and find a₀(z ~ 1) = (2.38 ± 0.1) × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻², with a₀(z) = a₀(0) + a₁z, a₁ = (1.59 ± 0.1) × 10⁻¹⁰. The characteristic acceleration rises from ~1.99 × 10⁻¹⁰ in the lowest-z bin to 2.71 × 10⁻¹⁰ in the highest.

Against branch (A), with E(z) = H(z)/H₀ and flat ΛCDM Ω_m = 0.315 ⇒ E(1) = 1.79 (Ω_m = 0.30 gives 1.76; immaterial):

| Normalization at z = 0 | Predicted a₀(z=1) | Observed 2.38 ± 0.10 | Tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Framework's own cH₀/2π = 1.04 | 1.86 | — | **5.2σ low** |
| Observed local a₀ = 1.20 | 2.15 | — | **2.3σ low** |

This is the useful structural point and I could not find it anywhere on the site: **at z = 0 the H₀ route and the Λ route are numerically degenerate; by z ~ 1 they differ by a factor E(1) = 1.79, and there is now a measurement in that window.** A 43-year-old degeneracy just became decidable, and the site — whose flagship parameter *is* that coincidence — has neither branch written down as a test.

The direction matters and it is not the one I expected. I initially assumed a cosmologically-tracking a₀ would fail by predicting *too much* dark matter at high z. It fails the other way: the paper states outright that the measured a₀(z) rises **faster** than H(z), so branch (A) evolves too slowly. Every bin and every normalization gives the same sign, spanning roughly 2.3–5.9σ.

Two honest caveats I'd want stated alongside, because they cut against a premature kill. First, the tension is *normalization-sensitive by a factor of two* (2.3σ against the local a₀, 5.2σ against the framework's own cH₀/2π), and 79 galaxies at z ~ 1 carry inclination, M/L and beam-smearing systematics that a single linear fit won't absorb — this is "disfavored," not "refuted," and the framework never committed to the branch in writing, so it should not touch the refutation count. Second, an evolving a₀ is **also available to MOND**, so this discriminates against constant-a₀ MOND, not against MOND. It is not a Synchronism-vs-MOND discriminator, and I want to be explicit that I checked before writing that sentence rather than after.

That paper does the opposite — it **tests and disfavors** an evolving a₀ at high z. A referee who follows the citation finds a paper arguing against the thing it's cited to support. Corrections under-travel; refutations over-travel (an uncomputable Bullet Cluster conflict counted as a failure; a plotter dramatizing a γ the site abandoned). The net effect is that `/for-researchers` and `/mond-unification` — the two pages a physicist opens first — describe an older, cruder, and simultaneously more-and-less damning version of the framework than the site actually knows about.
