## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, beginner, galaxy [404], what-synchronism-is-not, tools, consciousness, coherence-explorer, gamma-calculator, honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The honesty hooks me and the marching-band analogy lands, but the moment I touch a tool or a definition I hit physics jargon that nobody walks me through.

### Journey
I came in from a Reddit link, no idea what "Synchronism" was. The landing page opens with *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — that's a great hook, feels like a Veritasium video title. I liked that it immediately admits some predictions fail; that made me trust it more than the usual "we solved physics" crank site. The equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` flashed by and I skipped right over it — `tanh` and `ln` mean nothing to me — but the "marching band vs. wandering crowd" picture for coherence actually stuck. That's the one thing I'll remember.

I clicked **Beginner Path** because it promised "no equations, 30 min." Step 1 of 7 was fine until it dropped **MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)** on me with words like "degrees of freedom" and "Markov." I have no idea what those mean and nothing explained them. It says "no prerequisites needed" but that's not true — felt more like an intro-college page than a beginner one.

Then I tried to click the **Galaxy Curve Plotter** that the landing page advertised. The obvious-looking link (`/galaxy`) gave me a **404 error page**. That's the kind of thing that makes a casual visitor close the tab. I eventually found the real tools through a **Tools** page, where there's a nice menu of six things to play with.

The **What Synchronism Is Not** and **Honest Assessment** pages were the most refreshing things on the whole site — a framework openly saying *"0 confirmed predictions"* and *"the honest map of what failed — and why — is the actual product"* is wild and kind of admirable. But as a casual reader it also left me with the obvious question: *then why am I here?* The answer ("wrong theories motivate right questions") is interesting but a bit abstract for someone who just wanted a cool physics idea.

The **consciousness** page is where my "is this legit?" alarm half went off — connecting a brain equation to a physics density formula is exactly the move that pop-science crackpots make. To its credit, the page itself says the equation "cannot yet compute C for any real system" and was "rejected at p < 0.0001." So it disarms its own red flag, but a skimming reader who doesn't reach that caveat might bounce thinking "woo."

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (home, glossary, learning-paths, first-encounter, honest-assessment, interactive-tools, galaxy-plotter, equation-walkthrough, why-synchronism, test-catalog)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the information architecture; I don't judge the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Structurally this is a well-built site — consistent nav, real breadcrumbs, an honest badge taxonomy — but the terminology layer leaks: badges are used before they're defined, the beginner sequence reports three different lengths, and a glossary that exists is never reached from the pages that need it.

### Journey
I audited the navigation spine first. The top nav menu is **identical and complete on every interior page** I checked (Start Here, Learning Paths, Why Synchronism?, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment). That's the hardest thing to keep consistent on a hand-built site and it holds. Breadcrumbs are present and sensibly categorized on every interior page — `Home / Getting Started / …`, `Home / Interactive Tools / …`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / …`. The home page has no breadcrumb, which is correct (it's the root). Good IA hygiene overall.

Where it gets ragged is **terminology and the relationship between pages**.

**The badge system is defined in one place and used everywhere else with no local key.** The home page stamps claims with `Reparametrization`, `Speculative`, `Untested`, `Failed` — but never defines them on the page; the canonical definitions live at `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`. A reader meets the badges on the landing page and has to navigate away to learn what they mean. By contrast, the **Test Roadmap puts a badge legend at the top of the page** — exactly the right pattern. So the site already knows how to do this; it just hasn't done it consistently. Worse, there are actually **two badge families** (the descriptive set, and the MRH-relationship set: `Active-MRH / Parallel-Paths / Sidelined / Superseded / Audited-Negative`) and which family a page uses is not signposted — the home page and Why-Synchronism show only descriptive badges, the Test Roadmap and Honest Assessment show both. A documentation reader has no way to know these are two intentional vocabularies rather than drift.

**The Tools page introduces a badge value that isn't in either family.** Most tools are tagged `Model Explainer` (Coherence Explorer, γ Calculator, Phase Boundary Visualizer, Equation Walkthrough). `Model Explainer` is not one of the defined validation badges — it's a sensible category ("this illustrates the model, it's not a test result") but it appears nowhere in the badge legend, so it reads as an undefined term in the same visual slot as the defined ones.

**The beginner sequence reports three different lengths and two different durations.** The home page calls it a "6-step beginner sequence." Learning Paths says tracks have "6–8 steps." First Encounter renders as "**Step 1 of 7**." On duration, First Encounter says "**10-minute intro**" (~90 seconds × 7), while Pass 1 reports the path was advertised as "no equations, 30 min" upstream. A reader can't tell whether 6/7/8 are the same sequence miscounted or three different things. Pick one canonical step count and one canonical time and propagate it.

**The glossary is excellent and almost never reached when you need it.** 70+ terms, alphabetized (Latin then Greek), and every term I probed was there: MRH, C(ρ), γ, ρ_crit, MOND, A2ACW, N_corr, Presence, Coherence. The definitions are clean and current — Coherence is "How collectively a group of elements behaves, from sparse/independent (0) to dense/collective (1)," which matches the home page and First Encounter exactly (a terminology consistency win; this looks like a deliberate prior fix). But the jargon-dense pages — Why Synchronism, Test Roadmap, Galaxy Plotter — fire off MOND, SPARC, DESI, RAR, fσ₈, Gaia DR3, ΔBIC, EFE, ΛCDM, YBCO, BCS, Abrikosov-Gor'kov **with no inline definition and no inline link to the glossary entry**. The glossary exists only in the top nav. Standard docs practice would link first use of each acronym to its glossary anchor, or expand it once parenthetically.

One small editorial artifact: the glossary γ entry carries a visible changelog note — "(This entry previously stated the regimes backwards… corrected 2026-06-12)." Admirable transparency, but a published glossary normally doesn't show its own correction scaffolding; that belongs in a changelog, not the definition body.

I tested two tools (different from Pass 1's Coherence Explorer / γ Calculator).

The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** is the most polished page on the site from a docs standpoint: clean galaxy selector with classifications and V_flat, a four-curve chart (observed / Newtonian / Synchronism / MOND) with labeled axes, and genuinely good honest framing ("all three models fit about equally well… what matters is whether any makes a different, testable prediction," plus the note that the related Tully-Fisher test "triggered as Failed"). The clarity gap is purely terminological: RAR, SPARC, C(ρ), and especially "**ΔBIC=+184**" and "**γ=2**" are dropped in with no on-page or on-hover gloss. ΔBIC=+184 is doing real argumentative work and a non-statistician can't decode it.

The **Equation Walkthrough** ("Equation Anatomy") is a 6-step term-by-term tour of `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit+1))`. It honestly leads with "tanh and γ=2/√N_corr are motivated choices, not derived results," and each symbol gets a panel. Two doc issues: (1) it's titled/marketed as a "walkthrough/derivation" elsewhere but explicitly does **not** derive anything — it explains — so the label oversells; and (2) the whole site leans on the word "**compander**" as the category for C(ρ) (it's in the glossary as "sigmoid/compander function"), yet this tool — the one place a reader comes to understand the equation — explains the log-compression concept *without ever using or defining the word "compander."* The site's own preferred framing doesn't appear where it would land best.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (home, equation-walkthrough, parameter-derivations, for-researchers, galaxy-rotation, tier-1-existing, hard-problem, interactive-tools, gamma-calculator, phase-boundary-visualizer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of the physics content and its honesty layer; I can read every equation here)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: This is the rare "unified framework" site that has already audited itself to the conclusion most skeptics would have to do the work to reach — 0 derived parameters, 0 discriminating tests, the central γ ansatz openly flagged as sign-inverted — so my job shifted from debunking to spot-checking whether the self-audit is *honest* and *arithmetically correct*. It mostly is, with two real cracks: a surviving φ⁻¹ numerology label on a refuted value, and a dim-4 LIV channel the site softens to "tension" when its own logic points to "refuted."

### Journey
I went straight past the onboarding to the math. The core object is `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` with `γ = 2/√N_corr`. My first reflex was to check whether ρ_crit is being smuggled in as a critical density. It isn't, and the site doesn't claim it is: `C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = tanh(2·ln2) = tanh(1.386) ≈ 0.882`, not 0.5, and the glossary/derivation pages correctly call ρ_crit a "saturation knee," not a critical point. That's a good sign — a crank site would have planted ρ_crit at the inflection and called tanh a phase transition. This one explicitly says it is **not** a Landau/mean-field order parameter and files C(ρ) under the "sigmoid/compander family (μ-law, Hill, logistic, Naka–Rushton)." That is the correct category. tanh, logistic, erf, and a Hill function are interchangeable saturating maps with no privileged dynamics; calling this a "phase transition" would be a category error, and the site has pre-emptively conceded that.

**The γ = 2/√N_corr ansatz is the load-bearing novelty, and it's inverted — which the site states outright.** The motivation offered is a CLT analogy (fluctuations ~ 1/√N). But CLT/Ginzburg logic runs the *other* way: more correlated degrees of freedom → narrower fluctuation window → **sharper** transition. Here, larger N_corr → smaller γ → **flatter** tanh. The γ Calculator makes this concrete and damning: ideal gas (N_corr=1) gets the *sharpest* curve at γ=2.000, while a BCS superconductor (N_corr=10⁷) gets γ=6.3×10⁻⁴, the *flattest* — assigning the limpest transition to the system with the sharpest real-world T_c. To its credit the tool prints exactly this: *"assigns the sharpest coherence transition to the least-correlated system (ideal gas) and the flattest to the most-correlated (BCS superconductor) — the opposite of real condensed-matter transitions."* A grad student cannot ask for more candor than a tool that refutes its own formula in a caption. But it does raise the obvious question the site doesn't answer: if the central parameter relation is sign-inverted relative to its own stated motivation, in what sense is it "motivated" at all rather than just *fit*?

**Coherence is inverted from the QM meaning, and the framework owns it.** C=0 is "sparse/independent," C=1 is "dense/collective." So a BCS condensate or a BEC — the most quantum-mechanically *coherent* objects we have — land at C≈0 here (small γ ⇒ flat tanh ⇒ C stays near 0 at accessible densities). Calling that quantity "coherence" is an actively misleading choice given the audience most likely to read these pages, but the Phase Boundary Visualizer and glossary both flag the collision. I'll register it as a naming hazard, not a physics error.

**The parameter audit is the most impressive page on the site.** `/parameter-derivations` states flatly: *"Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations."* Every entry is correctly triaged — a₀ = cH₀/(2π) tagged "known since Milgrom 1983, not Synchronism-specific" (and it checks: cH₀/2π ≈ 1.05×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ≈ a₀); Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) tagged "Freeman's Law re-expressed"; R₀ = V²/(3a₀) tagged "dimensional bookkeeping, 3% agreement." The one candidate for a real derivation, A≈0.029 from a Jeans criterion, is marked **Audited-Negative** with the actual failure named: the 5% number used ρ_crit ∝ V^0.5, contradicting the framework's stated ρ_crit ∝ V², and the stated formula instead yields A≈4.6×10⁻⁵, ~600× off. This is exactly the chain-of-custody failure I would have gone hunting for, and they've already found it and published it.

**The galaxy test is honestly reported as a refutation, not a fit.** `/galaxy-rotation`: pinned γ=2 compander gives ΔBIC=+184 against MOND (decisive, ≫10) — refuted. Free γ converges to 0.49, at which point the RMS matches McGaugh's MOND to four decimals and the fit improvement over MOND is *zero*. So the only way to make C(ρ) fit galaxies is to let it become MOND. That is the textbook signature of a reparametrization, and the badge says "Reparametrization." Correct call. Same story on Tier-1: 11 tests, the discriminating ones either Failed (R²=0.14, TEST-03/05), Kill-Criterion-Triggered (TEST-02 self-eliminates to a Newtonian null 80× below Gaia DR3 reach; TEST-04a DESI fσ₈ shows enhancement where the framework needs suppression), or MOND-shared ("a positive result confirms Synchronism AND MOND equally"). Verdict on the page: 0 confirmed, 0 discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM. I went in expecting to have to *derive* that verdict; instead I was checking their arithmetic, and it holds.

### Where I'd push back (the two cracks)

**1. φ⁻¹ numerology survives on the consciousness page — and the arithmetic is wrong.** `/hard-problem` reports the original C≈0.50 threshold as refuted at p<0.0001 (good — and the page no longer claims C is computable, since D and S have no operational definitions). But it still labels the replacement value as *"C ≈ 0.64 ≈ φ⁻¹."* Two problems: (a) φ⁻¹ = 0.61803…, which is **not** 0.64 — that's a 3.6% mismatch dressed up as an identity; and (b) attaching a golden-ratio mystique to a number that only exists because the *previous* threshold was experimentally rejected is precisely the numerology move this site is otherwise so good at refusing. Either 0.64 is φ⁻¹ (it isn't) or the ≈φ⁻¹ tag is decoration on a refuted-then-replaced value. Strip the φ⁻¹ label; it's the one place the site indulges the pattern it elsewhere polices.

**2. The dim-4 LIV channel is softened to "tension" when the framework's own structure points to "refuted."** `/for-researchers` now correctly separates the two LIV faces: time-of-flight is "symmetry-protected and unreachable" (reflection symmetry kills the linear term; the quadratic sits at Planck scale, 10⁷× below bounds; and detection wouldn't distinguish it from generic QG anyway — all fair). But for the dim-4 preferred-frame face it quotes the real bounds (|c_LIV| ≲ 10⁻¹⁸ cavity MM, 10⁻²²–10⁻²⁹ Hughes–Drever) and a "named tension: absolute time ⊥ sub-10⁻²² Lorentz invariance," labeling it the framework's "sharpest refutation exposure." That's the right channel to point at — but a framework with a genuinely *absolute* time has no boost custodian, so a dim-4 c_μν at observable magnitude isn't a "tension," it's the expected outcome, i.e. a refutation unless a specific protection mechanism is exhibited. The page names the exposure but stops short of the verdict its own premise implies. "Sharpest refutation exposure" should probably read "refuted at dim-4 pending a stated protection mechanism." I can't see the back-end research from these pages, but on the page logic alone the hedge is too soft.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — physics correctly (and self-critically) represented — Presets compute γ=2/√N_corr faithfully (ideal gas 2.000, water 1.000, ferromagnet 0.200, BCS 6.3×10⁻⁴, BEC 2.0×10⁻³). The standout is that the tool *prints its own refutation*: it states the formula assigns the sharpest transition to the least-correlated system, "the opposite of real condensed-matter transitions." That is honest physics communication; the only gap is it never says the obvious next sentence — that this makes γ a fit, not a motivated quantity.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — partially representative — Correctly lands BCS and BEC at C≈0 (consistent with small-γ flat tanh) and ideal gas at γ=2, galaxies at the SPARC γ≈0.49. But with no ρ-axis it's a 1-D placement, not a phase diagram, and the page admits the full γ–ρ diagram isn't built — so the most physically interesting claim (where the *boundary* actually sits) is the one not yet visualized.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If γ = 2/√N_corr is sign-inverted relative to its own CLT motivation, is there *any* sense in which the √N scaling is independently checkable, or is the whole relation absorbed into the galaxy fit (which then collapses to MOND anyway)?
2. With absolute time as a stated ingredient, what is the proposed mechanism that would protect the dim-4 c_μν sector down to 10⁻²²? Without one, "tension" reads as a refutation the page won't name.
3. The replacement consciousness threshold 0.64 — is that a fitted value with a confidence interval, or another point estimate waiting to be refuted? The page doesn't give its uncertainty, only the ≈φ⁻¹ decoration.
4. ρ_crit is correctly a saturation knee, not a critical density — so what *sets* its value across domains (galaxies vs chemistry vs consciousness)? If it's calibrated per-domain, the "one equation from quantum to cosmic" framing is doing less work than the landing page implies.

### Verdict
As a grad student sent here to find the overclaim, I mostly found a site that had already found it for me: zero derived parameters (stated), the galaxy test collapsing to MOND (ΔBIC=+184, then γ→0.49), and the central γ ansatz carrying its own sign-inversion warning. The honesty is real and, unusually, arithmetically checkable — I verified ρ_crit isn't the C=0.5 point, that a₀≈cH₀/2π, and that the compander categorization is the correct one. The two places it still flinches are telling: a φ⁻¹ label glued to a refuted-then-replaced 0.64 (and 0.64≠0.618), and a dim-4 LIV channel called a "tension" when its own absolute-time premise reads as a refutation. Fix those two and the site would be doing something genuinely rare — a unified-framework page whose physics claims are *exactly* as strong as its own audit, no stronger.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, for-researchers, research-philosophy/A2ACW, test-catalog, honest-assessment, galaxy-plotter, parameter-derivations, hard-problem, coherence-explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I can read every claim against the current literature; the site does most of my refereeing for me)
- **Would return?**: yes — but as a referee, not a user; there is no live prediction here for me to test against my own data
- **Overall impression**: The honest verdict is *correct* and unusually well-audited — 0 novel surviving predictions, the one non-MOND channel (dim-4 LIV) self-refuted — but the site now has the opposite failure mode from a crank site: it **under-claims its refutations and over-claims its transferability**, and its single citable structural result is an un-attributed restatement of Milgrom.

### Journey
I skipped the onboarding entirely and went straight for the thing a referee cares about: **is there one prediction that differs numerically from MOND and ΛCDM and is within reach of data I trust?** The answer the site gives — repeatedly, on `/for-researchers`, `/test-catalog`, and `/honest-assessment` — is *no*: "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, 0 tests currently discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." I spent the session checking whether that verdict is *too harsh* (is there a survivor they've buried?) or *too kind* (are they calling refutations "tensions"?). It is mostly neither, but it errs in both directions in specific, fixable places.

**The one genuinely novel channel is the dim-4 preferred-frame LIV — and it is already excluded.** This is the most interesting physics on the site and it's buried under hedged labels. The logic is clean: an *absolute* time slicing (the framework's stated ingredient) breaks boost invariance, so there is no symmetry custodian for a dimension-4 SME coefficient c_μν. The framework's own anisotropy estimate is ~10⁻³ (dipole) / 10⁻⁶ (quadrupole). Cavity Michelson–Morley bounds |c| ≲ 10⁻¹⁸ and clock-comparison (Hughes–Drever-class) bounds reach 10⁻²²–10⁻²⁹. That's the page's own "11–19 orders of magnitude above existing limits." **That is not a "tension." That is a refutation of the only prediction on the site that isn't shared with Milgrom.** To the site's credit, the `/for-researchers` page I read *does* now carry the phrase "refutation-exposed" alongside the older "named tension: absolute time ⊥ sub-10⁻²² Lorentz invariance" — so this is sharper than a hedge, but it's double-labeled. A referee reads "refutation-exposed … named tension" and can't tell if the authors have committed. They should: with absolute time and no exhibited protection mechanism, dim-4 is **refuted**, full stop, and that refutation is the most scientifically valuable sentence on the entire site. Lead with it. (The time-of-flight dim≥5 channel genuinely *is* protected — reflection symmetry kills n=1, the n=2 term sits ~10⁷ below LHAASO's E_QG,2 > 6×10⁻⁸ E_Pl floor, and even a detection wouldn't distinguish it from generic QG. That hedge is correct.)

**A2ACW, evaluated as a method I might referee:** the page is admirably honest — 3,308 sessions, 1.4% internal-consistency survival, **0% novel-surviving yield after expert audit**, and an explicit disclaimer that it "cannot generate out-of-distribution novelty or detect systematic errors shared by the entire training corpus," with every component correctly attributed to prior art (Irving–Christiano–Amodei 2018 debate, CAMEL/MetaGPT roles, the MAST failure-mode taxonomy). As a referee I accept the framing ("a falsifiability filter, not a discovery method"). **But one claim is not supported: "the contribution is the controlled null result with measured sensitivity *and specificity*."** You cannot measure specificity without a labeled set of *genuine* novel discoveries that the filter is run against and correctly passes. An AI adversarial pair, by the page's own admission, cannot recognize OOD novelty — so the specificity denominator is empty. What they have is a sensitivity-on-positives number and a null yield; "measured specificity" overstates it. This matters because the 0% novel yield is then ambiguous in a way the page doesn't flag: it could be a true property of the *theory* (nothing novel to find) **or** a blind spot of the *method* (it would miss novelty if present). The site can't distinguish these, and shouldn't imply it can.

**The galaxy refutation is real but the ΔBIC=+184 drama is slightly inflated.** `/galaxy-plotter` and `/galaxy-rotation` pin γ=2 and report ΔBIC=+184 vs MOND as a decisive refutation, then note free-γ→0.49 recovers MOND to four decimals. As a modified-gravity person I'd push on the setup: **γ=2 was never anchored for galaxies in the first place.** γ=2/√N_corr with N_corr=1 is the *single-particle* limit; a galaxy of ~10¹¹ stars, taken at face value, would have enormous N_corr → γ→0 → a nearly flat C → essentially *no* boost, which fails even harder. So γ=2 is the *most charitable* pin, not a prediction that was made and broken. The honest one-liner is the one the parameter page already implies but the plotter doesn't say: "γ is an unanchored fit parameter; the data drive it to the MOND value; there is no independent prior selecting γ=2 (or any value) for a galaxy." Reporting ΔBIC=+184 as a "refutation of Synchronism" gives the impression a genuine prediction was tested, when what was tested was an admittedly arbitrary pin.

**Two transferability over-claims — the inverse of the LIV under-claim.** On `/honest-assessment`:

1. **The DESI "mechanism-class" box is drawn too wide.** The page says TEST-04a "constrains the entire coherence-damped structure-growth mechanism class: any framework that predicts growth suppression from density-coherence coupling sits in the same disfavored box," and explicitly lists "massive-ν, warm-DM, and certain f(R)." This is an overreach. The signal is a ~2σ, *post-hoc*, single-bin (LRG1, z=0.51) fσ₈ enhancement. A 2σ post-hoc preference does not "constrain" massive neutrinos or warm dark matter — those are alive and well in the DESI+CMB joint analyses, and their growth suppression is k-dependent and scale-localized in a way a uniform coherence damping is not. The transferable claim should be scoped to "uniform, scale-independent late-time growth suppression of the *Synchronism* form," not a roster of standard-model-adjacent mechanisms that the data do not in fact exclude. Elsewhere the site is scrupulous about not overclaiming; this is the one place it borrows DESI's authority to rule out things DESI doesn't.

2. **The density-locality no-go is presented as a Synchronism discovery; it's Milgrom's.** The "Wrong Variable" result — that any ansatz keyed on *local volumetric density ρ(r)* can't bridge galaxies and clusters without per-system recalibration of ρ_crit (10⁴–10⁶×) — is the single most citable structural statement on the site, and I'd happily cite it. But as written it has no attribution, and the core obstruction is **Milgrom's modified-inertia non-locality argument** (astro-ph/0510117): a local state variable can't reproduce the acceleration-keyed RAR (Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2017) that *non-local* enclosed-mass theories (Verlinde, MOG) satisfy. The novelty Synchronism actually adds is the *quantified* local-ρ instance and the cluster ρ_crit gap — that's a real, modest contribution. But a no-go presented without the Milgrom citation reads, to anyone in this subfield, as claiming a known theorem. Cite Milgrom at the top of that box and the result becomes *more* credible, not less.

**Parameter provenance: clean and correct.** `/parameter-derivations` states "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations" and correctly tags a₀=cH₀/(2π) as "noted since Milgrom (1983), not Synchronism-specific," Σ₀ as Freeman's-Law-re-expressed, R₀ as dimensional bookkeeping, A≈0.029 as Audited-Negative. I checked a₀: cH₀/(2π) with H₀≈70 gives ≈1.1×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², which is a₀. No complaint — this page is the model the rest of the site should imitate.

**The φ⁻¹ tag is *still* live on `/hard-problem` and it's worse than a grad student would catch.** The page reports C≈0.50 as refuted (p<0.0001), states plainly that "C cannot be computed for any real system" because D (dimensional embedding) and S (self-modeling depth) "lack operational definitions" — and then labels the replacement value "C ≈ 0.64 ≈ φ⁻¹." Three compounding problems: (a) φ⁻¹=0.61803, not 0.64 — a 3.6% miss sold as an identity; (b) it's a golden-ratio mystique pinned to a value whose *only* provenance is that the previous value was experimentally rejected; and (c) — the part only a methods-skeptic flags — **0.64 has no operational provenance at all**, because the page itself says C is uncomputable here. So φ⁻¹ is being glued not to a measured number but to an *admittedly unmeasurable* one. That's numerology on a placeholder. This is the single sharpest credibility leak on the site precisely because it sits on the page most likely to trip a crackpot-detector, and it contradicts the site's own stated standard one paragraph up.

### Where I landed on "what's actually new"
Nothing testable. The honest scorecard is right. The *intellectual* contributions that survive are all **negative/structural**, and they're worth a short methods note, not a physics paper: (i) a quantified demonstration that a local-density compander can't be a universal modified-gravity law (Milgrom instance — cite him); (ii) a clean mechanism-class refutation of *Synchronism-form* uniform growth suppression by DESI (scope it down); (iii) the dim-4 LIV self-refutation, which is the most honest "our novel channel is dead" statement I've seen on a unification site (commit to it). The genuine meta-result is the one the explorer loop seems to have already reached: **a unification ansatz built from a single local state variable, with parameters calibrated rather than derived, will reliably reduce to the best existing phenomenology (MOND) on the only scale where it fits, and be refuted everywhere it's distinct.** That's a real, publishable cautionary result about AI-assisted theory-building. It is not physics; it's epistemics — and the site is, to its considerable credit, already most of the way to saying exactly that.

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — physically careful — Carries the correct, prominent warning that its "coherence" is classical collective ordering, *anti-correlated* with QM phase coherence (BCS/BEC sit at low C despite max quantum coherence — the right caveat for my audience). Verified the math it exposes: at ρ=ρ_crit, C≈0.88 (=tanh(2 ln2), confirming ρ_crit is a saturation knee, not the C=0.5 inflection), and the 50% point at ρ≈0.32 ρ_crit. The honest gap, unchanged from what a grad student sees: log₁₀(ρ) axis with **no units and no material anchors**, so the curve is a shape, not a calibrated prediction — which is, in fairness, the deepest true statement about the whole framework (C is doubly unanchored: no protocol maps any measurable to C in any domain).

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is the dim-4 c_μν refuted or not? The site needs one verdict, not "refutation-exposed / named tension." If a protection mechanism for absolute-time→Lorentz-invariance exists, state it; if not, it's refuted — and that's the headline.
2. What is the specificity denominator for A2ACW? Without a labeled corpus of genuine novel discoveries run through the filter, "measured specificity" is undefined — so does the 0% novel yield diagnose the theory or the method?
3. Does the density-locality no-go reduce *exactly* to Milgrom's non-locality argument, or is there a residual obstruction unique to the compander form? If the latter, that residual is the actual contribution and should be isolated; if not, it's a citation, not a discovery.
4. The DESI mechanism-class claim — has it actually been checked that massive-ν / warm-DM growth histories fall in the same "disfavored box," or is that asserted by analogy? Those are k-dependent suppressions; a uniform coherence damping is not obviously in the same class.

### Verdict
I came to find the one new prediction and confirmed there isn't one — and, more usefully, confirmed that the site's own "0 discriminating" verdict is *correct*, which is rarer than it sounds for a unification framework. The remaining problems are no longer overclaims of success; they're an asymmetry of nerve: the site **under-states its one genuine refutation** (dim-4 LIV, which it should headline) while **over-stating two transferability results** (the DESI mechanism class, which doesn't reach massive-ν/warm-DM, and the density-locality no-go, which is Milgrom's and needs his name on it), and it still tolerates one indefensible numerology tag (φ⁻¹≈0.64 on an uncomputable value). Fix those four and the site becomes what it's clearly trying to be: an honest, citable null result about AI-assisted theory-building, with every claim exactly as strong as its own audit — no stronger, and, just as importantly, no weaker.
