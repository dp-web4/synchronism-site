## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Honest and oddly refreshing. It's a theory that tells you in the first paragraph that it failed. But once past the first two pages I was wading through acronyms, and I still can't tell you in one sentence what I'm meant to take away.

### Journey
I came from Reddit. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", is exactly the kind of hook that gets me to click. The next line stopped me: "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated. What follows is what we learned." I've never seen a site do that. It made me trust it more, and it also made me wonder why I should keep reading. My working answer was "to watch how an idea gets killed," and that is interesting to me, though the landing page doesn't quite say it. It does say "the honest map of where it fails is the product," which is close. I followed the Beginner Path (6 steps, "around 40 minutes").

**Why Synchronism** opens well: "Physics has a fragmentation problem." The "crowding makes the parts of a system act as one" line is the first time I understood the idea. The "(1)/(2) what changes if this works" part was clear. Then the page mentions Bose–Einstein condensates, superconductors, Tully–Fisher, SPARC/ALFALFA–SDSS and Hamiltonian, and I skimmed.

**First Encounter** is the best page on the site for me. It shows one part at a time with a Next button, which felt approachable. The electron-in-a-vacuum vs neutron-star-core contrast worked. The MRH explanation was useful ("'Markov' is the mathematician's word for 'only the current neighbors matter, not the history'"). But I hit the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` and nothing told me what tanh *does*. The page says it outputs "a number between 0 and 1" and later that "tanh is smooth, so nothing snaps here." I only found out it's an S-shaped dimmer when I opened the Coherence Explorer and on /core-idea ("flat, then steep, then flat again"). That sentence belongs on First Encounter, next to the equation. Part C lost me: "+0.55, measured −0.55" meant nothing to me beyond "they got the sign backwards." Part G ("Melting points: 53% error. Critical exponents: 2× off. Superconductor Tc: 6.5× wrong") is blunt, and I liked it, even though I don't know what a critical exponent is.

**Core Idea** has the dimmer-switch analogy, which helped. It also says the best-fit γ "runs down to 0.046" and "the best fit says the parts don't switch to acting together, the opposite of the idea." I read that twice. If I understood it, the data says the central idea is backwards? That seems like the headline, and it's buried in the middle of step 3. Then there's a jargon wall: Ising model, Landau theory, Naka–Rushton curves, Hill functions.

**What Synchronism Is Not** has a good plain-words box at the top ("Not proven. No prediction has been confirmed..."). Further down is the sentence that actually scared me: "a gap of 16 to 28 orders of magnitude, and it is excluded at face value by experiments that already exist" (about Lorentz invariance). I don't know what Lorentz invariance is. I do know that "28 orders of magnitude" and "excluded by existing experiments" sound like "this is dead." On step 4 of a beginner path, with no plain-words gloss, I didn't know how alarmed to be. The same page says "54 of 92 listed predictions (59%) were untested" in "the February 2026 tally." That's seven months old, and I wondered whether it's still true. It also mentions "3,308 AI-to-AI sessions (A2ACW protocol)" as the review process. As a Reddit visitor, "reviewed by AI talking to AI" made me more skeptical, not less. The page is upfront that nothing went to a journal, which I respected.

**Honest Assessment** (step 5) starts with a verdict I could follow: "0 confirmed predictions, 0 prospective predictions tested, 6 refutations executed." The plain bottom line ("As a theory of galaxies: fatal, by its own scoreboard") is clear. After that, the page is huge: the fetch estimated around 18,000 words and 11 sections, with RAR, BTFR, fσ₈, CHSH, ΔBIC and Ωm. As step 5 of a "40-minute" beginner path, that's a cliff. I read the top and the badge definitions (which are explained, good) and left.

Two small counting puzzles: the landing page says "We tested it", but the Honest Assessment says "0 prospective predictions tested", and What-It-Is-Not says "most predictions have never been tested." I think the difference is "tested after predicting" vs "checked against old data," but a casual reader will see a contradiction.

**Glossary**: 93 terms and no search box that I could find, so I scrolled. The dark matter and coherence entries are nice. The tanh entry ("smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1") is accurate but gives me −1 to +1 when every other page says 0 to 1. Nobody says the negative half isn't used here. The γ entry uses "ansatz" (what?) but at least flags that the formula is audited-negative. The **N_corr** entry, though, says flatly "The fundamental input to γ = 2/√N_corr... A crystal lattice might have N_corr = 10²⁴ (γ ≈ 10⁻¹², classical)" with no warning. If I landed there straight from a link, I'd think the formula is current.

I'd come back if there were a two-minute "what did we learn?" summary in plain words. Right now I'd have to piece together the takeaway myself.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is TEST-09 currently counted as a refutation or not? The detail card and the headline disagree, and "pending review" is the only word on only one page.
2. Which page is canonical for a test's status: Tier 1, Honest Assessment, or For Researchers? Right now each carries its own copy, and they have drifted (TEST-09, TEST-04a).

### Verdict
The skeleton is good: breadcrumbs work everywhere, learning paths are internally consistent, the glossary covers nearly every acronym I met, and Equation Anatomy is an exemplary explanatory page. The problem is consistency of state. The site revises itself faster than it propagates the revisions, so a detail card, a summary box, the canonical legend and the glossary can say four different things about one test. The fix is single-sourcing, not more notes. Every correction written inline in prose makes the next reader's job harder, even when the correction itself is honest.

### Method note
Following Pass 2, I pulled the raw page text and grepped every quoted item, then re-computed the numbers myself (Python, not the fetch summary). One fetch artifact: the /parameter-derivations summary attached "Fisher correlation +1.000000 with γ" to the B_max row. In the raw text the +1.000000 is the (ln γ, ln A) correlation. The floor is the parameter the galaxy data *is* sensitive to (χ²/N moves by up to 6.35×10⁴).

### Journey
A colleague sent the link. I skipped the beginner path and went straight to **/coherence-function**. I expected to find the usual "tanh because Ising mean-field" hand-wave. Instead the page says it itself: "motivated by the Ising analogy, not derived from it … Synchronism's C(ρ) is not the Ising equation" (there is no self-consistency loop). That's the right objection, and they raise it before I can. I checked the algebra:
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]: **correct**. At γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2): **correct** (exact, not asymptotic), so a₀ = 2a₀′: **correct**.

It cites Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024) at 8.7σ marginalized. That is the right literature. Then comes the box I disagree with. It says the power-law direct tail (1−C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ}, "fractional anomaly ≈ 5×10⁻⁶ at Saturn") is "a separate, smaller effect … it does not decide the Cassini verdict." I reproduce the 5×10⁻⁶ (x = 5.4×10⁵, γ = 0.489 → 4.97×10⁻⁶). In absolute terms that's δg ≈ 3×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² at Saturn, basically an a₀-sized extra pull. It also can't be absorbed into GM☉, because δg/g ∝ r^≈2 differs planet to planet (≈6×10⁻⁸ at Earth, 5×10⁻⁶ at Saturn). From memory of the literature (Sereno & Jetzer 2006; Blanchet & Novak 2011), planetary ephemerides are exactly what exclude slow-return μ functions like μ_simple, independently of the EFE. I haven't run this, so treat it as a grad student's estimate. But if it's right, this is the *one* place the γ ≈ ½ compander and McGaugh's exponential RAR function actually **differ**, and the Solar System picks McGaugh. That's a discriminating failure the site is currently filing as a footnote.

The same section says "the fit pins q ≈ 0.98, cross-validated by an independent free-Hill fit (n = 0.975)". I don't think SPARC can pin an *asymptotic* return exponent. In a one-parameter family, γ has to encode the transition *shape* over the probed range, and q is just what that shape is called asymptotically. The free-Hill fit uses the same data and the same regime, so it isn't independent. "SPARC selects the μ_simple *transition shape*" is supported; "SPARC pins the return exponent" is not.
