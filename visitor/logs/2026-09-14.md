## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Interactive Tools, Coherence Explorer, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Learning Paths, Honest Assessment, The Core Idea, Glossary, Equation Anatomy; I opened a few of them twice to reread)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science website I've ever clicked from Reddit, but I left not knowing whether the equation fits galaxies or doesn't, because two pages told me opposite things.

### Journey
The landing page hooked me right away. The headline asks "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" and then says straight out: "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." I have never seen a site do that. The scoreboard (0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations) made me trust it more, not less. The equation looked scary, but the line "a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are" told me I didn't have to understand it. Three doors ("No physics background?" / "Physicist?" / "Researcher?") made the next click easy.

**Why Synchronism? ("Start Here").** The first sentence is great. By paragraph 2 I was lost in a long bracket: "(curve-equivalence at fitted γ only — the framework has no dynamics of its own to inherit MOND's other predictions)". That is the second paragraph of the page for beginners. I skimmed past it. The galaxy numbers (14,610 = 175 + 14,435) add up.

**First Encounter.** This was the best page for me. A marching band is high coherence and a crowd wandering a plaza is low. An electron alone in space versus the inside of a neutron star. An atom doesn't "feel" the Andromeda galaxy. I got it. The page says "Section 1 of 7", but I could only see section 1. It's probably a click-through I couldn't operate from where I was, so I'm not counting it against the site. The "formal version, if you want it" of the MRH is clearly optional, and I liked being given the choice.

**Interactive Tools.** The cards have Beginner / Advanced / Speculative labels, so I knew where to click. I picked the two Beginner tools with pictures.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter.** This was the clearest single idea on the site: "The gray line sagging below the dots is the dark matter puzzle." Then it says the violet line, "the framework's real equation", "fails to fix it", and the disk "never gets dense enough for the coherence boost to turn on". The amber line fits only "because it was drawn to fit". I respected that. Further down it talks about a "quadrature branch" and a "division branch": in one, C has to get close to 1 to help; in the other, a *small* C gives a 1000× boost. I couldn't tell which way coherence pushes gravity. SPARC and kpc aren't explained on this page (the Glossary has both).

**Coherence Explorer.** The plaza/marching-band picture comes back, which helped. It has γ and ρ_crit sliders and a log/linear switch. Readouts at the default: C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785. It also tells me what to try ("Move γ from 2.0 downward and watch the curve flatten"). One thing tripped me. The text says "A density 1,000× bigger becomes only ~7 units bigger", but the axis is labelled log₁₀(ρ). From the Richter scale I know 1,000× is 3 steps in base 10, not 7. The Glossary later told me the equation uses a base-e log, so both are "right", but the tool shows one log and describes another.

**Learning Paths.** The Beginner Path is 6 steps, "~35 min on the short route", and the step times add up to exactly that. It warns me up front that step 5 (Honest Assessment) is long and to read only its summary box. That was thoughtful.

**Honest Assessment.** The page says it is ~18,000 words, and the note that the summary box was "added at a casual reader's request" made me feel heard. Here is the problem. The first box says: "Where it fits galaxies, it is MOND in different notation … a switch keyed on local density instead of acceleration (the data reject that at every knee tried)." Then the "What a non-physicist should take away" box opens with: **"The one equation fits galaxy rotation — but only as well as MOND … and never better."** Ten minutes earlier the Galaxy Plotter told me "the framework's real equation — fails to fix it." So does the one equation fit galaxies or not? I think the answer is "a version with a fitted dial that isn't really about density fits; the density version doesn't". But the sentence written for me says the opposite of what the tool showed me. The box for non-physicists also uses "TEST-09", "kill criterion", "3.3σ", "Milgrom's interpolating function" and "knee". The Glossary covers σ and kill criterion. It doesn't cover "interpolating function" or Milgrom.

**The Core Idea.** "Dimmer switch from sparse/independent to dense/collective" works. Then it quickly gets into the "Ising model", "real-analytic everywhere … no free-energy functional" and a "Hill index". This is step 3 of the *beginner* path. The headings "γ ≪ 1: Collective Regime" and "γ ≫ 1: Independent Regime" confused me. I had just learned that *C* near 1 means collective, and now a small γ means collective too. So there are two dials that both go up to "collective", in opposite directions? I also finished the page without learning the one thing I came for: **if coherence is high (or low), does gravity get stronger?** The Core Idea and Equation Anatomy both skip it.

**Glossary.** 77 terms, alphabetical, no search box that I could see. It had nearly everything I looked up (SPARC, kpc, MOND, σ, kill criterion, tanh, ln, ρ_crit, γ, MRH, coherence). Missing: "interpolating function" and "Milgrom", both of which are in the non-physicist summary box.

**Equation Anatomy.** It is a 6-step walkthrough; I could read step 1. It has nice worked numbers ("One particle: γ=2 (sharp). A million: γ=2×10⁻³ (flat)"). It says "ρcrit is near saturation, not the half-way point", with "At γ=2, C(ρcrit) ≈ 0.88". But the Coherence Explorer I had just used starts at γ = 0.49 and shows C(ρcrit) = 0.33, which is *below* half-way. The Equation Anatomy page never mentions 0.49. The two beginner tools left me with opposite pictures of where ρ_crit sits.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked (I read the rendered page; the dropdown needs JavaScript, so I couldn't switch galaxies) — intuitive — The best "aha" on the site: the gray line sagging below the dots *is* the dark matter problem. Showing that the real equation fails, and that the good-looking amber curve was hand-drawn, is unusually honest. The two-branch discussion lost me.
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked (rendered state only; I couldn't drag sliders) — mostly intuitive — The plaza/marching-band framing and the "try this" hints are good. I checked the three default readouts and they are consistent with each other. The ln vs log₁₀ mismatch confused me.
- **Equation Anatomy** — worked (step 1 of 6 visible) — intuitive — Good worked numbers. Its "ρcrit is near saturation" conflicts with the Explorer's default picture.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Does the one equation fit galaxy rotation or not? The Honest Assessment takeaway and the Galaxy Plotter seem to say opposite things.
2. If coherence is high, is gravity stronger or weaker? And why are there two "branches" that go opposite ways?
3. If every decisive test failed, what is the site still for? "The honest map of where it fails is the product" is a nice line, but what would a new test even look like now?
4. Why is γ ≈ 1 "where chemistry and biology happen"? That's stated in the Explorer with no explanation.
5. What do sections 2–7 of First Encounter cover? The page is my favourite and I could only see the first part.

### Verdict
As a Reddit clicker I stayed about 40 minutes, which almost never happens. The honesty (a scoreboard of zero confirmed predictions, a hand-drawn curve admitted to be hand-drawn) is the reason. First Encounter and the Galaxy Plotter are genuinely good science communication. But the one sentence written specifically for people like me ("The one equation fits galaxy rotation") contradicts what the tool I had just used showed me, and nothing on the beginner path says which direction coherence pushes gravity. So I understand the *mood* of the project better than its *idea*.

### Summary

The remaining problem is that at least three different "C" functions share one symbol. The equation on the landing page is not the one behind any galaxy fit statistic the site quotes.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "an interesting AI project". I expected a theory of everything with a tanh in it. What I found is closer to a public post-mortem, and I mean that as a compliment. I skipped the onboarding and went straight to derivations.

**Parameter Derivations.** The page opens by conceding that γ = 2/√N_corr is a "motivated ansatz", invoking the CLT "for correlated DOF where CLT doesn't apply". It says ρ_crit = A·V_flat² is "calibrated" with V_flat as the input, and ends with: "Zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." I checked what I could. A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²) has the right units (kg s² m⁻⁵ for a density per velocity²). The quoted 4.6×10⁻⁵ against the used 0.029 is a factor of ~630, so "600× off" is fair. a₀ = cH₀/2π gives ≈1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² at H₀ = 70, which is Milgrom's 1983 coincidence, and the page calls it a reparametrization. B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17 is labelled "asserted". Nothing here overclaims.

**The Core Idea.** The identity tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1] is correct: write tanh u = (e^{2u} − 1)/(e^{2u} + 1). The page correctly refuses the Ising/mean-field reading ("C(ρ) has no feedback loop", "no critical point and therefore no critical exponents"). That is right: there's no self-consistency equation, so this is a saturating map, not a Landau theory. My only quibble is "Hill-type … Hill index". C = 2H − 1 where H is a Hill function of (1+x), not of x. Near x → 0, C ≈ γx, which is linear with no sigmoid in x. It is an exact Hill function of x only at γ = ½, where C = x/(x+2), and "Hill index 2γ" holds only asymptotically.

For a framework whose quantum claim is "measurement = MRH boundary crossing", that's the obvious comparison class. I didn't open /measurement-without-observers, so this is untested, not refuted.
