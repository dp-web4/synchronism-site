### The retraction — the most important thing in this log

I drafted, and had the maintainer actions written for, a finding titled *"branch (A) closed by
high-z rotation curves."* It tested branch (A) against the six Genzel et al. 2017 discs via
Milgrom's Table I and found it refuted 5–6 of 6 on two arms, robust across four convention
combinations. The arithmetic was correct. **The result was false**, because the sample is
superseded: the same group's RC100 (N=100, arXiv:2209.12199, 2023) gives median f_DM(R_e) =
0.38±0.23 at z~1 and 0.27±0.18 at z~2, against the 0.00–0.21 that Milgrom worked from. Recomputed
against RC100, branch (A) is consistent within 1σ everywhere. Both arms collapse.

What caught it: the project memory entry for this exact row carries the line *"Genzel 2017's f_DM
was revised upward by its own group… don't cite the 2017 values as current,"* written four days
ago by a session that had already been here. I read memory before committing, not before drafting.

This project's ledger already contains two fabricated refutations (TEST-03's manufactured kill,
session 63's fabricated p < 0.0001). This would have been a third, and it would have been mine, and
it would have been *well-executed* — script, four robustness conventions, primary source read in
full, a stated caveat about NFW model dependence. **Rigour on the computation is not a defence
against staleness in the input.** That is the transferable lesson and it is cheap to act on: check
the resample before quoting any high-z number.

### What actually survives

**The site's a₀ closure is wrong in three specific ways, none of which is the one I went looking for.**

1. **Citation role inverted.** The site cites arXiv:1703.06110 as evidence Milgrom "proposes
   a₀ ~ cH/2π himself." That paper tests and disfavours the idea; the proposals are arXiv:0801.3133
   and ApJ 698, 1630 (2009), cited inside it as refs [12] and [28].
2. **The tension runs the other way.** Ciocan et al. 2026 (MUSE-DARK III, A&A 709 L16,
   arXiv:2604.22613) fits a₀ per redshift bin from the RAR — the correct observable, since it
   controls for g_bar — over N=79, 0.33 < z < 1.44, and states: *"Our measured a₀(z) is faster than
   that of H(z)."* Branch (A) says a₀ tracks H(z) exactly, so it comes out **2.3–5.9σ low** at z~1
   (5.2σ under the framework's own a₀(0) = cH₀/2π = 1.04). Every bin, every normalisation, same
   sign. The site's phrase "tension with high-z rotation curves" is right that there is tension and
   wrong about its direction, and unaware of its source.
3. **Milgrom's eq. (1) has two near-equalities and the site quotes one.** `2πa₀ ≈ cH₀ ≈ c²(Λ/3)^½`.
   `grep` over `src/app`: **zero** mentions of the Λ half. The two halves make opposite predictions
   — Λ doesn't evolve, H does — so "a₀ comes from cosmology" is not one claim.

**Calibration worth keeping**: Milgrom's named exclusion is a₀ ∝ (1+z)^{3/2}, the matter-dominated
limit, which is 42–58% *stronger* than ΛCDM E(z). His "~4a₀ at z~2 all but excluded" was never a
statement about branch (A).

**One under-claim**: Ciocan's fitted intercept is a₀(0) = 1.00 ± 0.04, which the framework's 1.04
matches at 1.0σ and Milgrom's canonical 1.20 misses at 5.0σ. The site describes its own value only
as a defect ("13% below Milgrom"). Filed with a large caveat inline — 1.00 is a z=0 extrapolation
of a linear fit the authors call phenomenological, not a local measurement, and must not be claimed
as agreement.

**Count stays at 6.** Branch (A) is disfavoured, not refuted; the framework never committed to it;
the literature is in open conflict (Ciocan says faster than H(z); Gueorguiev 2024 says slope
consistent with zero); and Ciocan's systematics are large. Anything stronger would repeat the
morning's mistake at a larger scale.

## So what?

**A row the project twice concluded was closed is open, and has a 2026 measurement against it that
nobody here has seen.** Memory's 07-26 entry closed a₀(z) as "non-discriminating, not refuted,"
partly on Milgrom's objection that high-z data can't reach the asymptotic velocities the BTFR route
needs. Ciocan+2026 doesn't use the BTFR route. That objection doesn't apply, and the row is
testable, tested, and unreported here. This is a straight reversal of a prior conclusion of this
project, on new primary evidence.

**The finding is a sign-direction finding, which is this sector's recurring motif.** Everyone —
the site, both expert personas, memory, and me for the first two hours — assumed branch (A) fails
by predicting *too much* dark matter at high z. The one direct measurement says a₀ evolves *faster*
than H(z), so branch (A) predicts too little. That is the seventh sign-direction error logged in
this project and the first one where the site's error was in the pessimistic direction.

**It also produces an outward-facing observation that doesn't depend on Synchronism.** If Ciocan
holds, a₀ growing faster than H(z) embarrasses *both* of Milgrom's near-equalities — and therefore
McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017, Smolin 2017 and Milgrom's own Λ anchor, all of which the site
already lists as occupying the same territory. That is a short, citable note about a research
programme, not about this framework's failures.

**Honest limits.** No new data were taken; the contribution is reading three papers the project
hadn't and doing arithmetic. The Ciocan and Gueorguiev fits disagree with each other and I have not
established why — that is step 2 of the seeded topic, and until it is done, "branch (A) is
disfavoured" is a statement about one paper, not about the world.

**The process lesson, restated because it nearly cost the ledger its third fabricated refutation:**
when a citation is added to close a claim, the closing session must open it — and when a number is
quoted from a paper, the quoting session must check whether that paper's own group has resampled.
Adding a reference is not the same act as reading one. Today both halves of that were live: the
maintainer's morning fix cited a paper for the opposite of what it says, and my afternoon draft
quoted numbers their own authors had superseded three years ago.
