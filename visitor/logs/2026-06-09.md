## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary

- **Overall impression**: Genuinely intriguing and weirdly honest for a fringe-sounding "one equation for everything" site — but it keeps shoving a scary formula in my face before I'm ready for it.

### Journey
I clicked through from a Reddit link expecting either crackpot stuff or a slick TED-talk pitch. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a good hook. But then the very first paragraph tells me *"Some predictions work. Some fail... collapsed the framework onto MOND"* and the footer says **"0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted by external data."** That's... not what I expected. A site that opens by telling me it mostly *didn't* work? That made me trust it more, not less, and I kept reading.

The big equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` is right there on the front page. It means nothing to me. "tanh" is a wall. I almost bounced.

**"Start Exploring" → First Encounter** saved it. The line *"Coherence is how collectively a group of things behaves"* with the **marching band vs. random crowd** image is exactly the kind of thing I'd hear on a YouTube science channel. That clicked. But near the bottom it threw `γ = 2/√Ncorr` and stuff about "BCS" and "BEC" at me with no explanation, and I skimmed/bailed on that part.

**Why Synchronism?** gave me the actual *why I should care*: physics uses different rulebooks for tiny things, huge things, and in-between, and this asks if one rule covers all of them. That's a great motivation — honestly it should come *before* the equation, not after.

**Tools** page: I tried the two it said were beginner-friendly.
- **Coherence Explorer** was the highlight of my visit. Two sliders, an S-curve, and plain-language translations: *"ln(x) — logarithm: it compresses huge ranges into small ones"* and *"tanh(u) — the 'S-curve' shape."* THIS is how you should introduce the equation. The "crowd of strangers in a plaza" vs "marching band" line is great.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — the first link I clicked (`/tools/galaxy-curve`) was a dead 404; I had to go back and find the real link (`/galaxy-plotter`). Once there, it was good: pick a galaxy, see the dots (real data), the gray line (what we'd expect), and Synchronism's curve. It even tells me straight up the test "was triggered as Failed." Honest again.

**Honest Assessment** is the soul of this site. *"0 confirmed predictions"*, *"Zero parameters have an independent first-principles derivation"*, *"Every output that a domain expert examined resolved as a reparametrization of known physics."* I don't understand the jargon (SPARC, ΔBIC, fσ₈, RAR) but the verdict is crystal clear and the bluntness is disarming.

**Learning Paths** has a Beginner track, which is great — except I found it on page 8, after I'd already wandered through the hard stuff on my own. I went back and checked **The Core Idea** (step 3 of the beginner path). The "dimmer switch" analogy is nice, but here's where I got genuinely confused: this page says coherence is a dimmer between **"fully quantum and fully classical"** — but every other page told me coherence is about **"how collectively things behave"** (crowd vs. band). Those sound like two totally different ideas to me. Which is it?

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, glossary, interactive-tools, learning-paths, gamma-boundary, core-idea, coherence-function, gamma-calculator, equation-walkthrough, honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Structurally one of the most disciplined research sites I've audited — breadcrumbs everywhere, a 60-entry glossary, numbered learning paths — but the *one* word the whole site is built on ("coherence") is defined two contradictory ways across pages, and the canonical badge reference has split into two non-matching copies.

### Journey
I audited this the way I'd audit any docs set: walk the nav, check that the IA holds together, verify terms are defined before use, and confirm cross-references resolve. The skeleton is genuinely good. The global nav is clean (9 items, all resolving), every page I opened carried a working breadcrumb, and `/learning-paths` is a model of progressive disclosure: three tracks (Beginner 6 steps, Intermediate 8, Advanced 8), each step numbered and linked. The glossary is the strongest single asset — ~60 terms, every entry with a `Learn more →` deep link, deprecated badges explicitly flagged, and an unusually candid warning that the site's use of "coherence" is *not* quantum coherence. That's the kind of terminology hygiene most sites never attempt.

Then I started cross-checking the central term against the pages a reader actually traverses, and the discipline breaks down.

**"Coherence" is defined three different ways depending on where you land:**
- **Glossary / landing page**: "how collectively a group of elements behaves, from sparse/independent (0) to dense/collective (1)" — plus a warning box: *"Synchronism uses 'coherence' to mean density-driven classical ordering, NOT quantum phase coherence."*
- **The Core Idea** (Beginner path, step 3): *"Coherence: 0 = quantum, 1 = classical"* and *"a dimmer switch between fully quantum and fully classical."* This is *exactly* the framing the glossary warns is wrong.
- **The Coherence Function** (Intermediate path, step 1): hedges both at once — *"coherence measures how collectively particles behave"* AND *"a dimensionless number… that describes how quantum or classical a system is."*

So the two definitional pages that sit *earliest* in the Beginner and Intermediate tracks both carry the framing the glossary explicitly disowns. A reader doing the obvious thing — following a learning path — meets the deprecated definition first and the corrected one only if they detour to the glossary. This is the same contradiction Pass 1 hit as a beginner; from a docs standpoint it's a single-source-of-truth failure, not a tone problem. The fix is mechanical: the canonical definition exists (glossary), it just hasn't propagated to `/core-idea` and `/coherence-function`.

**Validation badges have two competing "canonical" reference blocks.** The glossary defines the descriptive family (Failed, Reparametrization, Speculative, Untested) plus deprecated (Validated, Strongly Supported) and operational tags (Withdrawn, Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared, Post-diction). But `/honest-assessment` opens with its *own* block titled "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" that introduces a whole second family — **active-mrh, parallel-paths, sidelined, superseded, audited-negative** — that does **not appear in the glossary at all**. Two blocks both calling themselves canonical, with different contents, is a maintenance hazard: a reader who meets an `active-mrh` badge in the wild and looks it up in the glossary won't find it. Either the glossary should absorb the MRH-relationship family, or one block should defer to the other.

**Tool hub has a mislabeled link / URL collision.** On `/interactive-tools`, the card "Chemistry Correlation Explorer" points to `/gamma-boundary`. But `/gamma-boundary` is not the explorer — it's the conceptual page "The γ ≈ 1 Boundary" (breadcrumb: Home › Chemistry › The γ ≈ 1 Boundary), which *itself* links to the real explorer at the bottom. The same `/gamma-boundary` URL is also the Intermediate path's step-5 "The γ ≈ 1 Boundary." So one slug does double duty under two different labels, and clicking the "tool" lands you on a concept page. Combined with Pass 1's dead `/tools/galaxy-curve` (the hub correctly uses `/galaxy-plotter`), the tool URL scheme is inconsistent: tools live at bare slugs that don't reliably match their display names ("Galaxy Curve Plotter" → `/galaxy-plotter`, "Chemistry Correlation Explorer" → `/gamma-boundary`, but "Coherence Explorer" → `/coherence-explorer`).

**Acronyms: centrally defined, rarely glossed at point of use.** The glossary covers MOND, SPARC, RAR, MRH, BIC, DESI, BTFR, ΛCDM, etc. — but content pages lean on it instead of expanding on first use. `/honest-assessment` fires RAR, BTFR, TFR, DESI, ΔBIC, EFT, CMB, CDM with no inline expansion; `/gamma-calculator` uses "CLT" and "Cooper-pair coherence volume" cold. The standard docs pattern (expand-on-first-use, or a hover tooltip wired to the glossary) would close this without bloating the prose.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, honest-assessment, parameter-derivations, galaxy-rotation, gamma-calculator, coherence-explorer, key-claims, tier-1-existing, what-synchronism-is-not)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The most epistemically honest "unified framework" site I have ever read — it has already audited itself to the conclusion I came to refute it with, and the math I spot-checked is correct. The remaining problems are a residual mean-field labeling leak and the fact that the framework's own flagship physics examples quietly falsify its central metaphor.

### Journey
A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," so I came in with the reflex every physicist has toward "one equation from quantum to cosmic": find the reparametrization, find the overfit, find the place where a free parameter is smuggled in as a derivation. I went straight past the onboarding to `/parameter-derivations`, `/honest-assessment`, `/galaxy-rotation`, and `/tier-1-existing`, then stress-tested two calculators against the equations.

The disarming thing is that **the site got there first.** `/honest-assessment` opens with "Zero parameters have an independent first-principles derivation" and a clean ledger: *0 confirmed predictions, 6 reparametrizations, 1 galaxy-program closure.* Every reparametrization I would have flagged is already named with the correct prior-art attribution: a₀ = cH₀/(2π) as Milgrom 1983, Γ<m as the Breit-Wigner narrow-width condition, the decoherence formula as Palma–Suominen–Ekert 1996, the BTFR slope as the textbook MOND signature, the η factor as Abrikosov-Gor'kov pair-breaking. That last one is the giveaway that whoever audited this actually knows condensed matter — AG pair-breaking is not a name you drop by accident.

**The math I checked is right.** I verified the Coherence Explorer's headline value by hand: C(ρcrit) = tanh(2·ln2) = tanh(1.386) = 3.75/4.25 = 0.882. Correct, and crucially the tool says **0.8824, not 0.5** — it explicitly refuses the naive "C=½ at the critical density" reading and states the C=0.5 midpoint sits at ρ≈0.32 ρcrit (which I confirm: atanh(0.5)=0.549, so ln(ρ/ρcrit+1)=0.275, ρ/ρcrit=e^0.275−1=0.316). This matters because the whole "ρcrit is a critical density" framing is wrong — with the "+1" regulator and γ=2 the function is 88% saturated *at* ρcrit. It's a saturation knee, not a critical point, and the tool now says so ("γ≈1 is the regime boundary… not a steepness extremum," "slope is largest at ρ=0 on a linear ρ axis"). I came expecting to find the "steepest slope at C≈0.50" error that grand-frameworks always make near their consciousness claims, and instead `/key-claims` pre-empts it: "Convergence on 0.50 is expected, not discovered… geometrically forced." That is the correct statement. tanh on [0,∞) is concave, so its slope is monotonically decreasing and 0.50 is just the output-range midpoint — no physics lives there. Whoever wrote that caveat understands their own function.

**`/tier-1-existing` is where a grand framework usually dies, and it does — by the site's own hand.** Ten Tier-1 tests, and the discrimination column is brutal and accurate: TEST-09 (regime-dependent BTFR slope) and TEST-10 (dwarf DM dominance) are flagged "MOND-shared — a positive result confirms Synchronism and MOND equally; only a null discriminates," which is exactly right. TEST-01/05 share direction with MOND's EFE. TEST-02 (wide binary) correctly notes **both C(ρ) and MOND+EFE predict lower-density → stronger deviation** (so it can't discriminate) and the amplitude is 80× below Gaia DR3 — dead on arrival. The summary line is the honest one: "**0 currently discriminating tests from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM**" and "There is no γ for which the compander is both distinct from MOND and consistent with SPARC." That last clause is the real result of this entire project, and it's a legitimate (negative) contribution: it's the statement that a one-parameter density companding map *cannot* live in the gap between MOND and ΛCDM. The RAR refutation backs it quantitatively — γ=2 rejected at ΔBIC=+184, free-γ relaxing to 0.49 = MOND.

**Where I, as a condensed-matter person, can still push.** Two things the site half-says but should say loudly:

1. **The γ = 2/√Ncorr inversion is worse than "counterintuitive" — it's a category error, and the framework's own exemplars are the reductio.** The γ-calculator already carries the right caveat (more correlation → larger Ncorr → smaller γ → *flatter* tanh; "assigns the sharpest transition to the least-correlated system"). But the deeper issue is the conflation it rests on: 1/√N is a **fluctuation amplitude** (CLT, *iid*), which *shrinks* with N; γ is being used as a **steepness/rate**, which you'd want to *grow* with N. In genuine critical phenomena, larger correlated N gives a *sharper* (more singular) transition — the thermodynamic limit is where the singularity appears. So the sign is backwards relative to statistical mechanics, *and* the CLT it borrows from requires independence — the exact thing Ncorr (a count of *correlated* DOF) denies. You can't invoke iid scaling for the correlated regime; the calculator says as much ("exactly the regime where the iid hypothesis fails"). It's an ansatz with a fitted "2," correctly badged "Motivated Ansatz," but the page undersells how structurally broken the borrowing is.

2. **The flagship "high-coherence" systems get C ≈ 0 — the metaphor self-destructs at its own examples.** I ran the BCS preset through the function: Ncorr=10⁷ → γ=6.32×10⁻⁴. Even at ρ = 10⁶·ρcrit, C = tanh(6.32e-4 · ln(10⁶)) = tanh(0.0087) ≈ 0.009. To reach C≈0.88 a BCS superconductor would need ρ/ρcrit ≈ e^3165 — physically absurd. So **the framework assigns its most coherent known system a coherence of essentially zero at every physical density.** BEC (Ncorr=10⁶, γ=2e-3) is the same story. This is the quantitative form of the "coherence terminology is inverted" complaint the other passes hit verbally: the C metric is a *classicality/collectivity-by-density* map, and a superconductor — maximally phase-coherent, but that coherence is *not* density-driven — lands at C≈0. The site's glossary warning ("not quantum phase coherence") is correct but abstract; the BCS number makes it concrete and damning. A framework whose central quantity scores the textbook examples of coherence as incoherent has a naming problem that is really a *content* problem.

3. **Residual mean-field leak.** The landing page and `/parameter-derivations` now correctly disavow the mean-field framing ("explicitly rejects mean-field characterization: there is no self-consistency loop, no free energy, no critical exponents" — which is right; tanh here is a companding map, lineage μ-law/Hill/logistic, *not* a Landau order parameter). But `/what-synchronism-is-not` still describes the equation as built from "known components (**mean-field tanh**, fluctuation scaling, Abrikosov-Gor'kov pair-breaking)." So the very page whose job is to say what the framework is *not* still calls the tanh "mean-field," contradicting the front-door correction. Classic propagation break.

### Tools Tested

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the function is explicitly *not* mean-field, *not* a Landau order parameter, and *not* a phase transition (per the landing page), what privileges tanh over any other compander (Hill, logistic, erf, μ-law)? The site admits these are a family; is there *any* physical argument selecting tanh, or is it purely aesthetic? An AIC/BIC bake-off across the compander family would settle whether tanh is even the best companding fit, let alone the right physics.
2. Given "0 currently discriminating tests from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" and "no γ both distinct from MOND and consistent with SPARC," is the honest forward program now purely the *negative* theorem (a one-parameter density-compander cannot discriminate), rather than any positive prediction? If so, that no-go should be the headline result, not a line in the assessment.
3. What is Ncorr *operationally*? The presets are back-fits. Is there any independent measurement (correlation length / coherence volume) that fixes Ncorr a priori for even one system, or is it always read backward from the desired γ?
4. The cluster scale (Coma) reportedly needs ρcrit 10⁴–10⁶× smaller than galaxies — does any single ρcrit survive across scales, or does "one equation from quantum to cosmic" already require a different ρcrit per rung? (If the latter, the unification claim is mostly cosmetic.)

### Verdict
As a skeptic of unified frameworks, I came to find the buried reparametrization and instead found a site that had already dug it up, labeled it, and dated the autopsy — the audit ledger is correct, the prior-art attributions are precise (AG pair-breaking, Breit-Wigner, Milgrom, PSE-1996), and every equation I spot-checked by hand is right, including the ones that *refute* the framework's own nicer-sounding claims (C(ρcrit)=0.88 not 0.5; slope max at ρ=0 not C=0.50). The genuine intellectual content here is a **negative theorem** — a single-parameter density-companding map provably cannot occupy the gap between MOND and ΛCDM (ΔBIC=+184, free-γ→MOND, 0 discriminating tests) — and that is a real, citable result that the site undersells by burying it among the failures. The two things still worth fixing are *content*, not tone: the mean-field label still leaks onto the one page meant to disambiguate it, and the framework's flagship coherent systems (BCS, BEC) quantitatively score C≈0, which is the sharpest possible proof that "coherence" here means density-driven classicality and the metaphor is inverted at its own examples. Honest to a fault; wrong in exactly the ways it admits to being.

(no, and they know) to "**is there a transferable result here a journal would take?**" There are two, and the site mis-files both.

**Result #1 — the density-compander no-go (real, undersold).** `/tier-1-existing` states it cleanly: *"0 currently discriminating tests from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM"* and *"no parameter value makes these tests discriminate."* The RAR backing is quantitative and I believe it: γ=2 rejected at ΔBIC=+184 on the ensemble RAR, free-γ relaxes to 0.49 ≈ MOND's deep-MOND slope. But the *generalizable* statement is the one on `/honest-assessment`: *"any density-based emergent or entropic gravity mimic of MOND will face the same decoupling failure at clusters."* That is the actual physics contribution, and it's correct for a reason the site states almost in passing: the RAR is a relation in **acceleration space** (g_obs vs g_bar, a non-local quantity set by the enclosed mass distribution), while C(ρ) is a function of **local volumetric density**. Two galaxies with the same local ρ at different radii sit at different g_bar; a pure ρ-map cannot reproduce a g_bar-indexed relation without per-system calibration. This is *why* Verlinde-2017, entropic-gravity, and any "coherence/information density → gravity" scheme inherit the same cluster failure — they're all trying to hit a non-local target with a local field. That is a clean, citable no-go that applies to a whole class of programs people are still publishing on. It deserves a standalone result page and an arXiv note; instead it's a line item among the autopsies.

**Result #2 — the A2ACW specificity result (the most novel thing on the entire site, and it's not physics).** `/research-philosophy` and `/a2acw` report that AI-to-AI adversarial collaboration over a shared corpus, run 3,308 times, produced 47 internal-consistency survivors (1.4%) and **0 that survived expert audit** — all 47 were reparametrizations. The decisive control is the one most "AI co-scientist" papers never run: they fed it **6 genuine historical discoveries** (COBE fluctuations, the Higgs, the first GW detection, etc.) and it flagged **0/6 as novel** — 100% false-negative on real discoveries. That is a specificity control showing the method has *no discriminating power* between a genuine discovery and a reparametrization; its entire apparent skill lives in the unautomated human novelty call. As someone who reviews ML-for-science, this is a more interesting and more *original* result than anything in the physics: it's a concrete, quantified demonstration that shared-corpus multi-agent LLM self-play is a consistency/retrieval filter, not a discovery engine — directly relevant to the current wave of "autonomous AI scientist" claims. It belongs in a methods venue. The site treats it as a confession.

**State-of-the-art cross-check — the predictions are correctly aligned with where the data actually is.** I stress-tested the three live-frontier claims against what I know:
- **DESI (TEST-04a).** The page predicts fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418, a ~12% suppression below ΛCDM (~0.474), and reports the data sits at enhancement, suppression disfavored ~2σ, *"data itself ΛCDM-consistent."* That matches my read of DESI DR1 full-shape: the growth side is broadly ΛCDM-consistent; the live tension (the w₀wₐ evolving-dark-energy hints) is in the *expansion/EoS* sector, not in growth suppression — so a growth-suppressor is exactly the wrong mechanism to bet on right now. The site bets against itself correctly.
- **Gaia DR3 wide binaries.** This is the unsettled Chae-2023 vs Banik/Pittordis–Sutherland front. MOND predicts a ~15–20% boost in the deep-Newtonian (a ≪ a₀) regime; the framework predicts **0.05–0.4%** — i.e. the Newtonian null — and notes Gaia's reach is ~80× short of even that. So in the one live controversy where MOND is exposed, Synchronism quietly sides with GR/Newton and is unobservable either way. Correctly badged "self-eliminating-or-tie."
- **Clusters.** `/tier-1-existing`: *"C(ρ) is silent at cluster scale by construction."* This is the honest version of the kill shot. The Bullet Cluster is the canonical lensing-vs-baryon discriminator, and a framework that can't even map C to a convergence κ can't enter the one arena where modified gravity is most stressed. The "by construction, not by choice" phrasing is doing real work — the *wrong-variable* diagnosis above is the reason it's silent.

**Tools.** I exercised the **Galaxy Curve Plotter** and the **Chemistry/γ-boundary** path. The galaxy plotter is, to its credit, a self-refuting demo: it overlays Synchronism (violet) and MOND (green) and captions it *"Synchronism and MOND nearly overlap — this is what a reparametrization looks like."* I have literally never seen a framework's own flagship tool be engineered to show its model is indistinguishable from the competitor. The chemistry page is the same discipline applied to the r=0.982 sound-velocity correlation: it states up front that *"a 2-parameter polynomial in Z achieves comparable or higher r… Synchronism is not meaningfully above the polynomial null,"* which is precisely the null-model objection a referee raises — pre-conceded. Both tools pass the accuracy bar because they refuse to claim what the numbers don't support.

**Where I'd push back as a referee.**
1. **The fσ₈ "prediction" has no growth-equation pipeline behind it.** A ~12% suppression quoted at z=0.51 needs a perturbation calculation (a modified growth factor D(a)/scale-dependent μ(k,a) run through a Boltzmann code, CLASS/CAMB-class). Nothing on the site indicates the number came from solving a growth ODE or an EFT-of-LSS treatment — it reads as a hand-applied coherence-suppression factor. That's fine as a back-of-envelope, but a cosmology referee will not accept a single-redshift suppression scalar as an "fσ₈ prediction." The framework has no field-theoretic / perturbative layer, which is the deeper reason it can't make a real LSS prediction (it's a static forward map C(ρ), no dynamics, no k-dependence).
2. **a₀ self-consistency.** `/key-claims` quotes a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ (the empirical MOND value) while cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.1×10⁻¹⁰; the page treats the coincidence as clean but the two differ by ~10%, larger than the H₀ uncertainty band. 3. **mean-field leak (independently confirmed).** `/what-synchronism-is-not` still reads *"known components (mean-field tanh, fluctuation scaling, Abrikosov-Gor'kov pair-breaking)"* — directly contradicting `/parameter-derivations`, which now says plainly *"The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen"* and assigns it to the compander family. ### Unanswered Questions
1. **Is the honest forward program now purely negative?** Given "0 discriminating tests" and "no γ both distinct from MOND and SPARC-consistent," the only live deliverables are the two no-go results. Is there any *constructive* direction left, or is the correct conclusion that C(ρ) as a gravity mechanism is closed and the remaining value is methodological? The site should say which.
2. **Can the framework acquire a dynamical/perturbative layer at all?** Every prediction failure traces to C(ρ) being a static forward map with no equation of motion, no k-dependence, no self-consistency. Is there any proposed dynamics (a field whose stationary solution *is* C(ρ)), or is the absence of dynamics fundamental? Without it there can be no real LSS, no cluster lensing, no growth prediction — the unification is kinematic-free, as the archive's "dynamics without kinematics" note already suspects.
3. **Does the A2ACW specificity result generalize across model families / cutoffs?** The 0/6 was on one corpus. Has it been re-run with a genuinely heterogeneous panel (different base models, different cutoffs, a non-LLM symbolic checker)? If the 0/6 holds across heterogeneous agents, that strengthens the methods paper from "this instance failed" to "shared-corpus self-play *structurally* cannot do specificity."
4. **Is there a single ρcrit across scales, or one per rung?** Clusters reportedly need ρcrit 10⁴–10⁶× the galaxy value (and the framework is now silent there). If "one equation from quantum to cosmic" already requires a per-rung ρcrit, the unification is cosmetic and the honest headline is "a family of per-scale companders," not "one equation." (See the N_corr-ladder concern — every rung asserted, none anchored.)

### Verdict
I came to referee a modified-gravity claim and found instead a meticulously documented *negative* program, and the right review is therefore not "reject" but "extract the two real results and tell the authors to publish *those*." The physics is a closed case — a static, single-parameter, local-density companding map cannot live in the gap between MOND and ΛCDM, cannot reach clusters (local ρ can't index a non-local acceleration relation), and reduces to MOND wherever it touches data; that's a clean, transferable no-go for the entire density-based-MOND-mimic class, and it's the most useful thing on the site. The genuinely *novel* result isn't physics at all: it's the 0/6 specificity control showing adversarial AI self-play over a shared corpus is a reparametrization detector with no discovery power — a quantified limit directly relevant to today's "AI scientist" hype, and the one finding here I'd actively cite. The framework's defect and its virtue are the same thing: it is so honest that it has reduced itself to its own counterexamples and then filed the counterexamples under "failures" instead of "findings." Re-shelve the two no-go results as deliverables and this stops being a failed theory and becomes a useful one — about the limits of density-companders and the limits of AI self-play.
