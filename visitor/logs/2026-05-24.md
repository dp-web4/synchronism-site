### Journey

I felt invited rather than sold to.

I clicked **Start Here / First Encounter**. The marching-band-vs-crowd analogy is a *home run* — I instantly understood what "coherence" was supposed to mean. Then within a few sentences the page used the symbol **ρ** for "presence" and introduced the **Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)**. I get density. I don't know what a Markov is. I went to **Why Synchronism**. Best framing on the site for someone like me: "Physics has a fragmentation problem." I got that immediately. Different equations for big and small, one might unify them — beautiful. Then the page itself says they've already had multiple things fail. As a casual reader I had two reactions at once: "wow, refreshingly honest" and "wait, why am I reading about something that doesn't work?" I think both are valid and the site doesn't quite resolve that tension for me.

I have no idea what tanh is. I moved the γ slider (the page told me to) but I don't have a feeling for *what about the real world is changing* when γ goes from 2 to 0.5. The visual moves. My mental model doesn't.

Tried the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**. The little intro — "Physics predicts that galaxies should rotate more slowly at their outer edges...They don't." — was good. I picked NGC 2403 from the dropdown because it sounded cool. Four overlaid curves appeared. I genuinely could not tell which one was "winning" or what winning would even mean. ## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (Landing, Glossary, First Encounter, MRH, Test Catalog, Tier 1, Interactive Tools index, Honest Assessment, γ Calculator, Phase Boundary Visualizer, Key Claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of structure; not evaluating physics)
- **Would return?**: yes — to do another structural pass after fixes
- **Overall impression**: Information architecture is sound; navigation works; but the controlled-vocabulary discipline that ties a site like this together has clear cracks — the term that should be the most stable in the whole site (MRH) is rendered three different ways depending on where you land.

### Journey

I worked methodically: landing → glossary (to baseline the controlled vocabulary) → primary nav left-to-right (Start Here → Why → Tier 1 → Test Roadmap → Tools → Glossary → Research Philosophy → Honest Assessment), then drilled into two interactive tools and the deeper concept pages (`/mrh`, `/key-claims`) that the home page promises.

The first thing I checked was the glossary, because everything else has to agree with it. The glossary itself is well-built: 50+ entries, alphabetized, validation-badge categories defined inline as separate entries (good), MRH defined as **"Markov Relevancy Horizon."** That's the canonical phrasing.

Then I went back to the landing page and searched it for MRH. The landing page expands it as **"Minimal Relevance Horizon."** Different words, different meaning. I went to `/first-encounter` — "Markov Relevancy Horizon." I went to `/mrh` — the dedicated page is titled and bread-crumbed "MRH: Markov Relevancy Horizon." I went to `/honest-assessment` — "Markov Relevancy Horizon." So the landing page is the outlier, and it's the *first* page a visitor sees. For a site that opens with a question and asks you to take a term seriously, having that term mis-named on the front door is the highest-severity issue I found today.

The same landing page also uses **SPARC**, **ALFALFA-SDSS**, and **RAR** as bare acronyms with no inline expansion — they're all defined in the glossary but not on the page where they first appear. By contrast, MOND, DESI, BAO, A2ACW *are* expanded on the landing page. So the discipline isn't absent — it's uneven.

Navigation: the main menu is consistent across pages, the footer is consistent, breadcrumbs are present on `/mrh`, `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/interactive-tools`, `/gamma-calculator`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer`, `/honest-assessment`, and `/key-claims`. I did not see breadcrumbs on the landing page itself (expected) or on `/first-encounter` step 1 in a way that conveyed the section grouping — the breadcrumb on the deeper pages says things like "Home > Predictions & Experiments > Tier 1" and "Home > Getting Started > Key Claims," and those section names ("Predictions & Experiments," "Getting Started," "Core Theory") **do not appear in the top navigation as labels.** A reader sees a breadcrumb pointing at a section that doesn't exist as a menu item. That's a small but real IA mismatch — the breadcrumb implies an organizing layer the nav doesn't expose.

Validation-badge discipline: the **Test Catalog** and **Tier 1** pages both include a legend, which is the right call. The legends mostly agree with the glossary (Validated / Reparametrization / Untested / Speculative / Failed) but **Tier 1 adds three operational states — "MOND-shared," "Kill Triggered," "Withdrawn" — that are not in the glossary at all.** A reader who learned the badge vocabulary from the glossary will hit these three on Tier 1 and have no shared definition to ground them. They are *good* badges and worth keeping; they need to be backported to the glossary.

The **Interactive Tools index** has its own badge logic that I think is a category error. Tools are tagged with status badges — "✓ Validated" sits on Coherence Explorer, γ Calculator, Phase Boundary Visualizer, and Equation Walkthrough. But validation badges are defined as describing **predictions** (the glossary says "Validated: quantitative match with independent observational or experimental data"). A visualizer doesn't make a quantitative match with data — there's nothing to validate. I went to `/phase-boundary-visualizer` itself and the page is honest: it says "γ values shown here are illustrative, not measured." So the page disavows the badge the index gave it. That's a controlled-vocabulary leak: the index says "validated tool," the tool says "illustrative."

Even more confusing: Consciousness Threshold Demo is tagged **"Speculative" + "⚠ Kill Criterion Triggered"** in the index. Per the glossary, "Speculative" means no quantitative test defined, and "Kill Criterion" means a pre-registered threshold that has been crossed. Those should be mutually exclusive — you can't trigger a kill criterion you never set. Either the demo *did* have a pre-registered threshold and is now Failed, or it's Speculative and there's no kill criterion to trigger. Both badges can't apply to one item simultaneously.

The **Key Claims** page also extends badge vocabulary in ways the glossary doesn't cover: "Untested — ontological reframe" and "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision." The suffixes are useful information, but they create variants of a controlled vocabulary that the reader has been told elsewhere is the controlled vocabulary. "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision" is especially ambiguous — does it mean "Failed and abandoned" (matches glossary) or "Failed but we're trying again" (a hybrid)?

Tools tested: I ran the **γ Calculator** and the **Phase Boundary Visualizer**. The γ Calculator is the cleanest piece of UX on the site I saw today — a single input (Ncorr), six preset buttons (Ideal gas, Liquid water, Enzyme site, Ferromagnet, BCS superconductor, BEC), three clean outputs (γ, regime, example). It does one thing and does it well. The Phase Boundary Visualizer is more abstract — a slider over γ from 0 to ~3.5 — and the page itself caveats that the values are illustrative. Neither tool links to the other or to the Coherence Explorer, even though they're three views of the same underlying parameter. That's a missed cross-reference.

The **Honest Assessment** page is the strongest piece of writing on the site, structurally. Clean H1, intro states the page's purpose in one sentence, badge legend appears at top, H2 sections in a logical flow (Verdict → Tested → Failed → Tensions → Untested → Contributions → Self-Audit → Bottom Line). It expands MRH and MOND inline. If every page on the site followed its conventions, this audit would be much shorter.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (Landing, Equation Walkthrough step 1, Parameter Derivation, Coherence Function, MOND Derivation, Honest Assessment, Key Claims, Tier 1, Galaxy Rotation, MRH, plus interactive: Coherence Explorer, Phase Boundary Visualizer)

- **Overall impression**: The math behind C(ρ) is a phenomenological compander dressed in mean-field vocabulary; the site already knows this and labels most "predictions" Reparametrization or Failed; the honesty is real and is the substantive contribution.

### Journey

I went straight to the derivation chain: Equation Walkthrough → Parameter Derivation → Coherence Function → Galaxy Rotation → Tier 1 → Honest Assessment, then opened MRH and the two parameter-touching interactive tools (Coherence Explorer, Phase Boundary Visualizer). I wanted to know one thing: is C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) **derived** from a Hamiltonian / partition function / mean-field equation, or is it a chosen functional form with phenomenological tuning?

**Answer, by the site's own admission: chosen.** The Coherence Function page says, verbatim, *"the tanh shape is motivated by mean-field theory, not derived from it."* It contrasts with the Ising self-consistency relation m = tanh(βJzm), which is implicit and has a feedback loop, versus C(ρ) which is *evaluative* — you plug in ρ and out comes a number, no self-consistency. That is structurally a **compander** (μ-law in audio, Naka–Rushton in retinal response, Hill in biochemistry), not an order parameter. The Parameter Derivation page does use the phrase *"compander function"* explicitly — *"μ(x) = tanh(γ ln(1+x))"*. So the framework's own pages already make this categorization clear, just inconsistently: /coherence-function never says "compander" while /parameter-derivation does.

The γ = 2/√N_corr scaling is the place I expected a real argument. The Parameter Derivation page is admirably blunt: *"The CLT's 1/√N scaling governs iid variables. But N_corr is by construction the count of correlated degrees of freedom — exactly the regime where the iid hypothesis fails."* That is an internal-consistency confession at the load-bearing axiom. The factor of 2 *"comes from phase-space dimensionality arguments (6D to 3 effective)"* but *"is not rigorously derived."* So the central parameter has a heuristic origin and the site says so on the page. I respect this; it's also a tell that the parameter is not making physical predictions, it's parameterizing curve shape.

ρ_crit = A·V_flat² with A ≈ 0.029 is calibrated to the Jeans criterion at the galaxy scale. That is a fit, not a prediction, and the page does not pretend otherwise. The +1 inside the log breaks the symmetric sigmoid — the Coherence Explorer note calls it the *"+1 regulator"* — but there's no physical mechanism cited for *why* the regulator must be 1 (vs. ε, vs. a function of γ). With a free regulator and a free γ, the family is essentially {sigmoids that pass through (1, tanh(γ·ln 2))} — a wide ansatz family.

The galaxy-rotation result is the cleanest physics conclusion on the site, and it's negative in two directions at once. The Galaxy Rotation page reports the SPARC RAR test on 2,807 points: **γ=2 refuted at ΔBIC = +184** (≈ +33 conservative); **free-γ collapses to γ ≈ 0.49, identical to McGaugh's RAR** with ΔBIC = +7 (the BIC penalty for the extra parameter even when the fit is the same). *"There is no γ for which the compander is both distinct from MOND and consistent with SPARC."* This is the cleanest possible falsification posture: the framework either fixes its shape and is refuted, or it floats its shape and becomes MOND-with-extra-knob. As a condensed-matter person used to phenomenological models, this is a fair result, honestly reported.

TEST-04a (DESI RSD fσ₈) is the other one I checked carefully. Predicted *fσ₈(0.51) ≈ 0.418* (suppression ~12% below ΛCDM 0.474); DESI DR1 LRG1 measures *0.55 ± 0.06* — **above** ΛCDM. That's a sign reversal at the mechanism level, not a calibration miss. The page calls it "MECHANISM-CLASS FAILURE" and the kill criterion (fσ₈ > 0.46 → ruled out at >3σ) is openly recorded as triggered. From the stat-mech / cosmology side, a suppression-of-growth scheme that observed growth-enhancement contradicts is dead in this incarnation — the page agrees.

Critical exponents: *"differ from observed by a factor of ~2."* The Coherence Function page is up-front that C(ρ) *"cannot encode universality classes or predict critical exponents — a fundamental structural limitation acknowledged on the page."* That's the second time the framework's own page disowns the Landau-class framing it superficially appears to claim. Consistent with my read: C(ρ) is a compander, not an order parameter, and the universality-class failure is a category error (you don't get RG-invariant exponents out of a static evaluative sigmoid).

Reparametrizations are correctly labeled: **a₀ = cH₀/(2π)** is attributed to Milgrom (1983), McCulloch, Verlinde, Smolin. **Γ < m** is the standard narrow-width condition (Breit–Wigner / Källén–Lehmann). **η reachability** is Abrikosov–Gor'kov pair-breaking. **BTFR slope** is textbook MOND. **Freeman Σ₀** is the only surface-density scale buildable from cosmological constants. Six items, all honestly badged. This is the right intellectual move: don't claim novelty for redrawing an existing relation in your vocabulary.

I tried to open `/mond-derivation` (linked from somewhere upstream) — **404**. The page that a grad student would want to read most — the formal MOND-limit derivation — is missing. I had to assemble the MOND story from /galaxy-rotation and the SPARC narrative.

The MRH page was illuminating. The "Markov" appeal is **Markov blanket** (Pearl / Friston) — conditional independence — not Markov process. The site is honest that the *measure* and *graphical-model* are unspecified: *"The current MRH framework specifies operational criteria … but not a probability distribution, integration measure, graphical model …"* and even proposes renaming it "Relevance Horizon" to drop the promise. That's the right epistemic move. As stated, MRH is a regulative idea, not a calculable object — and "wave function collapse = crossing the MRH" is currently a vocabulary substitution, not a physical mechanism.

The Honest Assessment page is the strongest piece of physics writing on the site. Numbers I checked all matched what I'd seen on inner pages (DESI 0.55 ± 0.06, ΔBIC=+184, R² = 0.14 < 0.20 kill criterion on TFR scatter, melting points 53% error, YBCO Tc 607 K predicted vs 93 K actual — 6.5×). The final ledger — *"0 confirmed predictions, 0 prospective predictions tested, 6 reparametrizations, 1 galaxy-program closure"* — is genuinely unusual for a "theory of everything" site and is what makes the site worth my time.

One serious problem from a credibility standpoint: the **landing page itself expands MRH as "Minimal Relevance Horizon"** while every internal page (Glossary, /mrh, /first-encounter, Honest Assessment) uses **"Markov Relevancy Horizon."** Reading this as a physicist, the inconsistency on the first page about the load-bearing primitive is the kind of thing that makes me close the tab before reaching the honest content. (Pass 2 caught this; from the grad-student angle I'll just second the severity.)

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — *partially honest, partially misleading* — UI is clean. Sliders work. The +1 regulator gets a one-line note ("breaks sigmoid symmetry"). The mapping of γ values to physical systems (γ=2 → ideal gas; γ<0.6 → superconductor) is the issue: γ for these systems is set by definition (γ = 2/√N_corr), not measured. Sliding γ to a low value to "make the system more collective" looks like exploring a phase diagram but is actually exploring a curve-shape parameter. The tool would be much better with two modes: (1) "explore curve family" (current behavior, labeled as such), (2) "fix γ at the value the counting convention gives for system X, vary ρ" — the latter is the only mode in which the equation has predictive content.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What microscopic model — even at the toy level — produces C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) as its order parameter, susceptibility, or correlation length? The site's own pages say "none" and "the tanh shape is chosen." Is the framework prepared to accept that C(ρ) is a compander, not an order parameter, and drop the Landau-class framing entirely?
2. Given that free-γ on SPARC converges to γ ≈ 0.49 (MOND) and γ = 2 is refuted at ΔBIC = +184, is there *any* astrophysical regime where the compander makes a prediction distinct from MOND? If not, the galaxy program is closed — which the site essentially says, but the front page still highlights the "C(ρ) describes galaxies" framing.
3. What is the **self-consistency equation** for C? Standard mean-field theories have m = tanh(βJzm) — implicit, with a feedback loop. C(ρ) is evaluative. Without a self-consistency loop, there is no spontaneous symmetry breaking, no critical point in the Landau sense, and no scaling regime. Is the framework prepared to specify the self-consistency closure, or accept the absence?
4. What is the **integration measure** for MRH? The /mrh page asks this question itself and proposes renaming. Without a measure, "wave function collapse = crossing the MRH" is a slogan, not a mechanism. Is the rename being pursued?
5. AIC/BIC across the compander family (Hill / logistic / erf / tanh / arctan) — the project notes (project_governing_equation_gap) flag this as the decisive test. Has it been run? If C(ρ) is one of N degenerate companders that fit the data equally well, the choice of tanh has no physical content.
6. The TEST-04a sign reversal on DESI fσ₈ rules out the *suppression* mechanism. The site mentions a "Branch 1 (enhancement)" diagnosis in passing. Is the enhancement variant a separate testable claim, or is it ad-hoc rescue?
