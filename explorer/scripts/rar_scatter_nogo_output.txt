*** CONSTRUCTIVE: HOW NON-LOCAL MUST A DENSITY-KEYED THEORY BE? ***
==============================================================================
  A gradient-coupled (rather than algebraic) density field smooths rho over a
  range lambda -- this is the Burrage-Copeland-Millington 2017 escape route.
  Convolve the measured Sigma(r) with an exponential kernel of range lambda,
  and ask: at what lambda does the density variable become as good a predictor
  of the boost as g_bar is? That is a LOWER BOUND on the required coupling range.

  median SPARC disk scale length R_d = 2.36 kpc

   lambda (kpc)  lambda/R_d   sigma(logB|Sigma_l)   vs g_bar 0.1178
            0.0         0.0                0.1613             1.37x
            0.5         0.2                0.1593             1.35x
            1.0         0.4                0.1580             1.34x
            2.0         0.8                0.1572             1.33x
            4.0         1.7                0.1559             1.32x
            8.0         3.4                0.1525             1.29x
           16.0         6.8                0.1427             1.21x
           32.0        13.6                0.1670             1.42x
           64.0        27.1                0.1737             1.47x
            inf         inf                0.1947             1.65x

  lambda* (first grid point matching g_bar) = not reached on grid
  Interpretation: the smaller lambda*, the more 'local' a viable theory may be.
  lambda* comparable to or larger than the disk itself means the density
  variable only works once it has been integrated into something g_bar-like.

  NOTE: no kernel width recovers g_bar performance. Smoothing improves the
  local variable only to 1.21x at lambda ~ 7 R_d and then DEGRADES, because
  g_bar = G M(<r)/r^2 is not a smoothed density: it carries an explicit 1/r^2
  that no convolution of Sigma can generate. Making the coupling differential
  is not a free dial -- the data fixes the required kernel to the Newtonian one.
