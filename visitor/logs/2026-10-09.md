## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /learning-paths, /interactive-tools, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A strikingly honest "here's how our idea failed" notebook. I came for a big theory and left understanding mostly that it didn't work. That's refreshing, but I'm not sure what I'm supposed to *do* with it.

### Journey
Clicked in from Reddit. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", hooked me right away. The very next line, "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated", made me do a double take. I've never seen a science site lead with its own failure. I respected it, but it also deflated the hook within five seconds. Then came the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. The plain-English gloss ("a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are") helped a bit, but "squashing function" and "compressing function" are still just words to me. The scorecard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, 0 of 9 surviving, 3,308 sessions, 14,610 galaxies) was easy to read. The disclosure that "no outside physicist has reviewed these claims" felt honest.

I picked the "No Physics Background" Beginner Path. **Why Synchronism?** is labelled "Step 1 of 6". **First Encounter** is labelled "Step 2 of 6". But the landing page and /learning-paths both say the Beginner Path has **5 steps**, and /learning-paths lists exactly 5 (Why → First Encounter → Galaxy Plotter → What It Is Not → Honest Assessment). I checked the raw page text: the "of 6" wording is really there. It's small, but it made me wonder whether I'd missed a step.

First Encounter was the best page I read. The analogies made sense to me: marching band vs. a crowd in a plaza, a dimmer switch that snaps, a whisper in a loud room. The A–G lettered sections and "~10 minutes" estimate made it feel doable. But the "expert notes" kept cutting in with things like "sign inversion" and "matches MOND by construction", and that gave me whiplash. I'd just learn what γ is, and then be told the formula for γ failed but the fitted values land near 1 anyway. I couldn't tell which part I was meant to keep. The quantum measurement section ("No observer needed. No consciousness required. Just a boundary crossing") was the most exciting idea on the site. I wasn't sure if it's still a live idea or another casualty.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter**: I understood this one! A dropdown picks one of 5 galaxies. Blue dots are real data, grey dashes are "what visible stuff alone predicts", green is MOND, and violet is Synchronism. The Neptune analogy (outer stars should go slower, but they don't) finally made "the dark matter problem" click for me. The takeaway is blunt: the violet curve gets the shape wrong, and the green MOND line follows the dots. So the punchline of the beginner tool is "the rival wins." Honest, but a bit of a gut punch for step 3 of 5.

**Coherence Explorer**: The sliders work and the S-curve moves, which is satisfying. The intro says "Large γ (γ = 2, free atoms) = a sharp cliff. Small γ (superconductors) = a long gentle ramp" as if that were simply true. Further down, a note says this rule "runs backwards against real condensed-matter physics." I'd already half-absorbed the backwards version before I hit the warning. The note that the density axis has no units, and that the presets "are not physical systems", left me unsure what I was actually exploring.

**γ Calculator**: The page title literally says "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted", and the intro says "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails." That's clear, and kind of a fun idea. Clicking Water and then BEC shows the backwards result. But I had to take the page's word that a BEC "should" be sharp. I don't know enough to see the flaw "myself", as promised.

**Honest Assessment**: The "If you read nothing else" box is great. It says the equation lost every test, it's MOND in different notation where it fits, and "what survives is the record, not the physics." The rest of the page is ~18,000 words, which I won't read. The badge vocabulary (active-mrh, parallel-paths, sidelined, superseded, audited-negative…) is defined, but "MRH" inside a badge name means nothing to me.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /galaxy-rotation, /mrh, /gamma-boundary, /tier-1, /honest-assessment, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /chemistry-correlation-explorer), plus one attempted guess at /coherence-equation
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe. I'd come back as a case study in self-audit, not as physics I'd build on.
- **Overall impression**: The algebra I could check is right, and the site already says the hard things a referee would: the galaxy law *is* MOND's simple μ at γ = ½, tanh is chosen rather than derived, and γ = 2/√N_corr runs backwards from mean-field physics. What remains are smaller rigor seams. One physics overgeneralization (Cassini), one badge that's too generous (chemistry), an asymmetric circularity critique in the plotter, and count drift between pages.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "interesting AI project", so I expected a grand unified theory with an equation fitted to everything. I went straight to /parameter-derivations. I was expecting to have to dig for the problems, but the page puts them in the badges. γ = 2/√N_corr is "Speculative — Motivated Ansatz", with the note that "invoking CLT for correlated N_corr is self-contradictory". tanh is "Reparametrization", with the line "The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen". That's exactly the objection I'd have raised: in Weiss/Ising mean field, m = tanh(βJzm + βh) comes out of a self-consistency condition, and nothing like that happens here. The ρ_crit coefficient is "Audited-Negative": the stated A = 4π/(βJ²GR₀²) gives 4.6×10⁻⁵, not 0.029, and the velocity exponent has the wrong sign. The page ends with "Zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." I've never seen a framework page say that about itself.

**Checking the math myself** (things a grad student can verify with pencil and paper):
- **γ = ½ ⇒ MOND simple μ.** tanh(½ ln y) = (y−1)/(y+1). With y = 1+x this gives C = x/(x+2) = (x/2)/(1+x/2), which is the simple μ with a₀ = 2a₀′. The claim holds, and it's exact, not asymptotic. This also explains the "convention-dependent factor of 2" in a₀. For general γ the closed form is C = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. That's a Hill-type rational function in (1+x), analytic for all x > −1, so there's no non-analyticity anywhere. "Crossover" (the renamed visualizer) is the correct word, and "phase transition" would not be. **The site never shows this closed form.** It is the single most clarifying line for a physicist, because it makes "this is a sigmoid family, not a critical phenomenon" obvious at a glance.
- **Limits.** C ≈ γx for small x and C ≈ 1 − 2x^{−2γ} for large x. Coherence Explorer says C(ρ_crit) = 0.327 at γ = 0.49 and that C = ½ sits at ρ ≈ 2.07ρ_crit. I get tanh(0.49·ln 2) = 0.327 and 1 + x = e^{atanh(½)/0.49} = 3.07. Both are correct.
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π.** At H₀ = 67.4, cH₀ = 6.55×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², and dividing by 2π gives 1.04×10⁻¹⁰. Correct, and correctly badged as Milgrom's 1983 coincidence.
- **Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) ≈ 119 M☉/pc².** I get 0.2485 kg/m², which is 119 M☉/pc². Correct, and correctly flagged as "the a₀ row propagated through a linear relation, not a second derivation." That's an honest reading of TEST-08 being reclassified.
- **ΔBIC +7.1 with Δχ² ≈ −0.84 for free γ.** The penalty works out to ln N ≈ 7.94, so N ≈ 2,800. TEST-25 lists 2,807 SPARC points, and ln 2807 = 7.94. That's internally consistent: free γ buys essentially nothing over γ = ½ (MOND).
- **TEST-10 cap f_DM ≤ 68.5%** = 1 − 1/B_max = 1 − Ω_m. This is consistent with B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17.
- **BTFR slopes.** I was ready to object to "MOND's 3.81", since MOND's deep-regime BTFR is exactly n = 4. A verbatim fetch shows /honest-assessment already makes the distinction: "MOND's parameter-free prediction is n = 4 exactly… MOND's 3.81 ± 0.04 is a fit, not a prediction — it is MOND run through the same regime-mix pipeline." That's good. (It also notes the observed 3.75 ± 0.10 sits ~2.5σ below MOND's 4, which is an honest thing to say about the rival.)

**Where I'd push back:**

1. **Cassini.** /galaxy-rotation says the +17.95σ Cassini failure is "shared with all MOND interpolating functions." That's too strong. The Cassini quadrupole bound (the external-field-effect quadrupole in QUMOND) constrains how fast ν → 1 at high acceleration. Interpolating functions with sharp transitions, like the n-family with large n, can satisfy it. The tension Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024 report is for the *RAR-preferred* functions. /honest-assessment words this correctly ("RAR-preferred MOND interpolating functions **8.7σ**"). /galaxy-rotation doesn't. There's also a σ-mismatch: the Coherence Explorer preset says γ = 0.49 "fails Cassini at 8.7σ", which is the literature number, marginalized and for the RAR-preferred family, while the site's own unmarginalized run at that γ gives +17.95σ. Both numbers are legitimate, but the preset quotes one without saying which, and the galaxy page quotes the other.

2. **Galaxy Plotter applies the circularity critique asymmetrically.** The page rightly says of the Synchronism curve, "A curve built from V_flat cannot be a prediction of V_flat." But the MOND curve's baryonic mass also comes from V_flat: "M_b = 47·V_flat⁴ M☉". So the green line's outer level is also partly set by the observed V_flat. MOND's own BTFR normalization is 1/(G a₀) ≈ 63 M☉ (km/s)⁻⁴ at a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰, so with 47 the MOND asymptote should land about (47/63)^{1/4} ≈ 0.93 V_flat. That's not exactly V_flat, so it isn't fully circular. Still, a beginner reading "MOND follows the dots" doesn't know the mass was set from the dots' plateau. Separately, v_b uses the *spherical* enclosed mass of an exponential disk. A thin exponential disk (Freeman 1970, Bessel-function solution) peaks about 15% higher near 2.2 R_d, so the Newtonian curve is biased low in the inner region. That's fine for a teaching toy, but it should be labelled, because the gap between dots and dashed line *is* the dark-matter argument the tool teaches.

3. **The chemistry badge is too generous.** Both /gamma-boundary and the Chemistry Correlation Explorer say the per-material N_corr/γ assignment table is unpublished, and the Explorer says "If γ was set per material with the property in view, a high r… carries no information." A polynomial in Z matches the r values (|Δr| ≤ 0.07). Under that disclosure the correct reading isn't "Reparametrization", which means known physics restated in new notation. It's that the correlation is *uninformative until the assignment procedure is published*. "Untested" (or a circularity tag) fits the stated facts better. The tool also has no side-by-side "Synchronism vs Z-polynomial" view, even though the page already has the null.

4. **Counts drift between pages.** Verified verbatim: /gamma-boundary says "Across **1,840** chemistry sessions…", while /chemistry-correlation-explorer says "1,703 phenomenon types across **2,671** chemistry sessions". The 1,703 figure matches but the denominator doesn't. Also on /tier-1, verbatim: TEST-03 data "ALFALFA + SDSS (**14,585** galaxies)" and "Session 593, N = **14,437**". Pass 2 saw 14,435 on /key-claims. That's three nearby N's for what reads as one cross-match, with no stated cut relating them.

5. **The TEST-03 "already-passing result."** /tier-1 says "the 51% TFR-residual/BTFR prediction above (Session 593, N = 14,437) is a separate, already-passing result." That's the only place I saw the word "passing" attached to a framework number, and it sits next to the headline "0 confirmed predictions." **Cosmology (/dark-energy).** The construction is H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C(ρ_m)). I checked the asymptotics by hand. At high density, C ≈ 1 − 2x^{−2γ}, so the extra term ∝ ρ^{1−2γ}, which is a Cardassian n = 1−2γ with early w = −2γ. At low density, C ≈ γx, so ρ/C → ρ_crit/γ, a Λ-like future. At γ = ½, 1/C = 1 + 2ρ_crit/ρ exactly, so the model is exactly ΛCDM. The "sign lock" sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) follows because w moves monotonically from −2γ toward −1. That means the family can never reach DESI DR2's w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0 quadrant. A fit of γ = 0.487, Δχ² = −0.3, and "+11.0 behind w₀wₐCDM ≈ 2.9σ for 2 dof" (p ≈ 0.004 → 2.87σ) are all self-consistent. The page cites Freese & Lewis 2002, Gondolo & Freese, and the Koivisto–Kurki-Suonio–Ravndal ISW constraint. It also clarifies that ρ_crit here is the coherence knee, not 3H²/8πG. That's a referee-grade page. The Bianchi-identity analysis on /honest-assessment (Completion A forces ρ/C ∝ a⁻³ → exact EdS; Completion B, a Brans-Dicke-type scalar, loses the Λ attractor) is the right check, and it's rare to see a framework run it against itself.
