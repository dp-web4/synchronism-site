# Topic: Audit the 6 Measurement Protocols Against the Calibration Gap

**Priority: MEDIUM**

## Question

Of the six "testable protocols" on /measurement-without-observers, how many are actually runnable —
i.e., can specify γ or N_corr for a real apparatus — and how many are *unrunnable as stated*, the same
verdict the consciousness-threshold claims already carry?

## Context

Visitor Pass 3 (2026-07-24, grad-student persona) caught a badge inconsistency: the page badges
"Untested — 6 Testable Protocols" while the framework-wide finding (C doubly unanchored; no calibration
procedure maps any apparatus to γ/N_corr) is exactly the gap that got the consciousness sector
re-badged "unrunnable as stated." The maintainer shipped an interim caveat and re-labeled the badge
"Runnability Under Audit" on 2026-07-24 — this topic is the audit that discharges it.

## Why It Matters

The unrunnable/untested distinction is one of the site's genuine epistemic contributions (the third
category beyond refuted/untested). Applying it inconsistently across sectors is precisely the kind of
drift the site diagnoses in its own archive. Whichever way each protocol falls, the badge becomes honest.

## Method

For each protocol: (1) what observable does it name? (2) what γ/N_corr value does the prediction
require? (3) is there any procedure — even in principle with current instruments — that assigns that
value to the apparatus independently of the outcome being predicted? If (3) fails, badge unrunnable.
Match the standard applied in the consciousness-sector audit for consistency.
