**Short answer:** I haven't searched, so this is from memory. I don't think the specific tanh·ln form of C(ρ) is published. The structure is not new, though, and the "emergence" claim is largely a definition.

**1. The structure is a known class (Cardassian expansion).**
Writing H² = (8πG/3)·ρ_m/C(ρ_m) means H² = (8πG/3)·g(ρ_m) with g = ρ_m/C. That is the Cardassian framework: Freese & Lewis, "Cardassian expansion: a model in which the universe is flat, matter dominated, and accelerating," Phys. Lett. B 540, 1 (2002), arXiv:astro-ph/0201229. It modifies the Friedmann equation with a function of ρ_m alone and has no Λ. Your C(ρ) just gives a particular g(ρ). The same one-function dependence on ρ_m appears in unified-dark-fluid models such as the generalized Chaplygin gas. Examples are Kamenshchik–Moschella–Pasquier, PLB 511, 265 (2001), and Bilić–Tupper–Viollier, PLB 535, 17 (2002). Both citations are from memory, so treat them as uncertain on page details.

**2. A density-dependent or running G is also not new.**
Examples are running-G and RG-improved cosmology (Bonanno–Reuter and others) and scalar-tensor theories with G(φ). I can't give a precise citation for a G(ρ_matter) model specifically (uncertain). The usual problem with these models is consistency. If G varies, the Bianchi identity and energy conservation constrain the model. You can't simply replace G by G/C(ρ) in the Friedmann equation and keep the other equations unchanged. The program needs to say what the second Friedmann equation (the acceleration equation) is and how matter is conserved.

**3. The "emergence" is tautological, and the calibration is not a test.**
- Defining ρ_DE ≡ ρ_eff − ρ_m = ρ_m(1−C)/C is a relabeling of the modified equation. It isn't a derivation of dark energy.
- Setting C₀ = Ω_m gives H₀² = (8πG/3)ρ_m0/Ω_m. That is the definition of Ω_m, so "reproduces H₀ exactly" is an identity and not a prediction.
- The model does have one nontrivial feature. As ρ → 0, C ≈ γρ/ρ_crit, so ρ_eff → ρ_crit/γ. That is an asymptotic de Sitter constant, which acts as an effective Λ set by ρ_crit and γ. So a cosmological constant is built in through the parameters and not eliminated.
- The real tests are w(z), the expansion history H(z), and the growth of structure. With a single ρ_m-dependent function, the model has to fit SN Ia, BAO, and CMB. Cardassian-type models were already strongly constrained, and they generally do worse than ΛCDM at matching the CMB and BAO.
- Local Solar System and lab tests are also an issue. G_eff = G/C is larger than G at low density, which would be a problem in underdense environments unless it is screened.

**Verdict:** The construction is a special case of a Cardassian-type or generalized unified-dark-fluid model with a particular g(ρ). The tanh(γ ln(1+ρ/ρ_crit)) form is, as far as I know, unpublished. Doing a targeted search for it would be sensible before claiming novelty.
