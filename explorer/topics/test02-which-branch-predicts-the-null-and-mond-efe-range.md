# Topic: TEST-02 wide binaries — which model branch makes the 0.05–0.4% prediction, and what is MOND+EFE's honest range?

## Question
Two things the TEST-02 row now asserts (added 2026-09-06 from a visitor's reading) need verification against
primary sources rather than site memory:

1. **Branch.** The 0.05–0.4% Newtonian-null prediction is attributed to the density-keyed C(ρ/ρ_crit) branch
   (knee above stellar-neighbourhood density ⇒ C = γx ⇒ no boost). Compute it: at a wide-binary separation
   of ~5–20 kAU in the solar neighbourhood (ρ ~ 0.1 M☉/pc³), what is x = ρ/ρ_crit under the framework's own
   ρ_crit = A·V², and what deviation does C = γx actually give? Is 0.05–0.4% reproducible, and from which γ?
   Then: what does the acceleration-keyed branch (μ_simple(x/2) at γ = ½) predict for the same systems, with
   and without the EFE?
2. **MOND+EFE range.** The row's kill criterion quotes "MOND ~18% velocity deviation; Chae-type ~1.4× boost."
   With the Milky Way external field g_ext ≈ 1.8 a₀, what do AQUAL and QUMOND actually predict for the
   wide-binary boost in g (and in velocity) under the treatments in Chae 2023/2024/2025 and Banik et al.
   2024? Is "1.0–1.4× in g" the right bracket, and which end does each paper sit at?

## Context
Researcher visitor persona, 2026-09-06 (HIGH): "TEST-02 is the only entry where the framework predicts
something different from both MOND and Newton… But which model? The 0.05–0.4% is the density-keyed form.
The acceleration-keyed form that fits SPARC is μ_simple(x/2) and predicts the full MOND boost, modulo EFE.
Also, 'MOND ~18%' ignores the EFE… Stating MOND as a clean 18% flattens a live controversy." The maintainer
added a branch statement and the 1.0–1.4× bracket to the row from the persona's numbers; those numbers are
now on the site **unverified** and this topic exists to verify or correct them.

The archive's three-C proposal (`argument_of_C_three_functions_ledger_not_commensurable_20260824.md`)
already says "what does the framework predict for X has no single answer." TEST-02 is the cleanest
instance: the branch that makes the discriminating prediction is the branch SPARC refutes.

## Why It Matters
If the density-keyed null is the prediction, TEST-02 is moot (that branch is dead on SPARC at ΔBIC +2843).
If the acceleration-keyed branch is meant, TEST-02 is MOND's own wide-binary test and was never a
discriminator. Either way the row's "self-eliminating-or-tie" verdict survives, but the *reason* changes,
and the MOND comparison column should carry a range with the dispute named, not a point.
