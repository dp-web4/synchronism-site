## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (plus 3 dead links tried)
- **Understanding achieved**: minimal
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The hook is genuinely intriguing and the honesty about failures is refreshing, but every time I try to grasp "what is this actually saying," I hit a wall of unexplained Greek letters.

### Journey

I clicked in from a Reddit comment. The landing page hit me with **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** — that's the kind of question that makes me stop scrolling. I liked the upfront framing of "Some predictions work. Some fail. All are documented honestly." That earned my trust before the math did anything to lose it. The stat-counters (3,308 sessions, 14,760 galaxies, 1,703 chemical phenomena) made it feel like real work, not a manifesto.

I clicked **"Start Exploring"** which took me to `/first-encounter` — Step 1 of 7. The page is teaching me the word "presence" (ρ). The electron-in-vacuum vs neutron-star-core analogy was actually great. But then in parentheses it casually drops **"Markov Relevancy Horizon"** with no definition, and I'm just blinking at it. That's a phrase I'd need a glossary for, and it's on Step 1.

I jumped to **"Key Claims"** because I wanted to know what this thing actually claims. The dark matter pitch — "what we call dark matter might be leftover quantum fuzziness, not missing particles" — is the kind of sentence that gets a casual reader excited. I have no idea if it's true, but it's the first thing on the site that made me think "I want to tell my friend about this." The consciousness formula `C = f(γ, D, S) ≥ 0.50` lost me immediately. I don't know what any of those letters mean and the page didn't tell me on the spot.

I went to **"See What Failed"** out of curiosity. This was the best page on the site for me. A theory openly admitting its melting point predictions are 53% off and its superconductor predictions are 6.5× wrong is unusual. There's even a confessed transcription error ("n ≈ 2.2 has no archive source — it was a site→archive transcription error"). That's how you build trust with a skeptical reader. I came away thinking "these people are doing science, not selling a worldview."

Then I tried to find the friendly tour. I clicked **"Get Started"** in the nav and it just sent me back to `/first-encounter`. I tried `/why-this`, `/start`, `/tools`, `/calculator`, `/coherence-calculator` — all 404. (Eventually I found `/interactive-tools` and `/why-synchronism`, but only by going back to the landing and clicking specific in-body links.) For a casual reader, the navigation breaks the moment you guess a URL or share a link with someone.

I clicked **"Why Synchronism"** (Step 1 of the six-step pathway). It opens with "Physics has a fragmentation problem." That's a real headline — I've heard about quantum mechanics and relativity not getting along. But the page didn't tell me why I should care beyond "elegance." No relatable analogy, no "this would change X." It's written for someone who already wants unification, not for me.

I tried **"Core Idea"** hoping it would finally click. The headline "One function. Three parameters. Every scale from Planck to cosmic" was great. Then the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` showed up and I'm done. **I don't know what tanh is.** I know it's something. I do not know what it is. Below that, the explanation says "Presence: compatible structural elements within a system's relevancy boundary" — which is jargon explaining jargon. The page does helpfully offer an escape hatch ("Need an analogy first?"), which is honest, but I'm now three pages deep in "is this for me?"

The **Glossary** had 30+ terms. The first ones (Coherence Function, Gamma Parameter, MRH, Compression Action) were exactly the things I'd been bouncing off, defined in roughly the same vocabulary I'd already failed to understand. I noted the *acronym* A2ACW (AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop) — which is interesting in itself: AIs are stress-testing this thing.

I went to **Interactive Tools** and tried two.

The **Coherence Explorer** was fun for about 30 seconds. Two sliders (γ and ρ_crit), an S-curve that bends when I drag. The hint "At γ ≈ 1, the transition from quantum to classical is steepest" gave me one concrete thing to verify. But there's no "you are here" marker — what real-world system corresponds to where I am on the curve right now? Without that, it's a math toy.

The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the highlight of the whole visit. Pick a galaxy (DDO 154, NGC 2403, etc.), see three lines: dashed = what visible matter predicts, blue dots = what we actually see, violet = Synchronism's fit. The gap between dashed and dots is the "wow" moment — you can literally *see* the dark matter problem without anyone explaining it. And the violet curve hugging the dots makes the pitch ("we explain this without dark matter particles") visceral. **This is what every page on this site should aspire to be.** A casual reader gets the problem and the proposed answer in one image.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: yes (to audit again after fixes)
- **Overall impression**: Content is unusually disciplined for an indie research site, but the site has *two competing organizational systems* (top-nav 7-step pathway vs. /learning-paths three-track structure) and a validation badge taxonomy that is rigorous on the policy pages and silently violated on the technical pages.

### Journey

I went through the site methodically, header-link by header-link, then dove into supporting pages. My focus: are terms defined on first use, are validation badges used consistently, do navigation structures agree with each other, and do the interactive tools deliver what their labels promise.

**Top-level navigation** (six items: *Get Started, Tier 1 Tests, Predictions, Tools, Glossary, Honest Assessment*) is clean and clearly labeled. Good. But "Get Started" sends me to `/first-encounter` — which is **Step 1 of 7**. Meanwhile, `/learning-paths` exists as a separate page with **three tracks** (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced), each with its own 6–8 step sequence — and the **Beginner track does not start with `/first-encounter`**. It starts with `/why-synchronism`. So the site has two overlapping, non-aligned learning structures. As a tech writer, this is the single most fixable structural issue I saw: pick one canonical path, or make the relationship between them explicit (e.g., "First Encounter = the 10-minute warm-up; Learning Paths = the deeper journey").

**Validation badges** are the site's flagship structural commitment, and `/research-philosophy` and `/honest-assessment` define the six-badge taxonomy carefully (Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed). But `/parameter-derivations` introduces non-taxonomy labels — *Motivated Ansatz*, *Motivated Choice*, *5% Agreement*, *12% Error*, *3% Error* — used in exactly the same visual position as a badge would be. A reader trained by the policy pages will read these as official badges and be confused why "Motivated Ansatz" isn't in the glossary. Either promote them to first-class badges with definitions, or restyle them so they're visibly *not* badges (e.g., italicized inline annotations).

**Acronym hygiene** is mixed. The glossary expands MRH, MOND, ΛCDM, SPARC, RAR, CRT correctly. But across the body pages, I logged at least eight acronyms used without first-use expansion *on the page where they first appear to a reader following a normal path*: **MOND** (used on `/why-synchronism` and `/honest-assessment` without inline expansion — readers learn it only if they detour to the glossary), **SPARC, ALFALFA-SDSS, BTFR, BAO, DESI, Gaia DR3, GW-DM, PDG, CFD**. The site's own convention (per CLAUDE.md / glossary header) is "Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline" — which suggests the *intended* solution is term-highlighting + tooltips. If that system exists, several pages aren't using it; if it doesn't fully exist yet, it should.

**The acronym `A2ACW`** is special — it's central to the site's epistemics (the AI-to-AI adversarial protocol generates content) — but its expansion is inconsistent across pages. The glossary lists it as **"A2ACWAI"** (an unusual concatenation), `/research-philosophy` expands it as **"AI-to-AI Collaborative Whiteboarding"**, and a casual reader (Pass 1) read the same site and noted **"AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop"**. *At least two of these three are wrong, possibly all three.* For an acronym this load-bearing, pick one canonical expansion and propagate it everywhere.

**Math notation** is the casual-reader killer that Pass 1 already flagged, but I'll flag the structural version: the central equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` appears on `/core-idea`, `/equation-walkthrough`, `/parameter-derivations`, and others, and **`tanh` is never defined as "hyperbolic tangent"** and **`log` is never specified as natural-log vs. log-base-10**. Even a tech writer who knows these functions would flag this as a definition gap — and the glossary does have a `tanh` entry, but no page that uses the symbol links to it.

**Glossary structure**: 26 terms, alphabetical sort. The sort is broken for non-Latin characters — `γ`, `ρ_crit`, `η`, `ξ` are sorted by their *symbol* rather than by their *name*, which means a reader looking for "gamma" won't find it under G. Add either a "G — Gamma (γ)" alphabetical anchor or a Greek-letter sub-section. There's no search and no category grouping (physics terms vs. chemistry terms vs. epistemics terms vs. badges). At 26 terms this is tolerable; if the glossary grows past 40 it will become unusable.

**Information architecture inconsistencies**:
- `/first-encounter` shows "Step 1 of 7" — clear progress indicator. ✓
- `/why-synchronism` (allegedly Step 1 of 6 in the Beginner path) shows a breadcrumb (Home > Getting Started > Why Synchronism?) but **no step counter**. The same site uses two different progress-tracking conventions on its two entry-point pages.
- `/equation-walkthrough` shows "Step 1 of 6" but is **labeled "interactive"** while presenting as a static stepper with Previous/Next buttons. There may be expandable sections I missed via WebFetch, but if the only interactivity is paging, the label oversells it.

**Tier-vs-Top-5 overlap**: `/test-catalog` lists 24 experiments across 4 tiers; the Beginner-Intermediate-Advanced learning paths reference both "Top 5 Decisive Tests" and "Test Catalog (24 experiments)" without explaining whether the Top 5 is a curated subset of the 24, a separate hand-picked list, or something else. One sentence ("The Top 5 are the highest-discrimination subset of the 24 in Tier 1–2") would dissolve the ambiguity.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — minimally informative — Takes one input (N_corr, dimensionless) and outputs γ to four decimal places plus a regime label. The formula `γ = 2/√N_corr` lives in the footer rather than next to the calculator. No error-state guidance for invalid input. As an information-architecture artifact, it's also a perfect microcosm of the site's biggest gap: a tool that assumes you already know what N_corr means (the inline label "number of correlated particles" is the only definition; what *counts* as a correlated particle for, e.g., a chemical bond vs. a galaxy, is left to the reader).
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — moderately intuitive — Slider over γ from 0.5 to 3.5 with three labeled regimes. Real-world systems are labeled in the boundary region (liquid water, enzymes, chemical bonds, neural firing, galaxies at γ=2), which is exactly the "anchor the curve in real things" affordance Pass 1 begged for on the Coherence Explorer. The deeper-quantum and deeper-classical regions are under-labeled though; a reader can see "this slider does something" but not "and here is where my coffee, an electron, the moon would sit."
- **Equation Walkthrough** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — partially worked — misleading label — Appears in `/interactive-tools` as an interactive derivation. In practice it's a 6-step Previous/Next pager. The content is good (the derivation chain is real) but the *interaction* is just paging through static panels. Either add real interactivity or move it out of "Tools" into "Reading" and rename it "Derivation Walk-Through."

### Verdict

The site has the rare combination of strong epistemic discipline and weak structural propagation: the validation badge taxonomy is rigorously defined on the policy pages but is silently violated on the most technical pages, and acronym hygiene is good in the glossary but lapses on the body pages where readers actually first encounter the terms. Most of the friction I logged is not a content problem — the content is admirable — but a *consistency* problem, which is the cheapest class of fix to make. If the site's two competing learning structures were reconciled, the badge taxonomy were enforced on `/parameter-derivations`, and the promised hover-tooltips were actually implemented, the site would jump from "honest but uneven" to "honest and navigable" without changing a word of theory.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the framework as written; unconvinced of its physics)
- **Would return?**: yes (to watch the predictions, especially BAO 10⁻⁴ and wide-binary slope, get tested)
- **Overall impression**: An unusually self-aware framework whose honest pages quietly demolish the central claim its landing page makes — "one equation describes reality from quantum to cosmic" survives only because the equation is a four-constraint sigmoid that any monotone bounded transition would satisfy, and the galaxy "predictions" are admitted on `/parameter-derivations` to be calibrated through Vflat.

### Journey

I went straight to `/core-idea` to see the central equation, then to `/coherence-function` and `/gamma-parameter` for the derivations, then to `/parameter-derivations`, `/honest-assessment`, `/research-philosophy`, `/key-claims`, `/predictions`, `/test-catalog`, `/top-5-tests`, and tested `/coherence-explorer`, `/galaxy-plotter`, `/gamma-calculator`. I was not in a hurry to be impressed.

**The central equation** is `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))`. The site is honest about what this is: `/coherence-function` states *"Synchronism's C(ρ) is not the Ising equation … the tanh shape is motivated by mean-field theory, not derived from it."* That's the right thing to say. But the *site's marketing* — "one function. three parameters. every scale" — and the casual invocation of *m = tanh(βJzm)* on `/core-idea` will lead non-experts to read this as a derivation. The Ising self-consistency equation has *m* on both sides; this equation does not. The analogy is structural, not mathematical.

The four-constraint motivation (bounded [0,1], monotonic, smooth saturation, well-behaved at ρ→0,∞) is satisfied by *every* sigmoid I can name: logistic, erf, arctan, even ρ/(ρ+ρ_crit) (Michaelis–Menten). Tanh is a *choice* from a one-parameter family of indistinguishable shapes, and at small ρ, `log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1) ≈ ρ/ρ_crit`, so the function reduces to `tanh(γρ/ρ_crit)` — a textbook sigmoid in ρ. The "+1" inside the log is acknowledged as a regularization, not derived. The combination of log-then-tanh has no special privilege; it's two saturating functions stacked.

**γ = 2/√N_corr** is the load-bearing relation. `/gamma-parameter` justifies the √N_corr by CLT and the factor of 2 by "phase-space dimensionality (3 position + 3 momentum) yields a factor of 2 through contraction to effective DOF." A real CLT-style prefactor depends on the variance of whatever statistic you're tracking — there's no general theorem that gives "2." `/parameter-derivations` correctly badges this as **Motivated Ansatz**. Good. But: `/key-claims` and the Coherence Explorer treat γ thresholds (γ>1.4 quantum, γ≈1 chemistry/consciousness, γ<0.6 classical) as physically meaningful, which they only are if N_corr is operationally well-defined. **N_corr is the framework's biggest unmarked load-bearing parameter**, and no page tells me how to compute it for a galaxy, a brain, or a superconductor. The γ Calculator presets give "water = 4, enzyme = 50, ferromagnet = 10²³" without any methodology. For galaxies, the Galaxy Plotter sets γ = 2 ("uncorrelated stars," i.e. N_corr=1) — but if every star is uncorrelated, what is generating the coherence field that fits the rotation curve? The framework is using γ in incompatible senses across pages.

**The MOND issue.** This is the moment it crystallized. `/parameter-derivations` itself admits:

> "The 3–12% errors could reflect either (a) legitimate approximation limits in the dimensional analysis, or (b) **implicit calibration through choice of Vflat as input**. Distinguishing these requires independent derivation. **The landing page describes the scaling constants as 'fitted'** — that may be the more conservative and accurate framing."

That is the right concession. The galaxy plotter takes Vflat as input (47 km/s shown for DDO 154), produces a one-shape-parameter fit, and recovers a curve that matches SPARC. This is not a prediction; it's *MOND in different notation, calibrated through MOND's own fitted Vflat*, which is exactly what the **Reparametrization** badge is supposed to flag. And `a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²` is correctly badged "Reparametrization, 10%" — Milgrom himself noted this coincidence in 1983; expressing it via `c` and `H₀` doesn't add physics, only suggestiveness. `Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) ≈ 110 M☉/pc²` is Freeman's law re-expressed. Together: the galactic part of the framework is, by its own admission on the derivation page, MOND with cosmological-rescaling notation. **The honest pages contradict the landing page.**

**The honest assessment is the most credible page.** "0 confirmed predictions" after 3,302 sessions + 13 adversarial stress tests is a remarkable thing for a self-published framework to put at the top. The failure list — superconductor Tc 6.5× wrong (predicted YBCO at 607K, observed 93K), melting points 53% off, fractal coherence bridge 0/7 boundaries predicted, **dark-matter viscosity predicted with the wrong sign vs. Bullet Cluster** — is the kind of falsification log most TOE sites refuse to keep. The Bullet Cluster sign error is genuinely fatal to the "residual coherence as dark matter" picture in its current form, and the site keeps it on the page rather than burying it. That is real epistemic discipline. The badge taxonomy on `/research-philosophy` is well-defined; the issue is enforcement, not policy.

**What's actually new?** I went to `/top-5-tests` looking for at least one prediction that isn't either MOND or undefined. The five:

1. **BAO ~10⁻⁴ shift (density-dependent peak position).** No error bars. DESI Year-3 BAO precision on r_drag is around ~0.5%; a 10⁻⁴ density-modulation amplitude would only be detectable in cross-correlation with environment density and is not obviously distinguishable from EFT-of-LSS density-dependent bias absorbing the same shift. Without a sharp prediction (slope vs. density, scale-dependence) this is qualitative.
2. **Wide-binary density dependence.** "MOND density-independent, Synchronism density-dependent." Qualitative; no slope, no normalization. The Gaia DR3 wide-binary debate is currently model-discriminating *between* MOND variants (e.g., AQUAL vs. QUMOND with EFE on/off), so "density-dependent anomaly" without a numerical slope is consistent with multiple MOND variants and consistent with no-anomaly Newtonian fits with hidden binaries. Not currently a discriminator.
3. **EEG anesthesia phase transition (γ shift).** No γ value, no threshold value, no operational definition of N_corr for cortical networks. Untestable as currently specified.
4. **GW–DM correlation, |α| < 3×10⁻¹⁵ (from GW170817).** This is a *bound*, not a prediction — Synchronism is consistent with GR's prediction of zero, which means GW170817 already constrained the framework to be GR-equivalent at this level. As a discriminator, it discriminates only if a Synchronism-specific signal is predicted that GR isn't.
5. **Cosmic interference (~500 Mpc scale).** The site itself says "not yet a decisive test … not a prediction in the scientific sense due to pending derivation." Honest, but it shouldn't be in the Top 5 if it isn't a prediction.

Synchronism renames the environment-dimensionality parameter to N_corr and the inverse-coherence parameter to γ, but the predictive content is the same: macroscopic systems decohere fast, microscopic ones do not. **The framework does not derive the Born rule, does not predict any deviation from unitary QM, and does not give a decoherence timescale formula** — the page itself says *"scaling relationships... none have been run."* Calling this "dissolution of the measurement problem" overclaims: einselection already gives a *specific outcome selection* via the predictability sieve (commuting with the system-environment Hamiltonian).
