## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /dark-matter, /glossary, /honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: An unusually honest "I tried a big idea and it mostly failed" notebook. The first screen and the Galaxy Plotter work well for someone like me. Almost everything past the first scroll of any page is written for physicists.

### Journey
I came from a Reddit link. The headline, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, is exactly the Veritasium-style hook that gets me to click. The subtitle, "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything", lowered my guard in a good way. Within a paragraph I learned the idea "did not hold as stated", that it's run by AIs with one human overseer, and that no outside physicist has reviewed it. Then came "So is it alive or dead?", answered with "as tested physics it is dead so far". My honest reaction: refreshing, but slightly deflating. Why read on? The line that the honest map of failures "is the product" is the answer, but I had to think about it. The "AI-run research" angle is actually the most interesting hook for me, and the page barely explains it ("3,300 AI-to-AI sessions", "A2ACW" — what's that?).

Scrolling down the landing page, it falls off a cliff. "Scientific Outcomes" shows a table with TEST-09, TEST-25, a "Cassini/SPARC squeeze", "Bell/CHSH (bet B1)", a "withdrawn BAO item", and "preferred-frame bound of 15–27 orders of magnitude". There's also a "Methodology note" saying the audit instrument "false-flagged all six canonical discoveries it was tested on". I understood none of it. The "No physics background?" box near the top saved me by pointing me at the Beginner Path, so I took that.

**Step 1, Why Synchronism?** "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a good opener. The "What happened, in three lines" bullets are the clearest thing on the site: one equation for how crowding makes things act as one, tested mostly on galaxies, lost where it could be told apart, the bigger question is still open. The "Beginner path: you can stop here" signpost is great. I stopped there and moved on, as invited. The crowd-vs-marching-band picture stuck with me. The homepage said "~8 min", but the page itself shows no reading time.

**Step 2, First Encounter** ("10-minute guided introduction"). The one-section-at-a-time Continue button is nice. Section B finally told me what tanh is ("an S-shaped dimmer switch") and that "ln squashes big ranges". That was the first time an equation felt friendly rather than scary. Then Section C says the original idea for γ failed with a sign flip (+0.55 predicted, −0.55 measured), and the γ that works "reproduces MOND, so it adds nothing new". Section E gives "ρ_crit = A × V_flat²" and "a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π)", and I lost the thread. Section F (quantum measurement as "crossing the MRH", "quantum Zeno effect") was beyond me. Section G (melting points 53% off, a superconductor predicted at 607 K vs 93 K real) was clear and weirdly satisfying: concrete numbers, concrete failure. It took me longer than 10 minutes, mostly because I kept re-reading C–F.

**Step 3, Galaxy Curve Plotter.** This was the best page of my visit. "New here? Look at three things on the chart below": the dots (what telescopes see), the gray dashed line (visible matter only), and the green line (MOND). It tells me to ignore the rest. The gap between the dots and the gray line "is the dark-matter puzzle". That is the first time I've ever understood what a rotation curve is for. The violet line is labelled "This framework's equation (tested reading, failed)" and the text says it sags at the edges where the dots stay flat. I could actually *see* why the idea failed. "Just because a curve fits is not a discovery" and "the same curve wearing a different costume" are lines I'll remember.

**Interactive Tools index.** It says "Best first tool: Coherence Explorer" and "Where to start: Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment". Clear enough.

**Coherence Explorer.** The "One thing to try first" instruction (drag γ up to 2, then back to 0.5, and watch the S flatten) is exactly the guidance I need. But there's a "Reparametrization" banner at the top, and the page mentions ΔBIC, Cassini, "marginalized", "off-diagonal long-range order", ΛCDM, RAR, "compander" and "dex". It's labelled the beginner's first tool, but after the first two boxes it reads like a referee report.

**γ Calculator.** The H1 is "a γ formula kept to show how it fails". Honest, and a bit funny. The lesson (click "Ideal gas", then "BCS superconductor", and notice the more collective one gets the *flatter* curve) is a neat little demonstration of a backwards prediction, even if I don't know what BCS is.

**Dark Matter Reframed.** I came here for the question every Reddit thread asks: is dark matter real? The page says the framework has "two stories" that are "not compatible". In one, dark matter is renamed. In the other, there's no unseen mass and gravity is boosted where matter is sparse, which failed on galaxies. "Which one the framework keeps is an open decision." I left without a straight answer. In the spirit of "questions first", that's arguably fine, but it felt like the site hadn't made up its mind rather than like an open question I could chew on.

**Glossary.** "New here? Start with these ten" is great. But "128 terms" is intimidating, and the Dark matter definition mentions "ΛCDM" without saying what it is.

**Honest Assessment** (Beginner Path step 5). The "If you read nothing else" box is good and short. "Untested means nobody has looked; Failed means someone looked" is the clearest sentence on the site. The page says it is about 30,000 words, and the box itself still uses σ, "Cassini squeeze", "M/L" and "gas-dominated". I stopped at the box, as invited.

### Tools Tested
*Method note: browsing was through WebFetch, which returns page text and default state; I couldn't physically drag sliders. Readouts below are the defaults shown in the page text.*

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked (DDO 154 default; 5 galaxy buttons, a star-mass slider Υ_disk = 0.50, two optional overlay checkboxes); **intuitive**. The "look at three things" guidance is the best beginner instruction on the site. Seeing the violet curve sag below the dots made "it failed" concrete rather than just a claim.
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (γ = 0.49, ρ_crit = 1.00; readouts C(ρ_crit) = 0.3272, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.8259, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.9785; presets 0.046 / 0.49 / ½ / 2); **intuitive at the top, confusing below**. "Drag γ to 2 and back" is a good first action, but the presets are numbers, not things. I expected "galaxy / crystal / gas" buttons, and the page explains that the presets are γ values the site discusses rather than physical systems.
- **γ Calculator**: worked (N_corr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, C(ρ_crit) = 0.60; seven presets from Ideal gas to BCS superconductor); **intuitive as a lesson**. "Click Ideal gas, then BCS — notice the more collective one gets the flatter curve" shows a backwards prediction in two clicks. It's odd to have a calculator for a formula the page says is wrong, but the H1 is upfront about that.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the galaxy version of the equation is "just MOND in new notation", what is MOND, and is MOND itself right? The site defines it as a rival, but I don't know whether real physicists take it seriously.
2. Does the site think dark matter exists? I got "two incompatible stories, open decision".
3. What does it actually mean that the research is AI-run? Who decides what gets tested, and what is "A2ACW"?
4. What part of the idea is still alive in a way I could understand? The Honest Assessment box mentions "one open question about star clusters". What is it, in plain words?
5. Why is γ ≈ 1 called a "boundary" on one page and "nothing special" on another?
6. The homepage mentions Gaia DR4 on 2 December 2026. What will it test, and could it change the "dead so far" verdict?

### Verdict
As a casual reader I came away understanding more than I expected. I now know what a galaxy rotation curve is, why dark matter was proposed, and that this idea tried to replace it and failed in a way I could see on a chart. The honesty is the site's charm, and the Beginner Path's "you can stop here" signposts respect my time. But each beginner page has a short friendly top and then drops into physicist shorthand. The two tools billed for beginners (Coherence Explorer especially) need their technical tails folded away. The Galaxy Plotter shows how to do it.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /learning-paths, /interactive-tools, /equation-walkthrough, /crossover-regime-visualizer, /test-catalog, /tier-1-existing, /key-claims, /honest-assessment, /research-philosophy, /glossary, /mrh)

- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The navigation is unusually careful: track pages carry "Step N of M" and Prev/Next, and badge definitions are single-sourced. The weak spots are a breadcrumb hierarchy that doesn't match the nav, a status vocabulary that has grown to about 30 words, and acronym keys placed after the acronyms they define.

### Journey
I started on the landing page and mapped the IA. The top nav is flat, with 10 items and helpful tooltips ("26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)", "128 terms defined"). The footer repeats most of them and carries the scoreboard sentence: "0 confirmed predictions · 6 refutations executed under the current counting rule (4–8 under rules on file…)". That sentence is identical on every page I checked, and its by-kind breakdown (3 + 1 + 1 + 1) adds to 6. That's good discipline for a number that matters this much.

The landing page handles acronyms in two stages. First an "Abbreviations ahead" line *names* RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD and SPARC without expanding them. Then a full "Abbreviations used in the table below" key sits in Scientific Outcomes, well after most first uses. MOND, MRH and A2ACW are expanded inline at first use, which is the right pattern. But TEST-xx, S177, Gaia/DR4, M/L, Υ, B_max and a₀′ are used on the landing page and never expanded there. The badge explanation is good: "Badges used in the cards below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Failed" links to the canonical legend, and Reparametrization is defined inline. Speculative and Failed are reachable only through the link.

**Learning Paths** is the best-organized page on the site. It has seven paths, each with a step count and total time. The Beginner step times (8 + 10 + 6 + 9 + 4) add up exactly to the advertised "~37 min". One conflict: the intro calls the full Honest Assessment "the full 80-minute read", while step 5 says "about 30,000 words, about 2 hours". Also, the landing card labels Honest Assessment both "Shortcut" and "Beginner Path step 5 of 5". Labels on cross-listed pages are mostly consistent, except "Superconductivity (η)" (Advanced) vs "Superconductivity" (Chemistry) for the same URL.

I spot-checked track pages. /research-philosophy shows "Methodology Track — Step 1 of 6" with Next links. /mrh shows "Physics Track … Step 2 of 7" with Prev/Next at the top and bottom. /test-catalog shows "Advanced Path Step 8 of 8" with "← Prev: Falsifiability". /honest-assessment carries *two* indicators (Beginner 5/5 and Methodology 6/6), each marked "Final step". These wayfinding devices are well done.

**Breadcrumbs** are where the IA breaks down. They use section names that exist nowhere else:
- /mrh → Home / **Core Theory** / MRH
- /research-philosophy → Home / **Methodology** / Research Philosophy
- /test-catalog → Home / **Predictions & Experiments** / Test Roadmap
- /tier-1-existing → Home / **Predictions & Experiments** (linked to /test-catalog) / Tier 1
- /honest-assessment, /key-claims → Home / [page], with no section
- tools → Home / Interactive Tools / [tool]

None of "Core Theory", "Methodology" (as a section) or "Predictions & Experiments" appears in the top nav. As rendered, the middle level is plain text on /mrh and /test-catalog, so a reader can't click up a level. On /tier-1-existing the "Predictions & Experiments" level links to /test-catalog, so the section and the Test Roadmap page are the same thing under two names. Only the Interactive Tools breadcrumbs mirror a real nav item. (Also: in my fetched text each "/" separator appears as its own numbered list item. That may be a conversion artifact, but if the separators really are `<li>` elements, screen readers will read out "slash".)

**Badge vocabulary.** The canonical legend in /honest-assessment ("Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)") defines nine badges in two families. Five are MRH-relationship badges: Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative. Four are descriptive: Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed. Validated and Strongly Supported are marked retired. /research-philosophy now links each chip to the canonical legend rather than redefining it ("single-sourced 2026-08-08"), and /test-catalog's badge guide matches. Good. But /test-catalog shows how much vocabulary has grown on top of the nine. There are 7 *operational states* (Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Unrunnable as stated, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson) and 11 *lifecycle verbs* (Executed, Adjudicated, Closed, Retired, Reclassified, Never run as registered, Underpowered as registered, Ceiling Exceeded, Empty Intersection, Demoted — Exploratory, Closed by Execution), plus 3 retired labels. On /interactive-tools each card carries *four* classification axes: Kind ("Model Explainer"), Level, Status ("Artifact Lesson") and the page badge. Each axis gets a disclaimer that it isn't one of the others. A reader now needs a legend for the legend. One cross-page conflict: "MOND-shared" is listed among retired *badges* on /honest-assessment and among *operational states* on /research-philosophy.

Badges also carry free-text suffixes after an em-dash: "Speculative — Theoretical Framework", "Speculative — 6 experiment classes, none run; no rate parameter", "Audited-Negative — γ = 2/√N_corr — sharpness sign inverted". The landing page says these are "finding descriptors, not separate badges", which is fine. But the suffixes vary in grammar (category label / count / formula), and some contain a second em-dash, so the badge name is hard to separate from its gloss.

**Same thing, different names**, collected while reading:
- ρ_crit: "critical density" by name. Equation Anatomy calls it "a saturation knee, not a critical point (the 'crit' in the name is historical)". Key Claims calls it "coherence knee", "density knee" and "the knee". The spelling alternates between ρ_crit and ρcrit on the same page.
- γ: "regime parameter" (Key Claims claim 1) vs "coherence parameter" (claim 2). Equation Anatomy says it sets the slope, "not a sharpness". The Crossover tool says it was "first proposed as the sharpness… which it is not".
- The boost ratio: "B" / "B_max = 3.17" vs "γmax = 3.17" on Key Claims. The page flags the mislabel but leaves both in the text.
- N_corr vs Ncorr, mixed within paragraphs on the Crossover tool.
- Galaxy Curve Plotter (card title) vs Galaxy Plotter ("Where to start" line).
- Page title vs URL: Test Roadmap at /test-catalog, Equation Anatomy at /equation-walkthrough, "Decisive Tests (none currently running)" at /top-5-tests, Crossover Regime Visualizer (formerly Phase Boundary Visualizer, which the page explains).
- MRH: expanded consistently as "Markov Relevancy Horizon" (landing, glossary, /mrh H1). But /mrh itself says "The 'Markov' label sets an expectation that is not currently met" and floats renaming it "Relevance Horizon". The site's most central acronym is disowned on its own page.

**Internal count/status conflicts** (exact wording checked with a second fetch, since summaries can drop list items):
- /mrh: the mid-page badge reads "6 experiment classes, none run", and What's Untested says "Six experiment classes were sketched … but none have been run". The link directly below reads "See the 6 proposed tests (2 run, 4 never run)" → /quantum-predictions. Nothing on /mrh says these are different sets. I didn't open /quantum-predictions to reconcile them.
- /key-claims: "Session #581 (2026-02-08) audited 8 quantum claims and concluded: zero confirmed predictions, 4 reparametrizations, 1 refutation … 1 post-hoc fit". That accounts for 6 of the 8, and the other 2 are never mentioned.
- /key-claims: a 2026-10-06 revision note says "acronyms (… ALFALFA–SDSS …) are expanded at first use". ALFALFA–SDSS is used three times before its expansion ("Arecibo Legacy Fast ALFA HI survey × Sloan Digital Sky Survey") appears in a later subsection. The note describes the fix as made when it isn't.
- /tier-1-existing: "13 cards: the 10 original Tier 1 tests (TEST-01 to TEST-10; TEST-07 sits here but is not a Tier 1 test by its own heading)". So TEST-07 is counted among the ten Tier 1 tests while the same sentence says it isn't one, and /test-catalog places 01–10 in Tier 1 without that caveat.

**TEST numbering.** /test-catalog explains it well ("one flat namespace: TEST-01 … TEST-24", suffix `a` = amended, `s` = substituted). But that explanation is mid-page, below the protocol and pre-registration boxes. The landing page uses TEST-09/10/25 with no pointer to it. /glossary's "Bet B1" entry helpfully explains that Bet IDs (B1–B7) are a *separate* ledger from TEST-01–26. That's a second ID scheme a reader has to keep apart from the first.

**Inline revision notes.** Several pages (landing, Crossover tool, glossary entries such as a₀, Key Claims) carry dated "Revision note (2026-xx-xx)" paragraphs inside the reading text. They are honest and traceable, but they break the reading flow, and some describe wording the reader never saw. The /mrh and /honest-assessment pages would read more cleanly with these moved to a collapsed "Changes" block at the bottom of each page.

**/dark-energy.** I re-derived the sign lock. With ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C and C = tanh(γ ln(1+ρ/ρ_c)):
- At high density, 1−C ∝ ρ^(−2γ), so ρ_DE ∝ a^(−3(1−2γ)) and w → −2γ.
- At low density, ρ_DE → ρ_c/γ (constant), so w → −1.
- The evolution is monotone, so sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a): γ < ½ gives freezing (w₀ > −1, w_a > 0) and γ > ½ gives a phantom mirror.

The DESI DR2 quadrant (w₀ > −1, w_a < 0) is forbidden. ✓ γ = ½ gives ρ_DE = 2ρ_c exactly, i.e. Λ. ✓ The page's withdrawal of its own "3.4–6.3σ excluded" figure ("the fifth instance of an exclusion-flavoured number that did not survive execution") is the kind of note I wish more papers carried. **/mond-unification and a₀(z).** This turned out to be the most interesting physics on the site, for a reason the site underplays. Ciocan et al. 2026 (MUSE-DARK III) measure a₀(z ≈ 1) = 2.38, roughly double the local value, with a slope n = 0.71 ± 0.13 in a₀ ∝ E(z)ⁿ. Constant-a₀ MOND is out at ~5σ. ΛCDM+baryon simulations (Mayer et al. 2023) give n ≈ 1, and so does the framework's a₀ = cH(z)/2π. So the a₀(z) channel discriminates *against constant-a₀ MOND* and *for* {ΛCDM-emergent scale, a₀ ∝ H}. That isn't novel to Synchronism (cosmologically coupled a₀ is Milgrom's own prior art), but it is the one place the framework's commitments beat MOND's on data. Two referee problems with how it's quoted:
1. **Level vs slope.** "cH(z)/2π gives 1.86, ~9.8σ low" is driven mostly by the 13% z = 0 shortfall of cH₀/2π (1.04 vs 1.20). Anchored on 1.20, a₀ ∝ H gives 1.20 × E(1) = 1.20 × 1.79 = 2.15, not 1.86. The page itself says four z = 0 anchors "disagree by 69%". With an anchor spread that large, the level σ's (21σ for constant a₀, 9.8σ for the framework) are measurement-error-only and shouldn't be headlined. The slope is the statistic: n = 0.71 ± 0.13 vs 1 is (1 − 0.71)/0.13 = 2.2σ (the page says 2.1σ).
2. **Confidence levels.** The same paper's level errors are read as 95% CIs and its bin errors as 1σ ("the bars state no confidence level"). **CHSH (bet B1).** These are Kuramoto-lattice simulations at 400k trials per setting pair:
- Local construction: S = 1.98.
- Nonlocal-grid construction: S ≡ 2.00, no signalling.
- Global clock: S = 2.67, *with* signalling (far-marginal setting dependence 0.43).
- The framework's own Intent-density substrate: S = 1.85.

This is Bell's theorem verified on the framework's own substrate, and the page frames it correctly ("not a new result"). **A2ACW (/research-philosophy).** Is it sound for generating physics? On the site's own evidence, no. I respect how plainly the site reaches that answer:
- **Approval step uncalibrated.** PPV 0/9 on the audit. I verified the one-sided 95% upper bound: 1 − 0.05^(1/9) = 28%.
- **The specificity benchmark (0/6) is in-distribution.** It was written *and* scored by one model. The rule "detects 'has antecedents', which all non-trivial physics does".
- **Verdicts depend on the reviewer, not the claim.** Same-model re-runs agree with each other 86% of the time but with the cross-vendor human-relayed verdicts only 41–52%, and every disagreement was harsher.
- **The working error-correction was execution, not debate.** "Every demotion came from a later audit pass, not from the challenge cycle itself", and five of six refutations came from code run on external data.

That last point is the paper. "Adversarial LLM debate neither discovers nor corrects in physics; executed code against external data does" is a publishable, falsifiable methodological result, and the site has the ledger to support it.
