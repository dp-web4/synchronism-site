## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /gamma-calculator, /key-claims, /two-reframes)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Within 30 seconds I could tell this site isn't trying to sell me anything. "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." is the most honest opening I've seen on a big-idea physics site. But after the first screen of almost every page, I'm wading through acronyms.

### Journey
I came over from Reddit expecting a "theory of everything" crank site. The headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") looked like that. The subtitle, "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything", plus the line "It did not hold as stated", flipped my expectation right away. The scoreboard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations) is strange but compelling. Why build a site around an idea that failed? That question is what kept me clicking.

I followed the numbered Beginner Path. **Why Synchronism?** opens well with "Physics has a fragmentation problem." The crowd versus marching-band analogy landed. The three-line "what happened" box is great, and so is the explicit "you can stop here" point. Past that point the page becomes Langevin, Curie–Weiss, Tully–Fisher, YBCO and Bell/CHSH. I stopped, as the page told me I could.

**First Encounter** is the best page for me. Coherence is a marching band versus a plaza crowd. tanh is a "dimmer switch" (fine, I don't need to know more). MRH is the "bubble of neighbours that matter", and "an atom doesn't feel Andromeda" made it click. MOND and dark matter each got a one-line gloss, which I needed. It got harder in the γ section, where it reports a sign error (+0.55 predicted, −0.55 measured) without telling me what was being measured. It also lost me at "critical exponents" and "Tc" in the "where it fails" list. The page also says "a fitted γ ≈ 0.5 reproduces MOND by construction". I understood that as "if you tune it, it just copies the other guy", and I think that's the key point. It took me two re-reads, though.

The **Galaxy Plotter** gave me my "oh!" moment. The gray line (visible matter only) sags below the blue dots, and that gap *is* the dark matter problem. I've heard about it on PBS Space Time for years and never actually *seen* it. The violet line (this framework's equation) is just the gray line scaled up, so it still droops at the edge. The green MOND line follows the dots. The conclusion was clear: it failed and the rival works. The two checkboxes ("stand-in" curve, "second wiring that does nothing on this chart") confused me. Why offer a toggle that does nothing?

**Coherence Explorer**: I moved the sliders and watched an S-curve change shape. It was fun for about a minute. The page tells me up front the density axis "has no units" and it "does not model any real material". That's honest, but it left me unsure what I was learning. The presets are γ values (0.046, 0.49, ½, 2), not things. I was hoping for "water", "neutron star", "galaxy". The page points me to the γ Calculator for named systems.

**γ Calculator** does have named systems (ideal gas, water, galaxy, ferromagnet, superconductor). It opens with "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails". That made me laugh, and it is a hook. The key lesson is that superconductors, the *most* collective thing, get the *flattest* curve, which is backwards. Even I can see that's wrong. But "liquid water: γ = 1, Boundary" sat next to "galaxy: 17 particles moving together". A galaxy is 17 of something? I didn't get that.

**What Synchronism Is Not** has a perfect opening sentence. The 8 "is not" cards are readable in their bold first lines. The bodies are full of QFT, GR, CMB, EFE, A2ACW and "compander".

**Honest Assessment**: the page says it is ~18,000 words, and the "if you read nothing else" box is still dense for me. In one paragraph I hit "4 to 8 under counting rules not yet chosen", "at most 5 independent", "the γ = 2 pin (row #3) sits at the threshold once each galaxy counts once", and "a ceiling on how much extra gravity the formula can add" ("cap"). I came away with the top line (the one equation failed, and where it fits it's MOND in disguise). The "How fatal is this? As a theory of galaxies: fatal" line is the clearest sentence on the site. I'd put it at the very top of that box.

**Key Claims** has three claims and three badges ("Speculative", "Failed", "Reparametrization"). I didn't know what "Reparametrization" meant until I clicked through. The consciousness claim reuses the letters C and γ for something different from the physics equation, with a warning box saying so. That made me *more* confused. **Two Reframes** has nice analogies (CRT flicker, pendulum clock in a centrifuge), but it moves straight into Tsirelson bounds and Kochen–Specker.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked, intuitive. The best teaching moment on the site. Seeing the gray line sag under the dots made the dark matter problem concrete for me for the first time. The violet-line-is-just-gray-scaled-up conclusion was easy to follow. The extra checkboxes were confusing.
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked, somewhat confusing. The sliders respond and the S-curve is clear. But there are no units, no real systems, and no "what to notice" prompt, so I played with it without a takeaway. The note that the ρ_crit marker is *not* where C = 0.5 surprised me without explaining why it matters.
- **γ Calculator**: worked, intuitive at the surface level. The named presets were what I wanted. The "superconductors get the flattest curve, which is backwards" lesson landed. "Galaxy N_corr = 17" did not.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the main idea failed, what *is* still alive? The site says "the broader question remains open", but I couldn't find a one-sentence beginner answer to "so what's left to try?"
2. Who are the "AI agents" doing the research, and what does one human overseer actually do? This is mentioned on the landing page and is really interesting. I wanted a plain explanation.
3. What did "+0.55 predicted, −0.55 measured" refer to?
4. A galaxy is "17 particles moving together"?
5. Why does a dimmer-switch curve that copies MOND count as a *failure* rather than as "it got the right answer"? I think I get it (tuning a knob until it matches isn't predicting), but I'd like that said in one sentence on the plotter page.

### Verdict
I trusted this site more than any "one equation" site I've seen, because it tells me in the first line that its idea didn't work. The Galaxy Plotter alone was worth the click: I finally *saw* the dark matter problem. But the pages written for people like me (the Honest Assessment top box especially) still read like a lab notebook for insiders. The site's honesty would land harder if its most important beginner box were as plain as its homepage.

### Journey
I started at the landing page and mapped the top navigation: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Interactive Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment. Several pages are reachable only from the footer or from in-page links, including Key Claims and three Beginner Path steps (First Encounter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, What Synchronism Is Not). A first-timer finds them through "Start Here", which works, but the top bar alone doesn't show the main beginner route.

The landing page does acronyms well. An "Abbreviations ahead" line near the top links to a key (`#terms-on-this-page`, which exists; I checked the page source), and MRH ("Markov Relevancy Horizon") and A2ACW ("AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop") are expanded at first use. The expansions agree across the landing page, /glossary and /a2acw. The one variant is the For Researchers "Related Concepts" link, "A2ACW Protocol — AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration", which drops "Workshop". The badge legend above the claim cards is a good pattern: "Reparametrization · Speculative · Failed (the three that appear on the cards below; the full set of nine is at the link)". It also explains that text after a dash inside a badge is a descriptor, not a separate badge.

The **Glossary** is large (it says "118 terms") and covers everything I tested: MRH, A2ACW, EFE, compander, all five MRH-relationship badges, all four descriptive badges, and the retired ones (Validated, Strongly Supported). The badge system *is* documented. The problem is reaching it in context, see below.

**Learning Paths** is clean: seven paths, step counts, time estimates, and step labels that match the in-page "Step N of M" pills (Honest Assessment shows both "Beginner Path Step 5 of 5" and "Methodology Track Step 6 of 6", which is correct). Small slips: Intermediate step 9 is titled "Decisive Tests: Status (none active)" but lives at `/top-5-tests`, and the Advanced path says "Superconductivity (η)" while the Chemistry path says "Superconductivity" for the same URL.

**Breadcrumbs.** They work and use real links, but the middle segment names categories that don't exist anywhere in the navigation:
- Tier 1 → "Predictions & Experiments", which links to `/test-catalog`, a page titled "Test Roadmap"
- For Researchers → "Methodology", which links to `/research-philosophy`, a page titled "Research Philosophy"
- /phase-transitions → "Core Theory", which is plain text with no link
- Glossary, Learning Paths and Honest Assessment have no middle segment at all
- Tools pages → "Interactive Tools", which is consistent

So breadcrumbs reveal a hidden four-section information architecture (Core Theory / Predictions & Experiments / Methodology / Tools) that the top bar never shows.

**Status vocabulary** is the biggest structural issue for a documentation reader. A Tier 1 test card carries:
1. a *badge*, from one of two families (MRH-relationship or descriptive), with a free-text descriptor after a dash;
2. an *operational state* (Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Never Run as Registered, Reclassified);
3. sometimes a *lifecycle verb* (Underpowered as Registered, Demoted — Exploratory).

The Test Roadmap distinguishes these explicitly and says its lists are "complete", which I respect. But it then *names* operational states without defining them on the page ("see Glossary"). The concrete result is a contradiction on /tier-1-existing. The page-level badge says "1 more (TEST-04a) is not Failed: it is underpowered as registered … and not counted as a refutation." The TEST-04a card's own badge reads **"Failed — on σ₈ only (GR-conditioned); registered criterion not met; not counted"**. The descriptor walks it back, but the badge word a skimmer sees is "Failed". The Test Roadmap shows no badge for TEST-04a, only "Underpowered as Registered (not Failed…)". A revision note on the same page (2026-10-19) says a technical-writer pass already caught "badge put TEST-04a under Failed while Test Roadmap says it is not". The page-level badge was fixed, but the card badge still uses the word.

**The rename that stopped halfway.** The Crossover Regime Visualizer says it was "Formerly 'Phase Boundary Visualizer'; the URL carried that name until 2026-10-23", when a persona noted that the slug "still asserted the retracted claim in every link". The old URL now 308-redirects correctly, which is nice. But the same defect sits one link away: `/phase-transitions` serves an H1 of **"γ Regimes: Crossovers, *Not* Phase Transitions"**, and that page's first body sentence is *"The coherence function predicts three distinct regimes, with transitions between them. The most interesting physics happens at the boundaries — especially at γ ≈ 1."* This sits directly under a Reparametrization badge saying the γ ≈ 1 clustering "restates the Debye θ_D (ledger Bucket 3)". So the slug, H1, badge and lead sentence say four different things. "Ledger Bucket 3" is not a glossary term; it appears only inside another glossary entry.

**For Researchers** defers nearly all acronym definitions to a footer list. In its opening paragraphs I met ΛCDM, ΔBIC, SPARC, L2, L3, RG and CHSH before any expansion. It uses Audited-Negative / Failed / Untested badges and has no link to the badge key, unlike the landing page. For an audience page this is acceptable (researchers know ΛCDM), but L2/L3 and "density-keyed" are site-internal coinages, and they appear in the first screen.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /galaxy-rotation, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-plotter, /dark-energy, /honest-assessment, /phase-transitions, /measurement-without-observers, /tier-1-existing)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The badges are more honest than most published papers I've refereed for journal club. No "Validated" survives anywhere I looked, and "the Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen" is exactly right. The remaining defects are *internal* inconsistencies between pages: two galaxy-level γ intervals, a "critical exponents 2× off" badge on a function the derivations page says has no critical exponents, and a ΔBIC discount whose method isn't shown.

### Journey
I went straight to **/parameter-derivations** expecting the usual hand-waved mean-field story. Instead the page concedes everything I was going to object to. tanh is "a phenomenological choice from the sigmoid/compander family". The Ising m = tanh(βJzm) is noted, and then: "C(ρ) has no such self-consistency loop". It says "no critical point, no diverging correlation length, and no critical exponents". γ = 2/√N_corr borrows CLT scaling for *correlated* degrees of freedom, and the page flags that inconsistency itself. A = 4π/(β_J²GR₀²) evaluates to 4.6×10⁻⁵ against the 0.029 used, ~600× off. a₀ = cH₀/2π is "posited by dimensional analysis", and "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." As a condensed-matter person I have nothing to add, which is the point. The ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1) argument is given no justification at all, though. The page states its closed form and analyticity for x > −1 and moves on. It's the one modelling choice left completely unmotivated.

I checked the key identity by hand. tanh(½ ln(1+x)) = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), so at γ = ½ the acceleration-keyed compander *is* MOND's simple μ with a₀ = 2a₀′. Correct. That makes the "galaxy fit γ = 0.489" a MOND fit and nothing more. **/galaxy-rotation** says so, and it identifies a₀′/γ (≈1.07–1.09×10⁻¹⁰) as the only constrained combination. Also correct, since a₀′/γ = 2a₀′ = a₀ at γ = ½. Then I checked the deep-MOND line: "at g_bar/a₀ = 0.01 the μ-form gives g_obs/a₀ = 0.147 (deep-MOND √(2x) = 0.141)". With the symbol as printed (a₀, the MOND scale), simple-μ gives g_obs/a₀ = (x + √(x²+4x))/2 = 0.105, with deep limit √x = 0.100. The quoted 0.147 and 0.141 are right only if the symbol is **a₀′** = a₀/2. Then y = (x + √(x²+8x))/2 = 0.1465 and √(2x) = 0.1414. It's a notation slip, but it's in the sentence that certifies the identity. The companion claim, that g_obs = g_bar/C keyed on g_bar gives a constant additive acceleration (g_obs/a₀′ = x + 2 = 2.01), checks out and is a nice point.

The **ΔBIC story** is where I got stuck. γ = 2 gives +184 with 2,807 points treated as independent, "+10.9 recomputed with N = 166", and "≈ +11 at galaxy level, at the threshold". Changing only the k·ln N penalty moves BIC by ln(2807/166) ≈ 2.8, not 173. So the χ² term was rescaled, presumably by ~N_gal/N_pts, but the page never says how. Meanwhile the same page reports a galaxy-block bootstrap of the full-N ΔBIC = 179 [23, 351] with N_eff ≈ 150. If that interval is the honest galaxy-level uncertainty, its *lower* end is still > 10, which reads as "disfavoured" and not "at the threshold". These are two different statistics (a discounted point estimate vs the sampling spread of the undiscounted one), and the page needs to say which one carries the verdict and why.

**/tier-1-existing TEST-25** (Cassini Q₂ squeeze) is mostly right. Q₂ is the EFE-induced quadrupole in QUMOND, with bounds from Hees+2014 and the DE440 update. The 8.7σ is published and marginalized, the in-house 17.95σ is unmarginalized, and the page says so. The TEST-25 numbers also check out internally: profiled a₀′ = 5.33×10⁻¹¹ at γ = 0.489 gives a₀′/γ = 1.09×10⁻¹⁰, matching /galaxy-rotation. ΔBIC +7.1 = Δχ² (−0.84) + ln 2807 (7.94), also consistent, though /galaxy-rotation's "the fit improvement is zero" should say "−0.84". But the card says that at galaxy level "the interval is γ = 0.3–1.7, and the squeeze is at γ ≳ 1.5–2". That means the intersection is **non-empty** at galaxy level, so the registered kill doesn't fire there. Yet the badge is "Kill Criterion Triggered" and the row is counted in the 6. Worse, /galaxy-rotation gives the galaxy-level refit as γ ≈ 0.6 **[0.43, 0.95]**, which would keep the squeeze empty. Two galaxy-level γ intervals coexist and they flip TEST-25's verdict. The Honest Assessment's count-rule list (−1 for 2σ, −1 for the γ = 2 pin) doesn't include a "TEST-25 at galaxy level" entry.

**TEST-09/10** are the cleanest refutations on the site, and I'd lead with TEST-10. A boost bounded at B_max = 1/Ω_m cannot make asymptotically flat curves at g_bar ≪ a₀ at all. NGC 2915 needing B = 13.7 kills every Ω_m-based ceiling. The BTFR-slope test (n = 3.35 vs 3.75 ± 0.10, convention-dependent) is weaker, and the card is honest about that.

**/dark-energy**: H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C), so ρ_DE = ρ_m(1/C − 1). At γ = ½, 1/C − 1 = 2/x, so ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit = const. I re-derived it, and the early and late limits w → −2γ and w → −1 also check out (1/C − 1 ≈ 2x^(−2γ) for large x; ≈ 1/(γx) for small x). So the sign lock sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a) is real and forbids the DESI quadrant, and at γ = ½ it's literally ΛCDM. The badge says Reparametrization, which is correct. The fit γ = 0.487 (−0.021/+0.024) is ½ within ~0.6σ with Δχ² = −0.3, i.e. the data say "Λ". The page juxtaposes 0.487 with galaxy 0.489 but correctly explains that both are each dataset picking its standard model, since ½ is the special point in both variables.
