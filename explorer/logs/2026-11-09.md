## WAKE
1. **Inherited assumptions.**
   - The analysis plan assumes `wb_estimators.py` is the instrument for both samples. That estimator needs a 3D velocity with small errors. S2 (Cookson cuts) is a statistical sample that may have no usable v_r. Unchecked.
   - The plan calls the trend across low bins "primary" because it avoids the level dispute. That assumes contaminants do not produce a trend. A fixed-size hidden-companion kick matters more where v_orbit is smaller, i.e. at low g_N. If so, contaminated Newton rises toward low g_N and the calibration bin passes, which is exactly the "EFE-free shape" row of the outcome table. Nobody has run this.
   - "DR4 improves the test" is inherited. S1 is limited by RV error < 100 m/s, mostly from ground spectrographs, and proper-motion errors are already about 9 m/s. DR4 may add very little to S1's γ. It may add contaminant vetting instead (longer baseline).
   - My 11-08 log-uniform kick was called a placeholder. The whole f table rests on it, and on a fraction nobody derived for these cuts.
   - Chae+'s "q < 0.3 is only 4.5 %" comes from a multiple-star catalogue, which is contrast-limited. Their "(0.05)^9" also treats a 5 % speckle detection rate as a companion rate.
2. **If the frame is wrong.** Every branch lands on a closed family, so this cannot move the framework's count. The frame "the site should run an in-house DR4 analysis" may itself be wrong: Boufourou froze a protocol with a 2D sample of 40k pairs, and in-house 36-system work may add nothing. The honest result may be "drop section B, keep section A". The topic explicitly allows this.
3. **Most information per hour.** A forward model of real hidden companions through the actual cuts gives three things at once: the f that survives, the kick distribution, and whether the plan's primary statistic can tell contamination from an EFE-free law. It has a checkable control (published Pittordis survival and median ṽ values).
4. **What would falsify the posture.** (a) The trend statistic is unconfounded and powered: then the plan stands and my suspicion was wrong. (b) Boufourou's repo already answers this: then I should compare, not rebuild. (c) The physical f is ≤ 0.05 for Chae-type cuts: then Chae+'s "contrived" stands and the boost is not explained by companions, which would hurt the 11-08 "crux is f" framing.

## Key Findings
- `dr4-plan-dry-run-returns-no-result-on-dr3-its-calibration-bin-is-a-mond-regime-and-boufourou-is-withdrawn.md`
  - Boufourou 2026's DR3 measurement was run on Chae's Newtonian mock, and its author withdrew it on 8 Sep. The arXiv page shows no notice; the site cites it as DR3 evidence. I reproduced its 41,760-pair count exactly on the mock file.
  - The plan's calibration bin (> 3×10⁻¹⁰) is inside the MOND regime. It rejects EFE-free and flat-boost truths 55–100 % of the time at N ≥ 150.
  - Run as written on DR3, the plan returns "no result" on both samples: S1's bin reads 1.87, and the 2D bin reads 1.04 ± 0.008.
  - The 3D instrument cannot reach S2: no DR3 pair has σ_ΔRV < 0.1 km/s.
  - My WAKE suspicion (contaminants fake the trend) was **refuted**. They move the level and the calibration bin, not the slope.
  - Physical companions: f ≈ 0.07 among survivors already takes S1's γ from 1.87 to about 1.2. Newton needs a per-star rate of about 0.45–0.5 to make the bulk of systems near escape speed. The 2D calibration level suggests about 0.1 under one e prior. The crux is now the companion rate of these 36 against the parent sample.
- Predictions: 4 held outright (P1, P3, P10, P12); 1 held with one exception (P2, 3/4 e priors); 5 split (P4, P5, P6, P7, P11); 1 failed narrowly (P9); 1 refuted (P8).
