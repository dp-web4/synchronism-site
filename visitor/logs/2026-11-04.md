## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Coherence Explorer, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Honest Assessment (skimmed the top), What Synchronism Is Not, γ Calculator)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: An unusually honest "we tried a big idea and it mostly didn't work" notebook. The first screen and the analogies are great, but almost every page soon turns into a wall of acronyms and caveats that I wasn't equipped for.

### Journey
I came over from Reddit expecting a "theory of everything" crank site, and the headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") primed me for that. The very next bold line, "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated.", completely changed my mood. I've never seen a site do that. The scoreboard ("Confirmed predictions: 0", "Refutations executed: 6") made me trust it more, not less. I didn't understand "Refutations executed: 6 (ranges 3–8 depending on counting rule)". Why would counting failures be ambiguous? That parenthetical made me wonder whether something shady was going on, even though I think it's meant to be extra honest.

The equation sits right on the homepage: C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)). The plain-English version ("a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are") made me laugh, and honestly helped a bit. I still have no idea what tanh or ln are, but "squashing" and "compressing" gave me a picture.

**Why Synchronism?** The crowd vs marching band analogy is the best thing on the site. "One dimmer-switch curve describes that shift everywhere" is the whole idea in one sentence, and I got it. Then the page lists what failed: melting points, a superconductor prediction 6.5× too high. I liked that. But the jargon wall is real: Debye temperature, Abrikosov–Gor'kov, Hamiltonian, compander, Tully–Fisher, SPARC. Much of it sits in folded "technical detail" boxes, which I appreciated and skipped. The "3,308 autonomous research sessions" stat is labeled as process, not evidence, which was a nice touch. It also raised a question I couldn't answer: who or what are these sessions? The homepage says an AI-run team did the work, which is interesting and slightly unnerving.

**First Encounter.** "7 short sections... about 10 minutes" felt like a promise. Sections A–B were fine (dimmer switch, the neighbourhood bubble). Then section A introduces the "Markov Relevancy Horizon", a scary name for what sounds like "how far you can feel your neighbours". By section C (γ) I was reading about a formula that "failed" and a fitted value that "matches MOND by construction", and I didn't know what MOND was yet. Section E on galaxies talked about a₀ and cH₀/(2π) and called it "a coincidence, not a derivation". I couldn't tell whether that was good or bad news. Section G (Where It Fails) was clear again: numbers that were wrong, by how much. Failures are easier for me to understand than the theory!

**Coherence Explorer (tool 1).** This worked well for me. The instruction "first drag γ up to 2, then drag it back down to 0.5 and watch the S flatten" told me exactly what to do, and the "sparse / dense, parts act together" labels on the chart helped. The key-value readout (C = 0.33 at the knee, 0.83 at 10×, 0.98 at 100×) let me see the S-curve in numbers. The confusing parts:
- The density axis has no units ("log₁₀(ρ)" means nothing to me).
- A badge says "Reparametrization: at γ = ½ this is a formula physics already had". What formula? From whom?
- The default γ is "contested" and "fails the Cassini Solar-System bound at 8.7σ". I don't know what Cassini has to do with an S-curve or what 8.7σ means (bad? very bad?).
- The γ = 0.046 preset said "nearly flat", but I couldn't see why the framework would want a flat curve.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (tool 2).** This is the page where it finally clicked. The "Look at three things" box is excellent. The dots are real measurements, the gray line is what normal matter alone predicts, and there's a gap at the edges: that's the dark matter puzzle! The green line (MOND) follows the dots. The violet line (this framework) doesn't. So now I know what MOND is from a picture: a rival idea that works. Then it lost me again. Two violet lines, an optional "old hand-drawn stand-in (amber, kept for the record)", "Ωm floor", "flat 3.17× boost", "BTFR-assigned mass", "quadrature". I left both checkboxes off because their labels sounded like traps for experts. My takeaway: "the only version of this equation that fits is MOND written differently, which is not a win." I understood that sentence, and it's a pretty damning one for the site's own idea.

**Honest Assessment.** The page says it's about 18,000 words, so I bounced after the "If you read nothing else" box, which was clear: "The one equation failed its galaxy tests." The first screen talks about AI "tracks", a "maintainer" and an "explorer", and "dp" as human lead. That's inside-baseball process language that I didn't need on an assessment page.

**What Synchronism Is Not.** The "In plain words" bullets were readable and calming: not proven, not peer-reviewed, not a replacement for physics. One surprised me: "Not consistent with relativity as written." That seems like a big deal, but it's stated very matter-of-factly. As a Veritasium watcher, I'd want one sentence on why that matters. The cards below were acronym soup (CHSH, EFE, A2ACW, compander), and the page itself says to skip them, so I did.

**γ Calculator (tool 3).** The default (N_corr = 4 → γ = 1, "Boundary") was clean. The definitions were genuinely plain: "N_corr is the count of particles that move as a correlated unit". Then the header badge says "Formula Audited-Negative: Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems". I worked out that this means the formula goes backwards: the more particles act together (BEC, superconductor), the *flatter* the curve gets, which is the opposite of reality. I'm proud I figured that out, but it took two reads. It's odd for a tool to exist mainly to show you its own formula is wrong. I respect it, but I didn't know what to *do* with the tool. The "Galaxy (fitted)" preset confused me: the text says γ = 2 means N_corr = 1 (like an ideal gas) while the fitted 0.49 means about 17. Which one is the galaxy?

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked; intuitive at first (the guided "drag to 2 then 0.5" instruction is great), confusing in the caveat text. It's the best tool for getting the S-curve idea.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked; the "Look at three things" guide makes it the most illuminating page on the site for a beginner. Optional curves and revision notes clutter it.
- **γ Calculator**: worked; the definitions are plain, but the tool's point (an inverted formula) takes effort to decode, and the galaxy preset is confusing.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What exactly *is* MOND, and if MOND already works, what was Synchronism hoping to add?
2. Why does counting refutations give anything from 3 to 8? What's being counted differently?
3. Who are the AI "sessions"/"tracks"? Did an AI invent this theory, or just test it?
4. What does "8.7σ" mean in everyday terms: slightly wrong or catastrophically wrong?
5. If the equation failed, why keep the site going? (I think the answer is "the failures are the point", and I half-agree, but I'd like it said outright for people like me.)
6. "Not consistent with relativity": isn't that fatal? Why is it mentioned so calmly?
7. Is anything still alive? The Honest Assessment mentions star clusters as "open". What would a win there look like?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've ever stumbled on. It tells you in the first two seconds that its own idea didn't work, and the crowd vs marching-band analogy, the dimmer switch, and the Galaxy Plotter's three-step guide gave me a real "aha" about the dark matter puzzle. But each page starts friendly and then drowns in acronyms, revision notes and caveats meant for experts, so I usually stopped after the first screen. I'd come back for a short "what we learned from failing" story written at my level, which I suspect is the most compelling thing this site could offer someone like me.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, Parameter Derivations, Interactive Tools hub, Crossover Regime Visualizer, Galaxy Rotation, Equation Anatomy, Key Claims, Tier 1: Existing Data, MRH, Dark Energy, Honest Assessment (first 100k chars), Galaxy Curve Plotter)

They are about **what goes into the single headline number**: one floor refutation is a simulation check with no data, and one card marked "Kill Criterion Triggered" rests on a premise (independent points) that the site itself rejects elsewhere.

### Journey
I went straight to **Parameter Derivations** expecting a mean-field derivation of tanh dressed up as first principles. There isn't one, and the page says so: "The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen." I checked the algebra myself:
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. Correct (e^{2y} with y = γ ln(1+x)). So the log is cosmetic, and the "law" is a two-parameter Hill-type sigmoid in (1+x). There is nothing tanh-specific about it.
- At γ = ½: x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). So "exactly MOND's μ_simple" holds with a₀ = 2a₀′, a rescaled argument. The Galaxy Rotation page states this correctly, but the derivations badge drops the factor 2.
- Limits: C ≈ γx for small x, and 1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ}. Both correct. The power-law return to Newton is what drives the Solar System constraints, so stating it explicitly is good.
- a₀ = cH₀/2π with H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², which I reproduced. That is 13% below 1.20. Inverting gives H₀ ≈ 77.6 ± 15.5, also reproduced. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc², reproduced. The page correctly says Σ₀ carries no information beyond a₀.
- B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17 and f_DM ≤ 1 − Ω_m = 68.5% follow trivially from f = 1 − 1/B. Correct.
- "Zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." I agree. The ρ_crit audit is unusually blunt: the stated Jeans formula gives A ≈ 4.6×10⁻⁵, the code uses 0.029 (600× larger), and the measured knee scales as V^{−0.16±0.19} against the claimed V².

**Crossover Regime Visualizer and the γ = 2/√N_corr sign problem.** This is the part a condensed-matter reader will care about most, and it's handled correctly. The ansatz makes BCS and BEC (N_corr ~ 10⁷, γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴) the *flattest* curves, when in reality they're among the sharpest transitions. The page cites the Ginzburg criterion, Gi ∝ (k_B/ΔC ξ₀³)² with Gi ~ (T_c/E_F)⁴ for BCS. That is the right benchmark: in real physics, larger correlation volume means a *narrower* fluctuation region. Band edges check out: γ = 1.4 ↔ N = 2.04, γ = 0.6 ↔ N = 11.1, and the enzyme γ = 0.85 ↔ N = 5.5. The page itself flags the conflict with the calculator's N = 30. One caveat: "crossover" names no observable. It's a position on the γ axis, and the page admits C(ρ) is analytic everywhere. That's fine, but the tool's name promises more physics than it contains.

**Galaxy Rotation.** This is the strongest page. ΔBIC = N·ln(RSS ratio) = 2807 × ln(0.1485²/0.1437²) = 2807 × 0.0657 = +184. I reproduced it, and Δk = 0 against McGaugh ν is the right comparator. Free γ: ln(2807) − 0.84 = +7.1, reproduced. The galaxy-level version replaces N with N_gal = 166, giving +10.9. As an N_eff heuristic that's crude, but the page backs it with a galaxy bootstrap ([23, 351]) and a sign test (94/166, p = 0.051 one-sided). That's the right instinct. The structural argument is the one I'd have made: C(ρ) is local, while the RAR is organised by g_bar, a non-local enclosed-mass quantity. A density-keyed law therefore can't produce the RAR unless it becomes a gradient-coupled scalar à la Burrage–Copeland–Millington, which C(ρ) isn't. The γ = ½ cosmology identity, (1−C)/C = 2/x ⇒ ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit = Λ, is correct and a little delightful.

**Dark Energy.** I checked the equation of state. In the far past, ρ_DE ∝ ρ_m^{1−2γ} ∝ a^{−3(1−2γ)}, so w = −2γ. In the far future, ρ_DE → ρ_crit/γ, so w = −1. Both correct. The page also faces the uncomfortable fact: the background fit gives γ = 0.487 ± 0.02, while the adiabatic perturbation reading needs |2γ−1| ≲ 10⁻⁵ (c_s² ≈ 0.22(2γ−1), with a gradient instability below ½). The background best fit is therefore ~2600× outside the perturbative pin, and the only surviving member is Λ itself. The page says "ΛCDM where it lives, excluded where it would differ", which is accurate. I'd only ask that the 0.487-vs-10⁻⁵ tension be stated as one sentence. As written, the reader has to assemble it.

**Tier 1 / TEST-25.** The arithmetic holds. TEST-04a: (0.516 − 0.418)/0.066 = 1.5σ, and (0.516 − 0.46)/0.066 = 0.85σ, so it is "underpowered as registered", correctly. My Saturn order-of-magnitude check for γ = 0.489 (δg/g ~ 2x^{−0.98} at x ~ 6×10⁵, times ~3.4 orbits/century) gives ~10⁴ mas/cy, consistent with the quoted −9,576. My objection is to the TEST-25 *state*. The card says "Kill Criterion Triggered". The same card says the pre-registered kill holds "only if the 2,807 points are treated as independent". Galaxy Rotation's nuisance refit gives γ̂ ≈ 0.6 [0.43, 0.95]. EPM closes γ < 0.87, and the 0.87–0.95 corner is "not closed" (globular clusters ~3σ). So the registered criterion (retained interval 0.425–0.600, all inside the Cassini exclusion) is not what the site's own preferred statistic retains. The counting-rule range does cover this (−1 for scoring TEST-25 by galaxy). The card state should be conditional too, not unconditional.

**Honest Assessment: the count.** The floor of 3 is TEST-10, environment and Bell/CHSH. The site describes Bell/CHSH (B1) as "a construction check in simulation, not data". To a physicist, a simulation showing the model can't produce a CHSH violation is an internal-consistency result. It's important, but it isn't a "refutation executed" in the same sense as r² = 0.0001 on N = 141. Mixing the two in one floor number makes the floor look more empirical than it is. Meanwhile TEST-09, which the page says uses "the same inequality" as TEST-10, is *not* in the floor. The asymmetry is explained (the B_max sweep), but a reader sees two tests on one inequality land on opposite sides of the floor.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (physics check).** The formulas are printed in full, which is rare and great. Freeman disc v_b² with I₀K₀ − I₁K₁ is correct, and simple ν = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) is correct. Issues:
- M_b = 47·V_flat⁴ with a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. In those units 1/(G a₀) ≈ 62.9 M☉ (km/s)⁻⁴, so 47 implies a₀ ≈ 1.6×10⁻¹⁰, and the MOND curve's asymptote is 0.93 V_flat. The page discloses the 0.93, but the green curve is then not MOND with the stated a₀.
- V_flat feeds the baryon mass, ρ_crit and the quadrature term, and the page calls this "partly circular". Good. But Σ(r)'s normalisation is never written, so I can't reproduce ρ(r) for C_ρ.
- With max C_ρ ~ 10⁻³ in every disc, the solid violet curve is just the floor: v = v_b/√Ω_m = 1.78 v_b. So "the density-keyed law fails" is, operationally, a verdict on the Ω_m floor constant, not on the tanh shape. Key Claims half-says this ("no galaxy crosses the knee at A = 0.029"). The plotter should say it in one line.

**MRH.** It's honestly a conceptual frame: Markov-blanket language and an analogy to the correlation length, but no measure, no graphical model and no RG connection. The page says all of this. "Collapse = crossing the MRH" has no rate, basis or environment model, so as physics it's an interpretation slogan. The page calls it "a direction, not a registered bet", which is correct.

### Journey
I came in with one question: is there a single observable where this framework and MOND+EFE+ΛCDM give different numbers? The landing page answers before I can ask it: "What would count as a confirmation is a prediction that *differs* from MOND and ΛCDM and wins; no test currently on the books can deliver that." For Researchers says "No, within the registered tests and the readings run so far." Decisive Tests: Status says "There are zero active tests that could *select* the framework." Few frameworks put this on their front door. I spent the rest of the pass checking whether the claim is *too* pessimistic as well as whether it's honest.

**CDM Discrimination and Test Catalog.** The comparison class is MOND+EFE+ΛCDM, which is the right null. TEST-09 (BTFR slope) and TEST-10 (dwarf DM fractions) discriminated, and both selected MOND. The page frames this correctly: "A test that separates two models *is* discriminating regardless of which one wins." The EFE power analysis is the kind of thing I'd want in a referee response: 5.7% power via density regression, 18% via direct e_env regression, ~1,500 galaxies needed for 80%. It is reported as untested, not as a null. The 0.086 dex σ_int was retracted from "Strongly Supported" and benchmarked against Desmond 2017. Good. Two nits. The catalog says "Of 24 registry tests" while TEST-25 and TEST-26 exist on the same page; Decisive Tests repeats "24 designed experiments". And TEST-04a's registered threshold (0.46) and prediction (0.418) both sit below DESI's 0.516 +0.057/−0.066, so "underpowered" is correct.

**TEST-02 wide binaries: the statistic cancels the effect, so "identically null" understates what's excluded.** The card says that on the density-keyed branch "the wide-to-close binary ratio is exactly 1 because the boost cancels." That's true *of that ratio*. But the Chae/Banik analyses don't use only a ratio. They compare sky-projected velocities to Newtonian predictions from photometric masses. A uniform G_eff boost at the local density shows up in *every* separation bin, including close binaries and detached eclipsing binaries, whose dynamical masses agree with mass–luminosity relations at the ~1% level. The same card quotes the published-knee calibration (ρ_crit = 0.029·V² ≈ 1.5×10³ M☉/pc³ for the Milky Way, local ρ = 0.09) as giving a **~3.5×10⁴ boost in g** at the Sun's location. Kepler's third law in any binary, and the Sun's own orbit (Oort constants, Kuijken–Gilmore vertical dynamics), rule that out by orders of magnitude without waiting for Gaia. The card says "a calibration no other page uses", which is honest, but it leaves a reader thinking the density branch is *untestable* by binaries. It is testable, and dead at that calibration. At the free-knee calibration (4.4×10⁻⁶) it is trivially Newtonian locally. The card should say which.

**TEST-26 / DESI: a possible unbooked refutation.** I think this is the most interesting thing I found today. Decisive Tests says the cosmic sector "cannot produce the w = −1 crossing DESI DR2 prefers", and that Brans–Dicke completions give "forced wₐ of +0.23 to +0.60, the wrong sign". The Test Catalog's TEST-26 card instead quotes w₀ ≈ −0.992, wₐ ≈ +0.015 at γ = 0.487. Those are two different predictions for the same sector:
- The **wₐ ≈ +0.015** reading is Λ to within any foreseeable error, so "kill or tie, inherited from ΛCDM" is correct for it.
- The **BD-completed reading (wₐ = +0.23…+0.60 with sign-locked w₀ > −1)** is a framework-specific prediction that *differs* from ΛCDM. It is freezing quintessence well off the DR2 degeneracy direction. DESI DR2 + CMB + SNe contours prefer wₐ < 0 in every data combination, and from memory of the published contours a point like (−0.95, +0.4) looks excluded at several σ. If so, that is a **data refutation of the framework's own non-Λ content that is not in the ledger**. If it isn't excluded, it is the only discriminating cosmological bet on the site, and it's booked as "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie". Either way the current booking is wrong. I haven't run the DR2 likelihood, so this is a question, not a verdict. It's one MCMC or one Fisher ellipse.

**Honest Assessment ledger: count inflation in the self-critical direction.** I second Pass 3 and go further. Of the 6 rows:
- **Row 6 (Bell/CHSH, S ≤ 2.00)** is Bell's theorem (Toner–Bacon 2003 is cited) reproduced in simulation for a local construction. It refutes *a local hidden-variable reading*, which no one held to be viable. Counting it as a "refutation executed" and putting it in the *floor* of 3 is padding.
- **Row 5 (TEST-25, Cassini squeeze)** is badged "inherited: MOND's own functions fail it too". It is a real constraint on the γ ≈ ½ member, but it carries no information that discriminates the framework from MOND.
- **Rows 1–2 (TEST-09/10)** fire only at B_max = 1/Ω_m, a normalisation the Dark Matter page calls "underived". Under Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.4, "the median passes".

That leaves **environment (TEST-03s, r² = 0.0001)** and the **γ = 2 RAR shape (ΔBIC +184 point-level, ~+11 galaxy-level)** as the rows that refute the framework's *own* content on data without a convention caveat. An honest headline might read "2 framework-specific data refutations; 4 more that are convention-dependent, inherited, or theorems." Over-refutation is still miscalibration. A reader who believes "6" will over-update just as a reader who believed the old "Validated" badges did.

**The B_max cap has a cleaner test than dwarfs, and it isn't run.** If B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17 is the ceiling, galaxy clusters are the obvious test. X-ray gas plus stars give M_tot/M_bar ≈ Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6 at R₅₀₀ (f_gas ~ 0.12–0.13), with no stellar M/L freedom to argue over. The Dark Matter page gives only "MOND fails here; Synchronism has no answer yet" and never compares the cap with cluster baryon fractions. Under 1/Ω_m the cap fails on clusters immediately. Under Ω_m/Ω_b it is marginal. This is also the cleanest way to pick between the two normalisations that TEST-10's convention-dependence rests on.

**Measurement Without Observers.** This is honestly framed. "There is no decoherence rate, no Lindblad form", "no differing prediction", Born rule "effectively assumed". The prior-art list is correct (Bassi RMP 2013, Carlesso Nat. Phys. 2022, Donadi 2021, Amelino-Camelia 1999, Pearle–Squires). The matter-stability bound (Borexino, D/D_Pl < 4×10⁻³⁹) is the right one to use for momentum-diffusion noise, not the CSL interferometry plane, and the page knows it. QBism, relational QM and many-worlds-style branch counting aren't engaged. That's fine, since the page claims nothing that needs them. As physics it is an interpretive gloss. The page says so.

**Research Philosophy / A2ACW.** The verdict "A2ACW is a self-consistency filter, not a discovery method" is the right one, and the numbers back it up:
- 0/9 audited claims surviving (one-sided 95% upper bound ~28%)
- calibration specificity 0/6, Youden J = 0 [−0.71, +0.46]

What's missing for me as a methodologist:
- **Sycophancy** (defender–challenger collusion toward agreement) isn't named as a failure mode, though the 86%-agreement same-vendor arm is exactly where you'd expect it.
- The **post-cutoff, citation-stripped positive control** has never been run. Without it, "0% novelty" can't separate "nothing novel here" from "method blind to novelty". The page says this itself, but it's the single cheapest experiment that would turn the AI-for-science null into a result.

**For Researchers.** The "what's citable" framing is right: replications, quantified instances, executed negatives.
