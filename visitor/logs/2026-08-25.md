## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The metaphors are genuinely great and the honesty is refreshing, but the site spends so much energy telling me it failed that I never got a clear picture of what it was actually claiming.

### Journey

Clicked through from Reddit. The headline — **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** — is exactly the kind of thing that makes me click. Great hook. Veritasium would open with that.

Then, immediately below it, before anything else: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is (natural log? I've heard of it). I don't know what γ or ρcrit are. So within about eight seconds of landing, the page went from "exciting question" to "this is not for me." I almost bounced right there.

What kept me was the scoreboard: **0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations, 0 of 6 validated claims surviving audit.** My honest first reaction was confusion — is this a site *for* a theory or *against* one? I've never seen a physics site lead with its own failure count. I decided that was interesting enough to keep going. (I want to flag: that's a coin flip. Half the Reddit traffic bounces here.)

Clicked **"Beginner Path →"**. Step 1 ("Why Synchronism?") gave me the best thing on the whole site: **a crowd milling around a plaza behaves like independent individuals; a marching band behaves like one organism.** That I get instantly. Density → collective behavior. Suddenly the equation felt like it might be describing something real rather than being decoration.

Step 2 (First Encounter) was the strongest page I read. Almost no math, and "presence" got explained as just *how much compatible stuff is nearby*. The MRH idea — "a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy" — is a genuinely good line. If the landing page led with these two sentences instead of the equation, I'd have stayed without hesitating.

Then I went to **Tools**, because I learn by fiddling. And here's where I got confused in a way I didn't expect. Reading down the tool list:

- γ Calculator — "identify where the framework contradicts condensed-matter physics"
- Phase Boundary Visualizer — "observe how phase transitions display the framework's documented sign inversion error"
- Consciousness Threshold Demo — "a mathematical artifact of sigmoid midpoint assumptions, not empirical evidence"

Every single tool is advertised by the mistake it demonstrates. There's nothing here that just lets me *play with the cool idea*. I wanted a toy; I was handed seven autopsies.

I tried **Equation Anatomy** anyway and it was better than I feared — tanh described as a dimmer switch (input near 0 → out near 0, input huge → out near 1) is a good analogy, and "a number 1,000× bigger only comes out ~7 units bigger" made ln click for me for the first time in my life. Genuinely useful. But the same page casually name-drops BCS superconductors and the Central Limit Theorem, so I skimmed the back half.

The **Coherence Explorer** was the highlight — sliders, a curve that moves, plain-language captions. I got what γ does by dragging it. That tool should be linked from the landing page, above the fold, instead of the raw equation.

But it also produced my biggest single confusion. Equation Anatomy and the Glossary both told me ρcrit is "near saturation, not the halfway point — C ≈ 0.88." Then the Coherence Explorer opened with γ = 0.49 and showed me **C(ρcrit) = 0.3272**. So at the "critical density," is the system 88% coherent or 33% coherent? Those are opposite stories — one says "by the critical point it's nearly all collective," the other says "barely started." I assume the difference is the γ setting, but nothing on either page told me that, and I have no way to reconcile it myself.

Then a second one hit. The Glossary says coherence is **not** quantum coherence, and that superconductors — the most famously "coherent" things I've heard of on YouTube — have **LOW** C. That broke my brain a little. The whole site is built on a word that means roughly the opposite of what I came in believing. It's flagged honestly, but flagging it doesn't undo it; I spent the rest of my visit unsure whether I was reading "coherence" correctly on any given page.

I went to the **Galaxy Curve Plotter** because dark matter is what I actually came for. The plain-language setup is excellent — "physics predicts galaxies should rotate more slowly at their outer edges, like planets in the solar system. They don't." That's a proper Space Time-grade explanation. But then there are five lines on the chart, and *two* of them are Synchronism: a violet one (the real equation, which flatly doesn't work — max C ≈ 0.001) and an amber one that fits the data beautifully and is labeled as a hand-drawn stand-in that "fits because it was drawn to fit." Showing me a gorgeous curve and then telling me it's fake is admirably honest and completely disorienting. I stared at that chart for a while genuinely unsure which line I was supposed to take away.

Then I hit the thing that actually stopped my visit. The beginner path's **Step 5 is Honest Assessment**. I went there. It's dense with BTFR, RAR transition shape, fσ₈, ΔBIC — none of which I can parse — and the page's own advice for someone like me is to go read **"Why Synchronism?"** instead. That's **Step 1**. The beginner path, at step 5 of 6, tells beginners to go back to step 1. That's a loop. I never finished the path.

I checked the Consciousness Demo last, since consciousness is the thing that would make me share this with friends. Eight independent methods all landing on C ≈ 0.50 is a *fantastic* hook. And then the page immediately explains that (a) 0.50 is just the midpoint of the scale so anything would land there, (b) the eight methods aren't independent, and (c) the scatter is suspiciously tight, and (d) the one experiment cited measured a different variable entirely and a p-value that was quoted for a while turned out to have no source. By the end I understood that I should not be impressed, but I never understood what the claim actually *was*.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the answer at the critical density is 0.88 in one place and 0.33 in another, which number describes the world? (I think it's a γ setting, but I had to guess that.)
2. Why is it called *coherence* if superconductors — the thing I'd have bet money is maximally coherent — score near zero?
3. Did the framework actually get anything right, ever? I read six refutations and zero confirmations. Is there a single win I can hold onto, even a small one?
4. What's the difference between "this failed" and "this is just MOND rewritten"? Both verdicts appear a lot and I can't tell which is worse.
5. If the galaxy prediction gives C ≈ 0.001 — basically nothing — why is the galaxy result described as "collapsed onto MOND" rather than simply "didn't work"?
6. The site says 3,308 research sessions run by AI with no human intervention. Is that the actual point of the project? Nobody explained why that matters, and it's the most unusual thing here.

### Verdict

The ideas underneath this are genuinely exciting and the writing, when it relaxes, is as good as the science YouTube I watch — the marching band, the Andromeda-outside-the-atom's-bubble, the dimmer switch, the planets-should-slow-down-but-don't. I'd have shared any of those. But the site keeps burying them under an equation I can't read and a running tally of its own failures, and the one path built for me dead-ends at step 5 by telling me to start over. I left admiring the honesty more than understanding the theory, which I don't think is the intended outcome. I'd come back if someone told me a beginner version of the Honest Assessment existed — the failures are clearly the most interesting story here, and right now they're the part I'm least able to read.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is unusually disciplined for a research site — real breadcrumbs, a real glossary, a canonical badge legend — but three of its load-bearing vocabularies (ρcrit, the badge set, the test count) have drifted out of sync with the pages that use them, and the drift is concentrated exactly where the site is most careful elsewhere.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a handoff: walk the nav top to bottom, check that every label survives the click, check that every term is defined before it's used, and check that the counts agree with themselves.

**Nav and landing.** Nine top-level items. Clean, no dropdowns, stable across pages. The home page opens with `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` followed immediately by a symbol key defining C, γ, ρ, ρcrit, tanh, ln, and MRH. That symbol key is genuinely good practice — it's the thing most physics sites skip. First real snag: the home page also uses validation badges (Reparametrization, Speculative, Untested, Failed) in the "What Synchronism Covers" section with no legend and no link to one. The badges are nav item #1; their canonical definitions are on **Honest Assessment**, nav item #9. Every reader meets the vocabulary eight items before they can look it up.

**The Start Here problem.** Nav says "Start Here." The link goes to `/why-synchronism`. The H1 says "Start Here: Why Synchronism?". The breadcrumb's last segment says "Why Synchronism?". The Beginner path calls step 1 "Why Synchronism?". And `/start-here` **also resolves**, serving byte-identical content with no redirect. That's one page with two URLs and three names. From a docs standpoint this splits inbound links, splits any analytics, and — the reason I noticed it at all — makes a reader who arrives via nav and a reader who arrives via the learning path believe they're on different pages.

**Breadcrumbs.** They work, and they're consistent in *format*, but the taxonomy they describe doesn't exist anywhere else. I collected these middle segments:

- Home / **Getting Started** / Why Synchronism? · Learning Paths · Glossary · Honest Assessment · Key Claims
- Home / **Core Theory** / MRH · Critical Density
- Home / **Predictions & Experiments** / Test Roadmap
- Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator · Phase Boundary Visualizer · Coherence Explorer · Galaxy Curve Plotter

None of "Getting Started," "Core Theory," or "Predictions & Experiments" appears in the top nav, and none of them is a link — they're inert text. So the breadcrumb promises a three-level hierarchy the reader can see but never navigate, and the only middle segment that *is* real ("Interactive Tools") is the one the nav labels differently, as "Tools." Separately, Honest Assessment is a top-nav item filed under "Getting Started," which is a strange parent for the site's most advanced page.

**Terminology check — this is the significant one.** I tracked ρcrit across four pages:

| Page | What it calls ρcrit |
|---|---|
| Glossary | "Reference Density (Saturation Knee)" |
| Home symbol key | "saturation knee" |
| Coherence Explorer | "Reference density — saturation knee"; body text: *"ρcrit is a saturation knee, not a critical density in the phase-transition sense."* |
| γ Calculator | "ρcrit — critical density" |
| `/critical-density` | H1 **"Critical Density"**; opens *"The critical density ρcrit is the density at which the coherence function transitions from quantum (C → 0) to classical (C → 1)."* |

So the site has a page whose title, URL, and opening sentence all assert precisely the thing another page explicitly denies. I can't judge which is physically right — but I can say the site clearly *decided* (the glossary and the explorer both go out of their way to make the distinction), and the decision didn't propagate to the page that owns the term. A URL is the hardest thing in a docs set to change, which is probably why it's the last thing that got fixed.

Related, smaller: the C axis gets glossed three different ways — glossary "sparse/independent (0) → dense/collective (1)", Coherence Explorer "independent → collective", `/critical-density` "quantum (C → 0) → classical (C → 1)". Same axis, three vocabularies, and only one of them is in the glossary.

**Badge vocabulary.** The Honest Assessment legend is the best artifact on the site — three families (MRH-relationship, Descriptive, Deprecated) plus a clearly separated "Operational States (not badges)" list of ~17 entries. The glossary correctly points to it as canonical. Then I went to Test Roadmap and collected the status labels actually in use: Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative, Reparametrization, Untested, Speculative, Failed, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, **Unrunnable as Stated**, **Prospective**. The last two are not in the canonical legend, and the page's own "Canonical definitions →" link points at `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, which doesn't define them. A canonical reference that a page links to and that doesn't cover that page's vocabulary is worse than no reference — the reader follows the link, fails, and stops trusting the link.

**Counting check.** Test Roadmap: *"Synchronism has defined 24 specific, falsifiable experiments organized into four tiers."* Tier breakdown sums correctly: 10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24. But the same page then discusses **TEST-25** (added 2026-07-28) and **TEST-26** (proposed 2026-08-10), neither of which has a tier and neither of which is in the 24. The headline finding is stated as *"Of these 24 proposed tests, 0 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM"* — a denominator that excludes the two most recently added tests. And the Advanced learning path advertises step 8 as "Test Roadmap (**24 experiments**)", so the stale count has already propagated into navigation copy. This is ordinary docs drift, but it's drift in the one number a skeptical reader will check first.

**Tools index.** Seven cards, each with a badge (Model Explainer / Reparametrization / Speculative) *and* a "Level" field. The Level field carries two incompatible vocabularies at once: three cards say Beginner/Advanced (difficulty), four say "Artifact Lesson" (epistemic status). A reader scanning that column can't sort by either. The intro text does the right thing by distinguishing "content grouping tags" from "validation verdicts" — but then the card layout re-merges them in the same slot.

Also a straight factual mismatch: the Galaxy Curve Plotter card says *"See four curves"* and enumerates four (Newtonian, observed, Synchronism violet, MOND green). The page plots **five** — the fifth is the amber dotted hand-tuned stand-in, which the page describes at length. The card was written before the amber curve was split out (the page dates that recolor to 2026-07-23) and never updated.

**Tools tested.** I took the two Pass 1 didn't: γ Calculator and Phase Boundary Visualizer.

The **γ Calculator** is the cleanest single page I found. One input (Ncorr, default 4, linear 1–1000 or log 1–10⁷, dimensionless), one output (γ = 2/√Ncorr → 1.0000), a regime readout ("Boundary (γ ≈ 1)"), and — the part I want to praise specifically — a **numbered list of four caveats** stating that the formula is motivated rather than derived, that the direction of the mapping is inverted, that the preset values are back-fits, and that the framework's own galaxy parameters refute the formula. That's how you document a known-bad component. Every tool on this site should copy that structure.

The **Phase Boundary Visualizer** works and is honest about being an "artifact lesson," but it's where the terminology drift bit me. It names its three regimes **High-Ncorr (γ < 0.6) / Boundary (γ ≈ 1) / Single-particle (γ > 1.4)**. The Coherence Explorer names the identical range **"Collective (γ < 0.6)"**. Same boundary, same number, different noun — and neither noun is in the glossary. I also hit a usability wall: the slider runs 0 to 4, and the BCS/superconductor marker sits at γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, labelled *"~3 OOM off-scale left."* The tool's headline lesson is the superconductor sign inversion, and the superconductors are not on the chart. (OOM is also never expanded.)

**Learning paths.** Seven paths, every step linked, durations given, audiences described. Structurally the best-built page on the site. Two flaws: pages appear on multiple paths (Honest Assessment is Beginner step 5 *and* Methodology step 6; Coherence Function is Intermediate step 1 *and* Physics step 1), but the prev/next footer — I checked it on `/mrh`, which shows "← Prev: The Coherence Function / Next: Measurement Without Observers →" — can only encode one path and doesn't say which. A reader on the Intermediate path who lands on a shared page gets silently switched onto the Physics track. Second: I'll corroborate Pass 1's loop, from the IA side rather than the reading side — Beginner step 5 (Honest Assessment) routes non-physicists back to step 1, and Beginner step 6 is the Glossary. A path that ends on a reference work hasn't ended; it's stopped.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The math on this site is arithmetically clean and the self-audit is better than most published papers, but the framework's two load-bearing pieces — the γ(N_corr) relation and the boost ceiling B ≤ 1/Ω_m — are mutually inconsistent with the galaxy sector they're used on, and nobody has cross-multiplied them.

### Journey

I came in expecting to spend twenty minutes finding the reparametrization and leaving. Instead I spent the whole session on internal consistency, because the site had already found most of the reparametrizations itself. So this pass is mostly *checking the site's own math*, and the interesting failures are the ones where two correct pages disagree.

**First thing I did: kill the tanh.**

The site presents `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` as a mean-field-motivated sigmoid, with the Ising self-consistency equation `m = tanh(βJzm)` cited as the historical motivation. That motivation is void, and not for the reason the page gives.

Write `x = ρ/ρcrit`. Then `tanh(u) = (e^{2u} − 1)/(e^{2u} + 1)`, and `u = γ ln(1+x)` gives `e^{2u} = (1+x)^{2γ}`. So:

> **C(x) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1] / [(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]**

The `ln` inside exactly inverts the exponential inside `tanh`. There is no transcendental function here at all — this is a **one-parameter algebraic family**, a rational function of a power of (1+x). Whatever tanh contributes as a mean-field object (it's the Brillouin/Langevin function for spin-½, it arises from summing a two-state Boltzmann factor) is annihilated by the log that was placed inside it. The page's stated objection — that C is *evaluated* rather than *solved self-consistently* — is correct but secondary. The primary objection is that the composition is cosmetic. `/coherence-function` says tanh is "motivated, not derived"; it should say tanh **isn't there**.

Two immediate consequences the site half-has:

1. At **γ = 1/2**: `C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2)`, MOND's simple interpolating function exactly. The site states this on `/for-researchers`, correctly and prominently — credit where due. But it states only the γ=1/2 case. The general statement is stronger: setting `w = (1+x)^{2γ} − 1`, we get `C = w/(w+2) = μ_simple(w/2)` **for every γ**. So the entire family is MOND's simple μ composed with a monotone power-law remap of its argument. γ is not a new shape degree of freedom; it's a change of variable. That collapses the "which compander?" question the form-selection table spends effort on — every member of the family is μ_simple in disguise, and the *only* structural difference from MOND is the one the site already identifies: the argument is ρ, not g_bar.

2. **C is strictly concave on ρ > 0, for every γ.** Differentiating: `C'' = −γ(1+x)^{-2} sech²(γ ln(1+x)) · [2γ tanh(γ ln(1+x)) + 1] < 0` for all x > 0, γ > 0. There is no inflection point at any density. The S-curve everyone is looking at exists only in `log ρ`, where the inflection sits at x = 0 — i.e. at **zero density**, the boundary of the domain, not at ρcrit. The "phase boundary" imagery is a log-axis artifact.

That settles Pass 2's open question #1 with a proof rather than a vote: **ρcrit is not a critical density.** C is real-analytic and monotone on ρ ∈ (0,∞) with no singularity, no bifurcation, no non-analyticity, no diverging length. `/critical-density`'s opening sentence — "the density at which the coherence function transitions from quantum (C → 0) to classical (C → 1)" — is false as stated: C → 0 only as ρ → 0 and C → 1 only as ρ → ∞, both asymptotically, and neither happens *at* ρcrit. What actually happens at ρcrit is

> **C(ρcrit) = (2^{2γ} − 1)/(2^{2γ} + 1)** — which is **γ-dependent**: 0.327 at γ = 0.49, 0.882 at γ = 2.

So ρcrit doesn't even mark a fixed coherence value, let alone a transition. The Coherence Explorer and glossary calling it a "saturation knee" is the physically correct call, and `/critical-density` should be renamed rather than argued with.

**Then the thing that made me sit up.**

`/parameter-derivations` gives γ = 2/√N_corr. `/galaxy-curve-plotter` and the RAR refutation pin **γ = 2** for galaxies. Invert: γ = 2 ⟺ **N_corr = 1**.

The framework's galaxy sector asserts that a spiral disk is a **single uncorrelated degree of freedom**. Meanwhile the SPARC free-γ optimum, γ ≈ 0.489, gives N_corr ≈ 16.7 — seventeen correlated DOF in a galaxy. And if you take the γ relation at face value for a disk of ~10¹⁰ stars, γ ≈ 2×10⁻⁵ and the coherence at galactic densities is C ~ 10⁻⁸.

The two central relations of the framework are applied to the same object and never to each other. No page on the site multiplies γ = 2/√N_corr by the galaxy sector. This isn't a subtle audit finding — it's one substitution, and it says the pinned γ that carries refutation #1 (ΔBIC = +184) describes a one-particle system.

While I'm here: the sign inversion the site flags in its own γ caveats has a clean fix it doesn't mention. The site's CLT argument is that fluctuations scale as 1/√N. But the transition *width* in the control variable is ~1/γ, so "width ∝ 1/√N" reads as **γ ∝ √N**, not γ ∝ 1/√N. The formula looks like the intended relation written upside down. I want to be direct: that is **Bekenstein & Milgrom (1984) AQUAL**, `∇·[μ(|∇Φ|/a₀)∇Φ] = 4πGρ`, with `μ(|∇Φ|/a₀) → C(ρ)`. The site says the substitution *is* the whole difference and is correct to say so. But AQUAL is not cited on the page, and neither is the class of theories in which the coupling depends on local density — **chameleon (Khoury & Weltman 2004), symmetron (Hinterbichler & Khoury 2010), environment-dependent dilaton, Blanchet's gravitational polarization / dipolar dark matter (2007–2009)**. `/what-synchronism-is-not` mentions none of them. This is the framework's single most important prior-art exposure and the one place the citation list is thin.

That matters more than a missing footnote, because the screening literature already knows the punchline. The site's "locality no-go" — a ρ-keyed modification cannot reproduce a law organized in acceleration space — is a rediscovery of why density-screened gravity does not generate MOND phenomenology. Honestly rediscovered, clearly stated, badged. But rediscovered.

Then something more interesting happened. I took the field equation seriously and it closed the site's own biggest open question.

**The force-law "fork" is not a fork.** `/galaxy-plotter` presents two branches as symmetric candidates — quadrature `v² = v_b² + [V_flat·C]²` and division `g_obs = g_bar/C` — "refuted by the same galaxy from opposite sides." But integrate the site's own stated field equation over a ball:

∮ C∇Φ·dA = 4πG M(<r) ⟹ C(r)·4πr²·Φ′(r) = 4πG M(<r) ⟹ **g = GM(<r)/(C r²) = g_bar / C**

The division branch is *forced* by `∇·[C∇Φ]=4πGρ`. The quadrature branch is not a solution of that equation in any geometry, and cannot be: `V_flat` does not appear in the field equation. `V_flat` is the observed asymptotic velocity — a fitted property of the curve being predicted. The quadrature branch is a fitting function that takes the answer as an input.

So the fork that `/tier-1-existing` says blocks adjudication, and that three of the six refutations reportedly depend on, is closable by one paragraph of vector calculus using an equation already published on the site. Drop quadrature, keep division, annotate the six. I'd rank this the highest-value single edit available.

Two more things fall out of the same equation, and the site states neither:

1. **EFE = 0 is a theorem here, not an assumption.** C depends on ρ, not on ∇Φ, so the operator is *linear in Φ* at fixed matter distribution. Superposition holds; a constant external field adds and cancels. The site treats EFE=0 as a modelling choice. It is forced.
2. **But there is an external *density* effect.** C keys on total local ρ *including ambient medium*. Two satellites at matched external acceleration but different ambient gas density get different C. MOND+EFE keys on g_ext and predicts no such split. **That is a clean, live, unregistered discriminator**, runnable on existing SAGA/ELVES satellite samples.

I then went hunting for where ρ and g_bar decouple, because that is where the "strict submodel of MOND" claim must break. It breaks in at least three places with public data, and the catalog contains none of them:

- **Vertical disk structure.** At fixed R, ρ(z) drops ~1 dex per ~300 pc while the in-plane g_bar is flat and K_z rises then saturates. MOND boosts on total acceleration; C(ρ) boosts on ρ(z). These give qualitatively different K_z(z). Gaia DR3 vertical-Jeans / Σ(|z|<1.1 kpc) measurements exist now. This is a Tier-1, existing-data, zero-cost test.
- **Globular clusters vs. diffuse dwarfs at matched internal acceleration.** Same g_int, ρ differing by many orders of magnitude — a *pure* C-vs-μ discriminator by construction. And here the two theories say opposite things: for MOND, GC internal dynamics are EFE-dominated; for this framework, EFE=0 (see above), so GCs are clean.
- **Molecular clouds / GMC internal dynamics.** ρ is 10³–10⁶× the disk mean at still-low accelerations. C(ρ) ⟹ Newtonian (C→1, no boost); MOND ⟹ boost keyed to internal a. Larson-relation and virial-parameter data are decades old.

`/for-researchers` says "0 tests could have selected Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." Every registered galaxy-sector test is a **rotation curve in the disk plane**, which is precisely the regime where Σ(r) and g_bar(r) are both monotone and the two variables are degenerate by construction. So "0 of 24 could select" is currently a statement about the **catalog's regime coverage**, not about the framework. That is an *under*-claim, and it's the kind that costs a program its only remaining discriminating tests.

Last stop was the dark-energy sector, where I found a page-to-page disagreement that a referee would catch immediately. `/honest-assessment` writes the DESI DR2 result up as an executed no-go — "0 of 192 γ values reach the DESI quadrant," "3.4–6.3σ" — while `/test-catalog` registers TEST-26 as *prospective on DR3*. Those cannot both be right. And the number is over-committed on three counts, detailed below.

### Detailed Findings

**1. The DESI dark-energy no-go over-refutes, three ways.**
- *Statistics.* Quoting "3.4–6.3σ on DESI's own σ_wa" uses the marginal error on wa. w₀ and wa are strongly anticorrelated in the DESI posterior (ρ ≈ −0.9); the distance along the degeneracy direction is what matters. The correct statement is a Δχ² over the joint (w₀, wa) contour, or better, a model-vs-ΛCDM comparison. Marginal-σ on the correlated parameter is the classic way to manufacture a 6σ from a 2σ.
- *State of the art.* DESI DR2's own preference for evolving DE is **2.8σ (Pantheon+), 3.8σ (Union3), 4.2σ (DESY5)** — SNe-compilation dependent and still contested in the literature. A kill quoted up to 6.3σ against a signal whose own significance is 2.8–4.2σ and dataset-dependent is claiming more precision than the target has.
- *Specificity.* "w(z) runs monotonically to −1, so no phantom crossing" is the **generic single-field no-go** (Vikman 2005) — thawing quintessence has the same problem. DR2 disfavours a *class*; Synchronism is in it. This is not a Synchronism-specific refutation and should be badged the way TEST-25 already correctly is ("not a Synchronism-specific refutation").

**2. Undisclosed second theory in the DE sector.** The no-go is quoted "at every Brans–Dicke ω tested." Brans–Dicke appears nowhere on `/for-researchers`, whose field equation is `∇·[C∇Φ]=4πGρ`. So the DE sector runs on a covariant completion the theory page does not mention, and a reader cannot tell whether the galaxy sector and the DE sector are the same theory. The refutation ledger currently sums results from two possibly different theories under one count.

**3. TEST-10's kill rests on a sample maximum.** `f_DM,max ≤ 1 − Ω_m = 0.685`, median observed 0.755, max 0.927, "requires B ≥ 13.7." Two problems. (a) `/parameter-derivations` itself says the Ω_m vs Ω_m/Ω_b choice is ambiguous; at `1 − Ω_b/Ω_m = 0.843` the **median passes** and only the tail fails. (b) The surviving kill then rests on 0.927 — a **sample maximum**, an order statistic with unbounded sensitivity to one bad inclination or distance. I would not accept a refutation on a max in review. Report the exceedance *fraction* under both ceilings with a bootstrap CI.

**4. A2ACW has no positive control, and that is the whole methodology.** The two reported controls (temporal-asymmetry 0/6, vocabulary-asymmetry 4/6) both test whether A2ACW catches *demotions*. Neither tests whether it would **pass a genuine discovery**. Feed it six historically-validated discoveries in period-appropriate vocabulary. If it demotes those too, the 1.4% survival rate and 0/6 novelty rate are **instrument-limited** and the honest conclusion is "A2ACW has not been shown to discriminate novel from reparametrized physics" — a statement about the tool, not about Synchronism. As currently presented, 0/6 is read as a finding about the framework; without the positive arm it is not.

Also, the denominator: **3,308 sessions is not a sample size.** They are a sequential dialogue over a shared, evolving claim set, not independent trials. Printing "1.4%" beside it invites a reading it cannot support. And note the specificity failure that matters most: the vocabulary-asymmetry arm caught 4/6 prior-art demotions — but it did **not** catch the framework's own headline structural claim, which is AQUAL-with-a-substitution inside the screening literature.

**5. `/mrh` compares itself to the wrong prior art.** The page distinguishes MRH from Markov blankets by being "dynamical and scale-dependent." But the stated relation `P(X_fut | X_MRH, X_ext) = P(X_fut | X_MRH)` *is* the Markov blanket definition; "dynamical" changes nothing in the formula as written. Meanwhile the page's comparison list omits the actual strongest prior art: **Wilsonian RG and the EFT of large-scale structure**, where a dynamical, scale-dependent boundary below which degrees of freedom are integrated out is literally the defining construction. The hole in the comparison list sits exactly where the competition is.

**6. a₀ = cH₀/2π — the site is right, and could go further.** `/parameter-derivations` handles this well (Milgrom 1983, McCulloch, Verlinde, Smolin cited; badged Reparametrization). But `/key-claims`'s "6% low at SH0ES H₀ = 73" runs the inference backwards. If a₀ ∝ H₀, then a₀ fitted from the RAR is an **H₀-meter** — a bad one (±20% systematic), but the framing is more honest *and* more interesting than reporting a percentage offset. Same for Σ₀ = a₀/2πG, correctly badged as Milgrom's Σ_M.

**7. Cassini/TEST-25 needs its citation.** The +17.95σ solar-system squeeze against the tanh-log compander family is the same physics as **Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024)**, which excludes simple-μ-class interpolating functions for MOND too. The page correctly badges it non-discriminating but does not name the paper that already did it. Attribution here is cheap and it strengthens the badge.

### Tools Tested

- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — worked — clear UI, **wrong statistical test** — The instinct to build an explicit null is better than most published work, and the caveats (multiple comparisons, non-independence, monotonicity in Z) are all correct and all volunteered. But the executed comparison — data-r vs Z-polynomial-r, |Δr| ≤ 0.07 — cannot answer the question. When both γ and the target are near-monotone in Z, *any* smooth monotone function of Z scores r ≈ 1; the page says so itself and then treats the resulting near-tie as the result. The quantity that decides it is the **partial correlation r(γ, target | Z)**, which is one line of code and is not on the page. Second defect: showing 23 hand-curated top rows and disclosing the selection bias underneath is the wrong order of operations — the honest artifact is the full 1,703-point r-histogram with the null's distribution overlaid, and it would be a *better* display, not just a fairer one. Third: r = 0.000 appears twice, exactly, which I'd want to verify computes rather than defaults.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — the most scientifically candid tool I have seen on a framework site, and still two fixable defects — What it gets right is remarkable: every curve's formula printed, the hand-tuned stand-in recoloured and labelled as illustration, the per-galaxy-ρcrit vs single-global-a₀ parameter-count asymmetry stated in the framework's own voice, and the non-locality barrier spelled out. I checked the physics rather than the arithmetic. (a) **The disclosed force-law fork is not a fork** — Gauss's theorem on the site's own field equation forces `g = g_bar/C`, and quadrature takes `V_flat` (the observed answer) as an input, so it isn't a candidate theory. The tool presents them as symmetric, and that framing has propagated into the refutation ledger. (b) **The baryonic curve is a toy** — `M_b = 47·V_flat⁴` with fixed `h = 0.3 kpc` across DDO 154 through NGC 7331, when the real SPARC mass models are public. Any apparent near-overlap is partly the mass model's doing. On the positive side, I checked robustness the page doesn't claim: at γ = 2 and small x, C ≈ 2ρ/ρcrit, so max C ∝ 1/h — the "max C = 0.001, the knee never turns on" result survives ~3 orders of magnitude of error in the scale height. That robustness is a stronger claim than the number and the page should make it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is the field equation `∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ]=4πGρ` the theory, or is the Brans–Dicke completion used in the DE sector the theory? If both, do they agree in the weak field — and if not, what does "6 refutations" count?
2. Has anyone integrated the field equation over a ball? Because it closes the force-law fork that the site says is blocking three of its six refutations.
3. Why is there no test in a regime where ρ and g_bar decouple? Vertical structure, GCs at matched g_int, and GMC dynamics are all existing-data and all directly probe the framework's own stated difference from MOND.
4. What would A2ACW do with a real discovery? Until the positive control runs, "0 of 6 novel" is a statement about the instrument.
5. Does the framework know it is in the screened-modified-gravity family? The locality no-go it derived independently is a known result there.
6. If EFE = 0 is forced by the linearity of the operator, is the external *density* effect the framework's only genuinely non-MOND prediction — and why is it unregistered?

### Verdict

This is the most epistemically disciplined framework site I have reviewed, and the discipline has developed a characteristic blind spot: it is much better at auditing *results* than at auditing *coverage*. Six refutations are documented to a standard I'd accept in a referee report — and the ledger they populate rests on a force law the site describes as unresolved, when its own published field equation resolves it in two lines; on a DE sector running an undisclosed covariant completion; and on a test catalog whose galaxy arm samples exclusively the regime where the framework is degenerate with MOND by construction. The self-criticism is real but it is aimed almost entirely inward at numbers already computed, and the over-refutation is now the more common error than the over-claim.

The genuinely new content is one substitution inside Bekenstein–Milgrom's 1984 equation, and that substitution places the framework in the chameleon/symmetron family, which the site does not cite. That's the bad news for the novelty claim. The good news is larger than the site currently believes: `μ(g_bar) → C(ρ)` is a *real* structural difference, it is not nested in MOND outside thin-disk rotation curves, and it makes at least four predictions MOND cannot make — vertical K_z(z) profiles, globular-cluster vs. diffuse-dwarf dynamics at matched internal acceleration, GMC internal dynamics, and an external *density* effect at matched external field. All four are runnable on public data. "0 of 24 tests could select Synchronism" is not a fact about the theory. It is a fact about which 24 tests were written, and it is fixable this month.
