## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The honesty is genuinely disarming and the marching-band analogy landed instantly, but the site keeps telling me its central word means the opposite of what I'd assume, and never tells me why I — a non-scientist — should keep reading once I learn the idea lost.

### Journey

I got here from a Reddit comment. Landing page headline: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — that's exactly the kind of thing I click. Subhead immediately walks it back: *"An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything."* I respected that, and also felt slightly deflated, in that order.

Then, maybe four inches down the page, there it is:

`C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`

I don't know what tanh is. I sort of know ln is "the log button next to the other log button." The letters are Greek and I couldn't pronounce two of them out loud. On a page whose whole pitch is *one simple idea*, showing me the equation before showing me the idea is backwards. I nearly bounced right here and I want to be clear that I only didn't because I was curious what "0 confirmed predictions / 6 refutations" meant — that scoreboard is the single most interesting thing on the front page and it's the thing that kept me.

Clicked **Start Here**. This is good writing. "Physics is fragmented, here's a single dial that might connect it." Crowd vs. marching band. I got it in about fifteen seconds. Step 1 of 6 — okay, I'll do the six.

**First Encounter** (step 2) was the best page on the site for me. Presence = how densely stuff is packed. The Markov Relevancy Horizon — which is a scary name — got explained as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," plus a line about how a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy. That is a *great* sentence. No equations. I was fully with it.

**The Core Idea** (step 3) is where I fell off the wagon. It starts fine — dimmer switch, C goes 0 to 1 — and then within the same page I hit "Hill-type saturation function," "the Ising self-consistency equation," "resembles central-limit-theorem scaling," and "Naka–Rushton curves." I don't know what any of those are and the page doesn't stop to tell me. That's step 3 of a beginner path. It felt like the beginner path ended without warning me.

Worse, I hit this line: **"Not quantum coherence — superconductors score _low_ here."** I've watched a video about superconductors. Magnets floating, electrons all moving in perfect lockstep, zero resistance. That is the most marching-band thing I have ever seen. This site just told me the marching band scores as a crowd. I read that three times and could not resolve it. Nobody explains *why* on the page — it just asserts it and moves on. That single sentence did more damage to my confidence than the equation did.

I backed out to **Equation Anatomy**, which turned out to be what the landing page should have shown me. It explains ln as "a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger" and tanh as a dimmer switch. Both of those work! Why is this on the tools page instead of on the front page under the equation? Also mildly confusing: this page says "Step 1 of 6" while the beginner path also counts to 6, so for a minute I thought I'd been kicked back to the start of something.

Then I went and played with tools, then read **Honest Assessment**, then clicked **Consciousness** because obviously I clicked consciousness.

Honest Assessment is, weirdly, the clearest page on the whole site. Plain sentences: it fails its own tests, it predicts the wrong relationship between galaxy mass and spin speed, and when you let it adjust freely it just turns into MOND — a forty-year-old idea that already existed. I understood all of that with no physics. Then at the very bottom: the point is that the self-audit happened at all.

Which brought me to my actual problem with this site: **that "why should you care" answer is at the bottom of page eleven.** The front page gives me a big exciting question and a scoreboard that says the answer is no. Nothing between those two things tells a casual reader what they're supposed to get out of this. Scientists presumably know why a public failure log is valuable. I had to dig to find out.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — confusing — The curve moves, the crowd/marching-band labels are there, but with unitless density on a log axis and a ρcrit slider that only slides the picture sideways, I couldn't tell you what I learned. I want to type in "water" and see where it lands.
- **γ Calculator** — worked — partly intuitive — The presets are genuinely fun: ideal gas is 2, a superconductor is 0.0006. And then the red honesty box explains that this is backwards from real physics — the superconductor, the sharpest transition in nature, gets the flattest number. I actually understood that! It's the clearest demonstration of a failure on the site. But it also left me thinking: if the formula is known to be inverted, why is the equation still on the front page with no asterisk?
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — confusing — The setup is beautifully explained ("the gap is the dark matter problem" — perfect). The payoff is not. Five overlapping lines, one of them admittedly fabricated for illustration, and the conclusion softened by disclaimers. I had to read the text carefully to learn that the violet line loses.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Why does a superconductor score *low* on a scale whose top end is described as "everything moving together"? This is never explained, only repeated.
2. If the γ formula is documented as backwards, is the whole equation broken, or just that one piece? The site never tells me how much of the idea survives.
3. What is "coherence" actually measuring, in a sentence a non-scientist can repeat to a friend? I read four pages and still can't do it without saying "marching band," which the site says is wrong.
4. What is MOND? The Honest Assessment leans on "it just becomes MOND" as the punchline and I only know it's an old alternative to dark matter because that page said so in passing.
5. What am I, a curious person with no physics, supposed to *do* with this site? Learn a cool idea? Watch a theory die honestly? Both are fine — just tell me up front.
6. Six refutations "with 3–4 independent roots" — I saw that phrase repeatedly and never learned what a "root" is. Is six failures a lot? Is it three? The number seems to change depending on the page.

### Verdict

I came for "one equation explains everything" and stayed for something I didn't expect: a site that shows its own work failing, in public, with a scoreboard on the front page. That's rare and I liked it. But the site is currently written for people who already know why that's admirable — a casual reader gets the exciting question, then the bad news, and has to travel eleven pages to find out why the bad news is the interesting part.

Two fixes would change my experience completely: put the plain-English explanation of the equation *above* the equation, and explain the superconductor thing. Right now the site's own best analogy contradicts its own most-repeated caveat, and I bounced off that contradiction harder than off any of the math.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: This site has a genuinely excellent controlled vocabulary — roughly 30 precisely-defined status terms — buried as an unlinked mid-page section of a page most readers never reach, while the summary card on the landing page grades all three flagship claims one notch more favorably than the claim page it links to.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a rewrite: walk the nav, check every label against its destination, look up terms the way a reader would, and see whether the information architecture holds.

**The nav is well-built.** Nine top-level items, each with a descriptive tooltip, plus a footer that repeats the important ones. Breadcrumbs are present on every content page I hit. Learning paths are explicit and — this surprised me — the step numbering is *correct*. The landing page advertises "Deep dive · Advanced Path step 8 of 8" for Test Roadmap, and /learning-paths lists Test Roadmap as Advanced step 8. "Methodology Track step 1 of 6" for Research Philosophy: matches. "Beginner Path step 5 of 6" for Honest Assessment: matches. I cross-checked six of these and all six were right. That is unusual and someone deserves credit for it.

**Then I checked the labels against their destinations, and that's where it fell apart.**

The landing page has a card headed *"Where does Synchronism move the needle?"* with three items:

1. "new ontology with **testable consequences**"
2. "1 **untested**"
3. "1 galaxy rotation **reparametrization**"

I clicked through to /key-claims, which is where those three claims live in full. The badges there read:

1. "Speculative — **untestable as stated** — ontological reframe"
2. "Speculative (parent claim, **unfalsifiable as stated**)"
3. "**Failed**"

Every one of the three is softer on the landing page than on the destination page, and item 1 is a direct contradiction: "testable consequences" versus "untestable as stated," about the same claim, two clicks apart. Item 3 downgrades "Failed" — an adjudicated verdict — to "reparametrization," which the site's own legend defines as a *different thing* ("equivalent to existing physics in different notation"). For a site whose entire editorial premise is not softening bad news, this summary card is doing exactly that, and I don't think it's deliberate — it reads like a card that was written once and never re-synced when the claim badges were re-adjudicated.

**Then I went looking for what the badges mean.**

/research-philosophy has a section called "Validation Badge Taxonomy." It lists eleven badges in three families and defines none of them — it points at a canonical legend elsewhere. /key-claims says: *"Status labels (Untested, Speculative, etc.) follow the site's validation badge taxonomy — canonical reference on the Honest Assessment page."* The badges themselves are not clickable; I checked.

The canonical legend turns out to be a section of /honest-assessment, and it is **very good**. Nine badges in two families, two deprecated with migration instructions, nine "operational states," nine "status lifecycle verbs," each with a one-line definition and several with date stamps. This is better vocabulary discipline than most commercial documentation sets I've audited. It is also sitting in the middle of a long page that is Beginner Path step 5 of 6, reachable only by scrolling, with no anchor link from any of the ~29 places its terms are actually used.

So I did what a reader does and tried the glossary instead — which the site presents as Beginner step 6 of 6 and as the sitewide lookup. /tier-1-existing uses **19 distinct status tokens** across its twelve test rows. I checked them against the glossary. **Seven have entries. Twelve do not** — including SELF-ELIMINATING-OR-TIE, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, CEILING EXCEEDED, NULL-CLASS, ARTIFACT LESSON, PROTOCOL DEVIATION, DEMOTED TO TIER-2, RE-ADJUDICATED and RECLASSIFIED. A reader who hits "TEST-10 — FAILED, Ceiling Exceeded" and does the right thing has no path to what "Ceiling Exceeded" means.

**Then I found a token defined twice.** In the *same legend*, "Withdrawn" appears as an Operational State — *"Framework disowned the test — not from data refutation, but from internal contradiction, unmotivated amplitude, or supersession. Carries no badge"* — and again as a Status Lifecycle Verb — *"Applies to a test: retracted by the framework before execution."* Those aren't the same scope: the first covers supersession, the second is specifically pre-execution. /tier-1-existing marks TEST-04 "WITHDRAWN" and there is no way to tell which sense is meant. Two definitions, one token, one page.

**Counts hardcoded into the nav have drifted.** The header tooltip says "Test Roadmap — 24 proposed experiments by tier" and the page H1 reads "Untested — 24 Proposed Experiments." The page then lists TEST-25 (added 2026-07-28) and TEST-26 (proposed 2026-08-10), which it explicitly notes fall "outside the 24 count." It is 26. Separately, /test-catalog scopes Tier 1 as TEST-01–TEST-10 and summarizes "Zero Cost: 10," while /tier-1-existing opens with *"These 11 numbered tests (TEST-01 through TEST-10, plus TEST-25)"* — and one of those eleven, TEST-07, is labeled "DEMOTED TO TIER-2" while still occupying a Tier 1 row. The Glossary tooltip similarly hardcodes "72 terms defined."

**Terminology is overloaded in one place that matters.** On /interactive-tools, the tools carry tags — and two of those tags, "Reparametrization" and "Speculative," are *also* validation badges. Galaxy Curve Plotter is tagged "Reparametrization, Beginner." Is that the tool's category, or the verdict on the physics it plots? Both readings are supported by the site. Same for Consciousness Threshold Demo tagged "Speculative, Artifact Lesson."

I tested two tools. Then I checked the one definition every reader needs.

**The glossary defines Coherence as:** *"How collectively a group of elements behaves, from sparse/independent (0) to dense/collective (1)."* Meanwhile /phase-boundary-visualizer places superconductors, BECs, superfluids and Cooper pairs at the *low* end — and the site repeats the caveat "not quantum coherence, superconductors score low here" on several pages. A superconductor is the most collectively-behaving system the site names. The canonical glossary entry and the sitewide caveat cannot both be right as written. Pass 1 hit this from the analogy side; I hit it at the definition, which is worse, because the glossary is where a careful reader goes to resolve exactly that confusion.

### Tools Tested

- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — worked — intuitive, and unusually well-framed — This is the best-documented tool on the site. It states the reader's task explicitly ("catch the formula being wrong at every stop, not learn its placements"), carries a headline warning that it "animates a refuted relation," and includes a specific, checkable caveat: ideal gases and galaxies both land at γ = 2.0 because both are assigned N_corr = 1, "an artifact of how N_corr is counted." That is a tool that teaches you to distrust it, which is a hard thing to write and this one does it. Documentation gaps only: the γ axis isn't labeled as γ, the vertical axis isn't labeled at all, and "compander," "Cooper pairs," "ΔBIC" and "SPARC" all appear undefined on a page tagged for general tool users.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — worked — confusing — Filter tabs (All 23 / Neutral 4 / Coherence helps 12 / Incoherence helps 4 / Barrier-dominated 3) and a four-column sortable table. The caveats are strong and specific: 23 hand-curated rows out of 1,703 is flagged as a multiple-comparison artifact, and the null-model comparison is spelled out — a 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z matches within ±0.07 and sometimes wins. But the page never draws the conclusion those caveats add up to, and it never explains what the four filter categories *mean* ("Barrier-dominated" is a category name doing a lot of unexplained work). The heading is also the page's only structural element besides "Related Concepts" — there's no section framing between the table and the caveats. Compare to the Phase Boundary Visualizer, which tells you what to conclude in its first paragraph.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which is authoritative when the landing card and /key-claims disagree — and is there a process that keeps summary surfaces in sync with the badges they summarize, or is every card hand-maintained?
2. Which "Withdrawn" applies to TEST-04 — the supersession sense or the pre-execution sense? They imply different things about whether the test was ever viable.
3. Is Coherence "how collectively a group behaves" (glossary) or a density-driven classicality measure on which the most collective systems score lowest (everywhere else)? These need to be one sentence, not two pages apart.
4. Is Tier 1 ten tests or eleven, and is TEST-07 in it?
5. Why is the badge legend — the strongest piece of writing on this site — located inside step 5 of the Beginner Path rather than at a stable, linkable URL of its own?
6. Are the operational states and lifecycle verbs (EXECUTED, RE-ADJUDICATED, NULL-CLASS, CEILING EXCEEDED…) intended as reader-facing vocabulary or as internal audit shorthand? They're rendered as reader-facing chips on /tier-1-existing but are absent from the glossary, which suggests they were never scoped as public terms.

### Verdict

The vocabulary discipline here is real and better than most documentation sets I audit — roughly 30 status terms with precise, date-stamped definitions and an explicit deprecation-and-migration policy. The problem is entirely one of placement and plumbing: that legend has no URL of its own, no badge on the site links to it, the glossary only covers a third of it, and one of its terms is defined twice with different scope. Fix the linking and the site's structure would match the quality of its writing.

The one finding I'd escalate above all the navigation work is the landing-page claims card. On a site whose editorial identity is that failures stay visible, the summary surface currently reads one grade more favorably than the detail page it links to, on all three of its flagship claims — including "testable consequences" pointing at a badge that says "untestable as stated." That's not a nav problem, it's a credibility problem, and it's two clicks from the front door.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The self-audit is real and most of the math I checked is correct — but the site's headline number (6 refutations) is assembled two different ways on two different pages, one of its six is a theorem rather than a measurement, and the two flagship results it quotes side by side (ΔBIC = +184 and "free-γ → 0.49 = MOND") appear to belong to two different force laws.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project." I expected to bounce off it in five minutes. I didn't, and the reason is that /parameter-derivations exists and says, in its own words, *"zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations."* That is not a sentence you find on crackpot sites. So I did what I'd do with a referee assignment: recompute everything cheap.

**What checks out.** I verified more than I expected to.

- The Hill identity on /core-idea: tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. Correct — substitute e^u = (1+x)^γ into tanh. The asymptotic Hill index 2γ is also right.
- γ = 1/2 ⇒ C = x/(x+2). Correct, and it equals μ_simple(x/2) identically, so at γ = 1/2 the model **is** MOND's simple interpolating function with a₀ rescaled by 2. The site says this outright. Good.
- a₀ = cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 → 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Correct, and correctly attributed to prior art (Milgrom 1983; McCulloch, Verlinde, Smolin get the same factor).
- Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) → 118.7 M☉/pc². Correct, and the site correctly says this is "one number, not two" rather than independent evidence.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 18
- **Understanding achieved**: solid

- **Overall impression**: The galaxy sector's field equation — L2, ∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ, which the site says is "derived and used throughout" — is Refracted Gravity (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016), a published modified-gravity theory with rotation-curve fits, elliptical and cluster tests, and a covariant scalar-tensor completion, and it is cited nowhere on this site; once you see that, the ledger reads backwards, because the two things the framework actually adds to RG are the two things its refutations killed.

### Journey

I review for MNRAS and A&A. I came here expecting to spend ten minutes confirming it was MOND in a new coat, and I did confirm that — but not in the way the site itself claims, and the discrepancy is worth a referee report.

**Start with the honest question: what is new?** The site's own prior-art audits are good and unusually self-punishing. It correctly retires a₀ = cH₀/2π to Milgrom (1983), McCulloch, Verlinde. It correctly retires the entity criterion Γ < m to Breit–Wigner. It correctly retires shared-environment decoherence protection to Palma–Suominen–Ekert and resynchronisation to Viola–Lloyd dynamical decoupling. It correctly identifies that the free-γ SPARC fit lands on MOND's simple μ. That is four prior-art self-catches, which is four more than most preprints in this space manage.

**So I went looking for the one it missed, and it is the biggest one on the site.**

/honest-assessment now carries a correction saying the archive has stated field equations since 2025-12-01, and lists three:

- **L1** (postulated): ∇²Φ = 4πGρ/C — eliminated, because as ρ→0, C→γρ/ρcrit and the source ρ/C tends to a uniform density floor.
- **L2** (derived): **∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ**
- **L3** (spherical solution of L2): g = g_bar/C

L2 is the modified Poisson equation of **refracted gravity** (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016, arXiv:1603.04943): ∇·(ε∇Φ) = 4πGρ, where ε — the *gravitational permittivity* — is a universal monotonic function of the **local mass density** that boosts the field in low-density environments. The correspondence is not an analogy. It is the same equation with C ≡ ε, and C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) is the same object as RG's ε(ρ): a smooth step in log density with three parameters — a low-density floor, a threshold, and a sharpness. The framework's floor is B_max = 1/Ω_m ⇒ C_min = Ω_m; RG's is ε₀. The framework's threshold is ρ_crit; RG's is ρ_c. The framework's sharpness is γ; RG's is Q.

RG is not an obscure preprint. It has:
- rotation curves *and* vertical velocity dispersions for 30 DiskMass Survey galaxies, with mean best fit **ε₀ = 0.089 (+0.038/−0.035), Q = 0.47 (+0.29/−0.21), log₁₀[ρ_c / g cm⁻³] = −24.25 (+0.28/−0.20)** (Cesare et al. 2020, A&A 637, A70), with M/L consistent with SPS models;
- three nearby E0 ellipticals with parameters consistent with the disc sample, supporting a universal permittivity (Cesare et al. 2022, A&A 657, A133);
- a **covariant formulation** — a scalar-tensor theory whose scalar mimics dark matter *and* drives accelerated expansion, with an emergent acceleration scale a_Ξ ~ (2Ξ)^{1/2} ~ 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² (Sanna et al. 2023, A&A, arXiv:2109.11217);
- a galaxy-cluster kinematics test (A&A 2025).

/for-researchers carries a 33-entry citation list — Skordis & Złośnik AeST, Berezhiani & Khoury, Hinterbichler & Khoury, Joyce et al., Naik et al., Burrage et al. ×3, Babichev–Deffayet–Esposito-Farèse, Bekenstein–Milgrom. It is a genuinely well-read list. It contains **zero** RG references, and the words "refracted gravity" and "gravitational permittivity" appear nowhere on that page or on /dark-matter. The one published theory that occupies the exact niche the site claims to have eliminated is the one absent from the bibliography.

**Now the consequences, which are what make this worth a high severity rather than a footnote.**

**(a) The flagship "citable negative result" is scoped narrower than it is sold.** /for-researchers states the local-density no-go applies "exclusively to modifications using *algebraic* local-density coupling of the form C(ρ)·g as a multiplicative scalar on the force," explicitly excluding gradient schemes, non-local state variables and acceleration-keyed relations. That scope is honest and correct. But /honest-assessment sells the same result as *"knowing what a density-based coherence function structurally cannot do… eliminates a class of modified-gravity ideas in one stroke."* Those are different claims. And the framework's **own** field equation is on the wrong side of the line: L2 puts C **inside the divergence**, which is not "C(ρ)·g as a multiplicative scalar on the force." L2 reduces to L3 only under spherical symmetry — and RG's entire galactic mechanism is the term that survives when you *drop* that assumption: for flat systems the ∇C·∇Φ term bends field lines toward the disc plane and boosts the in-plane field. Disc galaxies are the least spherical objects in SPARC. So the site's no-go, and every galaxy refutation built on it, is executed on L3 — a solution branch that discards the only piece of L2 that does work in the literature. Whether that changes any verdict is an open computation, but nobody has run it, and the site currently reads as though L2 and L3 were interchangeable.

**(b) Read the ledger against RG and it inverts.** The site says "0 novel predictions; 6 refutations." Both halves cannot be true at once: if every ingredient is prior art, there is nothing framework-specific left to refute. Line the two theories up and the resolution is immediate — the framework differs from RG in exactly three places, and all three are the refuted ones.

Three observations follow, and I think they are the most useful thing I can give this project:

1. **B_max is not a prediction, it is the theory's regularisation constant with a cosmological number substituted into it.** L3 gives g = g_bar/C, which diverges as C→0 — you *must* impose a floor or the theory is undefined in the outskirts of every galaxy. RG calls that floor ε₀ and fits it. Synchronism sets it to Ω_m. That substitution is the framework's genuine novel content, it is falsifiable, and TEST-09/TEST-10 falsified it. Note what happens to those refutations at RG's fitted floor: the ceiling on dark-matter fraction becomes 1 − ε₀ = **91.1%** instead of 68.5%, so the site's quoted median f_DM = 0.755 passes comfortably and only the extreme tail (0.927) still exceeds. TEST-10 goes from "69% of SPARC exceeds the ceiling" to a marginal tail test. **The two refutations refute Ω_m-as-permittivity-floor, not density-keyed gravity.**
2. **ρ_crit ∝ V² is refuted, and RG supplies the option the site never lists.** /parameter-derivations enumerates three incompatible exponents — Jeans V^0.5, framework V^+2, MOND-matching V^−2. RG's answer is **V⁰**: one universal ρ_c across discs and ellipticals. Take the site's own knee at face value (assuming 0.029 is in M☉ pc⁻³ per (km/s)², which the site never states — Pass 3 flagged the same gap): at V = 100 km/s, ρ_crit = 290 M☉ pc⁻³, whereas RG's fitted ρ_c ≈ 8.3×10⁻³ M☉ pc⁻³. That is **~4.5 dex too high**, which is precisely, quantitatively, why the site finds "no galaxy of any mass crosses the knee." The framework didn't discover that density can't work; it discovered that its own knee is four and a half orders of magnitude above the empirically fitted one.
3. **γ ≈ 0.489 vs Q = 0.47 ± ~0.25.** The framework's free-parameter SPARC fit lands on RG's DiskMass fit for the corresponding shape parameter. The two enter their respective tanh's differently, so this is not an identity and I would not put it in a paper without checking the exact ε parameterisation — but if it survives that check it is a same-class-different-data replication, which is a *positive* result and the only one on the site. It is currently filed as evidence of collapse into MOND.

**(c) The dark-energy no-go is completion-dependent, and the published completion wasn't surveyed.** /honest-assessment reports "0 of 192 γ values reach the DESI quadrant" and w_a = +0.23…+0.60, "wrong sign in all four combinations (3.4–6.3σ on DESI's own σ_wa)." Two problems.

First, the covariant completion of *this exact Poisson equation* already exists and is a scalar-tensor theory in which the same scalar drives cosmic acceleration with a redshift-dependent effective w. The 192-value scan was run over the framework's home-grown completions; CRG is not among them. A no-go on a model class must survey the class's published member.

Second — and this is the one I would flag in a referee report regardless of RG — **"3.4–6.3σ on DESI's own σ_wa" is a distance from DESI's best fit, not an exclusion.** DESI DR2 prefers w₀w_aCDM over ΛCDM at roughly 2.8σ / 3.8σ / 4.2σ depending on which SN compilation is used (Pantheon+ / Union3 / DESY5); ΛCDM is not excluded. The site is over-refuting itself here, which is the mirror image of the error it exists to police — and it is the same error class as the ΔBIC = +184 that Pass 3 flagged for correlated-point inflation.

**(d) The A2ACW verdict has the same problem.** /for-researchers: "false-positive rate 100%, Youden's J = 0." /a2acw: "false-positive rate ≥~0.54," "Youden's J: 0," "prior-art rediscoveries caught 4/4." /research-philosophy: "4/6 caught by vocabulary-asymmetry," "0 of 6 demotions caught" by the temporal control. ### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — **physics internally inconsistent with the site's own field equation** — The MOND branch is right: ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) is the correct inverse of simple-μ. The framework branch is not the framework. It computes v² = v_b² + [V_flat·C]² — an additive graft whose amplitude is the *observed* V_flat and whose boost scales *with* C. The site's field equation L3 says g = g_bar/C, boost = 1/C, and f_DM = 1 − C — boost scales *inversely* with C. So the tool's headline lesson ("the disk sits below ρcrit everywhere, so the boost never turns on") is sign-inverted relative to L2/L3, where sitting below the knee pins every galaxy at the *maximum* boost 1/Ω_m = 3.17 and produces a uniformly rescaled but still-falling Newtonian curve. Both fail. They fail differently, and the site is showing the wrong failure. Separately, and concurring with Pass 3: the grey baseline uses spherical enclosed mass for an exponential disc, which is ~15% low in v at 2.2 R_d and inflates the very gap the tool exists to display.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — **the best-designed artifact on the site** — Eight approaches, mean 0.499, σ = 0.012, and the page's own verdict is that the convergence is a geometric artifact of taking the midpoint of a [0,1)-bounded range, that σ = 0.012 is "too small to be honest" and indicates shared derivation rather than independent validation, and that no calibration maps EEG/fMRI/IIT-Φ to the C-axis. It also carries a retraction banner for a fabricated p < 0.0001 that "entered in a 2026-06-23 editorial pass" with no repository support. I have reviewed papers that would not survive this page's standard. My one technical note: σ = 0.012 across 8 estimates is not merely "too tight" — it is testable. Under any plausible independent-estimator model the spread should scale as the estimators' own methodological dispersion; quoting the ratio (observed σ) / (bootstrapped σ under independence) would turn the rhetorical point into a number, and it would be the site's first *measured* demonstration of non-independence.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — worked — caveats correct, statistics incomplete — r = +0.982 for sound velocity, +0.979 electronegativity, +0.967 bulk modulus; 23 rows curated from 1,703; the null-model caveat and the multiple-comparison caveat are both present and both correctly worded. ### Unanswered Questions

1. **Has anyone compared this framework to refracted gravity?** If C ≡ ε, the site's galaxy sector is a re-derivation of a 2016 theory with ε₀ fixed to Ω_m instead of fitted — which is a legitimate (and now-refuted) variant, but must be framed as such.

4. **Is ρ_crit V-dependent at all?** RG fits a single universal ρ_c across discs and E0s. The site lists V^0.5, V^+2 and V^−2 as its three candidate exponents and never considers V⁰ — which is the only one with a published fit behind it.

### Verdict

This is the most rigorously self-audited crank-adjacent physics site I have ever read, and I mean that as a genuine compliment — the retraction banners, the audited-negative badges, the fabricated-p-value correction, and the γ Calculator's willingness to display its own contradiction are all better practice than a substantial fraction of the published literature. The site catches four prior-art collisions on itself. It does not catch the fifth, which is the field equation, and the field equation is the whole galaxy sector.

The correction is not "you're even more wrong than you thought." It is the opposite, and that is why I would come back. Once the framework is read as refracted gravity with the permittivity floor fixed to Ω_m and the threshold scaled as V² instead of held universal, the ledger stops being a list of six refutations of an unidentified object and becomes something much more useful: *two specific, novel, cleanly falsified parameter substitutions on a published theory, plus one unregistered structural commitment (EFE = 0).* That is a real, citable, negative result about a real theory. It is smaller than the site thinks its failure is, and much larger than the site thinks its contribution is.

My framing objection is the same one the site keeps almost making about itself: it has built beautiful machinery for auditing its own claims downward and none for auditing them upward. Every mechanism here — the badges, the demotion taxonomy, the A2ACW challenger role — is a ratchet that only turns toward "less." So the DESI number gets quoted as an exclusion when it is a distance, the Youden's J gets quoted as 0 when it is undefined, the ΔBIC gets quoted at +184 when the correlated-point correction is an order of magnitude, and the one prior-art hit that would have *raised* the site's estimate of its own coherence — an independent fit landing on a published parameter — never gets looked for. An honest ledger has to be able to move in both directions, and this one currently has a stop on one side.
