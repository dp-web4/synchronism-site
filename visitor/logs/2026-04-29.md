## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (plus 1 dead link)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Bold question, disarming honesty about failures, but the on-ramp from "cool question" to "I get the idea" has a broken step.

### Journey

I came in from a Reddit link cold. The landing page hooked me — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* with the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` right there. I don't know what tanh is, but the bold claims (dark matter without new particles, an equation for consciousness, "14,760 galaxies analyzed") plus the unusual line "Some predictions work. Some fail. All documented honestly" made me want to keep reading. That last bit is what pop-sci pages never say, so it bought trust.

I clicked **Get Started → First Encounter** because it sounded gentlest. The page said "One Equation, Every Scale" — but didn't actually show an equation. It talked about "presence" (which I sort of got: stuff packed into space) and "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)" (which I did not get at all — "compatibility boundary" doesn't help). I clicked the **Next →** button at the bottom. Nothing happened — the link had no destination. I tried guessing the URL `/first-encounter/the-claim` and got a 404. So the tutorial path just… stops, on step 1.

I went back and tried **"Need an analogy first?"** → /two-reframes. The CRT screen analogy genuinely clicked: a TV that flickers too fast to see looks "on" all the time, so quantum superposition might just be us being slow observers. That's the first thing today that gave me an *aha*. The pendulum-clock analogy for time dilation was less convincing — they admit you can't tell the difference experimentally, which made me wonder why I should prefer their framing.

I jumped to **Tools** and tried two:
- **Galaxy Plotter**: Pick a galaxy from a dropdown, see three lines. I gathered that real galaxies spin faster than gravity predicts, and their violet curve fits the data without invoking dark matter. Cool, but nobody told me what a "rotation curve" is.
- **Coherence Explorer**: Two sliders, a curve. I moved γ around and the curve got steeper. But I have no idea *why I should care* what coherence does at different densities. Felt like a math toy with no story.

I checked **Glossary** because terms like MRH, BTFR, RAR, CFD kept hitting me. Coherence and gamma had decent definitions. But "Presence" is defined using MRH, and MRH is defined using "state transitions" and "predictive sufficiency" — circular for someone without physics. **BTFR isn't in the glossary at all**, even though it appears elsewhere on the site. No search box.

Last stop: **Honest Assessment**. This was the most interesting page on the site. They flat-out say melting points were off by half, the "fractal coherence bridge" went 0-for-36, "0 confirmed predictions," "1.4% discovery rate is normal." That's the most refreshing thing I've read on a science site. But it sits weirdly next to the landing page, which sells me on "one equation describing reality." Two different vibes.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What does the equation actually *predict* in plain language? "Coherence goes up with density" — so what?
2. If MRH is so central, why doesn't the homepage have a one-sentence definition?
3. The honest assessment says zero confirmed predictions — is the framework's value in the *process* (generating questions) rather than the *answers*? If so, why doesn't the landing page lead with that?

### Verdict
The premise is genuinely intriguing and the failure-honesty is rare and valuable — those are the site's two strongest assets. But the casual on-ramp is broken: the very first tutorial page promises an equation it doesn't show, then the Next button doesn't work. I'd come back if a friend pushed me, but I wouldn't recommend it yet to my non-physics friends — they'd bail at step 1.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /learning-paths, /glossary, /test-catalog, /honest-assessment, /two-reframes, /coherence-function, /mrh, /parameter-derivations, /interactive-tools, /equation-walkthrough, /gamma-calculator)

### Journey

I worked methodically: top nav → "Get Started" branch → glossary → tools → predictions → assessment. The site has the *bones* of good IA — breadcrumbs are present and accurate on every page I checked, a glossary exists, learning paths offer three tracks, and validation categories are at least defined somewhere. That puts it ahead of most research microsites I audit. The problems are at the seams.

**Nav-to-URL mismatches.** Three label/URL drifts caught my eye in the first ten minutes. The top-nav item **"Tools"** points to `/interactive-tools` (label says "Tools," URL says something else — fine functionally, but it broke my mental model when I tried to remember the path). The top-nav item **"Predictions"** lands on `/test-catalog` (different word entirely). And on the tools index, the entry **"Coherence Demo"** points to `/consciousness-demo` — three different framings of the same thing on one page (label, URL, sidebar copy that calls it consciousness-related). For a site whose core asset is precise terminology, these small drifts compound trust issues.

**Acronym discipline is uneven.** The glossary covers the main ones (MOND, MRH, SPARC, RAR, presence, coherence, γ), but **BTFR** — which I saw on at least two other pages — isn't in the glossary, and there's no search box to confirm. On `/two-reframes`, I counted six undefined acronyms in a single page (CHSH, Tsirelson, LHV, MRH, QM, GR) before the second screenful. On `/parameter-derivations`, **SPARC** and **MOND** appear without expansion or glossary link. The glossary itself has minimal cross-linking between entries, so a reader who lands on "Presence" must manually find "MRH" — and "MRH" is defined using "state transitions" and "predictive sufficiency," neither of which is glossary-clickable.

**Validation-badge vocabulary forks.** This is the biggest IA issue I found. The site has *at least four* parallel labeling systems for claim status:

1. `/honest-assessment` defines six categories: Validated / Reparametrization / Speculative / Failed / Untested / [one more]
2. `/parameter-derivations` uses **different** labels: "Motivated Ansatz," "Motivated Choice," "Jeans Criterion | 5% Agreement," "Dimensional Analysis," "Freeman's Law Re-expressed," "3% Error" — with a note that two of these are sub-types of "Speculative" but the other four have no taxonomic home
3. `/test-catalog` uses **a third vocabulary**: "Defined / Completed / Zero Cost"
4. The landing page uses **percentages**: "89% Validated," "53% error"

A reader trying to internalize the site's epistemic stance has to hold four mental models. The `/honest-assessment` definitions section exists but is buried mid-page rather than serving as a global legend, and badges on other pages don't link back to it. There's no tooltip layer.

**Learning-path scaffolding is incomplete.** `/learning-paths` lists three tracks (Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced) with starting URLs but **no time estimates** for tracks (only the "10-minute" tag on First Encounter), **no prerequisites stated** for Intermediate or Advanced, and — confirmed by Pass 1 — **the Step 1 of 7 "Next" button on `/first-encounter` is dead**. So the structurally-promised linear path collapses on step 1 for the Beginner track. The other tracks may have similar issues; I didn't walk them step-by-step.

**Tone fork.** The landing copy ("One Equation, Every Scale," "89% Validated," "14,760 galaxies analyzed") and `/honest-assessment` ("0 confirmed predictions," failures documented in detail) are written as if by two different teams. They aren't connected by a bridge sentence on either page. This isn't bad — the honesty *is* a differentiator — but right now a reader has to discover the gap themselves.

### Tools Tested

- **Equation Walkthrough (`/equation-walkthrough`)** — worked — partially intuitive — labeled "Step 1 of 6," ← Previous / Next → buttons present, page-level promise that "each step adds one piece to the equation and explains why it's there" is a strong frame. But parameters shown in the equation aren't defined inline; the walkthrough leans on links out to `/parameter-derivations` and `/coherence-function` rather than embedding one-liners. For a guided tour this is the wrong direction — the tour should be self-contained.
- **γ Calculator (`/gamma-calculator`)** — worked — confusing — single input ("Ncorr (number of correlated particles)"), linear and log scaling, preset buttons. Output gives γ with a regime label (Quantum / Boundary / Strongly Correlated / Macroscopic Coherence). What's missing: a one-line "what this regime means physically" caption per output, no units stated (γ and Ncorr both dimensionless, but say so), and no audience guidance — who is this tool for? A reader who already knows what Ncorr means probably doesn't need the calculator; a reader who doesn't won't know what to type.

None of these are hard fixes individually; they're a cumulative debt from many independent additions. A focused two-day pass on terminology consistency and a global validation-badge legend would lift the site's apparent rigor by a full notch without touching any physics content.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/parameter-derivations`, `/coherence-function`, `/honest-assessment`, `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/equation-walkthrough`, `/mrh`, `/why-synchronism`, plus 2 tools)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the framework's claims and their epistemic status)
- **Would return?**: yes (specifically to see whether Tier-1 tests get run; the *process* is more interesting than the *physics* right now)
- **Overall impression**: An unusually self-aware reparametrization project with a tonal mismatch between its landing copy and its own self-assessment — the back end of the site already knows what the front end is still selling against.

### Journey

I came to this expecting either the standard TOE-crank pattern (one equation, every scale, no cost) or maybe a serious modified-gravity proposal. What I found is neither, and that's interesting in its own right.

The **landing page** is selling: "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" with C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) presented as the answer. Big numbers ("14,760 galaxies," "89% validated") flank the hero. As a TOE pitch it's the standard format. So I went straight to `/parameter-derivations` and `/honest-assessment` to see if there was anything underneath.

What I found at `/parameter-derivations` is the most surprising page on the site. It is, almost line-for-line, the page a careful referee would have *asked* the authors to write:

- **γ = 2/√N_corr**: badged "Motivated Ansatz." The text concedes the factor of 2 from "phase-space dimensionality (6D to 3 effective)" is "not rigorously derived" and that "integration introduces temperature- and mass-dependent factors, not a clean factor of 2." Translation: the √N_corr is central-limit-theorem flavor, the 2 is retrofitted, and the authors know it.
- **tanh form**: badged "Motivated Choice." The page invokes the mean-field Ising connection (m = tanh(βJzm)) and then *immediately concedes that C(ρ) lacks the self-consistency loop* that gives the Ising tanh its substance. They also note "other sigmoids (logistic, erf) share the same qualitative properties." A physicist reading this should take it as: tanh is a generic sigmoid here, not a derivation. The authors say so themselves.
- **a₀ = cH₀/(2π)**: badged "Dimensional Analysis." The page explicitly notes Milgrom (1983), McCulloch (2007), Verlinde (2017), and Smolin (2017) all arrive at the same dimensional relation. Synchronism does not own this relation and the page doesn't pretend to.
- **Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) ≈ 110 M☉/pc²** vs Freeman's empirical 124 M☉/pc² (12%): badged "Freeman's Law Re-expressed" with the explicit text "dimensional bookkeeping — expressing Freeman's empirical value via cosmological constants, not deriving it from physics." This was apparently *re-badged from "Validated"*, which is exactly the right move.
- **η reachability factor** = **Abrikosov–Gor'kov pair-breaking efficiency** (1960). All 23 superconductor predictions are "standard condensed matter in different notation."

This is unusually honest. Most framework-builders never identify their own reparametrizations.

The one piece of notation that genuinely worried me is **A = 4π/(α²GR₀²)**. Without context, "α²" next to "G" reads as the fine-structure constant — which would be a numerology disaster (and the page admits α_em gives A ≈ 550, off by ~20,000×). The α here is a *Jeans ratio* λ_Jeans/R_half. The page does state this explicitly, but the symbol choice is dangerous; any quick reader (or a screenshot pulled out of context) will misread it. Use a different letter. (See friction log.)

`/honest-assessment` then closes the loop with the bottom-line numbers any grad student needs: melting point 53% error, critical exponents 2× off, YBCO Tc predicted 607 K vs measured 93 K (6.5× wrong), fractal coherence bridge 0/7 boundaries on 36 tests, and "0 confirmed predictions" after 3,302 sessions. This is candid in a way that's almost disarming. The tonal collision with the landing page is the single biggest thing wrong with the site — *not* the physics.

`/coherence-function` did less well under inspection. The "mean-field justification for tanh" is hand-wavy: bounded, monotone, smooth saturation, correct limits. Those four constraints define an entire family — logistic, erf, atan/π — they don't pin down tanh. The page even admits this. The choice is aesthetic, not derived. There is no quantitative definition of presence ρ given on this page; "compatible structural elements within the system's MRH" is not an equation.

`/mrh` was where I stopped expecting derivations and started cataloging gaps. MRH is described as "minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence coherence evolution." That's prose, not math. The page claims wave-function collapse = MRH crossing, claims standard decoherence is a special case, but provides *no equation* differentiating MRH dynamics from the standard Lindblad/master-equation decoherence machinery. A physicist asking "what's the predictive content of MRH that GKSL doesn't have?" gets no answer.

`/test-catalog` and `/tier-1-existing` were the most productive pages. The framework has clearly *thought* about how to be falsified, even if it hasn't been falsified yet. Ten Tier-1 tests with named datasets (SPARC, Gaia DR3, ALFALFA-SDSS, DESI, Euclid), kill criteria, and zero-cost timelines. Three of them are genuinely discriminating against ΛCDM+MOND if the predictions are derived first-principles rather than retrofit:

- **TEST-04**: BAO peak shifts ~10⁻⁴ between high/low-density regions. ΛCDM predicts no such shift. Existing DESI + SDSS DR17 data should already constrain this.
- **TEST-07**: Galaxy cluster separations show oscillatory modulation at λ ~ 500 Mpc. Standard cosmology has no comparable oscillation at this scale.
- **TEST-02**: Wide-binary anomaly's environment dependence — the MOND community is already arguing about Gaia DR3 wide binaries, and adding a density-dependence prediction would be a useful third axis.

The thing I cannot verify from the public site is whether any of these predictions came *out of* the coherence function or were *fit to* observed phenomenology and then rationalized. That distinction is the entire scientific question. The 500 Mpc number especially needs a derivation page; I didn't find one.

I tested two tools.

The **γ Calculator** does what it says: γ = 2/√N_corr, gives Quantum / Boundary / Strongly Correlated / Macroscopic Coherence regimes with γ ≈ 1 the boundary at N_corr = 4. I ran N_corr = 1, 4, 100, 10²³ → 2.000, 1.000, 0.200, ~2×10⁻¹². The arithmetic is fine. The physics is the question. N_corr = 4 as the quantum/classical boundary is *not* a result that the standard literature recognizes; macroscopic quantum systems (superfluids, condensates, Cooper pairs) sit at N_corr ≫ 1 *and* exhibit quantum coherence. The "macroscopic coherence" label at low γ is doing a lot of work here, and the calculator doesn't expose the tension. Ncorr = 1 → γ = 2 labeled "Quantum" is also odd: a single uncorrelated particle is the most classical thing imaginable. The regime labels look reverse-engineered from desired behavior of C(ρ) rather than from N_corr semantics.

The **Coherence Explorer** plots C(ρ) cleanly with γ and ρ_crit as adjustable. It's a math sandbox: ρ and ρ_crit are dimensionless, no overlay of any actual physical data. Pedagogically fine for "look, this curve has a transition near γ = 1." Scientifically inert — a grad student can already integrate tanh in their head. Without a SPARC overlay or a chemistry-data overlay, the Coherence Explorer is a plot-the-equation tool, not an exploration of physics.
