## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /beginner, /why-synchronism, /two-reframes, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /honest-assessment, /key-claims, /consciousness)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The crowd-vs-marching-band idea clicked in about 20 seconds and I liked how openly they admit it didn't work — but I never figured out whether I was reading a theory or an obituary for one, and the word "coherence" means the opposite of what every physics YouTube video has taught me.

### Journey

I clicked through from Reddit. The headline — **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** — is a great hook. That's exactly the kind of thing I'd click.

Then directly under it: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`.

I'll be honest, my eyes slid right off that. I don't know what `tanh` is, I don't know what `ln` is, and there are two Greek letters. In the first three seconds of the page. I almost bounced right there — the only reason I didn't is that the subheading said "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything," which is a weirdly humble thing to say and made me curious.

Scrolling down I hit the scoreboard: **0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations**. And my honest reaction was: *wait, so it's wrong? Why does this website exist?* I mean that as a real question, not a snark. I've watched enough Veritasium to know that failed ideas are interesting, but nobody on the page told me *why this particular failure is worth 30 minutes of my time*. The site clearly thinks the failure is the point, but it never says that to me in plain language high up on the page. It says it in physics-jargon on the Honest Assessment page, four clicks later.

I clicked **"Beginner Path →"** because that's obviously for me.

It dropped me on a page that said **"Step 2 of 6"**. That was disorienting. I clicked the *beginning* of the path and landed in the middle? I backed up and found Step 1 ("Why Synchronism?") separately. Small thing, but it made me feel like I'd already missed something.

Step 1 was the best page on the site for me. **"Physics has a fragmentation problem."** Yes! I know that! That's the thing where quantum and gravity don't talk to each other, right? And then the crowd-in-a-plaza vs marching-band analogy. That's genuinely good. I understood it immediately and I could explain it to a friend. Density goes up, people stop acting like individuals and start acting like one thing. Got it.

Then Step 2 ("First Encounter") introduced **MRH — Markov Relevancy Horizon**. The plain-English gloss right after it ("the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter") is actually fine and I understood it. But the *name* is terrifying and I have no idea who Markov is or why he's in my bubble. If the bubble is the idea, call it the bubble.

I noticed a link that said *"Prefer analogies to equations? Try Two Reframes"* and I thought — yes, that's me, that's literally me. So I went.

**Two Reframes was my favorite page.** The CRT/old-TV thing — where the picture is stable if you look normally but is just one moving dot if you could see fast enough — is a genuinely great analogy and I think I actually understood something about superposition for the first time. (Minor thing: I'm 28 and I've never used a cathode ray tube. I know what one is from memes. A one-line "old tube TV" would've helped.) I also appreciated that the page told me straight up that the analogy table is "conceptual, not derived" — I don't know exactly what that means but I could tell it meant "we're hand-waving here" and I respected it.

Then I went to the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**, because dark matter is the thing I actually care about.

The setup text is excellent: *"pick a real galaxy and watch the gray line sag below the dots. That gap is the puzzle."* That is a perfect sentence. I picked NGC 3198, saw the gray line sag, saw the blue dots stay flat. **I got it.** That's the dark matter problem and now I've actually seen it instead of being told about it. Best moment on the whole site.

But then there are *five lines on the chart*, and two of them broke my brain:
- one is the framework's real prediction (which fails),
- one is a **hand-tuned illustrative stand-in**, which as far as I can tell is a made-up line that isn't a prediction of anything?
- and one is "MOND's **simple-ν interpolating function**," which is a string of words I cannot parse at all.

Why is there a fake line on the chart? I get that they're being honest by labeling it, but for me it just meant I couldn't tell which line I was supposed to be looking at. Three lines (visible matter / what we see / what the theory predicts) would have landed. Five made me feel like I was intruding on someone else's homework.

Then the **Coherence Explorer**. This is where I got genuinely confused, and I think it's a real problem, not just me being dumb.

The parameter is called **ρcrit — the "critical density."** In every pop-science thing I've ever watched, a *critical* value is the tipping point — the temperature where water boils, the point where the thing flips. So I assumed: at ρcrit, coherence should be 0.5, halfway, the flip point.

The tool's own readout says **C(ρcrit) ≈ 0.3272.**

So the critical density... isn't the critical point? The tooltip even says it "marks the saturation knee, not a true critical point" — but then *why is it called critical*? And the chart separately marks a "C = 0.50 midpoint" somewhere else entirely, which made it worse, because now there are two things that look like they should be the tipping point and they're in different places. I stared at this for a while and gave up.

Also the γ slider says it's "related to correlated particle count via **γ = 2/√Ncorr**." I have no idea what Ncorr is and the tool doesn't say.

Then I hit the real wall. **Honest Assessment.** I want to be clear that I *admire* this page — a website telling me its own idea failed, in detail, is the most trustworthy thing I've seen on the internet in a while. The superconductor line — *"607K predicted vs 93K actual"* — is fantastic. I understand that completely. It's off by a lot. Great.

But the page is a wall of **RAR, BTFR, SPARC, ΔBIC, σ₈, fσ₈, CHSH, a₀, ΛCDM**. I counted. In one page I hit ten acronyms I don't know, in sentences like "BTFR slope: 3.3σ deviation" and "RAR transition shape: ΔBIC=+184". I skimmed it and bounced. There's a glossary table *somewhere* apparently but it wasn't in front of me when I needed it.

Last stop: **consciousness**, because obviously. The page is titled "The Hard Problem: A Proposed Reframing" — and it assumes I know what "the hard problem" is. I sort of do, from a YouTube video, but it's never stated. Then it says mind = high-coherence phase patterns and there's "no gap to bridge."

And here's my biggest single confusion of the whole visit, which I only noticed by putting two pages side by side:

**The site says consciousness is HIGH coherence, and quantum stuff is LOW coherence.** But every science channel I've ever watched says *quantum coherence* is the fancy quantum superposition thing, and when it *breaks* ("decoherence") you get boring normal reality. This site has it exactly backwards from how I've heard the word used everywhere else. There's a section on Honest Assessment called "What 'Coherence' Does and Doesn't Mean Here," which suggests they know about this — but it's buried on the page most likely to scare me off, not on the beginner path where I formed the wrong idea in the first place.

I spent about 25 minutes total.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — *partly intuitive* — The galaxy dropdown and the core visual (gray line sagging below the blue dots) is the single most effective thing on the site; I learned something real in ten seconds. Ruined slightly by five simultaneous curves, one of which is explicitly a made-up stand-in. Axis labels ("kpc", "km/s") were fine — I know a kilometer per second is fast and I could infer kpc means "big distance."
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — *confusing* — Two sliders (γ default 0.49, ρcrit default 1.00) plus a log/linear toggle. The log/linear toggle meant nothing to me and I couldn't tell what changed. The three readouts (C at ρcrit, 10ρcrit, 100ρcrit) are the useful part — you can see the curve saturating. But the ρcrit-isn't-the-tipping-point thing (0.3272, not 0.50) actively taught me something wrong before correcting me, which is worse than not having the tool. The crowd/marching-band caption underneath is great and should be the *first* thing on the page.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the theory failed every test, what am I supposed to take away? Is the answer "nothing, and that's fine," or is there a surviving piece?
2. Why is `tanh` the right shape? The site keeps using it but I never saw a reason a beginner could hold onto.
3. Why is it called "critical density" if nothing critical happens there?
4. Is this site's "coherence" the same thing as the "quantum coherence" I hear about on YouTube, or a different thing with the same name? (I think it's the *opposite*, and I'd really like to be told directly.)
5. What is MOND and why does it keep showing up? It's clearly the main rival but I never got a one-sentence explanation of it.
6. If the dark matter idea failed and the consciousness idea can't be tested, is there anything here that *could* still turn out to be right?
7. Who is doing this and why? I never found out whether this is one person, a lab, or an AI project.

### Verdict

The core idea is genuinely graspable in under a minute — crowd versus marching band, density turns the dial — and the galaxy plotter gave me a real "oh, *that's* the dark matter problem" moment that no YouTube video has managed. That's a rare achievement and the site should build much harder on it. But everything around that core is pitched two levels above me: an equation with a hyperbolic tangent in the first screenful, an interactive tool whose central parameter is misleadingly named, and a "See What Failed" page — which the landing page actively invites me into — that is a dense field of unexplained acronyms. I came away trusting these people (nobody lies this thoroughly about their own failures) while still not being able to say what they found. I'd come back if someone told me a specific page had been rewritten for people like me; I wouldn't come back on my own.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15 (/, /beginner, /why-synchronism, /learning-paths, /key-claims, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /tools, /tier-1-existing, /test-catalog, /research-philosophy, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /equation-walkthrough)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structure), partial (physics — outside my ceiling and I didn't try)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is unusually disciplined — real breadcrumbs, a genuine single-source glossary, a canonical badge reference that pages actually link to — and the failures are almost all *propagation* failures: a term gets fixed in the glossary and the landing page, and the fix stops one hop short of the notation, the card copy, or the count in the intro sentence.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a release: walk the nav, walk the learning path, spot-check every cross-reference, and look for the same thing named two ways.

**The nav bar is good.** Nine items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment), no dropdowns, no orphan sections. Every page I opened had a real breadcrumb of the form `Home / Section / Page` — including `/research-philosophy`, which also carries a *separate* path indicator ("Methodology Track — Step 1 of 6 → Next: A2ACW Protocol"). Two navigation systems on one page, cleanly separated. That's better than most product docs I've worked on.

**The glossary is the strongest asset on the site and it is under-used.** It defines MRH, MOND, SPARC, BTFR, RAR, CHSH, ΔBIC, σ₈, fσ₈, a₀, ΛCDM, A2ACW, EFE, DESI, N_corr, and tanh — plus the whole badge vocabulary, in a labelled cluster, with an explicit pointer that the canonical copy lives on Honest Assessment. That's a properly maintained single source of truth.

And then `/honest-assessment` — the page the landing page sends casual readers to via "See What Failed" — uses RAR, BTFR, SPARC, ΔBIC, σ₈, fσ₈, CHSH, a₀ and ΛCDM **with no expansion on first use and no inline link to the glossary entries that already exist**. The definitions are written. They're just not wired to the place they're needed. This is a one-afternoon fix with a disproportionate payoff: first-use glossary links (or an acronym key block, which `/tier-1-existing` already does correctly and should be the template).

**Terminology: ρcrit.** I expected to find "critical density" used loosely, and I want to record that I didn't. The landing page says `**ρcrit** = saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)`. The glossary heads the entry **"ρ_crit (Reference Density)"**. The Coherence Explorer slider is labelled **"Reference density — saturation knee (ρcrit) = 1.00"**. The rename has landed in three independent places. *(Flagging for the maintainer: Pass 1 above attributes "critical density" to the Coherence Explorer. I fetched that page specifically and the tool does not say it. Don't spend a fix there.)*

The problem that *does* survive is one the prose can't reach: **the symbol itself still reads `ρcrit`.** "crit" is short for "critical." Every equation on the site — hero, walkthrough, calculator, glossary — carries an identifier that asserts exactly the thing the surrounding sentence spends a clause denying. Pass 1's confusion is the predictable outcome: readers trust notation over caveats. If the concept is genuinely a saturation knee, the identifier should be `ρ₀`, `ρ_ref`, or `ρ_knee`, and the changelog line "formerly ρcrit" costs one sentence. Renaming a symbol across a site is real work, so this is a decision to make deliberately, not a quick fix — but every year it isn't made, the caveat has to be repeated on one more page.

**Terminology: a number that disagrees with itself.** The glossary entry ends `C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = 0.88`. The Coherence Explorer, at its default γ = 0.49, reads `C(ρcrit): 0.3272` — and **does not print γ next to that readout**; γ appears only up in the slider label. A reader cross-checking the glossary against the tool sees 0.88 vs 0.33, a factor of 2.7, with nothing on either surface saying "these are the same formula at different γ." Both numbers are right. The presentation makes them look like a contradiction. Fix: print γ in the readout block, and write the glossary entry as the relation, not one evaluation — `C(ρ_crit) = tanh(γ·ln 2)` → 0.88 at γ=2, 0.33 at γ=0.49.

**Counts that have drifted from their own content.** This is the pattern I'd flag hardest, because it recurs across three pages independently:

- `/test-catalog` opens **"Synchronism has defined 24 specific, falsifiable experiments"** and later says **"Of these 24 proposed tests, 0 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM."** Two paragraphs down, the namespace section documents **TEST-25** *and* **TEST-26** as registered. The headline denominator excludes two catalogued tests; the "0 of 24" verdict is being computed over a set the same page says has 26 members.
- `/learning-paths` propagates the stale figure in its step label: "Test Roadmap (24 experiments)."
- `/tier-1-existing` handles this correctly — it states "These **11** numbered tests (TEST-01 through TEST-10, plus TEST-25)" and even records a hand-maintained correction from 10 → 11 on 2026-08-08. But the page renders **14 test cards** (the 11, plus TEST-03s, plus CLUSTER-SCALE, plus the TEST-04/04a pair reading as two). A reader who counts cards and a reader who reads the sentence get different answers, and neither is told why.

The root cause is uniform: these totals are hand-typed prose, while the underlying inventory lives elsewhere. Any count stated in a sentence will drift from the list it describes. Either derive them (the page already knows how many cards it rendered) or, at minimum, add the suffix/exclusion rule inline — "11 numbered tests; TEST-03s and CLUSTER-SCALE are protocol variants and are not separately numbered."

**A hole in the ID namespace.** `/test-catalog` declares one flat namespace, "TEST-01 … TEST-24 (Tier 1 = 01–10, Tier 2 = 11–14, Tier 3+ = 15–24)", and separately records that **the Cassini squeeze was renumbered 11 → 25 to clear a collision** found by an earlier visitor pass. Good — the collision was real and the fix is documented. But the declared Tier 2 range still opens at 11, and **TEST-11 now has no title**. A reader walking the namespace hits a declared-but-empty ID and cannot tell whether it's retired, reserved, or a rendering bug. One line — "TEST-11: retired 2026-08-10, renumbered to TEST-25; ID not reused" — closes it permanently.

**Card copy vs. the thing it describes.** `/tools` advertises the Galaxy Curve Plotter as "Pick a SPARC galaxy and see **four** curves comparing predictions and observations." The plotter renders **five**: Newtonian (dashed gray), observed (dots), Synchronism C(ρ) at γ=2 (violet), hand-tuned stand-in (amber dotted), MOND simple-ν (green dashed). A curve was added and the card wasn't updated. Trivial to fix; worth fixing precisely *because* it's trivial — the tools index is the page that promises readers they know what they're getting before they click.

**Badge vocabulary: the taxonomy has outgrown its own legend.** The system is well-governed in principle. There's a dated contract on `/honest-assessment` ("every badge has two parts... the formal status, drawn only from the two families below... any text after the dash is a free-text finding descriptor"), and pages link to that anchor rather than restating it. That's correct practice and I want to say so plainly.

The problem is size and inconsistent partial rendering. The canonical section defines roughly **29 distinct tokens** across five groups: 5 MRH-relationship tags, 4 descriptive, 2 deprecated, ~9 operational states (Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (RETIRED), Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat), and ~9 lifecycle verbs (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, Ceiling Exceeded). Meanwhile each consuming page shows a *different subset*: the landing page's legend line names 4 (Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed) but the page also renders **Withdrawn**; `/tier-1-existing` legends 8; `/test-catalog`'s guide shows 9; `/key-claims` shows none and links out. Four legends, four different vocabularies, one canonical list none of them matches.

Two structural notes on this. First, **"Withdrawn" and "WITHDRAWN" appear in two different groups** — an operational state and a lifecycle verb — which under the site's own two-part contract is a collision, not a synonym. Second, the distinction that actually matters to a reader is *state* (what is this claim's status?) vs. *event* (what happened to it, when?). Those are different data types wearing the same visual chrome. I'd split them: badges carry state only, and lifecycle verbs move into a dated one-line history under the claim. That also fixes the Withdrawn collision for free.

And one placement note: the canonical definitions live as an **anchor inside a long verdict page**. Every other page's legend link is a deep link into `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, which loads a substantial document to answer "what does amber mean?" A standalone `/badges` page (with Honest Assessment linking *to* it) is the conventional shape and costs nothing in content.

**The entry point has four names.** The nav says **"Start Here."** The landing CTA says **"Beginner Path →."** `/learning-paths` calls it **"Beginner — 6 steps, ~30 min."** The destination's H1 is **"Start Here: Why Synchronism?"** with the sub-line "Beginner Path — Step 1 of 6." All four point at `/why-synchronism`, so nothing is broken — but a reader building a mental map has to work out that these are one thing. Pick one label.

Separately: **`/beginner` serves First Encounter — Step 2 of 6.** The landing CTA correctly targets `/why-synchronism`, so this only bites readers who guess the URL, follow an external link, or land from search — but `/beginner` is the most guessable URL on the site and it deposits you one step into a sequential path, with the "← Prev" as the only route back to the beginning. It should redirect to Step 1.

**One thing I'd have expected to break and didn't:** the "Start Here" flow is genuinely sequential. Step 1 has a Next and no Prev; Step 2 has both, correctly labelled with destination titles rather than bare "Next" ("Next: The Core Idea →"). Cross-references I spot-checked resolved. `/key-claims` states its badge policy and links to the canonical anchor instead of forking a local copy. This is a site whose maintainers understand cross-referencing — which is exactly why the propagation gaps stand out.

**First thing I did was check the function.** Write x = ρ/ρ_crit, t = 1+x, s = t^(2γ). Then

    C = tanh(γ ln t) = (s − 1)/(s + 1) = 1 − 2/(s+1)

so at γ = 1/2, s = t and

    **C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), identically.**

That is MOND's "simple" interpolating function with the argument swapped from g_bar/a₀ to ρ/ρ_crit. I was ready to write this up as the finding of the session — and then found it stated, in exactly those words, on `/for-researchers`, with "the entire content of what makes this framework different from MOND in the galaxy sector" attached. Credit where due: the site names its own reparametrization at the algebraic level, not just the "converges to MOND when fit free" level. That is rarer than it should be.

**Second thing: is this a phase transition?** No, and the site half-knows it. Differentiate twice in t:

    dC/dt   = 4γ t^(2γ−1) / (s+1)²
    d²C/dt² = 4γ t^(2γ−2) [ (2γ−1)(s+1) − 4γs ] / (s+1)³

The bracket vanishes at s* = (2γ−1)/(2γ+1), which is **< 1 for every γ > 0**. Since s = t^(2γ) ≥ 1 on the physical domain t ≥ 1, the bracket is strictly negative everywhere. So **C is strictly concave on ρ ∈ [0,∞) for all γ; the steepest slope is at ρ = 0; there is no inflection point anywhere in the physical domain.** No non-analyticity, no order parameter, no symmetry breaking, no critical exponents. This is a crossover — and a maximally broad one, since a concave saturating function has no characteristic width at all.

`/gamma-boundary` gets this exactly right, and dates the retraction: the "maximum curvature at γ ≈ 1" rationale was withdrawn 2026-08-09, with the correct statement ("concave on the whole domain"). `/phase-boundary-visualizer` carries the matching caveat: "a smooth compander…with no critical point — 'boundary' here means a regime boundary along the γ axis, not a phase transition." Both correct. Both good.

But the Coherence Explorer, which is where a reader actually forms their mental picture, plots on a **log₁₀(ρ) axis with a "C = 0.50" marker**, and its interpretation text still says γ "controls transition sharpness." On a log axis *every* bounded monotone function looks sigmoidal — including plain μ(x) = x/(1+x), including a step-free power law. Sigmoidal-in-log-ρ carries **zero** information about whether a transition exists. The tool is showing the reader the one view in which the function's most important structural property (concavity, no inflection) is invisible. The linear toggle exists and shows the truth; it isn't the default.

5. **What N was ΔBIC = +184 computed over?** If 2,807 correlated RAR points, the number should be ~11 at N_eff ≈ 175. `/honest-assessment` mentions "conservative estimate ≥ 33," which suggests someone has already thought about this — where's that calculation?
6. **Is the mass cancellation the framework's actual death certificate?** ρ/ρ_crit ∝ 1/(G R_d h) means the framework predicts nearly identical C for every disk galaxy. That's a stronger and more elegant kill than any of the six on the ledger, and it needs no data — it's a two-line calculation from the framework's own ρ_crit ∝ V². The site states the cancellation but files it as a caveat rather than as the result.
7. Is there a machine-readable ledger (JSON/CSV) of tests and results? I'd like to check the numbers without re-reading prose, and two pages currently enumerate different "sixes."

### Verdict

This is a more honest document than most published papers I referee, and I mean that without irony: it names its own reparametrization at the algebraic level (γ=1/2 ⇒ C ≡ μ_simple, stated outright), it dates its retractions, it ships a calculator whose stated purpose is to demonstrate its own formula is sign-inverted, and every number I independently recomputed — a₀, Σ₀, the H₀ inversion, all three coherence readouts, the ν/μ inversion, the f_DM algebra — came out right. The "validated" badges are gone and the demotions look earned rather than performative.

The failures are of two kinds, and they're opposite. First, the site **under-supports its own load-bearing claim**: B_max = 1/Ω_m carries two of six refutations, is derived backwards on one page, admitted as an unsupported assertion on another, contradicted by the site's own plotter output by a factor of 300, and applied as a local bound when it's a cosmic mean. Second, and more surprising for a site this self-critical, it **over-refutes in the one place it most wants to be right**: TEST-09 scores Synchronism against its prediction (3.3σ) and MOND against its best fit (0.6σ), when MOND's actual prediction of n = 4 exactly puts it 2.5σ from the same data. The kill survives; the 5× margin doesn't. A framework that has worked this hard to be honest about its failures should be equally careful not to manufacture the size of them — and should notice that its cleanest kill, the mass cancellation in ρ/ρ_crit ∝ 1/(G R_d h), is sitting in a caveat box instead of on the ledger.

**Then I went looking for the force law, and the session turned.**

```
v(r) = √( v_b² + [V_flat · C(ρ(r))]² )
```

That is **not** g = g_bar/C. It is a quadrature-added halo whose amplitude is *proportional to* C. Under this implementation, low density → small C → **small** dark component → the curve collapses onto the baryon line. Under the stated mechanism, low density → small C → boost 1/C → the curve blows up. **The tool and the theory run in opposite directions on the same variable.**

And this resolves the paradox Pass 3 flagged. Two defects: the chart renders **9 labels for 8 approaches** (a "coherence threshold" bar appears that isn't in the convergence list), and the σ = 0.012 is never named as the tell — the caveat argues from the geometry when the number itself makes the case in one line. Badge "Speculative — no calibration exists" is right.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — **worked** — **the most informative page on the site, for a reason it doesn't intend** — Five SPARC galaxies (DDO 154, NGC 2403, NGC 3198, UGC 128, NGC 7331), five curves, every formula printed. Printing the formulas is what makes this tool valuable to a referee and it is why I found the force-law fork: the violet curve's `v² = v_b² + [V_flat·C]²` is visibly not the `g_obs = g_bar/C` that `/key-claims` states, and the discrepancy is invisible unless you read the formula rather than the caption. No EFE term, correctly, and the page says why. I'll also note the tool's honesty about its own baseline: *"the gray Newtonian curve uses spherical enclosed mass for what is physically a thin exponential disk… the visual 'dark matter gap' is modestly exaggerated near the peak."* Very few visualizations disclose that their headline gap is inflated by their own approximation.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Which force law?** Division, quadrature, or a modified Poisson equation? Nothing downstream of "boost B" is well-posed until this is answered, and the answer flips the sign of the galaxy-sector failure.
2. **Has the density-keyed model — the actual novel claim — ever been fitted to anything?** Every galaxy-sector fit I found on the site is in g_bar/a₀. If the answer is no, the front page's verdict on it should read *untested*, not *failed*.
3. **What is the mapping from the g_bar-fitted γ = 0.489 to the ρ-keyed γ?** Without it, the two-sector concordance and the Coherence Explorer's default are both quoting a number from a different model.
4. **What Δχ² does the dark-energy sector have against ΛCDM with both parameters free?** The 3.4–6.3σ figures come from forced-w₀ scans. The unforced number is the one a referee needs, and it should be small, because at γ ≈ 0.5 the sector is ΛCDM to 2%.
5. **Why is EFE = 0 blocked on a sign convention** when a local-density coupling gives EFE ≡ 0 in every convention by the equivalence principle? And given Chae et al. 2020/2021 are published, what stops this being registered today as refutation-only?
6. **Which six "genuine discoveries" calibrated specificity = 0/6, and were they scored retrospectively?** The J = 0 headline rests entirely on this and the protocol isn't stated.
7. **When do the other 41 candidates get audited?** With n = 6 the 0% novelty rate has a 95% upper bound of 50%, which is not a result.
8. **Is there a machine-readable ledger?** Three pages give three test counts, two pages enumerate different "sixes," and I recomputed a force-law inconsistency by hand from printed formulas. A JSON inventory with force-law, fitted-variable, and power-at-registration fields would make all of this checkable — and would have caught most of what I found.

### Verdict

I came looking for something new and found exactly one thing: keying the gravitational modification to local density rather than acceleration. That is a real structural proposal, it is *not* MOND, and — this is the finding I did not expect — **nothing on this site has tested it.** The RAR fit that supplies the site's most-quoted numbers was run in MOND's own variable with the novelty removed; the two headline dwarf/BTFR kills descend from a ceiling whose direction depends on a force law the site has never chosen and whose two candidate choices make the framework fail in *opposite directions*; the Cassini bound is credited prior art that kills simple-μ for MOND too; the environment test had a predicted lever three orders below its own noise floor; and the Bell result is a theorem from 1964. The framework may well be dead — the site's own Local-Density No-Go is the argument that would kill it, and it is far better than any of the six — but it has not been killed by the six things on the scoreboard.

So my review verdict inverts the site's. This is not a project that over-claims; it is a project that has become **so good at refuting itself that it has started refuting things it didn't test**, and it has built no instrument to catch that failure mode, because it never imagined needing one. The pre-registration gate on `/falsifiability` is exactly the right idea pointed in exactly one direction. Point a second one the other way — force law declared, fitted variable declared, power-at-registration declared, comparison model scored on its prediction — and recount. My guess is the honest ledger reads closer to "1 general mechanism-class no-go, executed and citable; 1 structural claim, untested; 3 inherited constraints," which is a considerably more interesting paper than "0 confirmed, 6 refuted," and has the additional merit of being what actually happened.
