**Short answer:** The field equation is not new. It is a known structure with a different argument in the coefficient. I don't know of a published paper with this exact C(ρ) and the Ω_m floor, so that specific choice may be unpublished, but I haven't verified that.

**What it reduces to**
- **Nonlinear Poisson / AQUAL form.** Bekenstein & Milgrom 1984 (ApJ 286, 7) introduced ∇·[μ(|∇Φ|/a₀)∇Φ] = 4πGρ from the Lagrangian −(1/8πG)F(|∇Φ|²) − ρΦ. The proposal is the same equation with μ → C(ρ), a density-dependent "gravitational permittivity" instead of a gradient-dependent one.
- **Gravitational dielectric.** Blanchet's dipolar dark matter (CQG 24, 3529, 2007; Blanchet & Le Tiec 2008, PRD 78, 024031) gives an effective permittivity from gravitational polarization. Both it and Milgrom's bimetric MOND (Milgrom 2009, PRD 80, 123536; uncertain) are comparable in spirit. Milgrom's QUMOND (2010, MNRAS 403, 886) is also related.
- **Density-dependent couplings.** Chameleon, symmetron, and variable-G scalar-tensor models (Khoury & Weltman 2004; Hinterbichler & Khoury 2010) give density-dependent effective G. They do so through a dynamical field, not an algebraic C(ρ).
- **Spherical symmetry.** Gauss's law gives C·g = g_N exactly, so g = g_N/C(ρ) follows trivially. It is the same trivial reduction as in AQUAL.

**Problems with "the natural one"**
1. **Momentum conservation.** The action S = −∫C(ρ)|∇Φ|²/8πG − ∫ρΦ is not closed if ρ is the matter density. Varying the matter degrees of freedom adds an electrostriction-like term proportional to C′(ρ)|∇Φ|². The force on matter is then not simply −∇Φ, and with g = −∇Φ as the force law, momentum is not conserved. Consistency requires either including that term or making C depend on a dynamical field.
2. **Not unique.** AQUAL-type and dielectric-type completions are equally natural, and the choice of C(ρ) is ad hoc.
3. **Phenomenology.** A boost capped at 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.2 cannot give asymptotically flat rotation curves, since the required boost grows as g_N falls. It also depends on local ρ, not on acceleration, so the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation is not reproduced. In the non-spherical case, the curl of g_N/C need not vanish, so the result differs from the algebraic law.

I'm moderately confident in these citations. Treat the Milgrom 2009 and Hinterbichler & Khoury page details as uncertain.
