## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /start-here, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /coherence-calculator, /two-reframes, /glossary, /consciousness)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The honesty is genuinely gripping — I've never seen a science site lead with "0 of 6 claims survived audit" — but about halfway into most pages the jargon wall hits and I start skimming.

### Journey

The landing page hooked me immediately. "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of question I click on, and the marching-band-vs-crowd analogy for "coherence" made sense right away. What really got me was the scoreboard: "Prospective predictions confirmed: 0" and "Refutations executed on external data: 4," right there on the front page. My first thought was "wait, they're telling me their idea *failed*?" That's the opposite of every crank physics site I've stumbled onto from Reddit, and honestly it made me trust them more. The line about "you get to watch a theory be taken apart honestly" is a great pitch.

I followed **Start Here**, which was gentle — the dimmer-switch explanation of the S-curve worked for me, and it repeated the honest "zero confirmed predictions" thing (melting points off by 53%, ouch). Then **First Encounter**, which was the most comfortable page for me: analogies first, no equations, and the line "A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy" actually made the MRH bubble idea click. But the symbols pile up fast — ρ, MRH — and I had to hold them in my head with no glossary popup on the page itself. The forward button at the bottom wasn't labeled, so I wasn't sure what step I was going to.

The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the highlight. Picking real galaxies from a dropdown and watching the gray "what physics predicts" line droop while the blue dots stay flat — I finally *get* what the dark matter problem is. One thing threw me: there's a violet solid line (their real equation, which "fails to fill the gap") AND a violet dotted line that's "drawn to fit." I stared at that for a while. Why show me a fake hand-drawn curve? I think the point is honesty — "here's what our equation actually does vs. what we wish it did" — but a casual reader could walk away thinking the dotted line is a second prediction.

**Honest Assessment** wore me out. I respect it — every failure has a number attached ("Failed — 6.5× Wrong" for superconductors) — but it's thousands of words, and terms like BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, and fσ₈ show up with no plain-English handhold. I skimmed the second half. Also, there's a warning that their "coherence" isn't the physics word "coherence"... but it comes after the page has used the word a hundred times.

The **γ Calculator** worked fine and the presets (ideal gas → Bose-Einstein condensate) were fun to click through. But the big banner saying "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted" confused me. So... the tool is demonstrating a formula they know is wrong? I get that it's honest, but as a casual user I wasn't sure why I was playing with it.

**Two Reframes** started as my favorite reading — the CRT screen analogy for quantum weirdness is genuinely vivid, and the pendulum-in-a-centrifuge idea for time dilation is the first time relativity felt graspable. Then out of nowhere: Tsirelson bound, Kochen–Specker, PR-box, superdeterminism. I bounced. Also, for a page built on two *visual* analogies, there are no pictures at all.

The **Glossary** was hit-or-miss — "Baryon" and "Coherence" read great, but "BIC" is just a formula. **Consciousness** was surprisingly readable and, again, self-critiquing ("no measurement procedure or unit" for two of its three variables), which I appreciated even if it means the exciting-sounding claim is basically an IOU.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /learning-paths, /glossary, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /why-synchronism, /honest-assessment, /key-claims, /coherence-function)

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs site: map the navigation, then check whether the promises the structure makes are kept page by page.

**Navigation and structure.** The header nav is clean and the same everywhere I looked: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. Breadcrumbs exist on every interior page I visited and follow a consistent Home / Section / Page pattern — they work. The footer repeats the nav and carries a stats line ("0 confirmed predictions; 4 refutations executed on external data") that matched **verbatim on every page I checked**. That kind of cross-page consistency on a load-bearing number is rare and worth calling out as done right.

**The learning paths are where the structure breaks its promise.** /learning-paths advertises three tracks: Beginner (6 steps), Intermediate (8 steps), Advanced (8 steps). I entered the first two. Beginner step 1 (/why-synchronism) says "Beginner Path — Step 1 of 6" in its header — good — but has **no labeled Next button**; the only route to step 2 is an inline "First Encounter →" link buried in body text. Intermediate step 1 (/coherence-function) is the mirror image: it **has** a proper labeled forward link ("Next: The γ Parameter →") but **no step indicator at all** — nothing tells you you're on a track or how long it is. Two paths, two different wayfinding patterns, each missing the half the other has. A user who starts a track has no reliable way to stay on it. (Pass 1 independently hit this on /first-encounter: unlabeled forward button.)

**Terminology: one canonical source, weak enforcement.** The glossary is genuinely good — 80+ entries, documented sort order, and 14 of the 15 key acronyms I checked are present (SPARC is expanded inline under its entry but has no standalone term). It even promises hover tooltips site-wide: "Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline." But the promise isn't kept uniformly. /key-claims uses roughly **12 acronyms undefined on first use** (CRT, MRH, PRL, DFS, CPMG, DD, IIT, SNARC, MOND, ΛCDM, RAR, M/L…). The landing page itself uses ΛCDM in hero copy without ever expanding it. Either the tooltip system doesn't cover these pages or these terms aren't wired into it — from the reader's side the effect is the same.

**The badge system has the same shape of problem.** /honest-assessment carries a well-placed canonical section ("Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)", top ~15% of the page) defining two families plus deprecated tags. Excellent. But in actual use across the site I counted at least five status-looking labels that appear **outside** that taxonomy: "Model Explainer" (on four of six tools — explained as "a content grouping, not a validation badge," but it renders exactly like a badge), "Naturalness Gap," "Wrong Category," "Wrong Direction," and "Refuted by Execution." A reader who dutifully reads the canonical taxonomy still can't decode every label they meet. Related: the site uses three overlapping words for a negative outcome — "Failed" (badge), "refutation" (footer count), "Refuted by Execution" (operational state) — and never states the distinction in one place.

**The coherence disambiguation is buried in the glossary too.** The main "Coherence" entry reads "How collectively a group of elements behaves…" with no caveat; the critical "C is NOT quantum phase coherence" note lives only in the C(ρ) sub-entry. The single most important disambiguation on the site — the one both /why-synchronism and /coherence-function eventually make — is missing from the exact place a confused reader would look first. Same sequencing flaw Pass 1 found on /honest-assessment: the caveat consistently arrives *after* heavy use of the term.

**ρ drifts between "presence" and "density."** The landing page defines ρ as "presence — the compatible structural elements available." /why-synchronism (beginner step 1) uses only "density"; the word "presence" never appears. /coherence-function then says "Physical density is one form of presence." So the beginner track teaches one vocabulary, the intermediate track another, and the reconciliation sentence lives on a page beginners aren't routed through.

**Tools.** The /interactive-tools index is well-organized — six active tools with difficulty labels, one Speculative tool honestly fenced off with the remarkable self-description "the site's largest credibility liability." One naming inconsistency: the card says "Equation Anatomy" but the URL and other references say equation-walkthrough. I tested two tools (details below). Both worked; both had the same documentation strength (a "What to notice" section telling you what to look for) and the same weakness (regime labels like BEC, BCS, N_corr used without on-page definition or visible tooltip).

**One count that doesn't reconcile:** the Chemistry Correlation Explorer's headline says "1,703 phenomena," the table shows 23 hand-curated properties, and the page never defines what a "phenomenon" is or how 23 relates to 1,703.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /coherence-function, /equation-walkthrough, /honest-assessment, /interactive-tools, /for-researchers, /two-reframes, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /tier-1-existing)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe — as a methodology case study, not as physics
- **Overall impression**: Every number I recomputed by hand reproduced, every reparametrization I spotted was already labeled as one by the site itself, and the framework is dead as physics by its own ledger — this is the strangest failure mode I've ever seen on an alternative-physics site: rigorous bookkeeping of a negative result.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," so I came in expecting the usual grand-unification site: one magic equation, cherry-picked fits, "validated" stamps everywhere. I went straight for the derivations to find the bodies.

**Landing page.** The equation is C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) and my immediate reaction was: that's not a phase transition, that's a compander — tanh of a log is a saturating rational function, and the "+1" kills any critical behavior at the origin. Then I scrolled down and the site *says this itself*: "phenomenological," compander family, Landau framing explicitly retired. The scoreboard says "0 prospective predictions confirmed / 4 refutations executed on external data." That inverted my prior about what kind of site this is, so I changed strategy: instead of hunting for overclaims, I started checking whether the self-audit is actually correct.

**Parameter derivations.** This page opens by disavowing its own title — "not a derivations page in the mathematical sense" — and then delivers the most damning parameter table I've seen a project publish about itself. Six parameters, zero first-principles derivations, stated outright. The standout items: (a) the calibration constant A carries an "Audited-Negative — Chain-of-Custody Failure" badge because plugging the stated formula's values gives A ≈ 4.6×10⁻⁵, 600× off the used 0.029, and the discrepancy propagated ~600 sessions before anyone re-ran it; (b) ρ_crit ∝ V² is sign-inverted against the V⁻² that tracking a MOND-style acceleration threshold requires; (c) Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) is explicitly flagged as "the a₀ row propagated through a linear relation, not a second derivation," which is exactly right — I checked, and it's an algebraic identity, not evidence. The γ = 2/√N_corr page even flags its own CLT misapplication: 1/√N scaling is an iid result, and N_corr is by construction the count of *correlated* degrees of freedom. That's the objection I came to make, pre-made.

**The math checks.** I recomputed everything checkable with a script. All of it reproduces: the Hill identity tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1] is exact (verified at three (γ,x) points); C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = 0.8824 as stated (correctly presented as a "saturation knee," not a midpoint — most sites would have sloppily called it the transition point); C(10ρ_crit) = 0.99986 ≈ 0.9999 ✓; a₀ = cH₀/(2π) = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² and Σ₀ = 119.0 M☉/pc² at H₀ = 67.4 ✓; boost ceiling 1/Ω_m = 3.17 ✓. I have refereed manuscripts where fewer of the numbers reproduced.

**The mean-field claims.** As a condensed matter student this is my home turf, and the site's self-critique is correct on every point I can adjudicate. The tanh-from-Ising disclaimer is exactly right: in Curie–Weiss, tanh appears inside a *self-consistency loop* m = tanh(βJzm), and it's the loop — not the tanh — that produces the phase transition; C(ρ) has no loop, so borrowing the tanh borrows the costume, not the physics. And the γ sign inversion is real: BCS transitions are among the *sharpest* in nature (the Ginzburg fluctuation window is tiny precisely because ~10⁶ pairs overlap within a coherence volume — mean-field is nearly exact), yet γ = 2/√N_corr assigns BCS the *flattest* curve. More correlation should sharpen, not smear. The site states this inversion in a red honesty box rather than burying it.

**Bell / Two Reframes.** The CHSH material is competent. Their substrate constructions get S = 1.98 (local) and S ≡ 2.00 (nonlocal-grid, no signaling), S = 1.85 on their own density substrate — never Tsirelson's 2√2 without signaling. That's just Bell's theorem functioning as designed, and the page knows it: "Declaring the substrate 'nonlocal by construction' does not *evade* Bell — it *chooses the nonlocal horn*, the same horn Bohmian mechanics sits on." They also volunteer the Kochen–Specker problem with their own CRT-scanning analogy (non-contextual hidden variables, excluded for d ≥ 3) and name the Born rule as "the actual hard problem." One quibble: the ledger calls this "Failed," which is fair against the registered bet (reproduce quantum correlations) but a reader could use one sentence noting that S ≤ 2 was the *guaranteed* outcome for any no-signaling local-realist construction — the bet was structurally unwinnable, which is itself the informative result. The absolute-time reframe is correctly priced too: preferred frame → dimension-4 LIV coefficients radiatively generated at α/π ~ 10⁻², versus bounds at 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰, with the correct citation (Collins et al., PRL 93, 191301) and — importantly — the two known perturbative escapes (SUSY; Pospelov–Shang scale hierarchy) named as existing-but-unexhibited. Calling that a "naturalness gap" rather than a refutation is the physically correct framing; naturalness problems are burdens, not kills, and the site resists the temptation to over-kill here. Mostly.

**Honest assessment + Tier 1 registry.** The galaxy-sector story is coherent and matches what I know of MOND phenomenology: free-γ fits converge to γ ≈ 0.49 and collapse onto MOND (ΔBIC = +7 vs +184 for the asserted γ=2); the bounded boost B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17 means no divergent deep-MOND limit, hence BTFR slope n → 2 asymptotically, hence the fitted n = 3.35 vs observed 3.75 ± 0.10 — a 3.3σ miss on their own >0.3 kill threshold. That chain is correct: MOND's n = 4 *requires* the unbounded √(a₀/g) boost, so a capped boost genuinely cannot get there. The registry even documents its own fabrications — a spliced (R², p, N) triple on TEST-03 that's "mathematically impossible at N = 14,585," a misattributed DESI fσ₈ value, the TEST-04a verdict delivered on σ₈ when the registered statistic was fσ₈ (where it's only ~1.5σ, below the demanded 3σ). The refutation count of 4 correctly excludes TEST-04a after that correction. What the registry does *not* disclose: registration dates for TEST-09 and TEST-10 themselves. Both executed 2026-07-14/15, both listed with sharp numeric kill thresholds (slope deviation >0.3; DM-fraction ceiling 68.5%), neither with a stated date on which those thresholds were fixed. On a site this obsessive about the TEST-04a post-hoc taint, the two newest and loudest kills lacking timestamps is a conspicuous gap — I assume the thresholds are structural (the 68.5% ceiling is just 1 − 1/3.17), but say so.

**Tools.** Details below; the Equation Anatomy walkthrough contains the single best mathematical sentence on the site (the Hill identity plus the admission that "the ln is cosmetic"), and the Phase Boundary Visualizer is an honest tool whose content is its own counterexample.

### Tools Tested
- **Equation Anatomy (/equation-walkthrough)** — worked — intuitive — the six-piece decomposition is clean, and it contains the site's best math: the exact identity tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ−1]/[(1+x)^2γ+1] (I verified numerically at three parameter points — exact), plus the frank admission that "the ln is cosmetic." This dissolves any residual mystique: the equation is a two-parameter Hill function. That this sits in a "Conceptual" tool rather than on the main equation page is a placement error, not a content error.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer (/phase-boundary-visualizer)** — worked — confusing in an instructive way — slider and regime cards function; the placements are physically backwards (BCS, the sharpest transition class in condensed matter, at the flattest end; ideal gas and galaxies coincidentally sharing γ = 2 via counting convention) and the page *says so* in its caveats. It's an honest tool whose pedagogical payload is its own counterexample; it needs to own that framing.

### Unanswered Questions
1. When exactly were the TEST-09 and TEST-10 kill thresholds fixed, and is the 68.5% ceiling derived from 1 − 1/3.17? The site's credibility standard demands the timestamps it applies to TEST-04a.
2. Is there a measurement protocol — even a proposed one — mapping any laboratory observable to C in any single system? The admission that none exists anywhere is the deepest problem on the site and it's stated once, on one page.
3. Given the Hill identity, is anything at all lost by retiring the tanh∘ln notation? If not, why does most of the site still lead with it?
4. After the galaxy sector's collapse onto MOND and the CHSH closure, is there any observable regime left where the framework differs from existing physics *and* the difference isn't already refuted? The bounded boost was the last candidate and it produced the BTFR kill.

### Verdict
I came to catch a crank site and instead spent an afternoon failing to catch an audit. Every number I could recompute reproduced — the Hill identity, the saturation values, Σ₀ from cH₀/(4π²G), the boost ceiling — and every reparametrization I spotted (a₀ = cH₀/2π, Σ₀ as Freeman restated, the MOND collapse at free-γ) was already labeled as such by the site itself, usually with a sharper critique than I'd have written. As physics, the framework is dead and knows it: zero derived parameters, a sign-inverted γ, a galaxy sector that is MOND with a bound that then fails BTFR, S ≤ 2 on Bell, and a 16–28 order-of-magnitude naturalness gap on its one genuinely novel commitment. What's left is a meticulous public autopsy, and the remaining rigor gaps I found are bookkeeping (missing registration timestamps on the two newest kills, a misplaced identity, an unframed reductio tool) rather than dishonesty. I wouldn't cite this as physics; I might genuinely cite it as a case study in what falsification discipline looks like when it's actually executed.

### Journey

I came to answer three questions: what's actually new, does any prediction differ from MOND + ΛCDM, and is A2ACW sound. I skipped the pedagogy and went straight to the researcher-facing surfaces.

**Novelty hunt: closed by the site itself.** The landing page states it outright — "the equation fits galaxy rotation — but only as well as MOND, an existing 40-year-old idea, and never better" — and /test-catalog delivers the terminal sentence: "Of these 24 proposed tests, 0 currently discriminate from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." That's not my finding; it's their disclosure. The interesting structure underneath: the framework's *only* structural difference from MOND is the bounded boost B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17, and that single difference generated both of the newest refutations — TEST-09 (BTFR slope n = 3.35 predicted vs 3.75 ± 0.10 observed, kill at 3.3σ) and TEST-10 (f_DM ceiling 68.5%, exceeded by 69% of SPARC outer points; dwarf spheroidals at M_dyn/M_bar ~ 10²–10³ make the cap absurd). The epistemically clean summary: the framework is distinguishable from MOND exactly where it is refuted, and unrefuted exactly where it is MOND. As a referee I'd call that a completed program.

**But the headline kill has a systematics hole the site itself documents — one page over.** TEST-06's caveat correctly notes the BTFR slope is pipeline-dependent at the ~3× level in scatter and materially in slope (Lelli et al. 2019: velocity-definition choice — V_flat vs W_P20 vs V_max — moves the fitted slope across roughly 3.0–4.1). TEST-09's execution reports n = 3.75 ± 0.10 without stating which velocity definition was used, and its kill threshold (deviation > 0.3) sits *inside* the known definition-dependent range. With V_flat (McGaugh-style) the observed slope runs steeper (~3.85), widening the kill; with linewidth-based W20 it runs shallower (~3.2–3.5), which could put the deviation under 0.3. The refutation is probably robust — the bounded-boost n→2 asymptote is structural — but "probably" is not what a registered kill should rest on, and the site applies exactly this scrutiny to TEST-06 while omitting it on the test that carries a quarter of the front-page refutation count. Pass 3 flagged the missing registration timestamps on TEST-09/10; this is the sharper version: the kill's *observable* is definition-ambiguous, not just its paper trail.

**A2ACW soundness.** The methodology page is more honest than most AI-for-science preprints I review: 3,308 sessions → 47 internal survivors (1.4%) → 6 audited → 0 novel; specificity 0/6 (all six genuine historical discoveries false-flagged as reparametrizations), sensitivity 6/6, stated Youden's J = 0 — an uninformative classifier, said in those words. The temporal-asymmetry counterfactual is the one genuinely interesting result: pairing models with different training cutoffs caught 0 of 6 demotions, median prior-art year ~1996 — so the failure is vocabulary lock-in / in-distribution blindness, not knowledge recency. But the load-bearing confound is admitted and unresolved: a single-arm, single-corpus design cannot separate "adversarial same-corpus AI cannot surface novelty" from "this seed framework contained none to find." The cross-vendor corpus control that would break the degeneracy is registered but unrun. Until it runs, /for-researchers overbills the null as a "citable artifact" — it's a citable *anecdote* with a registered path to becoming an artifact. Same page, same issue in miniature: the CHSH result (S = 1.98 local, S ≡ 2.00 nonlocal-grid, no signaling) is billed as transferable when it's a corollary of Bell's theorem — any no-signaling local-realist construction is guaranteed S ≤ 2; simulation adds pedagogy, not a result.

**The LIV sector is the best physics writing on the site.** Correct use of Collins et al. (PRL 93, 191301) for the non-Planck-suppressed dim-4 c_μν ~ α/π; correct bounds (10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰, hence a 16–28 OOM gap); and — rare for this genre — the two known perturbative escapes named honestly as existing-but-unexhibited (Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov 2005 SUSY; Pospelov & Shang 2012 scale hierarchy). "Naturalness gap, not refutation" is the correct verdict and most working phenomenologists would sign it. The DESI handling post-correction is also current: the 07-14 note citing Ishak et al. **Propagation gap I'd flag as a referee:** /key-claims establishes that no calibration protocol maps *any* observable to C in *any* domain ("unrunnable as stated" — their own third category), yet /test-catalog still lists Tier 2 pilot experiments (EEG, circadian, QC coherence, $50K–$500K) as if they were fundable proposals. Those pilots measure the wrong variable by the site's own adjudication; the roadmap hasn't absorbed the verdict. Whoever funds a Tier 2 pilot off that page would be spending real money on a test the framework has already declared uninterpretable.

**Smaller literature gaps:** TEST-05 names the non-EFE reinterpretations of Chae's ~4σ environment detection ("Freundlich et al., Paranjape & Sheth") without citing them; the dwarf-spheroidal 10²–10³ mass-ratio claim carries no citation (Walker & Peñarrubia 2011 or post-Gaia dispersion work would do); TEST-02 correctly frames Chae 2023 vs Banik 2024 as unresolved but the framework's own predicted amplitude (0.05–0.4%) is ~80× below the systematics floor either way — that test was never going to run, and the page says so.
