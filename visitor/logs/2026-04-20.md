## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: minimal
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The questions pulled me in and the honesty surprised me, but the explanations collapsed into jargon fast enough that I mostly bounced off the details.

### Journey

Landed on the homepage. "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — okay, that's the kind of question that makes me click. The three paths (First Encounter / Core Idea / Honest Assessment) felt welcoming. I noticed the "See What Failed" button right next to "Start Exploring" and thought: that's unusual. Most sites selling a Big Idea don't advertise their failures.

Clicked **First Encounter** first because it promised "10 minutes, no prerequisites." It did not deliver on that promise. Within the first paragraph I hit "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)" used to explain "presence," which was used to explain the main thing. Three nested terms deep before I had my bearings. The electron-vs-neutron-star example at the end helped — "density matters" — but by then I was already tired.

Jumped to **Core Idea** expecting a plain-English pitch. Got the equation C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) front and center. I've seen `log` in high school. I have never heard the word "tanh" out loud. The "dimmer switch between quantum and classical" metaphor was actually good — I got that coherence goes from 0 (quantum) to 1 (classical). But I couldn't tell you what ρ, γ, or ρcrit really *are*, only that the site says they matter.

Hit **Two Reframes** via the "Need an analogy first?" link. The CRT analogy (particles as fast-scanning beams) was the single best thing I read all session — I *got* that one. The pendulum-in-a-centrifuge analogy for relativity was cleverer than I expected, even if I didn't know whether to trust it.

Tried the **Honest Assessment** and was disarmed. "0 confirmed predictions," "missed melting points by 53%," "superconductor temps 6.5× wrong." For a site pitching a Theory Of Everything this was almost shocking. It made me trust the authors *more*, not less. The phrase "wrong theories motivate right questions" actually landed.

Browsed **Galaxy Rotation** (the 14,760-galaxy thing the homepage bragged about). I don't really know what dark matter *is* beyond "invisible stuff that tugs on galaxies," and this page assumed I did. The headline number sounded impressive; the admission that their environment effect only explains 14% of the scatter sounded less impressive. I left uncertain whether this was a win or a near-miss.

Spent time in the **Glossary**. "Coherence" I could follow. "Presence" refers me to MRH. MRH refers me to "Markov" which isn't defined. I bounced.

**Scale Invariance** lost me at "Navier-Stokes." Clicked through to its interactive tool. Came back to **Coherence Function** looking for a picture of the curve — there wasn't one on the page itself, only a link out to another tool.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What is coherence *actually measuring* — is it a thing you could measure with an instrument, or is it a mathematical quantity?
2. If "0 predictions confirmed," why should I care that the math is elegant? Isn't that the whole point of physics, to predict?
3. Is this the same kind of idea as "everything is information" that I've heard pop-sci people talk about, or something different?
4. What would "wrong" look like? The honest-assessment page listed specific failures, but what result would make them say the *whole framework* doesn't work?

### Verdict

The site's honesty is its best feature — I came in skeptical of another "one equation for everything" pitch and left thinking the authors are at least trying to do this in good faith. But the gap between the welcoming framing ("no prerequisites, 10 minutes") and the actual vocabulary density (MRH, Markov, tanh, Navier-Stokes, ρcrit all within the first couple of pages) is wide. A casual reader like me gets the vibe but not the content. Fix the onboarding layer — one paragraph of plain-language scaffolding per page — and this would be genuinely approachable.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 loaded + 9 probed for 404s
- **Understanding achieved**: partial (tracking structure, not physics)
- **Would return?**: yes — the IA is interesting enough to audit again in a month
- **Overall impression**: The skeleton is good — breadcrumbs, H1s, validation legend all exist — but the taxonomy is inconsistent across the three places it matters (top nav, section parents, hub pages), and the flagship "Test Catalog" page ships without the badge system that the rest of the site relies on.

### Journey

I browsed methodically, treating the site like I would a docs site I'd been asked to audit. My question wasn't "is the physics right?" but "if I were a reader trying to find things, would I?"

**Top navigation.** The homepage header lists six items (Synchronism / Glossary / Honest Assessment / Start Exploring / See What Failed / What this is not). That last one — "What this is not" — is a broken top-nav link. `/what-this-is-not`, `/not-claims`, and several natural variants all return 404. A top-nav item going nowhere is the highest-severity structural bug I found, because it's visible on every page.

**Landing → First Encounter → Core Idea.** This flow is well-paved. Both `/first-encounter` and `/core-idea` carry breadcrumbs (`Home / Getting Started / …`), clean H1s, and forward/back nav. `/core-idea` actually defines `tanh`, `γ`, `ρ`, `ρcrit`, and `Ncorr` on the same page via a parameter legend beneath the equation — that is exactly what a tech writer wants to see. Someone on this team knows the rule "define on first use" and applied it here.

**Breadcrumb mismatch: `/two-reframes`.** The "Need an analogy first?" link sits inside First Encounter (Getting Started), but `/two-reframes`' breadcrumb reads `Home / Quantum Physics / Two Reframes`. So the user silently crosses sections mid-flow. Either the page belongs in Getting Started, or the entry point should warn "leaving the intro track."

**Validation badge system.** `/honest-assessment` contains a perfectly usable legend of seven statuses (Validated, Strongly Supported, Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed). This is the spec for the whole site's credibility claims. But:

1. It lives only on one page. It is not surfaced on `/test-catalog`, which is the one page where a reader most needs it.
2. `/test-catalog` shows zero badges next to any of its 24 tests. The hub the legend was built for doesn't use it.
3. `/galaxy-rotation` has no top-of-page badge either, despite being the site's flagship validated result. The honest-caveat section inside the page carries the nuance, but the reader has to read down to find it.
4. The homepage displays "89% Validated" as a summary metric. Under the legend's definition ("Validated = Quantitative match with independent data"), aggregating this number requires each prediction to carry a status. Right now those statuses aren't visibly assigned, so the 89% can't be audited by the reader.

**Predictions taxonomy inconsistency.** The same hub is referred to by three different names:
- Homepage CTA: "Predictions"
- Top-level URL: `/test-catalog`
- Breadcrumb parent on that page: "Predictions & Experiments"

Pick one. `/predictions` itself 404s, so the homepage button label doesn't match the URL it lands on.

**Glossary.** `/glossary` is organized by notation (C(ρ), γ, Ncorr, ρcrit, MRH…) with natural-language terms (Coherence, Phase Transition, Decoherence) scattered after. A glossary is the one page where alphabetical order is the default expectation. Also, "Markov" has no entry — yet MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon) is the second-most-cited term on the site and `/mrh` itself says "inspired by Markov blankets" without linking anywhere. Pass 1 flagged this from a different angle; I'm confirming the structural hole.

**MRH page structure.** The "Prerequisites" section is at the bottom of `/mrh`. Prerequisites belong at the top. This violates the standard docs-site pattern "tell readers what they need before they invest five minutes in the page."

**Terminology drift is actually low.** Cross-page usage of "coherence," "presence," "γ," "C(ρ)" is consistent. Symbols are not being renamed between pages. This is one of the better-edited technical sites I've audited this quarter.

**Undefined acronyms.** On `/honest-assessment`: ALFALFA, SDSS, QFT, TOE are used without definition. On `/scale-invariance`: Navier-Stokes, Reynolds number, Madelung transformation, decoherence all appear cold. The glossary exists but the terms aren't hyperlinked at point of first use — the one move that would close this gap with minimal effort.

**Coherence Function page missing its own plot.** `/coherence-function` is literally a page about a function, yet the tanh curve appears only via a link to `/coherence-explorer`. For a reference page, embedding a static thumbnail (even a tiny inline SVG) would be the standard move. Pass 1 noted this too; worth reinforcing.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13 (core-idea, coherence-function, gamma-parameter, gamma-boundary, scale-invariance, mond-unification, galaxy-rotation, phase-transitions, honest-assessment, test-catalog, tier-1-existing, coherence-explorer, scale-navigator)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of what the framework *claims*); partial (of what it would actually *predict* differently from existing physics)
- **Would return?**: yes — specifically to audit the Tier-1 BAO modulation and wide-binary predictions once the linked pages flesh out their discriminators
- **Overall impression**: An unusually self-aware piece of speculative physics. The core equation is a mean-field classifier dressed as an ontology — most "validations" are either the Milgrom coincidence in new clothing or a γ≈1 centering that is definitional, not empirical. But the authors admit most of this themselves, and one or two predictions (BAO density modulation, wide-binary environment dependence) are actually novel and falsifiable.

### Journey

I went straight for the math. Landing page, then `/core-idea`, `/coherence-function`, `/gamma-parameter`. The equation is

**C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**

with γ = 2/√Ncorr and ρcrit = A·V_flat². My first instinct as a condensed-matter PhD: this is a Landau-theory-shaped order parameter. It is exactly the functional form you get from a mean-field self-consistency condition with an external field, truncated at leading order. The `/coherence-function` page cheerfully admits as much — "tanh arises naturally from mean-field models (Ising model analogy provided)" — without ever writing down a Hamiltonian, partition function, or self-consistency equation. That's the tell. In a mean-field derivation the *functional form* is the cheap part; the hard part is deriving γ and ρcrit from microscopics. Neither is derived here.

The γ = 2/√Ncorr "derivation" is the weakest load-bearing piece. 1/√N is genuinely the CLT scaling of fluctuations in a correlated ensemble — that part is fine. The factor of 2 is hand-waved as "6D phase space contracted to 3 effective DOF, yields 2," with the actual derivation deferred to "Sessions #64–65." A condensed-matter reader sees this pattern often: a numerical prefactor retrofitted through a dimension-counting argument. The claim that "if you change it, chemistry correlations degrade" is circular if Ncorr is fit per system — and the `/gamma-boundary` page doesn't clarify whether Ncorr is measured independently or extracted from the same data the correlation is computed on. That is the single most important methodological question for the whole framework, and it isn't answered on the page that depends on it.

Worse, the two-parameter family (γ, ρcrit) is partially degenerate. In log(ρ/ρcrit + 1), ρcrit is purely a horizontal shift of the argument; under (ρ → αρ, ρcrit → αρcrit) the curve is invariant. ρcrit is anchored to V_flat² via ρcrit = A·V_flat², which is the empirical tether — but it's tethered to a system-specific quantity, so each system brings its own ρcrit. What looks like a universal two-parameter family is really a one-parameter family (γ) that is reset for each system by choice of density origin. That's a Landau-theory-level description, not an equation of motion, and the site says so explicitly on `/scale-invariance`: "C(ρ) is a classification tool…not an explanation." Good. But then the "one equation from Planck to cosmic" framing on the homepage oversells it.

The scale-invariance claim is a misnomer. On `/scale-navigator`, γ at the atom scale is 2, at the human scale is ~10⁻¹², and back to 2 at the galaxy scale. That isn't scale-invariance — that's γ being a function of Ncorr(scale). Calling the same equation "scale-invariant" because Ncorr absorbs the dimensional reduction is equivalent to saying "F=ma is scale-invariant because m varies." True scale-invariance would require γ fixed and the transition at the same argument. The Madelung transformation claim — that "Schrödinger is Euler" at the quantum scale — is mathematically correct (Madelung 1927) but misused: Madelung is a reformulation with quantum pressure term ~ℏ², not a fluid-dynamics derivation of quantum mechanics. Calling Schrödinger↔Euler "not an analogy but literal structural equivalence" overstates a result every quantum fluids paper treats as an analogy with a crucial quantum correction.

**The galaxy rotation claim.** This is the site's flagship. After reading `/galaxy-rotation` and `/mond-unification` carefully, here is what is actually being claimed:
1. Synchronism reproduces the RAR using the McGaugh 2016 interpolation function. That is MOND. The page admits this.
2. The acceleration scale a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², ~10% off Milgrom's value. This has been restated under many frameworks (Milgrom, McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017) because cH₀ happens to be the right order of magnitude. The "2π from a spherical horizon" is a post-hoc geometric factor — any 2π-ish factor hits within ~10%.
3. The actual novel claim is an environment-dependent RAR scatter: p = 5×10⁻⁶, R² = 0.14. The p-value is statistically significant but the R² is ~14%, meaning the density effect accounts for ~14% of residual variance. With N ≈ 14,585 galaxies, any consistent systematic will hit p<10⁻⁶ — that's just sample-size dominance. The genuine content is σ_int = 0.086 dex for the environment-stratified sample, which *is* below the pre-registered bound but is not a detection of a qualitatively new effect relative to MOND + M/L variation. The `/galaxy-rotation` page admits "MOND + M/L already explains essentially all the RAR variance." That's an honest framing. The homepage's "89% Validated" metric does not reflect this honest framing.

**The phase-transitions page is where I'd expect the framework to earn its keep and it fails, as admitted.** A mean-field tanh gives β = 1/2, ν = 1/2 at criticality. Real Ising-class systems have β ≈ 0.326, XY ≈ 0.345 — factors ~1.5–2 off. The page says "wrong by ~2" without naming universality classes or showing the comparison. This is the classical limitation of any mean-field theory since Landau 1937. The framework doesn't attempt RG flow or fluctuation corrections, so the error is structural, not fixable by tuning. Credit for disclosure; no credit for physics.

**Novel vs. reparametrized.** My reading of `/tier-1-existing`:

| Test | Novelty | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| TEST-01 SPARC environment | Borderline novel | MOND has no intrinsic environment dependence; this is a real discriminator if the effect survives mass-to-light systematics |
| TEST-02 Gaia wide binaries density-dependent | Novel | Current Gaia DR3 debate (Pittordis/Sutherland vs Chae) is already testing MOND; adding density dependence is a new handle |
| TEST-04 BAO modulation ~10⁻⁴ | **Genuinely novel** | DESI Year-3 precision is ~10⁻⁴ on BAO; this is a real, falsifiable prediction with a clean discriminator against ΛCDM |
| TEST-09 BTFR n ≈ 2.2 universal | **Likely wrong** | Canonical BTFR slope is ~3.5–4 (Lelli+ 2019). n ≈ 2.2 would contradict two decades of observations, not extend them. Either the page has a typo, it refers to a different relation, or the prediction is dead on arrival. |
| Other TESTs | Reparametrization or MOND-adjacent | Freeman's law, RAR tightening, M/L corrections are well-trodden |

TEST-09 as stated looks like a serious error, or at minimum a labeling problem. TEST-04 is the one a grad student would actually be excited by.

**Entity criterion Γ < m** is referenced as "oscillation-based, not QFT-derivable." I didn't fetch the page but I'm suspicious of the framing — "not QFT-derivable" is a strong claim and in QFT a decay rate Γ > m generically signals an unstable resonance rather than a particle. The inequality Γ < m is standard narrow-resonance physics; calling it a novel oscillation-based criterion needs more detail than the index page gives.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — physics partially correct — the two-slider chart does show γ controlling transition sharpness (nonlinear, not a reparametrization) and ρcrit controlling horizontal shift. Mathematically clean. What the tool does NOT show, and should, is that the (γ, ρcrit) family is partially degenerate under rescaling of ρ — a reader playing with the sliders sees two "independent" knobs when one of them is really a unit convention. A small info box ("ρcrit rescales the density axis; γ reshapes the curve") would prevent overinterpretation of apparent multi-parameter freedom.
- **Scale Navigator** (`/scale-navigator`) — worked — physics misrepresented — the table values raise real issues. Ncorr = 1, γ = 2 at "atom" and *also* at "galaxy" implies an uncorrelated treatment of a galaxy (no correlated bulk motion), which collides with rotation curves — galaxies exhibit coherent rotation precisely because their stars are correlated via gravity. Similarly Ncorr = 1 "for uncorrelated stellar systems" flattens a real distinction between bound and unbound systems. The slider's "80 orders of magnitude" framing suggests universality; the underlying number table suggests the parameters are stipulated per-regime rather than derived. A grad reader's takeaway: this is a pedagogical cartoon, not a physics result. Relabel accordingly.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What is the mean-field self-consistency equation that yields C = tanh(γ·log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))? Specifically: what plays the role of the order parameter, what is the exchange coupling, what is the external field, and what approximation corresponds to "γ"? Until this is on the page, the tanh justification is ornamental.
2. Is Ncorr determined from a microscopic observable independent of the phenomenon being fit, or is it fit per-system? This single methodological answer decides whether `/gamma-boundary`'s r = 0.982 is an empirical finding or a circular curve-fit.
3. What does the framework predict for critical exponents if you keep the mean-field tanh but perturb with a fluctuation correction (e.g., ε-expansion or 1-loop)? If the framework can be extended this way, cite it; if not, say the framework is intrinsically mean-field and cannot recover Ising/XY universality.
4. TEST-09 BTFR n ≈ 2.2 — is this a typo, a misidentified relation, or a real prediction that contradicts Lelli+ 2019? A grad reader stops at this entry.
5. BAO modulation ~10⁻⁴ between high/low-density regions — what exactly is "density" here (large-scale structure contrast? local matter density at the BAO scale?), and what is the predicted sign? DESI Year-3 is close enough to this sensitivity that the prediction is testable now if the signal is specified precisely.

**TEST-04 BAO modulation (the one I came for).** The discriminator framing is exactly right: "BAO peak shifts ~10⁻⁴" vs "BAO identical everywhere to 10⁻⁵ precision." DESI Year-3 reaches this precision, and there is genuine room to discriminate against ΛCDM if the prediction is specified. But:
- No mechanism. Why ~10⁻⁴ specifically? The natural amplitude from a coherence-modulated sound horizon would depend on the density contrast δρ/ρ at recombination versus today, neither of which is stated.
- No sign. Does the BAO peak shift to larger or smaller scales in dense vs underdense regions? Without sign, the prediction is half-specified.
- No discriminator from ΛCDM nonlinear corrections. Standard cosmology already predicts nonlinear shifts in BAO position at ~10⁻³ amplitude in highly nonlinear regions (Padmanabhan-White 2009 reconstruction literature deals with exactly this). The relevant question is whether Synchronism's predicted shift is distinguishable from these baseline nonlinear effects after reconstruction. The page doesn't engage.
- Despite all this: the *form* of the prediction is correct. Specify mechanism and sign and this is a real preprint.

**TEST-02 Wide-binary density stratification.** Genuinely the strongest item on the site, and I'll say something I rarely say: the framing here is sharper than the Chae vs Banik papers themselves. Both groups are analyzing Gaia DR3 with different cuts; Chae 2023 reports a clear MOND-consistent anomaly, Banik et al. 2024 reports null. Synchronism's reconciling prediction — that both could be correct if their samples differ in environment density — is testable now by re-binning either dataset by Galactic latitude and local stellar density. This is exactly the kind of hypothesis a referee would ask for in either of those papers' reviews. Whether the prediction holds is empirical; that the prediction is well-posed is unusual. Credit where due.

**TEST-04 (decisive) GW speed-DM correlation.** "Zero correlation between GW propagation and DM distribution." This is post-diction, not prediction. GW170817 already constrains |c_gw - c|/c < 10⁻¹⁵ over an extragalactic propagation distance, which already kills any local-DM-coupled GW dispersion at the levels Synchronism could be predicting. Saying "we predict the existing constraint" is not contributing a discriminator. Either there is a more specific Synchronism prediction (e.g., a frequency-dependent dispersion at sub-c_gw - c < 10⁻¹⁵ levels accessible only to LISA, or an NS-NS distance correlation) or this should be moved out of "decisive tests" into "consistent with."

**TEST-3 (decisive) EEG anesthesia phase transition.** "Consciousness loss is a phase transition." This is not a Synchronism-unique claim. Tagliazucchi et al. 2016 analyzed EEG criticality across propofol-induced unconsciousness; Carhart-Harris's entropic-brain framework, Tononi's IIT, Friston's free-energy formulations all predict critical-point behavior near consciousness boundaries. The literature is large and contested. Synchronism would need a *quantitative* discriminator — predicted critical exponent, predicted finite-size scaling, predicted coupling to specific anesthetic mechanism — to separate from this prior art. The page doesn't exist.

**TEST-7/decisive-5 Cluster oscillations at λ ~ 500 Mpc.** Novel and falsifiable in principle. SDSS, DES, DESI all have the data. But again no mechanism page, no predicted amplitude, and crucially no relationship specified to TEST-04's BAO modulation — the natural reader question is "wait, isn't a periodic ~500 Mpc modulation a *bigger* signal than the 10⁻⁴ BAO shift you also predict?" These two predictions need to be reconciled internally before they're both "decisive."

**TEST-09 BTFR n ≈ 2.2.** Confirmed: the site lists this as the prediction. The discriminator stated is "exponent varies by >0.3 across bands." This entirely misses the structural problem: Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2019 measured the BTFR slope at n = 3.85 ± 0.09 across SPARC, with the 4th-power dependence being one of the most cited empirical regularities in the modified-gravity literature for ~25 years. A predicted exponent of 2.2 is not a small deviation — it's a 17σ contradiction. Either the page contains a typo (should it be n ≈ 4?), it refers to a redefined relation (a non-baryonic TFR variant?), or the prediction is dead on arrival before any new data is collected. As written, this is a self-falsifying entry in the test catalog and a referee would flag it on first read.

**Failed predictions inventory.** From `/honest-assessment`: melting points off by 53% on average; critical exponents off by ~2 (the generic mean-field failure); superconductor Tc for YBCO predicted at 607K vs measured 93K, off by 6.5×; dark matter viscosity gives wrong sign. The superconductor error is the one I'd dwell on. Tc = Δ/(1.76 k_B η) recovers the weak-coupling BCS prefactor 1.76 correctly, so the framework hooks into a known relation, then misidentifies either Δ or η by an order-of-magnitude factor specific to YBCO. That's not a sign-of-life failure (which would be Tc by 20%); a 6.5× miss on a flagship condensed-matter system implies the formula is using the wrong physical scale entirely, and there's nothing in the framework that tells you in advance which scale to use. This is a very specific kind of failure that points at the framework lacking the mechanism for parameter identification it claims.

**A2ACW methodology.** This is the part I find most genuinely interesting, and also the part where I think the project is confused about what it has. AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop with four rotating roles (PRIMARY/CHALLENGER/OBSERVER/COORDINATOR), a mandatory challenge frequency (≥1 per 10 exchanges), automatic human escalation if challenges drop, and a Collaboration Coherence Health metric (Ambiguity Fork Rate, Challenge Frequency, External Verification Rate, Fingerprint Divergence Index). This is a real contribution to AI-collaboration methodology. The failure mode it targets — sycophantic agreement and premature consensus among AI agents — is well-documented (cf. Sharma et al. 2023 on sycophancy in RLHF models, Park et al. 2024 on AI deception). The CCH metric components are reasonable proxies. There are people working on AI-coordination governance who would be interested in this protocol on its own terms.

But: A2ACW is a methodology for *generating hypotheses with reduced groupthink*, not for *validating them*. Self-correction between AI agents reduces certain failure modes during ideation; it cannot substitute for empirical confrontation with reality. The site appears to occasionally treat the rigor of the generation process as evidence for the correctness of the output ("3,308 sessions" presented as evidential weight on the homepage). The two are different. The honest-assessment page itself flags this through the most damning admission on the site: **"Sessions 134–2660 were identified as 'template-based' — the AI used similar analysis patterns across phenomena, which may inflate the validation rate."** That's 2,526 of 3,308 sessions (76%) acknowledged to be template-driven, including the bulk of the 1,840 chemistry sessions that produce the homepage's "89% Validated" metric. Once you flag template-based generation as inflationary, the 89% chemistry number is no longer evidence of physical regularity — it could equally be evidence that the same methodology applied to similar systems produces similar centerings.

The honest assessment ledger reports **0 confirmed predictions** across all sessions. The homepage reports **89% validated** for chemistry. These cannot both be the site's preferred framing.

**Methodological strength as a separate thing.** I want to be precise: the A2ACW protocol is publishable as an AI-collaboration paper *independent* of the physics. The role rotation, mandatory-challenge cadence, and CCH metric are concrete enough to evaluate, and the failure modes addressed are real and current. If the project bifurcates — physics paper for the BAO/wide-binary predictions, methodology paper for A2ACW with empirical evaluation of CCH against alternative protocols — both halves are stronger than the unified pitch.
