## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /beginner-path, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /equation-walkthrough, /gamma-calculator, /consciousness, /honest-assessment, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've ever seen — but the honesty is easier to follow than the actual idea.

### Journey

Clicked over from Reddit expecting either crackpot vibes or hype. Got neither. The landing page opens with *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* and the marching-band-vs-crowd analogy landed instantly for me. Then it immediately says *"Some predictions work. Some fail"* and **"0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted by external data"** — on the front page! That hooked me more than any claim would have. It reads like a theory that's live-streaming its own autopsy.

Followed the Beginner Path. Step 1 was mostly fine (crowd/band analogy again), but I hit words like "ansatz," "compander," and "Abrikosov-Gor'kov" on the *first beginner page*. I just skipped past them, but it felt like the training wheels came with a chainsaw attached.

"First Encounter" was the best-written thing I read — no equations at all, all pictures-in-words (single electron in vacuum vs. neutron star core). The "Markov Relevancy Horizon" bit — "everything beyond some horizon might as well not exist" — I *sort of* got it? Like how I don't care what someone in Tokyo is doing right now. But I couldn't tell you what decides where the bubble ends.

The Galaxy Curve Plotter was my favorite thing on the site. Pick a galaxy, see the gray dashed line (what gravity says should happen) miss the blue dots (what telescopes see). *"The gap is the 'dark matter problem.'"* I've watched three YouTube videos about dark matter and this one picture explained it better. And then the page tells you the theory's matching curve *"looks great but doesn't prove the idea"* because rival theories fit just as well. Appreciated that.

The Equation Walkthrough almost worked for me. The tanh-as-dimmer-switch analogy is genuinely good. But "ln" got one throwaway sentence and I never really recovered — every later formula had ln in it and I was just pattern-matching shapes.

The γ Calculator: I mashed the presets (Ideal gas → BEC) and watched the number change, which was fun for about a minute, but I honestly couldn't tell you what γ *is* or why I should care that water differs from a ferromagnet. The tool spends most of its screen warning me the numbers are "back-fits, not measurements" and describing a "structural inversion" — I trust it more but understand it less.

Consciousness page: this is what I came for as a YouTube-science person. "You = a brain pattern, like a song playing through speakers" — cool framing. But it immediately says it's "Speculative — not a finding" and "non-falsifiable as currently stated," so it deflated itself on purpose. Respect, but also: kind of a letdown?

Honest Assessment: skimmed it (it's huge). The "In plain terms" boxes saved me. What I took away: the equation ties a 40-year-old theory called MOND on galaxies, never beats it, and the big cosmic prediction went the wrong way. So... the theory doesn't work, and they're telling me so, at length.

Glossary: went to look up the words that had been bouncing off me. "Coherence" and "MRH" entries start well and then get harder. The tanh entry defines a simple thing simply, then adds "phenomenological choice motivated by Landau-universality," which is more confusing than tanh was.

Started at the landing page and mapped the top nav: 9 items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment, plus the wordmark). The landing page is long but has a clear top-to-bottom flow: hook → audience router ("No physics background? / Physicist? / Researcher?") → equation with inline symbol glosses → key claims → methodology → outcomes → coverage cards → wayfinding. The audience router is genuinely good practice.

Acronym hygiene on the landing page is better than most academic sites: MRH, MOND, RAR, BAO, A2ACW, and ALFALFA are all expanded at first use. Three slip through unexpanded: **SPARC** (first expanded three pages deep, on /key-claims), **ΛCDM**, and **CFD** ("a single-observer, CFD-like model" — the one place the site's "one distinctive move" is stated, and it leans on an undefined acronym).

The problems are at the edges, where individual pages drifted out of sync with it (see friction log).

Learning Paths: three difficulty tracks with numbered steps; the Beginner track (6 steps) matches what the landing page promises, step for step. The breadcrumbs are where the taxonomy wobbles: pages are breadcrumbed under section names ("Getting Started", "Core Theory", "Interactive Tools") that don't match the top nav — "Core Theory" (/mrh's breadcrumb parent) appears nowhere in the nav, and Honest Assessment, Key Claims, and Glossary all sit under "Getting Started" while the nav treats them as top-level. So the breadcrumb trail names categories you can't visit, in a taxonomy that exists nowhere else on the site.

The Glossary is the best single page on the site for my purposes — every entry has a short definition, an expanded paragraph, and a "Learn more" link, and hard entries (BIC, CHSH, Breit-Wigner) are written for non-specialists. But it's also where I found the site's worst consistency failure: the **a₀ entry** says a₀ "EMERGES from cosmology" and calls it "one of Synchronism's strongest results," while the landing page says the same result is "untested (a₀ ~ cH₀: dimensional rederivation, not independent convergence)" and /why-synchronism gives a third framing ("reproduced within 10% — but shared with MOND"). /why-synchronism (Beginner Step 1): the jargon Pass 1 complained about now mostly carries inline plain-language glosses ("phenomenological (plain words: picked because it matches the data...)"), which is the right pattern even if the page is wordy. But it has a structural fossil: a section titled **"What Worked"** whose first card ends in "Failed | TEST-03/05 Environment Run — Refuted by Execution." /mrh: the acronym page for the framework's most-used term is well-structured (definition → operational criteria → applications → "Open Gap"). ## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /equation-walkthrough, /key-claims, /interactive-tools, /honest-assessment, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /consciousness-demo, /galaxy-plotter, /two-reframes, /for-researchers)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The strongest critique of this framework is already on the site — my independent audit mostly reproduced theirs; what's left to fix is cross-page verdict consistency, not honesty.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," so I came in expecting either a crank unified theory or hype. I went straight for the equation and the derivations, planning to catch the usual sins: a sigmoid dressed up as Landau theory, fitted parameters presented as derived, MOND rediscovered and renamed.

They got there first. On every one of those counts.

**The equation.** C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)). First thing I checked: is this actually a phase transition? No — the argument is ≥ 0, so C lives in [0,1) with no critical point, no diverging correlation length, no exponents. The site says exactly this on /parameter-derivations, and explicitly rejects the Ising analogy on the correct grounds: Ising's m = tanh(βJzm) is a *self-consistency* equation; theirs is a direct map with no fixed-point iteration. That distinction is exactly what I'd write in a referee report, and they wrote it about themselves. They also state the identity tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1] — I verified it (it's exact; tanh(u) = (e^2u−1)/(e^2u+1) with e^2u = (1+x)^2γ), which means the "logarithmic compression" is cosmetic and the equation is just a Hill-type rational function. The site says the ln is "cosmetic" itself. I checked C(ρ_crit) at γ=2: tanh(2 ln 2) = 15/17 = 0.8824. Their number, to four digits.

**γ = 2/√N_corr.** This is the part I'd have led a journal-club takedown with: 1/√N is a fluctuation *width*, and putting it in the rate slot of tanh means the most correlated systems (BCS, N_corr ~ 10⁶⁻⁷) get the *flattest* transitions and a single free particle gets the sharpest — mean-field physics inverted. The site's own audit says "falsified placeholder" and adds the CLT-for-correlated-variables contradiction I was composing in my head as I read. Nothing left for me to catch.

**Parameter provenance.** /parameter-derivations is the page I came to attack and it's structured as a prosecution exhibit: A ≈ 0.029 has two incompatible scaling laws (V^0.5 from one session, V² site-wide) and the stated formula gives 4.6×10⁻⁵ — 600× off; worse, MOND-matching *forces* ρ_crit ∝ V⁻² (I re-derived it: at the transition radius r = V²/a₀, local density ~ V²/Gr² = a₀²/GV² — checks out), so the framework's V⁺² is sign-inverted, unfixable by recalibration. a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is correctly attributed to Milgrom-1983-onward dimensional analysis, and Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) is correctly flagged as the same number through a linear relation, not independent evidence — with the beautiful line that quoting 0.5% agreement against a quantity known to ±50% (Freeman's M/L) is "precision theater." The summary table's bottom line — zero parameters with first-principles derivations — matches what I found. I have never seen a framework website whose parameter page reads like the referee report I intended to write.

**Where I did find real problems** — all of them consistency, not honesty:

1. **/key-claims consciousness verdict contradicts itself in one card.** The badge line says "**both threshold values Failed**" citing Session 63 (t = 20.19, p ≈ 1.8×10⁻⁷ rejecting 0.50) — but the same card's caveat says that test "measured the *wrong observable*" (SNARC salience, not C) and the claim is "currently unrunnable." You cannot have both: if the only cited test measured the wrong variable, it can't ground a "Failed" verdict. /consciousness-demo gets this right ("Speculative — no calibration exists"; Session 63 "did not measure Synchronism's C"). The claims ledger is carrying a verdict its own evidence note retracts.

2. **Freeman's Law / Σ₀ numbers diverge across pages, and I can tell you why.** /key-claims: 119 M☉/pc², "≈4% error." /parameter-derivations: 123 M☉/pc², "0.5% match." /honest-assessment flags the inconsistency (119 vs 123.3) but leaves it unresolved. I recomputed: Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) gives 123.6 with H₀ = 70 and 119.0 with H₀ = 67.4. The pages are silently using different Hubble constants. Pick one H₀, state it, propagate — and honestly, given the ±50% on Freeman's normalization, report "matches to ~10⁻¹ precision, which is all the observable supports" and drop the sub-percent claims entirely (the site already knows this — "precision theater" — but still prints both percentages).

3. **DESI verdict strength differs by page.** /honest-assessment: "Kill criterion triggered on amplitude mismatch" (σ₈ = 0.841 ± 0.034 vs 0.76, 2.4σ). /for-researchers: "disfavored (2.4σ on σ₈, ~1.5σ on the registered statistic)... lacking discriminatory power." As a physicist: 2.4σ is not a kill by any convention I know, and ~1.5σ on the *registered* statistic is nothing. The /for-researchers framing is the defensible one; "kill criterion triggered" on /honest-assessment overstates its own failure. This site's characteristic error really does run in the self-refuting direction.

**CHSH.** The /two-reframes table is the kind of result I wish more people published: local substrate S = 1.98, nonlocal grid S ≡ 2.00, their own density substrate S = 1.85 — all ≤ 2 without signaling; 2.67 only by adding signaling. And the framing is exactly right: declaring the substrate "nonlocal by construction" doesn't evade Bell, it *chooses the nonlocal horn* — Bohm's horn. The triptych (real-local = 2 < Born cos² = 2√2 < PR box = 4) correctly localizes Tsirelson as a projection-law property. This is a clean negative result competently executed.

**BTFR (TEST-09).** The one place the framework genuinely differs from MOND — a bounded boost ceiling — produces a genuinely different prediction (deep-limit slope → Newtonian n = 2 rather than MOND's n = 4, net n = 3.35), and it failed against SPARC at 3.3σ past the pre-registered kill line. That's a real discriminating test, run, failed, reported. It's also the only one, which /for-researchers states flatly: "0 tests currently discriminating" it from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM.

### Tools Tested
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — worked — intuitive — This is how you present a null result interactively: 23 sortable properties with r up to 0.982, immediately undercut by a 2-parameter polynomial-in-Z null that matches (|Δr| ≤ 0.07) or beats every one, plus explicit multiple-comparison and non-independence warnings. That comparison — fitted stand-in vs. zero-tuning MOND — is the whole reparametrization verdict in one plot, and the page says so.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If γ = 2/√N_corr is a "falsified placeholder," why is it still printed inside the headline equation gloss on the landing page and walkthrough? Is there a candidate replacement, or is γ now officially a free per-system fit parameter with no microphysical claim?
2. Which verdict is canonical for the consciousness thresholds — /key-claims' "Failed" or /consciousness-demo's "unrunnable, no calibration exists"? These are different epistemic states and the pages disagree.
3. What H₀ is the site's standard? (The 119/123 split is exactly 67.4 vs 70 km/s/Mpc.)
4. Given "0 tests currently discriminating," is the program's stated purpose now the transferable negatives (locality no-go, CHSH substrate null, A2ACW null) rather than the framework itself? /for-researchers implies yes but no page says it as a mission statement.

### Verdict
I came to catch a sigmoid masquerading as Landau theory, a fitted parameter table masquerading as derivations, and MOND wearing a costume — and found all three already caught, correctly, by the site itself, with the Ising self-consistency distinction, the fluctuation-width-in-the-rate-slot inversion, and the "precision theater" critique stated more sharply than I'd have managed on a first read. Every number I could check by hand checked out (tanh(2 ln 2) = 15/17, the Hill identity, a₀ and Σ₀ arithmetic, the V⁻² requirement). What needs work now is boring by comparison: three cross-page verdict inconsistencies — one of which ("Failed" on a test of the wrong observable) repeats the site's documented habit of over-refuting itself. As physics, the framework is dead and knows it; as an artifact of how to publish negative results, it's the best I've seen, and I'd show /parameter-derivations to my group as a model of what honest parameter accounting looks like.

### Journey

I review for journals in this area, so I did what I'd do with a submission: skip everything that is known physics, hunt for any claim that (a) differs from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM, (b) has been tested, and (c) survived. My checklist: DESI treatment, the Gaia wide-binary handling (I know the Chae/Banik dispute well), the Bell/CHSH claims, the LIV sector, and the A2ACW methodology itself.

**What's actually new: nothing in the physics — three things in the negative-results ledger.** /for-researchers states the baseline flatly: "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, and 0 tests currently discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." I tried to falsify that self-assessment and could not. The genuinely transferable artifacts are: (1) the local-density no-go with its sign statement — a knee keyed on local volumetric density must fall as V⁻² to track an a₀ threshold, while the framework asserts V⁺² — which is profile-independent, correctly attributed as a quantified instance of Milgrom's non-locality argument, and correctly bounded by an escape taxonomy that names AeST (Skordis & Złośnik 2021) and superfluid DM (Berezhiani & Khoury 2015) as survivors; (2) the boost-ceiling pair TEST-09/TEST-10 (below); (3) the A2ACW program-level null (below, with a design objection).

**TEST-09/TEST-10 are the real event on this site, and the best work on it.** The bounded boost C(a) = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)x/(1+x) caps the gravitational boost at 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17 — the framework's *only* structural difference from MOND at its best-fit γ. That ceiling forces a Newtonian deep limit, hence a BTFR slope prediction n = 3.35 ± 0.07 that genuinely differs from MOND's 3.81. Run on SPARC (123 galaxies, Lelli 2016 mass models): observed n = 3.75 ± 0.10, kill criterion (>0.3) fires at 0.41, 3.3σ, and the page shows no parameter choice rescues it (best achievable 3.45). TEST-10 is the same ceiling read as a DM-fraction cap: f_DM ≤ 68.5%, versus 69% of SPARC exceeding it (median 0.755), with dwarf spheroidals at M_dyn/M_bar ~ 10²–10³ versus a cap of 3.17. This is exactly what a discriminating test looks like: a structural difference, a pre-stated threshold, external data, a fired kill, a no-rescue scan. Most modified-gravity papers I referee do not do this to their own models. The site also documents that these two tests previously carried a "MOND-shared" tie badge that was retired when someone actually computed both predictions — and inscribes the right rule: "a claimed tie carries the same execution burden as a claimed kill."

**But the front page hasn't caught up with the ledger — the headline stat is now wrong in the site's favor-of-failure direction, inconsistently.** The landing page says "Refuted by external data: 1" and attributes it to DESI fσ₈ (2.4σ) — the very test the site's own dated correction now describes as "the test as registered lacked the power to discriminate" (~1.5σ on the registered statistic). /key-claims also says "Refutations by external data: 1" but attributes it to the SPARC boost ratio instead. Meanwhile /tier-1-existing and /honest-assessment record TEST-09 *and* TEST-10 as executed kills on external data (2026-07-14/15). So the count appears stale (arguably 2, not 1), the attribution differs by page, and the one test still wearing the "Kill Criterion Triggered" badge in the /honest-assessment ledger table (TEST-04a, "2.4σ") is the one whose own section text on the same page retracts that strength. /tier-1-existing already has the correct label — "Disfavored on σ₈, Underpowered on Registered fσ₈" — it just never propagated to the ledger table or the landing headline.

**Wide binaries (my corner of the field): handled better than either camp handles the other.** TEST-02 states the framework predicts a Newtonian null at 0.05–0.4% velocity deviation, correctly notes this is far below the systematics floor of the current debate (the disputed Chae signal is a ~1.4× boost, i.e. ~18% in velocity; contamination from undetected hierarchical triples alone is a several-percent effect), presents Chae (2023–2025) vs Banik (2024) as an unresolved data-cut dispute rather than picking a winner, and draws the honest conclusion: "self-eliminating or tie" — no outcome selects this framework over the standard alternatives. The DESI treatment is similarly current: it cites the actual DESI modified-gravity paper (Ishak et al., arXiv:2411.12026, μ₀ = 0.11 +0.45/−0.54) and correctly reads its own 12% suppression as sitting within 1σ of GR there. The LIV sector is accurate and up to date — Collins et al. 2004 radiative c_μν ~ α/π, SME bounds to 10⁻²⁹⁻³⁰, the two named perturbative escapes (SUSY; Pospelov-Shang), LHAASO n=2 gap ~10⁷ — I'd sign off on that section as a referee.

**CHSH: correct, competently executed, not new.** All no-signaling constructions cap at S ≤ 2 (local 1.98, nonlocal-grid ≡2.00, their own density substrate 1.85); S = 2.67 only with signaling; the triptych (real-local 2 < Born cos² 2√2 < PR-box 4) correctly localizes Tsirelson in the projection law; and the page states the sharpest available objection against itself — a fast deterministic cycle through definite states is a non-contextual hidden-variable model, so Kochen–Specker excludes it at theorem level, before Bell even arrives. All of this is textbook-correct. Its value is that an "interpretation-flavored" framework actually ran the simulation and published the loss.

**A2ACW — the methodology question I came for. Verdict: the null is real but the design can't yet support its own headline.** The reported numbers: 3,308 sessions, ~47 internally-consistent survivors (1.4%), 6 externally audited, 0 survived; sensitivity 1.0, specificity 0, Youden's J = 0. The J = 0 framing ("a classifier that flags every demoted claim and every genuine discovery carries zero discriminating information") is the right statistic and a genuinely useful negative for the AI-for-science community. But: this is a single-arm, single-corpus retrospective with N = 6 — and the corpus itself was independently concluded to be a MOND reparametrization, so a 0% novelty yield is what a *perfect* filter would also report. The site states this confound honestly on /for-researchers ("cannot disambiguate whether the AI lacks out-of-distribution novelty or whether the seed framework contained none"). What I'd demand as a referee before this is publishable: a control arm — the same protocol run on a corpus containing known genuine discoveries post-dating the models' training cutoffs, to measure whether A2ACW can pass *anything* novel. And /research-philosophy has one sentence that drifts past the confound: it positions the all-reparametrizations meta-finding as "validation of the methodology's diagnostic power." A null on a possibly-null corpus validates nothing about diagnostic power; that sentence claims the very thing the single-arm design cannot establish, and contradicts the confound statement on /for-researchers.

### Tools Tested
- **Equation Anatomy (/equation-walkthrough)** — worked — intuitive — The only tool no prior pass tested. Six-step decomposition of C(ρ); the mathematical claims it makes are correct (I verified the Hill identity and C(ρ_crit) = 0.88 at γ=2 by hand), and the γ = 2/√N_corr step carries its "Audited-Negative" red box prominently, with both the sign inversion (BCS gets the flattest curve) and the CLT-on-correlated-variables contradiction stated on-tool. A walkthrough that teaches an equation while displaying the audit that killed its microphysical story is a genre I have not seen before; it works.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter (expert implementation check)** — worked — one real accuracy issue — Passes 1 and 3 tested honesty and UX; I checked the physics inside. MOND curve: standard simple-ν interpolation ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) with a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², no per-galaxy tuning — correct and fairly represented. Synchronism curve: ρ_crit refit per galaxy, disclosed on-page, correctly framed as BIC-dominated by MOND's single global constant. The flaw is the *Newtonian* baseline: spherical enclosed mass for an exponential disk instead of the Freeman thin-disk formula, which understates the baryonic prediction by ~15–20% near the peak and thereby modestly inflates the visual "dark matter gap" that is the page's pedagogical centerpiece (see friction log). Not fatal for outreach; a referee would make them fix it.

### Unanswered Questions
1. After the 2026-07-14/15 executions, what is the canonical refutation count and referent? The landing page, /key-claims, and the ledger currently give three different answers to "which single test refuted this framework."
2. Is a control arm planned for A2ACW — the same adversarial protocol run on a corpus with known post-cutoff genuine discoveries? Without it, J = 0 on N = 6 from a null corpus is an anecdote, not a measurement, and the one publishable methodology claim stays out of reach.
3. TEST-09's failure is attributed to "Ω_m in the floor and φ in the exponent." Is φ the golden ratio, and where is its derivation? If a fitted exponent is being written as φ, that's numerology the parameter-derivations page would normally catch — I couldn't find it addressed.
4. The DESI unfreeze condition (DR2 full-shape, fσ₈(z≈0.5) ≤ 0.46, ~Spring 2027) is well-posed — will the site commit now, in writing, to the verdict update rule for both outcomes? That's the pre-registration discipline the roadmap says it wants and has 0 completed instances of.

### Verdict
As a researcher I can be brief about the physics: there is nothing here that survives contact with data and differs from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM, the site says so itself, and my independent checks (wide-binary systematics floor, DESI μ₀ constraint, LIV bounds, CHSH cap, disk dynamics) confirm its self-assessment is accurate and current with the literature — which is rare. What this site actually is, is a well-run negative-results program: the boost-ceiling pair (TEST-09/10) is a model discriminating test honestly lost, the local-density sign no-go is citable, and the A2ACW J = 0 null is the most interesting artifact for my community — currently unpublishable for want of a control arm. The sharpest thing I can say is an irony: after eighteen months of documented over-refutation habits, the site's front page now *under*-counts its own refutations because the headline never caught up with last week's ledger. The failure modes have inverted from dishonesty-adjacent to bookkeeping — which is, for what it's worth, the failure mode real collaborations have.
