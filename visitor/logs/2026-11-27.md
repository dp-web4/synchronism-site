## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

> Method note (for the maintainer, outside the persona): no WebFetch tool was available in this session. Pages were fetched live with an HTTP GET and stripped to full visible text (scripts removed, collapsed `<details>` content included), so this pass read the whole server-rendered text rather than a summary. Sliders could not be dragged. The tools were tested on their server-rendered default state, preset lists and readouts, and the default readouts were checked by hand (noted where used).

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Coherence Explorer, Galaxy Curve Plotter, What Synchronism Is Not, Honest Assessment top, γ Calculator, Glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A strangely likeable site about an idea that lost. I came away knowing what it tried and that it failed, but the pages keep pulling me into rooms full of Greek letters and dated correction notes I never asked for.

### Journey
I came from a Reddit link expecting a "theory of everything" crank site. The first thing on the page is a note from the founder saying there is "no physics and no math, only magic" and that our models are "spells". My first reaction was "uh oh". The next paragraphs changed my mind: it says the theory "did not hold as stated" and that the site is about watching "a big idea get tested in public and lose honestly". I've never seen a site lead with its own failure. That hooked me more than a big claim would have. The "magic" framing still almost made me close the tab, and someone skimming only the first screen could take this for mysticism.

The hero section is a wall of text, though. One paragraph runs from the marching band through AI agents, the coherence disclaimer, superconductors, MOND and retrodiction, then into "alive or dead", Bell tests and Gaia DR4. I skimmed it. The things that stuck: one number from 0 (crowd) to 1 (marching band), it's computed from density, it was tested, and it turned into an older idea called MOND. The "New here? Press Start here now" line is good, and I did. The landing page has at least three "New here? stop scrolling" signs, which tells me the authors know the page is too long.

**Why Synchronism?** "What happened, in three lines" is the best thing I read all session: proposal, it lost (0 confirmed, 6 refutations), the bigger question is still open. The "why does matching an existing theory count as losing?" explanation finally made sense to me: copying the old answer doesn't earn you anything. I stopped where it said beginners could stop.

**First Encounter.** The density bubble (MRH) and the dimmer-switch explanations are friendly. The neutron star versus lone electron picture worked. Section C lost me: "predicted correlation +0.55, measured −0.55", "N_corr", "γ in three lines" with three numbered γ's. I understood "the knob they guessed was wrong and the knob that fits is MOND's", and that's enough. Section G's numbers (melting points 53% off, superconductor 607 K vs 93 K) were the clearest "it failed" moment I had: I can picture 607 vs 93.

**Coherence Explorer (tool).** The top of the page is a good beginner intro ("drag γ up to 2, then down to 0.5 and watch the S flatten"). It opens at γ = 1 with C(ρcrit) = 0.60, C(10ρcrit) = 0.98 and C(100ρcrit) = 0.9998 shown under "Key Values". There are five preset buttons. Two of them both seem to be about galaxies: "γ = 0.05 · density fit (lost)" and "γ = 0.49 · galaxy fit". As a beginner I can't tell why there are two galaxy fits on one slider, or which one this chart (which is about density) belongs to. The answer is in a "For physicists" fold. The axis label says "a galaxy disc sits ~10³ below the knee the framework asserts for it", which meant nothing to me.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (tool).** This is the page where I actually *saw* the dark-matter problem. The "three things to look at" intro is great: dots stay high, gray line sags, green (MOND) follows the dots, violet (this theory) doesn't. Five galaxy buttons (DDO 154, NGC 2403, UGC 128, NGC 3198, NGC 7331) and a "how heavy are the stars" slider. Then I got confused, because three passages describe the violet line differently for the galaxy the page opens on (DDO 154):
- The legend right under the chart: "The violet line is Newton's shape ×1.78 in speed", i.e. the same boost everywhere.
- The calibration readout above the chart (in a "For physicists" fold): "1.44× in speed at the disc's densest point, rising to the 1.78× speed ceiling where it thins out", i.e. *not* the same everywhere.
- "How to read this chart", step 3: at the default setting "the line is near the gray one where the disc is dense". But the readout says this galaxy is "too faint for the switch to turn on anywhere" and boosted everywhere.

As a beginner I trust whatever sits closest to the picture, so I'd believe the legend, which seems to be the outdated one. (Outside the persona: the 1.44 checks out, √(1/(0.315 + 0.685 × 0.244)) = 1.44, so the legend's constant ×1.78 describes the old "units hybrid" setting, not the default since 2026-11-14.) The paragraph "Why a second (amber) curve? … The solid one is what the equation actually produces" also reads as if the theory's curve is amber, when the intro just told me it's violet and amber is the hidden hand-drawn one.

**What Synchronism Is Not.** The five plain bullets near the top are excellent. "Not checked by science journals … AI models reviewing each other's work" was eye-opening: I didn't realize from the landing page how central the AI part is. One bullet introduces a "built-in universal clock" that breaks a rule of relativity. That's the first time I'd heard of a clock in this theory, and the beginner path never set it up.

**Honest Assessment (top only, as the Beginner Path says).** The no-jargon paragraph is honest and readable, but the count tripped me up: "six executed refutations resting on two framework-specific mechanism roots, **plus** one refuted registration, one constraint inherited from MOND, and one Bell/CHSH construction check." I read that as 6 + 3 = 9. The footer on every page says the six *include* those (3 + 1 + 1 + 1). Also, the page tells me to "read the box below and the one-paragraph plain summary under it", but the plain summary comes *before* the "If you read nothing else" box.

**γ Calculator (tool).** A clear "this is a broken formula, kept to show how it breaks" framing, which I liked. It opens at N_corr = 4, γ = 1.0000, "Crossover band". The presets run from ideal gas to BCS superconductor, and the quick-reference table makes the backwards trend obvious: the superconductor gets γ = 0.00063, the flattest. "Galaxy, fitted to data … N_corr ≈ 17, not a count of anything" made me laugh. It's honest, but it also says the number is meaningless, so why show it?

**Glossary.** I followed "ten words to learn first" (the anchor exists). The ten words are listed in one line, then straight below them comes a big table of label families (MRH-relationship badges, ledger classes, "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie", "Empty Intersection"…). It says "four labels are verdicts, and they are the only ones you need", then lists about twenty members. I backed out. The tanh entry says it "rises steeply at first, then levels off". First Encounter said the dimmer switch is "flat at first, then rising, then levelling off". Which is it? (I suspect one is drawn against density on a log scale and one isn't, but nobody told me that.)

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (server-rendered default) — intuitive top intro, confusing preset labels. Defaults γ = 1 → C(ρcrit) = 0.6000, C(10ρcrit) = 0.9836, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9998, 10–90% width 1.50 dex, midpoint at 0.732ρcrit. All of these check by hand (outside persona). Sliders not dragged.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked (default DDO 154, Υ = 0.5, archive calibration) — the best "aha" on the site for the dark-matter problem itself, but the legend under the chart contradicts the calibration readout above it (see friction). The readout's 1.44× checks by hand.
- **γ Calculator**: worked (default N_corr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, "Crossover band"; seven presets and quick-reference table rendered) — intuitive "watch a broken formula break" framing. Regime names don't match the Coherence Explorer's: the Explorer calls γ < 0.6 "many particles moving together", while the Calculator splits it into "Strongly Correlated" (0.2–0.6) and "Collective" (< 0.2).

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the theory failed, what is the "bigger question" that's still open, in one sentence I could repeat to a friend?
2. Why is the founder's note about magic and spells, and how does that connect to an equation about density?
3. Who are the AI agents, and has *any* human physicist outside the project looked at this?
4. What is the "universal clock", and why does it break relativity?
5. Is MOND then right? The plotter says MOND fails a Cassini test too. What is the current best explanation for the galaxy problem?
6. What happens on 2 December with Gaia DR4, and what should I look for?

### Verdict
As a curious Reddit visitor, I'd say this is one of the most honest science sites I've seen. It tells you up front that its big idea lost, and the Galaxy Plotter genuinely taught me what the dark-matter problem *is*. But the beginner signposts are fighting a flood of revision notes, Greek letters and bookkeeping. The places where the text contradicts itself (the plotter legend, the refutation count) are exactly where a beginner trusts the nearest sentence and walks away with the wrong picture.

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 read in substance (landing, Learning Paths, Interactive Tools, Glossary label map, Honest Assessment badge/state/verb sections, MRH, Key Claims, Test Roadmap, γ Regimes, γ ≈ 1 Band, Equation Anatomy, Crossover Regime Visualizer). I also checked headers, breadcrumbs and acronyms on 7 more pages (Coherence Function, Galaxy Rotation, Parameter Derivations, CDM Discrimination, Tier 1, For Researchers, γ Calculator regime table).
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the site's structure); partial (of its label vocabulary, which I could catalogue but not hold in my head)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an auditor. As a reader, maybe.
- **Overall impression**: The site has unusually strong information-architecture instincts: canonical definition sections, path-position bars, explicit "this is not a badge" disambiguations. But the vocabulary has grown faster than those instincts can keep it consistent, and the same concept is now named differently on neighbouring pages.

### Journey
**Landing → navigation.** The top nav is the same on every page I checked: Start Here · Learning Paths · Tier 1 · Test Roadmap · Interactive Tools · Glossary · Research Philosophy · For Researchers · Honest Assessment. That's good. The footer repeats it and carries the refutation summary, which is single-sourced (identical wording on every page I fetched). The landing page runs to about 9,400 words. The "Where to Start" section is the *last* H2, at line 174 of 197 in my text dump, after about nine thousand words. Before it come six separate "New here? / Start here" interjections telling the reader to stop. The interjections work, but they patch a page whose own wayfinding section is buried at the bottom. The six "Where to Start" cards (Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Galaxy Plotter, Test Roadmap, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment) also don't match the Beginner Path's five steps (that path has What Synchronism Is Not, not Test Roadmap or Research Philosophy). So there are two different "start here" sequences.

**Learning Paths.** This is a well-built page. It honestly states that the difficulty paths and topic tracks are *not* equivalent ("difficulty paths reach 10 pages no topic track does … topic tracks reach 14 the difficulty paths don't"), and it gives time budgets. Destination pages carry a "Physics Track · Step 2 of 7 · ← Prev / Next →" bar. When a page sits on two paths, it shows both bars stacked (Coherence Function: Intermediate step 2 *and* Physics step 1; Galaxy Rotation: Intermediate 5 *and* Physics 5). That is the right call. One quirk: the Learning Paths page lists *Decisive Tests (none currently running)* as Intermediate step 9, while Tier 1 describes the same page as "the May 2026 ranked shortlist, now empty". "None running" and "empty" are different claims.

**Breadcrumbs.** They are present on every interior page I checked. The middle crumb is a section name rendered as non-clickable italic text, with a tooltip ("Site section: 'Core Theory' (no index page; see Learning Paths)"). That's honest, but four of the section names I met (Core Theory, Cosmology, Chemistry, Predictions & Experiments) are dead ends. One inconsistency: **Tier 1's breadcrumb is Home / Test Roadmap / Tier 1** (Test Roadmap is its parent, and a real link), but **Test Roadmap's own breadcrumb is Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap**. The hierarchy changes depending on which page you are standing on. Equation Anatomy and the Crossover Visualizer correctly sit under Interactive Tools.

**Badges and labels.** This is the site's biggest documentation problem, and also the place where it has tried hardest. The Honest Assessment has a canonical "Status badge definitions" section (id `validation-badge-definitions`, a legacy id: the heading says they were "called 'validation badges' until 2026-11-16, though none of them is a validation"). It has a compact "nine badges in one table" summary, a "how to read a badge" contract (formal word, then dash, then free-text descriptor) and a "which label answers which question" key. The Glossary has a "How to read labels on this site" map. Both are good. But here is my inventory of the label systems a reader can meet:
1. Status badges, MRH-relationship family (5)
2. Status badges, descriptive family (4)
3. Retired badges still on some pages (3)
4. Scoreboard words (8, plus "outcome words such as Disfavoured")
5. Ledger classes (4)
6. Operational states / "test history" (7, including "Artifact Lesson (tools only)")
7. Lifecycle / history verbs (11: Executed, Adjudicated, Closed, Retired, Reclassified, Never run as registered, Underpowered as registered, Ceiling Exceeded, Empty Intersection, Demoted — Exploratory, Closed by Execution)
8. Caveats in brackets (Post-diction, Caveat)
9. Tool Kind / Level / State (3 slots)
10. Free-text badge descriptors, which are by contract "not additional badge types", yet routinely run to a sentence or more.

That's ten systems. The Glossary opens with "Four labels are verdicts, and they are the only ones you need". That's a good triage sentence, but "four labels" actually means four *label systems* containing roughly twenty members, and Pass 1 tripped on exactly that. The badge contract is also strained in practice. Several Honest Assessment H3 headings carry badges whose descriptor is a paragraph. RAR Transition Shape: "Failed — counted among the six (row #3); kill fired on the frozen-Υ +184, while the registered marginalized statistic reads ≈ 2σ (disfavoured); recount gates on the project lead". BTFR Slope's badge descriptor is about 60 words, with three convention values. When the descriptor is longer than the claim it labels, the badge is no longer scannable. The pill has turned into a status paragraph.

The lifecycle section also mixes two systems in one sentence: "Transitions: statuses move in one direction. Untested → Executed/Adjudicated → Failed (or survives)". Untested and Failed are *badges*; Executed and Adjudicated are *verbs*. The page has just spent three paragraphs telling me those are different axes.

**Acronyms.** First-use expansion is mostly good. I sampled about 40 acronyms on the landing page, Key Claims and Test Roadmap. MOND, SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, CHSH, DESI, KiDS, BAO, A2ACW, ΛCDM, EFE, IIT, PCI and QUMOND are all expanded at first use or covered by the landing page's "Abbreviations ahead" key. Misses:
- **LLR** appears on the landing page ("the LLR item", in a revision note) and on Test Roadmap ("excluded by SPARC and LLR") with no expansion. "Lunar laser ranging" is spelled out elsewhere on the landing page, but the two are never tied together. LLR is not in the Glossary.
- **CAI** on the landing page: the link text reads "how the substrate differs from CAI". The full name ('t Hooft's "Cellular Automaton Interpretation") appears in the preceding sentence, but the abbreviation is never introduced as "(CAI)". It's not in the Glossary.
- **PPN** on Test Roadmap ("PPN γ = 0 (Cassini …)") is not expanded and not in the Glossary. It is also a third distinct "γ" for a reader who already juggles the framework's γ and the Lorentz-type γ.
- **RG** on Test Roadmap ("RG's own record in this archive is mixed") is not expanded at that point. "Refracted Gravity" exists in the Glossary, but nothing on that page links the abbreviation to it.
- Key Claims has a meta note saying that "acronyms (MRH, CRT, PRL, CHSH, ALFALFA–SDSS, EEG/fMRI, AQUAL) are expanded at first use". On that page the note itself is CHSH's first textual occurrence. A note about first-use expansion shouldn't be the first use.

**MRH.** The term is used consistently: "Markov Relevancy Horizon" in all 16+ occurrences, with no "Relevance" variants. The page states the naming collision with the "MRH-relationship" badge family plainly ("named by analogy … say nothing about physics"). The page's information flow is abrupt, though. It goes from a lovely plain line ("the bubble of neighbors that matter") straight into a section full of SPARC/BTFR/ΔBIC/B_max/Ω_m/Fisher-correlation material ("Does the framework respect its own horizon?"). That section has no "technical section ahead" signpost, although other pages use one.

**Title, slug and heading drift.** The site has clearly worked on this before: the γ Regimes revision note records the slug, H1 and badge "said four different things", and the Visualizer was renamed for the same reason. One instance survives. **/gamma-boundary** has the HTML `<title>` "The γ ≈ 1 Boundary" (what a browser tab, a bookmark or a search result shows), while its H1 and breadcrumb say "The γ ≈ 1 Band (the 'boundary' reading is retracted)". The tab and the URL still assert the retracted reading.

**Regime names: the same γ ranges carry four vocabularies.** This is the clearest terminology defect of the pass:
| Page | γ > 1.4 | 0.6–1.4 | < 0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crossover Regime Visualizer (bar) | Single-particle | Crossover band | Collective |
| Crossover Regime Visualizer (index cards) | Single-particle | Crossover band | High-N_corr (strongly correlated) |
| γ Calculator (table) | Weakly Correlated | Crossover band | Strongly Correlated (0.2–0.6) **+** Collective (< 0.2) |
| γ Regimes page (diagram) | Few-body | **Boundary** | Many-body collective |
| Coherence Explorer (per Pass 1) | — | — | "many particles moving together" |

"Collective" means γ < 0.6 on the Visualizer and γ < 0.2 on the Calculator. The γ Regimes page, whose H2 says "boundary reading retracted", still labels its middle band "Boundary" in its own diagram. The crossover band's N_corr range is "3–11" on the Visualizer (which explains why: γ = 1.4 ⇔ N_corr ≈ 2.04, so N_corr = 2 falls in the top band) but "≈ 2–11" on the Calculator.

**Same item handled in opposite ways.** The Visualizer's revision note says "Phase transitions" was *removed* from the γ ≈ 1 band's system list because C(ρ) has no phase transition. The γ Regimes page, titled "Crossovers, Not Phase Transitions", still lists "Phase transitions cluster here (melting, boiling, sublimation)". I can see the intended distinction (real-world phase transitions vs. a transition in C), but the two pages draw it oppositely.

**Stale slot name.** The Interactive Tools index says the third slot is "State" ("called 'Status' until 2026-11-16"). The Crossover Visualizer's tool-type caption still says "the Interactive Tools index Kind and **Status** tags".

**Process notes in teaching text.** Equation Anatomy, the Visualizer and the γ Regimes page all embed dated revision history in the main flow: who caught what, which persona, on which day. Many of these sit in proper "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)" folds, which is the right pattern. But a lot of it is inline in the body, e.g. Step 3: "derived independently by two successive internal review passes, 2026-07-09 and 2026-07-10 — the audit machinery caught it, and the identity took a day to reach this page". For documentation, the rule I'd apply is: current claim in the body, history in the fold, always. The site has that convention. It just isn't applied everywhere.

**The Coherence Function.** I checked every algebraic claim I could. All of them hold (recomputed):
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = ((1+x)^{2γ} − 1)/((1+x)^{2γ} + 1), and at γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), exactly.
- dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x), so C is concave on x ≥ 0 with no critical point.
- The small-x expansion is C = γx − γx²/2 + …, and its gap from γx/(1+γx) is γ(2γ−1)x²/2, which vanishes at γ = ½.
- The large-x tail is 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ}.
- The 10–90 % widths are 1.91 / 1.50 / 1.33 / 1.20 dex at γ = ½ / 1 / 2 / 10. The γ → ∞ limit is log₁₀(atanh 0.9 / atanh 0.1) = 1.17 dex.
- The large-γ log-axis inflection 2u tanh u = 1 gives u ≈ 0.77, so x ≈ 0.77/γ.
- The "Λ at γ = ½" reading works because ρ/C = ρ + 2ρ_crit identically at γ = ½: a constant additive density, so ρ_crit = ρ_Λ/2. That is correctly called a reparametrization.

### Verdict
As a referee, I agree with the site's verdict and would sharpen it. The galaxy sector is Famaey–Binney's simple μ, the cosmology is ΛCDM (or prior-art Cardassian), and the only distinct branches are either excluded (density keying) or uncommitted (grid LIV). Nothing here is a new physics result, but there is something worth having: the lunar-ranging "cancels in the ratio, leaks into the rate" null for ambient-density-keyed gravity, a quantified local-density no-go, a well-constructed DR4 trend statistic, and a well-framed open question about whether LLM auditors can recognise novelty. The physics bookkeeping is better than most papers I review. The numbers I checked are right except where pages disagree with each other (the Cassini σ is the clearest case). The binding constraint is no longer data. It is the twelve undecided forks, and the fact that no human outside the project has yet been handed one of the citable results.
