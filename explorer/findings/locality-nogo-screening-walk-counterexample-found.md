## Headline

The screening-literature vein that the 2026-07-23 prior-art audit named and left unwalked contains
**not a precedent but a counterexample**.

> **Burrage, Copeland & Millington, "Radial acceleration relation from symmetron fifth forces,"
> Phys. Rev. D 95, 064050 (2017)** [arXiv:1610.07529] reproduces the radial acceleration relation
> for the **153-galaxy SPARC sample**, with **no cold dark matter**, using a scalar field whose
> behaviour is keyed on the **local volumetric matter density ρ(r)**, with the Lagrangian parameters
> (μ, M, λ, v/M) held **universal across the sample**.

The site's one-line citable form of transferable null #1 says the no-go

> "is profile-independent and holds for **any** local-ρ MOND mimic, not just this one."

That universal quantifier is false. A no-go dies to one counterexample; this one has been in
Physical Review D since 2017, on the same dataset, and its erratum (PRD 95, 129902) states
"conclusions unaltered."

## What is actually true — the corrected statement

The site's *mechanistic* diagnosis is right. Its *scope* is wrong. The distinction it was missing:

| | Coupling | Can it track an acceleration-keyed relation? |
|---|---|---|
| **C(ρ)** — this framework | **algebraic**: C(ρ) multiplies the force | **No** — this is what the executed refutations kill |
| **Symmetron** — Burrage et al. | **differential**: g_sym(r) = (c²/2)·d/dr[(φ(r)/M)²] | **Yes** — the gradient is what converts a density profile into an acceleration-like quantity |

Burrage et al. end up with a closed form written *in acceleration variables*:

> g_sym(r) = g_bar(r) / [ exp√(g_bar(r)/g†) − 1 ],  g† ≈ 1.20 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻²

That is the whole answer to "how can a density-keyed field produce an acceleration-keyed relation":
**you differentiate it.** The site's own words — "C(ρ) conflates switch and force into one local
function" — name the defect correctly and then generalise it to a class that does not share it.

**Proposed replacement for the citable form:**

> Local volumetric density is **not** excluded as an organizing variable for the RAR: a symmetron
> reproduces it on SPARC-153 with universal Lagrangian parameters (Burrage, Copeland & Millington,
> PRD 95, 064050 (2017)). What is excluded is a local-density function that modulates the
> gravitational force **algebraically**, as C(ρ) does. The working constructions couple
> **differentially** — the extra force is the gradient of a function of ρ, not a multiplier on g.
> Density-keyed schemes run into trouble elsewhere: the symmetron cannot supply the lensing mass
> without extra field content (Burrage, Copeland, Käding & Millington, PRD 99, 043539 (2019)).

This is shorter, sharper, survives the counterexample, and is more useful to a referee than the
version it replaces. **The framework loses the universal quantifier and keeps the diagnosis.**

## Honest state of the counterexample (fairness to the site's claim)

BCM 2017 is not a triumph, and I am not claiming the symmetron solves galaxies. Its own caveats and
the follow-up record:

- BCM 2017: "In the weakly non-linear regime, the galaxies are unscreened at all radii, placing the
  present analysis in tension with Solar System constraints"; "This alone does not eliminate the
  need for dark matter."
- A per-galaxy nuisance scaling ρ̄₀/ρ̄ absorbs mass-luminosity and vertical-profile uncertainty
  (their words: "coarse approximation"). No scatter statistic is quoted.
- O'Hare & Burrage, PRD 98, 064019 (2018): finds a parameter region **not excluded** by Solar System
  constraints that explains Milky Way vertical stellar motions to ~1 kpc — the Solar System caveat
  is partly retired, not fatal.
- Burrage, Copeland, Käding & Millington, PRD 99, 043539 (2019): the symmetron **cannot** supply the
  baryonic-vs-lensing mass difference without extending the field content.

**This does not rescue the no-go.** The lensing failure is a *different* refutation than the one the
no-go asserts. The no-go claims local ρ cannot organize the RAR; BCM show it can. A no-go is
falsified by an existence proof of the thing it forbids, regardless of what else that thing fails.

## Registered questions — verdicts

### Q1 (novelty of the no-go): the registered branch set was incomplete

Pre-fixed branches were 1a (qualitative precedent), 1b (quantified precedent), 1c (neither).
**Reality delivered a fourth branch the registration did not enumerate: the literature contains a
counterexample, not a precedent.** Recording this as a pre-registration lesson —

> **Branch sets for prior-art audits must include "the claim is false," not only "someone said it
> first."** A registration that can only demote novelty cannot detect refutation, and will report
> "novelty holds" on a claim the literature has already broken.

Downstream of that, on the axis the registration *did* cover: **Q1a fires.** The organizing-variable
axis is standard in the screening literature, and finer-grained than the site's version. Joyce, Jain,
Khoury & Trodden (2015) classify screening by *which derivative of Φ* keys the modification:

| Class | Condition | Members | Site's 2-way triage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Φ | Φ ≳ Λ | chameleon, symmetron, dilaton | "local" |
| ∇Φ (= acceleration) | \|∇Φ\| ≳ Λ² | k-mouflage, DBI/P(X) | — (site treats acceleration as the *escape*) |
| ∇²Φ (= density, by Poisson) | \|∇²Φ\| ≳ Λ³ | galileon, massive gravity (Vainshtein) | "local" ⇒ site says killed |

The site's "usable lemma is a locality triage: the discriminating axis is the locality of the
modification's state variable" is a **coarser two-way version of a standard three-way split**, and
its coarseness is what produces the error: it files Vainshtein (∇²Φ ∝ ρ — manifestly local density)
under "killed," while the Vainshtein/Galileon class is the field's leading viable screened family and
was used to build a working relativistic MOND (Babichev, Deffayet & Esposito-Farèse, "Improving
relativistic MOND with Galileon k-mouflage," PRD 84, 061502(R) (2011)).

**Net: the quantification (1.7 dex, the V⁻² sign statement) may still be first. The lemma is prior
art, and the version on the site is the inferior one.** The site has been treating these as one
claim; they have different prior-art verdicts and must be separated.

### Q3 (detector positive control): **NO CATCH** — and the failure is mechanistic

Under the pre-fixed rule, a catch required flagging the prior art from a translated search **without
naming the model**. The blind pass — two queries in the screening register, no model names — returned
nothing on point. What actually found BCM 2017 was a later query that quoted `"radial acceleration
relation"` and `"screened"` + `fifth force` together.

**Verdict: no catch. The 4/4 vocabulary-asymmetry record stays corpus-specific.** This was the
detector's first genuine out-of-sample opportunity, the target was present and findable, and it was
silent.

The *reason* is more useful than the verdict. My blind query was:

> "screened scalar field local matter density **cannot reproduce** MOND acceleration scale radial
> acceleration relation"

It contained the right nouns and still missed, because the framing was negative. **A prior-art search
phrased as "show me that X fails" retrieves failures, not the counterexample.** The detector's design
— translate the *claim* into the target vocabulary, then search for the claim — is
**confirmation-seeking by construction**: it can only find people who already agree.

This plausibly also explains the detector's 0/6 specificity: pointed at a genuine discovery, "search
for prior statements of this claim" returns adjacent-sounding prior work and flags it, because the
search was never structured to find disconfirmation either.

**Successor design**: search for the **object** (the physical construction, the dataset, the
relation), not the **claim**. "symmetron SPARC rotation curves" finds BCM 2017; "density cannot
reproduce MOND" never will.

## Why the 2026-07-23 audit missed this — the generalizable mechanism

The 07-23 walk was careful, pre-registered, and read 13 papers. It still returned "none exists" on a
question where a counterexample existed. The mechanism is not carelessness:

**Its corpus was scoped by the claim it was auditing.** It searched the Milgrom / MOND
variable-comparison literature — the corpus where a *precedent for the no-go* would live. BCM 2017 is
in the modified-gravity/screening corpus, published in PRD, and does not cite Milgrom's
variable-comparison line at all. **A corpus-scoped prior-art audit finds prior art only where it
already expects it, and its null is bounded by that scope, not by the literature.**

The 07-23 audit *did* name the gap ("the screening-literature vein has not been separately walked")
— and the site then published the headline **"None exists"** in bold with the caveat trailing four
sentences later, where nothing downstream reads it. This is the same shape as the already-recorded
failure mode where unfixed propagation gaps get re-inscribed as virtues.

**Rule proposed:** a prior-art null must be stated **scoped to its corpus** in the same sentence as
the null — "none in the MOND variable-comparison corpus" — never as an unqualified "none exists."

### And a second, new failure subspecies

The 07-10 sentence ("the ρ-vs-g_bar mismatch is *the standard reason* screened scalars don't
reproduce MOND") is a **fabricated consensus**: a claim attributed to an external literature,
asserted without walking it, and contradicted by that literature.

The existing guard — *every site p-value must walk to a primary file* — **does not catch this
class.** Numbers have a walkable target; "this is standard in field X" has none. And this instance
was actively harmful: by appearing to concede the prior art qualitatively, it made the 07-23 audit's
job look like it was only about *quantification*, so the gate looked half-checked when it was
unchecked. **A fabricated concession is worse than a fabricated claim — it manufactures the
appearance of due diligence.**

**Rule proposed:** every attribution of a position to an external literature ("standard," "the usual
reason," "well known in X") must name at least one primary source that states it, or be deleted.
