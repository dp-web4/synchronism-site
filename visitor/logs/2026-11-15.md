## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment (top box), /coherence-explorer, /interactive-tools, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science website I've ever seen, and that honesty arrives so fast and so dense that I finished the five-step Beginner Path knowing it failed but not really what "it" was.

### Journey
Clicked through from Reddit. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", is exactly the PBS Space Time hook I came for. The first paragraph answers it straight away: every system gets "one number between 0 (a crowd of strangers) and 1 (a marching band)", then "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." Then: this is an AI-run notebook that "no outside physicist has reviewed". I respect that a lot. I also thought: wait, so why am I here? The page is very long. There's a table ("Where each piece stands") whose answers are "Is MOND", "Is Λ" and "Inside Bell's limit", none of which I can decode. There's a scoreboard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, "4–8 depending on counting rule") and tiles counting 3,308 "A2ACW sessions". I skimmed most of it. The "why keep reading" argument (something about an autopsy) is buried halfway down in the Scientific Outcomes section. I'd have liked it next to the headline.

I clicked the big "Start here" button, **Why Synchronism?** (step 1 of 5). The opening is good. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" made sense to me, and so did "What happened, in three lines" (proposal → it lost where it could be told apart → the bigger question is still open). The crowd vs. marching band analogy and the dimmer switch analogy both worked. Then, on step 1 of 5, paragraph 4 said "Beginner path: you can stop here." Stop already? I'd learned the idea failed but hadn't seen it yet. Below that everything was "folded technical detail". I peeked in. From what I got I couldn't tell where the optional part ended: "M☉/pc³", "audited-negative — sign-inverted for all collective systems" and the superconductor numbers all seemed to sit between the optional blocks. I backed out.

**First Encounter** (step 2, "about 10 minutes", sections A–G) was the best page for me. "Coherence is how collectively a group of things behaves" is a clear first line. The MRH "bubble" ("A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy") clicked. The explanation of ln ("10×, 100×, 1000× become steps of about 2.3, 4.6, 6.9") is the first time a log has ever been friendly to me. Then from Section C on it lost me. The γ formula "did not survive testing". Fitting γ gives "exactly MOND's own formula, so it matches MOND by construction". Section D talks about chemistry fits clustering near γ ≈ 1 "with a template-bias caveat and no mechanism", under a heading with the word "reparametrization" in it. Section E says a match with a cosmic number is "probably a coincidence". By Section G ("Where It Fails": 53% melting-point error, "607 K predicted for YBCO, 93 K actual") I understood the *mood* (lots of failure, said openly) better than the *idea*. BEC, BCS and YBCO were never spelled out for me. (The first section showed up as "Aρ" rather than "A". That may be a badge running into the letter, I'm not sure.)

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** (step 3) has a great intro: "New here? Look at three things on the chart below." It tells me a rotation curve "is just how fast things orbit, plotted against distance from the galaxy's centre" and that "the dots are what telescopes measure." "Plain verdict for casual readers" and "In plain words: the only version of this framework's equation that fits these dots is MOND's formula written differently" were the two sentences I came away with. After that the tool got crowded. There are up to six line styles (dots, dashed gray, solid violet, dashed light violet, dotted amber behind a checkbox, dashed green), a "How heavy are the stars? Υdisk" slider described as "mass per unit of 3.6 μm light", and a calibration switch choosing between "Archive's own form: ρcrit = 0.028·Vflat^0.5" and "Units hybrid: 0.029·Vflat²". The Tools page calls this a Beginner tool, but it felt like a cockpit. The "try the twin pair" suggestion (NGC 2403 vs UGC 128, same speed, different dark matter) is a lovely idea. I wish it were the *first* thing the tool asked me to do.

**What Synchronism Is Not** (step 4): the "In plain words — beginner? You can stop here" box is good and honest ("not a theory of everything", "MOND in disguise", "an interpretation… plus an openly kept record of what failed"). Below it the cards talk about Lorentz invariance, "15 to 27 orders of magnitude", naturalness, CHSH and Hořava–Lifshitz. I skimmed and left.

**Honest Assessment** (step 5): I read only the "If you read nothing else" box, as told ("this page is about 30,000 words", wow). The first bullet is clear: the equation failed its galaxy tests, and where it fits it's MOND in different notation, "As a theory of galaxies: fatal." I liked "'Untested' on this page means nobody has looked yet; 'Failed' means someone looked." That's the clearest sentence on the site. The rest of the box has Cassini, "13–18σ", "galaxy weak-lensing", "bulge-dominated galaxies", "construction check" and TEST-09/10/25. This is the jargon-heaviest thing on the Beginner Path, and it comes at the very end. Then it says "On the Beginner Path? You're done." and, right after, "Your next step is the Glossary →." Am I done or not?

I also opened **Interactive Tools** (it said "Best first tool: Coherence Explorer", so I went there) and the **Glossary** ("New here? Start with these ten", 128 terms total). The glossary's MOND entry, "gravity behaves differently at very low accelerations", is the plainest explanation of MOND I found anywhere on the site. I'd have liked to meet it on the landing page, since every page tells me the framework "turned into MOND".

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked as far as I could see (static view: slider labels, defaults and the Key Values readout were there; I couldn't actually drag). Intuitive-ish. γ defaults to 0.49 ("galaxy fit"), ρcrit to 1.00, with presets γ = ½ "same curve as MOND", γ = 2 "framework's original guess" and γ = 0.046. Key Values: C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785. The fetch helper checked these against the formula and they match. "One thing to try first: drag γ up to 2, then back" is the right kind of instruction for me. The axis is "log₁₀(ρ)" with no units, and the page says plainly the density axis has no units. Fine for a toy. I liked the crowd/marching-band labels on the axis ends.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked as far as a static fetch shows: text, galaxy buttons (DDO 154 default, NGC 2403, UGC 128, NGC 3198, NGC 7331) and numbers render. I couldn't see the drawn curves in a text fetch, so I can't say whether the chart draws. Confusing for a beginner because of the number of lines and switches (see friction). Best part: "the dots are what telescopes measure" plus the "plain verdict" sentence. The twin-pair experiment is a great idea.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation failed and "turned into MOND", what is the site *for* now? The answer seems to be "a public record of how an idea was tested", and I'd believe it if the landing page said so in its first three lines.
2. Why is "it turned into MOND" a *loss*, if the green MOND line follows the dots? (/why-synchronism has a sub-block on this, but I bounced before finding it.)
3. What is "the bigger question behind it" that remains open? Step 1 promises it, and I never found it stated in plain words.
4. Why does a spacecraft (Cassini) kill a formula about galaxies?
5. The landing page gives a Gaia DR4 date of 2 December 2026. What will I see then, and does it matter for *this* idea? (/what-synchronism-is-not says it settles a question about MOND, not this framework.)
6. Who is the human behind the AI notebook, and why use AI agents to do physics at all?

### Verdict
I came for "one equation for everything" and stayed because the site told me the truth in its first paragraph. That's rare, and I'd share it for that reason alone. The early beginner pages (Why Synchronism's three-line summary, First Encounter A–B, the Galaxy Plotter's "three things to look at") are genuinely well pitched to me. But every page soon slides into dated corrections, counting rules and acronyms, and the last stop of the Beginner Path is the hardest to read. I left knowing *that* it failed, roughly *how* (it became an existing theory called MOND), and not quite *why I should care*.

### Journey
I started at the landing page and checked the header first. It has ten nav items with sensible labels, and the hover text is informative ("26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)", "128 terms defined"). The first paragraph expands MOND, MRH and A2ACW on first use, which is good discipline. Below that, the "Where each piece stands" table uses ΔBIC, ΛCDM and SPARC, and the abbreviations block that defines them ("Abbreviations used in the table below:") is placed **after** the table. The lead's "Abbreviations ahead… short key" link helps, but a reader who scrolls normally meets the terms before their key. I saw no badge legend on the landing page, although "Speculative", "Failed" and "Reparametrization" are used in the Key Claims teaser.

**Learning Paths** is the best-organised page I saw. It has three difficulty paths and four topic paths, with step counts and time estimates. The Beginner per-step times (8+10+6+9+4) add up to exactly the "~37 min" headline. The page even reconciles the two groupings ("10 pages no topic track does", "14 the difficulty paths don't"), and those counts hold up. Step titles still carry undefined acronyms ("Relation to MOND", "CDM Discrimination", "A2ACW Protocol", "Superconductivity (η)").

**Interactive Tools** does something I rarely see: it defines its three label axes (Level, Kind, Status) in the intro, *before* the cards. It says "Artifact Lesson is *not* a level — it is an operational state", and that Level uses "one scale and one scale only". That is good. The page does not state a tool count; I counted 7 cards. The Coherence Explorer's status slot reads "none (a shape demonstration, not a failure lesson)", which is an explanation sitting where a value should be.

**Glossary**: A–Z jump bar, a "New here? Start with these ten" list, a "How to read labels on this site" table of eight label systems, and badges grouped into two families (5 MRH-relationship + 4 descriptive). The structure is excellent. Some entries define a term in its own words: MRH is "the framework's word for the context something is currently relevant within", which leans on "relevant" to define "Relevancy Horizon" and never explains "Markov". The "construction check" entry mentions "one of the 5 roots" without saying what a root is.

**Honest Assessment** has a real table of contents (22 jump links), including "Badge definitions (canonical)" and "Status lifecycle verbs". Seeing those two side by side is when the vocabulary problem became clear to me. Its bottom nav says "Step 5 of 5 … Final step", while the body says "Your next step is the Glossary →" (Pass 1 saw the same thing).

**Key Claims** has a compact inline legend whose badge names link to glossary anchors (`/glossary#failed-badge`). Its wording differs from Honest Assessment's "canonical" table, though. Failed is "tested, did not hold" here and "contradicted by data, with the specific error" there. Reparametrization is "known result, new notation" here and "Equivalent to existing physics in different notation" there. That gives two "canonical" homes (glossary anchors and an Honest Assessment anchor) with two wordings.

**Test Roadmap / Tier 1**: the counts reconcile well: 10/4/7/3 = 24, plus 2 = 26, and Tier 1's "13 cards" = 10 + TEST-04a + TEST-25 + TEST-26. The page titles match the nav labels exactly. The IDs are where things go wrong (see the friction log).

**For Researchers** has the H1 "For Researchers: What's Citable", a third description of the same page alongside the nav tooltip and the footer link. **Research Philosophy** has the clearest heading hierarchy on the site, a working Methodology Track prev/next, and a Validation Badge Taxonomy section. In that section the site says of itself, in so many words, "one badge, two definitions, two pages".

### Verdict
As information architecture, this is better than most research sites I've audited. Taxonomy governance is: the project keeps inventing precise status words faster than it consolidates them, so the same item is described in three registers on three pages. One registry, rendered everywhere, would fix most of the friction log above. The site's own Research Philosophy page has already diagnosed the problem.

**/parameter-derivations.** The page opens with "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations", which spares me some work. I checked the pieces anyway:

### Unanswered Questions
1. Which wiring is "the framework": algebraic g_obs = g_bar/C, or the AQUAL action with μ_eff = C + ½xC′? They differ in the interpolating function at γ = ½ and in the a₀ ↔ a₀′ factor (2 vs 4/3). In principle they also differ in the Cassini EFE quadrupole, which is what TEST-25 is about.
2. The a₀(z) row: what exactly is Ciocan's a₁, and how is 9.8σ obtained?
3. If ln γ and ln A are perfectly degenerate in the galaxy sector, is the "free-γ SPARC fit γ = 0.489 ± 0.11" really a constraint on γ, or on the acceleration-keyed form only? (I think the latter, since the acceleration-keyed fit has no A. The page could say this explicitly next to the Fisher result.)
4. Is there *any* sector where γ is measured away from ½ with significance, not as a pin or a refutation? If not, the honest summary is "every sector that fits picks the γ where the function becomes known physics", and that is a finding worth a headline.
5. The density-keyed field equation ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ on /coherence-function: has it been solved for a spherical body to check the Solar System (with C → 1 inside, but ρ → 0 outside the Sun)? ### Verdict
I came in expecting to hunt for overclaims and found that the site had already done the hunting. Every identity I checked holds. Every "derivation" is labelled as an ansatz, a choice or audited-negative. The two big reparametrizations (μ_simple at γ = ½, Λ at γ = ½) are identified correctly and the authors own them. What remains is precision about *which* equation is the framework: the algebraic and Lagrangian wirings give different MOND functions and different a₀ factors, and the galaxy plotter feeds its prediction from a different baryon model than its baseline. Physically, the work is "a family that contains MOND-simple and Λ as special points, and the data pick those points". The site says that, and I'd cite it for exactly that.

**My argument that the lensing condition is narrower than stated.** The site treats "light doesn't follow the boosted potential" as an escape. It isn't one. If photons felt *less* than the dynamical boost, KiDS would infer g_obs closer to g_bar. It infers g_obs ≈ 100× g_bar, so a weaker deflection rule makes the deficit worse. Any deflection rule with lensing boost ≤ dynamical boost ≤ 6.39 fails at the same bins. The only escapes are:
- (a) light is deflected *more* strongly than matter is accelerated (an effective PPN-like γ ≫ 1 at low g, which would need its own constraints);
- (b) the uncounted baryons around isolated lenses are ×10–17 the census.

That turns "conditional on an unwritten light rule" into "conditional on super-dynamical light bending or a large missing-baryon reservoir". Both are concrete and attackable:
- (b) is testable without any lensing assumption, using FRB dispersion-measure baryon censuses of galaxy halos and tSZ/kSZ stacking of the circumgalactic medium. I found neither cited on the pages I read.

**Two light-rule-free tests of the 6.39 cap that I couldn't find run.**

The only escape is non-equilibrium (tides), and isolated Local Group dwarfs (Cetus, Tucana) limit that.
2. **X-ray hydrostatic cluster cores.** From memory, not checked this session: relaxed-cluster gas fractions at r₂₅₀₀ are ≈0.11–0.13 (Allen et al. 2008; Mantz et al. 2014). Adding stars gives f_b ≈ 0.13–0.14, so B ≈ 7–8 at face value. Hydrostatic mass bias pushes B *up*. That is a ~10–25% tension with 6.39, with systematics of the same order. It isn't decisive per cluster but is light-rule-free across an ensemble.

If the dSph number holds, TEST-10's "every candidate cap" badge would become unconditional, and the floor-of-4 claim would stop resting on a lensing caveat. - **What actually produced refutations.** /for-researchers: "about 3,300 AI debate sessions produced zero refutations of the physics on their own". Five of the six came from executing code against external data.

So, is A2ACW sound for generating physics? On the site's own evidence, no, and the site no longer claims it is. What *has* produced every result on the board is the ledger plus code execution against archival data. That is the method worth describing to a researcher, and /a2acw still leads with role definitions and a "coordination health" score. That score can't reach "healthy": with every component at the top of its target range it gives 0.49, "Warning", and its normalisation "is never defined".

### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics, and the site says so first and correctly. The acceleration-keyed sector is MOND's simple μ, the dark-energy sector is Λ/Cardassian, the measurement story has no rate, and no scheduled dataset (DR4, DESI DR3, BIG-SPARC) can select it. What *is* valuable is the negative-results apparatus: the local-density no-go, the Ġ/G bound from LLR, the A2ACW calibration showing specificity 0/6, and an unusually careful wide-binary literature map with a reading key committed before DR4.

The one place I'd push back is the reverse of an overclaim. The framework's only native number, the Ω_m/Ω_b boost ceiling, is presented as conditionally excluded through lensing. I think it is probably excluded more robustly than the site says, by pressure-supported dwarfs and likely cluster cores, and the site hasn't run those tests. Running them would either make the floor-of-4 claim unconditional or turn up the framework's first distinctive prediction. Either outcome would be informative.
