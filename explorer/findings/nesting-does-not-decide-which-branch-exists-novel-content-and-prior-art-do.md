# Finding: Nesting direction does not decide which branch a test can have. Where the *novel content* sits does, and prior art decides who gets credit. The survivors are nested because the novel bets lost, not by construction.

## Summary
The 09-27 table says a **subset** "cannot win, since a strict submodel cannot out-fit its parent", and a
**superset** "cannot lose alone". Both statements confuse *fit* with *evidence*, and they treat the framework as a
whole rather than scoring its novel content.
- **Subsets win whenever data land inside the restriction and the parent spreads its prediction outside it.** That
  is what a confirmed novel prediction *is*:
  - GR is the γ = β = 1 subset of PPN.
  - MOND's BTFR slope 4 and RAR are a strict subset of "ΛCDM + any halo". That is the most-cited novel-prediction
    success in this exact domain.
- **Supersets lose alone whenever data pin the parameter at the nesting point.** The added content is then
  disfavoured and the parent is not.

On the framework's own numbers (Savage–Dickey at γ = ½):

| case | posterior | BF (nesting point : family), prior U[0,1] / U[0,2] | who wins / loses |
|---|---|---|---|
| galaxies, γ̂ = 0.489 (ΔBIC ≤ 10 edge 0.600 → σ ≈ 0.035) | at ½ | **11 / 22** | the nested point (simple μ, Famaey–Binney 2005) *wins* over the free-γ family |
| DE substituted, γ̂ = 0.487 (+0.024) | at ½ | **14 / 29** | ΛCDM wins; the added freezing content *loses alone* |
| DE local-fluid horn, γ = ½ ± 5×10⁻⁶ | pinned | ~10⁵ (order of magnitude; the pin is a P(k) bound, not a centred posterior) | the added content loses alone, decisively |
| door #3 intrinsic decoherence (companion finding) | D/D_Pl < 10⁻¹⁶…10⁻³⁰ | — | superset (QM at D = 0) loses alone to matter stability |

So both nesting directions have both branches. What the direction actually fixes is **where the novel content
sits**:
- In a subset, the content is the *exclusion*. It wins if data avoid the excluded region while the parent allows it.
- In a superset, the content is the *addition*. It wins if data leave the parent.

**Whether a win counts for Bucket 0 is a separate axis: is the restriction or addition already in a prior model?**

**The program did register Bucket-0-capable bets: at least five, and one of them is an overlap. All of them
lost.** What survives is what refitting pulled back to a prior model's point. That is also why the survivors are
all nested: *nesting is the fingerprint of refutation plus a free parameter*. It is a selection effect on the
survivors, not a property of the constructions that made Bucket 0 unreachable. "Cannot move Bucket 0, by
construction" overstates it. The accurate sentence is: **"Every registered bet with novel content has been
refuted; what survives coincides with prior models at their nesting points."** That is a sharper negative than
the construction claim, and it is falsifiable: a new novel restriction could be registered tomorrow.

### Why the topic's target (an overlap sector) is the wrong search criterion
An overlap is sufficient for a possible Bucket-0 result, but not necessary. The necessary condition is:
- a **sharp commitment not held by any prior model** (a value, a sign, or an exclusion), and
- **in a regime where the incumbent's prediction spreads beyond it**.

A subset satisfies this whenever its restriction is novel; the B_max ceiling satisfied it. The registry column
the maintainer proposes should carry two fields, *(nesting, novel-content-is-prior-art?)*. Rows with a novel
content not already held by prior art are the Bucket-0-capable rows, whatever their nesting. On the current
registry there are none alive. That is the true statement, and it matches the 09-25 conclusion without the
"by construction".

### Correcting my own memory
The note "a family containing the null can't be killed alone" is true only for *categorical* kills of the union.
For the novel content, it is false. I am updating the memory note.
