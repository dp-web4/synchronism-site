**Short answer:** I don't know of any published model with this exact form. I answered from memory and ran no searches, so treat the citations below as unchecked. Mark the details as uncertain.

**What I believe is not published**
- I know of no paper with a period-dependent inertial mass m_eff = m[1+κ ln(1+P/τ)]⁻¹. That includes the Hubble-tied timescale τ = 10⁻⁶·2π/H₀ and the log form with κ = 0.31. The 10⁻⁶ factor and the log look like ad hoc fitting choices. I found no motivation for them in the literature.

**Closest published relatives (conceptually, not identical)**
- **Modified inertia (MOND-type):** Milgrom, "Dynamics with a non-standard inertia-acceleration relation," Ann. Phys. 229, 384 (1994). Milgrom, "The modified dynamics as a vacuum effect," Phys. Lett. A 253, 273 (1999). In these, inertia depends on acceleration, with the scale a₀ ~ cH₀. Your construction swaps acceleration for orbital period. For a circular orbit, a ~ v/P·2π, so the two are related but not equivalent. Period-dependence is non-local in time, whereas Milgrom's is local.
- **Quantised inertia:** McCulloch, MNRAS 376, L5 (2007), "Modelling the Galaxy rotation curves with quantised inertia" (uncertain on the exact title and page). It uses Unruh-wave and Hubble-horizon cutoffs to make inertia scale-dependent.
- **Wide-binary tests of such ideas (data side):** Hernandez et al. (2012, 2023, MNRAS), Chae (2023, ApJ 952, 128), Banik et al. (2024, MNRAS 527, 4573), and Pittordis & Sutherland (2018, 2023). Chae and Hernandez report a boost of about 1.2 above Newtonian beyond ~2–3 kAU. Banik et al. and Pittordis & Sutherland find no such boost.

**Physical problems (my own estimates)**
- At fixed gravitational force, v² scales as 1+κ ln(1+P/τ). At P = 10τ this gives a boost of about 1.74 in v². That is much larger than the roughly 1.2 boost Chae and Hernandez report, and it is excluded by the Banik et al. and Pittordis & Sutherland analyses.
- Sedna-like orbits (P ~ 10⁴ yr) and long-period comets would give a few-percent effect, which is probably constrained.
- Making m_eff depend on a trajectory-level quantity (the period) breaks the equivalence principle, locality, and Lorentz invariance. A particle can't know its "period" before it has completed an orbit. Unbound or eccentric orbits and many-body systems are ill-defined.

**Verdict:** I believe it is unpublished as stated. It is best read as a phenomenological variant of modified-inertia MOND, with Milgrom (1994) the closest antecedent. I'd run an extended literature search on "period-dependent inertia" and "timescale-dependent inertia" before claiming novelty.
