## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /beginner-path, /first-encounter, /tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /consciousness, /two-reframes, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A physics site that opens by admitting it has zero confirmed predictions and somehow makes that feel like the interesting part — but the honesty gets very technical very fast.

### Journey

Landed from the Reddit link. The headline — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — is exactly the kind of hook I click on. The marching-band-vs-crowd analogy landed immediately: dense = moves as one thing, sparse = individuals. I got that in under a minute, which is better than most pop-sci sites do.

Then the weird part: right under the big idea, the page says "Prospective predictions confirmed: 0" and "0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit." My first reaction was honestly confusion — is this site *for* the idea or *against* it? It took me a couple of sections to get that the failure-tracking IS the point ("the honest map of what failed — and why — is the actual product"). Once I got it, I liked it. It reads like the opposite of every overhyped "scientists SHOCKED" video thumbnail. But I almost bounced in that confused window.

Clicked the Beginner Path because the landing page promised it was "equation-free." Step 1 immediately showed me the equation with tanh, γ, and ρcrit in it. I don't know what tanh is (the glossary later told me — points for that), so this felt like a small bait-and-switch. The plain-English "dimmer switch" framing underneath rescued it.

"First Encounter" (step 2) was the best page I read — no equations, marching bands and crystal lattices, and the "MRH" idea explained as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," with "Andromeda is outside its MRH" as the example. "Markov Relevancy Horizon" is a terrifying name for a genuinely simple idea.

Tried two tools. The Coherence Explorer sliders made sense: drag γ, curve gets sharper or gentler; drag ρcrit, curve slides sideways. I couldn't tell you what the number "C(ρcrit) = 0.8824" means, but I could *see* the dimmer-switch behavior, which is what I came for. The Galaxy Plotter was heavier: five overlapping curves is a lot, and I had to reread the legend twice. But the story eventually came through — the gray line sags, the dots stay flat, that's the dark matter mystery. And I genuinely respected the dotted-violet line labeled as a "hand-tuned stand-in" that "fit because it was drawn to fit," next to the real equation's curve that "fails to fix it." I've never seen a science site show its own wrong curve on purpose.

Clicked "See What Failed" → the Honest Assessment page. Bounced off it in about three paragraphs. It's a forensic report — 8,000-ish words with things like "ΔBIC=+184" and "dim-4 SME Lorentz-violating coefficient" in section headers. I skimmed the opening verdict and the two "What this really means" plain-language boxes and left. Not mad about it — it's clearly for physicists — but there's no casual-reader off-ramp partway down.

Consciousness page: clicked it expecting the fun speculative stuff (I watch a lot of "is consciousness quantum?" videos). Got the most hedged page on the site — "Speculative — Proposed Reframing — not a finding," can't compute its own number for any real system, never actually tested. Honest, but as a casual reader it's a shelf of disclaimers around symbols like "γ « 0.001" and "dimensional embedding" I can't parse. Slight letdown.

Two Reframes: the first half is great pop-sci. The pendulum-clock-in-a-centrifuge analogy for relativity clicked for me. The CRT analogy mostly worked but — real talk — I'm not sure everyone my age has actually watched a CRT screen operate. It's becoming a retro reference. Second half of the page jumps to Bell's theorem and "Tsirelson bound" and I was gone.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — intuitive — the two sliders map directly to "sharper snap" and "slides sideways"; I understood the dimmer-switch idea from playing with it even though the printed numbers meant nothing to me

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation has zero confirmed predictions and the galaxy curve "fails to fix it," what is it that keeps the researchers going? (The site says wrong theories motivate right questions — but which question, concretely, is this one motivating now?)
2. What is density "of" exactly at the quantum end? I get atoms-per-volume for a gas — what's dense or sparse about one electron?
3. Is anyone outside this project working on this, or is it one group testing itself?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've ever stumbled into from Reddit, and that honesty is genuinely engaging — the marching-band framing and the two explorer tools gave me a real "I get it" moment within ten minutes. But the site's candor is written in physicist, not in English: the deeper I clicked, the faster the plain language ran out, and the consciousness page (the one a casual reader most wants) is the most fenced-off of all. I'd come back if a friend asked "what was that one-equation thing?" — I could now explain the dimmer switch, but not why anyone still believes in it.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /learning-paths, /tools, /glossary, /gamma-calculator, /phase-boundary, /honest-assessment, /key-claims, /for-researchers, /mrh)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structure); partial (content — physics is above my ceiling by design)
- **Would return?**: yes (professionally — this site is an unusually rich IA case study)
- **Overall impression**: Exceptionally disciplined self-labeling undermined by three overlapping label taxonomies, dead breadcrumb segments, and glossary/page definition drift.

### Journey

I audited methodically: top nav first, then the canonical-reference pages (glossary, honest-assessment badge definitions), then two tools, then three content pages to spot-check terminology against the canon.

**Navigation.** The top nav is clean and the footer mirrors it faithfully. Breadcrumbs exist on every page I visited — but the middle segment is broken everywhere. On /tools it renders as "Home / / Interactive Tools" (empty segment). On /gamma-calculator: "Home / / Interactive Tools / / γ Calculator." On /mrh and /key-claims, the category segment ("Core Theory," "Getting Started") is present but is plain text, not a link — there is no /core-theory or /getting-started index page to land on. So the breadcrumb *names* a hierarchy the site doesn't let you navigate. Either make the category segments link to section index pages or drop them; a breadcrumb that can't be clicked is a label, not navigation.

**Label taxonomies.** This is my biggest structural finding. The site runs **three parallel labeling systems**: (a) descriptive validation badges (Untested / Speculative / Reparametrization / Failed), (b) MRH-relationship tags (Active-MRH / Parallel-Paths / Sidelined / Superseded / Audited-Negative), and (c) content groupings ("Model Explainer" on tool cards). To the site's credit, /honest-assessment opens with a "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" section defining (a) and (b), and /tools explicitly disclaims that "Model Explainer" is "a content grouping, not the site's validation badge taxonomy." But a reader meets badges on the landing page and /tools *before* any path routes them through the canonical reference. Worse, badges are being composed into free-form compound strings: /key-claims has "Speculative (parent claim, unfalsifiable as stated) — both threshold values Failed" and /for-researchers has one that renders with no delimiter at all: "Untested — Time-of-Flight: Structurally UnreachableAudited-Negative — Preferred-Frame" (two badges collided into one string — looks like a missing separator in the badge component). When your trust system is the badge, badge rendering bugs are trust bugs.

**Glossary.** Genuinely good: ~80 terms, alphabetical with Greek symbols and badge terms grouped, hover tooltips promised site-wide, "Learn more →" cross-links. Two problems. First, **definition drift**: the glossary defines MRH as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" (spatial), while /mrh's formal definition is "the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system" (dynamical). Those aren't the same claim to a careful reader, and neither page acknowledges the other's phrasing. Second, **coverage gaps**: terms the site leans on — "kill criterion," "closed" (as a test status), ΛCDM, BIC/ΔBIC, SPARC, BTFR, RSD, "unrunnable as stated" — are used on major pages but absent from the glossary. "Kill criterion" is defined only on /research-philosophy yet appears in badges on /for-researchers and the landing ledger.

**Acronym hygiene.** Inconsistent by page. /key-claims does it right: "MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics)," "ΛCDM (Lambda Cold Dark Matter)," "IIT (Integrated Information Theory)" all expanded parenthetically. The landing page uses MOND, ΛCDM, SPARC, BIC undefined. /for-researchers uses RAR, BTFR, SME, GRB, LQG, SUSY, PR-box with zero expansions — defensible for the audience, except the site's own glossary tooltip system could cover this for free if those terms were in the glossary. /key-claims also cites internal session numbers ("#581," "#616") as evidence; an external reader has no way to resolve these — they need links to the research archive or should be footnoted as internal references.

**Learning Paths.** The page shows headers "By Difficulty" and "By Topic," but only the difficulty view renders content — "By Topic" is a header with nothing under it (or the toggle is invisible; either way, as shipped it reads as an unfinished section). Also, the landing page sells the Beginner Path as "~30 min," but /learning-paths itself gives no time estimates for any path. Small thing, but estimates should live with the paths, not only in the ad for them.

**The "phase" vocabulary problem.** The site knows C(ρ) "is a smooth compander … with no critical point" — /phase-boundary says so itself — yet the tool is named "Phase Boundary Visualizer," a related page is titled "Phase Transitions," and the disclaimer is re-issued locally ("'boundary' here means a regime boundary in γ, not a mathematical phase boundary"). This is the classic legacy-name pattern: every page pays a disclaimer tax instead of the name being fixed once. Either rename ("γ Regime Map" would be accurate) or hoist one canonical naming note and link to it, rather than three inline re-explanations that each phrase it slightly differently.

**Honest Assessment as reference doc.** Structurally the best page on the site: badge canon at top, anchor-linked mini-ToC, plain-language boxes ("In plain language — what failed," "What a non-physicist should take away"). But at ~15,000+ words it's doing three jobs at once — badge reference, audit narrative, and results ledger — and the badge canon deserves its own short URL (/badges) so other pages can link a reader to *just* the definitions without dropping them into an 18,000-word forensic report.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** — worked — intuitive controls, unusual documentation posture — the input ("Ncorr," with a clear definition and linear/log scale toggle), presets, and output regime labels are all well-labeled. What's remarkable as a docs artifact: three numbered caveats *precede* the tool, including "Preset Ncorr values are back-fits, not measurements" and a statement that the formula's behavior for highly-correlated systems is "backwards." I've never seen a tool ship with its own falsification notice above the fold. Honest, but the balance is off — the page spends more words undermining the tool than teaching it; a first-time user doesn't know whether they should bother dragging the slider.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — worked — partially confusing — single γ slider with endpoints labeled "Collective" and "Single-particle"; example systems appear along the axis with honest annotations (galaxies shown at both the refuted γ=2 and the free-fit γ≈0.49). Two writing issues: the inversion caveat ("real BCS/BEC transitions are among the sharpest in nature, yet the formula files them at the flat end") is repeated three times on one page with slightly different wording each time — say it once, well, in a box; and the page's own name contradicts its own naming note (see friction log).

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is there a canonical, single-URL badge reference, or is /honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions the permanent home? (Every badge chip should answer this with a link.)
2. Which definition of MRH is authoritative — the glossary's spatial "bubble" or /mrh's dynamical "degrees of freedom" formulation?
3. What happened to the "By Topic" view on /learning-paths — unfinished feature or broken toggle?
4. Are session-number citations (#581, #616) resolvable anywhere public, and if so, why aren't they links?
5. Is "closed" (as in "kill criterion triggered, closed") a badge, a status, or informal prose? It appears in ledgers but belongs to no defined taxonomy.

### Verdict
Structurally, this is one of the most self-aware science sites I've audited: it defines its labels, disclaims its own misnomers, and puts its plain-language summaries where readers can find them. But the trust architecture is fraying at the seams precisely because it's load-bearing — three overlapping badge taxonomies, a badge-rendering bug that fuses two statuses into one string, breadcrumbs that can't be clicked, and a glossary that disagrees with the page it links to. None of these are hard fixes; all of them matter more here than on a normal site, because this site's entire value proposition is that its labels can be trusted.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /tools, /equation-walkthrough, /galaxy-rotation, /measurement-without-observers, /honest-assessment, /core-idea, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /key-claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe — not for the physics, which is honestly self-refuted, but as a reference for what a real self-audit looks like
- **Overall impression**: The badges are honest to the point of self-immolation, and every number I recomputed checked out — but the site still sells its equation's *form* with a "compresses 80 orders of magnitude" story that its own algebra makes vacuous, and it never states the one identity that would settle it.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project, check the physics." My prior on grand unified one-equation frameworks is low, so I went straight for the throat: the landing ledger, then /parameter-derivations.

The landing page defused most of my standard attacks before I could make them. "Prospective predictions confirmed: 0," "0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit," galaxy rotation badged **Failed**, quantum badged **Reparametrization** with the Born rule explicitly filed alongside Zurek/Carroll-Sebens. That's the correct company — those derivations are all criticized for circularity, and the site says so itself on /measurement-without-observers. I've reviewed actual submitted manuscripts with less self-awareness.

/parameter-derivations is the page I expected to catch lying, and it didn't. Verbatim: "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations." The γ = 2/√Ncorr relation is flagged as borrowed CLT scaling with the honest kicker that "invoking CLT for correlated Ncorr is self-contradictory." The A ≈ 0.029 constant carries an Audited-Negative badge documenting that the only computation producing it used ρcrit ∝ V^0.5 while the framework asserts V² — a 600× internal inconsistency, stated on the framework's own site. And the Landau/mean-field framing has been properly retired: a smooth compander with argument ≥ 0 has no critical point, no diverging correlation length, no universality class. The sign inversion note is also correct condensed-matter physics — γ = 2/√Ncorr files BCS and BEC (the *sharpest* transitions we know) at the flattest end of the sigmoid. That's backwards, and the site says it's backwards.

I checked the arithmetic myself rather than trusting the page (this site has visibly had arithmetic incidents — the ledger documents a Freeman's Law error corrected 2026-07-09). All pass: C(ρcrit, γ=2) = tanh(2 ln 2) = 15/17 ≈ 0.8824 exactly as stated (so ρcrit is a near-saturation knee, not a midpoint — disclosed). a₀ = cH₀/2π = 1.082×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² with H₀ = 70 ✓. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 123.3 M☉/pc² ✓, and it's correctly badged: a₀ = cH₀/2π is the well-known MOND numerical coincidence (Milgrom noted the cH₀ scale in 1983), and Σ₀ follows from a₀ by algebra, so calling it an independent Freeman's Law "prediction" would be double-counting. The site calls both Reparametrization. Correct.

But then I found the thing that bothers me. Set x = ρ/ρcrit. Then:

tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1] / [(1+x)^2γ + 1] — **exactly**, from tanh(u) = (e^2u−1)/(e^2u+1).

I verified this numerically across γ ∈ {6×10⁻⁴, 0.49, 2} and x from 10⁻⁶ to 10⁴⁰: machine-precision identical. The celebrated equation is an ordinary rational (Hill-type) saturation function of (1+x)^2γ. There is no transcendental content; the tanh∘ln decomposition is notation. Yet /equation-walkthrough (Step 2) motivates the logarithm with "This compression lets one equation span 80 orders of magnitude of physical density," and /core-idea repeats "The logarithm compresses this range into something the tanh can work with." Given the identity, this motivation is empty — *any* saturating rational function "spans 80 orders of magnitude" for free; the ln does no special work that the exponent 2γ doesn't undo. /core-idea gets tantalizingly close to admitting this — it correctly places C(ρ) "in the family of μ-law encoders, Hill functions, and Naka–Rushton curves" — but never states the identity, so the reader is left thinking Hill functions are merely *similar* alternatives rather than *the same function*. For a site whose whole brand is "we identify our own reparametrizations," not disclosing that the flagship equation is itself a reparametrized Hill function is a conspicuous gap.

/galaxy-rotation is the strongest physics page. It leads with McGaugh's RAR interpolating function and says plainly that Synchronism's only addition is an environmental-scatter ansatz; that "standard MOND plus mass-to-light ratio corrections already explains essentially all of the RAR variance"; that the γ=2 transition shape was killed at ΔBIC = +184 and free γ converges to 0.49 ≈ MOND; and that MOND's own External Field Effect predicts the same environmental dependence, so the ansatz doesn't discriminate. It also states the locality problem crisply — C(ρ) depends on *local* ρ while the RAR is organized by *non-local* g_bar — which is the actual structural reason this class of theory can't reproduce MOND phenomenology, and the same argument correctly redeployed against the Bullet Cluster (lensing peaks track galaxies, not gas — fatal for any local-density-sourced gravity). And /key-claims carries the most quietly devastating admission on the site: mass cancels out of a disk's ρ/ρcrit entirely, so **no galaxy of any mass crosses the coherence knee for any calibration constant**. That's not a bad fit; that's the mechanism not engaging with the phenomenon at all. It's badged Failed. Correctly.

Two things did smell off. First, /measurement-without-observers headlines "MRH crossing *is* collapse — derived from γ" — but /parameter-derivations just told me γ = 2/√Ncorr is "not rigorously derived," so nothing downstream can be "derived from γ." The body of the page is honest ("interpretive overlay on decoherence, not a complete measurement theory"); the headline verb isn't. Second, /key-claims presents "8 Synchronism-based approaches converge on C ≈ 0.50 (range 0.48–0.52)" as convergent evidence for a consciousness threshold. Any function built to output into [0,1] via a sigmoid has 0.5 as its distinguished midpoint *by construction* — eight rescalings of the same output convention agreeing on the midpoint is what you'd expect if the approaches share the C-axis, and evidence of nothing if so. The page does mark the parent claim "unfalsifiable as stated" (and documents that an earlier "0.64 rejected at p<0.0001" claim was removed after an audit found it had no source — points for that), but the "convergence" framing still reads as positive evidence when it may be an artifact of the codomain.

### Tools Tested
- **Equation Anatomy (/equation-walkthrough)** — worked — intuitive but thin — six-step navigation through the equation's parts with correct variable glosses (it even discloses that C(ρcrit) ≈ 0.88 at γ=2, i.e. the "critical" density is nearly saturated — a caveat most sites would bury). But Step 2's logarithm-compression story is the one piece of physics motivation on the page, and it's the piece my algebra check kills. As a teaching tool it explains the notation, not the function.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — worked — well-designed disclosure, unauditable headline — sortable 23-row table with r-values from +0.982 down to −0.434, and the null-model result displayed *on the tool itself*: "a plain 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z … matches these correlations (|Δr| ≤ 0.07) and sometimes beats them," with the correct interpretation that high r evidences density-monotonicity, not C(ρ) physics. That's the right way to ship a dead result. The weakness is the "curated sample from 1,703 phenomena" framing with no definition of the counting unit.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Given the exact Hill identity, is there any claim on the site that is sensitive to the tanh∘ln parametrization specifically — or could every page be rewritten in terms of (1+x)^2γ with no loss? If the latter, the equation's "form" carries no physics at all and the site should say so.
2. What independently measures Ncorr? Every appearance I found back-fits it from a known γ regime; with Ncorr free, γ = 2/√Ncorr is a fit parameter wearing a derivation's clothes.
3. After the mass-cancellation result (no disk crosses the knee for any calibration), what galactic-dynamics content does the framework retain beyond the RAR it inherits verbatim from McGaugh's interpolating function?
4. Do any of the eight consciousness approaches produce the 0.50 threshold in a way that could have failed — i.e., was 0.50 ever at risk?

### Verdict
As physics, this framework is dead and the site knows it: zero derived parameters, the flagship galactic mechanism can't engage its own phenomenon (mass cancels), the transition shape is BIC-refuted, and the quantum sector is an interpretive overlay it honestly files next to Zurek. What's remarkable is that every one of those sentences is quoted or paraphrased *from the site itself* — the badges are honest, the reparametrizations are correctly identified (a₀ = cH₀/2π, Freeman's Law, the chemistry null model shipped on the tool), and every number I recomputed was right. The one substantive dishonesty-by-omission I found is that the site never states its central equation is algebraically an exact Hill function, while still selling the tanh∘ln form with a compression story the identity makes vacuous. ### Journey

I came to answer three questions: what's actually new, does any prediction differ from MOND + ΛCDM, and is the A2ACW methodology sound. The site answers all three itself, mostly correctly, which is not what I expected from an "AI-generated physics framework."

**What's actually new: nothing positive, four negative artifacts — and the site says so.** The landing page states plainly that "no test currently on the books" can deliver a prediction that differs from MOND and ΛCDM and wins. /for-researchers lists four "citable artifacts": a local-density no-go (the ρ_crit ∝ V⁺² vs. required V⁻² sign inversion), the A2ACW program-level null, the DESI growth-suppression negative, and the dim-4 LIV naturalness gap. My referee assessment: the no-go is a clean quantified instance of Milgrom's 1994 non-locality theorem (correctly credited to Milgrom "up top"); the LIV gap is Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia–Vucetich 2004 applied, with the two standard perturbative escapes (Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov 2005 SUSY; Pospelov & Shang 2012) correctly named as unexhibited rather than nonexistent. That's the right epistemic posture — "open custodial-mechanism gap, not a closed refutation" is exactly how I'd have made an author phrase it in review.

**Does anything differ from MOND + ΛCDM: one live branch, and it can only lose or tie.** /tier-1-existing is the most useful page on the site for my question. Its own tally — "0 currently discriminating tests from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" — survives my scrutiny. The single genuine difference is TEST-02: C(ρ) predicts ~0.05–0.4% wide-binary velocity deviation vs. MOND's ~18%, so a Gaia-confirmed Chae-type anomaly refutes the framework outright, while a Banik-type Newtonian result leaves it indistinguishable from ΛCDM. "Self-eliminating-or-tie" is accurate. One quibble: "kill branch adjudicable now" is optimistic — Chae vs. Banik has been unresolved for three years over sample cuts and eccentricity priors, and I see no sign of community convergence; adjudicable-in-principle, not adjudicable-this-quarter. TEST-01/05's environment signal is correctly marked as shared with MOND's External Field Effect, but the page undersells its own case: the EFE isn't just predicted to produce this signature, it has *claimed detections* in SPARC already (Chae et al. 2020, 2021). Citing those would make the non-discrimination verdict airtight instead of theoretical.

**The missed connections are my main finding.** The site's negative results keep landing next to literature it doesn't cite:

1. **Superfluid dark matter.** The framework's core intuition — coherence switches on above a galactic density threshold — exists in the literature as Berezhiani & Khoury (2015): dark matter Bose-condenses in galaxies (a local density criterion!) and the condensate's *phonons* mediate a MOND-like force. That construction is precisely the known escape from the site's local-density no-go: it converts a local criterion into non-local MOND phenomenology via a dynamical medium. A page that has proven "local ρ can't do this" and doesn't mention the theory that solved that exact problem is leaving its best contextualization on the table. Same family: chameleon/symmetron screening — density-dependent transitions are a mature field, and the "wrong variable" (ρ vs. g_bar) discovery is the standard reason screened scalars don't reproduce MOND.
2. **Tsirelson-bound literature.** /two-reframes says a substrate reframe "owes an account of why 2√2 and not 4" and frames this as an open debt. It is — but it's an open debt with a twenty-year research program attached: information causality (Pawłowski et al. 2009), macroscopic locality, the almost-quantum set. Presenting the question without citing these reads, to an expert, like the question was discovered here. It wasn't; what's local to this site is the *executed demonstration* that its own substrate caps at S ≤ 2, which is Bell's theorem doing its job and is honestly labeled as such.
3. **The S₈ tension is dissolving under the retrodiction.** TEST-04a was calibrated to the S₈/growth tension circa 2024. Recent weak-lensing reanalyses (KiDS-Legacy consistency with Planck) have substantially softened the tension the suppression prediction was built to explain. The framework retrodicted a tension the field is walking back — worth one sentence on /for-researchers, since it weakens even the retrodiction's motivation, not just its outcome.
4. **DESI DR2's actual anomaly is absent.** The honest-assessment's "unaddressed" list names H₀, JWST early galaxies, and primordial non-Gaussianity — but not the evolving-dark-energy preference (w₀wₐ at ~3–4σ) that is currently the field's live cosmological anomaly. A coherence-of-the-cosmos framework with nothing to say about it should say that it has nothing to say, in the same list.

**Is A2ACW sound?** As a self-audit: yes, and unusually honest — "0 of 6 audited (0 of 47 pending audit)" is a properly deflated denominator, and the page volunteers that "volume (3,308 sessions) is not evidence; the 0/6 retention rate is." As a generalizable methodology claim: not yet, for three reasons the page only partially owns. First, no control corpus — specificity was measured by feeding the detector six *genuine* discoveries (0/6 passed, i.e., everything gets flagged), but there's no run against a corpus of established published claims to calibrate the false-positive rate, and no planted-novelty run to measure sensitivity prospectively. Second, circularity: the "6/6 sensitivity" is scored against demotion labels produced by the same audit ecosystem being evaluated — the ground truth isn't independent of the detector. Third, n=6 from one corpus and one framework, which the page does state. The exportable nugget is the vocabulary-asymmetry result: 4/4 prior-art rediscoveries caught only after vocabulary translation. That's a genuinely useful, portable finding for the AI-scientist literature — LLM research programs rediscover known physics in idiosyncratic vocabulary, and translate-then-search catches what direct search misses. I'd referee a short methods paper on that specific claim favorably if it replicates on a second corpus.

**Consistency check that failed.** The landing ledger's RAR row reads "γ=2 refuted; ΔBIC ≥ +33," while /galaxy-plotter and /for-researchers cite ΔBIC = +184 for γ=2 (vs. +7 for free γ ≈ 0.49). If +33 is a different comparison (per-galaxy? subsample?) it needs a label; if it's stale, it's the kind of drift this site's whole apparatus exists to prevent. Similarly /cdm-discrimination — which to its credit reversed its own "below CDM" overclaim on 2026-07-04 — asserts 0.086 dex sits "inside that predicted range" while citing *no external CDM benchmark at all*. The relevant literature is live (Desmond 2017 argues ΛCDM semi-analytic scatter exceeds observed; hydro sims like IllustrisTNG land closer to 0.1 dex): the corrected verdict is plausible but currently uncheckable as written. And /honest-assessment says "CPSU 2004" without ever expanding the acronym — /for-researchers has the full Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia & Vucetich citation; the audit page should too.
