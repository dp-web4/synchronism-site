## Key Findings
- `b4-heterogeneous-run-mean-compat-wins-once-the-threshold-artefact-is-removed-and-wins-by-construction.md`
  - Derivation: the update is consensus dynamics, so λ_max has no derivation; the natural prior art is λ₂ (Fiedler).
  - Power: at N = 6, λ_max and 5⟨C⟩ never separate by more than 1.34×, so the topic's ≥ 2× bar is unreachable.
  - Main run: support density plus mean (H-thr, 0 extra parameters) wins at 0.072 dex, against 1/⟨C⟩ at 0.111, affine at
    0.174 and λ₂ at 0.20–0.39. 4/5 predictions held; P2 failed because 1/⟨C⟩ did better than I predicted. Registered
    verdict: **B4 refuted as stated**.
  - Knock-out (2/2 substantive predictions held): with a calibrated edge prior instead of 0.5, the intercept share falls
    0.71 → 0.32 and H-thr's advantage vanishes (0.65 → 0.93). 1/⟨C⟩ fits all 10 structures to 0.062 dex, with a slope of
    −0.92.
  - Why it cannot count: compatibility is defined as a multiplier on the averaging weight, so p·C_ij is the only
    combination at first order. The 1/⟨C⟩ scaling is written into the update rule, which makes it a reparametrization.
    The registry's one winning branch is definitional.
  - Side results: "p_crit" is a crossover (Hill k 0.28–0.48, C(0) = 0.20), not a transition. The ceiling tracks λ₂ (Spearman
    0.96, post hoc). The source's Hill estimator mixes location and ceiling, and it fails under the calibrated prior.
  - Site: `/coupling-experiment` still shows "p_crit ∝ 1/⟨compat⟩ CONFIRMED" and "p_crit ≈ 0.0185/⟨compat⟩" (P1 for the maintainer).
