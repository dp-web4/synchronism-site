## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /glossary, /what-synchronism-is-not)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A surprisingly honest "I tried a big idea and it mostly didn't work" site. It's oddly likeable, but the landing page buries me in test codes and σ-numbers before it tells me why a failed theory is worth my afternoon.

### Journey
I clicked through from a Reddit thread. The headline, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, is exactly the Veritasium-style hook that makes me click. The tagline above it ("An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything") set my expectations straight away. Then the first bold paragraph says *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* My honest reaction was whiplash. I came for a big idea and was told in the first ten seconds that it failed. It's refreshing (I've never seen a theory site do this), but I also thought "…so why am I here?" The answer ("the failure map is the product") does exist, but it sits in an Orientation section well down the page.

I kept scrolling, which was a mistake for someone like me. After a nice marching-band-vs-crowd analogy for "coherence", the page turns into tables: "ΔBIC +184", "TEST-09", "Cassini/SPARC squeeze +17.95σ", "Excluded at its own cap", "Is Λ", "a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π". I understood none of it. A first-time visitor also sees "Revision notes (history)" boxes, which made the page feel like an internal lab notebook rather than something written for me. The landing page also tells me the work was done by AI agents (~3,300 sessions) with one human overseer and no outside physicist review. For a Reddit visitor that is a big deal and changes how I read everything, yet I only found it mid-page.

I took the "No physics background?" route to **Start Here: Why Synchronism?** This was much better. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a sentence I get, and the "What happened, in three lines" box is perfect: here's the idea, it lost on galaxies, the bigger question is still open. The callout saying "you can stop here" and the folded "For physicists" section were respectful of my time. I stopped there as invited.

**First Encounter** (step 2) promised ~10 minutes in 7 short sections, A–G. Section B's "dimmer switch" picture of the S-curve was the clearest idea I picked up all day: a number from 0 (everyone doing their own thing) to 1 (everyone in lockstep), rising with crowding. Section A introduced "presence" and the MRH bubble ("an atom doesn't respond to a distant galaxy"), and I liked that too. Then Section C opens with *"the formula for γ failed"* and talks about a predicted +0.55 that came out −0.55. I'd met γ about thirty seconds earlier, so learning it and immediately un-learning it was disorienting. The section labels "Aρ", "Dγ", "Ea₀" put Greek letters on what should be friendly signposts. Section E says the a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π match is "probably a coincidence". That confused me later, because the landing page's status table lists that same formula as the one thing that "Survives, differs, unresolved".

Next I went to the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**. It opens with "New here? Look at three things on the chart below." It then walks me through blue dots (what telescopes see), a gray line (visible matter only, which sags: "the dark-matter puzzle"), green (MOND, a rival theory that fits), and violet (this framework, which doesn't). This was the single most understandable page on the site. I finally *saw* what "it failed" meant. Below the chart, though, it fills up with dated revision notes and terms like "ρcrit = 64 M☉/pc³", "boost ceiling 3.17×", "quadrature".

The **Coherence Explorer** has a great "One thing to try first" instruction (drag γ up to 2, then down to 0.5, and watch the S flatten). I could only see the page as fetched, so I couldn't actually drag anything (see Tools). The presets are labelled with numbers and jargon ("γ = 0.046, density version fit to real galaxies (SPARC)"), not with the marching band and plaza crowd I'd been promised. I wanted a "marching band" button.

The **γ Calculator** told me in its title that it is "a γ formula kept to show how it fails". As a casual visitor, I don't really want to play with a calculator whose output the page says is wrong. The suggested experiment (click "Ideal gas", then "BCS superconductor", and watch the curve go flat, "which is backwards") would be a neat lesson if I knew superconductors switch on sharply. I didn't, so the punchline missed.

The **Glossary**'s "New here? Start with these ten" list is exactly what I needed. tanh as "an S-shaped curve that rises then levels off" finally decoded the equation for me, and the σ entry ("2σ interesting, 3σ evidence, 5σ discovery") was great. Some entries still lean on other jargon. "Decoherence" is explained as "the MRH crossing", and MRH stands for "Markov Relevancy Horizon" with no word on what "Markov" means.

Last was **What Synchronism Is Not**. Its "In plain words" box (not proven, not journal-checked, not a replacement for current physics, clashes with relativity, not just philosophy) is the best five bullets on the site for someone like me. I'd have liked to read it *first*. The cards below it got heavy fast (one-loop estimates, comagnetometer bounds), but the page warned me they were optional.

### Verdict
The beginner path (Why Synchronism → First Encounter → Galaxy Plotter → What It Is Not) is genuinely good. Its plain-language summary boxes and the galaxy plotter's "three things to look at" are the best science communication on the site, and the honesty is disarming. The landing page undercuts all of this: past the hero, it reads like a dashboard for insiders. A casual visitor who scrolls instead of clicking "Start here" will likely bounce at the refutation table. The site's most compelling story ("watch a big idea get tested in public, and see exactly where it broke") should be the first thing I'm told, not something I piece together.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /interactive-tools, /learning-paths, /research-philosophy, /mrh, /test-catalog, /for-researchers, /glossary, /honest-assessment, /crossover-regime-visualizer, /equation-walkthrough)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial (I understood the site's structure well, but its content only partly)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is far more deliberate than most research sites: path step counts add up, there's a label map, and the glossary count is accurate. But the site has grown about 30 status labels, four places that each partly act as "the legend", and three 100k+ character pages. A reader can no longer hold the vocabulary in their head.

### Journey
I started with the header nav. It has nine items, each with a tooltip ("26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)", "128 terms defined"). The tooltips are a good idea, and the two I could check were accurate. I hand-counted 128 glossary entries, and the Test Roadmap gives exactly that 24 + 2 split. The landing page does something I'd recommend to other projects: an "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC): short key" line *before* the tables that use them. MOND, MRH and A2ACW are expanded inline on first use. That's textbook. The problem is that this discipline lives on the landing page and doesn't travel.

**Learning Paths** is the best-built page I saw. It has seven paths (3 by difficulty, 4 by topic). Every stated step count matches the list (5/9/8/7/6/7/6). The Beginner path's per-step times sum to its stated ~37 min. The intro's claim of "10 difficulty-only / 14 topic-only pages" checks out against the lists. Step pages carry a "Physics Track · Step 2 of 7" bar with prev/next links (I confirmed this on /mrh and /research-philosophy). One conflict: the page calls Honest Assessment "the full 80-minute read" in one place and "about 30,000 words, about 2 hours" in another.

Then I went looking for what the coloured badges mean. That was the longest trail of the session:
- **Research Philosophy** has a "Validation Badge Taxonomy" heading. Every chip there says "see canonical legend" and points to Honest Assessment. It also admits that the wider status vocabulary has grown to "40+ ad-hoc strings".
- **Test Roadmap** has its own "Validation Badge Guide": 9 badges, 7 "operational states, not badges", 11 "lifecycle verbs, not badges", and 3 retired labels. That's 30 labels a reader may meet on test cards.
- **Glossary** has a "How to read labels on this site" table (Label system | Question it answers | Where you see it | Members). Its rule, "If a label is not in the first two rows, it is not a badge", is the clearest single sentence about the label system on the site. **This table should be the legend**, and it is the hardest of the four to find.
- **Honest Assessment** holds the "canonical reference". It sits inside a page the site itself puts at ~30,000 words (190k characters), just after the refutation ledger. It has a table of contents, which helps.

So there is one source of truth, but four entry points of different scope. The one the rest of the site points to is inside the longest page.

The **Interactive Tools** hub gives each card three labelled slots (Kind, Level, Status) plus a "Badge on its page" with an ⓘ mark. The intro says "three separately-labelled slots" and then explains why the fourth isn't really a fourth. Cards marked **Beginner** use γ, C(ρ), ρ_crit, V_flat and SPARC without definition. The Coherence Explorer card shows the "Reparametrization" badge but never says what was reparametrized. Only the Galaxy card glosses its badge.

**Breadcrumbs** exist on every page I checked, but they're inconsistent:
- Section crumbs are links on some pages and plain text on others. "Methodology" links to /research-philosophy on /for-researchers, but on /research-philosophy itself it's unlinked text.
- "Core Theory" (/mrh) and "Predictions & Experiments" (/test-catalog) are never links. I couldn't find a landing page for either section.
- On tool pages, "Interactive Tools" is linked correctly.
- In every fetch, the "/" separators come out as their own numbered list items ("1. Home 2. / 3. Glossary"). If that reflects the real markup, a screen reader announces the slashes as breadcrumb steps.

**MRH** was a good concept page with one strong move: an "Open Gap" section admits that "the 'Markov' label sets an expectation that is not currently met" and floats a rename to "Relevance Horizon". I'd do it. Pass 1 already tripped on "Markov".

The two tools, the **Crossover Regime Visualizer** and **Equation Anatomy**, are covered under Tools. The **Test Roadmap** page and **For Researchers** (112k characters, with a tidy "Acronyms:" paragraph partway down) finished the tour.

2. What is the difference between an "operational state", a "lifecycle verb" and a "descriptor/qualifier" from a reader's point of view? Do I need to know any of them to understand a page, or are they record-keeping?

### Verdict
Structurally, this is one of the more carefully engineered documentation sites I've audited. Its counts reconcile, its learning paths have honest time estimates, its glossary count is true, and the Glossary's label map is genuinely good information design. Its failure mode is accretion, not neglect: each correction added a label, a note or a section, and nothing has been retired from the reader's view. The highest-leverage fix isn't new content. It's consolidation: one short legend page, about 9 labels a reader needs (the rest as prose), and the 190k-character Honest Assessment split along its own table of contents.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /galaxy-rotation, /key-claims, /dark-energy, /gamma-boundary, /tier-1-existing, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment#is-gamma-universal)

My remaining objections are about *which object* each verdict and tool refers to: the density law has at least three live calibrations, and the most-visited tool draws the one the Honest Assessment calls a units hybrid.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link with "interesting AI project", so I expected a grand-unified sigmoid with "Validated" stickers. The landing page took that expectation apart immediately. Its status table lists the density law as **Excluded** (ΔBIC +2,843), the acceleration version as **Is MOND**, cosmology as **Is Λ**, and quantum as **No number to test**. The footer counts 0 confirmed predictions. I did not find a single "Validated", "Supported" or "Established" badge on any page. /parameter-derivations does record that R₀ was "Validated" until 2026-09-06 and has since been downgraded to Reparametrization. That's the right direction.

So I stopped looking for overclaims and started checking arithmetic. On **/parameter-derivations** and **/galaxy-rotation**:
- **The tanh identity.** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]. ✔
- **γ = ½ gives MOND exactly.** At γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), with a₀ = 2a₀′. ✔ It holds at all x, as the page says, not only in the tail.
- **Deep-MOND limit.** At small x, C ≈ γx, so a₀ = a₀′/γ. ✔

**Candidate 1: the bounded boost (TEST-09/10).** A ceiling B_max on g_obs/g_bar is the one structurally non-MOND idea in the galaxy sector. Reading /tier-1-existing:
- TEST-09's own sweep says "TEST-09's kill does not survive its own sweep" (|Δn| = 0.41 fires at 3.175, but 0.297 and 0.26 do not at 5.39 and 6.39).
- TEST-10 has 28/153 discs over the cap at 6.39. Pass 3 found that none clear the trials correction.
- The landing row still says TEST-10 "fails under either floor". Pass 3 is right that the galaxy-scale bookkeeping is off.

From the outside I'd add that the result was predictable: B_max = Ω_m/Ω_b is the statement that no system is more baryon-poor than the cosmic mean. The galaxy baryon census (McGaugh, Schombert, de Blok & Zagursky 2010) already shows detected baryon fractions far below cosmic for everything below clusters. Brouwer+2021 lensing (cited correctly, A&A 650, A113) pushes g_obs/g_bar to ν ~ 10² at g_bar ~ 10⁻¹⁵. So the ceiling family dies on lensing and the baryon census whatever the rotation-curve convention, and only the lensing leg needs a deflection law. The site has the right kill. It leads with the weakest version (a 0.4 change in slope) and never names the prior-art one.

**Candidate 2: EFE = 0.** /tier-1-existing calls this "the framework's only structurally discriminating prediction against MOND". It also says the prediction "has no TEST ID and no live carrier". That sentence is the most important one on the site for my question, and it is buried in a long page. EFE = 0 belongs to the density-keyed branch, which is excluded at ΔBIC +2,843. The acceleration branch, keyed on total g, inherits the EFE. So the one discriminator sits on a dead branch. If anyone wanted to run it anyway, existing data already bear on it: Chae et al. 2020 (ApJ 904, 51) report an EFE signature in SPARC outer curves, and the Crater II / DF2-class dwarfs are textbook EFE probes. /cdm-discrimination proposes a ~1,500-galaxy power analysis instead. That is the right size for an environment *regression*, but the wrong test for EFE.

**Candidate 3: wide binaries.** /wide-binaries is a fair, current review. It covers the Chae vs Banik dispute and 2026 preprints (Chae et al., Saad & Ting, a withdrawn Boufourou that ran on a Newtonian mock, all of which I cannot verify from here). It also includes a self-check of Saad & Ting's public code, flagged as "one AI session's calculation", which is appropriately hedged. The MOND transition radius √(GM/a₀) ≈ 8.6 kAU for 1.5 M☉ matches the "~10⁴ AU" callout. 1. **The density-branch rows.** The page tabulates density-branch "velocity excess over Newton" from +64% to +223%, and +116% at the measured knee. A density-keyed boost does not depend on separation, so the wide-binary statistic should not see it. Masses come from mass–magnitude relations calibrated on dynamical masses of *close* nearby binaries (e.g. Mann et al. 2019) at the same ambient density, which absorbs a uniform G rescaling. /tier-1-existing says exactly this ("identically null, γ_g ≡ 1"). The wide-binaries page presents those percentages as if Gaia could measure them. They are absolute boosts at the Sun, and the observable that sees them is the Oort limit, which the page does use. The two pages need one sentence reconciling them.
2. **The registration.** The DR4 reading key was dated 2026-11-06, and the project lead has "neither adopted nor rejected" it 18 days before release. For a test that cannot raise the count this costs little, but it is the only dated forward test on the site, and the default ("dated before the data, not adopted") is weaker than a timestamped commit hash would be.

**Candidate 5: quantum.** /measurement-without-observers says flatly: "no decoherence rate, no Lindblad form", and the framework "adds no differing prediction either way". The bounds it cites are the right ones (Donadi 2021 underground X-ray; Carlesso 2022 LPF/cantilever; Bassi 2013 RMP; Borexino ¹¹C). **The methodology (A2ACW).** The answer to "is A2ACW sound?" is on the site, and it is *no, as a discovery filter*, which the site also says. The facts:
- 3,308 sessions produced ~47 internal-consistency survivors (1.4%), described as "not a discovery rate".
- In a 2026-05-22 calibration arm, the audit flagged **all six canonical real discoveries** as false (specificity 0/6).
- Youden's J ≈ 0 (range −0.71 to +0.46).
- PPV is 0/9 with a ≤ 28% upper bound.
- The adversaries "share the same training distribution".
- No outside physicist has reviewed anything. The only external feedback is other LLMs (Grok, DeepSeek, GPT).

The consequence the site doesn't draw: **J ≈ 0 means the audit's negative verdicts are as uninformative as its positive ones.** An instrument that rejects 6/6 real discoveries would also give "0 of 9 audited claims surviving" whatever those claims are. The landing scoreboard puts "Audited claims surviving: 0 of 9" next to "Refutations executed: 6", as if they were the same kind of evidence. They are not. The six refutations were executed on data (TEST-09/10, ΔBIC, Cassini) and stand independently of A2ACW. The 0/9 and every "Reparametrization (audit)" verdict rest on an uncalibrated LLM judgment. Some of those verdicts are surely right (the Debye one is algebraically obvious), but the instrument cannot tell you which.

**Questioning the frame.** As physics, this is a closed case, and the site closed it honestly. What it has that the literature doesn't is the **meta-record**:
- a 3,308-session LLM theory program, run in the open;
- its yield and its audit instrument's measured specificity;
- its corrections-of-corrections (e.g. the 07-27 correction replaced on 09-15 because "both were half-true");
- the drift of over-refutation.

For anyone working on AI-for-science evaluation, that is novel data. Right now it is scattered across /research-philosophy, /honest-assessment and revision-note folds. If I were advising, I'd say the site's real contribution is a *case study in machine-generated theory*, and it should be presented as one, with the physics as the worked example. That is a different, arguably more valuable, hero question than "what if one equation…".

### Unanswered Questions
1. Does any *live* branch make a prediction that differs from MOND+EFE or ΛCDM somewhere current or scheduled data reach? From what I read the answer is no. The site says "0 confirmed", but never states this stronger "0 live discriminating" result in one place, and it is the more useful number.
2. Is the 0.028·V^0.5 calibration's density branch excluded on the same grounds (ΔBIC, Oort, LLR 68/74), or only the V² hybrid? Pass 3 asked the plotter version of this.
3. Was the c_s² pin computed with the framework's ρ_DE treated as a barotropic fluid with p = p(ρ_m)? If the "dark-energy fluid" reading (gravity unmodified, Ġ = 0) is the survivor, what fixes its rest-frame c_s²? Under c_s² = 0 the pin disappears and the model is a smooth-DE Cardassian with no content beyond w(z).
4. A2ACW's calibration arm (0/6 specificity): which six discoveries, and what prompt? If the adversaries were told to find reparametrizations, 0/6 measures the instruction, not the instrument. Did any arm try the inverse (seed known reparametrizations and measure sensitivity)?
5. The Saad & Ting code check (separation error ~190% of s vs the stated ~0.1%): has anyone contacted the authors? If it holds, it matters for the field, not just this site. Sitting on it as "a question for the authors" without asking them is the one place where the site's honesty stops short of action.
6. RC100: the measured ratio is 1.11 vs 1.74 for a₀ ∝ H(z) and 1 for constant a₀. With what uncertainty? If the error is ≲ 0.3, the landing row "a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π survives" needs revisiting.

### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics, and the site says so more clearly than most published modified-gravity papers state their limits. Every live sector reduces to simple-μ MOND or ΛCDM at γ = ½. The only structural discriminators (the bounded boost, EFE = 0) belong to branches that are refuted on data or excluded outright. The cosmological sign lock is a class property shared with Λ. My substantive objections are about which evidence is which. The cross-scale γ agreement compares against a value the framework's own P(k) excludes. Data-executed refutations are presented alongside LLM-audit verdicts from an instrument with J ≈ 0. The single "differs from MOND" sentence is buried. What *is* new is the quantified record of an AI theory program auditing itself in public, failures and calibration included. That deserves to be the headline rather than a footnote to the physics.
