### Journey

I clicked through from Reddit expecting either crankery or a TED-talk "one equation explains everything" pitch. The headline is exactly that pitch — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — but the very next line says "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." I've never seen a science site do that. It made me trust it more and also made me wonder why I should keep reading.

Right under that is a scoreboard: 0 predictions confirmed, 6 refutations, 3,308 AI research sessions, 14,610 galaxies, 1,703 chemical phenomena, "0 of 6 validated claims survived audit," and a "1.4% internal-consistency survival rate." I understood maybe half of that. "Refutations executed" sounds like something from a courtroom. "A2ACW research sessions" meant nothing to me until the glossary (AI agents arguing with each other, apparently — that's actually the most interesting thing on the site and it's buried in an acronym).

The landing page has a "No physics background?" link that promises a Beginner Path with "6 steps, ~30 min, no equations required." I clicked it. Step 1 (Start Here: Why Synchronism?) shows the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` about a third of the way down, and talks about "tanh," "hyperbolic tangent," "Hamiltonian," "ansatz," "Abrikosov-Gor'kov." So the "no equations required" promise broke on step 1. The marching-band-vs-crowd analogy was good though — I got that "coherence" means "how much a bunch of things act like one thing." The page also says "zero confirmed predictions" again, which at this point I'd seen three times in two pages.

Step 2, First Encounter, was the best page I saw. Plain words, real analogies (marching band, crystal vs interstellar gas, a single atom "not feeling" Andromeda), "~90 seconds per section," and it said it needs no prerequisites. I only saw section 1 of 7 in what loaded, but I'd have kept going.

Step 3, The Core Idea, brings the equation back but this time explains it: "tanh is just an S-shaped curve — flat, then steep, then flat again," like a dimmer switch. That's the sentence I needed on the landing page. It should have been there. But then it name-drops "Hill coefficient," "Naka–Rushton curves," "Ising model" with no explanation and I started skimming.

Then I went to Tools. Seven tools, each with a tag in parentheses like "(Model Explainer)," "(Reparametrization)," "(Artifact Lesson)," "(Speculative)." I have no idea what "Reparametrization" or "Artifact Lesson" mean as labels. The page recommends Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment for newcomers, so I did that.

Dark Matter Reframed was the page I most wanted, because I actually know what dark matter is from PBS Space Time. It's clear: standard cosmology says 27% of the universe is dark matter; this site tried to explain it away with a gravity "boost" instead; the boost maxes out at about 3× and galaxy edges need up to 14×; verdict "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision." I got the story. I did not get "SPARC," "ALFALFA–SDSS," "BTFR," "ΔBIC=+184," or "3.3σ."

Honest Assessment I bounced off. It's enormous (the fetch estimated ~18,000 words), it has a legend with something like twenty different status badges and lifecycle verbs ("ADJUDICATED," "NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED," "Ceiling Exceeded"), and tables full of "ΔBIC" and "r²" and "fσ₈." I respect that it exists. I could not read it.

### Journey

Colleague said "interesting AI project," so I skipped the beginner path and went straight for the coherence function. First reaction to `C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`: this is a compander, not an order parameter. There's no self-consistency loop, no m on both sides, so calling it "mean-field" would be wrong. Second paragraph of the page says exactly that: "tanh is motivated historically by its appearance [in m = tanh(βJzm)], not derived from it; it is a phenomenological choice from the compander family." Fine. I looked for a /mean-field page anyway; 404. That's honest — there is no mean-field derivation and the site doesn't pretend there is one.

I checked the Hill identity myself: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. Correct, it's just tanh(u) = (e^{2u}−1)/(e^{2u}+1). At γ = ½ it's x/(x+2). Also correct. The Hill index n = 2γ reading is right, and the observation that the SPARC-preferred n = 0.978 ≈ 1 is the non-cooperative limit of a Hill function is the sharpest single sentence on the site: the data set the cooperativity to "no collective transition," which guts the framework's own premise. Somebody on this project understands what a Hill coefficient means.

Then the form-selection table on /coherence-function. Ten sigmoids on 2,807 SPARC RAR points. ΔBIC = +7.1 for free-γ tanh-log vs McGaugh's ν, and I checked: ln(2807) = 7.94, so Δχ² ≈ −0.84. The compander fits fractionally *better* and loses only the parameter penalty. The site says exactly this. Good. γ=2 pinned: ΔBIC = +184. Dead. The site says so on every page.

/parameter-derivations is where I expected the overclaiming, and instead found the site refuting itself parameter by parameter. a₀ = cH₀/(2π): I get 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ at H₀ = 67.4, 13% under Milgrom. Correctly badged "dimensional analysis," correctly attributed to Milgrom 1983 / McCulloch / Verlinde / Smolin. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG): I get 119 M☉/pc². Correctly identified as *not independent* of a₀ ("same gap, one number") — that's the kind of thing a reviewer would have to point out and the site did it first. B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17 → f_DM,max = 0.685; f_DM = 0.927 needs B = 13.7. All checks. γ = 2/√N_corr sign-inverted relative to mean-field (more correlated → sharper transition in reality, flatter here): correctly badged audited-negative.

The BTFR test (TEST-09) is clean: same V_flat estimator applied to observation (3.75 ± 0.10), MOND (3.81 ± 0.04) and Synchronism (3.35 ± 0.07), pre-registered kill at 0.3, fired at 0.41. That's how you run a kill test. The TEST-10 write-up is also unusually careful about convention dependence (1−Ω_m vs Ω_m/Ω_b changes the ceiling from 0.685 to 0.844, and the "69% of SPARC exceeds" headline survives only under one convention). I would have flagged that in review; they flagged it themselves.

So where's the problem? The argument of C.

/coherence-function says "every quantitative galaxy result on this site was computed with the argument gbar, not ρ." /galaxy-rotation says fits are "acceleration-space, keyed on gbar." /for-researchers says "C *is* the interpolating function μ by definition" and C(x) = μ_simple(x/2) "identically." Here's the issue: in MOND, μ takes g_obs/a₀ as argument (g_N = g μ(g/a₀)), and ν takes g_bar/a₀. They are different functions of different variables. If you take the framework's stated law g_obs = g_bar/C and feed C the argument g_bar/a₀', then at γ = ½ you get 1/C = 1 + 2a₀'/g_bar, i.e. **g_obs = g_bar + 2a₀'**. That's a constant additive acceleration, not MOND. I computed it: at g_bar = 10⁻¹² m/s² it gives 2.1×10⁻¹⁰ where MOND-simple gives 1.1×10⁻¹¹ — 20× off, and it would never produce "RMS identical to McGaugh to four digits." So the fit that got 0.1437 dex must have used C as an *implicit* μ(g_obs) (solving g_bar = g_obs C(g_obs/a₀')), in which case it literally *is* MOND simple-μ with a₀ = 2a₀' — or it used C in the ν slot with some other mapping. Either way the "identically μ_simple" claim is true as a function identity and *silent* about which variable was plugged in. The site already admits "three mutually exclusive force laws live on site"; this is a fourth ambiguity, sitting inside the one result presented as clean.

Related: the Galaxy Plotter's "Synchronism — REAL C(ρ), γ=2" curve is computed from `v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C(ρ))²`. That is neither g_obs = g_bar/C nor ∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ. It's a quadrature add-on the page itself calls the "quadrature branch, B ≈ 1." With ρcrit = 0.029 V_flat² the SPARC densities sit at x ~ 7×10⁻⁵ (per the Coherence Explorer), so C ≈ 2x ≈ 10⁻⁴ and the violet line is the gray line to within a pixel. The label "REAL C(ρ)" is true of C and misleading about the force law: the tool is showing that *this particular ad-hoc branch* does nothing, not that the framework's stated law fails. The division branch (B ≈ 1000, also mentioned) fails the other way. Fine — but pick one and label the plotted one as what it is.

Two field equations are cited: /honest-assessment quotes Appendix D as ∇²Φ = 4πGρ/C (source rescaling) and /for-researchers gives ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ (AQUAL-like, with C in the μ slot). These are different PDEs with different solutions for the same ρ. The second is described as "conserves momentum and remains linear in Φ." Linear in Φ, yes. Momentum conservation with C = C(ρ) — C depending on the *source*, not on |∇Φ| — only follows if the matter action is included, and then the matter equation of motion picks up a (dC/dρ)|∇Φ|² term so test particles don't follow −∇Φ. Nobody on the site seems to have written down the matter sector. Low priority given everything is dead anyway, but the sentence overclaims.

One possible over-refutation. /key-claims kills the dark-matter claim with a "mass-cancellation argument": ρcrit ∝ V² makes "mass cancel entirely from ρ/ρcrit — no galaxy crosses the coherence knee for any calibration value." I did the scaling: ρ ~ M/R³, V² ~ GM/R, so ρ/ρcrit ∝ 1/(A G R²). Mass cancels; *radius does not*. Whether a galaxy crosses the knee depends on the length scale hidden in A (which must carry units of 1/(G·length²) for the ratio to be dimensionless — units never stated anywhere). "For any calibration value" is only true if A is pinned by something else. It may well be pinned (the 600× discrepancy between the A ≈ 4.6×10⁻⁵ derivation and the 0.029 used suggests nobody knows), but as written the argument doesn't prove what it says. The site's other refutations are strong enough that it doesn't need a weak one.

Small things: the YBCO Tc failure ("predicts 607 K, actual 93 K, off 6.5×") implies Δ = 607 × 1.76 k_B ≈ 92 meV. YBCO's gap is 20–40 meV depending on doping and probe; with 30 meV the same formula gives ~200 K and "off by 2×." Which Δ was used? The failure is real either way; the multiplier isn't. And the Coherence Explorer defaults to γ = 0.49 with ρcrit normalized to 1, so C(ρcrit) = 0.327 and the C = 0.5 point lands at 2.07 ρcrit — both numbers I reproduced. The tool is numerically honest; the readouts just make it obvious that "crit" is a misnomer, which the glossary already concedes.

On badges: I didn't find a single "Validated" badge left standing. Everything I checked is Failed, Reparametrization, Speculative, or Audited-Negative, and each badge I spot-checked was justified by numbers I could reproduce. The MOND collapse is identified as a reparametrization rather than a success. The chemistry sound-velocity r = 0.982 is correctly labeled null-class after a degree-2 polynomial in Z beat it. The a₀ and Σ₀ "derivations" are correctly labeled dimensional restatements. The honesty is not in question. What's in question is whether there's anything left after the honesty.
