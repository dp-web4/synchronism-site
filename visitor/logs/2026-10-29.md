## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (home, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /what-synchronism-is-not)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A refreshingly honest site about an idea that didn't work, though everything past the first screen of each page is written for someone who already knows physics.

### Journey
I came from a Reddit link. The landing page headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") hooked me, and the second line hooked me more: *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* I've never seen a big-idea physics site open by saying it failed, and that made me trust it more than I expected. The marching band vs. crowd in a plaza analogy for "coherence" landed straight away.

Then the page fills up quickly. Right under the hook come numbers ("0 of 9", "6 refutations", "1.4%") with caveats about counting rules attached, then a long abbreviation list (RAR, BTFR, ΔBIC, N_corr, A2ACW…) and about nine nav sections. I didn't know what the numbers were counts *of*. I clicked "Start with Why Synchronism? (~8 min)".

**/why-synchronism**: The opening is good. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is something I've heard on PBS Space Time, and "one equation for how 'crowding' makes the parts of a system act as one" is a pitch I can repeat to a friend. A few screens in, it turns into YBCO, Abrikosov–Gor'kov, Curie–Weiss, Naka–Rushton, compander, Cassini, Ω_m. "~8 min" is not true for the full page; I skimmed and bailed to the next step. I did pull out the failures list, and the concrete ones are the most memorable thing on the page: a superconductor predicted at 607 K vs. a real 93 K, and melting points off by 53% on average. Wrong numbers I can check are oddly more convincing than right numbers I can't.

**/first-encounter**: This is the best page for me. "tanh" is explained as "an S-shaped curve, like a dimmer switch", which is perfect. **"ln" is never explained**, though. The formula has ρ/ρ_crit inside an ln, and I don't know what a logarithm does to a number, so I couldn't connect the dimmer-switch picture to the formula. The γ section ("how sharply the dial turns") was fine until "the sign came out backwards (predicted correlation +0.55, measured −0.55)". I get that backwards is bad, but I don't know what was being correlated with what.

**/interactive-tools**: A clear index with Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced labels and a "best first tool" pointer. This is good wayfinding. It's a little odd that two of the three "Beginner" tools are labelled as showing a failed formula ("Artifact Lesson").

**/coherence-explorer**: This is the best thing on the site for my level. The "One thing to try first: drag γ from 2 down to 0.5 and watch the S flatten into a gentle ramp" instruction is exactly what a newbie needs. Presets have plain labels. One confusion: ρ_crit is the "critical density", so I assumed the curve would be halfway (0.5) there. The Key Values panel says C(ρ_crit) = 0.33 at the default, and the page explains that ρ_crit is a "saturation knee", not the midpoint, which I had to read twice. Also, the default γ is 0.49, not 2 (the "original guess"), so the "try first" instruction starts from a value that isn't the default.

**/galaxy-plotter**: This is where things actually clicked. "A 'rotation curve' is just orbital speed plotted against distance from the centre", and then you watch the gray line (visible matter only) sag below the blue dots. I finally *saw* the dark matter problem rather than being told about it. Picking NGC 2403 and DDO 154 shows it clearly. The violet line (this framework's equation) is labelled "tested reading, failed": it's a constant 1.78× stretch of the gray line and still sags at the edge. The green MOND line follows the dots. Then the page says *"The framework's surviving branch is not a separate line… the live branch is MOND's."* After reading that sentence three times, I think the part of Synchronism that still works on galaxies *is* MOND, written differently. That's honest, but "surviving branch" and "live branch" are insider phrases. A plain "the only version that fits is just MOND renamed" would hit harder.

**/gamma-calculator**: The intro "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails" made me laugh, in a good way. Presets run from ideal gas to superconductor, and the numbers update. The idea that "more particles moving together should make the switch sharper, but this formula makes it flatter" is graspable. But "N_corr = count of particles that move as a correlated unit" is fuzzy, and I couldn't tell how anyone would *count* that for a galaxy. The page itself admits there's no way to count it, so the preset values were back-fitted. I was also confused that the "galaxy" preset gives γ ≈ 0.49 while elsewhere the framework's galaxy guess was γ = 2. Two different γs for galaxies, and only one is labelled.

**/honest-assessment**: The "If you read nothing else" box is great: *"The one equation failed its galaxy tests. Where it fits, it is MOND in different notation. As a theory of galaxies: fatal."* That's the clearest sentence on the site, and I'd put it on the landing page. The scoreboard ("0 confirmed, 6 refutations, 3–8 depending on counting rules not yet chosen") is honest but confusing. Why would the *count* of failures depend on a rule someone hasn't picked yet? As a casual reader, that sounds like hedging even though I suspect it isn't. The page is ~18,000 words; I read the box and the first section only. "What survives is the record, not the physics" stuck with me.

**/glossary**: It has 120 terms, an A–Z index, and a "New here? Start with these ten" list, which is nice. The tanh entry is good and ΔBIC is explained in plain words ("rewards fitting… penalizes extra adjustable knobs"). It has no search box, though, and at 120 terms on one page that's a lot of scrolling.

**/what-synchronism-is-not**: I went here to answer "is this a crank thing?" It mostly reassures: not a theory of everything, not peer-reviewed ("AI models reviewed each other's work, with a human overseeing"), not proven. But two headings tripped me: **"Not: Just notation"** and **"Not: Just philosophy."** Every other heading is something it *isn't*. These two read like the opposite, things it claims to be *more than*. And "Not: Just notation" seems to contradict the honest-assessment box I'd just read ("it is MOND in different notation"). I think the intended meaning is "the big claim is more than notation, and it could be wrong", but in a list of disclaimers it reads as a defence.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked, intuitive. The best beginner tool. The "try first" instruction is great. The Key Values check out (C at the knee 0.33, at 10× 0.83, at 100× 0.98 for γ = 0.49). The only snag is that the knee isn't the midpoint.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the only version that fits galaxies is MOND renamed, what is Synchronism's idea *for* now? Is anything still being tested that MOND doesn't already say?
2. What exactly does ln do in the equation, and why is it needed?
3. Why does the number of refutations depend on a rule nobody has chosen? Who chooses, and when?
4. How would anyone count "particles moving together" (N_corr) for a galaxy, or for water?
5. The homepage says AI agents did most of the research. How much of what I read was written by a human?

### Verdict
This is the most honest "one equation explains everything" site I've seen. It tells you up front that it failed, and the Galaxy Plotter taught me more about dark matter in two minutes than several YouTube videos have. But the beginner path doesn't stay beginner for long: each page starts in plain English and slides into expert vocabulary within a few screens. The clearest verdict sentence ("failed its galaxy tests; where it fits, it is MOND in different notation") is buried inside an 18,000-word page instead of greeting me on the landing page.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (/, /parameter-derivations, /galaxy-rotation, /honest-assessment, /dark-energy, /cdm-discrimination, /key-claims, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The algebra is right and the site is unusually candid that the framework reduces to MOND plus Λ. The remaining rigour problems are in the bookkeeping: what counts as a refutation, and leftover mechanism-language on pages whose badges have already moved on.

### Journey
I came in skeptical ("interesting AI project" usually means a grand unified framework) and went straight to /parameter-derivations. I didn't expect a page whose bottom line is "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." I checked the algebra by hand:

- **Closed form.** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ}−1]/[(1+x)^{2γ}+1] is correct. Small-x C ≈ γx is correct. Large-x 1−C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ} is correct. At γ=½, C = x/(x+2) = (x/2)/(1+x/2), which is the simple μ with a rescaled argument. **Correct.**
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π.** At H₀=67.4, cH₀/2π = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², 13% below 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. **Correct.** Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc². **Correct.**
- **The ρ_crit normalisation.** A = 4π/(β_J G R₀²) with G = 4.30×10⁻³ pc (km/s)²/M☉ and R₀ = 8 kpc gives 4.57×10⁻⁵. Against the calibrated 0.029 that is about 635×. **The page's "~600× off" is correct**, and so is its point that the "644× bridge" is not a unit conversion.
- **Mean-field comparison.** The page says the Ising m = tanh(βJzm) gets its tanh from a self-consistency loop, while C(ρ) is evaluated open-loop. That is the right distinction, and it is the first thing I would have objected to. The γ = 2/√N_corr sign-inversion critique is also right: finite-size rounding narrows with N, so sharpness grows, not falls.

/dark-energy and /galaxy-rotation hold the best observation on the site. At γ=½, (1−C)/C = 2/x exactly. Keyed on g_obs that gives MOND's simple μ; keyed on mean ρ_m it gives ρ_m/C = ρ_m + 2ρ_crit, which is exactly Λ. So "γ≈0.49 from SPARC and γ≈0.487 from DESI" is not cross-sector agreement. Both fits land on the one point where the function is two known standard models. The page says this has "no power to fail," which is the correct conclusion.

I checked the follow-ons:
- Calibrating C₀ = Ω_m gives 2ρ_crit = ρ_Λ for a flat universe. Correct.
- (k/aH)² at k = 0.1 h/Mpc today is (0.1·2998)² ≈ 9×10⁴. Correct.
- The fluid-Cardassian P(k) instability argument (Sandvik+2004, Amarzguioui+2005) is the standard result for this class. It is fairly cited.

/honest-assessment is where the friction starts. The footer reads "0 confirmed predictions; 6 refutations executed under the current counting rule (3–8 under rules on file…)". Two of the six don't hold up as decisive:
- **The RAR γ=2 row** is badged Failed with "Kill Criterion Triggered". The same page says the galaxy-level result is "ΔBIC ≈ +11, 1.6σ … disfavoured at the threshold, not decisive."
- **TEST-09 (BTFR slope)**: in its own words, "does not fire at the two baryon-budget ceilings, so it does not survive its own pre-fixed rule … whether it stays in the count of 6 is pending the project lead."

### Tools Tested
WebFetch can't drive the sliders. For each tool I read the page and then pulled the live JS bundle to check what it actually computes.
- **Coherence Explorer**: **worked**, and the physics is correctly represented. It computes `Math.tanh(γ*Math.log(ρ/ρc+1))`, defaults to γ=0.49, and works out the midpoint live as `exp(atanh(0.5)/γ)−1`. I checked the stated C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = tanh(2 ln 2) = 0.882 and the midpoint 0.316·ρ_crit by hand; both are right. The orthogonality note on "coherence" versus ODLRO is correct, and needed, for a condensed-matter reader. The only gap is the undefined "knee".
- **γ Calculator**: **worked**, and intuitive in that it is framed as an exhibit of a failed formula. γ = 2/√N_corr is computed correctly: BCS at N_corr = 10⁷ gives γ = 6.32×10⁻⁴, and γ = 2 maps to N_corr = 1. The caveat that preset N_corr values are back-fits is the right one. Keeping a known-wrong formula interactive works well as teaching.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: **worked**, with one mass-model flaw. Green MOND is the real simple ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y). The BTFR normalisation bookkeeping checks out: 1/(G a₀) = 62.8 M☉ (km/s)⁻⁴ against the coefficient 47 puts the flat level at (47/62.8)^¼ = 0.93 V_flat, as stated. The violet floored-division line at √(1/Ω_m) = 1.78 is correct. The flaw is the spherical-approximation baryon curve described in the friction log.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is there a field equation or action for C at all? /dark-energy says covariant completions fail the fit. If so, the cosmology is a background-level Cardassian ansatz with no perturbation theory of its own, and the P(k) instability is about a fluid reading the framework doesn't commit to.
2. Does TEST-10 survive at the Ω_m/Ω_b ceiling? (See the friction log.)
3. With tanh chosen, γ=½ forced, and a₀ = Milgrom's coincidence, is the only discriminating content left the a₀(z) slope? If so, why isn't that the headline open question instead of the refutation count?
4. Why does the footer lead with 6 when the page's own rules give 3–8, and the decisive subset looks like 4?
5. What would "C" mean operationally for a lab system (a superconductor, say) independently of fitting γ? Without that, the condensed-matter analogies on the tool pages are decoration.

### Verdict
This is the most self-critical framework site I have read. Every derivation I checked by hand is right, and the reparametrizations are found and labelled by the site itself, including the MOND-at-γ=½ / Λ-at-γ=½ double identity I would have led with as the killer objection. My remaining objections are bookkeeping, not physics. The refutation headline counts two rows the site's own text calls marginal or convention-dependent. One Failed card still speaks in mechanism language. The plotter's baryon curve uses a spherical approximation that is wrong by about 13% at the peak. I'd recommend it to a colleague as a case study in honest negative results, not as physics to build on, and I think the site would agree with that.

### Journey
I came to answer one question: does any prediction here differ from MOND + ΛCDM and survive? I skipped the beginner path and went from the landing page to /for-researchers. The answer is on the page, twice: "No, within the registered tests and the readings run so far," and "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters." I then spent the session checking whether that "no" is right, or whether the site is underselling or overselling something.

**What the framework reduces to.** Passes 1–3 found this already; I confirm it. At γ = ½, tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = x/(x+2). Keyed on g_obs it is the simple μ; keyed on mean ρ_m it is ρ_m + 2ρ_crit = Λ. SPARC gives γ = 0.489 and DESI DR2+CMB+SN gives γ = 0.487 (+0.024/−0.021). Both sectors therefore land on the one point where the function is two standard models. Most theories reach that point by tuning, and the site is correct to call it "no power to fail." I did notice one missed question. The two sectors use different keyings (acceleration vs density), so the two knees are independent parameters. The site therefore can't turn the double identity into an a₀–Λ relation, the a₀ ≈ c√Λ coincidence that would be the one structurally interesting output. I found no page that asks whether a single knee could set both, or says that it can't.

**Hunting for a win branch.** I went through every test card on /tier-1-existing and /decisive-tests:
- **TEST-02 (Gaia DR4 wide binaries):** "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie," and correctly so. A Newtonian null kills the acceleration-keyed compander, but it kills MOND too, so it's a squeeze, not a split. The site cites Chae 2020 and Paranjape & Sheth 2022 on the EFE. I didn't see Banik+2024 (MOND excluded at ~16σ in wide binaries) or Chae 2024, and those are the live debate.
- **TEST-25 (Cassini quadrupole):** this is the Hees+2014/2016 EFE-quadrupole constraint on the simple ν, inherited from MOND. "Not a Synchronism-specific refutation" is the right label. The card gives two significances, 8.7σ published and +17.95σ in-house, without explaining why the in-house value is 2× larger.
- **TEST-26 (DESI DR3):** the best card on the site. DR2 prefers w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0. The page states that no completion of C produces the interior maximum in ρ_DE(x) needed to reach that quadrant, and that the only non-Λ outcome is Cardassian prior art (Freese & Lewis 2002). Its kill also refutes ΛCDM, so it can't single out the framework. That is a correct class-level no-go. The card doesn't print DR2's numerical (w₀, wₐ) or its significance (2.8–4.2σ depending on SN sample), so a reader can't see how close the kill already is.
- **TEST-09/10 (boost ceilings):** these are the only rows that discriminate against MOND (MOND's 3.81 ± 0.04 BTFR slope passes, the framework's 3.35 fails). This also answers Pass 3's open question: TEST-10 has a convention-free exclusion, B_max ≲ 14 from NGC 2915 (f_DM = 0.927 → B = 13.7). That is above the Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.3 ceiling, so **TEST-10 is the robust member of the pair.** But the ledger row on /honest-assessment calls TEST-10 "convention-dependent" and leads with the 69%-over-68.5% headline, which is the convention-dependent half. The robust result also rests on one galaxy's distance and inclination. I'd want the number of discs with B > 6.3, not only the maximum.

**a₀(z).** The site's two descriptions of this result don't match:
- /for-researchers calls a₀ ∝ H(z) "prior art … not a Synchronism-specific prediction" (Genzel+2017, Milgrom 2017). RC100 gives a ratio of 1.11 (constant MOND = 1, a₀ ∝ H = 1.74) and post-hoc n = 0.0 (95% ±1.0).
- /honest-assessment calls n = 1 "this prediction." It also says "Constant a₀ (n = 0) is excluded by the bins at any level (5σ)," from Ciocan's n = 0.71 ± 0.13.

A 5σ exclusion of constant a₀ from high-z rotation curves would be a MOND-killing result. A referee would ask immediately whether ±0.13 is 1σ or derived from 95% CIs, and how pressure support and beam smearing are handled. The site needs one statement of this result with the CI level printed, and one answer to whether n = 1 is the framework's prediction or prior art. As written, it is both.

**A2ACW.** /research-philosophy and /a2acw are candid: "a self-consistency filter — not a discovery method"; "not a generator of physics." The calibration statistics are correct. Literal rule: sensitivity 3/3, specificity 0/6, J = 0. The Clopper–Pearson bounds 0.292 and 0.459 give J ∈ [−0.71, +0.46], and 0/9 has a two-sided 95% upper bound of 0.34. Three points on the methodology:
1. The benchmark is 9 textbook items, written and scored by one model that "knew every answer." It measures recall of the canon, not novelty detection. The site says so, but still prints a J interval as if it were a measurement.
2. The two-rater agreement figures (run-to-run 0.86, cross-vendor 0.41–0.52, "no κ") are the most interesting numbers on the methodology pages. Cross-vendor agreement at about half of within-vendor suggests the "verdict" depends on the auditing model. The site states this result but never draws that conclusion.
3. Labelling "0 of 9 audited claims survived" a "positive predictive value" is a slip. Survival under an uncalibrated audit isn't PPV against ground truth.

The one experiment that would make A2ACW a contribution has not been run: the post-cutoff, citation-stripped discrimination arm. Without it the 0-of-9 audit carries no information, and the site agrees.

**Quantum.** /measurement-without-observers is honest: "an interpretive overlay on decoherence," Speculative badge, no rate parameter. The one quantitative item is the D/D_Pl < 4×10⁻³⁹ bound from Borexino ¹¹C, using bound-state heating rather than interferometry. That is the right instrument choice for random-displacement noise. But the mechanism paragraph still runs through γ = 2/√N_corr ("N_corr rises … γ falls, MRH contracts"), and the site marks that relation audited-negative. The page builds its story on a relation its own audit retired.
