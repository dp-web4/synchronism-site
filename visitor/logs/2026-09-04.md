## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The big idea is genuinely exciting and the honesty is refreshing, but almost every page told me the thing I'd just been shown is broken, and I left unable to tell what — if anything — is still standing.

### Journey

I clicked this from a Reddit comment. The headline is a great hook: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* That's exactly the kind of question a PBS Space Time episode opens with, and I was in.

Then immediately, right under the headline, there's the equation:

**C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**

Four symbols I can't read. I know what ln is vaguely (a log button on a calculator?), and "tanh" is a total blank. To the site's credit there's a little legend right below it defining each piece, and "S-shaped saturation function" for tanh is honest and short. I didn't bounce. But my first emotion was "oh, this isn't for me," and I only kept going because the *next* thing on the page was so unusual.

That next thing: **"0 confirmed predictions; 6 refutations executed"** and something about a **"1.4% internal-consistency survival rate"** across ~3,300 research sessions. I have literally never seen a science website lead with its own scorecard of failures. My honest reaction was split — half "wow, respect," half "wait, so is this whole thing wrong? why am I here?" There's a button labeled **"See What Failed"** sitting right next to **"Start Exploring."** I found that genuinely disorienting on a first visit. Nothing on the landing page told me *why this is still worth 30 minutes of my time* if the answer to the headline question is apparently "no."

I went to **Start Here → Why Synchronism?** This was better. The crowd-vs-marching-band metaphor is great — *"a crowd milling around a plaza behaves like independent individuals; a marching band behaves like one organism"* — that clicked instantly, and I could suddenly see what "coherence" is supposed to mean. But three paragraphs later I'm reading about melting-point failures and superconductivity failures again. The beginner page spends a big chunk of itself on things that didn't work, in language I can't evaluate.

**The Core Idea** gave me the dimmer switch: *"Think of it as a dimmer switch from sparse/independent to dense/collective."* Between the marching band and the dimmer switch I think I actually get the concept now. Roughly: things that are spread out act independently, things that are packed in act as one unit, and this equation is a dial that describes the switchover. If that's right, I got there — but I got there from two metaphors, not from the equation, and I'm not confident I'm right.

Then the page hit me with *"6 of 6 badges previously labeled 'Validated' have been demoted to Reparametrization"* and a bunch of ⚠ symbols with 2026 dates. I don't know what "reparametrization" means. I don't know what a badge is at this point — nobody has told me. I skimmed past all of it.

**Dark Matter Reframed** is the page I actually came for. Dark matter is the thing I've watched videos about. This page lost me completely. Within a screen or two: BAO modulation, CFD viscosity, SPARC, ALFALFA-SDSS, Tully-Fisher, ΔBIC=+184, 3.3σ. None of those are explained where they appear. The one sentence I understood was the setup — dark matter as *"matter in a different coherence regime"* rather than missing stuff — which is a genuinely cool idea I'd have loved two more paragraphs on. Instead the page pivots into a wall of acronyms and a "viscosity sign error." I bailed.

**Honest Assessment** — I clicked it because the site keeps pointing at it. It's clearly the heart of the project and I could feel that, but it's the most technical page on the whole site. Six refutation tables, sigma values, a comparison against theories I've never heard of (TeVeS? AeST? MOG?). One sentence did land, and it landed hard: *"the methodology that produced this audit is the contribution; the physics just provided the test case."* That reframed the whole site for me — oh, the *point* isn't the equation, it's the way they checked themselves. **That sentence should be on the landing page.** It's the answer to "why should I still be reading this," and it's buried nine pages deep.

**What Synchronism Is Not** is where I found the thing that surprised me most: the research is *"3,308 AI-to-AI sessions"* — AIs reviewing each other's derivations. That is a genuinely striking fact and it's item 3 of 6 on a page most casual readers will never open. I'd been reading the whole site assuming humans did this work. Finding out otherwise, four pages in, made me re-read everything I'd already seen with different eyes. Not necessarily worse eyes! But I felt like I should have been told earlier.

**Glossary** — 72 terms, alphabetical, no search box. When I wanted to look up "reparametrization" mid-read I had to scroll. The definitions themselves are the best writing on the site: *"MRH: An 'event horizon for influence' — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter."* That's a beautiful one-liner. More of the site should sound like the glossary.

### Tools Tested

- **Equation Anatomy (/equation-walkthrough)** — worked — **intuitive, best thing on the site for me**. Six steps, one piece of the equation each, with a Previous/Next. *"ln grows slowly. Doubling x doesn't double ln(x)... A number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger"* and *"tanh is like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1"* — those two sentences taught me more than every other page combined. **This should be linked directly from the landing-page equation.** The one thing that deflated me: partway through, a red box says γ is "known-broken" with an inverted sign. So I just learned the equation and was told a piece of it is wrong. I don't know what to do with that.
- **Coherence Explorer (/coherence-explorer)** — worked — **mostly intuitive**. Two sliders (γ = 0.49, ρcrit = 1.00) and a curve that updates. I dragged γ and watched the S get steeper — that's a nice, immediate "oh, *that's* what sharpness means" moment, and the crowd/marching-band framing is repeated here which helped. The x-axis says "log₁₀(ρ)" which meant nothing to me, and the "key values" table (C at ρcrit, 10ρcrit, 100ρcrit) I ignored entirely. Also several caveats about what "the formula gets wrong."
- **γ Calculator (/gamma-calculator)** — worked — **confusing**. You type in N_corr and it gives you γ. But I don't know what N_corr is in real terms, and the page itself says the tool is *"not a physical estimate, but a live demonstration of the formula's central defect"* with four numbered problems including one where "more correlation → smaller γ" gives the opposite of real physics. So: a calculator whose stated purpose is to show you it doesn't work. As a casual visitor I genuinely didn't know whether I was allowed to be interested in the number it gave me.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If there are zero confirmed predictions and six refutations, is there anything here that's still thought to be *true*? I never found a clear "here's what survived" list.
2. What is a "reparametrization" and why is it apparently bad news? Six things got demoted to it and nobody told me what it means.
3. Who did this research? I only found out it was AI-to-AI sessions by accident, four pages in.
4. Is the marching-band/dimmer-switch idea itself in trouble, or just the specific equation used to describe it? Those feel like very different situations and the site doesn't separate them for me.
5. What actually *is* dark matter under this framework, in words? I read the page and still can't say it back.
6. What are all the colored badges and who decides them?

### Verdict

The honesty is the most memorable thing about this site and I mean that as a compliment — I've never seen anything like the landing-page failure scorecard, and the "the methodology is the contribution" line genuinely reframed the whole project for me. But that reframe arrives nine pages in, and until it does I'm reading a site that keeps handing me an idea and then taking it away, in vocabulary I can't check. The metaphors (marching band, dimmer switch, MRH as an event horizon for influence) are excellent and there aren't enough of them; the equation walkthrough is the best on-ramp and it's not where a new visitor lands. I'd come back if someone linked me a specific page, but I wouldn't explore on my own — I don't have the tools to tell what's still standing, and the site never told me which parts I'm allowed to believe.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The writing is unusually precise and the glossary is excellent, but the site has built a 25-label controlled vocabulary and then documented it in one anchor inside its longest page — the taxonomy is doing enormous work that the information architecture doesn't support.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set I'd been handed cold: walk the nav exhaustively, check every label against its destination, check every acronym against its first use, and see whether the terminology holds still across pages.

**Structure first.** The global nav is flat — eight items, no dropdowns: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. Every page I opened had breadcrumbs, which is more than most technical sites manage, and the learning-path pages carry a step indicator ("Beginner Path — Step 1 of 6"). `/honest-assessment` even shows *dual* path membership — "Step 5 of 6 (Beginner Path); Step 6 of 6 (Methodology Track)." That's a genuinely thoughtful touch; most sites pick one parent and lie about the rest.

But the breadcrumbs describe a site that the nav doesn't. Collecting the middle segments as I browsed: *Getting Started* (`/why-synchronism`, `/learning-paths`, `/honest-assessment`, `/key-claims`), *Cosmology* (`/dark-matter`), *Predictions & Experiments* (`/tier-1-existing`, `/test-catalog`), *Methodology* (`/research-philosophy`), *Interactive Tools* (all seven tools). That's a five-category IA — and none of those five categories exists as a nav item. The nav is flat and the breadcrumbs are hierarchical, and they disagree about what the site's top level is. "Tier 1: Existing Data" and "Test Roadmap" sit as top-level siblings in the nav while their breadcrumbs both say they live under "Predictions & Experiments," a section a reader can never navigate to.

The same drift shows in the labels themselves. The nav says **Start Here**; the breadcrumb for that exact page says **Getting Started**. The nav says **Tools**; the breadcrumb says **Interactive Tools**; the URL is `/interactive-tools`; the page heading is "Interactive Tools." Three names for one section. Neither of these is confusing enough to lose a reader, but both are the kind of thing that compounds — a reader building a mental model of the site has to hold two labels for every one place.

**I chased what looked like broken links and they weren't.** `/tier-1-existing` says the full Tier 1–4 taxonomy lives on `/test-roadmap`, while the nav's "Test Roadmap" points at `/test-catalog`. Both URLs render the same page, so I checked the HTTP layer rather than guessing: `/test-roadmap` returns **307 → /test-catalog**, and `/start-here` returns **307 → /why-synchronism**. These are deliberate aliases, not duplicate content, and a made-up URL correctly 404s. Good — the routing is sound. The only nit left is that in-page prose links to the alias rather than the canonical, so readers take a redirect hop, and 307 (temporary) is a slightly odd choice for what look like permanent aliases. Low stakes.

**Terminology is where the real problems are.**

The site runs a controlled vocabulary — validation badges — and it is *everywhere*. Landing page, learning paths, tool cards, claim headers, page headers. `/dark-matter` is headed "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision." `/galaxy-plotter` is tagged "Reparametrization." The badges are load-bearing: they are how the reader is supposed to tell a live claim from a dead one.

The canonical definitions exist, and they're good. They are a section titled "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" inside `/honest-assessment` — the longest, most technical page on the site. Two pages link to it (`/key-claims` and `/research-philosophy`, both correctly deep-linking `#validation-badge-definitions`). Every *other* page that displays a badge — the landing page, `/learning-paths`, `/interactive-tools`, `/dark-matter`, every tool page — shows the badge with no path to its definition. `/learning-paths` displays badge language and has no explanation anywhere on it. A reader meets the vocabulary five pages before they meet the dictionary.

And the vocabulary is *large*. Counting from the canonical section: 5 MRH-relationship tags, 4 descriptive tags, 2 deprecated tags, ~9 operational-state labels (Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat), and ~9 lifecycle verbs (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, Ceiling Exceeded). That's roughly **29 distinct labels in one badge system**, spanning five groupings that are themselves not clearly distinguished for the reader. The page is admirably self-aware about this — it notes the vocabulary "was used with precision across test cards but documented nowhere" (added 2026-07-23) — but documenting it in one place is only half the fix. Nobody can hold 29 labels, and the reader has no way to know that "Withdrawn" (operational state) and "WITHDRAWN" (lifecycle verb) are two different things, or aren't.

Then `/interactive-tools` adds a *sixth* family. Tools are tagged "Model Explainer," "Reparametrization," "Speculative," "Artifact Lesson." The page is careful — it says these indicate content category, "not validation verdicts." Good disclaimer. But "Reparametrization" and "Speculative" *are* validation badges from the canonical taxonomy, "Artifact Lesson" is an operational state from it, and "Model Explainer" appears nowhere in it. Four labels drawn from three different systems, rendered in the same visual chrome, with the distinction carried entirely by one sentence of prose. A reader who skims will merge them.

**Symbol overloading.** `C` means the coherence function C(ρ) across the physics pages, and on `/key-claims` it also means the consciousness quantity in `C = f(γ, D, S) ≥ 0.50`. To the site's real credit, `/key-claims` *explicitly flags* that this C "differs functionally from the physics construct C(ρ) despite identical notation" — that is exactly the right thing to do and I want it noted as a win. But the same page then glosses **γ as "coherence parameter,"** where every other page on the site calls γ the *sharpness* dial ("sharpness dial," "transition sharpness," γ = 2/√N_corr). That one is not flagged, and unlike the C collision it isn't a deliberate reuse — it reads like a drafting slip in a site where γ is the single most-repeated symbol.

**Acronyms — and the fix already exists on this site.** `/dark-matter` is the worst case I found: I counted 16 acronyms (CDM, MOND, EM, LUX, XENON, PandaX, LHC, SPARC, ALFALFA, SDSS, BTFR, BIC, RAR, BAO, CMB, CFD) and exactly one — MOND — is expanded on first use. This is the page a general reader is most likely to land on from a search for "dark matter."

Meanwhile `/tier-1-existing` carries a dedicated **"Acronym key"** section defining MOND, RAR, BTFR, EFE, DESI, SPARC, and DM. That's the pattern. It works, it's already written, and it's on exactly one page. Porting that block to `/dark-matter`, `/test-catalog`, and `/honest-assessment` would close most of this gap without any new writing.

I did chase down one thing I'd half-assumed was an error: CFD. It appears unexpanded twice on `/dark-matter` ("the CFD viscosity interpretation," "The CFD reframing maps coherence C to inverse viscosity"), and the landing page uses "a single-observer, **CFD-like** model." I went to the glossary expecting a bespoke coherence-flavored expansion and found the standard one: "CFD — Computational Fluid Dynamics: Numerical simulation of fluid flow using discretized equations of motion on a grid." So the usage is consistent and correct. It's purely a first-use expansion problem, not a terminology collision.

**The glossary** is the best-written artifact on the site and the hardest to use. 72 terms, alphabetical, Greek symbols sorted after Latin, badges clustered separately — all sensible decisions, all documented in a note. There is no search box, no filter, no alphabet jump-links. When I wanted to check "kill criterion" mid-read I scrolled. The definitions themselves are models of concision: MRH as "an 'event horizon for influence' — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"; kill criterion as "a specific, pre-registered outcome that would falsify a prediction if observed." I spot-checked for gaps and found CMB, CDM, and YBCO missing — all three are used unexpanded on pages a reader reaches early.

**Number consistency check.** This is the one that concerns me most, because it's the kind of error this particular site can least afford. The landing page's Research Activity block states **"Galaxies analyzed: 14,610."** `/why-synchronism` says **"14,760 galaxies (SPARC + ALFALFA-SDSS)."** `/dark-matter` explains the composition: **"The '14,760 galaxies' figure pools two different measurements: 175 resolved SPARC rotation curves ... and 14,585 ALFALFA–SDSS Tully–Fisher objects."** 175 + 14,585 = 14,760, so the two deep pages agree with each other and with their own arithmetic. The landing-page figure is 150 short and matches nothing. On a site whose entire proposition is "we audit ourselves in public," a headline metric that disagrees with its own breakdown is a disproportionate credibility cost for a one-line fix.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter (`/galaxy-plotter`)** — worked — **intuitive, best-labeled tool on the site**. A dropdown of five SPARC galaxies (DDO 154, NGC 2403, NGC 3198, UGC 128, NGC 7331) drives a rotation-velocity-vs-radius plot with five curves, and — crucially — *every curve is labeled in prose, not just by color*: Newtonian visible-matter-only, observed, Synchronism's C(ρ) at γ=2, a hand-tuned stand-in explicitly marked as the pre-2026-07-08 display, and MOND's simple-ν function. Keeping a superseded curve on the chart and labeling it as historical is a documentation decision I'd steal. The readout "max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on" is a model of a plain-language result line. Documentation gaps: the "Reparametrization" badge isn't linked to its definition, and BTFR/SPARC/kpc appear unexpanded — though this page does link out to the glossary, which most don't.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`)** — worked — **clear, and unusually honest about its own axis**. A slider walks γ from 0 to 4 across three labeled regimes (γ < 0.6 strongly-correlated, γ ≈ 1 boundary, γ > 1.4 uncorrelated), with real systems placed along it (BCS superconductors at γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, off-scale left; water/enzymes 0.49–1.0; ideal gases and galaxies at 2). Two things I'd call out as good practice: each regime card carries a **"reality check"** line naming where the prediction diverges from observation, and there's an explicit **"Naming note"** clarifying that C(ρ) is a smooth *crossover*, not a thermodynamic phase transition — i.e. the page corrects the misreading its own title invites. That is exactly what a naming note is for. The gap: the tool is described as "audited-negative," the term is never defined on the page, and "audited-negative" is one of the least self-explanatory labels in the taxonomy — it's the one most in need of the link that isn't there.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`)** — worked — **structurally fine, semantically unlabeled**. Filter tabs by regime (All 23, Neutral 4, Coherence helps 12, Incoherence helps 4, Barrier-dominated 3) over a sortable table of properties, Pearson r values (−0.434 to +0.982), category, and regime. Sorting works, counts in the tab labels are a nice touch. Two documentation problems: the four regime names are the entire point of the interface and none is defined on the page; and the tool honestly discloses that it shows "23 hand-curated rows from 1,703 phenomena" while noting "targets are near-monotonic in Z, so any smooth monotonic curve scores r ≈ 1 by construction" — a caveat that undercuts the whole table, placed *below* it. If the correlations are artifacts by construction, that sentence belongs above the data, not under it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Why is the canonical badge taxonomy a section inside `/honest-assessment` rather than its own page? It's referenced as "canonical," it's linked with a fragment identifier, and it's the site's controlled vocabulary — every signal says it wants to be a page.
2. Are "Withdrawn" (operational state) and "WITHDRAWN" (lifecycle verb) the same label, or two? Same question for the whole operational-state / lifecycle-verb split — what distinguishes the families?
3. Is "Model Explainer" a validation badge, a content category, or informal shorthand? It's rendered like a badge and isn't in the taxonomy.
4. Where did 14,610 come from? It's not 14,760, not 14,585, and not 14,585 + 175. Is there a third dataset count somewhere that I didn't find, or is it stale?
5. The five breadcrumb categories — are they intended as future nav sections, or vestigial? If they're the real IA, the nav should show it; if they're not, they shouldn't be in the breadcrumb.
6. Is γ on `/key-claims` the same γ as everywhere else? "Coherence parameter" vs "sharpness dial" is either a wording slip or an undocumented second meaning, and I can't tell which from the page.

### Verdict

As documentation this is well above average: breadcrumbs on every page, learning paths with step indicators and honest dual-path membership, a glossary whose definitions are genuinely well-crafted, clean routing with deliberate aliases, and an "Acronym key" pattern that solves the site's biggest terminology problem on the one page that uses it. The self-correcting habits are visible in the prose — dated corrections inline, a "reality check" line on every regime card, a naming note that pre-empts a misreading of the page's own title.

The gap is that the site has built a large, precise controlled vocabulary and hasn't built the retrieval layer for it. Roughly 29 badge labels across five families, defined once, in an anchor, inside the longest page, linked from two of the dozen-plus pages that use them. Everything else I found is ordinary maintenance — expand acronyms on first use, port the acronym key, add glossary search, reconcile nav and breadcrumb labels. The exception is the 14,610/14,760 mismatch, which is small to fix and disproportionately expensive to leave: it is a self-audit site whose top-line number doesn't match its own breakdown.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The self-criticism is real and the arithmetic I spot-checked all reproduces — but the site consistently reports *empirically* what it could prove *algebraically*, and one pre-registered kill criterion appears to have been softened after the fact.

### Journey

A colleague sent me this as "an interesting AI project," which usually means I'll spend ten minutes finding the reparametrization and leave. That is not what happened. I went straight past the landing page to `/equation-walkthrough`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/galaxy-rotation` and `/tier-1-existing`, and I checked the math with a Python REPL open next to the browser.

**Things that check out.** a₀ = cH₀/(2π) at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.0422×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², 13.2% below Milgrom's 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ — the site says 13%. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 119.0 M☉/pc² — the site says 119, and the claimed algebraic identity with cH₀/(4π²G) is exact. C(ρ_crit) at γ=2 is tanh(2 ln 2) = 0.8824 — the site says ≈0.88 and correctly warns that ρ_crit is therefore *not* the half-way point (half-max sits at 0.316 ρ_crit). TEST-09's "3.3σ" is (3.75−3.35)/√(0.10²+0.07²) = 3.28σ. TEST-10's B ≥ 13.7 is 1/(1−0.927). The BTFR normalization M_b = 47 V⁴ is McGaugh's. I did not find a single arithmetic error. That is rarer than it should be and I want it on the record before I start complaining.

**Then I did the algebra the site didn't do.** The headline function is tanh(γ·ln(1+x)). But tanh(ln u) = (u²−1)/(u²+1) identically, so with u = (1+x)^γ:

> **C(ρ) = [(1+ρ/ρ_crit)^{2γ} − 1] / [(1+ρ/ρ_crit)^{2γ} + 1]**

There is no transcendental content in this function at all. It is a Möbius map of a power law — a one-parameter algebraic family in the exponent 2γ. The `/equation-walkthrough` page gives tanh and ln separate sections, each with its own physical story ("dimmer switch," "compresses scale"), which invites the reader to believe the composition carries the union of those meanings. It carries neither. Their composition is elementary and the decomposition is, physically, a fiction.

**And this is where it gets sharp.** `/galaxy-rotation` reports that the free-γ compander converges to γ ≈ 0.49 with RMS "identical to McGaugh to four digits," ΔBIC = +7.1, and calls this an empirical collapse to MOND. Put γ ≈ 0.49 into the closed form: 2γ ≈ 0.98 ≈ 1, and at 2γ = 1 exactly,

> C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2)

That is **Milgrom's simple interpolating function with a₀ → 2a₀**. Not "MOND-like." Not "statistically indistinguishable from." It is the simple μ function, rescaled. I checked the residual at the actual fitted γ=0.49 rather than the round number: agreement with x/(x+2) is 2.0% at x=0.01 falling to 0.2% at x=100 — 2% or better across four decades in acceleration. The reason the fit "converges to γ≈0.49" is that the fitter is walking downhill toward an exact identity, and the reason RMS matches McGaugh to four digits is that simple-μ has been known to fit the SPARC RAR about as well as McGaugh's exponential form for a decade.

The site earned this conclusion the expensive way — 2,807 RAR points and a BIC — and then reported it as a fitting outcome. It is two lines of algebra and it is *stronger* as algebra, because a fit can always be argued with and an identity cannot. This is the single biggest missed opportunity on the site.

**The rotation-curve failure is misdiagnosed.** `/galaxy-plotter` shows the violet Synchronism curve undershooting and explains: disk densities sit orders of magnitude below ρ_crit, so the coherence boost never activates. That framing says the problem is *normalization* — pick a smaller ρ_crit and it would work. It wouldn't. C is monotonically increasing in ρ, and ρ(r) in a disk is monotonically decreasing in r, so the boost term V_flat·C(ρ(r)) **always declines outward**, for every ρ_crit. Flat rotation curves need the opposite. I swept ρ_crit over eight orders of magnitude against an exponential disk: for large ρ_crit the curve falls *faster* than Newtonian; as ρ_crit → 0 the boost saturates to C ≈ 1 everywhere measured and you get v² = v_b² + V_flat², a constant additive offset with a spurious inner hump — a two-parameter fudge, not a curve shape. There is no ρ_crit in between that works. The failure is in the **monotonicity**, not the scale, and it is the same sign error that shows up as "MOND requires ρ_crit ∝ V⁻², framework asserts V⁺²" on `/parameter-derivations` and as the γ sign inversion on `/equation-walkthrough`. Three pages report three symptoms of one structural fact. Nothing connects them.

**On that γ inversion** — the site flags it (larger N_corr gives flatter transitions; real condensed matter does the opposite) and flags the CLT self-contradiction (CLT needs independent variables, N_corr counts correlated ones). Both correct. But neither page states the obvious diagnosis: 1/√N is the finite-size *rounding width* of a mean-field transition. γ is a *sharpness*, which is the reciprocal of a width. Somebody wrote down σ/√N — which is why the CLT got invoked — and assigned it to the slot where √N belonged. That is a specific, correctable provenance error, not a mysterious inversion, and the repair (γ ∝ √N_corr) is one character. Nowhere does the site ask what happens to the SPARC fit if you flip it. I'd want to know, and so would anyone who reads that page.

**The methodology page is where I stopped being charitable.** `/tier-1-existing` is the best artifact on the site — genuine pre-registration, explicit kill criteria, dates of execution. Then TEST-04a: registered kill criterion is **"fσ₈(z=0.51) > 0.46"**. Observed: **0.550 ± 0.062**. The criterion fired. The page instead reports "exceeds 0.46 by only ~1.5σ, below >3σ threshold" and files it as underpowered rather than killed. The ">3σ" gloss is not in the registered criterion, which is a value threshold, not a significance threshold. Converting one into the other *after seeing the data* is exactly the move pre-registration exists to prevent. On a site whose central claim is that its methodology is the contribution, this is the most damaging item I found — worse, in my view, than any physics failure, because the physics failures are honestly reported and this one is a crack in the instrument doing the reporting.

Relatedly, the headline "**6 refutations executed on external data**" does not survive the site's own table. TEST-03 was never run as registered (a substituted protocol was), and TEST-04 was withdrawn on the framework's *own* forecast without ever touching DESI data. Counting a self-withdrawal as a refutation-on-external-data inflates the number. As-registered kills against external data: four. The page's own "3–4 independent roots" line is the honest figure; the landing-page banner isn't.

**Tools.** Both are physically literate, which I did not expect. The γ calculator prints the inversion as a warning on the tool itself rather than burying it — good practice. The galaxy plotter shows the framework's real curve failing next to the illustrative one it replaced, and labels which is which. But the plotter uses a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ for its MOND comparison while `/parameter-derivations` presents a₀ = cH₀/(2π) = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ as the framework's derived value. The site's own tool doesn't use the site's own constant, and 13% in a₀ is not cosmetic at the RAR knee.

**Loose ends.** `/key-claims` asserts that "mass cancels from a disk's ρ/ρ_crit ratio entirely — no galaxy, of any mass, crosses the coherence knee." That's a strong structural no-go stated without the algebra. Using the site's own scalings (M_b = 47V⁴, R₀ = V²/3a₀, ρ_crit = 0.029V²) I get ρ/ρ_crit ∝ V⁻⁴ — steeply mass-dependent, spanning ~10³ from V=50 to V=300 km/s. Either a different ρ is meant, or the claim is overstated; I can't tell which from the page.

Finally, `/mrh` defines the horizon as "the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence coherence evolution," notes it behaves like a correlation length ξ, and then says the formalism "is not yet Markov." Nobody on that page says the words *renormalization group*. Scale-dependent boundaries, a correlation length, coarse-graining, and a question about what survives when you integrate out short distances — that is the RG, it has been quantitative since 1971, and it is sitting right there. Of everything I read, this is the one place the framework has something and doesn't know it.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What does the SPARC fit do if γ = 2/√N_corr is repaired to γ ∝ √N_corr? The site flags the inversion everywhere and tests the consequence nowhere.
2. Is there *any* observable in the framework that isn't a monotone function of local ρ? 4. A framework can survive an underived parameter; it can't survive one that is both underived and load-bearing.
5. Given that the compander at fitted γ *is* simple-μ with a rescaled a₀, what is left of the claim that C(ρ) is a new function rather than a change of variables?
6. If a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is taken seriously, H₀ must be ≈77.6 to recover Milgrom's a₀ — the site says so. ### Verdict
This is a framework that failed, documented by people who understood that it failed, and that combination is rare enough that I'd send it to a methods seminar even though I wouldn't send it to a journal. The physics is a reparametrization of MOND with a sign error, and — this is the part the site hasn't noticed — the reparametrization is provable in two lines rather than inferable from a BIC. ## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The site's verdict "0 discriminating tests vs MOND, by execution" is true of the tests it ran and false of the framework — a density-keyed modification is *not* degenerate with an acceleration-keyed one outside rotating disks, and the site retired that discriminator on a qualitative argument without checking the numbers.

### Journey

I came to this the way I come to any "AI-generated physics" claim: assume reparametrization, look for the two lines that prove it, leave. Pass 3's colleague already found those two lines and they're correct — tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) is a Möbius map of a power law, 2γ≈1 is Milgrom's simple μ. I'm not going to re-litigate that. I went looking for something else: **is there a place where this framework and MOND must disagree, and did anyone check?**

The site says no. `/for-researchers`: *"0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, and — on the discrimination axis — 2 executed tests that discriminated, both of which selected MOND."* `/honest-assessment`: *"net discriminating galaxy tests vs MOND: 0, by execution."* The galaxy sector is *"a strict submodel of MOND,"* so discrimination is *"impossible by mathematical structure alone."*

That last clause is where I stopped agreeing.

**The EFE was retired on the wrong argument.** `/tier-1-existing` caveat 2 says the External Field Effect is *"the only structurally discriminating prediction against MOND"* but that registration is *"blocked on theory, not data"* because two C conventions give opposite signs. `/galaxy-rotation` goes further and retires it outright: *"MOND's own External Field Effect predicts the same qualitative environment dependence (Chae et al. 2020/2021), so the effect does not discriminate the two."*

Same **qualitative** dependence is not the same prediction. MOND's EFE is a functional of the external **acceleration** g_ext ~ GM/r². A C(ρ) framework's environmental sensitivity — whatever the sign convention — is a functional of the external **mass density** ρ_ext ~ M/r³. Those are different functions of distance from the perturber. They agree in sign and disagree by orders of magnitude, and there is a well-studied class of systems where they diverge maximally: satellites far enough out that the host's acceleration still dominates while the host's density does not.

**Crater II is the case, and it is already published.** Numbers I ran (M ≈ 3.2×10⁵ M☉, R_h ≈ 1.07 kpc, D ≈ 120 kpc, MW baryonic M ≈ 6×10¹⁰ M☉):

| quantity | value | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| Crater II internal g | 3.9×10⁻¹⁴ m/s² | ~3×10⁻⁴ a₀ — deepest MOND regime known |
| MW g_ext at 120 kpc | 5.8×10⁻¹³ m/s² | **g_ext / g_int ≈ 15** |
| Crater II internal ρ | 6.3×10⁻⁵ M☉/pc³ | |
| MW baryonic ρ at 120 kpc | ≲10⁻⁶ M☉/pc³ (generous) | **ρ_ext / ρ_int ≲ 0.016** |

At one object, the environment is **15× louder than the system in acceleration and ~60× quieter in density.** MOND therefore says Crater II is EFE-dominated and must be anomalously cold; McGaugh (2016) published σ_EFE = 2.1 (+0.9/−0.6) km/s *a priori*, against an isolated-MOND value roughly twice that, and Caldwell et al. (2017) measured 2.7 km/s. That is the single cleanest EFE detection in the literature. A framework whose modification reads local density has no channel through which the Milky Way at 120 kpc can suppress anything — it sits on the isolated branch, at maximum boost, and over-predicts by about a factor of two.

**This is a discriminating test, on public data, at $0, and it is not in the catalog.** Neither are NGC 1052-DF2 / DF4, where the same logic runs the other way: ultra-diffuse, ultra-low density, so C(ρ) demands *maximal* boost, while the objects are observed to be near-Newtonian and MOND accommodates them through the group's external field (Haghi et al. 2019). The framework's sign convention ambiguity does not protect it here — under *either* convention a density-keyed C cannot reproduce an acceleration-keyed suppression, because the two environmental proxies differ by ~10³ in the ratio that matters.

**And this explains why the framework "collapses to MOND" in the first place — which is a stronger result than the collapse itself.** For a thin exponential disk, g ≈ 2πGΣ and ρ = Σ/2h, so ρ = g/(4πGh). Disk scale heights are quasi-universal (h ≈ 0.1–0.3 kpc, near-independent of galaxy size), which makes ρ and g **collinear across the entire SPARC sample by geometry.** Any C(ρ) calibrated on disks is therefore forced into a C(g) family — the free-γ fit walking downhill to simple-μ isn't a coincidence, it's the sample enforcing a degeneracy the theory never claimed. The site reports the collapse as a fact about the theory. It's a fact about the *test set*.

Break the geometry and the degeneracy breaks with it. Pressure-supported systems distribute mass over their full radius rather than a thin sheet, so at fixed acceleration they sit a factor ~3–10 lower in ρ. At the fitted γ = 0.49 that is not a nuance:

```
C(ρ_crit)        = 0.327
C(0.3 ρ_crit)    = 0.128   → boost differs by 2.6×
C(0.1 ρ_crit)    = 0.047   → boost differs by 7.0×
```

**Every one of the 11 Tier 1 tests is on rotationally supported disks or on cosmology.** SPARC (×5), ALFALFA-SDSS TFR, DESI, Cassini. There is not one pressure-supported system in the ledger. The framework declared itself indistinguishable from MOND using exclusively the sample class in which it is indistinguishable from MOND by construction. That is a sampling artifact wearing a no-go theorem's clothes, and it is the most consequential thing I found.

**TEST-04a: everyone is using the wrong statistic.** The registration is fσ₈(z=0.51) > 0.46, prediction 0.418, ΛCDM 0.474, observed 0.550 ± 0.062. The page reports *"exceeds 0.46 by ~1.5σ."* That number measures the distance from the **decision boundary**, which is not a hypothesis and has no sampling distribution. The statistics that mean something:

```
obs vs Synchronism (0.418):  2.13σ
obs vs ΛCDM       (0.474):  1.23σ
Δχ² = 3.03  →  ~1.7σ preference for ΛCDM
```

So the honest read is "mildly disfavors Synchronism, cannot discriminate." Not "underpowered," not "killed" — *uninformative*, and for a stateable reason. Worse, requiring the observation to clear 0.46 by 3σ puts the effective kill line at 0.646, which is **2.8σ above ΛCDM's own prediction.** As re-adjudicated, the criterion can only fire on data that simultaneously refutes ΛCDM. A kill criterion that requires the standard model to die first is not a kill criterion. Pass 3 flagged the post-hoc softening; the deeper problem is that the softened version is unfalsifiable in principle, not merely underpowered in practice.

**And even a clean success wouldn't discriminate.** A ~12% growth suppression points the same direction as the S₈ tension and is degenerate with massive neutrinos, evolving dark energy, and baryonic feedback — a crowded corner of parameter space with at least four occupants. A registration that specifies only a threshold, and not what else predicts the same suppression and how you'd tell them apart, buys nothing on confirmation. Half a pre-registration.

**The prospective registration is decaying while it waits.** The page defers to DESI DR2, *"spring 2027."* DR2 full-shape clustering is already in circulation in 2026 — conference presentations, a DR2 publications page, and a DR2 Lyα full-shape validation paper that, notably, **removed fσ₈ from the final analysis** after finding it biased on mocks and unstable under reasonable modeling choices. That last point matters twice: it's a live systematics warning against the exact observable this test is registered on, and it means the target is not the fixed quantity the registration assumes. Lock and timestamp the DR2 registration now, publicly, with a hash — otherwise by spring 2027 it will be another retrodiction and the site will have to litigate that too.

**Nobody has asked what the framework says about w(z).** a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π ties the framework's only cosmological handle directly to the expansion history, and DESI's headline result is evolving dark energy at multi-σ. The framework has a forced opinion here and has never stated it. `/parameter-derivations` gets close — it notes a₀(z) rises 3.03× by z=2 while the ceiling falls to 1.08, and correctly calls that an internal contradiction — but treats it as an internal bookkeeping problem rather than as contact with the biggest live anomaly in the field.

**B_max = 1/Ω_m — the diagnosis nobody wrote down.** The site says it's *"asserted, not derived,"* carries *"the framework's entire discriminating weight,"* and drives two of the six refutations. True but incomplete. 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17 is a **cosmic mean**. Using a globally-averaged cosmological ratio as a ceiling on a *local* dynamical quantity is a category error: bound systems are by definition overdensities, and no theorem makes a global mean bound a local ratio. That isn't an underived parameter, it's a misapplied one — and it predicts its own failure mode exactly. TEST-10 finding 69% of galaxies above the ceiling, with a tail requiring B ≥ 13.7, is what you get when you apply a mean to objects selected for being far above the mean. Saying "asserted" records the gap; saying "cosmic mean applied locally" closes it.

**A2ACW: the best result on the site, and it has no control group.** `/research-philosophy` is the page I'd actually send to colleagues. The retrospective — take six claims later demoted, re-run adversarial challenge with different-training-cutoff models, catch **0 of 6** — is a real, cleanly-designed negative result about the current limits of AI-generated science, and *"two AI models trained on the same physics corpus will share the same blind spots — they jointly miss what the literature missed"* is the correct interpretation of it. The line *"provenance, not assurance"* is the most epistemically careful sentence on the site.

But the 0/6 is confounded, and the confound is fixable. The stated conclusion is "A2ACW cannot see past its corpus." The unexamined alternative is "A2ACW kills nearly everything" — a 1.4% survival rate is aggressive enough that a 0/6 catch rate on *demoted* claims tells you little until you know the false-kill rate on *good* ones. **The missing arm is a positive control:** feed A2ACW a set of genuine historical results the models can be date-shielded from, and measure how many it kills. If it kills them too, the finding is miscalibration, not blind spots — a completely different paper with a completely different remedy. Running one arm of a two-arm design and reporting the single-arm result as a structural property is exactly the methodological move this site exists to criticize.

Second problem with the same number: **3,308 sessions is not 3,308 independent claims.** Sessions revisiting one claim are correlated, so "1.4%" has an unknown effective denominator and is not a rate in any statistical sense. Report the count of distinct claims, or drop the percentage from the landing page.

**Credit where due.** Reclassifying the entity criterion Γ < m as a standard narrow-width/quasiparticle condition known since 1936 is the correct call and not an easy one to make about your own only-surviving-novelty. Caveat 1 on `/tier-1-existing` — that TEST-09 and TEST-10 share a structural root and honest independent roots are 3–4, not 6 — is more honest than the landing banner it contradicts. And retiring TEST-07 for having *"no amplitude derived, no mechanism specified"* rather than running it and claiming a null is the right instinct.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer (`/coherence-explorer`)** — worked — **numerically exact, and the most conceptually honest tool here**. I checked every displayed value against the closed form at the defaults (γ = 0.49, ρ_crit = 1): C(ρ_crit) = 0.3272, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.8259, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.9785, and the stated C = 0.50 crossing at 2.07·ρ_crit — all four reproduce to four digits, and the Möbius closed form agrees with the tanh evaluation to machine precision. Two design decisions I'd call excellent: it defaults to **γ = 0.49, the SPARC-preferred value**, rather than the framework's asserted γ = 2 that was refuted at ΔBIC = +184 — a tool that ships with the fitted value instead of the doctrinal one; and it tells the user outright that this is *"a one-parameter family plus a pan control,"* i.e. that ρ_crit carries *"no shape information."* That is the correct statement about the function's structure and I have never seen an interactive concede it. The gap: it stops one step short. Having established that ρ_crit is pure translation, it should show the closed form and say the family is indexed by the single exponent 2γ — at which point the reader sees for themselves that 2γ ≈ 1 is simple-μ, and the site's central negative result becomes visible in the tool rather than argued in prose three pages away.

- **Consciousness Threshold Demo (`/consciousness-demo`)** — worked — **an interesting genre and an under-executed instance**. Eight "independent" methodological routes to a consciousness threshold (phase transition, integrated information, neural binding, self-modeling, metabolic criticality, mirror self-recognition, anesthesia onset, sleep-wake boundary), hover to expand, mean C = 0.499, sd = 0.012 — and the page's own read is that the convergence is an artifact of every route inheriting the output-range midpoint of a [0,1)-bounded function. That is correct and it is the right thing to build: an interactive whose purpose is to show the reader *why not to believe it*. Two problems. First, the "too small to be honest" observation is left as prose when it's a computable statistic (see friction log) — on a site that criticizes vibes-based adjudication, that's the wrong place to stop. Second, and more serious: **the eight approaches are never shown to be derivable from the tanh assumption.** The claim is that all eight "share the same tanh-based assumptions," but mirror self-recognition and anesthesia onset are empirical clinical constructs with no obvious route into a [0,1) coherence variable. Either each mapping is written down — in which case the artifact claim is demonstrated — or the eight are just eight numbers someone assigned. As it stands the page asserts its own debunking as confidently as a credulous version would assert the finding, and the reader has no way to check either. The self-critique needs the same evidentiary standard as the claim.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is Crater II's predicted velocity dispersion under C(ρ), under each sign convention? This is a one-afternoon calculation against a published measurement and it is the framework's only live shot at a discriminating result — win or lose. Why has nobody run it?
2. Has any pressure-supported system ever been tested? Not one appears in Tier 1. If the answer is "the ontology only speaks to rotating disks," that should be stated as a scope limit rather than presented as a structural no-go.
3. Was the false-kill rate of A2ACW ever measured against known-good physics? Without it, the 0-of-6 result has two readings and the site has committed to one.
4. Under the framework, what is w(z)? a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π makes this obligatory, and DESI's evolving-dark-energy result is the field's loudest current signal.
5. Is the DR2 registration hashed and timestamped anywhere public? "Prospective" is a claim about a document's existence at a moment in time and needs an artifact, not an assertion on a page that can be edited.
6. Does C(ρ) mean local density, enclosed density, or environmentally-smoothed density? Every environment test (TEST-03s, TEST-05) used an *external* density proxy while the mechanism is *local* — the framework's own note says the local mechanism gives a ~10⁻³ dex lever. Two of the six refutations may have tested a quantity the theory doesn't predict.
7. The site says C(a) — an acceleration-keyed bounded form — is "the only form whose EFE prediction differs from MOND," and rejects it for failing the RAR. But C(a) is not this framework; the framework's distinctive commitment is C(**ρ**). Why was the ρ-keyed EFE prediction never computed at all?

### Verdict

The methodology page is publishable and the physics ledger is not, and the site knows both — that combination is rare enough that I'd cite `/research-philosophy` in a paper about the limits of automated discovery. But the site has talked itself into a stronger negative than it earned. "Discrimination impossible by mathematical structure alone" is true on rotating disks, where a density-keyed and an acceleration-keyed modification are degenerate because h/R is quasi-universal, and it is false everywhere else. Every executed test lives inside that degeneracy. Crater II sits outside it, has a published a priori MOND prediction and a published measurement, costs nothing, and would discriminate — and it is absent from an eleven-entry catalog that includes a $10M+ tier.

My real concern is that the failure discipline has become its own attractor. TEST-04a was softened until it couldn't fire; the EFE was retired on a qualitative hand-wave; the A2ACW null was reported without its control arm. Those are three instances of the same reflex — reaching the conclusion "nothing here discriminates" faster than the evidence gets there. A framework this thoroughly refuted has earned the right to one more honest swing, and there is a live one sitting in the Local Group.
