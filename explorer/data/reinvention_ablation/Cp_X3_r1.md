**Short answer:** I don't know of a published model with this exact form, so I believe it is unpublished. I answered from memory and did not search, so treat the citations below as unverified.

**Nearest relatives**
- **Milgrom's modified-inertia MOND.** Milgrom (1983, ApJ 270, 365) first proposed the modified-inertia reading. Milgrom 1994 (Ann. Phys. 229, 384, "Dynamics with a nonstandard inertia-acceleration relation") develops it as a nonlocal action. Those versions make inertia depend on acceleration, or on the trajectory's history, with a scale a₀ ~ cH₀/2π. This construction instead uses a period-dependent mass and a time scale τ, so it is not the same. It is conceptually the same kind of "inertia depends on the orbit" idea.
- **McCulloch's MiHsC / quantized inertia.** McCulloch (2007, MNRAS 376, 338) proposed that inertia is reduced when the Unruh wavelength approaches the Hubble scale. In that model the reduction depends on acceleration, and it also appears in wide-binary discussions. It is the closest published case of a mass that drops at Hubble-scale cutoffs. It has no logarithm, no κ and no period.
- **Other period-dependent proposals.** I recall none, and the factor τ = 10⁻⁶·2π/H₀ looks ad hoc. I know of no published model with a ln(1+P/τ) inertia law.

**Wide-binary data the model would need to match**
- Hernandez et al. (2012, MNRAS 424, 1304, and later papers).
- Chae (2023, ApJ 952, 128), who claims a MOND-like boost.
- Banik et al. (2024, MNRAS 527, 4573), who find no boost.
- Pittordis & Sutherland (2018, 2023).

The results are contested, so the model's prediction is not clearly tested.

**Physics concerns, which I haven't fully checked**
1. **Equivalence principle.** A mass that depends on P breaks the weak equivalence principle unless the gravitational mass is also rescaled. It also depends on the orbit and not on the local state, so it is not a local property of the particle. For an eccentric or multi-body system, P is ill-defined.
2. **Newton's laws.** Effective G is multiplied by [1+κ ln(1+P/τ)]. At P = τ this is about 1.2. For galactic periods of about 2×10⁸ yr it is about 3.4, so v is boosted by about 1.8. The boost keeps growing logarithmically with P. That does not give flat rotation curves or the Tully–Fisher relation (v⁴ ∝ M), which come from a₀ scaling.
3. **Solar-system limits.** For planets P ≪ τ, so the effect is small. Outer-body and comet orbits with P up to about 10⁶ yr would need checking.

**Conclusion:** The model is probably not published as stated. It looks like an ad hoc variant of Milgrom-style nonlocal modified inertia or McCulloch's MiHsC, with a period-based cutoff. Before any claim of novelty, run a dedicated search (arXiv "period-dependent inertia", "time-nonlocal inertia", "wide binaries").
