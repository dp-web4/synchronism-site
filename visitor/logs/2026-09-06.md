## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /start-here, /first-encounter, /dark-matter, /tools, /coherence-explorer, /equation-walkthrough, /consciousness-demo, /honest-assessment, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A physics idea that openly says it didn't work, which is weirdly refreshing, but I never quite figured out what the idea *is* in plain words before the site started explaining why it failed.

### Journey

Came in from Reddit. The landing page headline "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of hook that gets me. Then the very next line: **"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."** I actually laughed. I've never seen a site do that. It made me trust it more, but it also confused me about why I should keep reading. If it didn't work, what's the pitch? The page does answer that lower down ("the honest map of what failed is the actual product") but by then I'd already scrolled past the equation, which looks like `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I have no idea what tanh or ln mean. The little explanation next to it ("0 = sparse, 1 = collective") helped a bit, but the symbols still felt like a wall.

The scoreboard was memorable: 0 predictions confirmed, 6 refutations, 0 of 6 validated. Honestly it reads like a sports team that went 0-6. I respect it, but a casual reader is going to wonder whether it's worth the 30 minutes the Beginner Path asks for.

Clicked **Start Here**. The marching band vs. crowd analogy landed immediately. That's the best sentence on the site for someone like me: "a crowd behaves independently, a marching band acts as one organism." I wish that was the first thing on the landing page instead of the equation. This page also mentioned "melting point predictions averaging 53% error" and "superconductor predictions off by orders of magnitude" — I don't know what the theory has to do with melting points, so that came out of nowhere.

**First Encounter** repeats the marching band thing (good, consistent) and then introduces "presence" and "MRH — Markov Relevancy Horizon." I got the "bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" picture. But I don't know what "Markov" means and the page didn't say. "Presence is density — but which stuff counts?" is a good question and I'm not sure I got the answer.

**Dark Matter** page: this is the one I actually wanted, because dark matter is the thing I know from YouTube. The page opens with a badge that says "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision" before I've read a single sentence of the idea. The idea itself ("what if there is no missing matter, only matter that doesn't participate in electromagnetic interactions?") sounded interesting but then it jumped into MOND, BAO, CMB, SPARC, "viscosity sign error"... I bounced about halfway.

**Tools** page was nice: cards with badges telling you what you're looking at. I tried the Coherence Explorer, Equation Anatomy, and (because it was called "Consciousness" and I couldn't resist) the Consciousness Demo.

Peeked at **Honest Assessment** — way too long and technical for me, full of test names like TEST-09 and TEST-25 and things like "ΔBIC=+184". Skimmed the headings and left. The **Glossary** was actually good; tanh and coherence and MRH all have plain definitions there. But I only found it because I went looking.

### Unanswered Questions
1. In one plain sentence: what does this theory say the universe does that regular physics doesn't? I read nine pages and still can't repeat it to a friend.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /coherence-function, /parameter-derivations, /dark-matter, /honest-assessment, /mrh, /key-claims, /tier-1-existing, /equation-walkthrough, /gamma-calculator, /coherence-explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes, as a case study in self-audit; no, as physics
- **Overall impression**: The math I could check is correct, the badges are honest to the point of self-flagellation, and the site has already written most of the referee report I would have written. What it has not done is state clearly, on the pages where it matters, that there are two different models (density-keyed and acceleration-keyed) and that every number quoted comes from one or the other.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project." I skipped the marching band and went to the equation. `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. My first reaction as a condensed-matter person: tanh is the mean-field Ising shape, but Ising is `m = tanh(βJzm)`, an implicit self-consistency equation. Here ρ goes in and C comes out. No feedback, no free energy, no critical point. I expected to have to argue this. Instead the /coherence-function page says it for me, verbatim: "The tanh shape is motivated by the Ising analogy, not derived from it; it is a phenomenological choice from the compander family." Good. That is the correct statement and most frameworks in this genre never make it.

**The algebra.** I checked the identity `tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^(2γ) − 1]/[(1+x)^(2γ) + 1]`. Correct (e^(2y) with y = γ ln(1+x)). The derivative `dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x)` is correct, equals γ at x=0, and is monotone decreasing. At γ = 1/2 the expression is `x/(x+2)`, which is MOND's simple μ(u) = u/(1+u) with u = x/2. Correct, and this is the whole game: at the free-fit value γ ≈ 0.49 the "coherence function" *is* Milgrom's simple interpolating function with a rescaled argument. The site says so on three pages. One imprecision: the page says the general-γ form is "exactly a two-parameter Hill function." It is not. `(u^n − 1)/(u^n + 1)` in u = 1+x is Hill in x only when n = 1; for other n it is Hill in a transformed variable w with w^n = ((1+x)^n − 1)/2. The walkthrough's phrasing "Hill-type rational function" is the accurate one. Minor, but the form-selection table lists "Hill (n=1)" as a separate row from "tanh-log," so the word "exactly" invites the question why they are two rows.

**The form-selection table** is the best thing on the site for someone like me. Ten companders against 2,807 SPARC points. tanh-log with γ pinned at 2: ΔBIC +184. Free γ: converges to 0.489, ΔBIC +7.1. Hill n=1 (= MOND simple μ): ΔBIC −0.7. I checked whether these hang together: free-γ at 0.489 is functionally the same curve as Hill n=1, so the only difference should be the one-parameter BIC penalty, ln(2807) ≈ 7.9. The table gap is 7.8. That is an internal consistency check the site doesn't point out but passes. The conclusion "tanh carries no statistical content" is the right one.

**Parameter derivations.** This page is a graveyard, and it knows it. γ = 2/√Ncorr is flagged as sign-inverted, and it is. Finite-size scaling gives transition width ∝ L^(−1/ν); more correlated volume means sharper, not flatter. Even the naive CLT argument the ansatz gestures at gives fluctuation ∝ 1/√N, so *sharpness* ∝ √N, i.e. γ ∝ √Ncorr, the inverse of what is written. The site's own γ Calculator then files BCS superconductors, among the sharpest transitions in nature, at γ = 6×10⁻⁴, the flattest curve on the page. ρcrit = A·V_flat² with A ≈ 0.029 M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻² gives ρcrit ≈ 10³ M☉/pc³ at V = 200 km/s. The solar neighborhood is ~0.1 M☉/pc³. So no disk ever gets near the knee, C ≈ γx everywhere, and γ and A collapse into one number (the page reports Fisher correlation +1.000000, which is what you'd expect for a model equal to its own linearization). The page also correctly notes that mimicking an acceleration threshold with a density threshold needs ρcrit ∝ V⁻² (at R = V²/a₀, ρ ~ a₀²/(GV²)), the opposite exponent from the asserted V⁺². All of this is stated as audited-negative. I have nothing to add except that it is right.

a₀ = cH₀/2π: I get 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² with H₀ = 67.4. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc². Both correct, both badged "reparametrization," both attributed to Milgrom's 1983 coincidence. Correct handling. R₀ = V²/(3a₀) still wears a deprecated "Validated" badge with "~3% accuracy"; the natural MOND transition radius is V²/a₀, so the 3 is a fitted factor and "3% accuracy" of a one-parameter fit against an unstated comparison set is not a validation of anything.

**Dark matter / Tier 1.** B_max = 1/Ωm = 3.17 implies f_DM ≤ 1 − Ωm = 0.685; SPARC's tail at f_DM = 0.927 needs B = 1/(1−0.927) = 13.7. Both numbers check. This is a genuine structural refutation, framework-specific, not shared with MOND. TEST-09 (BTFR slope 3.35 predicted vs 3.75 observed, 3.3σ) is the same root: a bounded boost caps the deep limit at n → 2 instead of MOND's n = 4. The site says on the Honest Assessment page that "everything that distinguishes this framework from MOND is a single asserted constant" and two of six refutations test it. That is the correct summary. The Cassini/SPARC squeeze (+18σ) is correctly labeled as inherited from MOND (Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024). The Bell/CHSH "refutation" is Bell's theorem applied to a classical substrate; calling it a theorem-level entry rather than a data refutation is honest.

**Where the site actually confused me**, and this is the substantive friction: there are two models here and the pages slide between them. (1) Density-keyed C(ρ/ρcrit): predicts Newtonian null for wide binaries, can't produce environment dependence, never reaches the knee in any disk, "boost never turns on" (galaxy plotter). (2) Acceleration-keyed C(g_bar/a₀), used as a QUMOND interpolant: fits the RAR at γ ≈ 0.49, is exactly MOND, and fails Cassini. The form-selection table on /coherence-function never says which argument x is. The Coherence Explorer caption says γ = 0.49 "matches galaxy data" and "fails Cassini," which is only true of model (2), while the same page says zooming to x < 10⁻³ reveals the SPARC regime where C = γx, which is model (1). A reader who does not already know the distinction will think one model both fits the RAR and is linear over all of SPARC, which is impossible. The /tier-1-existing EFE entry is the only place the split is stated plainly ("density-keyed C(ρ) predicts Newtonian null; acceleration-keyed C(a) becomes MOND-degenerate"), and it's buried in a closed-gap note.

**MRH.** The page proposes a definition ("minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution") and then admits there's no probability distribution, no graphical model, and no mapping from coherence dynamics to a conditional-independence condition. So it isn't a Markov blanket; it's a name. The page says so. No Pearl or Friston citation, which is odd given the borrowed vocabulary.

**Landau.** "Critical exponents off by ~2×" is loose (mean-field β = 0.5 vs 3D Ising 0.326 is ~1.5×; ν 0.5 vs 0.63 ~1.3×) but immaterial, since the page's stronger and correct point is that C(ρ) has no d, no n, no symmetry input, so it cannot encode a universality class at all.

### Journey

I came in knowing the genre: an AI-collaboration project claiming one equation covers quantum-to-cosmic. I expected overclaims and went looking for the novel prediction. The landing page pre-empted me. "0 prospective predictions confirmed, 6 refutations executed, 0 of 6 'validated' claims survive audit" is above the fold. Fine. Then the real question: is any of the 6 a refutation of something that *wasn't* already MOND?

**Honest Assessment.** The ledger splits the six as 2 mechanism roots (TEST-09 BTFR slope 3.75±0.10 vs predicted 3.35±0.07; TEST-10 dwarf f_DM max 0.927 needing boost ≥13.7 vs ceiling 3.17), 1 refuted registration (environment, r²=0.0001), 1 MOND-inherited (Cassini/SPARC squeeze, +17.95σ), 1 theorem (CHSH substrate S≤2). That split is correct and honest. TEST-09 and TEST-10 share a single root — the boost ceiling B_max = 1/Ω_m — so they are really one refutation counted twice, which the site itself acknowledges on /parameter-derivations ("this single parameter constrains TEST-09 and TEST-10 equally"). The CHSH item is a corollary of Bell's theorem for any local substrate; calling it a "refutation" is generous to the framework, since it refutes a class the framework never had a reason to be in.

**What is structurally distinct from MOND.** Exactly one thing: the bounded boost. MOND's deep regime is ν → √(a₀/g) unbounded; the framework caps the boost at 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17. That cap is what predicts BTFR slope 3.35 instead of 4 and f_DM ≤ 0.685 instead of →1. Both were tested against SPARC and both lost. So the one place the framework differs from MOND, the data picked MOND. The site's verdict "where it differs from MOND, external data refutes it" is accurate, not rhetoric.

**Coherence function.** The form-selection table on 2,807 SPARC RAR points is a MOND interpolating-function bake-off. Hill n=1 (simple μ) wins at ΔBIC −0.7; tanh-log with free γ=0.489 is last at +7.1. The identity tanh(½ ln(1+x)) = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) is exactly right (I checked it). The fitted γ=0.489 sits 2.2% from ½. That's the whole galaxy sector in one line: the framework's best fit is MOND's simple function with a rescaled argument, which is why it inherits Cassini. Hees et al. 2014/2016 already showed simple-μ-class functions fitting rotation curves are excluded by the Cassini quadrupole; the site's +17.95σ is that result re-executed. The "published independently at 8.7σ" attribution on the honest assessment has no citation attached — Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024 is the obvious candidate but the number should be sourced.

**Dark energy.** This is the page that surprised me. The substitution ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C gives w running from −2γ in the far past to −1 in the far future, monotonically, for all γ. That forces sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a). DESI DR2 prefers w₀ > −1 with w_a < 0, the opposite quadrant — a phantom crossing this construction cannot produce. Offsets quoted at 3.4–5.4σ. And at γ=½ the background is *algebraically ΛCDM* — the same γ where the galaxy sector becomes simple-μ MOND. The page's line "a theory whose best fit is its own disappearance" is the most precise sentence on the site. My question: why is this not the seventh refutation? The page says the substitution violates the Bianchi identity of the field equation, so it isn't a covariant prediction. That's a legitimate reason not to count it, but then the only version of the dark-energy sector that exists is dead at >3σ and the ledger reads "silence." I'd want a row that says so.

**Parameter derivations.** Zero first-principles derivations, stated up front. The sensitivity table is the most useful thing on this page and should be on the landing page instead of the session count: moving γ by 40× and A by 10⁹ changes χ²/N by 1.07–2.7×, while the boost floor moves it by 6.35×10⁴. Fisher correlation ρ(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000. That is a rigorous statement that the galaxy data measures one number (the boost ceiling) and nothing else. The consequence stated on the page — "for a density-keyed algebraic modification whose knee is calibrated above galactic midplane densities, the interpolating function is unidentifiable; the model equals its own linearisation to better than observational precision" — is a real, citable, framework-independent result. It applies to any ε(ρ) model with a knee above ~10⁻² M☉/pc³. Refracted Gravity (Matsakos & Diaferio; Cesare et al.) is named as the counter-example with the knee inside the sampled range, which is the right comparison. This should be a two-page note, not a subsection.

The H₀ inversion needs a caveat. Inverting a₀ = cH₀/2π with a₀ = 1.20×10⁻¹⁰ gives 77.6 km/s/Mpc, but with McGaugh's ±0.24 systematic that's 77.6 ± 15.5, consistent with both 67.4 and 73. So TEST-02's discriminating claim depends on which of Pass 3's two models you mean, and the page doesn't say.
