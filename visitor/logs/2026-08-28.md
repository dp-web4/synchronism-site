# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-08-28

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, The Core Idea, What Synchronism Is Not, Interactive Tools index, Galaxy Curve Plotter, γ Calculator, Coherence Explorer, Honest Assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most brutally honest science site I've ever clicked into from Reddit — and also one of the most exhausting, because every page seems to be arguing with itself in footnotes I can't read.

### Journey

**Landing page.** The headline grabbed me — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" That's exactly the kind of thing I'd watch a 20-minute video about. The marching-band-vs-crowd idea landed instantly: dense stuff acts together, sparse stuff acts alone, and there's one dial. Nice.

Then, still in the hero, it tells me the equation *already failed its key test* and "collapsed onto MOND." I don't know what MOND is. So within about 15 seconds the site told me its big idea is broken and turned into something I've never heard of. I kept reading mostly out of confusion about why the site exists.

Scrolling down, the page turns into a scoreboard: "TEST-09 & TEST-10," "CHSH null," "ΔBIC=+184," "16–28 orders of magnitude," "1.4% survival rate." I have no idea what any of the test names are. What I *did* get was the big number: **0 confirmed predictions**. That's stated maybe five times on one page. Also "3,308 AI-to-AI sessions" — I had to reread to figure out that means AIs argued with AIs about this. That's actually the most interesting sentence on the page and it's buried under a paragraph of caveats.

Three "who are you?" entry boxes — non-physicist / physicist / researcher — that's helpful. I picked the Beginner Path ("6 steps, ~30 min, equation-free"). Note: it is not equation-free. The equation shows up on Step 1.

**Step 1 — Why Synchronism?** Good opening: physics is split into separate rulebooks, what if one curve connected them. Crowd vs. band again. "Plain-English version" of the idea is genuinely readable: an S-curve from 0 to 1, a "dial" for how sharp the jump is. Then the "optional" math expands, and right below it there's a red-ish box saying the dial formula is "audited-negative" and "inverted for collective systems," followed by a fine-print block with "ansatz," "Hamiltonian," "compander," "μ-law audio companding," and "Hill/Naka–Rushton." I skipped all of it. The superconductor bit I *did* understand: they predicted 607 K and the real answer is 93 K. That's a miss I can feel. I'm on Step 1 of a beginner path and I already know it's wrong twice.

**Step 2 — First Encounter.** Best page on the site for me. "A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence." Neutron star "10¹⁴× denser than lead" — great. Then "presence (ρ)" — okay, density with a fancier name, I can live with that. Then "Markov Relevancy Horizon" appears with the explanation "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence." I bounced off that sentence completely. The atom-doesn't-feel-Andromeda line right after it saved it. Small confusion: the page says "Beginner Path Step 2 of 6" at the top and "Section 1 of 7" further down — two progress bars nested in each other. Am I 1/7 through step 2/6? Is the whole thing 42 pages?

**Step 3 — The Core Idea.** "Dimmer switch" — good. Then a graph of the S-curve with two markers explaining that the halfway point isn't at the middle (C=0.5 at "0.32·ρcrit" and C≈0.88 at ρcrit). I don't know why I'm being told this. Then the page becomes a list of things the site used to say and now retracts: "the ln does no work," "decorative vocabulary, not physics," "category error," and a second retraction dated August about the best fit contradicting the central claim. It reads like the corrections column of a newspaper, but longer than the article. For a Step 3 beginner page, the ratio of "here's the idea" to "here's why the idea is wrong" felt like 1:4.

**Step 4 — What Synchronism Is Not.** The six headers are great for someone like me: *Not a Theory of Everything, Not a replacement for ΛCDM or MOND, Not journal-reviewed, Not proven, Not just notation, Not just philosophy.* If the page were only those six headers plus one sentence each, I'd have understood the site's stance in 60 seconds. Instead each one expands into ΛCDM, QFT, EFE, "triple-conditional," "waveguide QED." One thing jumped out even to me: "Not just philosophy" says the consciousness equation is "specific and falsifiable," but the landing page badge on Consciousness said "Speculative — Untestable as Stated." Which is it?

**Tools index.** Nice that every tool has a tag before you click. But the tags are "Model Explainer," "Reparametrization," "Artifact Lesson" — I don't know what an Artifact Lesson is, and "Reparametrization" as a *category of tool* is baffling. I did notice the first tool is listed as "Coherism Explorer" while the actual page is "Coherence Explorer" (could be a typo on the index).

**Galaxy Curve Plotter.** This was the moment the whole thing clicked. Dashed gray line (what gravity from visible stuff *should* give) sags; blue dots (what we actually see) stay flat; "the gap is the dark matter problem." I've seen that graph on YouTube a dozen times and this is the first time I've clicked a dropdown and watched it change for five real galaxies. Excellent. Then the confusing part: the site's *own* curve (violet) just hugs the gray line and the legend says "boost never turns on." So the tool is a demo of the site's own idea failing while MOND's green line fits with "no per-galaxy tuning." And there's a dotted amber line that the page admits was "drawn to fit." Why is it on the chart at all? The big block of formulas below the plot is skip territory.

**γ Calculator.** The subtitle under the title is literally "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems." So the first thing the calculator tells me is that it's wrong. I played with the presets anyway (BCS superconductor = 10 million particles, ideal gas = 1) and the number and a curve moved — fun for about 30 seconds. But there's an annotation that renders as "ρcritC(ρcrit) = 0.60" — looks like two labels mashed together. And that number bugged me: the Core Idea page said C≈0.88 at ρcrit, the Coherence Explorer says 0.3272, this one says 0.60. Three pages, three values for what sounds like the same thing. (I'm guessing it depends on the dial setting, but nobody says so.)

**Coherence Explorer.** Two sliders, a curve, and a box of numbers. Sliders are responsive. I didn't really know what I was looking for once the curve moved — it's an S-curve that gets steeper or shifts sideways. A note says the default view "actually shows MOND." Again MOND, still not defined for me anywhere I looked.

**Honest Assessment.** Skimmed the top. The scoreboard table (0 confirmed / 6 refutations / 0 derived parameters) is the clearest thing on the site. Then a badge-definition section with what I counted as 14 different labels (five colors, four tags, five "operational states"). I stopped reading there.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — intuitive — The dropdown of 5 real galaxies and the "gap is the dark matter problem" text made this the single most educational thing on the site. Confusing that the site's own curve is the one that doesn't fit and a "hand-tuned" fake curve is drawn alongside it. Formula block below is skip-only.
- **γ Calculator** — worked — confusing — Presets are fun (10 million particles → tiny γ). But the page tells me the formula is backwards before I touch it, and I couldn't tell what I was supposed to learn from moving the number. Label collision on the graph annotation.
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mildly confusing — Sliders are responsive and the curve responds. I didn't know what a "good" curve looked like, so it was pretty but purposeless for me. "Default view shows MOND" — still didn't know what MOND is.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is MOND, in one sentence, and is "it collapsed onto MOND" good news or bad news?
2. Does this explain dark matter or not? The landing page says "dark matter mechanism structurally failed" but the whole galaxy plotter is about the dark matter gap.
3. What does "reparametrization" mean? The one place it was explained — "same mathematics wearing new costume" — was great. Why isn't that line everywhere the word appears?
4. Is the consciousness part testable or not? Two pages disagree.
5. If 0 predictions worked and 6 tests failed, what is the site asking me to *do*? Read, think, argue? Is there a "what happens next" page?
6. What is "presence" and why not just say density?
7. What does it mean that 3,308 AI sessions did this "with no human in the loop"? Is that a feature or a warning?

### Verdict
I came for "one equation for everything" and got a very long, very honest confession that the equation doesn't work yet — which is oddly compelling, but the confession is written for physicists, not for me. The galaxy plotter and the marching-band analogy got me 80% of the way to understanding; the retractions, test IDs, and undefined MOND took most of it back. I'd send a physics-major friend here; I probably wouldn't come back myself unless the beginner path actually stayed beginner.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 (landing, Interactive Tools [both `/tools` and `/interactive-tools`], Honest Assessment, Learning Paths, Glossary, Key Claims, For Researchers, Test Roadmap, Tier 1: Existing Data, MRH, Equation Anatomy, Phase Boundary Visualizer, Chemistry Correlation Explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I did not evaluate the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes — as an auditor; the site is an unusually rich case study in what happens to an information architecture under continuous correction
- **Overall impression**: The glossary and badge taxonomy are more complete than most science sites I've audited, but the site never *uses* them at the point of need — acronyms are defined thousands of words after first use, the badge vocabulary has sprawled to ~29 labels plus a dozen ad-hoc ones, and the breadcrumbs describe a section hierarchy that the navigation bar doesn't have.

### Journey

**Landing page.** Header nav has nine items: Home, Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. Footer nav repeats these and adds Key Claims, For Researchers, Research Archive, Report an Issue. So two of the three audience entry points (Key Claims for physicists, For Researchers for researchers) are reachable only from body cards and the footer — the header nav is beginner-shaped while the audience cards are three-way. The nav label is "Tools" and the page title is "Interactive Tools"; small, but it's the kind of thing I'd normalize.

Terminology on the landing page: MOND *is* expanded ("Modified Newtonian Dynamics — the 40-year-old rival gravity theory"), which surprised me given Pass 1's complaint — but the expansion sits well below the hero, where "collapsed onto MOND" is the first use. First-use rule violated, definition present. Not defined anywhere on the page: ΛCDM, CHSH, RAR, BTFR, ΔBIC, SPARC, ALFALFA, SDSS, DESI, fσ₈. The badge line says "Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed — definitions on the Honest Assessment page," and only Reparametrization gets an inline gloss. The landing page's beginner card says "6 steps, ~30 min, no equations required"; the Learning Paths page describing the same path says "No background required" and makes no equation-free claim. The overclaim originates on the landing card, not the path.

**Learning Paths.** Clean. Seven paths (three by difficulty, four by topic), step counts match listed steps, every step is linked. Breadcrumb: *Home / Getting Started / Learning Paths*. This is the site's only real sitemap — pages like The Coherence Function, MOND Unification, A2ACW Protocol, Born Rule Derivation exist only here and in cross-links. A2ACW is used in the Methodology path title without expansion.

**Breadcrumbs across the site.** I collected them: *Getting Started* (Learning Paths, Glossary, Key Claims, Honest Assessment), *Core Theory* (MRH), *Methodology* (For Researchers), *Predictions & Experiments* (Test Roadmap, Tier 1), and *Interactive Tools*. None of *Getting Started / Core Theory / Methodology / Predictions & Experiments* exists as a nav item, a landing page, or a menu group. The breadcrumbs describe a four-section hierarchy that lives only in the breadcrumbs. Filing an 81-heading Honest Assessment page under "Getting Started" also doesn't match how the page is used.

**Honest Assessment.** This page is doing three jobs: (a) the canonical badge reference, (b) the results ledger, (c) the correction log. It opens with the badge taxonomy *before* the "Why read an audit of a theory that didn't pan out?" intro — so a reader arriving from a `#validation-badge-definitions` link is fine, but a reader arriving from the nav gets a reference table before being told what the page is. I counted the taxonomy: 5 MRH-relationship tags, 4 descriptive tags, 2 deprecated, 9 "operational states," 9 "lifecycle verbs" — about 29 labels, with a "Transitions" section on top. Two of the "operational states" — "89% Boundary-Consistent" and "Template Bias Caveat" — are specific findings, not states; they don't belong in a taxonomy. The page's own TOC lists 11 sections against 81 headings; the correction blocks ("CORRECTED 2026-08-09", "Correction (2026-07-10)", "Correction (2026-07-09)", "Verdict correction (2026-07-04)") are headings at the same level as the sections they correct, so the TOC can't represent them.

**Glossary.** Genuinely good: ~85 headwords, every badge has an entry pointing to the canonical anchor, and 19 of the 20 terms I spot-checked are present with plain-language definitions ("MRH: 'event horizon for influence' — bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"; "presence: how much compatible stuff is nearby — generalized density"). The missing one is **boost** — which is the operative word in two of the six counted refutations (TEST-09/TEST-10 "bounded boost," B_max) and in the "boost ceiling" that Honest Assessment calls "the framework's only structural difference from MOND." The site's most consequential term has no definition. Also: the glossary's plain-language definitions are *better* than the ones on the entry pages. The landing page defines presence as "the compatible structural elements available within a system's relevancy boundary"; the glossary says "how much compatible stuff is nearby." The First Encounter page (per Pass 1) gives the formal MRH sentence; the glossary gives the bubble sentence. The ordering is inverted — formal on the entry pages, plain in the reference.

Nav top says "72 glossary terms" (For Researchers header) versus what reads as ~85 entries. Stale count.

**Key Claims.** Three claims, badges Speculative / Speculative / Failed. No badge legend on the page, just a link. No prev/next even though it's the physicist entry point. The consciousness badge reads "Speculative (parent claim, unfalsifiable as stated)" — a badge that needs a parenthetical sentence is a badge that's doing the wrong job; either the taxonomy has a label for this ("unrunnable" appears elsewhere on the site) or it should. Undefined at first use here: QM, DD, IIT, ENAQT, SNARC, PRL, BEC/BCS. The Dark Matter claim is badged Failed while the body says "consistent within systematics" about the a₀ relation — the reader has to infer that the badge is about the mechanism and the "consistent" is about a coincidence the framework disowns. That's a scoping problem a subheading would fix.

**For Researchers.** Breadcrumb *Home / Methodology / For Researchers*. Eleven status labels in use: Audited-Negative, Executed Closure, Registered Null, Pending Cross-Vendor Control, Failed, Disfavored, Post-hoc, Underpowered to Discriminate, Untested, Structurally Unreachable, "Preferred-Frame + Dim-4: Naturalness Gap." Only three (Audited-Negative, Failed, Untested) are in the canonical taxonomy. No legend link on the page. 17 of 23 acronyms undefined at first use (MDAR, EQG, LHAASO, LQG, RSD, DR1/DR2, PR-box…). This is the page for the audience most able to cope, but it's also the page that claims to be "what's citable," so vocabulary control matters most here.

**Test Roadmap and Tier 1.** The TEST-ID scheme is explained well (flat namespace, `a` = amended, `s` = substituted, "must not be read as the parent's result") — the best piece of reference writing on the site. But the Roadmap is still titled "24 experiments" in the Advanced path and in the For Researchers header while the page itself lists TEST-25 and TEST-26. Tier 1 has an "Acronym key" section — roughly 3,000 words below the opening paragraph that uses MOND, RAR, BTFR, EFE, DESI, SPARC, DM. Tier 1 also shows the append-fix pattern in status labels: TEST-04a is printed "Kill Criterion Triggered" and a 2026-07-14 note underneath says the criterion was not met as registered; TEST-10's lead "69% of SPARC exceeds ceiling" stands with a note that the lead is wrong. The status label is the thing a scanner reads; the correction is the thing they skip. Tier 1 also uses "Empty Intersection" as a status word that appears in no taxonomy.

**MRH page.** Defines Markov Relevancy Horizon formally and has a plain build-up before the box. Badge: "Speculative — Theoretical Framework." It never mentions that "Active-MRH" is also the name of a badge family used across the site. A reader who sees "Active-MRH" on a test card, looks up MRH, and finds a physics concept about degrees of freedom has no way to connect the two. Overloading the site's central acronym as an inventory-status prefix is the single terminology decision I'd push back on hardest.

**Tools index.** I fetched both `/tools` and `/interactive-tools`; both serve the same "Interactive Tools" page with breadcrumb *Home / Interactive Tools*, no canonical or redirect notice. Verbatim card text: card 1 reads "Coherence Explorer" — I could not reproduce Pass 1's "Coherism" typo. Each card carries two tags: a *kind* (Model Explainer / Reparametrization / Speculative) and a *level*. The legend says "Level tags use one scale — Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced — plus **Artifact Lesson**." So the level scale has a fourth value that isn't a level: γ Calculator, Phase Boundary Visualizer, and Consciousness Demo have no stated difficulty because their level slot is used for a status. Those two axes need to be three, or Artifact Lesson needs to move to the kind slot (it's already defined as a kind in the Honest Assessment "operational states" list — so the site itself files it two different ways).

**Terminology drift on ρ_crit.** Landing: "saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)." Glossary: "Reference Density (Saturation Knee)." Equation Anatomy: "characteristic scale parameter," then "not the midpoint of C." Three pages, three names, and the symbol still says *crit*. Every page spends a sentence un-saying the subscript. Similarly, "coherence" gets a standing disclaimer ("not the physicist's quantum coherence"; the glossary adds that "Classical" is used to mean the *opposite* of standard usage) on every page that uses it. When a term needs a disclaimer at every occurrence, the term is wrong, not the reader.

**γ overloading.** Honest Assessment states it plainly: the same symbol γ is used for the sharpness parameter (γ = 2/√N_corr), for the fitted RAR exponent (γ ≈ 0.49), and — in the Quantum Arc section — for a cosmological growth index, and notes the collision was fixed in the archive (Session #615) but "the site itself retains the collision." A site that knows it has a symbol collision and documents it rather than fixing it has made a documentation decision I'd reverse.

### Tools Tested
- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — worked (Step 1 only; the stepper is client-side and I could not advance it through fetch) — intuitive — Symbol table before Step 1 is exactly the right pattern: C, ρ, ρ_crit, γ, tanh, ln all defined up front. Weaknesses: no stated learning objective for the six steps, "Step 1 of 6" rendered where every other tool shows its tag, and the red "known sign inversion" box appears before the reader has seen the equation it inverts.

*TEST-26 dark-energy no-go (Top 5 page).* This is the only live forward-looking kill-or-tie I found: the sector forces w_a ∈ [+0.23, +0.60] and "0/192 γ values reach the DESI quadrant." Two problems. First, DESI DR2 BAO + CMB + SNe already prefers w_a < 0 at 2.8–4.2σ depending on the SN sample — a positive-w_a model is already disfavored by the same combination; waiting for DR3 to "register prospectively" is theater unless the DR2 posterior is stated. Second, comparing to a CPL quadrant is not a test; the framework's w(a) should be fit directly to the DR2 likelihood. And since the site elsewhere says w runs from −2γ to −1 with γ ≈ 0.49 from SPARC, w₀ ≈ −0.98 — ΛCDM-degenerate. So TEST-26 is a tie by construction unless γ_cosmo ≠ γ_galaxy, and the site never says whether γ is shared across sectors.

*EFE = 0.* Correctly identified as the Strong Equivalence Principle, refutation-only, ΛCDM-degenerate. Missed connection: the Gaia DR3 wide-binary debate (Chae 2023 vs Banik et al. 2024) *is* an EFE test — EFE-free MOND predicts a much larger wide-binary boost than MOND-with-EFE. The site has a wide-binary test (TEST-02) and an EFE discussion on separate pages and never connects them.

*Quantum sector.* Measurement Without Observers is einselection with "MRH crossing" in place of "pointer basis," and the page's own comparison table ("Observer irrelevant; any macroscopic coupling suffices") is Zurek 1991 verbatim in spirit. The two "post-dictions" on Quantum Predictions — T₂ ∝ 1/(1−c) for correlated dephasing and cos²(πd/λ₀) revival — are standard open-systems results, and the page says so. Nothing here differs from decoherence theory. The six "untested protocols" (EEG phase locking $150K/12 mo, circadian γ $50K, minimal cell $200–500K…) are listed with budgets and timelines while the same site says "no protocol maps any observable to C" — a costed, scheduled experiment that cannot be run is not a protocol; it's a placeholder wearing a budget.

*LIV artifact.* The 10⁻² one-loop dim-4 estimate vs 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰ bounds is the Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia & Vucetich (2004) argument; it applies to any discrete substrate and is not framework-specific. It is correctly labeled a naturalness gap, but the source is uncited on the page I read.

*CHSH artifact.* A simulated local substrate gives S ≤ 2. That is Bell's theorem, and the site says so ("a corollary"). It is nevertheless counted as one of "6 refutations executed on external data (…laboratory)." A theorem corollary demonstrated in simulation is not an executed test on external data; counting it that way inflates the ledger by one.

**A2ACW — is the methodology sound?** The protocol page is unusually candid: sensitivity 6/6, specificity 0/6 (COBE, Higgs, GW all flagged as reparametrizations), Youden's J = 0. The site draws the right conclusion — the detector cannot discriminate. What it does not do is close the loop on what *would* make the null result publishable:
- No control arm. The 1.4% "internal-consistency survival" has no comparison rate from a deliberately wrong toy framework run through the same protocol. Without it, 1.4% is uninterpretable.
- The denominator is 3,308 *sessions*, not claims; sessions are not the unit of hypothesis generation.
- The temporal-asymmetry control (0/6 caught retrospectively) tests prior-art *detection*; the interesting question is prior-art-free *generation*, which can be probed with a temporal holdout (models with a cutoff before a known post-cutoff result, asked to derive it). This is the one experiment that could separate "the corpus has no novelty" from "the framework has no novelty," and the page says the degeneracy is unresolved without proposing it.
- "Domain-expert audit" — human or model? Same vendor as the generators? For Researchers lists "Pending Cross-Vendor Control"; the A2ACW page does not mention it.
- The CCH health score (0.25/0.25/0.30/0.20 weights, 0.70/0.50/0.30 cutoffs) is admitted to have "no empirical validation." Fine — then don't call the quantity a metric.
Verdict on soundness: as an audit protocol it is honest and reasonably specified; as a *discovery* methodology it is a null with an inadequate control, and the site knows it.

### Verdict
There is nothing here that differs observationally from MOND + EFE + ΛCDM, and the site says so — the honesty is real and the self-audit is more rigorous than most modified-gravity phenomenology I referee. But it audited itself against the wrong literature: the galaxy sector is Refracted Gravity with the floor pinned to Ω_m, a published theory with covariant completion and rotation-curve fits that use exactly the local-density coupling the site calls a no-go, and that comparison is missing from every page. The one parameter-free prediction the program ever made (boost floor = Ω_m) was tested and killed by TEST-10, which is a better story than "0 confirmed." The A2ACW null is publishable as a negative result only with a control arm it does not have. Fix the RG citation, the TEST-04a data point, and the count inconsistencies, and I would cite the For Researchers page; as it stands I would cite nothing.
