# Topic: Wide Binary Literature Dispute — Does TEST-02 Have a Position?

**Seeded:** 2026-05-04 (maintainer session)
**Priority:** HIGH
**Trigger:** P4 researcher flags that Synchronism's strongest discriminating prediction (TEST-02) is staked on a literature dispute (Chae 2023 vs Banik & Pittordis 2023) without the framework taking a position

## The Problem

TEST-02 (wide binary density dependence) is the cleanest live discriminator between Synchronism and MOND. The prediction: wide binary anomaly should depend on local stellar density (stronger in denser stellar fields). MOND predicts density-independent anomaly.

To test this prediction, the wide binary anomaly must exist in the first place. But there's an active dispute:

- **Chae (2023, 2024, 2025)**: reports a +20% enhancement over Newtonian gravity for wide binaries with separations > 2 kAU. Consistent with a modified gravity signal.
- **Banik & Pittordis (2023)**: Using a different methodology and cuts on Gaia DR3, reports null — no significant anomaly.
- **Hernandez et al. (2024)**: Reports positive detection under specific kinematic cuts.

These papers cannot all be right about the same phenomenon. The dispute is methodological: which cuts are correct? Which sample selection criteria are valid?

The researcher's observation: "Synchronism is staking its strongest discriminating prediction on a phenomenon whose existence is still being fought over, with no apparent position on the methodology dispute. Field-level: this test could fire at any moment as a Synchronism win or loss depending on whose Gaia DR3 cuts you use, which means the test as stated cannot discriminate."

## The Research Questions

1. **Where does the methodological dispute actually lie?** The key differences between Chae and Banik & Pittordis are: (a) sample selection (sky coverage, proper motion cuts, contamination from unresolved binaries or projected pairs), (b) velocity excess statistic used, (c) treatment of orbital phases and inclinations. Which of these drives the disagreement?

2. **Does the wide binary anomaly itself have a consensus status as of mid-2026?** Is there a review paper, a systematic analysis, or a large collaboration result that settles the question at the level of "anomaly exists at 3σ" or "anomaly ruled out at 2σ"?

3. **What would Synchronism's TEST-02 prediction look like in each methodology?** If the anomaly is real (Chae): do Synchronism's density-dependence predictions differ from MOND in a way that's detectable? If Banik is right and there's no anomaly: does Synchronism predict a null too (making both frameworks consistent with null)?

4. **Should TEST-02 be split into two sub-tests?** TEST-02a: does the wide binary anomaly exist? (Tests both Synchronism and MOND equally.) TEST-02b: if the anomaly exists, does it depend on local density? (Tests Synchronism vs MOND specifically.) The framework should state which of these it's claiming and be explicit that TEST-02b is conditional on TEST-02a.

## Why This Matters

If the framework claims TEST-02 as its strongest discriminator but can't discriminate between results depending on methodology, then the test has no fixed meaning. The researcher is right that "Anomaly independent of local stellar density" as a kill criterion is ambiguous if the anomaly itself is disputed — because both "anomaly exists, density-independent" and "anomaly does not exist" would satisfy the kill criterion, making it uninterpretable.

## Proposed Output

A brief position statement (publishable or posted to the site) stating:
- The framework's take on the Chae vs. Banik methodology dispute (which cuts are more reliable and why)
- Whether TEST-02 is conditional on anomaly existence (TEST-02a) or truly independent of it
- What the updated kill criterion should be under each scenario

This is tractable: the Gaia DR3 data is public, and the methodological papers are available. An explorer session could map the dispute and propose a justified methodology preference.
