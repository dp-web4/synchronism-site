## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, start-here, learning-paths, first-encounter, core-idea, tools, coherence-explorer, galaxy-plotter, consciousness-demo, honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Wonderfully honest and inviting at the front door, but the "no equations" beginner path gets steep by step 3, and the site is more "science autopsy" than the pop-science ride the headline promises.

### Journey
The landing page hooked me immediately — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of question I click on. The marching-band-vs-plaza-crowd analogy made "coherence" instantly graspable. I also noticed right away the site says "Zero confirmed novel predictions" on its own front page, which is... unusual? Kind of respectable, actually.

I clicked "Start Here" from the nav and landed on what said it was step 2 of a 6-step journey — slightly disorienting, like walking into a movie a scene late. I backed up to Learning Paths, found the Beginner Track, and did First Encounter, which was genuinely great: no equations, "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" made MRH make sense, and "Everything outside the bubble can be ignored" is a nice sticky phrase.

Then the Core Idea page. The dimmer-switch analogy was good, but suddenly I'm reading about the "Ising model," "Landau-universality," and "central-limit-theorem scaling" with no explanation. The landing page promised the beginner path had no equations, and here's the formula plus grad-school name-drops. I skimmed hard here.

The Coherence Explorer tool was fun — I get what the sliders do (γ = "how quickly the snap happens," like a dimmer). But side notes like "+1 regulator inside the ln breaks sigmoid symmetry" and sudden mentions of BCS superconductors lost me completely. The Galaxy Plotter was my favorite: "galaxies should rotate more slowly at their outer edges... They don't" is a perfect puzzle setup, and the "Plain verdict for casual readers: these curves look great but don't prove the idea" line is exactly the kind of translation I need. The stuff around it (ΔBIC, reparametrization) I just skipped.

I clicked the Consciousness Demo because, well, consciousness. It shows eight methods converging on a magic number and then tells you the convergence is "a geometric artifact, not an empirical finding" — a trap diagram that teaches you to spot traps. Honestly the coolest page on the site, though if I'd been less careful I might have missed the "Empirically refuted (2026)" banner in my excitement.

I ended on the Honest Assessment and bounced fast. It's a huge wall of researcher-speak with no plain-language summary at the top. I got the headline ("this didn't work") but not the why.

### Journey
I audited methodically: landing → glossary → entry path → learning paths → the badge reference → key claims → two tools.

The landing page is well-structured — three audience-segmented entry points, a six-step "Where to Start" sequence, and honest framing. But it uses validation badges (Reparametrization, Speculative, Untested, Failed) with only one inline gloss, deferring definitions to the Honest Assessment page. Acceptable pattern *if* every badge is a link — worth verifying they all are.

Then the first structural contradiction. The header's "Start Here" points to `/why-synchronism`, which correctly announces "Beginner Path — Step 1 of 6" with a breadcrumb (Home / Getting Started / Why Synchronism?). Second contradiction: the landing page's six-step sequence is **Why → First Encounter → Core Idea → Test Catalog → Research Philosophy → Honest Assessment**, but the Learning Paths Beginner track is **Why → First Encounter → Core Idea → What Synchronism Is Not → Honest Assessment → Glossary**. They diverge at step 4. Both are presented as "the" beginner path. A reader following one and cross-referencing the other will believe they've skipped content.

The Glossary is the best page on the site from a documentation standpoint: ~65 terms including every validation badge, every dataset acronym, Greek symbols, with "Learn more →" links throughout. Two issues: badge terms cluster together rather than alphabetically (the header promises "listed alphabetically"), and *both* "Validated" and "Strongly Supported" appear as deprecated with near-verbatim deprecation notes — neither tells the reader which current badge replaced it.

Honest Assessment does contain the canonical "Validation badge definitions" section, and Key Claims links to it explicitly with anchor — the taxonomy chain works. But the Honest Assessment page itself is ~15 sections, 8–10k words, with no table of contents and no executive summary, and uses RAR, ΔBIC, MOND, SPARC, BTFR before defining them, despite a glossary one click away. Nothing on these pages links terms to glossary entries at first use — the glossary exists but the content doesn't lean on it.

Terminology: the site knows "coherence" collides with quantum phase coherence — the same ⚠ physicist warning appears in three separate glossary entries — but the fix is repetition rather than resolution. Worse, Key Claims uses C = f(γ, D, S) for consciousness and only a footnote admits it's "a distinct, undefined construct — not the same function as the physics C(ρ)." Reusing the letter C for two admittedly different functions, disclosed in a footnote, is a genuine documentation defect.

Tool layer: the Tools page mixes two label taxonomies in the same visual slot — "Model Explainer" (a tool *type*) sits next to "Reparametrization" and "Speculative" (validation *badges*). A reader who just learned the badge taxonomy will look for "Model Explainer" in it and not find it. Slugs are inconsistent: the tool named "Equation Anatomy" lives at `/equation-walkthrough`, and the galaxy tool answers at both `/galaxy-plotter` and `/galaxy-curve-plotter` with different pages linking to each.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary

- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The site has already done the demolition a referee would do — every reparametrization I went hunting for was pre-flagged by the site itself — but the phase-boundary tool ships a physical inversion (most-correlated systems get the *flattest* transitions) without stating it, and the flagship honesty page never confronts that "coherence" here means nearly the opposite of what a physicist reads.

### Journey
A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," so I skipped the on-ramp and went straight for the load-bearing pages: parameter derivations, then the MOND story, then the audit trail.

**Parameter derivations.** I expected the usual grand-framework move — dress a fit as a derivation — and instead found the page dismantling itself. γ = 2/√Ncorr: the page states outright that invoking the central limit theorem for *correlated* degrees of freedom is self-contradictory (it is — CLT needs independence, and Ncorr by definition counts non-independent modes), and that the factor of 2 from "6D phase space contracted to 3" doesn't survive actually integrating out momenta. ρ_crit = A·V²: marked "Audited-Negative — Chain-of-Custody Failure," with the admission that the only computation producing A ≈ 0.029 assumed ρ_crit ∝ V^0.5 while the framework asserts V², and that the stated formula gives 4.6×10⁻⁵ — off by 600×. The a₀ section I checked by hand: cH₀/(2π) with H₀ ≈ 70 km/s/Mpc gives 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², the page says exactly that, quotes Milgrom's 1.20×10⁻¹⁰ as a 10% miss, and files the whole thing under "Dimensional Analysis" noting McCulloch/Verlinde/Smolin got the same coincidence. The arithmetic is right and the badge is right. The page ends with "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations." I have never seen a framework site write that sentence about itself.

**Core idea / mean-field story.** The tanh is *motivated* by Ising m = tanh(βJzm) and the page immediately concedes C(ρ) is not a self-consistency equation — density feeds forward, nothing feeds back, so the analogy is decorative. It also volunteers that Landau critical exponents come out wrong by ~2×. Correct concessions, both. What the site calls C(ρ) is a sigmoid in log-density — a compander, and the parameter page uses exactly that word. Once you see "compander," the whole "phase transition" vocabulary on nearby pages reads as loose, but at least the load-bearing page is precise.

**Galaxy rotation / MOND.** This is where grand frameworks usually die quietly; here the death is published. The RAR function displayed is McGaugh et al. 2016 verbatim, and the page says so — "Synchronism's specific contribution is not the function itself." The transition-shape test is reported with the numbers a skeptic wants: γ=2 refuted at ΔBIC = +184; γ freed converges to 0.49 and lands exactly on MOND with ΔBIC = +7 (pure parameter penalty). Conclusion stated on-site: no γ makes the compander both distinct from MOND and consistent with data. The environment-scatter prediction tripped its own pre-registered kill criterion (R² = 0.14 vs required ≥ 0.20). And the wide-binary discriminator is conceded to collapse against MOND+EFE. As a MOND-literate reader I came ready to write "this is MOND in a costume" — the site beat me to it, with a Reparametrization badge on the plotter itself.

**Honest assessment / for-researchers.** The Lorentz-violation treatment is more sophisticated than I expected: tree-level c_μν = 0 by substrate universality, but the one-loop radiative correction ~α/π ~ 10⁻² against cavity/comagnetometer bounds of 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰ — a 16–28 order-of-magnitude fine-tuning gap, framed as a *naturalness problem with unexhibited escapes*, not a refutation. That's the correct epistemic category (same kind as the electroweak hierarchy problem) and most critics wouldn't grant the framework that nuance; the site applies it to itself. DESI: the 2.4σ tension is pinned on amplitude (σ₈ ≈ 0.76 predicted vs 0.841 ± 0.034), with the single-bin "enhancement" explicitly demoted to non-load-bearing. Consciousness Claim 2 is marked "unfalsifiable as stated" because nothing maps any measurement onto the C-axis — the right verdict, and stronger than "speculative."

**Quantum pages.** "MRH crossing is collapse" is standard decoherence with a renamed boundary; the page's own text separates *when* collapse occurs from *what probabilities* outcomes carry and admits the Born rule is assumed, with the coherence-conservation argument placed at "the same logical status as Zurek's envariance" — i.e., circular. Zero of the six designed protocols have been run, and the page says so.

Then I stress-tested two tools, and found the one real physics problem of the visit (see Tools Tested).

### Tools Tested
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — worked — intuitive UI, physically misleading content — Placing ideal gases and galaxies both at γ = 2 is honestly flagged on-page as a counting artifact, which is good. But the tool's whole premise — position systems by transition sharpness — inherits the γ = 2/√Ncorr inversion silently: it tells a condensed-matter student that BCS superconductors have the smoothest quantum-classical transition, which is backwards, and the quoted BCS value doesn't even fit on the axis. This is the one place I found where the site's physics representation is wrong-by-omission rather than pre-audited.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — worked — honest to a fault — 23 hand-picked rows from 1,703 phenomena with Pearson r up to 0.982, and directly beneath: selection inflates effect sizes, rows aren't independent, and a 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z matches or beats the correlations (|Δr| ≤ 0.07). That null-model disclosure is textbook. The correlations are, by the site's own analysis, density-monotonicity dressed up — and the site says so. Only gap: γ undefined on-page.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Given free-γ fits converge to MOND and C has no measurement protocol, is there *any* observable, even in principle, that distinguishes C(ρ) from an interpolating function? The local-density no-go suggests the answer at galaxy scale is no — does the framework accept that, or is there a proposed non-local extension?
2. Is there a candidate scale-invariant recipe for Ncorr? Every use I found either asserts it (BCS = 10⁷) or back-solves it from a fitted γ (galaxies: 0.49 → 17). Without a counting rule, γ = 2/√Ncorr absorbs any value.
3. Which way should sharpness actually go? If the framework bit the bullet and said γ *increases* with correlation, would anything else break? Nobody on the site seems to have run that.
4. The naturalness-gap framing names two perturbative escape routes — is anyone attempting to exhibit one for this substrate class, or is that permanently parked?

### Verdict
As physics, there is nothing here that survives as novel — and the remarkable thing is that the site itself is the source for that verdict: zero derived parameters, convergence to MOND under free fits, an unmeasurable central variable, and a preferred frame with a 16–28 order-of-magnitude naturalness gap are all stated on-site with correct statistics and, as far as I checked, correct arithmetic. Every trap I set for hidden reparametrization was already sprung and labeled. What remains genuinely wrong is narrower but real: the phase-boundary tool presents the γ–Ncorr inversion to exactly the audience (condensed-matter students) who will be actively misled by it, and the honest-assessment page audits everything except the name of its own central variable. Fix those two and this becomes something I'd assign to a first-year as a case study in what self-auditing looks like — not because the framework works, but because the documentation of *why it doesn't* is better than most published null results.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, for-researchers, test-roadmap, a2acw, tier-1-existing, honest-assessment, tools, consciousness-demo, galaxy-plotter, key-claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: There is no novel physics here and the site knows it — what it has instead is three genuinely citable *negative* results and a referee-quality self-audit; the residual problems are that the flagship DESI refutation is framed inconsistently across pages, and the one remaining overclaim-shaped content (the "47 research contributions") sits un-badged.

### Journey
I came to answer three questions: what's actually new, does anything discriminate from MOND+ΛCDM, and is the AI methodology sound. I skipped the pedagogy entirely.

**What's actually new: nothing in the physics, three things in the metadata.** The for-researchers page is the most self-aware framework page I've refereed. It states the current tally outright — "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, 0 tests currently discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" — and correctly attributes its own core obstruction to prior art: "The core obstruction is *not* ours. Milgrom showed MOND-as-modified-inertia must be strongly non-local." What it claims as contributions are three negative results: (a) a *quantified* local-density no-go — density-keyed modifications fail via three independent routes (ΔBIC=+184 ensemble rejection, ~1.7 dex offset, cluster miscalibration by 10⁴–10⁶×); (b) the DESI amplitude kill, which the site correctly generalizes to the entire growth-suppression mechanism class, not just its own instance; (c) the A2ACW program-level null. I checked the load-bearing citations: Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Gambini–Pullin PRL 93, 191301 (2004); Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov PRL 94, 081601 (2005); Pospelov & Shang PRD 85, 105001 (2012); McGaugh et al. 2016 for the RAR function — all real, all correctly characterized. The LHAASO quadratic LIV bound (E_QG,2 > 6×10⁻⁸ E_Pl) is the right number. The a₀ = cH₀/2π arithmetic is right and correctly filed as dimensional analysis with the McCulloch/Verlinde/Smolin coincidence noted. This level of citation hygiene is rare even in refereed papers.

**Does anything discriminate: no — and the one prediction that genuinely differs is a liability.** The test catalog is honest: 24 proposed tests, 0 discriminating. TEST-02 (Gaia wide binaries) is the cleanest illustration of self-elimination: the framework predicts a *Newtonian null* (0.05–0.4% deviation) where MOND predicts ~18%, so confirming the null confirms Newton equally and detecting the anomaly confirms MOND — no outcome selects Synchronism, and the signal is quoted as far below DR3 systematics anyway. The only place the framework makes a prediction that actually differs from everything else is the dim-4 preferred-frame sector — and there it predicts c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻² against bounds of 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰, a 16–28 order-of-magnitude naturalness gap. The site's framing of this as a naturalness problem with two named-but-unexhibited perturbative escapes (SUSY; Hořava-class scale hierarchy) rather than a refutation is the correct epistemic category, and frankly more nuance than most critics would grant it. So the answer to "does any prediction differ from MOND+ΛCDM?" is: yes, exactly one sector differs — in the direction of being 16–28 OOM excluded absent an escape nobody has constructed for this substrate class.

**But I caught the site disagreeing with itself on its flagship refutation.** On /for-researchers and the landing page, TEST-04a (DESI fσ₈) is killed on *amplitude*: predicted σ₈ ≈ 0.76 vs observed 0.841 ± 0.034, "2.4σ disfavored." On /tier-1-existing, the same test is reported as "observed fσ₈ ≈ 0.55 (enhancement, not suppression)... disfavored ~2σ," and /honest-assessment still carries "post-hoc retrodiction — wrong direction." These are different statistics and different sigmas for the same kill. As someone who works with the DR1 full-shape data: the z≈0.51 LRG bin's fσ₈ excess is a single-bin fluctuation that the DESI collaboration itself does not treat as robust; the ensemble amplitude is the defensible statistic. Pinning the kill on "wrong direction" is fragile — if DR2 (growth results expected ~Spring 2027) pulls that bin down toward ΛCDM, the "enhancement" framing evaporates while the amplitude tension may well persist. The site has the right analysis on one page and the outdated one on two others. For a site whose entire value proposition is audit integrity, the flagship refutation must be framed identically everywhere.

**Is A2ACW sound: as a discovery methodology, no — and the site's own null is the interesting result.** The protocol (PRIMARY/CHALLENGER/OBSERVER/COORDINATOR roles, 3,308 sessions) is disclosed as assembled prior art. The audit outcome: 6/6 demoted claims caught, but 0/6 genuine physics discoveries passed — every established result fed in was flagged as a potential reparametrization. The site's own conclusion — "same-corpus adversarial AI pairs filter for internal consistency but cannot generate or detect novelty... a retrieval aid, not a detector" — is the correct reading, and as a program-level null about in-distribution AI self-play it is genuinely citable in the AI-for-science literature.
