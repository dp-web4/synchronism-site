## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /interactive-tools)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've seen. It told me in the first screen that its big idea didn't work, and I'm still not sure why I should keep reading.

### Journey
I came from a Reddit link expecting a "theory of everything" pitch. The headline is a question ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"), but the subheadline and first paragraph walk it back right away: "Not a Theory of Everything," the equation "did not hold up as stated," the work was done by AI agents, and no outside physicist has reviewed it. That honesty is disarming, and it made me trust the site more. It also left me asking: if it already failed, what is there for me to learn? The landing page names a lot of things I don't recognise (ΛCDM, ΔBIC, SPARC, CHSH, A2ACW, Youden's J, fσ₈). I skimmed past them and clicked the big "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)" button.

**/why-synchronism** was the best page for me. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a hook I get from PBS Space Time. The crowd-in-a-plaza versus marching-band analogy landed right away, and so did the dimmer switch. Then the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` arrives. I don't know what tanh or ln are, but the dimmer-switch picture let me skip the symbols and keep the meaning. After that the page quickly turns into a list of failures: melting points 53% off, a superconductor predicted at 607 K against a real 93 K, and "0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations." Terms like YBCO, Abrikosov–Gor'kov, Hamiltonian, compander and "audited-negative" went over my head. The "8 min" promise held roughly, though the page itself doesn't say how long it takes.

**/first-encounter** has a nice stepped structure (A–G, a Continue button, a progress bar), and it's the first page that felt like a lesson rather than an audit. "Presence" and the "atom doesn't feel Andromeda" line (MRH) worked for me. Section F, "No observer needed. No consciousness required.", is the most exciting sentence on the site for a YouTube-physics person, because it promises to settle the measurement problem. Then the page immediately says it's "an interpretation, not a testable prediction" and conflicts with the "quantum Zeno effect", which I've never heard of. I felt teased. Section C says the fitted γ ≈ 0.5 "matches MOND by construction." I learned MOND is a rival to dark matter, but I couldn't tell whether "by construction" means cheating or just "the same thing."

**Tools.** The Coherence Explorer is clean: two sliders and a curve, and the blurb says plainly that it "does not model any real material, and the density axis has no units." The Galaxy Curve Plotter was the moment I actually understood the dark-matter problem. "A rotation curve is just orbital speed plotted against distance from the centre", the outer stars should slow down, and they don't. That one sentence was worth the visit. The legend lost me, though: "Synchronism, division reading (failed): g = gbar/C, γ=2, with a flat 3.17× boost on this disk," "Same Cρ, quadrature reading (untested display): inert at Cρ ≈ 0.001." I couldn't tell which line was the theory I came to see. The γ Calculator calls itself "a live demonstration of the formula's central defect." It's odd to be offered a calculator whose stated purpose is to show that the calculator is wrong.

**/honest-assessment**: the "If you read nothing else" box is perfect for me: one equation, lost every test, "6 refutations, 0 predictions confirmed," and where it fits galaxies "it is MOND in different notation." I stopped there. The page is enormous (my fetch said ~158k characters), with section after section of ledgers and acronyms.

**/glossary** answered my main question (tanh = "smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1"; MOND defined plainly). I found it only by going looking, though; the pages don't link those terms to it inline where I needed them. **/interactive-tools** usefully says "Where to start: Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment."

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (default γ=0.49, ρcrit=1.00 gives C(ρcrit)=0.3272, C(10ρcrit)=0.8259, C(100ρcrit)=0.9785). Intuitive: two sliders, four presets with plain labels. I noticed the honest note that the x-axis "has no units," which made me wonder what the curve is *of*, but at least it doesn't pretend.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked (5 galaxies, DDO 154 default, Vflat 47 km/s). Confusing legend, but the best explanation of the dark-matter problem on the site. The checkbox "Show the old hand-drawn stand-in … kept for the record" is a nice honesty touch, though I didn't know why I'd want it.
- **γ Calculator**: worked (Ncorr=4 → γ=1.0000, "Boundary"; presets from ideal gas γ=2 to BCS 6.32e-4). The idea of "particles moving together" was understandable. I didn't understand why I'd use a calculator built to show its formula is wrong until I read the caveat about the inverted direction.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the main equation failed everywhere it was tested, what is the site *for* now: a lesson in how science works, or is there still a live idea being tested? The landing page hints at "open questions," but I couldn't find the one live bet a non-expert could root for.
2. Is the "no observer needed" view of quantum measurement still on the table, or is it dead too?
3. What does it mean that AI agents did the research? Who are they, and should I trust their audit of themselves?
4. Why 14,610 galaxies on one page while the database in the glossary (SPARC) has 175?
5. What is "coherence" a measure *of*, in a concrete example I could picture (a glass of water? a star?), given that the explorer's axis has no units?

### Verdict
As a Reddit drive-by, I came for a bold claim and found an unusually candid lab notebook. The honesty earned my respect, and the galaxy rotation-curve explanation taught me something real. Past the first paragraph of most pages, though, the language turns into auditor shorthand. The site never told me what is still alive and worth getting excited about, so I'd bookmark it as "interesting failure," not come back for updates.

Cards also mix a badge with a descriptor ("Failed — Mechanism Under Revision"), so a reader can't tell which part is the controlled vocabulary.

**/learning-paths** is the strongest page structurally. It has seven paths (three by level, four by topic), each with ordered steps, a step count and a time estimate, and the Beginner path has per-step times that add up (8+10+6+9+4 = 37 min, as stated). Intermediate, Advanced and all four topic tracks give only a total, with no per-step times. Step labels carry status annotations ("Decisive Tests: Status (none active)", "Test Roadmap (26 tests: 24 in the original registry + 2 added later)"), which makes the nav labels read like changelog entries. There's a typo in the intro: "Separately,1 of the 7 paths" (missing space).

**/coherence-function** shows that path navigation works. It also shows a separate "Next" for the Physics Track. Path-aware next links are a feature I rarely see done well. One problem: the "Core Theory" breadcrumb level doesn't correspond to anything in the top nav or on /learning-paths, so the breadcrumb hierarchy doesn't match the site's visible hierarchy. ### Tools Tested
- **Equation Anatomy** (/equation-walkthrough): works. Previous/Next stepper, "1 / 6" counter, starts at Step 1. Mostly intuitive. Clear touch: "Step 1 of 6 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)" prevents confusion with the Learning Path step counters. A "Read this before Step 1" warning sits up front. ### Unanswered Questions
1. Which page is the single source of truth for badge definitions: /glossary or /honest-assessment ("canonical")? If both, what keeps them in sync?
2. Are "Core Theory" and "Predictions & Experiments" real section pages? The breadcrumbs imply a hierarchy that the top nav doesn't show.
3. Is there a style rule for first-use acronym expansion? Coverage varies a lot from page to page, which suggests none is enforced.
4. Is ρ "presence" or "density"? Two pages give two different primary definitions.
5. How many label vocabularies exist? I count badges (9), retired badges (2), operational states, lifecycle verbs, descriptors, content groupings (Model Explainer, Artifact Lesson), tiers (cost), readiness labels, and two test-ID schemes (TEST-nn, B1–B7). Is there one page that maps them all?

### Verdict
The scaffolding here is better than on most documentation sites I audit: path-aware prev/next, honest rename notes, a typed glossary, and revision notes that admit past errors in place. Most of the friction is a vocabulary that has grown faster than its governance. There are at least six overlapping label systems, acronyms are expanded on some pages and not others, and the glossary's own ΔBIC entry still cites a figure it withdraws two sentences earlier. Two mechanical fixes would remove most of this: a first-use `<Term>` auto-linker and a lint that flags withdrawn figures.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /galaxy-rotation, /coherence-function, /measurement-without-observers, /dark-energy, /honest-assessment (refutation ledger + RG passages), /interactive-tools, /galaxy-plotter, /chemistry-correlation-explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes, but as a case study in self-auditing, not for new physics
- **Overall impression**: The algebra I checked is right, and the site's own conclusion ("every sector is a known model at γ = ½") is correct. The one closed-form claim that fails my check is a sentence saying the coherence function is "identical" to Refracted Gravity's permittivity, which is true only at γ = ½.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "an interesting AI project." I skipped the beginner path and went straight to **/parameter-derivations**, expecting the usual "we derived tanh from mean field" overreach. That isn't what I found. The page says tanh is "a phenomenological choice from the sigmoid/compander family." It contrasts it explicitly with the Ising self-consistency m = tanh(βJz·m) ("no such self-consistency loop") and says the form has "no critical point anywhere," so there's no Landau story. That is the right answer, and a better one than most popular "emergent gravity" sites give.

**Checking the math on /parameter-derivations.**
- Closed form: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. Correct, since e^{2γ ln(1+x)} = (1+x)^{2γ}.
- Limits: C ≈ γx at small x, and 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ} at large x. Both correct. A WebFetch summary rendered the second as "2x − 2γ", but the raw HTML has a superscript, so that's a rendering artefact and not an error.
- γ = ½ gives x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), i.e. MOND's simple μ with a₀ → 2a₀, exactly and for all x. Verified.
- A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²) with β_J = 1, R₀ = 8 kpc, G = 4.30×10⁻³ pc (km/s)²/M☉: I get 4.57×10⁻⁵, which is 635× below 0.029. The page already reports 635× ("a simulated-visitor grad-student persona recomputed 635×"), and a 2026-10-13 note explains that β_J = 4.5 comes from moving R₀ to 0.07 kpc, since only the product β_J·R₀ enters. My predecessor's complaint was absorbed within a day.
- Solar-neighbourhood exclusion: ρ_crit = 0.029 × 229² = 1.52×10³ M☉/pc³. With ρ_local ≈ 0.1, x ≈ 7×10⁻⁵, C ≈ γx ≈ 3×10⁻⁵, so the boost is about 3×10⁴. That matches the quoted ~3.5×10⁴. The page concludes "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." I agree.

**/galaxy-rotation.** The fit form is g_bar = g_obs·tanh(γ ln(1 + g_obs/a₀′)), which is a μ-function keyed on g_obs, i.e. ordinary MOND. The free-γ SPARC fit lands at 0.489. I checked the reported ΔBIC = +7.1 with Δχ² = −0.84: ln(2807) = 7.94, and 7.94 − 0.84 = 7.1. Consistent; the extra parameter buys nothing. The page also notes that a₀′ moves 3.6× across Υ_disk while the identified combination stays at 1.07–1.09×10⁻¹⁰. That is correct: the deep limit gives g_obs = √(g_bar·a₀′/γ), so a₀′/γ is what the data constrain. cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 is 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ (I get 1.042). The page labels this Milgrom's 1983 coincidence, not a derivation, which is right. One more identity checks out: keying the γ = ½ form on g_bar gives g_obs = g_bar + 2a₀′, a constant offset. Badges: "Failed" for the density form, "Reparametrization" for the acceleration form. Both are honest.

**/dark-energy** is where I expected a hidden ΛCDM, and the page found it first. With G_eff = G/C(ρ̄), ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C. At γ = ½ this is ρ_m·2ρ_crit/ρ_m = 2ρ_crit, an exact constant, so the model is exactly Λ for all time. In general, w runs from −2γ early (from 1 − C ∝ x^{−2γ}) to −1 late. I derived both independently and they match the page. The page then makes the point I was about to make: γ_cosmo = 0.487 vs γ_galaxy = 0.489 "agree at 0.1σ", but "γ = ½ is exactly Λ and exactly MOND's simple μ … with no power to fail." That is the correct reading of an agreement that would have been easy to oversell. The sign lock sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) follows from monotone w between −2γ and −1, and it does exclude DESI's preferred quadrant. The page also correctly identifies the family as Cardassian-class (Freese & Lewis 2002), with no new degree of freedom.

**/coherence-function → Refracted Gravity.** The page says density-keyed form (b) "is Refracted Gravity's field equation (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016), with a permittivity identical to C_Ω in closed form." I checked this. RG's permittivity has the shape ε₀ + (1−ε₀)·½[1 + tanh(Q ln(ρ/ρ_c))], and its inner bracket is y^{2Q}/(1 + y^{2Q}). The framework's C is [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. These coincide only at γ = Q = ½ with ρ_c,RG = 2ρ_crit. At γ = 2 (the registered pin) and x = 1, Synchronism gives 0.882 and RG (Q = 2, y = x/2) gives 0.059. At x = 0.1 the values are 0.19 and 6×10⁻⁶. At small x the first is linear (≈ 2x) and the second quartic. So the sentence is true at γ = ½ and false at the γ the galaxy sector registered. This matters downstream. /honest-assessment tests "Density-keyed, γ = 2, knee at Refracted Gravity's fitted 0.0083 M☉/pc³", which puts RG's knee into the framework's function. That is not RG unless the function is RG's. The same page separately says the solar-system grid included "Refracted Gravity's own form," so the site seems to know the two differ. The "identical" sentence is the defect.

**/measurement-without-observers** is honest at the badge: "Speculative — Interpretive Overlay on Decoherence — No Differing Prediction." The body still presents the chain "N_corr ↑ → γ = 2/√N_corr ↓ → MRH shrinks → … Measurement = MRH boundary crossing" as the mechanism, even though γ = 2/√N_corr is audited-negative and sign-inverted elsewhere. The page does call itself "downstream of a negative audit." The "N_corr ~ 10²³ → no return" argument is standard thermodynamic irreversibility of decoherence and adds nothing new. Two things here are good. The matter-stability bound (Planck-rate diffusion heats H at ~2×10⁻⁴ eV/s) is the right first check before anyone talks about interferometry. And the page correctly concedes that a physical, N_corr-dependent MRH would be collapse-class and would face Donadi+2021 and Fein+2019. The page does not engage with why decoherence alone fails to give definite outcomes (the problem of outcomes), even though it quotes that limitation. "No observer needed" is therefore a restatement of the decoherence programme plus an assertion, not an answer.

**Badge honesty.** I saw no "Validated" or "Supported" badge on any page. All badges were Failed, Reparametrization, Audited-Negative, or Speculative, and each one I checked matched the content. If anything, the site over-labels: it counts the Bell/CHSH "local arm = Bell's theorem" construction check among the 6 refutations. A framework failing to beat Bell's theorem is not really a refutation by external data, and /dark-energy flags that split itself.

### Journey
I came in hunting for one thing: a surviving prediction that differs from both MOND and ΛCDM. The landing page answers that before I could ask: "What would count as a confirmation is a prediction that *differs* from MOND and ΛCDM and wins; no test currently on the books can deliver that." /for-researchers repeats it: "No, within the registered tests and the readings run so far." **Tier 1, card by card (/tier-1-existing).** I read the cards for which ones even *claim* to discriminate. TEST-09 (BTFR slope) and TEST-10 (dwarf DM fraction) discriminate, and MOND wins both, because the framework's bounded boost (B_max) cannot reach the deep-MOND tail. TEST-05 discriminates structurally, since the density lever is undetectable against MOND+EFE's ~0.09 dex. TEST-25 (Cassini × SPARC) is "Failed, inherited from MOND": the site stands behind Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024)'s marginalised 8.7σ rather than its own unmarginalised +17.95σ, and explains the gap. That is the correct choice. TEST-02 (wide binaries) is properly handled. Banik+2024, Pittordis & Sutherland 2023, Chae and Hernandez are all cited, and the DR3 dispute is correctly described as turning on hidden-triple modelling. The density branch is identically null in the wide/close ratio (γ_g ≡ 1, since the ambient boost cancels). The acceleration branch *is* MOND+EFE and is already failed via Cassini. The DR4 analysis ("a Chae-type ≈1.4× boost needs γ ≲ 1, where Cassini fails; a Newtonian null fails every γ ≲ 3; only ≈1.1–1.2× lands on γ ≈ 1.5–2") is the right way to frame DR4 as a one-parameter squeeze rather than a split. One aside: a WebFetch summary of /for-researchers told me Banik is "not mentioned". The raw HTML shows Banik cited 12 times across the Tier 1 and Honest Assessment pages, so that was a fetch artefact, not a site gap.

**Dark energy (/dark-energy).** This is the best page on the site for a cosmologist, and it reaches the right answer for the right reason. Read locally, ρ_DE = f(ρ_m) is a barotropic fluid Cardassian. The adiabatic c_s² ≈ 0.22(2γ−1) enters growth multiplied by (k/aH)² ~ 10⁴–10⁵, so P(k) forces |2γ−1| ≲ 10⁻⁵. The page correctly identifies this as the Sandvik–Tegmark–Zaldarriaga–Waga (2004) / Amarzguioui–Elgarøy–Multamäki (2005) result for the class. My own check: at the DESI best fit γ = 0.487, c_s² ≈ −0.006, which gives a Jeans growth rate of order (k/aH)²|c_s²| ~ 10² per Hubble time², so "~10× ΛCDM power at k = 0.01" is if anything conservative. The page also handles the EFT-of-DE objection I was about to raise. With c_s² = 0, or a rest-frame c_s² = 1 ("silent quartessence", Reis+2003), the pin disappears. The postulate doesn't supply either, so the pin binds the local reading as stated. The DESI DR2 numbers are right: 3.1σ for DESI+CMB and 2.8/3.8/4.2σ with Pantheon+/Union3/DESY5. The sign lock sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) puts the family in the quadrant opposite DESI's preference, with ΛCDM on the boundary. So the sector can only be ΛCDM or worse, and the page says exactly that. The cross-sector "γ_cosmo 0.487 vs γ_gal 0.489, 0.1σ" is correctly downgraded to "no power to fail". Under the local reading, 0.487 is excluded anyway.

**The two candidate contributions.** I looked for anything citable, since that is what a referee asks.
1. *The locality no-go* ("a knee keyed on local volumetric density must fall as V⁻² to track a₀"). The algebra is right: r_t = √(GM/a₀) ∝ V² under the BTFR, so ρ̄(<r_t) ∝ a₀²/(GV²). But it is a one-line corollary of the constant transition *surface* density Σ ~ a₀/G that MOND has always had. The site itself concedes that Joyce, Jain, Khoury & Trodden (2015) give a strictly finer classification by which derivative of Φ keys the modification, and that the classification files viable Vainshtein/Galileon theories under "local density". So the claim "appears to be the first for the algebraic-coupling class" is defensible only as a quantification, and is not a result I'd referee as novel.
2. *The LLR argument for density-keyed G_eff* (the Sun moves through the disc, so G/C(ρ) drifts; Ġ/G ≈ 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁵ yr⁻¹ at 1–10 pc smoothing against Hofmann & Müller 2018's (7.1 ± 7.6)×10⁻¹⁴). I checked the value and it is correct. It is the one argument here I hadn't seen applied to this class, and it generalises beyond this framework. Even at kpc smoothing, the Sun's vertical oscillation through the ~300 pc disc scale height gives |dlnρ/dt| ~ 10⁻⁸ yr⁻¹. So LLR bounds |dlnG_eff/dlnρ| ≲ 10⁻⁵ at the solar position for *any* local-density-keyed G_eff (my estimate, not the site's). That is a model-independent statement worth a literature search and, if it isn't there, a short note. The site presents it only as "excludes 68 of 74 laws" of its own grid, which undersells it.

**Where the ledger hasn't caught up with itself.** The site's best correction is the effective-N one. A galaxy-block bootstrap gives N_eff ≈ 150, so the γ = 2 pin's ΔBIC +184 becomes ≈ +11, which is 1.6σ, or 1.9–2.3σ with nuisances refitted. - The /glossary ΔBIC entry says "+184 … or ≥+33 … Both values far exceed the >10 threshold — the γ=2 form is categorically rejected regardless of which figure is used". Pass 2 flagged the ≥+33. The bigger problem is "categorically rejected", which the galaxy-level figure contradicts.
- A TEST-02 passage calls the acceleration branch "MOND-degenerate (refuted by SPARC RAR at ΔBIC=+184)". That conflates two things: the acceleration-keyed *free-γ* fit is MOND and fits fine (ΔBIC +7.1 vs McGaugh), and +184 refers only to the γ = 2 pin. I couldn't confirm whether this passage is inside a history block. If it is, the history label isn't visible in text extraction.
- The density-keyed ΔBIC +2843 (23 occurrences) is a point-count figure with no galaxy-unit companion anywhere I looked. Dividing by the measured inflation (~2807/150) it would still be ~+150, so the verdict doesn't change, but it should carry the same treatment as +184.

**The V⁻² "exclusion".** /parameter-derivations and /honest-assessment say the measured "Jeans knee" scales as V^(−0.16 ± 0.19) on SPARC, so the framework's V⁺² is excluded at ~11σ, "and MOND's required V⁻² at ~10σ too". Read literally, that says SPARC excludes the density scaling of MOND's own transition at 10σ, on data MOND fits to a few percent. That can't be right as stated. The mean enclosed density at the radius where g_bar = a₀ is 3a₀/(4πG r_t), with r_t ∝ V², so it scales as V⁻² on any sample that obeys the BTFR. The pages themselves say "mean-enclosed and isothermal estimates both give exactly −2". So the measured "Jeans knee" is a different object from the MOND-transition density (the /for-researchers text calls the regression one "of disc surface density on V"). The 10σ excludes *a V⁻² law for that measured knee*, not MOND's requirement. That distinction has to be in the sentence, or a referee will read it as a claimed 10σ anti-MOND result.

**The withdrawn magnitude is still the "citable form".** /parameter-derivations: "The 240×–300,000× magnitude is withdrawn: it used ρ_crit = 0.029·V², but A = 0.028 was derived … for ρ_crit = A·V^0.5." /honest-assessment and /for-researchers then give the "one-line citable form" as "inverted sign, 240×–300,000× magnitude error", with the note "kept here because this is its citable form". A withdrawn figure cannot be the citable form. Cite the measured exponent instead.

**A2ACW (/research-philosophy, /for-researchers).** The methodology section is better than I expected and the framing is honest. It calls the protocol "a self-consistency filter — not a discovery method, and not a calibrated reparametrization detector" and says "the protocol is assembled prior art" (Irving, Christiano & Amodei 2018; CAMEL/MetaGPT). The 0-of-9 result is labelled instrument-uncalibrated, the out-of-distribution arm (post-cutoff, citation-stripped) is "never run", and the cross-vendor arm gates on access. My assessment: as a method for *generating* physics it is unsound, and the site doesn't claim otherwise. As a filter it has a known asymmetric failure mode (shared corpus means shared blind spots), and the site names it. The one citable question it poses, "Does adversarial LLM audit mistake real novelty for prior art?", is a good question and the cheapest thing on the site to actually answer. Seed the protocol with post-cutoff results whose novelty is established, strip the citations, and measure the false-reparametrization rate. That is one afternoon of runs, and it would be the site's first positive *method* result either way. Also, several corrections are credited to a "simulated-visitor researcher persona". That labelling is honest, but those catches are the same model family auditing itself, so they don't count toward the external review the site correctly says it lacks ("No record of an outside physicist reviewing these claims exists").

**/where-its-useful is in a different register from the rest of the site.** Every physics page hedges every claim. This one says "Same concept, production vocabulary — not an analogy", "sits in the equation by name, not by analogy", "This is delivered, demonstrable value", "Both claims are true." "Not an analogy" is a strong identity claim with no test proposed. That software uses a vocabulary shows the vocabulary is usable, not that the physics concept and the design pattern are one thing. It's the one page where I felt sold to.
