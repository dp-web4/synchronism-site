# Topic: Is the LLR bound on density-keyed G_eff already in the literature?

## Question
The site's most portable negative is `/for-researchers#gdot-llr`: any G_eff = G·f(ρ_s), with ρ_s smoothed on 1–10 pc, drifts as the
Sun moves through its neighbourhood. Lunar laser ranging then gives |d ln G_eff/d ln ρ_s| ≲ 1×10⁻⁸–1×10⁻⁷ at 2σ (corrected
2026-11-18 from a central-value quote). Has anyone published this argument, or an equivalent, for environment-dependent couplings?

## Context
A researcher persona (2026-11-18) called it "the most portable result on the site" and asked whether it had been compared with
the chameleon / symmetron / screened-scalar literature, or with Ġ/G-from-environment bounds. The record says no. Every prior-art
identity so far (Famaey–Binney, Cardassian, Refracted Gravity, Collins et al.) arrived by retrieval, never by protocol. This is the
same check on the archive's best outward-facing claim, run *before* anyone cites it.

## What to do
- Search for: Ġ/G from the Sun's motion through the local density; environment-dependent G and LLR; screening models' predictions for
  time variation of G along the solar orbit; Damour/Nordtvedt-type arguments with a spatially varying scalar. Read abstracts verbatim
  and record locators.
- Check whether screening (chameleon or symmetron) escapes the bound by construction, because the field responds to the local
  Newtonian potential rather than the smoothed density. If it does, the bound's scope sentence must say so.
- Re-derive the bound with the 2σ edge, and with a gradient set by the measured local stellar density field (Gaia), rather than an
  e-fold over ℓ.

## Why It Matters
If the argument is new, it is the archive's strongest citable negative, and its scope has to be exact before anyone outside reads it.
If it is prior art, the register must credit it, as it does for the four earlier identities.
