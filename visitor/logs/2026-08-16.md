# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-08-16

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (Home, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Core Idea, Learning Paths, γ Calculator, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Honest Assessment, Glossary, Key Claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The analogies are genuinely great and the honesty is refreshing, but the "beginner" path stops being beginner around step 3 and I never found out why I should care about a framework the site keeps telling me didn't work.

### Journey

I clicked through from a Reddit link. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a great hook. That's exactly the kind of thing I'd click on a Veritasium thumbnail.

Then, about two scrolls later, the site tells me: **0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations, 0 of 6 "validated" claims survived audit.** I actually respect that. I've never seen a site do that. But I also sat there thinking: *so… why am I here?* The page answers its own headline question with "no" before I've had a chance to get interested in the question. Somewhere in the first screen I need a sentence like "even though it failed, here's the interesting part" — the site does eventually say it's "a research tool that maps density to coherence," but that's at the bottom and it's not a reason to keep reading.

The equation is right there on the landing page: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I don't know what tanh is. I know what ln is, vaguely, from a calculator button. Seeing it that early made me think "this isn't for me" — I only kept going because the Beginner Path button was next to it.

**Beginner Path step 1 and 2 were good.** "Why Synchronism?" explained the problem clearly: physics uses different equations at different scales, what if one worked everywhere. **First Encounter was the best page on the site for me.** Marching band vs. random crowd. Crystal vs. thin gas between stars. A single electron in vacuum vs. a neutron star core "10¹⁴× denser than lead." And the MRH thing — "a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy" — I got that instantly. That's a genuinely good explanation of a genuinely weird idea. If the whole site were written like that page I'd have read all of it.

**Step 3, "The Core Idea," is where I fell off.** It starts wonderfully — the dimmer switch, "nothing happens at low density, a small change flips it in the middle, past the knee more density barely matters." That's the clearest tanh explanation I could ask for, and it's exactly what I needed on the landing page. But then within the same page I'm reading about "the Ising model self-consistency equation," "critical exponents (β, ν) are undefined by construction," "abandoning criticality means abandoning universality classes," and an identity showing the equation is "algebraically identical" to a Hill function. I skimmed all of that. I have no idea whether it was good news or bad news for the theory. It reads like the page was written for a beginner and then a physicist came along and stapled their argument to the bottom of each section.

I tried the **Learning Paths** page hoping for a friendlier route and instead got seven paths plus a warning that the difficulty paths and topic tracks "are not equivalent" — one covers 10 pages the other misses and vice versa. So whichever I choose, I'm told upfront I'll miss things. That's more decision than I wanted to make.

I tried two tools (below), then went to **Honest Assessment** because the site keeps pointing there. It's the most-cited page and the least readable one: `ΔBIC = +184`, `r² = 0.0001`, "3.3 standard deviations," "SPARC," "H₀ tension," "Lorentz invariance gaps (16–28 orders of magnitude)." I understood the shape of it — everything they tried, failed — but not a single one of the individual failures. The **Glossary**, on the other hand, was excellent. It defines *everything*, including tanh, ln, and MOND, in language I could follow. I just found it last instead of first.

**Key Claims** was the one page where the honesty broke down for me. Each claim gets several paragraphs of impressive-sounding explanation and *then* a status badge. By the time I hit "Failed" on the dark matter claim I'd already half-absorbed the idea as true. Put the verdict first.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** — worked — confusing — The S-curve graph and the preset buttons (ideal gas, liquid water, ferromagnet, BEC, superconductor) are a great idea; clicking a real substance and seeing where it lands is exactly the kind of thing that makes an abstract idea click. But the whole tool sits under a banner saying its formula is sign-inverted and gives backwards answers for every collective system, plus four numbered warnings. I got the shape of the S-curve, and no confidence at all in the number it printed. It also assumes I can supply "N_corr," which I cannot.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — confusing — This one nearly landed. Picking DDO 154 and seeing the dashed "what gravity predicts" line fall away while the observed dots stay flat is the clearest picture of the dark matter problem I've seen anywhere, including YouTube. Then four more curves and a page of caveats about parametrization buried it. If the default view were two curves and one sentence, I'd have shared it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the framework has 0 confirmed predictions and 6 refutations, what is the site actually *for*? Is it a record of an idea that died, or an idea still being worked on? I genuinely couldn't tell after 10 pages.
2. Is "collapsing to MOND" bad? I read it four times and still don't know if it means the theory is wrong or that it accidentally rederived something respectable.
3. What *is* ρcrit, in units I could picture? Everything else got a "stars per cubic light-year"-style example; the knee never did.
4. Why is a calculator that the site says is sign-inverted still on the beginner path?
5. Superconductors "score C ≈ 0" and the site says that's fine because this isn't quantum coherence — but superconductors are the most collective thing I know of. That was explained three separate times and still felt like the theory calling the most coordinated thing in physics uncoordinated.

### Verdict

The honesty is the best thing here and also, weirdly, the biggest barrier — the site is so busy auditing itself that it forgets to make me care first. First Encounter and the dimmer-switch analogy prove the authors can explain hard ideas beautifully to someone like me; the problem is that every page then hands the microphone to a physicist arguing with themselves. I'd come back if the beginner path stayed beginner all the way through step 6 and told me, in one sentence up front, why a failed framework is still worth 30 minutes of my evening.
