# Closing Artifact: Challenger

**HELD (survives)**
- A driven, saturation-pinned source with net throughput Q and a baseline sink gives a far-field profile ΔI ∝ Q/r in 3D (1/r in 2D). This follows from conservation plus steady state (Laplace/Kirchhoff transform) and is a valid conditional result.
- Saturation does supply a stable, persistent core. If drift exists, its strength depends on D′(I), so a constant-R control yields zero force.
- The Primary conceded early and honestly: the exponent is a Laplace result, orbits and the equivalence principle are not defended, and the open-system cost and the weakness of the hop rule are acknowledged.
- The Round 3 test design is falsifiable (sign, exponent, linearity in Q, superposition, D′ = 0 control).

**NARROWED (must be scoped)**
- The claim is limited to a static, overdamped, test-probe scalar profile. It is not "gravity."
- "Pattern" must mean a driven open structure (pump plus sink), not a closed stable entity. The baseline is an input.
- Attraction exists only under a chosen hop convention (Itô, λ = 1). The force is −λ∇D, so it is zero (Fick), half (Stratonovich), or full depending on discretization. It is also a tracer accumulation effect (p ∝ 1/D), not a demonstrated force.
- Superposition and a constant coupling hold only at first order in ΔI/I_max. G becomes environment-dependent (chameleon-like), so the weak-field regime must be tuned to a fixed ambient saturation.
- "M" is throughput Q, with no demonstrated additivity or equality to inertial and gravitational mass.
- The EM and nuclear rows of the unification table are not examined here and remain stated assertions (static charges vs. "time-averaged ≈ 0", and oscillating sources radiating as 1/r).

**FAILED (unsupported / overreach)**
- "Saturation explains inverse-square": it is Laplace plus dimensionality, and a non-saturating diffusion or chemotaxis model reproduces it.
- Derivation of attraction from "transfer resistance." The whitepaper's own statement (less resistance toward a saturated core) contradicts R(I) → ∞ near saturation. Fick transfer gives no drift or a repulsive one, and the symmetric exclusion hydrodynamic limit gives no tracer drift.
- Equivalence principle for matter: a saturated body has D → 0, so under the departure-cell rule its mobility collapses. Fixing this requires an extra composite rule.
- Orbits, T² ∝ a³, and inertia: first-order drift gives infall with no closed orbits, and the second-order axiom is absent.
- Mass identification (Q ≡ M), G, and the claim of "quantitative predictions" and unification. These are not derived, and the patch count (hop convention, source and sink, inertia, mass, composite mobility) exceeds the single output obtained for free.
- Distinguishability from a Keller–Segel chemotaxis model. No test separates saturation as the origin from a posited drift coupling.

**WOULD SETTLE (decisive evidence/test)**
- A microscopic conservative lattice gas with exclusion and a pinned core in which tracer drift is nonzero and attractive without per-case tuning, with measured value and sign, n = 2.0 ± 0.1 across R(I) forms, linearity in Q, and a null D′ = 0 control.
- A two-mobile-core run where each body's acceleration scales with the other's Q and is independent of its own Q (additivity and equivalence).
- An explicit, minimal inertia mechanism that yields a bound Kepler orbit (T² ∝ a³) and not just infall.
- A saturation-specific prediction that a chemotaxis or linear scalar model does not make, for example a nonlinear ΔI²-dependent correction that is consistent with Cassini and lunar laser ranging bounds without tuning.
- A derivation of EM (including static Coulomb with sign) from the same rule, or an explicit retraction of the unification claim.

HEADLINE: NARROWED
