# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-08-09

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understood the big idea in about a minute and I trusted the site immediately because it kept telling me it was wrong — but by the third page I couldn't tell whether I was learning a theory or reading its autopsy.

### Journey

I clicked through from Reddit expecting crackpot stuff. The landing page said **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** and I liked that it was a question. Then right below it was a scoreboard saying **0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations, 0 validated claims**. That's not what I expected on a physics site and honestly it's why I kept reading. Nobody selling me something puts their loss record at the top.

The equation showed up on the landing page: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is. But I *did* get "coherence is a number between 0 and 1", which felt like something I could hold onto.

I hit the big **"Start Exploring"** button. It dropped me on **First Encounter**, which is labeled **Step 2 of 6**. I sat there for a second wondering what I'd missed. There's a separate, smaller "Beginner Path" button that goes to Step 1. Nobody clicks the small button first.

**First Encounter** was the best page on the site for me. Marching band vs. people wandering a plaza — I got it instantly. Crystal vs. gas between stars — got that too. An electron vs. matter in a neutron star "10¹⁴× denser than lead" — great, that's a real picture. Then out of nowhere: **MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)**. It says it's "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," which is fine, but then it says it's "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence" and I stopped reading that paragraph. I don't know what a degree of freedom is.

I went back to **Step 1, Why Synchronism?**, which I should have started at. This one actually manages expectations up front — "zero confirmed predictions," "the question is live, not resolved." It has a crowd/marching-band analogy again (fine, repetition helps me). But then in one screen I got hit with *phenomenological*, *Hamiltonian*, *ansatz*, and *compander*. Two of those got a quick gloss. Two didn't. "Ansatz" and "Hamiltonian" mean literally nothing to me and I skipped those sentences.

The thing that stuck from that page: **"Predicted 607K for YBCO; actual 93K."** I don't know what YBCO is but I can see 607 vs 93 and that's a miss by a lot. And **"melting points: 53% average error."** Those two numbers taught me more about the state of the theory than any of the prose.

**The Core Idea (Step 3)** is where I started to lose the thread. The headline is "One Function. **Three Parameters.** Every Scale from Planck to Cosmic." So I tried to count the three. The list underneath has *four* things — ρ, C, γ, and ρcrit. When I read closer, ρ is what you feed in and C is what comes out, so those aren't knobs at all. The actual dials seem to be γ and ρcrit — two. I genuinely could not work out what the third parameter was supposed to be. That matters to me because on another page it says **zero parameters have a first-principles derivation**, i.e. every dial is fitted to data. "Three parameters" made it sound like more theory and less fitting than there actually is.

Then that same page said tanh "recalls the Ising model self-consistency equation," and "C(ρ) is real-analytic everywhere on its domain — no singularity, no free-energy functional." I bounced. That's three pages into a path advertised as "no physics background needed."

I tried tools next.

**Equation Anatomy** rescued me. This is the single best thing on the site for someone like me. "**tanh** is like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1." And for ln: "a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger." That's it — that's all I needed, and it took ten seconds. I would move this tool *above* the equation on the landing page. Right now the equation appears on the homepage, on Why Synchronism, and on Core Idea before anyone tells me what the symbols are.

Then partway through the walkthrough there's a red box labeled **"Audited-Negative"** saying the γ formula is "Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems." I have no idea what that means but red box + "sign inverted" reads as "this part is broken." I'm being taught an equation and simultaneously told a piece of it is broken. I don't know what to do with that.

**Coherence Explorer** worked and was fun. Dragging γ down flattens the curve, dragging it up sharpens it; ρcrit just slides the whole thing sideways. I could see the S-shape. Two problems. First, the default γ is **0.49**, but the walkthrough and the galaxy plotter both talk about **γ=2**, and the γ Calculator page says γ = 2/√N_corr. I couldn't tell which value is "the" value or whether that's even a settled question. Second, the x-axis says **log₁₀(ρ)** with no units and no landmarks. If it labeled "air," "water," "lead," "neutron star" on that axis I would instantly know where everything in my life sits on this curve. Right now it's an abstract S-curve floating in space.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** — this is where I got most confused, and it's the confusion I'd bring to the comments section. The page tells me *"all three models fit galaxy rotation curves about equally well."* Then it tells me *"the solid violet line — the framework's real equation — fails to fix it."* And under the chart it says *"max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on."* So: does it fit or doesn't it? I stared at this for a while. My best guess is that "equally well" means MOND and dark matter and a *hand-tuned* version all fit, while the theory's *actual* equation doesn't — and there's an amber dotted line labeled "hand-tuned stand-in, illustration only" that supports that reading. But I had to reconstruct that myself from three statements in different places. Just say it in one sentence: "MOND fits. Dark matter fits. Our equation doesn't — here's the number: 0.001 where we'd need about 1."

Last stop, **Honest Assessment**, because "See What Failed" was right there on the homepage and it's the most interesting button on the site. Tonally this page is excellent. *"When its galaxy predictions were tested against real telescope measurements, it failed every decisive test."* That's clear and I respect it.

But the counting is a mess. The header says **6 refutations**. A section further down says **"four executed refutations."** Then it says 6 again, and then there's a paragraph explaining that the count *used to* be 4 because a scope word accidentally filtered out two failures. I understood the explanation, but only after reading it three times, and 4 and 6 are both still sitting on the page as live numbers. Same thing with the dwarf galaxy result: it says **69% of galaxies exceed the cap**, then immediately says under a different convention "the reported median actually passes, and the 69% figure does not hold." So does 69% of the evidence stand or not? I can't tell, and this is the site's own headline failure.

And the page is dense: ΔBIC, RAR, fσ₈, CHSH, SPARC, "Cassini quadrupole fails at +17.95σ." This is **step 5 of the beginner path**. There is a good no-jargon paragraph buried in it — *"It puts a hard cap on how much 'missing gravity' a galaxy can display — and about two-thirds of real galaxies display more than that cap allows"* — and that paragraph should be the top of the page, not inside it.

I did find the **Glossary** and it's genuinely good — tanh, ln, MOND, MRH, ΔBIC, σ, SPARC, CHSH, reparametrization were all there in one or two plain sentences each. It says "hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline," which is a great feature, but the terms that stopped me — MRH on First Encounter, ansatz and Hamiltonian on Why Synchronism — didn't appear to be hooked up as hover terms where I hit them. And there's no search box on the glossary, so I was scrolling to find things.

### Tools Tested

- **Equation Anatomy** (/equation-walkthrough) — **worked** — **intuitive** — Best page on the site for my level. The dimmer-switch line for tanh and "1,000× bigger comes out ~7 units bigger" for ln both landed immediately. Six steps with Previous/Next, easy to follow. The only jolt was the red Audited-Negative box mid-lesson — I couldn't tell if it invalidated what I'd just learned.
- **Coherence Explorer** (/coherence-explorer) — **worked** — **mostly intuitive** — Sliders did what I expected: γ steepens the S-curve, ρcrit slides it sideways. The prompt to "drag γ down and watch it flatten" gave me something to actually do. Held back by an unlabeled x-axis and a default γ that disagrees with the γ used elsewhere on the site.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — **worked** — **confusing** — Five real galaxies, five curves, clear axes (kpc vs km/s). Mechanically fine. But I had to reverse-engineer the conclusion from three statements scattered around the page. Real credit for the amber curve being explicitly labeled "hand-tuned, illustration only, not theory-derived" and for the caption "max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on" — that caption is the honest punchline and it's in the smallest text on the page.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is the theory dead or alive? The homepage asks "what if one equation described reality" and the assessment page calls it "fatal, by its own scoreboard." I still don't know if I'm reading an active research program or a post-mortem — and I'd read either one, I just want to know which.
2. Which γ is the real one — 2, or 0.49? Different pages use different values and none of them told me the other exists.
3. Is it 4 refutations or 6?
4. Does the 69%-of-dwarf-galaxies failure count, given the page says it doesn't hold under a different convention?
5. If all the parameters are fitted to data and none are derived, what makes this different from just drawing a curve through the points? The site half-answers this with the word "reparametrization" but never says it plainly to a beginner.
6. What's the *one* thing this framework predicted that nothing else did? I couldn't find it, and I think the answer might be "nothing yet," but I'd rather be told that in one sentence than infer it.
7. Small one: what is YBCO?

### Verdict

The honesty is real and it's the reason I stayed — a site that leads with a 0–6 scoreboard has earned more trust from me in thirty seconds than most science content earns in an hour. The analogies (marching band, plaza, dimmer switch) are genuinely good, and Equation Anatomy is the best explainer of a scary-looking equation I've read.

But the beginner path breaks around step 3 and doesn't recover: it teaches an equation on three pages before defining its symbols, then hands me a page full of ΔBIC and fσ₈ while still calling itself beginner-level. And the site's own headline numbers disagree with each other — 4 vs. 6 refutations, 69% vs. median-passes, γ=2 vs. γ=0.49. On a site whose entire pitch is rigorous self-honesty, a casual reader who can't check any of the physics *can* check whether the numbers match, and right now they don't. That's the thing I'd fix first, because it's the one flaw that undercuts the site's actual strength.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The prose is unusually clear and the breadcrumbs are near-perfect, but the site is running at least four incompatible status vocabularies at once — and the headline number ("6 refutations") is computed from labels that the canonical badge legend does not contain.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set: walk the nav, walk the breadcrumbs, check whether the controlled vocabulary is actually controlled, and see whether a term is defined at the point of first use or only somewhere a reader has to go find.

**Navigation.** Nine top-level items, flat, no grouping, no search. That's serviceable for a site this size. Two label mismatches, though: nav says **"Start Here"** and lands on a page titled **"Why Synchronism?"**; nav says **"Tools"**, the page title and the breadcrumb both say **"Interactive Tools."** Users build a mental map from nav labels, and it should match the H1 they land on.

The bigger structural issue is that the **breadcrumbs describe a site the navigation doesn't**. Every page I hit had a clean breadcrumb, and they consistently assert a four-section hierarchy:

- Home / **Getting Started** / Honest Assessment, The Core Idea
- Home / **Predictions & Experiments** / Test Roadmap, Tier 1: Existing Data
- Home / **Methodology** / Research Philosophy, For Researchers
- Home / **Interactive Tools** / γ Calculator, Phase Boundary Visualizer

Those four section names appear *nowhere* in the top nav or footer. So the site has a real IA — a good one — and only exposes it in the trail after you've already arrived. Related: "Tier 1: Existing Data" sits in the top nav as a **sibling** of "Test Roadmap," but its own breadcrumb correctly files it as a **child** of the Predictions & Experiments section that contains Test Roadmap. The nav is flattening a two-level structure and losing the relationship. (I also probed `/getting-started` on a hunch; it 307-redirects rather than resolving to a section page, which confirms the sections are organizing concepts with no page behind them.)

Credit where due: the breadcrumbs themselves are consistent, correctly ordered, and present on every page I visited including deep tool pages. That's better than most documentation sites manage.

**Step indicators.** `/first-encounter` says "Beginner Path — Step 2 of 6" with proper "← Prev: Why Synchronism?" / "Next: The Core Idea →" links. Good pattern. But `/core-idea` carries **two simultaneous position markers**: "Beginner Path Step 3 of 6" *and* "Philosophy Track Step 1 of 7." A page can legitimately belong to two paths, but then the Prev/Next control has to know which path I'm on. As rendered I can't tell whether "Next" will advance my beginner path or my philosophy track, and there's no path selector or "you are on: Beginner" state. That's the one navigation bug I'd call functional rather than cosmetic.

Also, `/learning-paths` offers **8 paths** — 3 by difficulty, 4 by topic (plus a dated correction note, "corrected 2026-08-05," clarifying that difficulty and topic paths cover different content). Eight overlapping paths across ~74 pages, with pages belonging to multiple paths and no visible progress state, is a lot of scaffolding. I'd want to know whether anyone completes them.

**Terminology — the main finding.** The site declares a controlled vocabulary. `/honest-assessment` has a "Validation badge definitions" section near the top (correctly placed *before* the badges are used on that page), with two families plus deprecations:

> Active-MRH · Parallel-Paths · Sidelined · Superseded · Audited-Negative — Untested · Speculative · Reparametrization · Failed — *(deprecated: Validated, Strongly Supported)*

Nine current terms, cleanly defined, one sentence each. This is a good taxonomy. Then I went and counted what the rest of the site actually uses.

`/tier-1-existing` labels its 13 rows with: *Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Never Run as Registered, Protocol Deviation, Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Disfavored (not Kill Criterion Triggered), Re-adjudicated, Demoted to Tier-2, Underpowered as Registered, Ceiling Exceeded, Empty Intersection.* Exactly **one** row (TEST-08, "Reparametrization") uses a term from the canonical list.

`/test-catalog` adds *Proposed, Pre-registered & Completed, Executed, Refuted, Closed, Unrunnable as stated.*

`/key-claims` adds *Untestable*, "untestable as stated," and "parent claim, unfalsifiable as stated." Note that **"Untestable" is not in the taxonomy at all** — and the page's own pointer sentence names it as if it were: *"Status labels (Untestable, Speculative, etc.) follow the site's validation badge taxonomy."* It doesn't.

`/interactive-tools` adds a third dimension: *Model Explainer, Artifact Lesson, Beginner, Advanced* — mixed in the same tag row as *Reparametrization* and *Speculative*, which ARE verdicts. The page tries to fix this with a note that "Model Explainer means 'shows how the equation works,' not a verdict on whether the equation is correct" — but that note only disclaims one of the six tags, and leaves a reader to work out that two tags in the same visual row are verdicts and the rest aren't. **"Artifact Lesson" is defined nowhere** — not on the tools page, not on `/research-philosophy`, not in the 72-term glossary.

That's roughly **30 distinct status strings** in service of a 9-term declared taxonomy. For a docs set this is the highest-value fix available, and it has a sharp consequence: the site's single most-quoted number, **"6 refutations executed on external data,"** is a count of rows labeled *"Kill Criterion Triggered" / "Failed" / "Ceiling Exceeded" / "Empty Intersection"* — and three of those four labels are absent from the legend a reader is sent to. The number cannot be audited from the vocabulary that defines it.

**Same object, four names.** The relation γ = 2/√N_corr is described as "Audited-Negative" (equation walkthrough), "a refuted relation" (phase boundary visualizer), "inverted formula structure per Caveat 2" (γ calculator), and "the galaxy-scale pin — refuted on SPARC data" for the derived γ=2 (core idea). A reader meeting these in different orders will not reliably conclude these are all the same finding. Pick one phrasing and one severity word.

**Symbol naming.** ρ is called "Presence" on `/first-encounter` and `/core-idea`, "density" in the equation gloss, and appears as bare "log₁₀(ρ)" in the Coherence Explorer. `/core-idea` splits the difference: *"Presence (ρ): local density — how many relevant elements are packed in a given region."* Two names for one symbol is survivable if you always print both; right now the tools print neither.

**"Three parameters."** `/core-idea`'s headline is *"One function. Three parameters. Every scale from Planck to cosmic,"* and the three it names are **C, γ, ρcrit**. C is the function's *output*. A function's output is not one of its parameters. The free parameters are γ and ρcrit — two — which is also what the Coherence Explorer's two sliders show. (Pass 1 hit this independently and counted four items; the underlying defect is the same and it's a definitional error, not just a counting one.)

**Definition-at-first-use.** Mixed. `/key-claims` and `/research-philosophy` do this well — MRH, IIT, DD, MOND all get parenthetical expansions on first use. But **SPARC, ΛCDM, RAR, BTFR, CHSH, SME** are used bare on `/for-researchers`; **BCS and BEC** are used bare on the phase boundary visualizer; **CDM, MOND, RAR, BTFR, CHSH, A2ACW** are all bare on `/learning-paths`. The glossary carries 72 terms and covers most of these — but it has **no search box and no A–Z jump index**, so retrieving one term from 72 means scrolling. For a glossary that the site advertises as the backstop for every undefined term, a filter box is the single highest-leverage change on the page.

Terms I checked that are **missing from the glossary entirely**: *ansatz, Hamiltonian, YBCO, artifact lesson.* The first three are exactly the words that stopped Pass 1 cold, which suggests the glossary is being maintained against the physics vocabulary rather than against the vocabulary the pages actually use.

**A2ACW** is expanded as "AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop" on the landing page and `/research-philosophy`. The **glossary entry does not include the expansion** — it defines the protocol functionally ("Protocol where AI agents stress-test each other's claims…"). Neither does `/for-researchers`, which describes it via "adversarial pairs from AI Safety via Debate." A reader who meets the acronym first on `/learning-paths` and looks it up in the glossary still won't learn what the letters stand for.

**Inventory arithmetic.** `/test-catalog` tabulates Tier 1 = **10** tests; `/tier-1-existing` opens with *"These **11** numbered tests (TEST-01 through TEST-11)"* and then lists **13 rows** (adding TEST-04a and TEST-03s). Meanwhile TEST-07 is labeled "Demoted to Tier-2," which should move it out of the Tier-1 count and into Tier 2's. Three numbers for one set. The catalog's own reconciliation — *"only 11 tests have numbered IDs; the remaining 13 exist as proposals across tiers but lack specific TEST-NN designations"* — is honest, but it means the top-nav promise of "24 proposed experiments by tier" is 11 identified items plus 13 unaddressable ones. Unaddressable items can't be cited, linked, or tracked; a reader who wants to follow up on a Tier-3 proposal has no handle for it.

**Rendering nit.** On `/key-claims` the first badge came through as **"Speculativeuntestable as stated — ontological reframe"** — badge label and qualifier with no separator. This may be an artifact of how I retrieved the page rather than a live CSS bug, so it's worth an eyeball in a browser before anyone chases it.

So `γ = 1/2` is not "in the simple-μ family" — it **is** Milgrom's simple interpolating function, identically, with the factor of 2 absorbed into `ρcrit`. **Then I hit an outright math error.** `/gamma-boundary` — the anchor page for the entire chemistry sector, carrying "1,840 chemistry sessions," "1,703 distinct phenomena," "89% boundary-consistent" — states:

> "At γ ≈ 1, the coherence function has maximum curvature. Small changes in density produce maximum change in coherence."

This is false, and not marginally. From the Hill form with `t = 1+x`:

```
dC/dx = 4γ · t^(2γ−1) / (t^(2γ) + 1)²        →   dC/dx |_{x=0} = γ
```

The slope at the origin is **γ**, monotonically increasing in γ without bound. There is no maximum at γ ≈ 1 or at any finite γ. I checked the log-density reading too, in case "curvature" meant slope in `ln ρ`: `dC/d ln x = 4γ(1−1/t)·w/(w+1)²` with `w = t^{2γ}`, whose maximum over x rises like ≈ γ for γ ≪ 1 and *saturates* at ≈ 0.45 for γ ≫ 1. Monotone increasing and saturating — still no interior maximum. The most one can honestly say is that γ ≈ 0.5–1 is roughly where sensitivity stops improving, which is a knee in a saturation curve, not "maximum curvature."

7. Does anything in the framework survive if you grant it the AQUAL completion? My read is no — the six refutations are all shape-of-C and boost-ceiling results — but the site never runs that counterfactual, and it would sharpen the verdict considerably.

### Verdict

This is a more honest document than most published papers, and the parts I could check with pen and paper mostly check out — the Hill identity, the MOND ν-function, the ΔBIC bookkeeping, the DDO 154 coherence value, the a₀ arithmetic, the Landau-inapplicability argument. The "MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17}" framing is a genuinely good piece of reasoning that explains the entire empirical record without reference to any data.

The failure mode has inverted, though, and that's what I'd flag hardest. This site no longer over-claims; it over-refutes. The +184 headline depends on treating 2,807 correlated rotation-curve points as independent and shrinks to ~+7 under a routine covariance correction. The 8σ a₀ figure is only 8σ if you discard the systematic that is 12× larger — and the page that says so honestly is not the page the number travels from. And "no field equation exists" presents a 1984 textbook construction as an unbridgeable gap. A framework that inflates its own kill statistics is making the same class of error as one that inflates its confirmations; the direction is more sympathetic but the epistemics aren't better.

The one thing I'd fix today is `/gamma-boundary`. "At γ ≈ 1 the coherence function has maximum curvature" is false, it's the stated physical rationale for a 1,703-phenomenon claim, and the correct derivative is already written out on another page of the same site.

That reframes the headline: **"6 refutations" is not six failures of a theory. It is one asserted constant being wrong, plus results that don't belong to this framework at all.** More on those below.

**Decomposing the six.** Reading the ledger as a referee, the entries fall into three incommensurable classes:

| # | Entry | What it actually refutes |

**The TEST-11 attribution problem — my headline finding.** `/tier-1-existing` describes TEST-11 as: free-γ SPARC fit converges γ ≈ 0.489, then "applying Hill identity tanh formula as QUMOND interpolation makes parameter-free Cassini quadrupole prediction," yielding an empty intersection at +17.95σ, executed 2026-07-23.

That is, to within the choice of interpolating-function family, [Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024), MNRAS 530, 1781](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/530/2/1781/7641422). Their result: in AQUAL and QUMOND, the interpolating functions that fit the RAR (shape parameter δ ≈ 1 — the Simple IF and the RAR IF) are in **8.7σ tension** with the Cassini quadrupole bound, which demands δ ≳ 2.5. The tension "persists across all IF families tested." The Cassini quadrupole channel itself is [Blanchet & Novak (2011)](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/412/4/2530/1020883), with Q₂ = (3±3)×10⁻²⁷ s⁻².

Now combine that with Pass 3's identity: at γ = 1/2, C = x/(x+2) ≡ μ_simple(x/2) **exactly**. The SPARC fit lands at 0.489. So TEST-11 took the simple-μ function, put it in QUMOND, and hit the Cassini wall — which is precisely the published finding, two years old, for MOND. Three consequences:

1. **It is not a Synchronism-specific refutation.** It refutes the RAR-preferred IF family in modified-gravity MOND. Synchronism inherits it by being in that family.
2. **The site applies it asymmetrically.** TEST-09 and TEST-10 declare MOND the winner. TEST-11 kills a function MOND also uses — and the site's own `/galaxy-plotter` draws its "MOND (green dashed)" reference curve with `ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y)`, **the simple-ν function**, i.e. the exact object TEST-11 excludes. The tool that shows MOND winning is plotting the function the site's own test says is Cassini-dead. Either apply the squeeze to both or to neither.
3. **The magnitude is inflated ~2×.** +17.95σ vs a published 8.7σ for the same physics. Desmond+ marginalize over a₀, mass-to-light, and RAR-fit uncertainty; a calculation that fixes γ and a₀ will produce a larger σ. This is the same over-refutation signature Pass 3 documented for ΔBIC = +184 and the a₀ 8σ.

There is also a straight internal contradiction here. `/honest-assessment` states: **"There is no field equation anywhere in this framework's galaxy sector — no action, no Lagrangian, no covariant formulation, no dynamics."** TEST-11 computes a *quadrupole moment of the solar-system metric*. You cannot do that without a field equation; the site adopted QUMOND to do it. So the site both asserts that no dynamics exists and reports a result that presupposes one. Pass 3 flagged the AQUAL point on general grounds; TEST-11 makes it concrete — the completion isn't hypothetical, the site already used it.

**The two headline galaxy numbers describe different models.** This one I'd raise in review. `/galaxy-rotation` reports the RAR fit: free-γ gives Δχ² = −0.84 against McGaugh's IF on 2,807 points — a *marginally better fit than MOND*. `/galaxy-plotter` reports, for DDO 154, `max C on this disk = 0.001`.

These cannot both be the same function. If the fitted compander used `x = ρ/ρ_crit` with the site's stated calibration `ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat²`, then C ≈ 10⁻³ across every SPARC disk and the model is Newtonian — which would be rejected against MOND at a ΔBIC in the thousands, not −0.84. A fit that ties MOND to within Δχ² < 1 must be running on `x = g_bar/a₀`, the acceleration variable. So:

- **The acceleration-space compander** is what ΔBIC = +184 and γ = 0.489 refer to. It is MOND with a relabelled interpolating function — and `/core-idea` already proves the tanh∘ln is notational.
- **The density-space compander** is the framework's actual and only structurally novel commitment. It has *never been BIC-compared to anything*, because it produces no boost at all.

`/galaxy-rotation` knows the variables differ ("C(ρ) is a function of local density, while the RAR is organized by g_bar… a pointwise map cannot in general reproduce an acceleration-space relation"). What it doesn't say is that its own headline statistic was computed on the wrong side of that distinction. **The site's most-cited galaxy number refutes a model the framework does not hold, and the model it does hold has never been fit.**

**The framework is linear in the only regime anyone has tested.** For x ≪ 1, `tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = γx + O(x²)`. Pass 3's DDO 154 numbers give x = 6.6×10⁻⁴, and γx = 2×6.6×10⁻⁴ = 1.3×10⁻³ — reproducing the plotter's "max C = 0.001" to the digit, confirming the linearization is exact at the 0.1% level. So on the entire physically accessible domain:

```
C(ρ) ≈ (γ/ρcrit)·ρ  —  linear in density, one effective coefficient, no tanh, no ln
```

The compander, the Hill index, the saturation knee, the γ-sharpness story, the whole sigmoid — all of it describes a regime no galaxy occupies. This is a sharper statement than "reparametrization of MOND": in the density reading, the flagship equation is not MOND, it is a one-parameter *linear* density perturbation whose amplitude is numerically zero. That's worth one paragraph on `/core-idea`, and it's a stronger closure than any of the executed tests.

**TEST-04a was arithmetically incapable of firing.** Registered: fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418 vs ΛCDM 0.474; kill criterion `fσ₈ > 0.46` for >3σ. DESI DR1's statistical uncertainty on fσ₈ for LRG1 (0.4 < z < 0.6) is ≈ 0.066 ([DESI 2024 V, arXiv:2411.12021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12021)). The full prediction–ΛCDM separation is 0.056 — **0.85× the 1σ error**. A test whose entire signal is smaller than its own error bar cannot reach 3σ against anything, and that was computable at registration, before the data. The site's status line says "underpowered as registered" and "~1.5σ on the registered statistic," which is right; but `/honest-assessment` still leads with "**2.4σ tension**," a number obtained on **σ₈, not fσ₈** — a different observable, and one DESI derives under a ΛCDM+BBN prior, which makes quoting it against a modified-growth prediction circular. Registered on one statistic, adjudicated on another, headlined with the second. As a referee that's an automatic reject of the test, independent of which way it came out.

Related and actionable: the site says DR2 outcomes were pre-committed in writing on 2026-07-17. DESI DR2 full-shape clustering results have been presented publicly ([PIRSA, April 2026](https://pirsa.org/26040071)). If DR2 fσ₈ is available, the site's one genuinely prospective registration is **adjudicable now** — that would be the first prospective test in the ledger's history, and it's sitting unrun.

**The four citable artifacts, as a referee.** `/for-researchers` nominates four survivors. My assessment:

1. **Local-density no-go** — *weakest as stated, strongest with scoping.* The site itself cites the counterexample: Burrage, Copeland & Millington fit 153 SPARC galaxies with a symmetron scalar keyed on local volumetric ρ(r). So there is no no-go on local density per se. What survives is narrower and real: **algebraic, pointwise** C(ρ) fails, while **differential** (gradient/screening) couplings do not — because the screening field integrates ρ over a Compton-wavelength neighbourhood and thereby recovers the non-locality that g_bar carries. That distinction is publishable as a short note, but only if the abstract states it. Right now the title claim is refuted by its own citation.
2. **A2ACW program-level null** — *not publishable.* n = 6, single-arm, no control corpus, and the 3,308 → 47 → 6 cascade is a selection funnel whose denominator is uncontrolled. Sensitivity = 1.0 and FPR = 1.0 on six items gives Youden's J = 0, but the 95% CI on specificity with n = 6 and zero successes runs to roughly 0.46 — you cannot distinguish "zero discriminating information" from "modest information" at that N. The site names the missing experiment correctly (same protocol on a corpus containing post-cutoff discoveries). Until that arm exists, this is a pilot observation, not a null result.
3. **Dim-4 LIV naturalness gap** — *correct, not new.* This is Collins, Perez, Sudarsky & Urrutia (2004) applied to a new framework. The site says so. It should also say that custodial mechanisms exist in the literature (SUSY suppression to dimension-6, Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov 2005; Pospelov–Shang), so "no custodial mechanism exhibited" is a statement about this framework's silence, not about the impossibility of one. The landing page's "11–28 orders of magnitude" and `/for-researchers`' "16–28" still disagree (Pass 3 caught this; it's still live).
4. **CHSH substrate null** — *zero novelty, and the site knows it.* It is Bell's theorem. It should not also appear as refutation #6 in a count advertised as "executed on external data." A theorem is not external data.

Net: **0 of 4 are new results.** One (#1, scoped to algebraic coupling) is a new *instance* of a known obstruction and is worth ~3 pages in a journal. The site's own summary — "the contribution is the controlled null result, not the protocol" — is honest about #2, and the same sentence should be written for #1, #3, and #4.

**What I'd actually want and can't get.** There is no preprint, no DOI, no analysis repository, no data release. For a project whose stated product is "an arriving expert should not have to excavate the failure documentation to find what survives it," the survivors are not in a form anyone can cite. I cannot reference a Vercel URL in a referee report. If exactly one thing gets built next, it should be a 6-page arXiv note on the algebraic-vs-differential local-density distinction, with the SPARC fits and the BCM comparison, and nothing else in it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Was TEST-11 run in ignorance of Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024), or was the overlap known and not recorded? The answer determines whether the literature-review step exists in this pipeline at all — and TEST-02 (wide binaries) is in the same paper.
2. In what variable was the ΔBIC = +184 / γ = 0.489 RAR fit performed — `ρ/ρcrit` or `g_bar/a₀`? Δχ² = −0.84 against McGaugh's IF is only attainable in the second. If it's the second, what has ever been fit in the first?
3. If the density-space compander produces C ≲ 10⁻³ on every disk, on what data was γ = 0.489 measured, and is that γ the same object as the γ in `C(ρ)`?
4. Has the DESI DR2 full-shape fσ₈ been checked against the 2026-07-17 pre-commitment? DR2 full-shape results have been presented publicly; this would be the ledger's first genuinely prospective adjudication.
5. Does anything in the framework distinguish itself from MOND once B_max is removed? If B_max is the only distinguishing content and it's asserted, the honest statement is "this is MOND with an extra assumption we made up and then refuted" — which is a clean result and shorter than the current page.
6. What is the control-arm design for A2ACW, concretely — which post-cutoff discoveries, which corpus, which models? The site names the missing experiment repeatedly but never specifies it, and it's the only remaining path to a publishable result from the methodology side.
7. Why is the site's Cassini tension 2× the published one? If the difference is fixed vs marginalized nuisance parameters, that's the third instance of the same over-refutation mechanism (after ΔBIC N_eff and the a₀ 8σ) and deserves a named entry in the site's own failure taxonomy.

### Verdict

The central intellectual move here — recognizing that the galaxy sector is a strict submodel of MOND and therefore could only tie or lose, and saying so before appealing to any data — is genuinely good reasoning, and I have read funded papers that don't reach that level of self-clarity. The site's answer to my one question is correct: there is nothing new here, and the reason is structural rather than empirical.

But the ledger that carries that verdict is not sound as constructed. It counts a theorem, an inherited 2024 MOND constraint, and four tests of two asserted constants as six refutations of one framework; it computes its sharpest number in a formalism it simultaneously claims doesn't exist; its two headline galaxy statistics are from different models; and its "citable artifacts" include one refuted by its own citation and one that is Bell's theorem. The over-refutation Pass 3 identified isn't a tendency, it's a mechanism — fix nuisance parameters, drop the dominant systematic, count corollaries as roots — and it now has three instances.

The salvageable result is real and it is one paper: *algebraic pointwise density coupling cannot reproduce an acceleration-organized relation; differential density coupling can, and here is the quantified instance.* That's a legitimate short note with a live counterexample to bound it. Everything else on this site is either MOND, ΛCDM, Bell, or a constant somebody asserted. The site is about 80% of the way to saying exactly that, and the last 20% would make it shorter.
