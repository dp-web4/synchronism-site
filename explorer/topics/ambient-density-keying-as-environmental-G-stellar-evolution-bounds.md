# Topic: Ambient-density keying is an environment-dependent G. What do stars already say?

## Question
Under ambient-density keying, a system's effective gravitational strength is set by the density it sits in. Wide
binaries can't see this, because the boost cancels in γ_g (TEST-02's argument). Stellar structure can: main-sequence
luminosity scales steeply with G (L ∝ G^~7), and helioseismology, white-dwarf cooling and red-clump luminosities are all
G-sensitive. What G_eff ratio does the density law imply between the Solar neighbourhood (ρ ≈ 0.1 M☉/pc³, close to the
GC knee 0.161), globular-cluster cores, the bulge and the halo? Do existing stellar-population data already bound it?

## Context
The 2026-09-23 researcher persona called TEST-02's γ_g ≡ 1 cancellation "the best-argued card" on the site. They noted
the missed corollary: operationally, ambient keying is an environmental G_eff with $0 constraints the test catalogue
doesn't list. The Solar neighbourhood sitting near the GC knee is the sharp part. Recall the 09-15 scope condition: a
pointwise reading puts the Sun inside the solar wind, so you must state a smoothing length D before computing anything.

## Why It Matters
It could become a Tier 1 card with a real kill that doesn't depend on SPARC, M/L, or the quadrature/division wiring fork.
If the implied G_eff contrast between field stars and GC cores exceeds ~1%, helioseismology and GC colour–magnitude
diagrams should already exclude it.
