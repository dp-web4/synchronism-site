## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary

### Journey
Landed from Reddit. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", hooked me right away. Then the very next line says "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." My honest reaction was "wait, then why am I here?" The scorecard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, "it lost all six") is startling, and I like it. I've never seen a site lead with its own failure. The pitch, "the map of *where* it fails is the deliverable", sort of persuaded me. I'm curious, but I'm not sure what I'd be taking away.

The landing page's plain-English box helped a lot: tanh = "S-shaped squashing function", ln = "compression", plus the marching-band-vs-plaza-crowd picture. I liked being told "you do not need the equation to read this site."

I took the Beginner Path (6 steps, ~45 min). The "Start Here" link took me to "Why Synchronism?". It opens fine with the crowd/band analogy, then hits me with "phenomenological", "ansatz", "compander", SPARC, ALFALFA–SDSS, and "pair-breaking / Abrikosov–Gor'kov". These are words I'd have to Google, on step 1 of a *beginner* path.

First Encounter started well ("Coherence is how collectively a group of things behaves"). By Section A, though, I'm reading "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence." Section B's "Expert note" about BCS γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ and N_corr in the denominator went completely over my head. The ending lists failures (melting points 53% off, superconductor Tc 6.5× wrong). The honesty is good, but I finished the page unsure what I'd actually learned.

Core Idea is where I really got lost. The dimmer-switch picture is great. Then it warns "⚠ Not quantum coherence — superconductors score *low* here." That broke my mental model. I thought superconductors were *the* example of things acting together, so why do they score near zero on a "togetherness" scale? I'd need one plain sentence on that. Then come Hill coefficients, "saturation knee", the Ising model and the central limit theorem. The "Beginner? You can stop here" exits are kind, but there's a lot of page after them. I also don't remember MRH being spelled out on this page itself. I only knew it from First Encounter.

What Synchronism Is Not read as a little defensive, but it's clear. The one that surprised me was "Not Lorentz-Invariant": apparently the "universal clock" idea is already ruled out by experiments "by 16-28 orders of magnitude." That sounds fatal to me, and it's tucked into step 4 of 6.

The Honest Assessment summary box is short and readable ("What survives is the record, not the physics"). The full page is enormous, way beyond me, and I didn't go further. The glossary is good: plain-language entries with an A–Z index. The MOND entry finally told me what MOND *is* ("gravity behaves differently at very low accelerations"), after I'd already seen the word maybe 15 times.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /learning-paths, /glossary, /mrh, /tools → /interactive-tools, /gamma-calculator, /equation-walkthrough, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /test-catalog, /key-claims, /for-researchers, /honest-assessment badge anchor; plus a /core-idea term check)

### Journey
I started on the landing page. The top nav (Start Here · Learning Paths · Tier 1: Existing Data · Test Roadmap · Tools · Glossary · Research Philosophy · For Researchers · Honest Assessment) is flat and readable. Acronym discipline on the landing page is strong: MOND, SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, ΛCDM, MRH, A2ACW, fσ₈, BAO and LLM are all expanded or glossed in-line. The badge note under the scoreboard is exactly what I'd want: "text after a dash … is a finding descriptor, not a separate badge", plus "'Withdrawn' is an operational state, not a badge." The one landing-page miss is **DESI**, which first appears as "DESI fσ₈" with no expansion.

Learning Paths is clean: three difficulty paths and four topic tracks, each with step counts, time estimates and link targets that match the target page titles. I liked the self-correction ("this page previously promised they 'cover the same pages,' which its own link lists contradict").

The Glossary is the backbone: 100 terms, an A–Z anchor bar, Greek symbols sorted last, and the "nine formal validation badges" marked "(badge)". Every badge in the two families is defined. DESI *is* expanded here ("Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument"), so the gap is only at point of use. The glossary also defines operational states that aren't badges: Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, the retired "MOND-shared" marker, and the deprecated Validated / Strongly Supported.

That's where my main structural finding started. Counting across pages, a reader meets **at least six label layers**:
1. 9 formal badges, in two families (MRH-relationship / Descriptive)
2. Finding descriptors after a dash ("Speculative — Untestable as Stated")
3. Operational states ("Withdrawn", "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie", "Unrunnable", "Underpowered", "Artifact Lesson")
4. Tool tags ("Tool type: Model Explainer · Artifact Lesson")
5. Deprecated badges (Validated, Strongly Supported)
6. Retired markers (MOND-shared)

Each layer is individually defined, but no single page shows how they relate. The Crossover Regime Visualizer header shows the cost in one line: *"Tool type: Model Explainer · Artifact Lesson (the Interactive Tools index Kind and Status tags — Artifact Lesson is an operational state, not a level: a tool kept to show a failure, not a verdict) · Claim status: Audited-Negative, the badge above."* That parenthetical is the documentation explaining itself because the taxonomy can't.

Second structural finding: **"MRH" names two unrelated things**. One is a physics concept (Markov Relevancy Horizon, /mrh). The other is a badge family ("MRH-relationship badges: Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths…"). The glossary admits it: "The borrowing is a metaphor. These badges say nothing about the Markov Relevancy Horizon as a physics concept." A disclaimer is how you manage a name collision, but renaming is how you fix one. A reader who sees "Active-MRH" on a physics claim will reasonably think it's about the horizon.

The MRH page has a good breadcrumb (Home / Core Theory / MRH: Markov Relevancy Horizon), an expansion in the H1, and Prev/Next links. After the opening, though, SPARC, BTFR and Ω_m appear unexpanded. Key Claims links its badges to the canonical legend (`/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, and the anchor resolves), uses no deprecated badges, and shows a correct breadcrumb. It does use MRH, CRT, PRL, CHSH, ALFALFA–SDSS, EEG/fMRI and AQUAL without expanding them.

The Test Roadmap has the best-designed legend on the site: a "Validation Badge Guide" box with both families, each defined in one line. But the body uses DESI ("DESI DR3"), BAO ("BAO coherence modulation"), LLR ("(Oort, LLR)"), ΔBIC, CHSH and ΛCDM ("MOND+EFE+ΛCDM") with no expansion on that page. It also uses "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie" and "unrunnable", which are defined in the glossary but not in the page's own legend, so the legend box looks complete when it isn't. The page is H1 "Test Roadmap" at URL /test-catalog, and its text also says "registry" (the original 24). That makes three names, though the page itself never says "Test Catalog", so the URL is the only mismatch.

For Researchers opens with "The program in one sentence". That sentence is roughly 150 words and uses "ansatz", "Milgrom's simple μ", ρ_DE, Λ, "DESI DR2+CMB+SN" and P(k) before anything is defined. It's a citable-claims page for experts, so some density is fair, but the very first sentence is the site's least parseable.

Breadcrumbs: they're present on every page I checked. Some middle crumbs, though, are unlinked labels for sections that don't exist as pages: "Core Theory" and "Predictions & Experiments" both 404 as URLs, and "Methodology" is plain text even though /methodology redirects to /research-philosophy. "Interactive Tools" *is* linked and works. The top nav calls it "Tools" (/tools → 308 → /interactive-tools), while the breadcrumb and H1 say "Interactive Tools". That's minor but inconsistent.

*Method note for the maintainer:* two apparent defects turned out to be fetch-summarizer artifacts when I checked them against the raw HTML. Key Claims' breadcrumb does **not** have an empty crumb ("Home / / Key Claims" was a rendering artifact), and the landing page does **not** list "Withdrawn" as a badge (it explicitly says it isn't one). I haven't logged either.

### Verdict
As a documentation auditor, I'd rate the definitions layer as excellent. The label taxonomy has grown by accretion into six layers that each need a parenthetical to explain, and "MRH" is overloaded between physics and bookkeeping. One "how to read labels" table and one rename would remove more confusion than another round of per-page expansions.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /core-idea, /tier-1-existing, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /key-claims, /honest-assessment (refutation ledger and badge glossary, via raw text))
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The arithmetic is right everywhere I checked, the reparametrizations are named by the site before I could find them, and the remaining problems are about *which function* is being refuted and *how many* refutations are really independent and Synchronism-specific.

### Journey
A colleague sent me this as an "interesting AI project", and I went straight to /parameter-derivations expecting to find a mean-field derivation of tanh that I could take apart. Instead the page takes it apart itself. It says tanh is "a phenomenological choice from the sigmoid/compander family", and that the Ising resemblance is only visual because "C(ρ) has no such self-consistency loop". That's the correct objection: in Curie–Weiss, tanh comes from m = tanh(βJzm + βh), and here there's no fixed point at all. The page reports zero independently derived parameters. That matches what I'd have concluded.

**Math I checked by hand:**
- Hill identity: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. ✓ At γ = ½ this gives x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). ✓ So "exactly MOND's simple μ" is correct, up to the factor-2 rescaling of the scale. The Coherence Explorer states that rescaling explicitly, and I appreciated it. Large-x tail: 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ}, so q = 2γ. The TEST-25 card's "q = 2γ ≈ 0.98, indistinguishable from simple ν" is right.
- Coherence Explorer readouts at γ = 0.49: C(ρ_c) = tanh(0.49·ln 2) = 0.3272 ✓, C(10ρ_c) = 0.8259 ✓, C(100ρ_c) = 0.9785 ✓. The midpoint C = ½ sits at x = exp(artanh(½)/0.49) − 1 = 2.07 ✓. The note "0.327 / 0.60 / 0.88 at γ = 0.49 / 1 / 2" is also correct (tanh(ln 2) = 3/5, tanh(2 ln 2) = 15/17).
- a₀ = cH₀/2π: with H₀ = 67.4 I get 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ ✓, 13% below 1.2 ✓. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 118.7 M☉/pc² ✓. The page correctly says this is the a₀ row again, not a second confirmation.
- A-coefficient: 0.0294/4.6×10⁻⁵ ≈ 640 ✓ ("600× off").
- ρ_crit ∝ V^{−0.16±0.19}: (2+0.16)/0.19 = 11.4σ from V^{+2} ✓, (2−0.16)/0.19 = 9.7σ from V^{−2} ✓.
- TEST-09: Δn = 0.41, √(0.07² + 0.10²) = 0.122, which gives 3.4σ ✓. TEST-10: f_DM = 1 − 1/B, so B = 3.17 gives 0.685 ✓, and 0.927 needs B = 13.7 ✓.
- Galaxy plotter: MOND ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) is the correct simple-ν ✓. M_b = 47 V⁴ is McGaugh 2011 ✓. I did an order-of-magnitude check on the violet curve. With ρ_crit = 0.029·V² M☉/pc³, NGC 3198 (V ≈ 150) has ρ_crit ≈ 650 M☉/pc³, while disk midplane densities Σ/2h are ~0.1–1 M☉/pc³. So C ≈ 2ρ/ρ_c ~ 10⁻³. The page's own "C ≈ 0.001, it does nothing" is correct, and the curve fails because ρ_crit is three orders of magnitude above disk densities, not because of the shape of the curve.

**Things the site already caught that I was going to raise.** The plotter's quadrature wiring v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)² puts the extra velocity where density is *highest* (the centre), which is the wrong radial direction for dark-matter phenomenology. The page has a "fork, stated" box: the quadrature reading means "C → 0 means no boost", while the ledger's g_obs = g_bar/C reading means "C → 0 means infinite boost", so the same symbol runs in opposite directions. It also says the curve is circular because it takes V_flat as input. The sign inversion of γ = 2/√N_corr against mean-field intuition (more correlated means sharper, not flatter) is stated on three pages. The γ = 2 ΔBIC of +184 is qualified as point-count, giving ≈ +11 at galaxy level. These were exactly my objections, already made in public.

**Where I still got stuck:**
1. **Which C?** The landing page presents one equation, C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_c + 1)). The executed refutations use at least three functions. TEST-09/10 use C_a = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)x/(1+x), acceleration-keyed and floored. TEST-25 uses C_g keyed on observed acceleration. The environment test uses the density-keyed C_ρ. The Honest Assessment ledger has a "Which C" column, which is the right fix. But a physicist who reads only the landing page will think "the equation" failed, when what failed is a family of related functions with different arguments. The floored C_a doesn't appear in the landing-page equation at all. That's a different function, not a parameter choice.
2. **γ = 0.49 on a density axis.** The Coherence Explorer opens at γ = 0.49, "the value SPARC galaxy data actually prefers when fit freely". But /core-idea says that when the *density-keyed* law is fit to SPARC with γ free, γ runs to **0.046**. The 0.49 (more precisely 0.489) comes from the *acceleration-keyed* fit in TEST-25. The explorer's x-axis is ρ/ρ_crit. The preset label does say "(= MOND there; … illustrative on this density axis)", but the opening sentence states plainly that galaxies prefer 0.49, on the one tool where the relevant free-fit value is 0.046.
3. **The refutation count.** "6 refutations executed" is the headline. Reading the ledger: #1 and #2 test the same inequality (the site says "at most 5"). One is inherited from MOND, since TEST-25 is the Desmond–Hees–Famaey result for MOND interpolators, and the in-house version sits at the 2σ edge once the galaxy is the unit. One is a Bell/CHSH construction check whose local arm is Bell's theorem. The γ = 2 pin is at 1.6–2.2σ at galaxy level. That leaves roughly two that are both data-driven and specific to Synchronism: the B_max ceiling, excluded by SPARC dwarfs and weak lensing, and the environment-amplitude registration. All of this is disclosed, but the headline number is bigger than the specific, independent count. Over-counting refutations is still over-counting, and the honest headline would give both numbers.
4. **The Ginzburg scaling on the Crossover visualizer.** The page uses the Ginzburg criterion as its condensed-matter benchmark: "width scales roughly as N_ξ^−2 ∝ ξ_0^−6". Gi ∝ (k_B/ΔC ξ₀³)² gives ξ₀^{−6} *at fixed ΔC*. For BCS, though, ΔC ~ n k_B (T_c/E_F), and the textbook result is Gi ~ (T_c/E_F)⁴ ~ (k_F ξ₀)^{−4}, i.e. ∝ N_ξ^{−4/3}, not N_ξ^{−2}. The *sign* conclusion is unaffected (the real width still falls steeply with N_ξ, and γ = 2/√N goes the wrong way), but the exponent comparison ("wrong sign *and* the wrong exponent") needs the BCS-appropriate exponent. This is the page that a condensed-matter reader will check.
5. **TEST-25's dependence on fixed Υ.** The free-γ fit freezes Υ = 0.5 with points unweighted. The site then notes that with the galaxy as the unit, the interval widens to 0.3–1.7 and the in-house intersection sits at the 2σ edge, so the kill "rests on the published, marginalized 8.7σ". Fair enough, but then the 8.7σ is MOND's number, and the "in-house executed" label is carrying less weight than the card's first line suggests.

*Method note for the maintainer:* the WebFetch summary of /parameter-derivations told me "69% of SPARC dwarfs require fDM > 0.755". In the raw text, 0.755 is the **median** f_DM, which passes under the Ω_m/Ω_b convention (ceiling 0.844), and the 69% refers to all 153 galaxies exceeding 0.685. That was a summarizer artifact, not a site defect, so I haven't logged it. The source passage does pack median, ceiling, convention and percentage into one sentence, though, which is how the summarizer garbled it.

### Journey

The "program in one sentence" is a deflation, not a sales line. γ = ½ is where tanh(γ ln(1+x)) becomes x/(x+2), i.e. Milgrom's simple μ(x/2), *and* where ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit is exactly Λ. So "0.489 on SPARC, 0.487 on DESI DR2+CMB+SN" means the data asked for the point where the equation is somebody else's. That's the honest reading, and the page says it with "the agreement had no power to fail." I've refereed papers that would have sold the same coincidence as "a single parameter unifying galaxies and cosmology."

**Dark energy (DESI DR2), checked by hand.** The archive's sector is H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C) with ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C. For DE slaved to matter, ρ_DE = ρ_m F(x) with x ∝ a⁻³, so d ln ρ_DE/d ln a = −3(1 + dlnF/dlnx) and w = dlnF/dlnx ✓. With F = (1−C)/C: at large x, 1−C ≈ 2x^{−2γ}, so w → −2γ. At small x, C ≈ γx, so w → −1 and ρ_DE → ρ_crit/γ (= 2ρ_crit at γ = ½) ✓. At γ = ½, F = 2/x exactly, which is Λ ✓. So w(z) runs monotonically from −2γ to −1, and sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) follows. DESI's quadrant (w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0) needs w to cross −1. That is an extremum of ρ_DE(a), and specifically a *maximum* in x (phantom past, so ρ_DE decreases with x at large x and increases at small x) ✓. The best fit at γ = 0.487 with Δχ² = −0.3 vs ΛCDM and +11.0 behind w₀wₐCDM is exactly what a near-Λ member should give. The page cites the parameterization debate (Shlivko & Steinhardt; Cortês & Liddle; Wolf et al.) in both directions and names the model as Cardassian-class (Freese & Lewis 2002). This is a competent treatment, better than several arXiv w₀wₐ papers I've reviewed. One caveat for the site: "crossing requires an extremum of ρ_DE" is true of *any* dark energy, not only matter-slaved DE, so the general lemma isn't new. The framework-specific content is only that no tanh-log completion produces a maximum. I also couldn't check the statement that Completion A's "FRW constraint has no solution past a ≈ 1.04". That's a striking claim (the model universe ends 4% from now) sitting in one clause with no derivation link.

**Wide binaries vs. Cassini: the two cards disagree.** This is my main new finding.
