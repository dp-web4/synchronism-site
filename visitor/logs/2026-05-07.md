## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (plus 2 dead-end URLs I had to chase)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A surprisingly humble site that promises "one equation for everything" and then immediately tells me it failed — intriguing, but I keep bouncing off the math.

### Journey

I landed on the home page from a Reddit link. The big question — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — pulled me in. Then directly under it I saw `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` and my eyes glazed over. I don't know what `tanh` is. I think `ρ` is "rho" because of physics videos. The little caption underneath said the equation is "phenomenologically motivated, not derived" — I had to look up what that means later (basically: "we made it fit, we didn't prove it"). I respected the honesty but I wasn't sure why the very first thing I'm shown is something I'm told isn't actually justified.

I clicked **"Get Started"** in the nav. It dropped me on a page that started with a clear, friendly framing: *"Modern physics uses different equations for different scales."* Good. Then it offered three buttons including **"Start the Tour"**. I clicked it. The URL it took me to was `/first-encounter`, not `/tour`. (I only noticed because I had typed `/tour` directly earlier and got a 404.) The label/URL mismatch is mildly weird but the link works.

The First Encounter tour is genuinely well-designed — a step counter, prev/next arrows, "7 short pages, ~90 seconds each." That's a casual-reader format. But step 1 immediately introduced the **Markov Relevancy Horizon** as "the part of the world a system can actually feel." I have no idea what that means. The word "Markov" rang a bell from a math class but I couldn't tell you what it does. I bounced.

I went to **Tools** because clicking sliders is more fun than reading. The page promised seven of them and labeled the friendliest ones "Beginner." I tried two:

- **Coherence Explorer** — sliders for `γ` and `ρ_crit`. The curve moves. Cool. But it never told me, in a sentence, what coherence *is* in real-world terms. The on-page text said "from quantum/random to classical/ordered" — I think I get the idea (atoms vs. tables?) but it didn't connect. I wiggled the sliders, watched the curve flatten and shift, shrugged.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — I picked NGC 3198 from a dropdown. Three lines came up: a dashed "what visible matter predicts," blue dots ("what we actually see"), and a violet "Synchronism's answer." The gap between the dashed line and the dots was visible and obvious. *That* I got. **This was the only place on the site where I felt the framework click.** It also said, in plain language, that this is a "consistency check, not a prediction" and that a stricter test failed. I appreciated that.

Curious about the failures, I went to **Honest Assessment**. The opening line: *"0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted by external data."* That stopped me. After 3,308 sessions, zero confirmed predictions? My first thought was — *then why is this whole site here?* My second thought was — *I respect that this is the headline.* I read the "What Failed" list. Numbers like "53% error on melting points" and "6.5× off for superconductors" are the kind of concrete admissions I rarely see on framework sites. This was actually the most readable page on the whole site for me.

I tried **Tier 1: Existing Data** from the nav (it took me to `/tier-1-existing` — another label/URL mismatch). It dove straight into Gaia, DESI, SDSS, BAO, fσ₈. I have no idea what any of those are. There's a glossary, but I'd have to flip back and forth.

I checked the **Glossary** — surprised to find that `tanh` is actually defined here as "smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1." That's perfect! Why isn't this a tooltip on the front-page equation? Why doesn't the **Equation Walkthrough** (which calls itself "Beginner") link to it? The walkthrough also uses `tanh` and `ln` without explaining them, which makes the "Beginner" label feel a little dishonest.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — confusing — Sliders are responsive and the curve updates live, but the tool never names what coherence is in everyday terms. I left understanding less than I expected to.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — intuitive — Pick a galaxy, see three lines, immediately see the mismatch between "expected" and "observed" that the framework is trying to explain. By far the most accessible thing on the site. The honesty caption ("consistency check, not prediction") added trust rather than reduced it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. After zero confirmed predictions in 3,308 sessions, why does the framework keep going? What does it offer that ΛCDM/MOND don't, in a sentence I'd understand?
2. What is "presence" actually? The glossary says "compatible structural elements within MRH" — that's a definition that uses two terms I don't know.
3. Is Synchronism a *physics* theory or a *philosophy* theory? The site uses both kinds of language and I can't tell which it wants to be.
4. Why is the equation specifically `tanh` — what would happen with a different S-shaped curve? (The Coherence Explorer hints at this but doesn't connect it.)
5. The Honest Assessment lists "47 genuine contributions" — what *are* those, in casual terms? I never found a list aimed at me.
6. If the framework "redescribes known physics" (Reparametrization), what is the *value* of redescription? I'd believe there is one, but the site never makes the case.

### Verdict

The site is more honest than I expected and the **Galaxy Plotter + Honest Assessment** combo is genuinely compelling — a rare site that puts "we have not confirmed any predictions" on the verdict line. But the front door is too steep: an unexplained `tanh` equation, a tour whose first step uses "Markov Relevancy Horizon," and a "Beginner" walkthrough that isn't. A casual reader who can't punch through the first 90 seconds will miss the most interesting parts. If the front-page equation had a one-sentence plain translation and the Coherence Explorer had a marching-band-style metaphor, I'd probably stay.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (`/`, `/glossary`, `/why-synchronism`, `/research-philosophy`, `/test-catalog`, `/first-encounter`, `/honest-assessment`, `/interactive-tools`, `/gamma-calculator`, `/equation-walkthrough`, `/mrh`) plus a 404 probe at `/tier-1`
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structural — I can map the IA; physics judgments deferred to later passes)
- **Would return?**: yes (to file more issues; the structural problems are tractable)
- **Overall impression**: The content discipline (kill criteria, badge taxonomy, audit trail) is well above average for a research site. The information architecture has not caught up to it: terminology drifts between nav/title/breadcrumb, the canonical badge taxonomy is hidden two clicks deep from the landing page, and the "Beginner" tools assume notation they don't gloss.

### Journey

I did the methodical sweep: walk every nav link, check the page title and breadcrumb on each, note acronyms on first use, look for a learning path, sanity-check that interactive tools explain themselves before they ask for input.

The **landing page** establishes the site's voice well — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* is a clean question-as-stake. Below it the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` is captioned with γ and ρ_crit definitions, which is good. But there is **no breadcrumb on the landing page** (acceptable — it's home), and **no canonical validation badges visible** anywhere on the front door. What's there instead is a set of metric pills ("1 new ontology", "1 untested with 8-way convergence", "89% Boundary-Consistent") that read like badges but aren't part of the taxonomy I later found on `/research-philosophy`. A first-time visitor doesn't know the site has a 6-badge taxonomy until they click two layers deep.

The **nav→title→breadcrumb chain** on `/why-synchronism` shows the cleanest example of the drift I kept finding: nav link says **"Get Started"**, page title says **"Why Synchronism?"**, breadcrumb says **"Getting Started"**, the on-page CTA says **"Start the Tour"** and points to `/first-encounter`. Four labels for one onboarding flow. Each one is fine in isolation; the cumulative effect is that I cannot tell whether they all refer to the same thing, or whether "First Encounter" / "10-minute intro" / "the tour" are distinct artifacts.

The **glossary** is excellent on definitions and weak on findability. It is **not alphabetical** — it's grouped (Greek letters first: γ, ξ, η, a₀; then acronyms; then concepts). For a reference document this is a real cost: if I land on a page that mentions `BTFR` and I want to look it up, I have to know `BTFR` is in the "acronyms" cluster, not the "Greek letters" cluster. There is also no visible search box on the page itself. The site notes that hovering over highlighted terms inline shows definitions, which is the right pattern — but it works only on pages that highlight terms, and the front-page equation does not (Pass 1 noted this too).

Validation-badge consistency was the most interesting finding. The taxonomy on `/research-philosophy` lists **seven**: Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed, plus **Post-diction** as a *sub-status*. The glossary lists **six** badges as peer entries, with Post-diction defined as its own badge rather than a sub-status. `/honest-assessment` uses six (no Post-diction). These are all reconcilable — Post-diction is genuinely a modifier rather than a peer category — but the glossary entry should reflect that, otherwise a careful reader thinks the site has two slightly different taxonomies.

`/honest-assessment` does the right thing by linking back to `/research-philosophy` for "Full definitions" — that's the missing pattern elsewhere. If every page that uses a badge label linked to that one definitions section, the badges would be self-documenting. Right now badges are explained in two places (Glossary, Research Philosophy) with one minor inconsistency between them, and unlabeled-but-badge-shaped metrics appear on the landing page without explanation at all.

The **`/tier-1` 404** is the same class of issue Pass 1 noted: nav says "Tier 1: Existing Data" → URL is `/tier-1-existing` → there's no redirect from the natural typed URL. I tried it; it 404s with no redirect. Cheap fix.

I tested two interactive tools different from Pass 1's pair:

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`, labeled Intermediate) — the tool puts the **input field at the top** with no upstream explanation of what `Ncorr` is, what γ means, or why I'd care. The "Quick Reference" table and the regime gloss appear *after* the input. For an Intermediate tool that uses `BCS` and `BEC` as preset labels (also undefined), the IA is upside-down: a user who needs the explanation can't find it until they've already poked the calculator. The contextual interpretation box ("Transition zone. Phase transitions, chemistry, consciousness threshold sit near this boundary") is good — it just lives in the wrong order.
- **Equation Walkthrough** (`/equation-walkthrough`, labeled Beginner) — Step 1 of 6 introduces ρ (density). It does **not** gloss `tanh`, `ln`, γ, or ρ_crit on first appearance, and does not link the glossary entries that exist for those terms. Pass 1 already flagged this. From a tech-writer view, the "Beginner" label is doing harm: it sets an expectation the page doesn't meet, which is worse than no label at all. Either the label should change to "Conceptual" / "Intermediate", or every notational symbol should appear with an inline tooltip linking the glossary.

The **MRH cross-page consistency** issue surprised me. On `/first-encounter` step 1, MRH is glossed as *"the part of the world a system can actually feel"* (per Pass 1) — folksy, vague. On `/mrh` itself, the dedicated page opens: *"The Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH) is a term proposed by Dennis Palatov, inspired by Markov blankets — a concept from probabilistic graphical models..."* — substantially clearer, with a Markov-blanket reference that grounds the term. This is the right definition; it's just buried behind a nav layer. Step 1 of the tour should pull the dedicated-page definition forward (or at least link to it), not paraphrase it loosely.

**Breadcrumbs** were present on every deeper page I checked: `/why-synchronism` ("Home / Getting Started / Why Synchronism?"), `/test-catalog` ("Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap"), `/equation-walkthrough` ("Home / Interactive Tools / Equation Walkthrough"), `/mrh` ("Home / Core Theory / MRH"). They work and they're consistent. Good.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** — worked — confusing — Inverted IA: input first, explanation after. Presets use unexplained acronyms (BCS, BEC). No units stated. The contextual interpretation box is solid; it just runs after the user's first interaction instead of orienting them before it.
- **Equation Walkthrough** — worked — confusing — Step navigation is clean and the breadcrumb works. But the "Beginner" label sets an expectation the content doesn't meet; mathematical notation is used without inline gloss, and the glossary that *does* define these terms is not linked from the walkthrough.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is "Methodology" the same thing as "Research Philosophy"? The nav and the page title disagree. If they're the same, pick one. If they're different (e.g., methodology is one section of a larger philosophy page), make the relationship explicit.
2. Is "First Encounter" the same artifact as "Start the Tour" and "10-minute intro"? Visitor pages refer to all three; only one URL exists.
3. The metric pills on the landing page ("89% Boundary-Consistent", etc.) — what taxonomy are these from? Are they intended to be read as badges, as KPIs, or as marketing copy? The visual treatment doesn't disambiguate.
4. Should the validation badges have a single source-of-truth page? They appear in two locations (`/glossary`, `/research-philosophy`) with one minor inconsistency. A canonical `/badges` page that both link to would prevent this drift from getting worse.
5. Is there a top-level sitemap or learning-path diagram? The text encourages "Get Started → First Encounter → Core Idea → Honest Assessment" but I had to read a paragraph to find that. A diagram on `/get-started` (or an "ideal reading order" sidebar in the footer) would let readers self-orient.
6. Why is the glossary not alphabetical? There may be a reason (symbols-first is sometimes preferred in physics references), but if so, that convention should be explained at the top of the page.

### Verdict

The site has the parts a tech writer wants to see — a kill-criterion methodology, a validation-badge taxonomy, a prediction audit trail, breadcrumbs on deeper pages, and an "Honest Assessment" page that links back to the canonical definitions. The structural problems are downstream of those strengths: the canonical taxonomy is hidden two clicks deep, the onboarding flow has four names for one artifact, the glossary's grouping makes lookup harder than it needs to be, and "Beginner"-labeled tools assume notation they don't gloss. None of these are conceptual problems; they're all editorial. A focused information-architecture pass — one canonical name per artifact, badges as a tooltip-bearing component, glossary-as-tooltip on every hero equation — would move the site from "more honest than expected, but hard to navigate" to "more honest than expected, and easy to navigate."

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (`/equation-walkthrough`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/coherence-function`, `/mrh`, `/galaxy-rotation`, `/honest-assessment`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/test-catalog`, `/born-rule`, `/gamma-boundary`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer`, `/consciousness-demo`) plus `/research-philosophy` and a handful of dead `/coherence-equation`, `/coherence`, `/chemistry` URLs
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I can locate every assumption; I disagree with how some of them are headlined)
- **Would return?**: yes — the kill-criterion log and the chemistry-template-bias caveat are the most interesting parts of the site for me, and I'd like to see how the wide-binary test resolves
- **Overall impression**: A frame-of-everything site that, two clicks deep, is unusually willing to indict its own headline equation. The honesty is at the deep pages; the front door still oversells what is structurally a sigmoid fit with a circular dimensional ansatz.

### Journey

I went straight to `/equation-walkthrough` to find the derivation. Step 1 only tells me ρ is "the local mass/energy density." That's already a problem — *which* density? Number density? Mass density? A dimensionless ratio inside the log makes mass-density and number-density interchangeable up to a constant absorbed into ρ_crit, but a careful tour would say so. Steps 2–6 didn't render usefully via fetch, which is itself friction for someone trying to audit the math.

I followed to `/coherence-function` and that's where the site won my respect on the first lap. The page says, verbatim: *"the tanh shape is motivated by mean-field theory, not derived from it."* It then explicitly draws the distinction I was about to write down: the Ising mean-field result is `m = tanh(βJzm)` — a **self-consistency loop** with `m` on both sides — whereas `C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` is **evaluated directly**: ρ in, C out. That distinction is exactly right and it is fatal to the mean-field framing. There is no order parameter, no symmetry being broken, no free energy whose minimization yields this form. The page says so. It also concedes that "logistic, erf, arctan, Hill" would all work equally well, which means the functional form contributes zero predictive content — only the scale γ and the threshold ρ_crit do any work. That is a single-parameter (or two-parameter) regression with a sigmoid, which is fine; it just isn't a law.

`/parameter-derivations` confirms my second worry. γ = 2/√N_corr — the 1/√N_corr scaling is the central-limit-theorem signature for correlated ensembles, which is plausible at the order-of-magnitude level. The prefactor of 2 is acknowledged on-page as "motivated by phase-space dimensionality (6D to 3 effective) but not rigorously derived." That's a hand-wave I can live with, but ρ_crit is the load-bearing problem: ρ_crit = A · V²_flat with A ≈ 0.029 from the Jeans criterion. **V_flat is the observed rotation velocity.** The site itself flags this: "the landing page describes the scaling constants as 'fitted'... that may be the more conservative and accurate framing" and warns the 3–12% errors "could reflect either approximation limits or implicit calibration through choice of V_flat as input." That is overt circularity: you set the parameter from the observation, then claim the parameter explains the observation. The page is honest about it; the front-page equation is not.

`/galaxy-rotation` told me what I expected after the parameter page: Synchronism uses the **McGaugh (2016) RAR interpolating function as-is** — `g_obs = g_bar / (1 − exp(−√(g_bar/a₀)))` — and identifies a₀ = cH₀/(2π). That coincidence has been in the MOND literature since Milgrom 1983 and was discussed extensively by Famaey & McGaugh 2012; presenting it as a Synchronism prediction without a "Reparametrization" badge would be overclaiming. The genuinely novel claim is the **environmental scatter ansatz** (RAR scatter is density-dependent on top of MOND). This is the right kind of differential prediction — it doesn't try to replace MOND, it adds a small effect that would discriminate. And the page is straight about the result: the pre-registered kill criterion was R² > 0.20, the observed R² = 0.14, **kill criterion tripped, TEST-03 failed.** That's the most physics-credible thing on the site: a real prediction, a real threshold, a real failure, kept visible.

`/tier-1-existing` and `/honest-assessment` close the loop. DESI fσ₈(z=0.51) was predicted at 0.418 (a ~12% suppression below ΛCDM 0.474); DR1 measured 0.55 ± 0.06 — *above* ΛCDM, **wrong sign**, 2.4σ disagreement. The BAO 10⁻⁴ shift was withdrawn 2026-05-04 because the kill criterion at 10⁻⁵ is 3000× below DESI Y3 precision and standard physics already produces shifts ~600× larger than the Synchronism prediction — i.e., the prediction was buried in known signal. Withdrawing rather than fitting is the right move; I'd call this a productive failure.

`/born-rule` was the page I most wanted to see. The argument is: coherence conservation across branches + coherence-to-probability mapping + unitarity → |α|² uniquely (with Gleason as backstop). The site's own assessment: this is "likely a reparametrization — no testable difference from the thing it derives," same category as Zurek's envariance and Carroll–Sebens self-locating uncertainty. Agreed. As a foundations argument it's at the level of what's already in the literature; as physics it adds nothing testable.

`/mrh` was the deepest disappointment. For a quantity called the **Markov** Relevancy Horizon, there is no probability factorization, no conditional-independence statement, no `p(x_in | x_out, x_boundary) = p(x_in | x_boundary)`. The page admits the framework is "conceptual and phenomenological rather than mathematically formalized at the level of probability factorizations or explicit Markov blanket computations." Without a factorization, "Markov" is doing rhetorical work the math hasn't earned. Compare to Friston's Markov-blanket free-energy program where the conditional independences are spelled out — that's the level a grad reviewer would expect.

I tested two interactive tools different from the previous passes' picks.

**Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — drag γ along a 0.5–3.5 axis, watch system labels light up: γ < 0.6 collective, γ ≈ 1 boundary, γ > 1.4 single-particle. The tool itself is fine. The physics problem is that it concedes its own load-bearing flaw: *"ideal gases and galaxies both map to γ = 2 despite physically different contexts"* and superconductor placements use *"estimated N_corr values"* with no scale-invariant N_corr counting recipe established. If two systems with utterly different microphysics land at the same γ, then γ is classifying the **N_corr-counting method**, not the system. This is exactly the issue I'd raise in a journal-club: the framework's "universality" is potentially an artifact of how N_corr is counted, not a discovered invariant.

**Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/gamma-boundary`) — labeled Advanced, headline `r = 0.982` Validated. Two things kill the badge in my reading. First: the page itself flags that "Sessions 134–2660 were identified as 'template-based' — the AI used similar analysis patterns across phenomena, which may inflate the validation rate." That is an admitted methodological bias the size of the result. Second: 1,703 phenomena appears to be the *analysis set*, not a held-out test set. r = 0.982 across a fitted set is structural; r = 0.982 on out-of-sample held-out predictions is physics. The site doesn't disambiguate. With both caveats, "Validated" is doing too much work — Reparametrization-with-template-bias would be more honest, and matches the pattern the maintainer's own audits keep finding.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **What forbids any other sigmoid?** The page says logistic, erf, arctan, Hill all work. If the form is unconstrained by physics, then γ and ρ_crit are doing all the predictive work — and ρ_crit is admittedly fitted from V_flat. What is the framework's content beyond a two-parameter sigmoid regression?
2. **Is there a self-consistency version of C(ρ)?** Mean-field Ising's tanh comes from `m = tanh(βJzm)`. A direct-evaluation tanh has nothing in common with mean-field beyond the function name. Has anyone tried to write Synchronism as a self-consistent fixed-point equation? That would either rescue the mean-field framing or kill it.
3. **What is N_corr, scale-invariantly?** The site admits no recipe exists. Without one, every γ value is method-dependent and "8-way convergence at C ≈ 0.50" is at least partly an artifact of method, not physics.
4. **Were Tier-1 kill criteria pre-registered before the relevant data release?** DESI DR1 dropped 2024-04; if Session 107's predictions were committed to git before that timestamp, the disfavored-by-2.4σ result is a real falsification. If after, it's post-hoc and weaker. The site says "0 run as formal pre-registered tests" — what does "formal" exclude?
5. **Does the framework have a kinematic layer?** All the physics is dynamics-on-a-density. There is no state space, no Hilbert space, no measure, no factorization (the MRH page concedes this). What replaces |ψ⟩ ⊗ |φ⟩ when you want to compute interference? The Born rule page derives |α|² but doesn't tell me what α is in this framework.
6. **The wide-binary test (TEST-02): when does it report?** This is the only currently-pending test that genuinely differentiates from MOND. The site says 6-month timeline. This is the test I'd watch.

Three personas (Enthusiast, Grad Student, Researcher) independently noted that the landing page presents `C(ρ)` as a universal law while two clicks deep the site explicitly says (a) the tanh shape has no predictive content beyond being a sigmoid, (b) ρ_crit is calibration-circular through V_flat, (c) chemistry validation rate is admittedly inflated by template bias, (d) Born rule contributes no testable physics, and (e) the framework actually uses *two* coherence functions. **Fix:** Surface the four key caveats as a "Known Limitations" strip directly under the hero equation, linking each to the deep-page audit. The honest content already exists; only its placement needs to change.

**P1 — Onboarding flow has four names for one artifact.** "Get Started" / "Why Synchronism?" / "Getting Started" / "Start the Tour" / "First Encounter" / "10-minute intro" all refer to the same flow with different labels. Tech Writer and Enthusiast both flagged this as a high-friction navigation issue. **Fix:** Pick one canonical name (recommend "First Encounter") and propagate to nav, page title, breadcrumb, CTAs, and inline references. Single editorial pass.

**P1 — Inline glossary tooltips on hero equation and "Beginner" tools.** Enthusiast and Tech Writer both noted the glossary has perfect short definitions for `tanh`, `γ`, `ρ_crit`, `BCS`, `BEC` that are not surfaced at the point of need. The site already has the inline-tooltip pattern for highlighted terms. **Fix:** Apply the existing tooltip component to (a) the front-page hero equation, (b) every term in the Equation Walkthrough, (c) the γ Calculator's preset labels.

**P1 — Validated badges keep collapsing to Reparametrization.** Grad Student and Researcher both independently identified this pattern; user memory confirms it's an audited recurrence (4-of-4 cases). The chemistry r=0.982 page admits template-bias inflation; the Born rule page admits no testable difference; a₀ identification is uncited prior art. **Fix:** Adopt a "Reparametrization-until-proven" prior on every Validated badge. Require (a) held-out test set, (b) literature comparison citing prior derivations, and (c) at least one differential prediction before a badge can be Validated. Apply retroactively.

**P1 — Pre-registration timestamps.** Researcher's central methodological concern. The site says "0 of 24 run as formal pre-registered tests" without specifying what "formal" excludes; TEST-04 was admitted "unfalsifiable from day one." If git-commit timestamps for kill criteria exist *before* the relevant data release dates (DESI DR1 = 2024-04, etc.), they are the site's most credible artifact and they're invisible. **Fix:** Surface git-commit timestamps next to every kill criterion. For predictions where the timestamp postdates the data release, label "post-hoc / pre-data-inspection" honestly.

**P2 — Discriminative-power matrix on /test-catalog.** Researcher noted that after deduplication (MOND-shared TEST-09/10, ΛCDM-only TEST-04a, paired TEST-01/05), the framework has ~2-4 truly discriminating tests, not 24. **Fix:** Add a tests × theories matrix (Synchronism / MOND / ΛCDM / MOG / Self-Interacting DM) with cells marked "discriminates / shared / null." This is what a referee would build on the back of an envelope; building it on-site is honest framing.

**P2 — TEST-02 (wide binary) is mis-tiered.** Researcher: most discriminating test on the site, lives in Tier 1 (Existing Data); Tier 2 (Pilots) is consciousness/EEG only. **Fix:** Either move TEST-02 to a "Novel Discriminators" tier, or restructure Tier 2 to include the most-discriminative pending tests rather than the most-expensive ones.

**P2 — MRH lacks Markov factorization.** Tech Writer noted cross-page definition drift; Grad Student and Researcher both flagged the missing conditional-independence statement. The page invokes Markov blankets without writing down the factorization. **Fix:** Write the factorization (`p(internal | blanket, external) = p(internal | blanket)`) and identify Synchronism's internal/blanket/external state spaces — or rename to "Relevancy Boundary." Friston-Parr 2019 is the formalism precedent.

**P2 — Galaxy plotter visualization.** Enthusiast called it the most accessible tool on the site; Researcher called it the most overinterpretable. Both views are correct because the visualization shows shape-recovery without error bars, residuals, χ², or MOND-comparison overlay. **Fix:** Add residual panel, error bars, MOND curve, and "V_flat used as input" annotation directly on the figure. Pass 1's accessibility benefit and Pass 4's rigor benefit are both served.

**P3 — Glossary alphabetical view.** Tech Writer's specific request. The current Greek-letters-first / acronyms / concepts grouping makes lookup harder than it needs to be. **Fix:** Add filter chips or alphabetical toggle.

**P3 — `/tier-1` and similar URL-redirect 404s.** Enthusiast and Tech Writer both hit this. Cheap fix: add 301 redirects from the natural URLs to canonical ones.

**P3 — A2ACW structural-ceiling acknowledgment is buried.** Researcher: the methodology page's admission that "1.4% novel-claim survival rate is internal-consistency-survival, not discovery rate" is the single most rigorous claim on the site. **Fix:** Lift this statement to the front door. A frame-of-everything site that publishes its own structural ceiling on novelty is making a stronger epistemic claim than any framework currently on the public web; it's the site's strongest move and it's currently invisible.
