# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-08-19

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, why-synchronism, first-encounter, core-idea, interactive-tools, coherence-explorer, galaxy-plotter, equation-walkthrough, consciousness-demo, glossary; honest-assessment skimmed)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understood the *idea* in about two minutes and admired the honesty, but by page three I couldn't tell what I was supposed to do with a site whose every exhibit is a label explaining why the exhibit doesn't work.

### Journey

Clicked in from Reddit expecting either crackpottery or something cool. Got neither, which was itself interesting.

**Landing page.** The headline is "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" and directly under it is "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" My brain read those in the wrong order and got whiplash — the big text tells me it's *not* the exciting thing, then the smaller text asks the exciting question. Then paragraph two tells me the central hypothesis failed. That's three tone changes before I've scrolled.

I want to be clear that I don't mind the honesty. What I mind is that nothing on the landing page told me why I should keep reading *given* that it failed. "Come see a thing that didn't work" needs a hook, and there wasn't one. Something like "here's what we learned by being wrong in public" would have kept me. Instead the very first thing I got was a scoreboard reading **0 confirmed predictions**. I nearly bounced right there and I think most Reddit clickers do.

The equation is displayed straight away: **C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**. All five symbols are defined right underneath, which is genuinely good — I've seen popsci sites drop equations with no key at all. "tanh" meant nothing to me but "S-shaped saturation curve" plus later "dimmer switch" got me there.

**Beginner Path Step 1 (Why Synchronism?).** The marching band vs. a crowd in a plaza analogy is the single best thing on the site. I got it instantly and I'd repeat it to a friend. Density goes up → the crowd starts moving as one thing. Great.

Then, still on Step 1, I hit: "Environment-dependent predictions largely refuted by execution (r²=0.0001)" and the γ relation being flagged **audited-negative**. I don't know what r² is. I don't know what audited-negative is. This is step one of six on a path explicitly labelled for beginners and it's already showing me its own audit trail. I skimmed the rest.

**Step 2 (First Encounter).** Better. "Presence" = how much stuff is near enough to matter, and the **MRH** = the bubble of neighbours that count, everything outside gets ignored. The isolated-electron vs. neutron-star contrast is vivid. This page is the one that actually taught me something. I'd move it in front of Step 1.

**Step 3 (Core Idea).** Lost me. It says the curve's real midpoint sits at 0.32·ρcrit, then hands me `tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]` and says this makes it identical to "Hill-type saturation functions." I don't know what a Hill function is and the page doesn't say. I understand *that* this is meant to be a deflating discovery; I don't understand *why* it deflates.

**Tools.** See below. I played with two properly and looked at a third.

**Consciousness demo** — this is the page I clicked fastest, because of course it is. Eight different methods all landing on C ≈ 0.50, mean 0.499, spread 0.012. For about ten seconds I thought "whoa." Then the page tells me the agreement is geometric, not empirical: 0.50 is just the middle of the 0-to-1 range, so of course everything lands there. That's a good, clear self-take-down and I followed it completely. It's also the clearest writing on the site — whoever wrote "consistent with the threshold being real AND with it being a mathematical artifact" should write the landing page.

**Honest Assessment** — I clicked this because the landing page lists it under "Summary." It is not a summary. My rough sense is it's a dozen sections and something like 12–15 thousand words, full of BTFR, RAR, σ₈, SPARC, CHSH. I read the first screen and left. Calling it "Summary" on the front page is a bait-and-switch even if unintentional.

**Glossary** — 72 terms, alphabetical, and the definitions are *good*. "Presence = how much compatible stuff is nearby." "Reparametrization = known physics in new notation." "MRH = an event horizon for influence." If these plain-language versions appeared inline the first time each term is used, half my friction disappears. I only found the glossary because I went looking.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (/coherence-explorer) — worked — mostly intuitive — Two sliders, γ and ρcrit, a curve, a C=0.50 line. I understood immediately that γ changes how steep the S is and that ρcrit slides it sideways. That's a good tool. The caveat box then tells me γ runs "backwards" relative to real physics — superconductors, which have famously sharp transitions, get the flattest curves here. I could actually follow that, and it's the clearest failure explanation on the site. But: the default γ is 0.49 with no note on why, and the readouts (0.3272 / 0.8259 / 0.9785) are just three bare numbers with no sentence telling me what to notice about them.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — worked — confusing — Five real galaxies in a dropdown, and the core puzzle (visible matter says the curve should droop; the dots say it stays flat) is genuinely visible at a glance. That part is excellent. Everything after that assumes I know what a rotation curve is, what kpc means, and what a "boost" is. And the hand-tuned amber curve sitting on the same axes as the real prediction is a trap — it's the curve that *fits*, and it's the one my eye goes to.

- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (/consciousness-demo) — worked — intuitive — Eight bars converging on 0.50, hover for method. Visually satisfying and then honestly dismantled. Best-explained tool on the site; also the only one where I understood both the claim and the objection without help.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the theory failed, what did we actually learn? The site tells me *that* it failed six ways but never tells me what the failures rule out for anyone else. That's the sentence I'd need to justify sending this to a friend.
2. Why is it 14,610 galaxies on one page and 14,760 on the next?
3. What is "coherence" actually a measure *of*? The glossary says it's about acting collectively, but a page told me superconductors — the most collective thing I've heard of — score *low*. So does high coherence mean ordered or disordered? I finished the site genuinely unsure.
4. What are steps 4, 5, and 6 of the Beginner Path?
5. What's a "boost"? It's in the galaxy plotter legend, the honest assessment ("boost ceiling"), and I never found a plain-English definition.
6. If tanh was chosen because it fits and not because theory demands it — which the site says outright, twice — was there ever a reason to expect this to work? I couldn't tell whether that admission is a small caveat or the whole story.

### Verdict

I came for "one equation explains everything" and got an autopsy, which is more interesting than I expected and less useful than I hoped. The honesty is real and unusual — the consciousness demo talking itself out of its own best result is the most memorable thing I've read on a science site in a while — but the site is organised as an audit, not as an explanation, and a casual reader gets handed the audit before the explanation. Fix two things and I'd stay: tell me on the front page why a failed theory is worth my ten minutes, and stop putting statistics in the beginner path.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 (landing, learning-paths, first-encounter, core-idea, what-synchronism-is-not, mrh, glossary, interactive-tools, gamma-calculator, phase-boundary-visualizer, chemistry-correlation-explorer, test-catalog, tier-1-existing, honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: good

- **Overall impression**: The navigation layer is better than most documentation sites I audit — breadcrumbs, prev/next, and dual-path step indicators all work — but the controlled vocabulary underneath it has grown to ~29 status tokens across five families with no single-source legend, and the site's most load-bearing word ("coherence") is disclaimed three pages after it's introduced.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set: walk the nav, check every label resolves, check terminology against a glossary, check whether a reader arriving cold at a deep page can orient.

**Navigation — genuinely good, and I want to say so before I start complaining.** Every content page I opened carried a breadcrumb (`Home / Getting Started / The Core Idea`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1: Existing Data`, `Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator`). Prev/next links work. `/core-idea` correctly shows **both** its path positions — "Step 3 of 6" on the Beginner Path *and* "Step 1 of 7" on the Philosophy Track, each with its own next-link. That's the hard case in multi-path IA and the site solved it. The glossary's "Learn more →" backlinks from term to usage page are the right pattern. This is a well-structured site.

**The Beginner Path is fully enumerated** — steps 4/5/6 are What Synchronism Is Not, Honest Assessment, Glossary — listed on `/learning-paths` and referenced inline on the landing page. (Pass 1 reported not finding them; they're there, but they're in body prose on the landing page and in the tiles they're not, which is probably how they got missed. Discoverability issue, not a missing-content issue.)

**Then I started checking the badges, and that's where the audit turns.**

The landing page does the right thing: its four domain badges carry a line reading "Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed — definitions on the Honest Assessment page," linking to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`. Good practice.

So I followed the link. The canonical reference defines badges across **five families**: MRH-relationship (5 tags), Descriptive (4), Deprecated (2), Operational states (9), and Status lifecycle verbs (9). That is roughly **29 distinct status tokens** a reader must hold. They are the canonical reference for the entire site — and they live at an anchor inside a page I estimate at 15,000–18,000 words, which is simultaneously the site's full audit, Beginner Path step 5, and the landing page's "Summary" link. One page doing four jobs, one of which is "be the data dictionary."

Then I checked whether that vocabulary is actually held consistently:

- **The glossary carries 9 of the ~29.** MRH-relationship and Descriptive families are defined there. "Artifact Lesson," "Model Explainer," "Kill Criterion Triggered," "Null-Class," "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie," and "Sign Correction" are not glossary entries.
- **`/tier-1-existing` uses at least two labels I could not find in the canonical list** — "PROTOCOL DEVIATION" and "DEMOTED TO TIER-2." If the canonical reference is canonical, usage has outrun it.
- **"Withdrawn" appears in two families at once** — once under Operational states, once under Status lifecycle verbs. A token that belongs to two categories in its own data dictionary is a defect regardless of whether the meanings coincide.
- **`/test-catalog` presents a "Validation Badge Guide" covering seven categories.** `/honest-assessment` covers ~29. Two legends, two different scopes, no statement of which is authoritative.

**The tools page compounds this.** `/interactive-tools` puts a badge on every card: Model Explainer, Reparametrization, Artifact Lesson, Speculative. But "Model Explainer" is not a verdict — the canonical reference says outright that it "is a content grouping… with no verdict content," whereas "Reparametrization" and "Speculative" *are* verdicts about the physics. **Two different taxonomies are being rendered in the same visual slot**, so the card grid silently invites the reader to compare a shelving label against a scientific finding.

Worse, the labels don't survive the click:

| Tool | Label on `/interactive-tools` | Label on the tool's own page |
|---|---|---|
| γ Calculator | Artifact Lesson | "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems" |
| Phase Boundary Visualizer | Artifact Lesson | no label — prose caveat "This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose" |
| Chemistry Correlation Explorer | Reparametrization | no label — a methodological caveat box |

The index promises "Each card shows an epistemic status badge so you know what you're looking at before you click in." The badge then does not appear on the thing you clicked into. Anyone arriving from search or a direct link gets a different status vocabulary — or none — than anyone arriving from the index.

**The coherence problem.** This is my highest-severity finding and it's a pure writing issue, not a physics one. "Coherence" is the site's central term and I collected four different definitions:

- Landing: *"Coherence is a smooth S-curve from sparse/independent to dense/collective…"* — this defines the **curve**, not the quantity. Coherence is a number between 0 and 1; the S-curve is the shape of the function that produces it. Defining a quantity as a curve is a category slip, and it's in the site's most-read sentence.
- Glossary: *"How collectively a group of elements behaves, from sparse/independent (0) to dense/collective (1)."* — this one is correct and plain. Use this everywhere.
- `/first-encounter` (step 2): *"Coherence is how collectively a group of things behaves. A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence."* — good, consistent with the glossary.
- `/core-idea` (step 3): *"Coherence: 0 = sparse/independent, 1 = dense/collective. ⚠ Not quantum coherence — superconductors score low here."*

That warning on step 3 is the single most important sentence about the term on the entire site, and it is **the third place the term is introduced**. The landing page and `/first-encounter` both introduce coherence with no such caveat. Pass 1's unresolved question ("does high coherence mean ordered or disordered? I finished the site genuinely unsure") is caused precisely by this ordering. The caveat belongs at first use, on the landing page, in the same breath as the definition.

Related, smaller: **MRH's referent shifts.** The glossary and `/first-encounter` call it *"an 'event horizon for influence' — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"* (a **boundary**). `/mrh` defines it as *"the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system"* (a **set**). Is MRH the neighbors, or the line drawn around them? A reader can't tell, and "horizon" pulls one way while the formal definition pulls the other.

**Counting.** The Advanced path labels its last step "Test Roadmap (24 experiments)"; `/tier-1-existing` navigation references "24 proposed experiments by tier"; but `/test-catalog` describes 24 core tests **plus TEST-25 and TEST-26**, and `/tier-1-existing` carries a 2026-08-12 correction stating "There is one flat namespace, TEST-01 … TEST-26." The namespace grew; the nav labels didn't. Note this is the same class of defect Pass 1 caught with 14,610 vs 14,760 galaxies — a number inlined into prose in one place and maintained in another.

**Information architecture, one structural note.** The top nav has 8 items, and two of them are parent and child: "Tier 1: Existing Data" is a subset of "Test Roadmap," but they sit as siblings. A reader can't tell from the nav that clicking Test Roadmap would have included Tier 1.

**Acronyms.** The glossary is strong here — BTFR, RAR, CHSH, ΛCDM, DESI, SPARC, fσ₈, BAO, N_corr, and tanh all have entries. But first-use expansion is missing in the pages themselves: the landing page uses ΛCDM, BTFR, RAR, CHSH, DESI and fσ₈ bare, and `/what-synchronism-is-not` — Beginner Path **step 4** — uses ΛCDM, MOND and QFT without expansion. The definitions exist; they're just not reachable at the moment of confusion. Inline glossary links on first use would close this without adding a word of body text.

**Missing entry.** "Boost" is used on `/galaxy-plotter`, in the Honest Assessment ("boost ceiling"), and on the Tier 1 pages, and has no glossary entry. Pass 1 hit this independently.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — intuitive — Single input (N_corr, 1–1000 linear or 1–10⁷ log toggle), seven presets (ideal gas → BCS superconductor), outputs γ = 2/√N_corr with a regime label and a live curve. Default N_corr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, "Boundary (γ ≈ 1)" — the arithmetic is self-consistent with the glossary formula, which is more than a lot of calculators manage. **Documentation-wise this is the best-written tool on the site**: N_corr is defined at point of use ("the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units") and γ gets a plain gloss ("how abruptly a system switches from 'independent individuals' to 'acting as one'"). Both definitions appear *before* the input, which is exactly right. The one flaw is labelling: the page header reads "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems" while the index card calls the same tool "Artifact Lesson," and neither string links to the legend that defines it.

- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — intuitive — One γ slider (0–4) sweeping across three labelled regimes: Collective (left) / Boundary γ≈1 (centre) / Single-particle (right), with named systems placed along it and a "reality check" line per regime. The framing sentence — *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose"* — is excellent technical writing: it tells you what you're about to see and why before you touch anything, which is the correct order for a demo of a known-wrong thing. "Sign inversion" is explained in operational terms (BCS superconductors have sharp transitions but the formula assigns them flat curves — backwards) rather than as jargon. My only complaint: the page carries no status badge of any kind, so the disclosure lives entirely in prose that a skimmer will scroll past.

- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — worked — mostly intuitive — Filter tabs (All / Neutral / Coherence helps / Incoherence helps / Barrier-dominated) over a sortable table of 23 curated phenomena, r-values from Sound velocity (+0.982) down to Magnetic susceptibility (0.000). The self-refutation is stated cleanly: a 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z matches these correlations (|Δr| ≤ 0.07) and sometimes beats them, so *"High r here is evidence of density-monotonicity (known chemistry), not of C(ρ)-specific physics."* That's a well-constructed sentence. But "null model," "r," and "phenomena" are all undefined on the page, and the filter category names ("Barrier-dominated") aren't glossed. The tool assumes a statistics vocabulary the rest of the beginner track doesn't build.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which legend is authoritative — the ~29-token list on `/honest-assessment`, the 7-category guide on `/test-catalog`, or the 9 badge entries in the glossary? Nothing states the precedence.
2. Are "Model Explainer" and "Artifact Lesson" badges, categories, or both? They sit in the badge slot, are defined in the "Operational states" family, and are described as content groupings. Three different things at once.
3. Is a status label allowed to exist if it's not in the canonical list? "PROTOCOL DEVIATION" and "DEMOTED TO TIER-2" are in use and (as far as I can find) undefined.
4. Is MRH a set or a boundary?
5. Is the test count 24 or 26, and which pages are supposed to be updated when it changes?

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," which usually means I'll be closing the tab in ninety seconds. I didn't.

**Landing → /core-idea.** Central equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. /core-idea claims an exact identity to Hill-type saturation: `tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]`. I checked it — it's correct, trivially, by writing tanh in exponentials and multiplying through by (1+x)^γ. Good. But the cleaner statement is one step further and the site doesn't take it: setting z = (1+x)^2γ − 1 gives **C = z/(z+2) exactly** — Michaelis–Menten in z with half-saturation constant 2. That form makes the whole structure legible in one line, and it's where the interesting problems live (below).

**Straight to /parameter-derivations,** which is where I expected the site to fall apart, and where it instead did the single most impressive thing on it. The page opens by stating "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations" and then audits each one against itself: γ = 2/√N_corr is flagged as self-contradictory (CLT scaling applied to a count of *correlated* dof — exactly the regime where iid fails); a₀ = cH₀/2π is correctly attributed to Milgrom 1983 and independently to McCulloch/Verlinde/Smolin rather than claimed; Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) is correctly identified as **not independent evidence** since Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) is a linear map of the a₀ row. That last one is the kind of thing referees miss. I checked the numbers: a₀ = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² at H₀ = 67.4, and Σ₀ = 119 M☉/pc². Both correct to the digit.

The a₀ comparison is also done *right*, which is rare: g† = 1.20 ± 0.02 (random) ± 0.24 (systematic), giving 0.66σ — consistent. Most people would have divided by the random error and reported 8σ. Whoever fixed this deserves credit; it's the correct handling and it's stated explicitly.

**Then the tools,** and then the trouble started.

### What I checked, with the arithmetic

**1. The Coherence Explorer's numbers are exactly right.** Defaults γ = 0.49, ρcrit = 1. Readouts C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785. I reproduce 0.32714, 0.82584, 0.97852. No complaints on the implementation. The tool also now carries the correct caption — *"the curve you are looking at on load is MOND's μ, not an alternative to it."* That's true and it's the most important sentence on the site.

**2. It is exactly true, and I verified it algebraically, not numerically.** At γ = 1/2, 2γ = 1, so C = [(1+x) − 1]/[(1+x) + 1] = **x/(x+2)**. And μ_simple(y) = y/(1+y) at y = x/2 is (x/2)/(1+x/2) = x/(x+2). Identical, not approximate, for all x. So at γ = 1/2 the coherence function *is* the simple interpolating function under the substitution y = ρ/(2ρcrit). The framework's own SPARC fit prefers γ = 0.485–0.49. The model's best-fit configuration is MOND's μ to within half a percent in the exponent.

This fact appears in a tool caption and **nowhere on `/mond-unification`**, which is the first page a physicist opens. That page discusses tidal-dwarf σ intervals and EFE corrections and never states the one-line identity that settles the question. Highest-value propagation gap I found.

**3. ρcrit is not the knee, and it's off by a factor ~2.** The parameter is called "critical density" and "saturation knee" everywhere. At the default γ = 0.49, C(ρcrit) = 0.327, and C = 0.5 is reached at **ρ = 2.07 ρcrit**. The inflection point in log ρ also sits at ≈ 2.05 ρcrit (I solved (1−σ)/σ = 2γ·tanh(u) numerically). So every natural definition of "the transition" lands at ~2ρcrit, not ρcrit. The cause is the `+1` regulator inside the log: without it, ρcrit would be exactly the transition point. This factor of 2 propagates straight into ρcrit = A·V_flat², i.e. into A. The A audit finds a 600× error and a sign-inverted velocity exponent but never mentions this definitional offset sitting underneath both.

**4. There is no inflection point in density at all — the "S-curve" framing is wrong.** Differentiating twice:

  C''(x) = −γ·sech²(u)·[2γ·tanh(u) + 1] / (1+x)²,  u = γ·ln(1+x)

Every factor is positive for x > 0, γ > 0, so **C'' < 0 everywhere: C(ρ) is strictly concave on ρ > 0.** It is sigmoidal only against log ρ. But the site uses order-parameter vocabulary throughout — "S-curve," "critical density," "phase transition," "transition sharpness" — and the γ Calculator advertises "a live coherence S-curve." An order parameter has a transition. This function has a saturation. It is a logarithmic compander (μ-law, as /parameter-derivations itself half-concedes by listing the sigmoid/compander family), and the compander reading dissolves several of the site's own puzzles at once. This is a framing error, not an arithmetic one, but it's the framing of the entire site.

**5. Low-density behaviour makes γ and ρcrit degenerate at leading order.** Expanding for x ≪ 1: ln(1+x) ≈ x, tanh(γx) ≈ γx, so **C ≈ γ·ρ/ρcrit** — linear in ρ, for *every* γ. Only the ratio γ/ρcrit appears. Going to second order, C ≈ (γ/ρc)ρ − (γ/2ρc²)ρ², and the ratio of the two coefficients is 1/(2ρc), independent of γ — so the parameters *are* separately identifiable, but only by resolving O((ρ/ρc)²) curvature in the sparsest part of a disk, which is precisely where the photometry is worst. That's a clean explanation for why free-γ fits keep landing on a degenerate ridge, and it belongs on the site as an affirmative statement about what the data can and cannot constrain.

It also breaks the stated Hill interpretation. A Hill function with index n goes as xⁿ at small x. This one goes as **γx, linearly, for all γ**. At γ = 2 a true Hill-4 function would rise as x⁴; the actual function rises as 2x. So "Hill coefficient n = 2γ" is right asymptotically at x ≫ 1 and wrong in the x ≪ 1 regime — which is the only regime galaxies occupy. The identity on /core-idea is correct; the *interpretation* hung on it is not valid where the framework is applied.

### 1. The site is refuting two different theories under one name

`/galaxy-rotation` lists three "mutually exclusive readings" of how C couples to gravity:

- **quadrature**: `v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)²`
- **division**: `g_obs = g_bar / C` (this is the form implied by the site's own `f_DM = 1 − C` identity)
- **direct**: `g_obs = C · g_bar`

These are not three parametrizations of one model. They have **opposite radial dependence**, and the site's two headline galaxy results each use a different one.

C(ρ) is strictly increasing in ρ for every γ > 0 and every ρcrit > 0. In an exponential disk ρ falls monotonically outward. Therefore:

- Under **quadrature** (what `/galaxy-plotter` plots as "the framework's real equation"), the added term `(V_flat·C)²` falls outward while the required boost rises outward. The gradient has the wrong sign, and no choice of A, γ, or ρcrit can repair it — the sign is a property of the monotonicity, not of the parameters. This is a *parameter-free* failure and it is far stronger than the magnitude statement the page actually makes ("max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on"). The site is reporting a magnitude failure where it has a sign failure.
- Under **division**, C → 0 in the outskirts gives B = 1/C → ∞. That has the *right* sign — and it diverges, which is precisely why a ceiling is needed. **B ≤ 1/Ωm = 3.17 is the regulator for the division form.** It has no function at all in the quadrature form.

Now put those together. At the plotter's own numbers (max C ≈ 0.001), the division form gives B = 1/C ≈ 1000, capped at 3.17 — so the boost is **saturated at the ceiling across the entire disk**, which is the exact opposite of "boost never turns on." A constant boost B = 3.17 doesn't flatten a rotation curve; it rescales it by √3.17 = 1.78 and leaves it declining. That is a clean, immediately falsifiable prediction, and it is what TEST-10 kills (f_DM ≤ 0.685 vs. observed max 0.927).

**Consequence for the refutation ledger:** TEST-09 and TEST-10 test the ceiling, i.e. the *division* form. `/galaxy-plotter` and the "max C = 0.001" headline test the *quadrature* form. TEST-25's Cassini squeeze runs through a QUMOND realization, i.e. a third structure again. The honest-assessment page already discounts 6 → "3–4 independent roots" by merging TEST-09/10 and TEST-01/05. That merge is right but it stops one level too early: the remaining roots are not independent *theories being tested* — they are one undetermined coupling law being tested in three incompatible forms, with the results reported as though they accumulate.

The ledger should say: **the framework does not currently specify how C couples to gravity; until it does, the refutation count is not well-defined, because different entries refute different models.** That is a stronger and more useful statement than "6 refutations, 3–4 roots," and the site has every ingredient for it on `/galaxy-rotation` already.

### 2. The Brans–Dicke completion grid is excluded by Cassini before DESI is consulted

`/honest-assessment` describes Completion B as "C promoted to a Brans-Dicke-type scalar pinned to its algebraic trajectory," scanned over **ω = 0, 1, 5, 50** across 192 γ values, concluding that 0/192 reach DESI's preferred quadrant.

The Cassini Doppler-tracking bound (Bertotti, Iess & Tortora 2003) gives γ_PPN − 1 = (2.1 ± 2.3)×10⁻⁵, and for Brans–Dicke γ_PPN = (1+ω)/(2+ω), hence **ω ≳ 4×10⁴** for an unscreened massless scalar. The scanned grid tops out at ω = 50 — nearly three orders of magnitude inside the excluded region. No solar-system bound on ω appears anywhere on the page, while the *same spacecraft* is cited elsewhere on the site for TEST-25's +17.95σ.

There are only two ways out and the page must pick one:

- **(a)** The bound applies. Then the entire Completion B analysis is a scan over already-dead models, and the DESI result adds nothing — the completion was excluded by 2003 data, not by DESI DR2. That is a *cleaner* no-go than the one claimed, and it should replace it.
- **(b)** "Pinned to its algebraic trajectory" means C is not a dynamical scalar with a free ω but a prescribed background function of ρ. Then the PPN analysis doesn't apply in the usual way — but neither does the label "Brans–Dicke-type scalar," and a pinned non-dynamical field is not a covariant completion in any sense a referee would accept. It also reopens the Bianchi-identity problem the page raises against Appendix D.

Either resolution is publishable; the current state (BD language, sub-Cassini ω, no mention of the bound) is not.

### 3. The 3.4–6.3σ is not a tension statistic

The page says that when the family is "forced to DESI's w₀," it produces wₐ = +0.23…+0.60, "yielding mismatches of 3.4–6.3σ on DESI's own σ_wₐ."

That construction does not measure tension. Forcing w₀ to a value the model cannot reach and then reading off the residual in the orthogonal parameter is a *conditional* offset divided by a *marginal* error bar. It ignores the w₀–wₐ covariance (which in DESI's own contours is strongly negative — the degeneracy axis runs roughly along w₀ + 0.3wₐ), and it evaluates the model at a non-optimal point by construction. The correct statistic is Δχ² between the best-fit family member and best-fit w₀wₐCDM, with the dof stated and w₀ profiled, not fixed. My expectation is that a properly profiled comparison lands well below 3σ, because the substituted family *contains* ΛCDM at γ = 1/2 (the page says so: "the γ = 1/2 branch of the substituted model is exactly ΛCDM") and therefore inherits ΛCDM's 2.8–4.2σ and cannot exceed it.

Credit where due: the page **does** now state the γ = 1/2 ≡ ΛCDM fact and does distinguish the substituted family from Completion B ("Completion B has no ΛCDM member"). That distinction is real and Pass 3's reading of this section as self-contradictory does not survive it — the two σ values belong to two different model classes. What remains wrong is narrower and purely statistical: a forced-parameter offset is being reported in σ units as though it were a likelihood-ratio tension.

And `/test-catalog` already says TEST-26 "cannot select the framework." A kill-or-tie test with no selecting branch belongs in the ledger as *unpowered*, not as a refutation.

### 4. Correction to Pass 3: the plotter's "735× contradiction" is not a contradiction

Pass 3's highest-severity finding was that `/galaxy-plotter` carries "max C = 0.001" alongside "ρ/ρcrit = 0.60 at A = 0.029," incompatible by ~735×. Checking the page directly, the sentence reads **"at A = 4.6×10⁻⁵ the ratio for NGC 3198 is 0.60 and the curve would lift off"** — a *different* A, and the page explicitly labels the gap "the 635× between them is a law swap, not a scale choice."

Since ρ/ρcrit ∝ 1/A, and 0.029 / 4.6×10⁻⁵ = 630, the two numbers are the same quantity at the two ends of the documented law swap: 0.001 × 630 ≈ 0.63 ≈ 0.60. **The page is internally consistent and it flags the discrepancy itself.** Pass 3 read a conditional as unconditional. I flag this because an over-refutation *of the site* propagates downstream exactly as fast as an over-refutation *by* it, and this one was rated `high` with a recommendation to "recompute."

I ran the mass model independently as a check: M_b = 47·V_flat⁴ with V_flat ≈ 150 km/s gives 2.4×10¹⁰ M☉; an exponential disk with R_d ≈ 2.8 kpc gives Σ(0) ≈ 483 M☉/pc², and ρ(0) = Σ/2h with h = 0.3 kpc gives ≈ 0.8 M☉/pc³. Against ρcrit = 0.029·V_flat² = 652 that's x ≈ 1.2×10⁻³ — the plotter's order of magnitude. The arithmetic closes.

One genuine robustness note the page should carry: **h = 0.3 kpc is held fixed across all five galaxies**, from DDO 154 to NGC 7331. Real scale heights vary by more than 3× across that range and dwarfs are proportionally much thicker. It does not change the verdict — x ≈ 10⁻³ survives a factor of a few — and saying so is a *credit* to the result, not a caveat. State it and the robustness is established in one line.

### 5. The plotter draws MOND with the interpolating function its own catalog excludes

TEST-25 on this same site reports a Cassini discrepancy of +17.95σ for the γ ≈ 0.489 compander and notes the equivalent MOND result, "Desmond et al. 2024 reported 8.7σ tension," explicitly stating the exclusion "also excludes MOND's RAR-preferred IF family at γ ≈ 0.5."

The RAR-fitting, Cassini-surviving choice is McGaugh's exponential ν(y) = 1/(1 − e^(−√y)); plot that, or plot both with the simple ν labelled "excluded by Cassini — shown for comparison with TEST-25."

### 6. TEST-02: the *external* data is not adjudicable either

`/tier-1-existing` calls TEST-02 "adjudicable now but self-eliminating — no outcome favors Synchronism." Correct and admirably blunt: a pre-registered test with no favorable branch is not a test, and it should not be counted among the 24/26 registered experiments without that label attached at the count.

But the entry understates the problem in the other direction. The registered signal is at the 0.05–0.4% velocity level, and the wide-binary literature does not currently agree at anything like that precision — Chae reports a multi-σ gravitational anomaly, Banik and collaborators report Newtonian consistency on overlapping Gaia DR3 samples, and the disagreement is driven by the treatment of undetected close companions and the eccentricity prior, not by the data. **No wide-binary test discriminates anything at 0.4% right now**, for any theory. The entry should say that the external adjudication is blocked independently of the framework's self-elimination, otherwise a reader concludes the data are ready and only Synchronism isn't.

### 7. A2ACW, read as a methods submission

`/research-philosophy` claims the contribution is "the controlled null result" — that AI-AI adversarial collaboration cannot substitute for out-of-distribution evaluation by domain experts. If this arrived on my desk as a methods paper I would return it as **major revision, promising**, on four grounds:

**(a) There is no positive control.** The retrospective control (models with later training cutoffs, "0 of 6 demotions caught; median prior-art year ~1996") establishes specificity ≈ 0 on prior-art detection. There is no complementary experiment feeding A2ACW a *genuine* historical discovery in period-appropriate framing to see whether it can pass anything. Without that, "1.4% survival" is not an operating point — it's an unlabeled number that could equally reflect Challenger aggressiveness. Report sensitivity and specificity on a labeled claim set, or don't report a yield.

**(b) The null hypothesis is unstated.** 47/3,308 is only meaningful against the rate a non-adversarial or randomly-permuted pipeline produces on the same claim stream. Run the Defender alone. Run the pair with the Challenger's role prompt shuffled. If those also yield ~1.4%, the adversarial structure is doing nothing and the paper's subject doesn't exist.

**(c) The unit of independence is wrong.** 3,308 sessions are not 3,308 independent draws — they share a persistent framework document, they are nested within topics, and later sessions condition on earlier survivors. Quoting 47/3,308 as a rate is the same effective-N error that already forced ΔBIC 184 → 33 elsewhere on this site. The site has diagnosed this error in its physics and not applied it to its methodology.

**(d) The stated conclusion is confounded four ways.** "Two AI models trained on the same physics corpus will share the same blind spots" attributes the failure to the corpus. But same-corpus is confounded with same-vendor, same-post-training, and same-prompt-scaffold. The registered cross-vendor control is exactly the right experiment and is **not yet run** — so the headline claim currently rests on the one arm that cannot separate its own factors. Until it runs, the honest title is "AI-AI adversarial collaboration under a shared scaffold failed to detect prior art in 6/6 audited cases," which is narrower, defensible, and still worth publishing.

The framing is also one class too wide. "Cannot substitute for out-of-distribution evaluation" is a claim about the method; what was measured is that *this* pairing, on *this* corpus, missed prior art with median year 1996. That's a prior-art-retrieval failure — a retrieval problem with an obvious non-AI fix (literature search) — not a demonstration about adversarial evaluation in general.

### 8. What I'd actually keep

Two things on this site are worth a researcher's time, and neither is the framework.

**The negative result, correctly scoped.** "Gravity modifications keyed on local volumetric ρ(r) through an *algebraic pointwise multiplier* fail systematically, quantified on SPARC." The prior-art claim as stated ("ρ(r) was never in any tested variable set") is too strong — the density-keyed modified-gravity literature is large and includes chameleon (Khoury & Weltman 2004), symmetron (Hinterbichler & Khoury 2010), and the environment-dependent dilaton, and the symmetron in particular is *literally* a field with a critical density above which the symmetric phase is restored, i.e. the genuine order-parameter-in-ρ structure that this site's vocabulary keeps reaching for and its tanh-compander does not have. But those are all *screened field* constructions, and the narrow claim survives: nobody had published the quantified failure of the naive algebraic version, because everyone assumed it without checking. Assumed-without-checking is exactly what's worth publishing. Scope it to "algebraic pointwise coupling," cite the screening literature as the reason the gap existed, and it's a clean short paper.

**The one open question the site has and doesn't recognize as its best asset.** `/galaxy-rotation` observes that Burrage escapes via ∇(f(ρ)) rather than a multiplier. That inverts into a well-posed research question nobody on the site has asked: *what is the weakest non-pointwise functional of ρ that reproduces the RAR at observed scatter?* It's local-ish, it's testable, it has an obvious null (the answer may be "none below full non-locality"), and it's genuinely open. That question is more valuable than everything in the test catalog.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Which coupling law is the framework?** Quadrature, division, or direct multiplication. Every executed test assumes one, they disagree in radial sign, and the ledger sums across them. Nothing else on the site can be adjudicated until this is fixed, and it is a theory question, not a data question.
2. Does the Cassini PPN bound ω ≳ 4×10⁴ apply to Completion B? If yes, the covariant no-go is 2003 physics, not DESI physics. If no, why not — and is the object still a scalar field?
3. In the DESI comparison, was w₀ profiled or fixed, and against how many dof? The answer determines whether "3.4–6.3σ" is a tension or an artifact of the construction.
4. What is A2ACW's **sensitivity**? Specificity ≈ 0 is measured. Has the pipeline ever been shown to pass a claim that was independently true and novel?
5. Would the cross-vendor control change the conclusion? Until it runs, "same corpus ⇒ same blind spots" is one arm of a four-way confound.
6. What is the weakest non-pointwise functional of ρ that reproduces the RAR at observed scatter? This is the site's best question and it is not in the test catalog.

### Verdict

The novelty audit comes back the way the site says it does: **zero novel predictions, and I could not construct one.** A strict submodel of MOND with an underived ceiling can tie at best, the galaxy sector collapses to μ_simple at the γ its own fit prefers, and the dark-energy sector's substituted family contains ΛCDM exactly. The site's self-assessment is accurate, and `/for-researchers` stating "literally MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17} — a strict submodel" in those words is more candor than the literature normally produces.

But the ledger is measuring the wrong thing. The framework does not specify how C couples to gravity — the site itself lists three incompatible forms with opposite radial sign — and the executed tests are distributed across them: the plotter refutes quadrature, TEST-09/10 refute the ceiling that only division needs, TEST-25 refutes a QUMOND realization. Summing those into "6 refutations, 3–4 roots" manufactures independence between refutations of *different models*. Worse, the plotter's headline is a magnitude claim ("boost never turns on") where the physics gives a parameter-free sign claim, and under the other form the same C ≈ 0.001 implies the boost is *pinned at the ceiling everywhere* — the opposite headline from the same number. Fix the coupling law and I expect the count to fall to roughly one root plus one unpowered growth test.

What survives is worth publishing, scoped properly: the quantified failure of *algebraic pointwise* density coupling, cited against the screening literature that explains why the gap existed. And the A2ACW null is a real methods result being over-generalized — narrow it to "this pairing, this scaffold, prior-art retrieval," run the cross-vendor arm and a positive control, and it's a solid short paper. I'd referee that one favorably. I would not referee the physics, because there is currently no single theory to referee.
