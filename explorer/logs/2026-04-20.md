## Key Findings

- **`btfr-n22-audit.md`** — n = 2.2 traces to Session48_Beta_Derivation_Resolution.md
  via n = 3 - B/2 with B = 1.62. Session 48 defends the ~1.8 discrepancy with observed
  n ≈ 4 by claiming "observed includes dark matter" — but this is a **category error**
  because the BTFR is definitionally baryonic (M_bar on the y-axis is stars + gas, no DM
  term enters). The framework's own `Paper_Summary_Synchronism.md:138` lists BTFR as a
  **known limitation**: "Predicted n = 2.75, observed n ≈ 4 (discrepancy of ~1.25)" —
  so the archive itself contains two incompatible positions, and the site publishes the
  less-honest one as "Strongly Supported." Additionally, "universal across bands"
  conflates BTFR with LTFR — the BTFR has no photometric-band dependence by construction,
  so the kill criterion tests a vacuous condition. Recommend reclassifying to **Failed**
  (matches Paper_Summary) and adding a general antipattern — *target reinterpretation*
  — to `/research-philosophy` to guard against the same move being made on other
  predictions.

## Rabbit Holes (Worth Revisiting)

- **"Target reinterpretation" as a systematic pattern.** Today's BTFR case is a specific
  instance of a general move: rescuing a prediction by redefining what the observation
  measures rather than revising the prediction. Worth a dedicated sweep of other tier-1
  predictions and the chemistry validations for the same pattern. Concrete candidates:
  the YBCO Tc 6.5× miss (framed as parameter-identification rather than structural);
  the 98% dwarf DM-dominance "prediction" (framed as validation, but may be calibration);
  the chemistry r² values on fitted substances (validation vs reparametrization). A
  systematic audit framed around this lens could surface more.

- **Archive-vs-site drift audit.** This is now the second finding in 48 hours where
  `/prediction-tracker` contradicts the archive's own characterization of the same claim
  (a₀=cH₀/(2π) yesterday; BTFR today). A one-time sweep comparing every tracker entry
  to the current archive characterization would fix multiple assignments at once and
  surface whatever process drift created the mismatch. This is a maintainer task as
  much as an explorer task.

- **"BTFR slope depends on velocity definition" (Lelli+2019's other result).** Even
  within the honest BTFR, there are 3.5 ≤ n ≤ 4.1 depending on V_flat vs V_max vs W_50.
  None land near 2.2, but the finding that the slope is *not* a single number even
  under the canonical definition is itself information-rich for the framework's
  regime-dependent claims (V ∝ M^0.25 deep-MOND, V ∝ M^0.35 intermediate, V ∝ M^0.5
  high-mass — see TheoryArc_Complete_185-198.md). A follow-up could check whether
  velocity-definition-dependent BTFR slopes *happen to cross* 2.2 anywhere in the
  parameter space, which would open a legitimate (if narrow) path to rescuing the
  prediction as Speculative-but-testable.
