## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A refreshingly honest "we tried this and it mostly didn't work" notebook with a great crowd-vs-marching-band hook, but nearly every page soon fills with symbols, survey names and revision notes, and I kept losing the thread.

### Journey
Clicked through from Reddit. The headline, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, is a great hook, the kind of thing a Veritasium thumbnail would say. The one-line idea right under it is the best thing on the site for me: every system gets a number from 0 (*"a crowd of strangers"*) to 1 (*"a marching band"*). I got that immediately.

Then, within the first screen, came: *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* and *"As tested physics, dead so far"*, *"As a public record of finding that out, alive."* Honestly, that was whiplash, but it also made me curious. I've never seen a science site open by telling me its own idea failed. There's a "Full answer ↓" link to why I should keep reading, which helped. What scared me off a bit was everything after it: an equation with `tanh` and `ln`, a "Terms on this page" key with 20+ abbreviations (BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, dex, SPARC, DESI, BAO, KiDS…), a "Methodology note" about 3,300 AI sessions and a "1.4% internal-consistency survival rate", and a table with labels like "Excluded at its own cap" and "Is Λ". It's nice that most terms are defined, but a page that needs a 20-item glossary before I scroll halfway tells me it isn't meant for me. The routing box ("No physics background? → Beginner Path, 5 steps") rescued me, so I followed it.

**Step 1, /why-synchronism.** The top is short and good: "Physics has a fragmentation problem", then the question of whether one principle could connect the separate parts, then "What changes if this works?" Everything else sits in a fold labelled "For physicists", and I liked being allowed to skip it. The three-line "what happened" summary says the galaxy test "collapses to MOND". I'd met MOND once on the landing page, but I still couldn't tell you what it is.

**Step 2, /first-encounter.** "No prerequisites needed", about 10 minutes, sections revealed one at a time with a Continue button. The marching band and crowd idea comes back, along with a neutron star "packed 10¹⁴× denser than lead" (cool) and tanh "like a dimmer switch" (helpful). Section C is where I got lost: it teaches me the γ formula and then, in the same breath, *"In short: the formula for γ failed."* As a beginner I'm being asked to learn something and unlearn it on the same screen. Section E, "a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π: a coincidence?", was the most intriguing moment for me. It's a real mystery hook, though I don't know what H₀ is. Section F, "Measurement = MRH crossing", lost me entirely. The landing page had said this coherence "is not quantum coherence", yet here it's being used to explain quantum measurement, and the γ presets include BEC and superconductors, which are the quantum examples I know from YouTube. Is it quantum or not?

**Step 3, /galaxy-plotter.** The intro is excellent: *"New here? Look at three things on the chart below."* It also explains a rotation curve in plain words ("just how fast things orbit, plotted against distance from the galaxy's centre") and says outer stars should move more slowly, like the planets do, but don't. That's the dark-matter puzzle explained better than most videos I've watched. The slider label "How heavy are the stars?" is great plain language. But right above the chart is a switch between "Archive's own form: ρcrit = 0.028·Vflat^0.5" and "Units hybrid: 0.029·Vflat²", which means nothing to me, and the legend has six line styles (dots, dashed gray, solid violet, light-violet dashed, amber dotted, green dashed). The page is also very long, roughly 49,000 characters, much of it revision notes and "for physicists" boxes. For step 3 of a beginner path that's a lot. The takeaway did land: the framework's line (violet) misses the dots and MOND's line (green) follows them.

**Step 4, /what-synchronism-is-not.** Best beginner page after the landing hook. A box near the top says *"In plain words — beginner? You can stop here."*, followed by five plain bullets ("Not proven", "Not checked by science journals", "Not a replacement for today's physics", "Not compatible, as it stands, with a basic rule of relativity", "Not just new labels, and not just philosophy"). I could actually repeat these to a friend. Below that, words like "ontology", "Born rule" and "Hořava–Lifshitz" turned up undefined, but I'd already been told I could stop.

**Step 5, /honest-assessment.** The page says about itself that it is "about 30,000 words". Luckily the Next link from step 4 drops me at "If you read nothing else", where the first bullet is: *"The one equation failed its galaxy tests. Where it fits, it is MOND in different notation. As a theory of galaxies: fatal."* That's the clearest sentence on the site, and I understood it. There's also "untested is not the same as failed", which I liked. I skimmed the rest: a ledger table, nine badge definitions, "Structural Tensions"… and bounced.

Then I went to the tools.

### Tools Tested
*(Caveat: my browser view only shows what the page renders before any interaction, so I could see the controls, default readouts and instructions but couldn't actually drag sliders. That limits how well I could judge whether the tools "work".)*

- **Coherence Explorer (/coherence-explorer)**: loads with defaults (γ = 1.00, ρcrit = 1.00, log scale), a chart with C(ρ) from 0 to 1, and readouts C(ρcrit) = 0.6000, C(10ρcrit) = 0.9836, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9998. **Intuitive at the start, confusing in the presets.** The opening is honest and helpful: *"What this toy is for: seeing the shape of the S-curve… It does not model any real material, and the density axis has no units."* There's also a clear *"One thing to try first"* prompt (drag γ from 2 to 0.5 and watch the S flatten), which is exactly what I need. But the preset buttons are labelled with things like "the density version's free fit to galaxies… ΔBIC +2843", and a badge says "Reparametrization: at γ = ½ this is … MOND's, fed acceleration; ΛCDM in cosmology". I can play with the shape, but I don't know what any setting *means*. The page even says "No setting here is one the framework predicts," which is honest but leaves me wondering why I'm turning the dial.
- **γ Calculator (/gamma-calculator)**: an Ncorr box (default 4 → γ = 1.0000) and presets (Ideal gas, Liquid water, Galaxy, Enzyme site, Ferromagnet, BEC, BCS superconductor). **Clear about its purpose, odd as a toy.** The headline is *"γ Calculator — a γ formula kept to show how it fails"*. The "Try it" prompt (click Ideal gas, then BCS superconductor, and watch the curve collapse to nearly flat) is concrete, and I can see the point: the formula gives the *least* organized thing (a gas) the sharpest switch, which is backwards. That's actually a nice "aha" about why the idea failed. But the result label "Crossover band (γ ≈ 1; the 'boundary' reading is retracted)" confused me. Retracted by whom, and what was the boundary reading?
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter (/galaxy-plotter)**: five galaxies (DDO 154 default, NGC 2403, UGC 128, NGC 3198, NGC 7331), a star-weight slider (Υdisk = 0.50), and the calibration switch. **Best explained, most cluttered.** The "Try the twin pair" prompt (NGC 2403 vs UGC 128, both with flat speeds around 130 km/s) is a nice idea, but I don't know what to look for in the comparison. Real SPARC data instead of made-up points is reassuring.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the galaxy version is "MOND in different notation", what *is* MOND, in one sentence, and why don't physicists already accept it? The site kept saying MOND as if I knew.
2. What does "density" mean for a galaxy versus an atom? Is it literally the same kind of number on the same scale?
3. Is this about quantum stuff or not? (See the friction row above.)
4. What happens on 2 December 2026 with Gaia DR4, and what result would make the idea "alive" again versus finish it off? That's the most exciting thing on the site and it got one line.
5. a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π "a coincidence?" sounds like the real mystery. Is that still open, and is it this project's idea or someone else's?
6. Who is "dp", and why were AI agents doing the research? The landing page mentions it but doesn't say why that's interesting (or worrying).

### Verdict
This is the most honest science website I've ever seen. It tells you up front the big idea didn't pan out, and the best one-liners ("crowd of strangers vs marching band", "Where it fits, it is MOND in different notation. As a theory of galaxies: fatal.", "untested is not the same as failed") are genuinely clear. But almost every page, even the beginner steps, quickly fills with symbols, survey names and correction notes, so I spent most of my time skimming past things I couldn't parse, and I left knowing *that* it failed far better than *what it was* or *why I should care about what's left*. I'd come back if someone posted "here's what Gaia DR4 said" on Reddit in December.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /learning-paths, /interactive-tools, /research-philosophy, /honest-assessment (badge legend + TOC), /glossary, /test-catalog, /key-claims, /crossover-regime-visualizer, /equation-walkthrough)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure, not the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an auditor. The site documents its own vocabulary unusually carefully, and that care is what makes the drift between pages easy to see.
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is strong: a single nav, paths with honest step counts, a 128-entry glossary that matches its count, and one named canonical badge legend. The status vocabulary, though, has grown into about 30 labels across four tiers, and two renames are half-propagated: "validation badges" became "status badges" yesterday, and γ is variously a "steepness", a "sharpness", "not a sharpness", and a "regime parameter".

*(Method caveat: I read pages through a text extractor, not a browser. Long pages (landing ~51k chars, Honest Assessment ~190k) were only partly verbatim. Breadcrumb separators showed up as "/" list items, which I first misread as empty breadcrumb segments. Each friction row below is from verbatim text unless marked "unverified".)*

### Journey
**Landing → nav.** The top nav has ten items, all with useful tooltips ("128 terms defined", "26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)"). One page carries two labels: the nav says **"Start Here"**, the footer says **"Start Here: Why Synchronism?"**, and Learning Paths step 1 says **"Why Synchronism?"**. The tooltip bridges them, so this is mild. The landing page expands MOND, MRH and A2ACW at first use. RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm and ΛCDM appear first in an "Abbreviations ahead" line or in running text, and are expanded only later in the key ("Abbreviations used in the table below"). LLM and DR4 are not expanded at first use. For a page this dense the key is a reasonable compromise, but the rule is "listed first, expanded later", not "expanded at first use".

**Learning Paths.** This is the best page on the site. Seven paths, each with a step count and a time, and the Beginner path's per-step minutes (8+10+6+9+4) actually sum to the stated ~37 min. A bolded correction admits that "The two groupings are not equivalent". Steps carry inline status ("The γ ≈ 1 Band (boundary reading retracted)", "Decisive Tests (none currently running)"). CDM, MOND, A2ACW, γ and η appear in step labels without gloss. That's fine in the Advanced path but jarring in the shared list.

**Interactive Tools.** There's a clear "Where to start: Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment". Each card has Kind / Level / State / Badge, and the page states that Level "uses one scale and one scale only". The Consciousness card honestly says it is "not an interactive tool". Unexpanded on this page: BCS, BEC, SPARC, V_flat, EEG/fMRI/IIT/PCI*.

**Hunting for the badge legend.** This was the most informative part of the audit. The nav tooltip for Research Philosophy says **"Validation taxonomy, kill criteria, methodology"**. On that page all nine badge chips read only **"see canonical legend"**, and the text says definitions "now live in exactly one place", on Honest Assessment. There is also no section called "kill criteria"; the nearest is "1. Falsifiability First". So the tooltip promises two things the page hands off or names differently. Honest Assessment's legend is reachable from a good table of contents ("Badge definitions (canonical) → #validation-badge-definitions"). Its heading now reads *"Status badge definitions (canonical reference; called "validation badges" until 2026-11-16, though none of them is a validation)"*. The rename hasn't reached the anchor id, the Research Philosophy tooltip, the Research Philosophy anchor `#validation-badge-taxonomy`, or Key Claims' link text ("validation badge taxonomy").

**Where badges are defined.** In practice they're defined in at least three places, despite "exactly one place". These are the Honest Assessment legend (canonical), the Glossary's "(badge)" entries, and the Test Roadmap's "Status Badge Guide". Key Claims links its chips to the *glossary* anchors (`/glossary#failed-badge`), not to Honest Assessment. Wording differs slightly between copies. Audited-Negative is "closed audit finding; durable; does not move" on Honest Assessment and "Closed audit finding on a historical track; durable negative result; does not move" in the Glossary. The differences are harmless today, but this is how copies drift.

**How many kinds of label are there?** Test Roadmap's guide has 9 badges, 7 "operational states (not badges)", 11 "lifecycle verbs (not badges)" and 3 retired labels, so 30 terms. Research Philosophy lists only 3 operational states (one retired), and itself admits to "40+ ad-hoc strings across five pages". The same label lands in different tiers on different pages:
- **Empty Intersection** is a *lifecycle verb* (Test Roadmap; Glossary table), an *operational label* (its own Glossary entry), and an *ad-hoc string used but undefined* (Research Philosophy).
- **Ceiling Exceeded** is a *lifecycle verb* in the Glossary table and an *(operational label)* in its Glossary entry.
- **Null-Class** is an *operational state* in the Glossary table and a *"finding descriptor, not a badge"* in its entry.
- **Failed** is a *descriptive badge* in the Glossary intro but is called an operational label inside the Empty Intersection entry.

**Glossary.** It's excellent as a reference. The stated "128 terms" matches the count. Entries are alphabetical with a Greek section, and there's a "New here? Start with these ten" line. Every acronym I tested had an entry except **LLM** and **ontology** (Pass 1 also hit "ontology"). The landing page links `/glossary#start-with-ten`, but the "start with ten" line rendered as a bold line rather than a heading in my extract (unverified whether the id exists).

**Test Roadmap.** The H1 is "Test Roadmap" but the URL is `/test-catalog`, the same name/slug split as Equation Anatomy (`/equation-walkthrough`). It states "26 tests" and has *no test cards*: the cards live on the tier pages. A reader who clicks "Test Roadmap" expecting a list of 26 tests gets prose naming them. The tier tiles read 10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24, with TEST-25 and TEST-26 filed under Tier 1 despite the stated "Tier 1 = 01–10". The page explains this ("Tier 1 page has 13 cards"), but the tiles and the headline count still disagree visually. To its credit, the page flags its own ID problems openly (one environment test under three IDs, an 11 → 25 renumbering).

**Key Claims.** It's well structured, with three claims, a badge each, and a linked legend. Its revision note says acronyms "are expanded at first use", but **ALFALFA–SDSS** and **EEG** are used before their expansions, so the page fails its own stated rule. ΔBIC, SH0ES, BTFR, BEC/BCS, GRW/CSL and DESI DR2 are unexpanded.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Which page is the canonical home for a term's definition: the Glossary or Honest Assessment? Pages disagree (Key Claims → Glossary; Research Philosophy → Honest Assessment), and the Glossary itself defers to Honest Assessment for badges.
2. Is the 30-label status vocabulary (9 badges + 7 operational states + 11 lifecycle verbs + 3 retired) meant for readers, or is it an internal bookkeeping system that leaked onto public pages? If the second, the public pages could show only the 9 badges.
3. What does γ *do*, in one sentence everyone agrees on? I can't write the docs until the site can.
4. Is there one site-wide rule for acronyms (expand at first use per page, or rely on the key/glossary)? Key Claims states the per-page rule; the landing page follows the key approach.
5. Why isn't there a single sortable index of all 26 tests? The Test Roadmap is the obvious place for one.

### Verdict
As information architecture, this site is better than most science documentation I audit. The navigation is consistent, the learning paths are honestly costed, the glossary count is true, and pages correct themselves in the open. Every failure I found is drift between copies of the same fact: a renamed label that reached the heading but not the anchors or tooltips, badge definitions said to live in one place that live in three, a label filed in different tiers on different pages, and above all γ, whose one-line definition contradicts itself across four pages. The fix is structural, not editorial. Render vocabulary from one source and grep for the retired wording, rather than hand-editing each page.

**Parameter Derivations.** The page says "Despite the page title, this is not a derivations page", which is honest and correct. I checked:
