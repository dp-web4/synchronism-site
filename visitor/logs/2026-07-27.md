## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary

### Journey

I clicked in from Reddit. The landing page asks *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — that's a great question, exactly the kind of thing Veritasium would open with. Then, about two inches below it, the equation itself:

**C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))**

I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ρ is. I know ln vaguely from a calculator button. My honest first reaction was "oh, this isn't for me" — and I only kept going because the marching band line right after it was so good. *"A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence."* That I get instantly. That should be **above** the equation, not below it.

The other thing on the landing page that stopped me: **"0 confirmed predictions; 4 refutations executed on external data."** I genuinely could not tell whether that was a confession or a boast. Is 0 confirmed predictions bad? String theory has 0 confirmed predictions and people write bestsellers about it. Nobody on the page told me how to read that number, so I sat there for a second wondering if I'd landed on a debunking site *about* Synchronism rather than the site *for* it.

I took the Beginner Path since it promised "6 steps, ~30 min, no equations required."

**Step 1 (/why-synchronism)** — "Physics has a fragmentation problem." Good framing, I've heard the quantum-vs-gravity thing on YouTube. But the promise said "no equations required" and the equation is right there on step 1. Small thing, but I noticed.

**Step 2 (/first-encounter)** — the best onboarding page on the site. Marching band, crystal vs. gas, and then ρ explained as just "density — how much stuff is packed into a given space." That's all I needed! One sentence and a scary Greek letter became "how crowded is it." Then MRH — "Markov Relevancy Horizon" — showed up. The plain-English version is lovely: *"a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy."* But the formal parenthetical ("minimal set of degrees of freedom") right next to it made me feel like I'd been caught not being a physicist. Why show me both?

**Detour to /two-reframes** (a sidebar offered "prefer analogies to equations?" — yes please). The CRT analogy is the single most memorable thing on this site. Old TV screen scanning so fast it looks like a solid picture; slow the observation down and it's one dot flying around. Using that for quantum superposition is genuinely a "whoa" moment. The pendulum-clock-in-a-centrifuge one for relativity is even clearer. And then the page tells me the CRT model "is not yet mathematically formalized" and that something called the Kochen–Specker theorem is a "theorem-level exclusion" of the core framework. So... the coolest idea on the site is, as far as I can tell, already ruled out by a theorem? Nobody explained what that theorem is or how bad "excluded" is. I left that page more confused than I arrived, which is a shame because I arrived excited.

**Step 3 (/core-idea)** — the "dimmer switch" metaphor for the equation is great. Then mid-page there's a proof that tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) is "exactly a Hill-type rational function," and I bailed on that paragraph entirely. Also a note that the six "validated" badges have been demoted to "pending." I don't know what those badges were, I never saw them, and now I'm reading their obituary.

The thing that broke my brain most: a footnote saying "coherence" here does **not** mean quantum coherence, and that superconductors — the thing every YouTube video calls the most coherent stuff in the universe — score **low** on this scale. That's the central word of the whole framework meaning roughly the opposite of what I've been taught it means. That needs to be in bold on step 1, not in a footnote on step 3.

**Step 5 (/honest-assessment)** — this is where the beginner path fell over for me. This is step 5 of 6 on a path advertised as beginner-friendly, and it opens into BTFR, RAR, SPARC, fσ₈, ΔBIC, EFE, σ_int, 3.3σ kill criteria. There *is* a "what a non-physicist should take away" box, but it's buried partway down after I'd already scrolled through the wall. Content-wise it's admirable — "essentially nothing worked as originally predicted," said out loud, on the site's own page — I've literally never seen a theory's own website say that. I respect it enormously. But as the beginner's fifth step it's like being handed the exam after four lessons.

**/key-claims** — three claims, and their labels are "speculative, untestable as stated," "unfalsifiable as stated," and "failed." As a casual reader that reads as three strikes. I think the site means something more nuanced ("untested" ≠ "wrong"), but the badge words don't carry that nuance to me, and the definitions live on a different page.

**/galaxy-rotation** — I clicked this because dark matter is the thing I actually care about. First paragraph: RAR, enclosed-mass acceleration, SPARC, photometry. I never got the one paragraph I needed, which is "stars at the edge of galaxies go too fast, and nobody knows why." Bounced in under a minute.

**/glossary** — genuinely good, and it rescued several pages retroactively. The MOND definition ("gravity behaves differently at very low accelerations") is exactly right for me. The problem is it's step **6** — the last thing on the path — when I needed it at step 3. Nothing on the jargon-heavy pages linked me here at the moment I was lost.

I'd come back if there were a plain-language "so what's still alive, and why should you care that we tried?" page — right now I've got a marching band, a CRT screen, a dimmer switch, and the strong impression that everything I just read didn't work out.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a release: walk the nav, check every label against its destination, check every defined term against its uses, and see whether a reader who follows the site's own pointers ever hits a dead end or a contradiction.

**Navigation and breadcrumbs — this part is genuinely good.** Eight primary nav items, all resolving. Breadcrumbs are present and correctly scoped on every single page I visited, including deep tool pages (`Home / Interactive Tools / Phase Boundary Visualizer`). The breadcrumb categories — *Getting Started*, *Predictions & Experiments*, *Methodology*, *Interactive Tools* — are a coherent four-bucket IA and they're used consistently. Several pages also carry a path-position marker ("Beginner Path — Step 3 of 6", "Methodology Track — Step 1 of 6"), which is exactly the orientation affordance most doc sites forget. `/core-idea` even correctly shows dual membership (Beginner step 3 **and** Philosophy step 1). I want to say this plainly because the rest of my report is critical: the information architecture is not the problem here.

**The glossary is excellent and under-used.** 110+ entries, alphabetical with Greek sorting after Latin, badge terms clustered as a group, and a "rewritten 2026-07-17" date stamp. Definitions are tight and written for the right audience ("ln — natural logarithm, base e (≈2.718)" sitting next to "ΔBIC — BIC difference between two models; >10 = very strong evidence"). Two problems: it's step **6 of 6** on the beginner path, and the nav tooltip advertises it as **"30+ terms defined"** when it holds 110+. That tooltip is stale metadata underselling the single best asset on the site by roughly 3.5×. Also, `/first-encounter` contains the sentence "See the Glossary for the full definition" (re: MRH) **without a link on it**. That's the canonical docs failure — a cross-reference in prose that the reader has to go find manually.

**Then I started checking status labels, and the floor gave way.**

The site declares a canonical badge taxonomy at `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` — the anchor works, the section is real, and it's well written: two families (MRH-relationship: Active-MRH / Parallel-Paths / Sidelined / Superseded / Audited-Negative; descriptive: Untested / Speculative / Reparametrization / Failed), plus deprecated Validated / Strongly Supported, plus operational Kill-Criterion-Triggered / Withdrawn / Post-diction. As a reader I cannot tell whether *Disfavored*, *Underpowered*, *Pending*, *Never run as registered*, *Registered Null*, *Structurally Unreachable*, and *REFUTED* are seven distinct epistemic states with real distinctions, or seven writers' synonyms for four canonical ones. **Symbol definitions drift too.** γ is the framework's second-most-important symbol. It is:
- "**coupling strength**" on `/core-idea` (parameter table, `γ: 2/√Ncorr: coupling strength`)
- "**transition-sharpness coefficient**" in the glossary

Meanwhile `/tier-1-existing` tells the reader "the testing hierarchy (Tiers 1–4) is defined on the Test Roadmap" and links to **`/test-roadmap`**. **Acronyms on the landing page.** MOND, MRH, BAO, A2ACW, and tanh are all expanded on first use — good discipline. ## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /core-idea, /honest-assessment, /galaxy-rotation, /key-claims, /two-reframes, /for-researchers, /tier-1-existing, /interactive-tools, /gamma-calculator, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /consciousness-demo) + 3 tools

- **Overall impression**: The algebra I checked is correct and the self-demotions are the most honest I've seen on a framework site — but the two relations at the center of the model (γ = 2/√N_corr and the γ = 2 galaxy pin) are never multiplied together on the same page, and when you do it yourself the framework says a galaxy is an ideal gas.

### Journey

I went straight past the onboarding to `/core-idea` and started checking algebra.

**The Hill identity is correct.** The site asserts

> tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)²ᵞ − 1] / [(1+x)²ᵞ + 1]

Verified in one line: with u = γ ln(1+x), e^u = (1+x)^γ, so tanh u = ((1+x)^γ − (1+x)^−γ)/((1+x)^γ + (1+x)^−γ), multiply through by (1+x)^γ. Correct. The site's conclusion — "notation, not mechanism" — is right, and it's a genuinely creditable thing to publish about your own headline equation.

I'd push it one step further than the site does, because the identity says more than "any saturating function would do." It says C depends on (ρ/ρ_crit) **only through the combination (1 + ρ/ρ_crit)^{2γ}** — i.e. C is exactly a two-parameter Hill function with Hill coefficient **n = 2γ**. Three consequences the site doesn't draw:
- γ is a **Hill coefficient / log-log slope**, not a coupling. (Pass 2 already flagged the "coupling strength" label as inconsistent naming; from the physics side it isn't just inconsistent, it's the wrong *kind* of quantity.)
- In the sparse limit C ≈ γ·(ρ/ρ_crit) — **linear**, no threshold, no symmetry breaking. C cannot be an order parameter in any Landau sense, which is why the "phase transition" vocabulary keeps needing damage control on the tool pages.
- The "spans 80 orders of magnitude" selling point is a property of every Hill function with small n. It's not evidence for anything.

The site *does* say the Ising resemblance (m = tanh(βJzm)) is "visual only; no feedback loop exists here." **On the inversion itself, the site's diagnosis is incomplete.** It calls γ = 2/√N_corr a "dimensional ansatz inspired by fluctuation theory." The actual problem is a category error about what 1/√N governs: 1/√N is the CLT scaling of the **standard error of a sample mean**, i.e. how tightly an average is determined. It is not the width of a transition in an order parameter. In mean-field theory the transition gets *sharper* as coordination number z grows — the free-energy barrier scales with the number of correlated degrees of freedom, and the Ginzburg criterion says mean-field becomes *more* accurate, not less, as N_corr rises. So the correct scaling direction is γ increasing with N_corr (γ ∝ √N_corr or ∝ N_corr), and the framework has it upside down not by a sign slip but by importing the wrong theorem. Worth stating on the page: readers with stat-mech training will make this diagnosis anyway, and it's better made by the site.

### Journey

**The framework's only structural difference from MOND is a bounded boost, and its ceiling is underived.**

`/honest-assessment` is admirably direct: **B_max = 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17** is "the only structural difference from MOND," and every galaxy-sector failure cascades from it. Good. But nowhere on the site is that ceiling derived, and the identification is physically unmotivated in a specific, checkable way.

A boost is a ratio of dynamical to baryonic mass. The cosmic ratio of those two quantities is **Ω_m/Ω_b = 0.315/0.0493 = 6.40**, not 1/Ω_m = 3.17. Those are different numbers with different meanings, and TEST-10's verdict flips between them:

| Ceiling | Value | f_DM cap | Observed median f_DM = 0.755 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/Ω_m (site's choice) | 3.17 | 0.685 | **exceeds — kill fires** |
| Ω_m/Ω_b (baryon budget) | 6.40 | 0.844 | **passes** |
| SPARC maximum f_DM = 0.927 | requires B ≥ 13.7 | — | exceeds both |

So the TEST-10 kill fires on a choice of cosmic ratio that has never been derived on the site. This is exactly the sensitivity the program handled correctly for TEST-09 (a velocity-definition robustness sweep, pre-registered rule, all 11 runs adjudicated). The analogous sweep for TEST-10 is over the *ceiling's definition*, and it wasn't run. To be clear about which way this cuts: the kill still stands, because f_DM,max = 0.927 requires B ≥ 13.7 and blows through both ceilings. But the *reported* result ("69% of galaxies exceed") is convention-dependent and the robust statement is the maximum, not the median.

**And TEST-10's outcome was decidable from the literature before any rotation curve was touched.** The stellar-mass–halo-mass relation (Behroozi et al. 2013/2019; Moster et al. 2013) has peak M_*/M_halo ≈ 0.02 at M_halo ~ 10¹² M☉ and 10⁻³–10⁻⁴ for dwarfs. Baryon-to-halo ratios of 10–10³ have been standard knowledge for a decade. Any framework with a hard boost ceiling of 3.17 is excluded by the SHMR by one to three orders of magnitude. Spending an execution cycle on SPARC to discover this is not wrong, but it's late, and a researcher reading the ledger will notice that the framework's two "executed refutations on external data" (TEST-09, TEST-10) are both consequences of the same underived constant, both derivable from textbook results, and are therefore **one refutation reported twice**.

The reframe I'd urge, because it's the difference between a self-flagellation log and a citable result: *a bounded-boost modified-gravity class with ceiling B_max is excluded by SPARC dwarfs for B_max ≲ 14.* That's a constraint on a model class, stated in a form another author can use. It's the single most publishable sentence on the site and it isn't written anywhere.

**"0 tests currently discriminate from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" is now false in the site's own favor-inverted direction.**

This claim appears on `/for-researchers`, `/test-catalog`, and `/honest-assessment`. But look at the record:

- **TEST-09**: observed n = 3.75 ± 0.10. MOND 3.81 ± 0.04 → **0.6σ, passes**. Synchronism 3.35 ± 0.07 → **3.3σ, fails**.
- **TEST-10**: MOND median residual −0.03 (~3%). Synchronism +0.18. MOND passes, Synchronism fails.

Those are discriminating tests. They discriminated. The sign went against the framework. A test that separates two models at 3.3σ *is* a discriminating test regardless of which model wins — that is what discrimination means. What the site means, I think, is "0 tests could select Synchronism over MOND," which is a different and true statement. As written, the site is booking its two strongest empirical results as zero. Split the claim:

> 0 executed tests could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM. 2 executed tests discriminated between them, and both selected MOND (TEST-09 at 3.3σ, TEST-10 on the boost ceiling).

**The ΔBIC = +7.1 free-γ result is being read backwards, and the correct reading is sharper.**

`/galaxy-rotation` reports, on N = 2,807 SPARC points: γ = 2 pinned → ΔBIC = +184; γ free → **ΔBIC = +7.1**; verdict "free-γ fitting collapses to MOND equivalence."

Run the BIC bookkeeping. ln(2807) = **7.94**. One extra free parameter costs +7.94 in BIC before any likelihood is considered. Observed ΔBIC = +7.1 therefore implies Δχ² = 7.1 − 7.94 = **−0.84** — the compander with free γ fits the data *marginally better* than McGaugh's ν, and loses on BIC purely as a complexity charge for having a knob MOND doesn't need.

That is a much stronger statement than "collapses to MOND equivalence." It says: the compander is a strictly-worse-parameterized reparametrization of the RAR that buys Δχ² < 1 for one added degree of freedom. **The local-density no-go has real content and a weak novelty claim.**

The physics is right, and stated well: C(ρ) is pointwise and local, g_bar is an enclosed-mass non-local quantity, cluster ρ_crit needs 10⁴–10⁶× the galaxy value, and the sign statement (a density knee must scale V⁻² to track an acceleration threshold; the framework asserts V⁺²) is framework-independent and correct. I checked the scaling and I agree with it.

The novelty claim — *"prior work tested acceleration vs. radius/frequency/size/surface density, but never volumetric ρ(r) explicitly"* — will not survive review. Local-matter-density-keyed gravity modification is an entire subfield:

- **Symmetron screening** (Hinterbichler & Khoury, PRL 104, 231301, 2010) is *literally* a scalar whose Z₂ symmetry is restored above a critical local matter density ρ_crit. It has an action, a genuine order parameter, a real phase transition, and a tanh-like field profile. It is the field-theoretic object this framework has been trying to hand-build.
- **Chameleon** (Khoury & Weltman 2004) keys the thin-shell condition on the local density/potential.
- And the dedicated observational tests of density-environment-dependent gravity already exist: **Jain, Vikram & Sakstein (2013)** on unscreened void dwarfs, **Desmond & Ferreira (2020)** on ~24,000 galaxies.

Two consequences. First, the no-go must be **scoped**: as written it says local-density modification is "excluded," but symmetron/chameleon models are local-density-keyed, action-based, and alive. The defensible theorem is narrower and more interesting: *local volumetric density cannot reproduce the RAR/BTFR, because those relations are organized in acceleration and a density key introduces cross-system scatter of ~1.7 dex* — which is, incidentally, roughly the conclusion the screening community reached by a different route and is why nobody keyed a MOND substitute on ρ after 1983.

Second, and this is the part I'd put in the referee report: the site's own methodology sector reports a **vocabulary-asymmetry test that caught 4/4 prior-art rediscoveries after notation translation**. That test has never been run on artifact #1. The framework's flagship surviving contribution is the one claim that has never been through the framework's own working detector.

**A2ACW: the detector metrics are mislabeled, and the null is underpowered by the site's own standard.**

`/a2acw` reports:
- "**Sensitivity**: 6/6 demoted claims caught"
- "**Specificity**: 0/6 genuine discoveries passed (all flagged as reparametrizations)"

These are swapped. With "genuinely novel" as the positive class, correctly identifying 6 non-novel claims as non-novel is **specificity** (true-negative rate = 6/6), and failing to pass 6 known-genuine discoveries is **sensitivity = 0/6** — a 100% false-negative rate. As printed, the page claims perfect sensitivity for a method that missed every real discovery it was shown.

`/for-researchers` compounds it: "**Measured false-positive rate: 6/6 = 1.0**" and "**Youden's J = 0**." The 6/6 figure is a **false discovery rate** (FP/(FP+TP)), not a false-positive rate (FP/(FP+TN)) — the latter needs a true-negative count that this design doesn't have. And J = sensitivity + specificity − 1: with the site's own numbers (sens = 0, spec = 1) J = 0, but with the labels as printed on `/a2acw` (sens = 1, spec = 0) J is also 0 — the metric is degenerate here and carries no information, which is worth saying rather than reporting as a measurement. Meanwhile `/research-philosophy` states "the document does not provide a formal Youden's J calculation." So J is asserted on one page and disclaimed on another.

The deeper problem: **the program-level null is the only claim on this site that never gets an "underpowered" badge, and it is underpowered.** N = 6. ### Verdict

The physics is settled and the site says so accurately: the framework's only structural departure from MOND is a bounded boost whose ceiling is undefended, and that ceiling is excluded by the stellar-mass–halo-mass relation by one to three orders of magnitude — a conclusion available from the literature before SPARC was touched. Fix the methodology sector to the standard the physics sector already meets, file one genuinely prospective prediction before Gaia DR4, and this becomes a research program with a negative result worth citing rather than an unusually well-documented obituary.
