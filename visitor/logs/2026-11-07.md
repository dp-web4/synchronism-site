## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /what-synchronism-is-not, /interactive-tools, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /learning-paths)

### Journey
I came from Reddit expecting the usual "one equation explains everything" crank site. The headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") fit that. Then the bold first line said **"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."** I wasn't expecting that, and it's the reason I kept reading. It also said AI agents did the research with one human lead, and no outside physicist has reviewed it. That's honest, though it made me a bit wary.

The rest of the first screen lost me fast. In the opening paragraphs and the claims card I hit tanh, ln, γ, ρ_crit, MOND, MRH, ΛCDM, retrodiction, A2ACW and a "Terms on this page" key of about 20 abbreviations (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, dex, CHSH, fσ₈, Youden's J…). The scoreboard says "6 refutations executed (4–8 depending on the rule)", "0 of 9 audited claims surviving" and "the instrument is uncalibrated." I understood "0 confirmed predictions" and nothing else in it. I couldn't tell whether 6 refutations is good or bad, and I didn't know what an "instrument" is when the auditors are AI agents. There are also four different equations on the landing page. As a casual reader, the one I saw was the one I wanted to see.

I clicked **Start Here → Why Synchronism?**. The "What happened, in three lines" box is great: one equation for how crowding makes parts act as one, tested on galaxies, it lost, but the big question stays open. I understood it. The crowd-in-a-plaza vs marching-band picture is the best thing on the site. The sentence "*'Coherence' here is not quantum coherence*" confused me, though. Coherence was the one word I thought I half-knew from YouTube, and now it means something else. Further down the page I skimmed past "sign-inverted", "compander" and "Hill/Naka–Rushton".

**First Encounter** is the friendliest page. It promises 10 minutes and seven short sections, and uses the dimmer switch, the marching band, and "a whisper in a loud room". It explains tanh as an S-curve, and that worked for me. Section C lost me: "γ = 2 loses to MOND; the fitted γ ≈ 0.5 is MOND itself." That's three γ values and a theory I'd only just met. I took it to mean the knob that worked just turned the equation into somebody else's older idea. If that's right, the page should say it in those words. "a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π): a coincidence, not a derivation" means nothing to me.

**What Synchronism Is Not** has a "beginner, you can stop here" box and five plain bullets (not proven, not journal-reviewed, not a replacement, etc.). That part was good, and I stopped there as invited. When I scrolled anyway, the Lorentz card ("one-loop estimate… c_μν ~ α/π ≈ 2×10⁻³") was a wall.

**Learning Paths** says Beginner = "No physics background needed", 5 steps, ~37 min. It also warns that Honest Assessment is an 80-minute read and to stick to its summary box. I'm glad it warned me.

I opened **Honest Assessment** and read the "If you read nothing else" box. Its first bullet, "The one equation failed its galaxy tests", was clear. The page says it is about 18,000 words, and I didn't go further.

**Glossary**: I liked the "New here? Start with these ten" list (Coherence, Presence, γ, tanh, MOND, Dark matter, Reparametrization, Refutation, σ, MRH). It's exactly what a newcomer needs, so it should be linked from the first screen. The definitions I checked were plain ("Unseen mass proposed to explain why the outer parts of galaxies orbit faster…"). But 123 terms on a 5-page beginner path shows how much jargon there is.

### Tools Tested

### Journey
A colleague said "interesting AI project", so I expected a grand-unification pitch. The landing page undercut that within a screen: "0 confirmed predictions", "Refutations executed: 6 (4–8 under rules on file)", "dead as far as it has been tested". I went straight to the theory.

**/parameter-derivations.** The page opens with "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations." I checked the algebra:
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. This is correct (write tanh(u) with e^{2u} = (1+x)^{2γ}).
- Small x: C ≈ γx. Large x: 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ}. Both correct.
- At γ = ½ the function is (y−1)/(y+1) with y = 1+x, which is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). Correct, so the "exactly MOND's simple μ at γ = ½" Reparametrization badge is right.
- The mean-field section says what I would have said. The Weiss tanh comes from a self-consistency loop (m = tanh(βJzm)), while this tanh is evaluated directly at the input with no fixed point. A saturating compander has "no critical point, no diverging correlation length, and no critical exponents." That is the right objection, and the site raises it against itself.
- The γ = 2/√Ncorr ansatz is called sign-inverted: mean-field sharpness grows with N, while the ansatz falls with N. Correct.
- The Fisher correlation between ln γ and ln A is +1.000000. That says only one combination of the two parameters is identified. The page states this.

**/galaxy-rotation.** RMS is 0.1437 dex for McGaugh's ν and 0.1437 for the compander with free γ (γ = 0.49), on 2,807 points. That is the same function, so the free-γ ΔBIC of +7.1 is just the ln N penalty for the extra parameter. The γ = 2 pin gives ΔBIC +184 at point level. The galaxy-level figure is "166 × ln(RSS ratio) = +10.9". I reproduced both numbers: 0.1485²/0.1437² gives ln = 0.0656. But scaling a pooled RSS ratio by N_gal is a heuristic, not a likelihood. The block bootstrap [23, 351] is the better statement, and the page has it.

**/dark-energy.** ρ_DE ≡ ρ_m(1−C)/C is a definition: any H(z) can be written that way. The "Reparametrization — ΛCDM where it fits" badge is earned. At γ = ½, ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit is constant, which is exactly Λ. So the one γ where the galaxy sector works is the γ where cosmology reduces to a cosmological constant. The page itself says "γ ≈ ½ twice is not a cross-sector agreement." That's the most important sentence on the site, and it's honest.

I checked Ġ/G = 3H₀ dlnC/dlnρ. At γ = ½, dlnC/dlnx = 2/(x+2) = 1 − C, and with C₀ = Ω_m this gives 3(1−Ω_m)H₀ ≈ 1.4×10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹. Correct, including the sign. Against LLR's 2σ edge ((7.1 + 2×7.6)×10⁻¹⁴) that is about 630× too large. So the uniform-G/C reading is dead, as the page says. The "at least 527× for every γ" bound is a minimum over γ, so it's consistent with this.

**/honest-assessment.** The page is 175k characters of text, and my fetch truncated it twice. The ledger itself is good:
- TEST-10 (106/153 discs above the 1/Ω_m cap, three at ≥3σ at the most permissive cap) is the one refutation on the framework's own content using external data.
- TEST-25 is labelled "inherited from MOND". That's fair: the framework adopted simple μ, so it inherits simple μ's Cassini problem.
- Bell/CHSH is a construction check, not data. Counting it among "refutations executed" next to data tests mixes two kinds of result, though the row does say "not data".

**/cdm-discrimination.** The page retracts its own −6.2σ and 32σ claims and now says the 0.086 dex "is not a BTFR scatter". That's good practice.
