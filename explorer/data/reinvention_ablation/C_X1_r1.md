**Short answer:** The ingredients are well known, but I don't know of a published paper that makes this specific bound. I can't confirm that from here, so treat it as a likely-novel packaging of known pieces.

**Known background**
- **Environment-dependent G or scalar coupling.** This covers chameleon, symmetron and dilaton screening, and Vainshtein-type mechanisms.
  - Khoury & Weltman, PRL 93, 171104 (2004), and PRD 69, 044026 (2004).
  - Hinterbichler & Khoury, PRL 104, 231301 (2010).
  - Brax, van de Bruck, Davis, Shaw, PRD 82, 063519 (2010), on the dilaton (citation details uncertain).
  - Uzan, Living Rev. Relativ. 14, 2 (2011), a review of varying constants.
- **Local or spatial variation of G tied to matter density.** Barrow & Mota, Class. Quantum Grav. 19, 6197 (2002), "Local and global variations of G", is probably the closest precedent. They discuss G varying with local density and its consequences for Solar-System and Galactic tests (details uncertain).
- **Lunar laser ranging (LLR) bounds on Ġ/G.**
  - Williams, Turyshev & Boggs, PRL 93, 261101 (2004), gives about (4±9)×10⁻¹³ yr⁻¹.
  - Hofmann & Müller, Class. Quantum Grav. 35, 035015 (2018), gives roughly 10⁻¹³ to 10⁻¹⁴ yr⁻¹. The exact value is uncertain.
- **Ġ from motion through a gradient.** The relation Ġ = v·∇G is a standard convective-derivative argument. It is used for cosmological or Galactic-scale G variations and for chameleon time variation along the Earth's orbit.

**What looks new, or at least unpublished**
- Using a smoothed density ρ̄_ℓ as the argument of f, so that the Sun's Galactic motion sets a drift. Known work typically uses the local density, a screening-radius criterion, or a cosmological time dependence instead.
- Turning the LLR bound into a form-independent bound on d ln f/d ln ρ as a function of ℓ.

**Checks and caveats**
- The linearization makes the bound form-independent only if f varies slowly over the density excursions the Sun experiences. Strongly nonlinear screening, such as chameleon thin shells, breaks this.
- ℓ must be large enough to exclude the Sun's and the Earth's own density. Otherwise the Earth–Moon system sits in a self-sourced region, and the ambient gradient is not what matters.
- With v⊙ ≈ 20 km/s ≈ 2×10⁻⁵ pc yr⁻¹, your quoted bounds of 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁵ imply gradient scales L ≈ 2 pc to 2 kpc. The range is plausible but depends on what you assume for ∇ ln ρ̄_ℓ. Check that this assumption is consistent across ℓ = 1–10 pc.
- Other bounds likely dominate. Planetary ephemerides, binary pulsars and Cassini-type tests constrain the same coupling, and environmental-screening papers usually argue that the Solar-System density already suppresses any such effect.

**Recommendation:** Search arXiv and ADS for "spatial variation of G", "galactic gradient" with "Ġ", and "density-dependent gravitational constant" before claiming priority. Cite Barrow & Mota (2002) and the chameleon and symmetron papers as context.
