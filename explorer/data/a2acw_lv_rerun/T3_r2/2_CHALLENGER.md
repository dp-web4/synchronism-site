## Press 1: Where the analogy breaks (it is stronger than the Primary conceded)

1. **Integration yields no interference.** Time-integrating a CRT gives a *sum of intensities* (Σ|aᵢ|²), an incoherent mixture. Wave behavior needs *amplitude* addition with cross terms (|Σaᵢ|²). "Slow sampling = wave" therefore reproduces smoothness but not fringes. The slit mapping has no phase-carrying quantity, so the wave/particle claim fails even as an intuition pump.
2. **"Raster uncertainty" is epistemic and removable.** A pixel-duration dot is perfectly predictable once you know the clock. Quantum outcomes in non-commuting bases are not removable that way. "One cycle phase fixes all outcomes" is a *noncontextual hidden-variable model*, which Kochen-Specker rules out for d ≥ 3 **without any locality assumption**. So Bell is not the only wall, and the nonlocal escape does not rescue it.
3. **The §5.4 escape routes are mutually exclusive with the text.** The only ways out of CHSH are nonlocality (contradicts "nothing travels"), superdeterminism (contradicts independent witness timing), or giving up realism (contradicts "always definite"). Synchronism must pick one.

## Press 2: Does sync explain collapse, or only perception?

The whitepaper's own "observation collapse" bullet is "syncing with the dot and finding it always in the same spot thereafter." On a real CRT that is false, because the dot keeps moving. Collapse is physically *repeatability*: an immediate re-measurement gives the same outcome. If the pattern keeps cycling and sync has "no effect," repeatability needs a locking mechanism. That locking is exactly an observer effect, and §5.5's Zeno bullet ("synchronization timing influences system behavior") concedes it. As written, sync timing explains at most *which phase is reported*, not post-measurement stability.
