## Point 1: Bell/CHSH. The mapping fails at the settings, not just the correlation

Your concession ("explains classical-style correlation only") is correct but incomplete. The CRT picture doesn't even reach classical CHSH structure.

- The §5.4 claim "whatever sampling rate you use reveals the same result on both" yields only perfect correlation at *identical* settings. Bell tests turn on *different* settings (a, b) and require E(a,b) = −cos(a−b), which gives S = 2√2.
- The model needs a specific place where the setting enters. If the setting is "sampling phase," the shared cycle phase is a local hidden variable λ, and S ≤ 2 follows directly. §5.4 itself says "Hidden variables: intent patterns contain all necessary information" and "Determinism," so the text asserts the premise Bell's theorem rules out.
- "Deterministic synchronization perceived as probabilistic due to sync uncertainty" is an ignorance interpretation of probability. It is exactly what Bell/KS constrain.

**Q1:** Write E(a,b) for a synchronized-cycle model, say where the setting enters, and show it exceeds 2 for CHSH. Otherwise, state that the model evades Bell only by giving up locality or measurement independence, and say which.

## Point 2: Sampling versus collapse. The analogy inverts the physics

You claim temporal integration versus point sampling explains wave versus particle appearance. This breaks in three places.

1. **Single-electron double slit.** Each detection is already a "pixel-duration," point-like event, yet interference accumulates over many events. Visibility is set by *which-path distinguishability*, not by detector integration time. Changing τ (within a stable-source regime) doesn't switch the pattern. Changing path-marking does. That is §5.5's "intent pattern modification," which contradicts §5.3's "just resampling."
2. **Internal scale contradiction.** §5.1 sets the period T at Planck time (about 10⁻⁴³ s). Every lab detector then has τ/T ≳ 10³⁰, so all detectors are always in the "slow, integrating" regime. The model then predicts wave behavior everywhere and has no mechanism for particle-like clicks. V(τ/T) is degenerate.
3. **Aliasing is not a quantum phenomenon.** Time-averaging a periodic signal is ordinary signal processing. Standard QM already contains it (time-resolution limits, rotating-wave averaging). Your narrowed claim is true but doesn't distinguish Synchronism from anything.

**Q2:** Give one observable where Synchronism's V(τ/T) differs from standard which-path-distinguishability visibility. Also explain how the model reconciles "measurement doesn't touch the pattern" (§5.3, §5.5 "no disturbance") with "path marking requires pattern modification" and "synchronization timing influences system behavior" (Zeno, §5.5).

## Hidden premises I'm flagging

- **Passive sampling.** The claim assumes the witness's sync is causally inert on the system. §5.5's Zeno bullets and §5.5's which-path bullets deny this.
- **Ignorance reading of probability.** "Unpredictable dot location" in a CRT is ignorance of a deterministic raster. Quantum randomness would have to be the same thing, which is the contested point.
- **Pre-existing definite phase.** This is contextuality (Kochen–Specker) territory, and you already conceded there is no mapping.
- **Equivocation on "measurement."** "Measurement" switches between perceptual sampling (CRT) and physical interaction (detector). The analogy is only valid for the first, and quantum puzzles live in the second.
