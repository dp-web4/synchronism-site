**Short answer:** I think the specific construction is probably not published as stated. Its ingredients are all standard, so it is a recombination of known pieces rather than a new mechanism.

**Known pieces**
- **Environment-dependent G.** Chameleon, symmetron and related scalar-tensor models make the effective coupling depend on local density. Khoury & Weltman, PRL 93, 171104 (2004), and Hinterbichler & Khoury, PRL 104, 231301 (2010), are the standard references. Mota & Shaw, PRL 97, 151102 (2006), treat strongly coupled versions.
- **Reviews of varying G and its bounds.** Uzan, Rev. Mod. Phys. 75, 403 (2003), and Living Rev. Relativ. 14, 2 (2011). Both discuss Ġ/G from spatial gradients of a scalar field as the Sun moves through them.
- **LLR bounds.**
  - Williams, Turyshev & Boggs, PRL 93, 261101 (2004), gave Ġ/G = (4±9)×10⁻¹³ yr⁻¹.
  - Hofmann & Müller, Class. Quantum Grav. 35, 035015 (2018), is tighter at about 10⁻¹³ yr⁻¹ (citation uncertain).
- **Galactic or dark-matter-induced drifts.** Variations of "constants" driven by the Sun's motion through a spatially varying field have been considered, e.g. in the ultralight dark-matter literature (Hees, Minazzoli et al. 2018, uncertain). Mach-like or Sciama-type ideas link G to the cosmic matter distribution, but I know of no paper using a smoothing length ℓ in exactly this way.

**Your claim**
- The estimate Ġ/G ≈ v⊙·∇ln ρ̄ · dln f/dln ρ is just the chain rule. The "independent of the form of f" statement holds only to first order in small variations.
- The bound is not a published result as far as I know, but it is a straightforward application of the known LLR limit.

**Caveats**
- **Numbers.** With v⊙ ≈ 20 km/s ≈ 2×10⁻⁵ pc/yr and a gradient scale L, the bound is dlnf/dlnρ ≲ 5×10⁻⁹ (L/pc). Your 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁵ range therefore requires L of order 2 pc to 2 kpc. Please state which L you assume for each ℓ.
- **The Sun's own mass.** For ℓ of 1–10 pc, the smoothed density includes the Sun's own mass (~1 M⊙ pc⁻³), comparable to the ambient ~0.1 M⊙ pc⁻³. That makes the gradient along the Sun's path ill-defined.
- **Fluctuations.** Stellar encounters and cloud crossings give fluctuating, not smooth, ρ̄ over decades.
- **Screening.** In chameleon-type models, Earth–Moon dynamics are dominated by the Earth's own density and screening, so the response to the ambient density is suppressed. The bound then constrains only unscreened f(ρ̄) models.
- **Other effects.** Such a G would also differ across space, violating the strong equivalence principle. Nordtvedt-effect and other tests may be stronger.

I'd recommend a literature search on "spatial variation of G, Ġ/G, galactic gradient, LLR" before claiming novelty.
