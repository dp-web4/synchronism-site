## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The landing page and the equation walkthrough are genuinely great at pulling a non-physicist in — and then the "no physics background needed" beginner path walks that same person straight into ΔBIC, fσ₈ and Abrikosov-Gor'kov.

### Journey

I clicked this from Reddit expecting either crackpot physics or a slick theory-of-everything pitch. Got neither, which is why I stayed. The headline is a *question* — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — and then immediately under it, a scorecard saying **0 predictions confirmed, 6 refutations executed, 0 of 6 "validated" claims surviving audit**. I have literally never seen a science site lead with its own failures. That's the hook. I read the whole landing page.

One thing snagged me right there though. The scorecard puts "3,308 research sessions / 14,610 galaxies analyzed / 1,703 chemical phenomena" right next to "0 predictions confirmed." My gut read those big numbers as brag numbers, and then the next line says nothing worked. For about thirty seconds I thought the page was contradicting itself. It isn't — but nobody told me how to read those two things together.

I hit **Beginner Path →** and landed on **Why Synchronism?**. This is Step 1 of 6 on the path labeled *"No physics background needed. Plain language, core concepts only."* Here is what Step 1 actually contained: "audited-negative," "γ = 2/√Ncorr is inverted for collective systems," "Bose-Einstein condensates score incorrectly," "BTFR slope," "reparametrization," "MOND," "phenomenological." I understood the marching-band-versus-crowd analogy — that one is great, I got it instantly — and then I understood approximately nothing for the next several paragraphs. If I hadn't been curious about the honesty angle I would have bounced right here, on the first page of the beginner path.

**First Encounter** (Step 2) was better. Ninety-second sections, no-background-needed framing, marching band again. But it introduces ρ as "**presence**" — and the landing page and Core Idea both call ρ "**density**." I spent a while genuinely unsure whether presence and density are the same thing or two different quantities. (The glossary eventually told me presence is a generalized density. Nobody told me on the pages where I got confused.) It also drops "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)" and glosses it as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" — which I liked! — and then immediately re-glosses it as "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence," which took the understanding back away.

**The Core Idea** (Step 3) is where I met the equation properly: C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)). The "S-shaped dimmer switch" image is the single best thing on this site for someone like me. But the page also assumes I know Hill coefficients, Ising models, central limit theorem, Naka-Rushton curves, and what a "compander" is. I do not.

Then I went to **Tools**, because sliders are where I actually learn things.

**Coherence Explorer** — I dragged γ around and watched the S-curve get steeper and flatter. This worked and it taught me something real: the dial genuinely does control how abruptly a system flips from "crowd" to "band." Two snags. First, the note tells me to "move γ from 2.0 downward and watch the curve flatten" — but the slider *starts* at 0.49, so the instruction doesn't match what's on my screen and I had to drag *up* first to follow it. Second, and this bugged me more: the chart draws a reference line at C = 0.50, and the parameter is called ρ**crit** — "critical" density. Every instinct I have says the critical point is where the curve crosses the middle line. It isn't. The readout right there says C(ρcrit) = 0.3272. Nothing on that page explains why the "critical" density isn't the halfway point.

**Equation Anatomy** — this one is the best page on the site for me and it's not close. It takes the scary formula apart in six steps and explains ln as "a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger" and tanh as "like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0, input very large → output near 1." That is *exactly* the right altitude. I understood the equation after this page. Whoever wrote this should be handed the beginner path.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** — I picked NGC 3198 and DDO 154 out of the dropdown. Actual galaxies, actual measured dots, curves that don't match. I could see the problem with my own eyes: the gray "visible matter only" line droops, the blue observed dots stay flat, there's a gap, that gap is what people call dark matter. Great. Then there are five lines and one of them is a dotted amber curve labelled as a hand-tuned illustration that was "formerly displayed." Why am I being shown a curve you took down? I get that it's an honesty thing, but as a casual reader it was the line I had to work hardest to dismiss, and the legend also says "max C on this disk: 0.001" which meant nothing to me.

**Consciousness Threshold Demo** — I clicked this because consciousness is the thing I actually care about. Eight different approaches all landing on C ≈ 0.50, mean 0.499. For one second that looked stunning. Then the page tells me the convergence is "geometrically forced," all eight share the same assumptions, they're not independent, and no mapping exists from any brain measurement to this axis at all. So the demo's entire content is a chart that exists to show you the chart means nothing. I respect it enormously and I also don't know what I was supposed to *do* with it.

**Honest Assessment** (Step 5 of the *beginner* path) is where I fell off. There is one paragraph in there — "What failed, in one paragraph, no jargon" — that is clear and honest and told me the story. The rest is ΔBIC=+184, 2.4σ, fσ₈, SPARC, RAR, dex, Ω_m, and ∇²Φ = 4πGρ/C sitting in the middle of a sentence. Plus dated corrections stacked inside the text ("Corrected 2026-07-14," "CORRECTED 2026-08-09") that break the thread every few paragraphs. I could extract "it didn't work." I could not extract *how they know*, which is the part I came for.

**What Synchronism Is Not** (Step 4, also beginner) has waveguide QED and Abrikosov-Gor'kov in it. That's a beginner page.

Last stop, **Glossary** — and it's good! MOND, MRH, presence, tanh, compander, ΛCDM, BTFR, reparametrization, all defined in plain-ish language. The problem is that I only found it at the end. Every page that used these words could have linked to it and none of them appeared to.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — the γ slider taught me the core idea faster than any prose on the site; undercut by an instruction that doesn't match the default value and by "critical" density not being the midpoint.
- **Equation Anatomy** — worked — intuitive — best thing on the site for my level. The ln and tanh explanations are the only place a formula stopped being scary. Six steps was the right length.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — mildly confusing — real galaxies and real dots made the dark matter problem visible to me in one glance. Too many curves, one of them retracted, and an unexplained "max C = 0.001" in the legend.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — confusing on purpose — a chart built to demonstrate that its own striking result is a geometric artifact. Admirable, but as a casual visitor I couldn't tell what I was meant to take away besides "don't be impressed."

### Unanswered Questions
1. Are "presence" and "density" the same thing? I still only half-know.
2. Why is ρcrit called *critical* if the curve doesn't do anything special there?
3. What is an "Artifact Lesson"? It's a badge on two tools and I never found it defined.
4. If the galaxy test "collapsed onto MOND" — does that mean Synchronism is wrong, or that it's right but says the same thing as an idea that already existed? The site clearly distinguishes these and I still couldn't tell which one happened.
5. The site says it's "dead as tested physics but alive as a public record." So is anyone still working on the physics, or is this now a project about how to do research honestly? Genuinely unclear what I'd be coming back for.
6. What would it actually take for this to be *right*? Every page tells me what failed; none told me what a win would look like in words I understand.

### Verdict

The honesty is the product and it works — I trusted this site within a minute, which is more than I can say for most physics-adjacent things I get sent from Reddit. The equation walkthrough and the galaxy plotter proved that this material *can* be explained to someone like me, and the marching-band-versus-crowd image is genuinely good. But the beginner path is mislabeled: three of its six steps (Why Synchronism, What Synchronism Is Not, Honest Assessment) are written for people who already know what BTFR and ΔBIC and Abrikosov-Gor'kov are. The site has the pieces for a great beginner experience and has them sequenced in a way that makes a beginner quit at Step 1. I'd come back if someone told me the failures had been rewritten in the same voice as the equation walkthrough.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The site has an unusually disciplined *idea* of its own vocabulary — a canonical badge-definitions block, a 72-term glossary, consistent breadcrumbs — and almost none of it is wired to the places where a reader actually meets the words.

### Journey

I audited this as a documentation set, not as physics. I checked every top-nav destination, followed cross-references, and tracked one vocabulary — the validation badges — across every page that uses it. That last exercise is where the report lives.

**Navigation.** Top nav is nine flat items: Home, Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. Flat is defensible at this size. But the breadcrumbs describe a *different* structure than the nav does. Breadcrumbs read `Home / Getting Started / Learning Paths`, `Home / Core Theory / MRH`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1`, `Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator`. Four section names — Getting Started, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments, Interactive Tools — none of which is a nav item, and none of which I was ever able to reach as a page. A breadcrumb's middle crumb is a promise that a level exists. Here it's decorative. Either surface those four as real nav groups (which would genuinely help — nine flat items already reads as a bag), or drop the middle crumb.

Smaller version of the same thing: nav says **Tools**, the destination page and its own breadcrumb say **Interactive Tools**. Pick one label per destination.

`/research-philosophy` breaks the breadcrumb pattern entirely. Instead of `Home / … / Research Philosophy` it shows *"Methodology Track (Step 1 of 6) → Next: A2ACW Protocol."* That's a path-progress widget occupying the breadcrumb slot, with no Home crumb at all. It's also a page reachable from top nav that presents itself as step 1 of a track I hadn't chosen. Same issue in reverse on Honest Assessment: top-nav destination *and* step 5 of the Beginner Path. The nav and the paths are two competing IAs over the same pages and neither acknowledges the other.

**Two URLs, one page.** `/tier-1-existing` says the tier hierarchy "is defined on the [Test Roadmap](/test-roadmap)." Top nav's Test Roadmap points at `/test-catalog`. I fetched both. They serve the same content — same breadcrumb, same first three headings ("Test Roadmap" / "What's Already Been Analyzed" / "Overview by Tier"), same TEST-26 entry dated 2026-08-10. So nav links one URL and body prose links the other for an identical page. Nobody's blocked by this, but it splits inbound links and search indexing, and it's the kind of thing that quietly becomes two divergent pages later.

**The badge vocabulary — the main finding.** I counted the distinct status labels a reader is shown, page by page:

| Page | Badge inventory presented | Legend on page? |
|---|---|---|
| Landing | 4: Reparametrization, Speculative, Untested, Failed | Partial — defines Reparametrization inline, links canonical |
| Glossary | ~14, incl. all 5 MRH-relationship tags | Yes (it *is* the definitions) |
| Test Roadmap | 9: 5 MRH-relationship + 4 descriptive | Yes, with "Canonical definitions →" link |
| Key Claims | 3 in use, 0 defined | No — links canonical. **Correct behaviour.** |
| Interactive Tools | 6 distinct kinds (see below) | Partial — legend covers 3 of them |
| Tier 1: Existing Data | 13, incl. MOND-shared (retired), NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, CLOSED BY EXECUTION, FAILED — ROBUST EMPTY INTERSECTION | None found |
| Honest Assessment | ~30 across 4 families + 9 lifecycle verbs | Yes — this is canonical |

That's four different subsets of one vocabulary taught as if each were the whole thing. The landing page is the worst offender precisely because it's the most confident: it teaches four badges and calls Reparametrization "this site's most common verdict," which primes a reader to believe they now know the scheme. Two clicks later, on Tier 1, they meet `Active-MRH`, `MOND-shared (retired)`, `UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED` and `FAILED — ROBUST EMPTY INTERSECTION` with no legend and no link to one. The whole MRH-relationship family — five tags, apparently the preferred family — is invisible from the landing page.

The fix is not "put the full list everywhere." It's what Key Claims already does, and does well: use the badges, define none of them locally, link once to the canonical block. Every page should do exactly that, and the landing page should stop teaching a four-item subset.

**Badges are three unrelated axes wearing the same chip.** On `/interactive-tools` the labels attached to tools are: `Beginner`, `Advanced`, `Model Explainer`, `Artifact Lesson`, `Reparametrization`, `Speculative`. Those are three orthogonal things — *who it's for*, *what pedagogical role it plays*, *whether the physics survived audit* — rendered identically. The page's own legend covers three of the six and explicitly warns that "Model Explainer indicates pedagogical function, not correctness validation" — which is exactly the confusion the shared visual treatment creates in the first place. When your legend has to say "this badge doesn't mean what the badge next to it means," the badges need different shapes, not a footnote. Split them: audience as a small grey pill, verdict as the coloured badge, pedagogical role as a line of description.

**Same tool, two different verdicts on adjacent pages.** The tools index badges γ Calculator as **Artifact Lesson**. The γ Calculator page itself headlines *"Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems."* Both are real labels in the canonical taxonomy; neither page says they're the same judgement, and the tool page defines and links *neither* term. `Audited-Negative` appears there as a bare heading. A reader who cared enough to click has no way to learn what it means without guessing that the Glossary or Honest Assessment has it. (It does — both do.)

**The Glossary is a terminal node.** It's genuinely good: 72 terms, alphabetised, badge families grouped, plain-language definitions, breadcrumbed, correctly placed as Beginner step 6. And nothing links *into* it. I checked `/mrh`, `/coherence-explorer` and `/honest-assessment` specifically: no inline glossary links on any of them, only the global header link. `/mrh` uses Markov blanket, decoherence, phase transition and correlation length ξ without on-page definitions and without pointing anywhere. If I had to pick one change for this site, it's this: linking first use of every glossary term from the page where it appears would fix a large share of what all four personas are going to report, and it's mechanical work, not writing work.

**Acronyms on first use.** Systematically not expanded. Landing page uses SPARC, BTFR, RAR, DESI, ΛCDM, CHSH, σ₈, fσ₈, Ω_m with no expansion; it *does* expand MOND, MRH, A2ACW and BAO, so the convention exists and is applied unevenly. Honest Assessment is better — it expands MRH, MOND, SPARC, EFE, GR, CDM, TFR, BAO, LIV, QFT, CMB — but still runs BTFR, RAR, DESI, CHSH, RSD, EFTofLSS and SME bare. Note that BTFR and RAR are expanded *nowhere* I visited, including the page that leans hardest on them.

**One definition, two registers, no bridge.** The landing page glosses MRH as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that actually influence a system." The `/mrh` page defines it as "the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system." Both are good sentences for their audience. The problem is that neither acknowledges the other: the landing page doesn't signal it's simplifying, and the dedicated page never offers the plain version. A reader arriving from the landing page has to work out unaided that these are the same concept.

**A correction that exists three times and isn't at the point of use.** The landing page already says ρcrit is a "saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)." The Phase Boundary Visualizer says "despite the historical name, C(ρ) is a smooth compander … with no critical point." Honest Assessment and the Glossary carry it too. The Coherence Explorer — the one page where a reader is actively dragging ρcrit around and watching a chart — draws a horizontal reference line at C = 0.50 described as the midpoint, and carries no version of the note. The site has written this correction at least three times and not put it where it's needed. That's a propagation problem, not a writing problem.

**Two reference values for γ, unreconciled.** Coherence Explorer opens at γ = 0.49. The γ Calculator plots a grey comparison curve labelled "γ = 2 galaxy baseline." I'm not equipped to say which is current and I'm not trying to — but a reader moving between two tools in the same section sees two different canonical-looking values for the same parameter and nothing on either page explains the relationship. One sentence on each would do it.

**Test ID conventions.** The Roadmap defines the suffixes: `a` = "amended sub-test," `s` = "substituted protocol, different sample." Tier 1 then describes TEST-04a as a "replacement test." Amended and replacement aren't the same word, and for an ID scheme that's load-bearing across two pages, the gloss should be identical or the suffix should be defined once and only once. Also worth noting: the anchor convention `/tier-1-existing#TEST-04a` is excellent and I wish more of the site used deep anchors like this.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** — worked — mostly intuitive — Genuinely well-built: presets from ideal gas to BEC, a live S-curve, N_corr defined on the page as "the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units," which is exactly how you define a term. Undercut by two things: the page's own verdict heading uses an undefined, unlinked term (`Audited-Negative`), and the tool's four classification labels ("Weakly Correlated (γ-sharp)", "Boundary (γ ≈ 1)"…) are a *fifth* label vocabulary that doesn't appear in the badge taxonomy at all.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — worked — intuitive — The best-documented tool I used. It states its purpose plainly ("see which physical systems the γ formula groups together — and why that grouping is now known to be misleading"), redefines the misleading term inline ("despite the historical name…no critical point"), and puts the killer caveat in a call-out box: "Ideal gases and galaxies both map to γ = 2.0, despite having completely different microphysics." It also links out to both the Glossary and the γ Calculator — the *only* page in this audit that linked into the glossary from its own body. This page is the site's own house style, already written. Copy it.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Which badge family is a reader supposed to use? The landing page teaches the descriptive four; the Test Roadmap gives the MRH-relationship five equal billing; Honest Assessment lists ~30. Is there a hierarchy between the families, or are they alternatives?
2. Are "Artifact Lesson," "Model Explainer" and "Beginner/Advanced" meant to be badges at all, or captions that got badge styling?
3. What is the intended relationship between the top nav and the Learning Paths? Several nav destinations are mid-path steps, and several path steps aren't in nav. Is one of them the primary IA?
4. Is `/test-roadmap` or `/test-catalog` the canonical URL?
5. Is there a style guide or badge single-source-of-truth behind this? The consistency is too good in places (breadcrumb format, anchor convention, ID suffixes) to be accidental, and too uneven in others to be enforced.
6. `Untested` appears in the landing page's four-badge set but I didn't find it in use on Key Claims. Is it live, or a leftover from an older taxonomy?

### Verdict

This is a documentation set with a real spine — a canonical definitions block, a deep-linkable ID scheme, a 72-term glossary, a consistent breadcrumb format, and at least one page (the Phase Boundary Visualizer) that is genuinely exemplary. The failure is uniformly one of *propagation*: every correction, definition and legend I looked for turned out to already exist somewhere on the site and to be missing from the page where a reader needs it. That's the good kind of problem — nothing here needs to be written, only wired — but it's currently costing the site more clarity than any individual page's prose does. Link the glossary inline, make every page point at one badge definition instead of teaching a subset, and split the three things the badges are currently conflating.

### Summary

- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The self-audit is real and unusually good — but the site keeps stopping one algebraic step short of its own sharpest conclusion, and in two places its kill statistics are computed against the wrong error bar.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," which usually means I'm about to read a theory of everything written by someone who has never fit a rotation curve. I opened the landing page ready to bounce and instead got a scorecard: 0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations executed, 0 of 6 "validated" claims surviving audit. **First stop, the equation.** `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. /equation-walkthrough gives the closed form immediately — `tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]`, a Hill function — and I checked it: correct. It also says outright that "tanh and γ = 2/√Ncorr are motivated choices, not derived results (there is no derivation to walk through — only choices to examine)." Good. That's the sentence most frameworks won't write.

But sitting with the Hill form for thirty seconds gets you somewhere the site never goes. **Set γ = 1/2.** Then `C = [(1+x) − 1]/[(1+x) + 1] = x/(x+2)`. And MOND's simple interpolating function is `μ(y) = y/(1+y)`. Put `y = x/2`: `μ(x/2) = (x/2)/(1 + x/2) = x/(x+2)`. **These are the same function, identically, with ρcrit absorbing a factor of 2.** Not "MOND-like." Not "reduces to." Equal.

Now cross that against the site's own results. /honest-assessment reports that when γ is freed on the SPARC RAR, the fit returns γ ≈ 0.49. The site reads that as "the free fit lands near MOND." The correct reading is stronger and worse: **the free fit lands exactly ON MOND's simple μ, to within 2% of the parameter.** Combined with the framework's own `f_DM = 1 − C`, C *is* an interpolating function — the equation's entire content in the galaxy sector is a reparametrized μ. The site has both halves of this on separate pages and never multiplies them together. /gamma-boundary — the page literally named for γ values — contains no mention of γ = 1/2, no MOND identity, and no discussion of the γ–ρcrit degeneracy this implies. That's the biggest miss on the site, and it's a miss in the direction the site claims to care about.

**Second stop, the derivations.** /parameter-derivations is the most honest page I have seen on a site like this. γ = 2/√Ncorr: "Speculative — Motivated Ansatz," with the admission that "invoking CLT for correlated Ncorr is self-contradictory." Correct, and worse than stated — 1/√N in fluctuation theory is a *width*, so more correlation should mean a *sharper* transition, and this formula gives a flatter one. The site says this. The /gamma-calculator tool says it in the badge. Fine.

ρcrit = A·V_flat²: "Audited-Negative — Chain-of-Custody Failure," stated formula gives 4.6×10⁻⁵ against the used value 0.029, 600× off. a₀ = cH₀/2π: "Reparametrization," with McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017, Smolin 2017 credited and Milgrom's own 1983 observation acknowledged. ### Where the math actually breaks

**(1) The galaxy plotter implements a force law the site does not have.**

/galaxy-plotter's violet "Synchronism (real)" curve is coded as

    v(r) = √(v_b² + [V_flat·C(ρ(r))]²)

That is an *additive* coherence-weighted halo term. But /galaxy-rotation states the framework's boost as `B = 1/C`, i.e. `g_obs = g_bar/C`, giving `v² = v_b²/C`. These are not the same model and they are not even the same limit:

| | plotter, `v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)²` | stated law, `v² = v_b²/C` |
|---|---|---|
| C → 0 | extra term → **0** (pure Newtonian) | boost → **∞** |
| C → 1 | adds a fixed flat term | no boost at all |

They are opposite. Worse, look at where the plotter's extra term switches on: it scales with C, and C rises with density, and density falls outward. So the plotter's dark-matter-like component is **largest in the galaxy centre and vanishes in the outskirts** — exactly inverted from where every rotation curve needs it. That inversion is structural, not an amplitude problem.

Which matters, because the legend's diagnosis is `"max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on"` — blaming the *amplitude*. But even if C were 1 everywhere, this form would add a rigid flat term at all radii including r → 0, and would still fail. The plotter is showing a sign failure and labelling it a magnitude failure. Fixing ρcrit would not save it.

**(2) ΔBIC = +184 audits the negligible term and not the large one.**

/honest-assessment defends the RAR γ=2 kill: "The γ=2 result is a pure likelihood verdict (Δk = 0): ΔBIC = +184 is decisive misfit," and flags one caveat — "Open bookkeeping item: whether a₀ was floated in both arms, i.e. whether Δk = 1 or 2."

That caveat is worth `k·ln(N) = ln(2807) ≈ 8`. The caveat that is *not* flagged is worth a factor of sixteen. The fit uses "N = 2807 real SPARC points" drawn from ~175 galaxies. Points within one galaxy share a distance, an inclination, and a mass-to-light ratio — they are nowhere near independent, and with Δk = 0, ΔBIC *is* Δχ², which scales with the number of independent constraints, not the number of rows. At N_eff ≈ N_galaxies ≈ 175, **ΔBIC = +184 → ≈ +11.**

The verdict survives — +11 is still "very strong" on Kass–Raftery — but the headline number is an order of magnitude too big, and a page whose entire thesis is "we over-claimed" is presenting it as decisive while auditing the ln(N) term instead. This is the same failure mode the site catches everywhere else; it just hasn't caught it here.

**(3) The a₀ comparison is quoted against the random error only — and it cuts *toward* the framework.**

/mond-unification gives `a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²` against "Milgrom's observed value" 1.20×10⁻¹⁰, a "13% error." I checked the arithmetic (H₀ = 67.4 ⇒ cH₀/2π = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰): correct.

But McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016 quote **a₀ = 1.20 ± 0.02 (random) ± 0.24 (systematic)** ×10⁻¹⁰, the systematic being dominated by stellar M/L. Against the full budget:

    (1.20 − 1.042)/√(0.02² + 0.24²) = 0.66σ

That is **consistent**, not a 13% failure. The ±0.24 appears nowhere on /honest-assessment — I searched for it specifically. Same issue shadows the "~8σ per bin" RAR residual claim: M/L systematics are the dominant uncertainty on RAR normalization and no page I visited carries them.

I want to be clear this is an *over*-refutation, not an overclaim. The site is being harder on itself than the data warrants. But a stat quoted against the wrong error bar is wrong in either direction, and a site that has built its credibility on statistical honesty should not be inflating its own kills.

**(4) The MOND reference curve uses an interpolating function the site's own TEST-25 excludes.**

The plotter's green comparison is `ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y)` — I verified this is the correct inverse of simple-μ, and `v = v_b√ν` is right. But *simple*-μ is precisely the family killed by Solar-System quadrupole constraints: the site's own TEST-25 reports the "Cassini discrepancy: +17.95σ," and Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024) exclude simple-μ at 8.7σ from Cassini. Meanwhile /galaxy-rotation quotes MOND as the RAR e-function `1/(1 − e^(−√y))`.

So: two different MOND functions on two pages, differing by ~0.1 dex through the transition, one of them excluded by a result the site itself reports, with no note on either page. When the plotter's takeaway is "MOND tracks the data, Synchronism doesn't," the MOND being drawn should at least be a live one.

**(5) The plotter is normalized by the relation the framework fails.**

**(6) The site is speaking Landau and doing something else entirely.**

/gamma-boundary gets the mathematics exactly right — and I checked all of it. The derivative `dC/dx = 4γ·t^(2γ−1)/(t^(2γ)+1)², t = 1+x` is correct, `dC/dx|_{x=0} = γ` is correct, and the concavity claim — "**concave on the whole domain** — d²C/dx² < 0 for every x ≥ 0 — so there is no inflection point to sit at" — is correct for all γ > 0 (the log-derivative of dC/dt is `2γ − 1 − 4γu/(u+1)` with `u = t^(2γ)`, which is −1 at x=0 and decreasing thereafter). The S-shape is an artifact of the log axis — toggle /coherence-explorer to linear x and the sigmoid disappears into a plain saturating curve. What is left is a logarithmic compander, which is a perfectly respectable object; it just isn't Landau theory.

And yet the vocabulary is all Landau: "Phase Boundary Visualizer," "transition sharpness (γ)," ρ_**crit**, "γ boundary," "critical density." The one page that gets it right — the glossary/landing note that ρcrit is a "saturation knee" — is contradicted by the naming of half the site. For a condensed-matter reader this is the single most misleading thing here, and it's misleading in a way the site's own math has already refuted. ### 1. The dark-energy refutation is an artifact of forcing w₀, and at the site's own γ the sector is ΛCDM

`/dark-energy` gives the sector cleanly. From `H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C)` it defines `ρ_DE ≡ ρ_m(1−C)/C`, and states the central structural result:

> "w runs from w = −2γ in the far past to exactly w = −1 in the far future, for every γ"

with the consequence `sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ)` — no w = −1 crossing. Then:

> DESI DR2 prefers "w₀ > −1 with wₐ < 0" across all four data combinations; the framework's predictions are "offset 3.4–5.4σ across the combinations" **when forced to match DESI's w₀**.

Take the site's own numbers and substitute. The galaxy sector's SPARC-preferred value is **γ ≈ 0.489** (`/tier-1-existing`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer`). Then:

    w(far past) = −2γ = −0.978
    w(far future) = −1
    ⇒ |w(z) + 1| ≤ 0.022 for all z

**At its own fitted γ, this dark-energy sector is ΛCDM to within 2.2% in w, at every redshift.** DESI DR2 disfavours ΛCDM at roughly 2.8σ (Pantheon+) to 4.2σ (DES-Y5) depending on the SN compilation. A model that sits 0.022 away from w = −1 is disfavoured at *that same significance and no more*. There is no 3.4–5.4σ. That number is what you get by pinning w₀ to DESI's w₀ ≈ −0.75 — a value the model cannot occupy — and then measuring how wrong wₐ comes out. Conditioning one parameter on the data and reporting the residual of the other is not a joint fit and it does not produce a σ you can quote.

This matters more than a bad number, because of what it implies for the whole DE sector:

- The sign lock is **not a framework-specific prediction**. It is the standard no-go for any minimally-coupled single scalar, and it is trivially satisfied by ΛCDM (w = −1, wₐ = 0). The framework's DE sector makes *the same prediction as ΛCDM* and fails in *exactly the same way*. It carries zero framework-specific information.
- **TEST-26 therefore has no power.** `/test-catalog` registers it honestly as "kill-or-tie," which is right, but the page presents it as the sector's live discriminator. It is not: DR3 confirming a crossing would kill ΛCDM, thawing quintessence, freezing quintessence, and this framework in one stroke. A test that fires on a whole class is not a test of a member of that class.
- **The kill criterion is parameterization-dependent.** DESI's "crossing" is a property of the CPL form. With w₀ ≈ −0.75, wₐ ≈ −0.86 the CPL line crosses w = −1 at a = 1 − (1+w₀)/|wₐ| ≈ 0.71, i.e. z ≈ 0.4 — inside the data. But non-parametric reconstructions of the same data do not require a crossing; the crossing is substantially forced by CPL's two-parameter rigidity. Meanwhile the framework's own w(z) — monotone from −2γ to −1 — is **not** CPL. Comparing a non-CPL w(z) to CPL-compressed (w₀, wₐ) contours without projecting the model through the same compression is a methodological error, and it is the error that generates the "wrong-sign wₐ."

Separately: `/key-claims` lists three claims (QM, consciousness, dark matter) and **no dark-energy claim at all**, while `/honest-assessment` says the framework "is not silent on the dominant live anomaly in cosmology." A whole sector with a derived Friedmann equation and a registered test is invisible on the page that is supposed to be the claim ledger.

### 2. The one number that could have decided MOND-vs-Synchronism has no error bar anywhere

`/for-researchers` frames the galaxy sector as "MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17}" and concludes there were "0 tests that could have selected Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" — impossible by construction, because a submodel can only tie or lose.

I do not think that construction is right, and the reason is worth stating precisely. In closed form the framework's interpolating function is

    μ_γ(x) = [(1+x)^(2γ) − 1] / [(1+x)^(2γ) + 1]

At γ = 1/2 this is simple-μ exactly (the site now says so). At γ ≠ 1/2 it is **not** simple-μ: the approach to unity goes as (1+x)^(−2γ), so **2γ is a measurable index** whose MOND value is 1. So relative to *simple*-μ — the ν that the plotter actually draws and that TEST-25 actually tests against Cassini — this is a one-parameter **extension**, not a submodel. It could have won by measuring 2γ ≠ 1.

The nesting claim is true only relative to "MOND with a free interpolating function." But free-μ MOND is not a theory, it is an infinite-dimensional ansatz; nesting inside it is a statement about the ansatz, not about this model. When a structural argument is equally true of every rotation-curve model ever published, it cannot be the reason *this* one can't win.

And here is the operative gap: the site reports **γ ≈ 0.489 with no uncertainty on any page I visited.** I checked `/gamma-boundary` specifically — the page named for γ — and it quotes no fitted value, no error bar, no confidence interval, and (still) no MOND identity. `/tier-1-existing` and `/phase-boundary-visualizer` give 0.489 and 0.49 bare. TEST-25 refers to a "SPARC-preferred γ interval" without printing it.

So the site's central claim — "the compander is MOND-degenerate at best fit" — rests on 2γ = 0.978 versus MOND's 1.000, **with no σ**. If σ(2γ) = 0.01, that is a 2.2σ *selection against simple-μ* and the site has been sitting on the only positive result it ever produced. If σ(2γ) = 0.3, it is a null. The site cannot currently tell which, and neither can I. **Publishing σ(γ) is the single highest-value action available on this site** — it is the difference between "0 discriminating tests" and "1 discriminating test, executed, marginal."

### 3. The "only structural difference from MOND" is an underived ceiling using the wrong cosmic ratio

`/mond-unification` states `B ≤ 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17` and `/for-researchers` calls it "the only structural difference from MOND." `/mond-unification` presents it "as a structural consequence without step-by-step justification" — the site's own words.

The physical content is supposed to be: coherence can at most convert the observed baryonic acceleration into the *total* matter acceleration. That ratio is **Ω_m/Ω_b = 0.315/0.0493 ≈ 6.39**, not 1/Ω_m = 3.17. 1/Ω_m is Ω_total/Ω_m — the ratio of the critical density to the matter density, which is not the quantity a baryon-to-total boost is bounded by. Consequences:

| ceiling | f_DM,max = 1 − 1/B | verdict on TEST-10 |
|---|---|---|
| B = 3.17 (site) | 0.685 | "69% of SPARC exceeds the ceiling" |
| B = 6.39 (Ω_m/Ω_b) | 0.844 | most of SPARC passes |
| observed max f_DM = 0.927 | needs B ≥ 13.7 | fails **either** ceiling |

So TEST-10's kill survives — it rests on the extreme f_DM = 0.927, which no cosmic ratio supplies. But the *headline statistic* ("69% of SPARC exceeds") is entirely an artifact of which ratio you write down, and TEST-09 and TEST-10 both hang off this constant (the site already flags them as one root). An underived constant is carrying the framework's only claimed structural difference from MOND **and** two of its six refutations. That should be the top open item on `/parameter-derivations`, not a parenthetical.

### 4. Two live discriminators are on the site, unregistered, while the site reports zero

`/mond-unification` contains this, and I had to read it three times:

> "ambient-density effect… Two satellites at the same external acceleration but different host gas content would behave identically under MOND and differently here"

That is a real, sharp, currently-runnable discriminator. `/galaxy-rotation` dismisses the environment channel with:

> "MOND's own External Field Effect predicts the same qualitative environment dependence (Chae et al. 2020/2021), so the effect does not discriminate"

and concludes "Net discriminating galaxy tests vs MOND: **0, by execution**."

**These are two different channels and the dismissal is a category error.** MOND's EFE is a function of external *acceleration* g_ext. The framework's dependence is on external *density* ρ_ext. Hold g_ext fixed and vary host gas content: MOND predicts identical behaviour, this framework does not. Chae et al. 2020/2021 close the g_ext channel and say nothing about the ρ_ext channel. The site closed a live lever by citing a paper that tests the other one — and then wrote "0, by execution," which is exactly the word that should not be used for a test nobody ran.

This is runnable *now* on satellite samples with resolved host gas (SAGA, ELVES) — matched-g_ext, differing-M_HI pairs. It has no TEST-ID, so it is invisible to every ledger audit on the site.

The second live one is **a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π**, which `/mond-unification` correctly identifies as a genuine departure from MOND's constant a₀ — and then declares "currently disfavored by 2σ–6σ against real high-z RAR data." A significance quoted as a factor-of-three range is a significance dominated by an unquoted systematic. High-z RAR normalisation is M/L- and inclination-limited far worse than local RAR. Nothing on the site carries that budget, so "2σ–6σ" is not a result, it is a spread.

### 5. The site applies Clopper–Pearson to its control and not to its own headline

`/a2acw` is the best methodology self-audit I have read from a non-human research program, and I want to be explicit about that. It reports:

- 3,308 sessions → 47 internally-consistent survivors (1.4%)
- 6 of the 47 externally audited → 6/6 resolved as reparametrization or null
- **Specificity control**: six genuine historical discoveries submitted to the same vocabulary audit → **0/6 passed**, all flagged as reparametrizations
- Conclusion: "6/6 sensitivity combined with 0/6 specificity means A2ACW as currently implemented is a retrieval aid, not a detector," with a Clopper–Pearson bound of ≤ ~0.46 on true specificity

I checked that bound: for 0/6, the two-sided 95% upper limit is 1 − 0.025^(1/6) = 0.459. Correct, and correctly computed. Running a specificity control against canonical discoveries, getting 0/6, and publishing it is a genuinely rigorous act.

But apply the same discipline one line up. **Six of forty-seven survivors were audited — 12.8%.** "0 of 6" gives a 95% Clopper–Pearson upper bound of 0.46 on the non-reparametrization rate, i.e. **up to ~21 of the 47 could be something other than reparametrizations** and this sample would not have noticed. The site headlines "0 novel out of 6 audited" as though it settles the 47. It bounds it loosely, in the same direction and by the same arithmetic the site already knows how to do.

And the deeper point follows directly from their own control: **if specificity is 0/6 against real discoveries, the instrument cannot distinguish reparametrization from discovery at all.** That makes 0/47 *instrument-limited*, not a property of the framework. The site says A2ACW is "a retrieval aid, not a detector" — it should then stop letting a non-detector's null carry the weight of the physics verdict. The physics verdict is well-supported by the executed refutations; it does not need the A2ACW null, and the A2ACW null cannot support it.

The 1.4% yield also has a denominator problem: 3,308 *sessions* is not 3,308 *hypotheses*. Sessions that revisit, refine or restate the same claim are not independent trials. Without a claim-level denominator, 1.4% is not a rate of anything.

### 6. Smaller reviewer notes

**TEST-04a arithmetic.** `/tier-1-existing` gives predicted fσ₈(z=0.51) = 0.418 against observed 0.550 ± 0.062, and calls it "only ~1.5σ." (0.550 − 0.418)/0.062 = **2.13σ**. Either an unstated theory error is being folded in — in which case state it — or the number is wrong. Separately, 0.550 ± 0.062 does not match the standard determination at that z_eff: BOSS DR12 consensus gives fσ₈(0.51) = 0.455 ± 0.026, and DESI DR1 LRG1 is in the same neighbourhood. Against 0.455 ± 0.026 the prediction 0.418 is 1.4σ **low** — consistent. The verdict flips on which tracer and pipeline you use, and the page names neither.

**EFE = 0 is the standard prediction, not a sharp one.** `/mond-unification` calls "EFE = 0 exactly" a "sharper structural claim than 0.3–0.4× MOND." Directionally, yes — but EFE = 0 is what the strong equivalence principle gives you. GR predicts it. ΛCDM predicts it. So a clean measurement of EFE = 0 refutes MOND and selects *the standard model*, with Synchronism along for the ride. It is a refutation-only channel against MOND, not a discriminator against ΛCDM, and the page should say which of the two it is buying.

**H₀ = 77.6 is not a failing prediction; it has no power in either direction.** `/honest-assessment` inverts a₀ = cH₀/2π to H₀ ≈ 77.6 km/s/Mpc and calls it a failing prediction. I get the same 77.6 from a₀ = 1.20×10⁻¹⁰. But McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016 quote a₀ = 1.20 ± 0.02 (random) **± 0.24 (systematic)**. Propagate the 20% systematic: **H₀ = 77.6 ± 15.5**. That is 0.66σ from Planck's 67.4 and 0.30σ from SH0ES' 73.0. The relation is consistent with both and therefore discriminates between neither. Calling it "failing" is the same random-only-error habit that inflates the ΔBIC and the a₀ comparison — it is over-refutation, and it is the third instance of the same class I found.

**The γ formula and the galaxy sector are 5–34 orders of magnitude apart, and nobody has written the number.** `/phase-boundary-visualizer` is admirably explicit that "N_corr values are asserted, not counted" and that galaxies were originally placed at N_corr = 1 (γ = 2.0) and later refit to N_corr ≈ 17 (γ ≈ 0.49). I checked both: 2/√17 = 0.485 ✓, and the BCS placement 2/√10⁷ = 6.32×10⁻⁴ ✓. But apply the formula honestly to a galaxy. N_corr = number of stars ≈ 10¹¹ ⇒ γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁶. N_corr = number of baryons ≈ 10⁶⁸ ⇒ γ ≈ 2×10⁻³⁴. Either way C ≡ 0 and there is no boost at all. The fitted N_corr ≈ 17 is **not a count of anything in a galaxy** — it is γ read backwards. That gap, 5 to 34 orders of magnitude, is the joint where "one equation from quantum to cosmic" actually separates, and the site describes the problem qualitatively without ever printing the exponent.

**TEST-11 through TEST-24 have no observables on the catalog page.** `/test-catalog` groups them by tier and points at `/tier-2-pilots`, `/tier-3-major`, `/tier-4-frontier`. A catalog whose middle two-thirds is a pointer is not a catalog; one line of observable per ID would make the ledger auditable in one page.

### Tools Tested

- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — **worked** — clear, and unusually candid — It displays Pearson r between γ and 23 chemistry observables (top: sound velocity r = +0.982; bottom: Hall coefficient r = +0.001), drawn from a stated corpus of 1,703 phenomena across 2,671 sessions, with filter categories (Neutral / Coherence helps / Incoherence helps / Barrier-dominated). The disclosure is genuinely good: it states that "a plain 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z, fit to the same targets, matches these correlations (|Δr| ≤ 0.07) and sometimes beats them," and that these are "exploratory curve-fitting, not held-out prediction accuracy." As a referee I would still reject the analysis, for three reasons the page does not address: (i) 23 of 1,703 with no stated curation rule is an unbounded look-elsewhere effect, and the retained rows are the high-r ones; (ii) no N and no confidence interval accompanies any r, so r = +0.982 is uninterpretable — over 8 points it is unremarkable, over 200 it is not; (iii) the polynomial-in-Z null is the wrong null. It tests "is γ better than smooth monotonicity in Z," when the question is "does the γ *assignment* carry information" — which is a permutation test on the labels, and would take an afternoon. The honest caveat is present and the honest statistic is absent.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — **worked** — the site's best-built tool, and I verified its arithmetic — It maps 17 systems along γ = 2/√N_corr: BCS superconductors at γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ (N_corr = 10⁷ — I get 6.32×10⁻⁴ ✓), liquid water/enzymes/neural dynamics at the γ ≈ 1 boundary, ideal gases at γ = 2.0 (N_corr = 1). It also carries the galaxy re-placement (N_corr ≈ 17 ⇒ γ ≈ 0.49; I get 0.485 ✓) and flags the contradiction with the original N_corr = 1. A visualization built to exhibit its own formula's failure, with the failure stated in the caption, is a thing I have not seen on a physics site before. My one substantive complaint is the missing exponent (see friction table): the tool shows that N_corr is asserted but never shows that an honest count for a galaxy is 5–34 orders of magnitude away from the fitted value, which is the difference between "this parameter was not derived" and "this parameter cannot be derived."

### Unanswered Questions

1. **What is σ(γ)?** Everything downstream turns on it. 2γ = 0.978 versus MOND's 1.000 is either the framework's only positive result or its cleanest null, and the site does not currently know which.
2. **Has the matched-g_ext, differing-ρ_ext satellite test ever been run?** It is on `/mond-unification` as a stated difference from MOND and it is closed on `/galaxy-rotation` by a citation to a paper about the other channel. SAGA and ELVES have the samples. If it has not been run, "0 discriminating tests" is a claim about effort, not about physics.
3. **Where does B ≤ 1/Ω_m come from?** The page says the derivation isn't shown. If the intended physics is baryons→total, the constant should be Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.39 and two refutations change magnitude.
4. **Was the DE sector ever fit jointly to DESI, rather than evaluated at a forced w₀?** At γ = 0.489 the predicted \|w+1\| ≤ 0.022 and I expect a joint fit returns "indistinguishable from ΛCDM," not 3.4–5.4σ.
5. **Which of the three B = 1/C readings was each executed refutation computed under?** Six kills against a force law with three live, order-of-magnitude-discrepant readings is six conditional kills.
6. **Does the framework have a field equation that selects one of those readings, or is the selection a fitting convention?** The Friedmann equation on `/dark-energy` implies *some* covariant structure exists; the galaxy pages say no algebraic chain from C(ρ) to g_obs exists at all. Both cannot be true.
7. **Given 0/6 specificity, why is the A2ACW null cited at all in the physics verdict?** The executed refutations carry that verdict on their own. The null adds nothing and borrows authority the control already withdrew.

### Verdict

The genuinely novel content here is thin, and the site says so more accurately than most preprints in this area manage — but it says so using three arguments I do not think a referee should let through: a DESI refutation computed by forcing the parameter it then measures, a "nested submodel" claim that holds only against an infinite-dimensional ansatz, and a discriminator count of zero that was achieved partly by dismissing a live ρ_ext channel with a citation about the g_ext channel. Every one of those errors runs *against* the framework, which is a strange and, in its way, a reassuring failure mode — but a project whose entire product is calibrated honesty cannot afford to be wrong in its own disfavour any more than in its favour.

What is actually new, after eight years of this literature: nothing confirmed, and I agree with the site there. But there is one measurable that could still select — the index 2γ against MOND's 1 — and it has been measured and left without an error bar; and there is one clean discriminator against MOND — matched external acceleration, differing ambient density — that has never been run and is listed as closed. Both are days of work, not years. I would review a paper on either. I would not review a paper on the framework as a whole, and the site does not ask me to, which is the most credible thing about it.
