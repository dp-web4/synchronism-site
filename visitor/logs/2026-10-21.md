## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment, /what-synchronism-is-not, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest "one equation for everything" site I've ever clicked from Reddit. I understood the story ("they tried it and it mostly didn't work"), but I never understood what the equation was *supposed* to buy me, and below the first screen almost every page drowns me.

### Journey
I came from Reddit expecting a crank site. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", plus the subtitle "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything", plus "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated" disarmed me in about five seconds. That's a great hook. The scoreboard (0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations) is surprising and makes me trust the site more, not less.

The landing page also says coherence "is not quantum coherence, and a superconductor scores low on it." That stopped me. Superconductors are the one "everything acting together" example I know from YouTube. Why would they score low? The landing page gives no reason. (Later the γ Calculator kind of explained it: the old formula runs backwards for superconductors. But that's four clicks away.)

I clicked "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)". The top part was readable: physics uses different rules at different scales, and this asks whether one rule about "how packed together things are" could connect them. The page tells me I can stop after the top section, which I appreciated. Below that, it turns into Tully–Fisher, Ncorr, Cassini, Bell/CHSH and "boost ceiling" fast. I skimmed and hit "Next: First Encounter".

First Encounter is the best page for me. The marching band vs. crowd analogy works. So does "tanh is like a dimmer switch", the first time tanh meant anything to me. But I got lost on γ. Section B says the original γ formula failed, and fitting galaxies gives about 0.5, "the value that reproduces MOND". Then the galaxy section says stars are "N_corr = 1, γ = 2, the pinned value that loses to MOND". So is the galaxy number 0.5 or 2? I think one is the "original guess" and one is the "fitted value", but I had to reread twice. The γ ≈ 1 section ("1,703 chemical phenomena" landed near it) sounds impressive, then immediately says "fitted, not predicted… no mechanism". I can't tell if I'm supposed to be impressed.

Coherence Explorer: I dragged the γ slider and the curve went from a ramp to a cliff. That made the dimmer-switch idea click. Then the page says the default γ "fails a Solar System test at 8.7σ". I don't know what σ means, though the page does say it's "standard deviations". So why is the default setting a failed value? I guess because it's the galaxy fit, but that's odd for a toy.

Galaxy Curve Plotter: this is the page I'd send a friend to. "New here?" tells me exactly what to look at: blue dots (real speeds) stay flat, the gray line (just visible stuff) sags. *That's* the dark matter problem, and I finally get it from a picture. Then I see the violet Synchronism line… sags too. The green MOND line follows the dots. So in the one picture I understood, the theory loses. That's honest, but it contradicts what I thought First Encounter told me ("γ ≈ 0.5 reproduces MOND"). If it reproduces MOND, why doesn't the violet line sit on the green one? I suspect the answer is "the fitted version is basically MOND, the version as written fails", but no plain sentence on the plotter connects the two.

γ Calculator: the intro says "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails." That made me laugh. Clicking presets: Ideal Gas gives γ = 2.000 (sharp), BEC gives 2.00e-3 (flattest). The page explains this is backwards, because superconductors have sharp transitions. OK, that's the superconductor answer from the landing page! It should be linked from there. "2.00e-3" is scientific notation a lot of my friends wouldn't read.

Honest Assessment: the "If you read nothing else" box and "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" are exactly right for me. The page then admits it is "~18,000 words", so I stopped there. The "6 refutations… could be anywhere from 4 to 8 depending on counting rules the project lead hasn't chosen" note is weird to a newcomer. How do you not know how many times you failed? But it's honest.

What Synchronism Is Not: the one-line list (not a theory of everything, not peer-reviewed, not proven…) is helpful. I came here to understand "coherence isn't quantum coherence", and this page never mentions it (I checked: the phrase "quantum coherence" doesn't appear). It's about 3,000 words.

Glossary: 114 terms, alphabetical. The definitions I looked up were clear (tanh: "smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1"; MOND: "gravity behaves differently at very low accelerations"). But there's no "the 10 words you need first" list, and I wouldn't know which 10 of the 114 matter.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (server-rendered readouts: C(ρcrit) ≈ 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) ≈ 0.8259 at γ = 0.49). Intuitive. Dragging γ is the single best "aha" for what the equation does. The note that ρcrit is "not the midpoint" was more than I needed but harmless.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked, five real SPARC galaxies. Very intuitive thanks to the "New here?" box. This is where I finally understood the dark matter problem. It's also where the Synchronism line visibly loses, which I respect, but it isn't reconciled with First Encounter's "reproduces MOND" phrasing.
- **γ Calculator**: worked. Presets give γ (Ideal Gas 2.000, Water 1.000, Galaxy back-fit 0.485, BEC 2.00e-3). It answers the landing page's superconductor puzzle, but nothing links the two.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the whole thing failed, what is the "one equation" still good for? Is it a teaching toy now, or is something still being tested? (The Honest Assessment mentions globular clusters and dark energy as "open", but I'd never find that.)
2. Does the equation match MOND or lose to it? I saw both, and I think the answer is "the fitted version is MOND in disguise, the original version loses", but I had to infer it.
3. Why does a superconductor score low on "coherence"? I only got this from the γ Calculator by accident.
4. Who is "the project lead", and why does an AI-run project need a human to decide how to count its failures?
5. Is there a short video or one-picture summary? The galaxy plotter picture did more for me than all the text.

### Verdict
This is the first "unify physics" site I've seen that leads with its failures, and that makes me trust it enough to click around. First Encounter and the Galaxy Plotter's "New here?" box are genuinely good pop-science. What I can't get out of it is a straight answer to "so does the equation work on galaxies or not?" The text says it "reproduces MOND", and the picture shows it losing to MOND. That one contradiction (really two γ's, explained badly for my level) is what a casual reader walks away confused about.

### Verdict
As information architecture, this is better than most science sites I audit. Navigation, breadcrumbs, badge counts and glossary are consistent with each other, and the site corrects its own documentation in public. The structural debt is accretion: five label taxonomies separated by disclaimers rather than a map, beginner-labelled cards written in maintainer vocabulary, and inline revision history at 10+ date stamps per 1,000 words on the pages a skeptic is sent to. The fixes are editorial, not architectural: one label-map table, one rewritten card, and moving the parenthetical history into the revision-note blocks that already exist.

### Summary

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "interesting AI project". I went straight to **Parameter Derivations**, since that's where grand-unified frameworks usually hide their fitted constants. The page opens by saying it isn't a derivations page, which removed most of my planned attack. I re-did the arithmetic anyway:

- **Closed form**: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]. Correct. At γ=½ it is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). Correct. The small-x limit γx and the large-x limit 1−C ≈ 2x^−2γ are both correct. The "not Landau" paragraph is right: there's no self-consistency loop, so there's no critical point, only a crossover. The Curie–Weiss finite-size width ∝ N^−1/2 is also stated correctly. Calling the γ = 2/√N_corr sign inversion a defect is fair: more correlated units should sharpen a mean-field transition, not flatten it.
- **A**: 4π/(G R₀²) at R₀ = 8 kpc gives 4.57×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻². Correct, so the "600× off" stands. Solving for β_J·R₀ at A = 0.029 gives 317 pc, matching the page. ρ_crit = 0.029·229² = 1.52×10³ M☉/pc³ for the MW. Correct. 6.5×10² M☉/pc³ = 4.4×10⁻²⁰ g/cm³. Correct.
- **Fisher correlation of (ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000**: this follows analytically. Below the knee, C ≈ γρ/(A V²), so the data only see γ/A. The page's claim that "these are one parameter in the galaxy sector" is right, and it's the most useful sentence on the page.
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π** at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰. Correct. **Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG)** gives 119.0 M☉/pc². Correct. Milgrom's a₀/G at 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ gives 861. Correct.
- **Ciocan level table**: E(1) = 1.790 at Ωm = 0.315. I reproduced every row: 1.79/1.86/2.15/2.91, and the ~9.4σ, ~9.8σ, +0.5σ and −2.3σ (using the lower 95% CI side ÷1.96 and propagating the anchor error). E(2) = 3.03 and 1−Ωm(z) = 0.214 at z=1, 0.075 at z=2. Correct.

**Relation to MOND**: the Step 2 rewrite is good. A ρ_crit-filled Hubble sphere gives g = (4π/3)Gρ_crit(c/H₀) = cH₀/2, not cH₀/2π, and the page now says so plainly. I checked the self-force F = −(1/8πG)∫|∇Φ|²∇C d³x for ∇·(C∇Φ) = 4πGρ through the stress-tensor identity, and it's right. So is the claim that the variational striction term cancels it. The isolated deep-MOND σ = (4GMa₀/81)^¼ = 9.4 km/s for 10⁷ M☉ is also correct, and it does undercut the old TDG card's intervals.
