## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /consciousness-demo, /honest-assessment, plus a glossary lookup)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A strangely honest site that tells me up front its big idea failed. I respect that, but I'm still not sure what I'm supposed to take away besides "it failed, and here's how carefully."

### Journey
I came over from Reddit. The headline, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, is the kind of hook I click on. The subhead straight away says *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* That stopped me. I've never seen a site do that, and it made me trust it more. It also made me ask: why should I keep reading about an equation that doesn't work? The page's answer is that "the honest map of where it fails is the product." That's an interesting idea, but it's a researcher's reason, not a curious reader's reason.

The plain-language gloss of the equation, *"a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are"*, made me laugh. It didn't help, though. I don't know what squashing or compressing a number means. The scoreboard (0 predictions confirmed, 6 refutations, 0 of 9 claims surviving) was clear and memorable. The activity numbers (3,308 AI-to-AI sessions, 14,610 galaxies) were impressive, then confusing. Is that a lot? Is AIs arguing with each other good or bad? The landing page also says "No outside physicist has reviewed these claims." Honest, but a bit alarming.

I clicked **Start Here** (/why-synchronism). The crowd-vs-marching-band picture of "coherence" finally gave me something to hold on to: density as a dial between "everyone doing their own thing" and "everyone in step." That's the best idea on the site for someone like me. The page then quickly gets into MOND, Bose–Einstein condensates and Tully–Fisher scatter, and I skimmed.

**First Encounter** was the most readable page. The steps go electron → presence → dimmer switch → galaxies → quantum measurement → where it fails, and that's a good story shape. "tanh = S-shaped dimmer switch" worked for me. The MRH "bubble of neighbors that matter," with Andromeda being too far away to count, also worked. Then the γ section lost me. I'm told the original γ formula "did not survive testing." A section later, galaxies use "stars are uncorrelated, γ = 2." Then the fitted galaxy value is apparently ≈ 0.5, "matching MOND." So which γ is the real one? Is 2 wrong and 0.5 right? And if 0.5 just equals MOND, is that a win or a loss? I couldn't tell. "Melting points: 53% error, superconductor Tc 6.5× wrong" was blunt and easy to understand.

**Core Idea** says "In plain words…" at the top, which I liked. It soon turns into Hill functions, Naka–Rushton curves, μ-law encoders, compander and Ising model. I bounced. "Why log? (Honest answer: it's cosmetic)" made me smile, but it also made me wonder why the log is in the headline equation at all.

The tools page had beginner labels, which helped, and told me where to start. On **Coherence Explorer** the sliders worked and the curve moved. It even shows the chart's "C = 0.50" line sitting at about 2× ρ_crit, not at ρ_crit. I didn't know why that matters, but I believed it. On **Galaxy Plotter**, the explanation of the dark matter problem (outer stars go as fast as inner ones, so something invisible is adding gravity) was the clearest physics explanation I've read on the subject in a while. The chart has **five** lines, though. One is the theory, and its label literally says "the equation never switches on here." One is amber, labeled "NOT the theory — illustration stand-in." One is MOND. So the theory's line does nothing, and a fake line shows what it *would* look like if it worked? That took me three reads. It works as a demo of failure, but my first reaction was "why is there a fake line on a science chart?"

I clicked **Consciousness Threshold Demo** because who wouldn't. Pleasant surprise: it debunks itself. Eight approaches "converge" on C = 0.50 only because 0.5 is the middle of a 0-to-1 scale. That's a genuinely good lesson for me about fooling yourself, and I'd share it. I wouldn't have known that's what "Artifact Lesson" meant without reading the page, though.

**Honest Assessment** opens with a crisp verdict ("Every decisive test it ran on real data, it lost"), then says the page is about 18,000 words. Within one screen I saw γ, ΔBIC, Ω_m, f_DM, RAR, BTFR, N_corr and "TEST" numbers. I left. A glossary lookup was fine (100 terms, and tanh, MOND and dark matter are explained simply). But the γ entry says "γ = 2/√N_corr" as if that's the definition, while First Encounter told me that formula didn't survive.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked. Intuitive. The sliders move the S-curve, and the crowd/marching-band framing is right on the tool. The presets (0.49 / 0.5 / 2) mean nothing to me without the γ backstory. The "backwards vs real physics" caveat went over my head.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked. The dark-matter explanation was excellent. The legend was confusing: five lines, one marked "NOT the theory," and the theory line flat because it "never switches on."
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo**: worked. Clear, and a satisfying self-debunk. The best "aha" I had on the site.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation failed everywhere it was decisively tested, what *is* still alive? Is anything left to hope for, or is this a closed case?
2. Is "matching MOND with γ ≈ 0.5" a success, a failure, or neither?
3. Why keep a log inside the equation if it's "cosmetic"?
4. Who is dp, and why is one person with thousands of AI sessions doing physics? Is that a new way of doing science, or a warning sign?
5. What would a positive result have looked like? What were they hoping to see?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've stumbled onto. The opening line, First Encounter and the consciousness demo are genuinely good for a curious reader. Beyond those three, the site quickly turns into a researcher's notebook. The γ story is the biggest tangle, the theory pages and Honest Assessment drown me in acronyms, and I left respecting the honesty more than understanding the idea. I'd recommend it to a friend as "watch a theory get killed by its own authors," not as "learn a new idea about physics."

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /learning-paths, /glossary, /interactive-tools, /test-catalog, /tier-1-existing, /honest-assessment, /coherence-function, /mrh, plus the /gamma-calculator and /phase-boundary-visualizer tools). I also tried /core-theory, which returned 404.

### Journey
**Landing.** The top nav is clean: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment. Acronym discipline on the landing page is good. MOND, A2ACW, SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, CHSH and ΛCDM are all expanded on first use, mostly in table footnotes. One gap: the density-law section mentions an "Ω_m-floored variant" with no gloss, and that is the first unexplained term a careful reader hits. The landing page also calls "Withdrawn" "an operational state, not a badge," which is a useful distinction. It sent me looking for where operational states are defined (see below).

**Learning Paths.** The page is well built. The Beginner path openly tells you to read only the "If you read nothing else" box on Honest Assessment, and gives two time estimates ("~45 min on the short route; ~2 h 40 m reading every page in full"). That's good expectation-setting. The note "The two groupings are not equivalent" (difficulty paths vs topic tracks) is honest. It also raises a problem I found later: one page can belong to two paths at once.

**Coherence Function → MRH (step counters).** /coherence-function shows **two** step indicators, "Intermediate Path — Step 1 of 8" and "Physics Track — Step 1 of 7", but only **one** Next link, "The γ Parameter →". That is the Intermediate path's step 2. On the Physics Track, step 2 is MRH: /mrh shows "Physics Track — Step 2 of 7" with "← Prev: The Coherence Function". So a Physics-track reader who clicks Next on step 1 lands on the Intermediate path's step 2 and silently leaves their track. MRH's own counter, prev/next and H1 ("MRH: Markov Relevancy Horizon", expanded at once) are fine.

**Breadcrumbs.** They work and are consistent. The middle segments ("Core Theory", "Predictions & Experiments") are plain text, not links, and neither name appears in the top nav. /core-theory is a 404, so a reader who tries the obvious URL hits a dead end. This is minor; the leaf links all work. (One fetch of Honest Assessment showed "Home / / Honest Assessment" with an empty segment. A second fetch of the markup showed a normal two-segment trail, so I'm treating that as a fetch artifact, not a defect.)

**Glossary.** Strong. It has 100 terms and opens with a two-family badge explanation (MRH-relationship vs Descriptive), and every current badge has its own entry. The retired badges (Validated, Strongly Supported) are marked "⚠ Retired." I checked Active-MRH against Honest Assessment's "canonical" definition and the wording matches exactly. Gaps:
- No entries for **Withdrawn, Closed, Executed, Registered/pre-registration, Never Run as Registered, Underpowered as Registered, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie**. These are the words the Tier 1 and Test Roadmap pages actually use most. "Root" has an entry, which helps.
- **γ entry**: "γ = 2/√N_corr. A motivated ansatz, not a derivation." Further into the same entry it says audited-negative, but the first line reads as the current definition (Pass 1 hit this too).

Read in sequence, a non-physicist concludes that the most collective systems score as the least collective. If that is the "formula is backwards" lesson, the entry should say so in those words. Otherwise it reads as a contradiction.

**Interactive Tools index.** It has a good, explicit legend. Each card has three slots: Kind (e.g. "Model Explainer"), Level (Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced), and Status (amber, square-cornered, "deliberately styled so it cannot be mistaken for a difficulty"). Two snags: "Equation Anatomy" is labeled Beginner *and* Audited-Negative, and the γ Calculator is Beginner *and* "Artifact Lesson". So the first two tools a beginner meets after Coherence Explorer demonstrate a refuted formula. That's fine, as long as the card description leads with it (the γ Calculator card does: "see why the formula is audited-negative").

**Test Roadmap.** The intro is precise: "24 specific, falsifiable experiments (TEST-01 through TEST-24; TEST-25 and TEST-26 below were added after the registry closed and are listed but not in this count)." The tier sums (10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24) check out. Still, the reader has to hold three numbers (24 registered, 26 listed, 10 in Tier 1) plus a second ID scheme ("Bets (B1–B7)"). The Learning Paths page labels this step "Test Roadmap (26 tests: 24 in the original registry + 2 added later)", so the two pages lead with different numbers. The badge guide on this page is introduced as "Canonical definitions (Honest Assessment)". Two lifecycle labels used on Tier 1, "Kill Criterion Triggered" and "Demoted — Exploratory", aren't in this list either.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /for-researchers, /coherence-function, /parameter-derivations, /mond-unification, /galaxy-rotation, /honest-assessment, /dark-energy, /tier-1-existing, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer)

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "interesting AI project", so I went in expecting a grand unified theory and checked the math first. The landing page opens with a question and states 0 confirmed predictions and 6 executed refutations in the first screen. That disarmed me faster than I expected.

**/coherence-function.** I re-derived everything by hand:
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. Correct.
- At γ = ½ this collapses to x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). Correct, and the page writes the x/2 explicitly instead of hiding it in "identically MOND".
- Small-x limit γx: correct. Large-x limit 1 − 2(1+x)^{−2γ}: correct.
- dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x): correct. The second derivative is −γ[2C·C′(1+x) + (1−C²)]/(1+x)² < 0, so the "no inflection on x ≥ 0" claim checks out.

The page also says the function is a compander rather than an order parameter, and that the Ising analogy is "motivational, not class membership". As a condensed-matter person, that is exactly the objection I was loading up, and it was already conceded. The form-selection table (Hill n=1 wins, tanh "carries no statistical content") is the right experiment.

**/parameter-derivations.** It opens: "not a derivations page in the mathematical sense". I checked the numbers:
- a0 = cH0/2π at H0 = 67.4 gives 1.043×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Correct, and 13% below 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. Correct.
- Σ0 = a0/(2πG) = 0.249 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc². Correct.
- 1/Ωm = 3.17 and the 68.5% DM-fraction ceiling = 1 − Ωm. Both consistent.

The γ = 2/√N_corr critique is correct and well put. The CLT doesn't apply to correlated units, and the sign is inverted relative to mean-field intuition, because more correlated units should mean a sharper transition.

**/dark-energy.** This is the cleanest piece of physics on the site, so I checked it all:
- With G_eff = G/C and C = x/(x+2) at γ = ½, ρm/C = ρm + 2ρcrit exactly. So ρDE = 2ρcrit = Λ, which is correct and a nice identity.
- Far past: ρDE ∝ a^{−3(1−2γ)}, so w = −2γ. Correct. Far future: ρDE → ρcrit/γ, so w → −1. Correct.
- The sign lock sign(w0+1) = sign(wa) follows, and so does the incompatibility with DESI's w0 > −1, wa < 0 quadrant.
- The perturbation argument pins |2γ−1| ≲ 10⁻⁵, i.e. it *is* ΛCDM. This page has clearly been through the Jeans-term check.

**/mond-unification.** σ = (4GMa0/81)^{1/4} for 10⁷ M☉ gives 9.42 km/s, but only with a0 = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ (the observed value). With the framework's own cH0/2π it gives 9.07 km/s. That is minor, but it is the first sign of the a0 bookkeeping problem below.

**/galaxy-rotation and /tier-1-existing.** x is defined once as g_obs/a0′. Tier 1 gives the profiled a0′ = 5.33×10⁻¹¹ m/s². Because C = μ_simple(x/2), the effective Milgrom scale is 2a0′ = 1.07×10⁻¹⁰. That is 2.5% from cH0/2π, not 13%. **Nowhere did I find which of a0′ or 2a0′ the "a0 = cH0/2π" prediction is supposed to equal.** If it is a0′, the prediction is off by a factor of 2. If it is 2a0′, it is better than the site claims. Either way the 13% figure compares to McGaugh's a0, not to the scale this framework's own fit produces.

**/honest-assessment.** The +184 is now qualified everywhere I looked: galaxy-level N_eff ≈ 150, ΔBIC ≈ +11, "1.6σ out-of-sample, 2.2σ in-sample, disfavoured at the threshold, not decisive". Good. But it is still counted as one of the "6 executed refutations". A 1.6σ out-of-sample result against a ΔBIC > 10 kill line that it clears by 0.9 is not, to me, a refutation in the same sense as TEST-09's 3.3σ. The honest-assessment wording ("not decisive") and the headline count ("6 refutations") are now in tension.

**TEST-25.** The headline +17.95σ (unmarginalized) sits beside the published 8.7σ (Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024). Tier 1 also says the galaxy-level ΔBIC ≤ 10 interval is γ = 0.3–1.7. Once γ is that loose, I'd want to know whether 17.95σ survives at all. As a reader, I can't tell which number the site stands behind.
