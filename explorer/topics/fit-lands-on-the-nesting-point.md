# Topic: Does a one-parameter superset of an incumbent always fit to the nesting point, and is that the transferable result?

## Question
Free γ lands at 0.489 in galaxies (the family is MOND's simple μ at γ = ½) and at 0.487 in the dark-energy sector (the
family is exactly ΛCDM at γ = ½). Is this what *any* one-parameter family that nests a well-fitting incumbent does? If
so, can the ~2% offset from ½ be predicted from the incumbent's own residual structure? And does it predict where the
next sector will land?

## Context
Visitor 2026-09-15, passes 3 and 4 (Q5 in both). Related findings already on record:
- The archive (08-12, 08-14) says the "cross-sector concordance" has no power.
- Fitting γ is out-of-sample *harmful* (08-20 CV).
- The local fluid DE horn pins γ = ½ to 10⁻⁵ (explorer 09-14).

The visitor reframed all of these as one methodological law: "fit γ goes to the nesting point." That is a claim about
model selection in general, not about Synchronism.

## Asks
1. **Null simulation.** Generate mock SPARC RARs from exact simple-μ MOND with SPARC's error model and fit free γ. Is
   γ̂ = 0.489 ± 0.11 what pure MOND data produce, including the sign of the offset? Do the same for DESI mocks drawn from
   ΛCDM.
2. **Which sectors does the family *not* nest an incumbent in?** Only there is a fitted γ informative. List them and say
   whether any has data.
3. **Prior art.** Nested-model boundary behaviour (Self & Liang 1987; "parameter on the boundary"; Occam penalties for
   nested extensions). Is "free shape parameter lands on the incumbent" a named result?

## Why It Matters
If ask 1 reproduces 0.489 from MOND mocks, the site can replace scattered caveats with one sentence: the 0.489 digits
are what MOND data produce. It would also be a clean, framework-independent note, and it tells a reader in advance what
any future γ fit will say.
