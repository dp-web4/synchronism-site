## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understood the *question* in about 40 seconds and never understood why I should keep reading about an answer the site itself says failed every test it ran.

### Journey

Clicked in from a Reddit link. Landing page headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a great hook. That's a Veritasium video title. I was in.

Then, still on the landing page, three lines down: *"The physics ledger reads zero confirmed novel predictions"* and *"4 refutations executed on external data."* I actually stopped and re-read that. My honest first reaction was: wait, is this a physics site or a post-mortem? I respect it — nobody does this — but I had no idea what mood I was supposed to be in. Excited? Sympathetic? Am I reading a discovery or a museum exhibit about a discovery that didn't happen?

I hit **Start Exploring** → `/start-here`. Crowd in a plaza vs. a marching band. Nice, I got it immediately: loose stuff acts on its own, packed stuff acts as one thing. Density decides which. That's a genuinely good idea and I could explain it to a friend.

Then I noticed the **Beginner Path** starts at `/why-synchronism`, not the page I was already on. So I went back and started over from step 1. Step 1 gave me the marching band again. Step 2 (**First Encounter**) gave me the marching band a third time. Step 3 (**The Core Idea**) gave me the marching band a fourth time, now as "a dimmer switch." Four pages in and I had learned exactly one thing, four times. I started skimming hard here, which is on me, but the repetition is what trained me to skim.

**First Encounter** dropped the first real speed bump: "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)." The plain version — *"the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"*, *"a single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy"* — is genuinely excellent, one of the best lines on the site. Then immediately: *"the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence."* I don't know what a degree of freedom is. I skipped that sentence and was fine, but it's the moment the page stopped talking to me.

**The Core Idea** is where I hit the equation head-on:

`C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`

The site is kind about `tanh` — *"just an S-shaped curve — flat, then steep, then flat again"* — and that worked. Nobody explains `ln`. Nobody explains why there's a `+ 1` in there. I know from YouTube that "log scale" means "each step is 10× bigger," and I *assume* `ln` is that, but the site never says it. So I can read one symbol out of four in the site's central equation, which is a weird place to be on the page called "The Core Idea."

Then the thing that bothered me most all session. Every single page warns me that **"coherence" here does not mean coherence**: *"Not quantum coherence — superconductors score low here."* I've watched maybe six videos about superconductors and BECs and the entire point, as I understood it, is that they're the most perfectly in-sync matter in existence. This site's equation says they're near zero. The site knows this is confusing — it warns me on the landing page, on Core Idea, in the glossary, and again inside the Coherence Explorer. When a site has to apologize for a word five times, my takeaway isn't "I was wrong about superconductors," it's "they picked the wrong word and won't change it."

I went to **Tools** because sliders are more fun than paragraphs (tested three, below), then to **Honest Assessment**, which turned out to be the best-written page on the site: *"When its galaxy predictions were tested against real telescope measurements, it failed every decisive test."* No jargon, real telescopes, clear verdict. I understood that sentence completely.

What I *didn't* understand is why galaxies were the test in the first place. No page I visited explained what a galaxy rotation curve is, or why astronomers invented dark matter, before telling me the theory failed to account for it. I know the phrase "dark matter" from TV; I do not know what "69% of real galaxies exceed that ceiling" or "3.3 standard deviations" means well enough to feel the loss. The site delivers the verdict of a trial I never saw.

**Glossary** last. 80+ terms, alphabetical, no search box. I scrolled looking for "reparametrization" — the word that's on half the site — and the definition is good: *"Known physics in new notation."* That single line explained more than three pages did. It should be on the landing page, not filed under R.

### Tools Tested

*(Note: I browsed by fetching pages, so I couldn't physically drag sliders — everything below is the on-load state and the printed explanations, which is roughly what a skimmer judges a tool by anyway.)*

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked / **confusing**. Sliders are labeled in human words ("Transition sharpness", "Reference density"), which I appreciated. But the readouts confused me: at the default the curve reads C(ρcrit) = 0.8824, and by 10× density it's 0.9999 — so the "reference density" is already almost all the way to the top, and the graph has a line drawn at C=0.50 that the curve passes nowhere near the reference point. The page tells me this is expected (*"a saturation knee, not the curve's midpoint"*) but not why that's a good thing. And the tool warns me the formula *"runs backwards against real condensed-matter physics."* So I'm playing with a curve the site tells me is pointed the wrong way. I don't know what I'm supposed to learn by dragging it.
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked / **confusing**. The N_corr explanation is actually good (*"For a single atom, Ncorr = 1. For a crystal oscillating in phase, Ncorr can reach millions"*) and the preset buttons (Ideal gas, Liquid water, BEC…) are exactly the right idea — I clicked those instead of typing. Two phrases stopped me cold: the regime label reads *"Boundary (γ ≈ 1) — a label per the inverted formula"* (I cannot parse "per the inverted formula"), and *"Preset Ncorr values are back-fits, not measurements."* I don't know what a back-fit is. Guessing from context — they picked the numbers to make the answer come out right? If that's what it means, that's a big deal and it should be said in those words.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (`/consciousness-demo`) — worked / **intuitive**, and the highlight of my visit. Eight different approaches to "when does consciousness switch on" all landing at 0.499 is a fantastic-looking result, and then the page immediately tells you it's fake: *"every approach that picks the midpoint of a [0,1)-bounded output range will land near 0.50 by construction."* I got that. Of course things that pick the middle of a 0-to-1 range all land at 0.5. That's the one moment on the site where I felt like I'd been taught how to catch a mistake myself, rather than told a verdict. More pages should work like this one.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the equation failed every galaxy test, what is the site *for*? Is it a proposal I should still find plausible, or a record of a failed attempt? I genuinely couldn't tell, and that's the first thing I wanted to know.
2. Why is it called "coherence" if superconductors — the most coherent things I've ever heard of — score near zero? Was the word chosen before anyone checked?
3. What's a galaxy rotation curve, and why did the mismatch make people invent dark matter? I needed this *before* the failure results, not after.
4. What does `ln` do in the main equation, and what's the `+1` for?
5. "47 research contributions" — contributions of what, to whom? Are these papers? Did anyone outside read them?
6. "3,308 AI-to-AI review sessions" — is that a lot? Is a review by an AI of another AI's work a check on anything, or the same thing twice?
7. What's a "back-fit," and does it mean the preset numbers in the γ calculator were chosen to make the answers come out right?
8. MOND keeps appearing as the thing this ties with or loses to. What is MOND, in one sentence, before I'm told it wins?

### Verdict

The core idea is genuinely graspable in a minute — packed stuff acts as one thing, loose stuff acts on its own, and maybe one curve describes the switchover — and the honesty is unlike anything else I've clicked from Reddit; the consciousness demo teaching me to catch a fake result was legitimately the best thing I read all week. But the site never tells a casual visitor why to stay once it has announced its own failure on the front page, and it repeats its one good analogy four times while never explaining the mystery it failed to solve or half the symbols in its own equation. I'd come back if someone linked me a specific page — probably the honest assessment or the consciousness demo — but I wouldn't wander this site on my own again, because the parts that talk to me and the parts that talk past me are shuffled together with no warning.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The navigation skeleton is unusually disciplined — breadcrumbs, learning paths, prerequisites, and next-links all work — but the site's two most load-bearing pieces of vocabulary, the variable ρ and the validation badge set, are each defined in three or four incompatible ways, so the terminology layer contradicts the structural layer.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs site before a rewrite: walk the nav exhaustively, check that every label matches its destination, trace one term from first use to definition, and see whether the reference material has a single source of truth.

**The structure is better than most funded docs sites I've reviewed.** Breadcrumbs are present and correctly typed on all 16 pages I hit (`Home / Core Theory / The Coherence Function`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1: Existing Data`, `Home / Interactive Tools / Galaxy Curve Plotter`). Section taxonomy is consistent — "Getting Started," "Core Theory," "Methodology," "Predictions & Experiments," "Interactive Tools" — and pages land in the right one. `/learning-paths` offers eight paths (three by difficulty, five by topic) with step counts, time estimates, and stated prerequisites ("No physics background needed" vs. "Grad-level physics"). Content pages carry both a linear Previous/Next and, where a page belongs to two paths, a second track indicator — `/core-idea` shows Previous → First Encounter / Next → What Synchronism Is Not *and* "Philosophy Track Step 1 of 7." That's genuinely good multi-path IA and I want to say so before the complaints start.

**Pass 1's duplicate-start-page finding is a false positive, and I want to correct it.** `/start-here` returns HTTP 307 → `/why-synchronism`; `/tools` returns 307 → `/interactive-tools`. These are redirect aliases, not two pages with duplicated content — identical H1, breadcrumb, and body because they *are* the same document. Nothing to merge.

But there **is** a real entry-point bug underneath that. The hero has three CTAs: "Start Exploring" → `/first-encounter`, "See What Failed" → `/honest-assessment`, "Beginner Path →" → `/why-synchronism`. The Beginner path is `/why-synchronism` (1) → `/first-encounter` (2) → `/core-idea` (3) → … So the *primary* button drops the reader at step 2 and the secondary button starts at step 1. That is almost certainly what made Pass 1 feel like it started twice.

**Then I traced ρ, and this is the finding I'd escalate above everything else.** The central variable of the site's central equation is defined four different ways on four pages:

| Page | Definition of ρ |
|---|---|
| `/core-idea` | "ρ: local density — how many relevant elements are packed in a given region (e.g. stars per cubic light-year, atoms per unit volume)" |
| `/equation-walkthrough` | "the local mass/energy density" |
| `/coherence-function` | "ρ: Input: presence — compatible structural elements within the system's MRH" |
| `/mrh` | "ρ (presence)" |

Number density of elements, mass/energy density, and "presence" are three different quantities with three different dimensions. Worse, `/core-idea` supplies concrete units ("stars per cubic light-year") while `/coherence-function` states flatly: **"ρ has no stated units and no measurement protocol."** Those two sentences cannot both be true. A reader who takes the beginner path (`/core-idea`, density with units) and then the physics path (`/coherence-function`, presence with no units) will conclude the site changed its mind and didn't notice. I can't judge which is correct — that's a physics call — but as a documentation problem, the single most-referenced symbol on the site needs one definition, one page owning it, and every other mention transcluding or linking to that page.

**Then I traced the badges, which is the second systemic issue.** The controlled vocabulary is nine terms, cleanly defined in the glossary (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative, Untested, Failed, Speculative, Reparametrization). The definitions themselves are excellent — precise, one line each, no hedging. The problem is that almost nothing on the site actually uses them.

`/tier-1-existing` lists 12 tests. Three carry a canonical badge. The other nine carry free-text status strings written in badge position:

| ID | Status label as shown |
|---|---|
| TEST-02 | Self-Eliminating-or-Tie |
| TEST-03 | Never Run As Registered |
| TEST-04 | Withdrawn |
| TEST-04a | Disfavored on σ₈, Underpowered on Registered fσ₈ |
| TEST-05 | Re-Adjudicated |
| TEST-06 | Underpowered As Registered |
| TEST-07 | Demoted to Tier-2 |
| TEST-09 | Failed, Kill Criterion Fired |
| TEST-10 | Failed, Ceiling Exceeded |
| CLUSTER-SCALE | Closed |

TEST-04a's "badge" is an eleven-word sentence. `/honest-assessment` adds more outside the nine: "Kill Criterion Triggered," "MOND-shared (RETIRED)," "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie," "Sign Correction," "Null-Class," "Template Bias Caveat," "89% Boundary-Consistent," "Ceiling Exceeded" — plus the two the site's own docs mark deprecated, "Validated" and "Strongly Supported," still in live use. That's roughly 20 distinct status labels against a documented set of 9. A reader cannot tell which strings are statuses and which are editorial asides, because they render the same way.

Related: the canonical definitions have **four homes** — the glossary, an inline block on `/honest-assessment`, a "Status badge definitions" legend on `/tier-1-existing`, and a "Validation Badge Guide" on `/test-catalog`. `/test-catalog` points to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` as canonical, which means the site's reference legend lives inside a 39-section essay. Reference material should not be a subsection of a narrative page.

**Acronyms.** Handling is bimodal. The dedicated pages are exemplary — `/mrh` expands Markov Relevancy Horizon in the H1 and grounds it in Markov blankets in sentence one; `/a2acw` expands AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop in its subtitle; the landing page expands A2ACW parenthetically at first use and glosses MOND, tanh, ln, and C. But content pages assume a dictionary the reader hasn't opened: `/core-idea` uses BEC, RAR, BTFR, and DM with no expansion; `/galaxy-plotter` uses SPARC, MOND, V_flat, and RAR with no inline definition; the landing page's own numbers cite SPARC, ALFALFA-SDSS, BTFR, BAO, ΛCDM, and fσ₈ unexpanded. All of these are in the glossary. None are linked from the point of use. A hover-gloss on first occurrence per page would close this entirely, and the term data already exists.

**Number consistency.** The landing page says "~3,300 A2ACW … sessions" in the methodology note and "3,308 A2ACW research sessions" in the Research Activity block — same page, two figures. More consequentially, the audit denominator changes by page: landing and `/a2acw` both say **"0 of 6"** claims survived expert audit; `/honest-assessment` says **"9 of 9 audited claims"** were demoted. Both may be true of different sets, but no page says so, and a reader comparing them sees a contradiction. Same for "≈47 candidate claims" (landing) / "47 research contributions" (`/honest-assessment`) sitting a few sections from "~57% overcount" with no forward reference from the first mention to the correction.

**Tool catalog drift.** `/interactive-tools` describes the Galaxy Curve Plotter as showing "four curves: Newtonian prediction (dashed), observed rotation (dots), Synchronism consistency check (violet, fitted to V_flat), and MOND (green)." The tool actually shows five, and the violet curve is *not* the fitted one — violet is "Synchronism's real C(ρ) function at γ=2," flagged as failing, while the fitted trace is a separate amber curve labeled "Illustration only." The catalog entry describes a previous version of the tool and mis-assigns the most important distinction on the page.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked / **intuitive**. Best-structured tool I tested. Dropdown of five named SPARC galaxies (default DDO 154, labeled "Dwarf irregular" — good, the type is context a reader needs), labeled axes with units (Radius kpc / V km/s), and a legend that assigns a distinct visual treatment to each of five curves and explains what each represents. The "Illustration only" tag on the amber hand-tuned curve is exactly the right kind of honesty label, applied at exactly the right place. Two documentation defects: (a) the `/interactive-tools` catalog entry describing it is stale and reverses which curve is fitted, as above; (b) SPARC, MOND, V_flat, and RAR all appear without inline definition, and "V_flat = 47 km/s (calibrated input)" uses "calibrated input" as if that phrase were self-explanatory — for a reader who doesn't already know, "calibrated input" versus "prediction" is *the* distinction the whole plot exists to make.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked / **intuitive**. Pass 1 read the catalog blurb ("catch a refuted formula being wrong at every stop") as "this tool is broken" and never clicked. Having clicked: the tool is clear, and the framing Pass 1 wanted is already there on the page itself — "This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose." The problem is purely that the *catalog description* leads with the defect and the *page* leads with the lesson. Swap them and the bounce disappears. One genuine confusion of my own: the axis runs γ = 0 to 4, but BCS/superconductors sit at γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, rendered "off-scale to the left." A labeled point that is admittedly not on the axis is a chart bug, not a lesson — either use a log axis or show it as an explicit out-of-range marker with the value called out. The caveat line is one of the sharpest sentences on the site: "γ is classifying the *counting method*, not the system."
- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — worked / **intuitive**. Six steps, Previous/Next, one equation component per step. This is the correct pattern and it should be the model for the beginner path generally — Pass 1's complaint about the same analogy four times is a symptom of the beginner path lacking exactly this "one new idea per step" discipline. Step 1's ρ definition ("the local mass/energy density") is where I first noticed the ρ conflict. `ln` is explained well and concretely; the `+1` is not explained at all, only absorbed into a Hill-function identity that a reader at this level won't parse.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which definition of ρ is authoritative — number density of elements, mass/energy density, or "presence" — and does the choice change the equation's meaning? Every other page depends on the answer.
2. Does ρ have units or not? `/core-idea` supplies them, `/coherence-function` denies they exist.
3. Is the nine-badge vocabulary a spec that current content violates, or a description of legacy content that new labels are free to extend? The answer determines whether the ~11 extra labels are bugs or features.
4. Which is the canonical badge reference — the glossary or `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`? Two pages define all nine; only one claims canonicity.
5. Are "0 of 6 audited" and "9 of 9 audited" describing the same audit at different times, or different audits? No page relates them.
6. Is "Withdrawn" (TEST-04) a badge, a lifecycle state, or both? The landing page calls it "an operational state" as distinct from the badges; `/tier-1-existing` renders it in the badge slot.
7. Why does the Beginner path list Glossary as step 6 (a reference work as a terminal reading step), rather than linking it contextually from steps 1–5 where the unfamiliar terms actually appear?

### Verdict

The information architecture here is the strongest part of the site and nobody would guess it from the surface: consistent breadcrumbs across every page, eight learning paths with honest time estimates and prerequisites, correct multi-path step indicators, working redirects for legacy URLs, and a step-per-concept walkthrough tool that is a model of its type. The failure is entirely in the terminology layer sitting on top of it — ρ is defined three incompatible ways and its units are asserted and denied on adjacent pages, and a well-designed nine-term badge vocabulary has been overrun by roughly twenty ad-hoc status strings including two the site itself marks deprecated. Both are fixable without touching a single physics claim, and both are the kind of defect that makes a careful reader distrust the content for reasons that have nothing to do with whether the content is right.

**Where I stopped agreeing.** Three places, all of them cases where the site under-proves its own negative result and one where it over-claims a positive one. These are in the friction log; the two that matter most are worth spelling out here because they're additions, not corrections.

*(1) The MOND collapse is an algebraic identity, not just a BIC verdict.* The site reports that the free-γ compander fit converges to γ ≈ 0.49 with "identical residuals" to MOND, and separately that this equals a "return exponent q = 2γ." *(2) The γ sign inversion has a rigorous form the site doesn't use.* Every page that touches γ = 2/√N_corr says the sign "runs backwards against condensed-matter intuition" and leaves it there. It's stronger than intuition. Identify N_corr with the number of degrees of freedom in a correlation volume — the only identification that makes the name mean anything — so N_corr ~ (ξ/a)^d. Then γ = 2/√N_corr ~ (a/ξ)^(d/2), and since ξ → ∞ at any critical point, **γ → 0 at every continuous phase transition**. The framework's transition is maximally flat exactly where real transitions are maximally sharp. That's not an intuition clash, it's a limit. Anti-correlated across roughly eleven orders of magnitude, in the direction the site says. It should say it with the Ginzburg criterion and be done.

*(3) The equation contains no temperature, and that — not the missing feedback loop — is why the chemistry and superconductivity applications had to fail.* `/coherence-function` correctly says tanh is analogy, not derivation, and gives the right reason as far as it goes: Ising's m = tanh(βJzm) is a self-consistency fixed point, C(ρ) is evaluated directly. There is no energy-scale ratio anywhere in C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) — no T/T_c, no J/kT, nothing. Every ordering transition in condensed matter happens at essentially **fixed density while temperature varies**: melting, BEC condensation, superconducting T_c, ferromagnetic T_C. That single observation explains the 53% melting-point error and the 6.5×-wrong YBCO T_c structurally, rather than as two separate empirical disappointments. `/core-idea` gets close — "no singularity and no length scale in the equation" — and should add "and no temperature," because that's the one that does the work.

**On the YBCO failure specifically**, since it's my field: the site attributes it entirely to η turning out to be Abrikosov–Gor'kov pair-breaking efficiency (1960), which is a correct novelty audit. YBCO is a d-wave, strong-coupling superconductor with an observed antinodal ratio of ~5–8; even clean d-wave BCS gives 4.28. Applying 3.53 to a cuprate is a category error before η enters. Structurally, C(ρ) is a function of local density. A uniform external gravitational field does not change local density. So the equation as written has **no external-field dependence at all** — its natural prediction is EFE = 0, not 0.3–0.4×. Where the fractional factor comes from is shown nowhere; `/parameter-derivations` doesn't list it. A number that is neither derived nor calibrated nor badged, sitting on a page the site links from its researcher route, is precisely the failure mode the rest of the site was built to prevent.

Empirically, this is not an open test. Chae et al. (2020, ApJ 904, 51) report the EFE detected at >4σ in a blind analysis of 153 SPARC galaxies and at 8σ–11σ individually in "golden" high-external-field galaxies, at the amplitude MOND predicts. A framework predicting 0.3–0.4× MOND's EFE is in tension with that; a framework predicting zero EFE is refuted by it. Either way, `/test-catalog`'s headline — *"of these 24 proposed tests, 0 currently discriminate from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM"* — is contradicted by a claim the site is already making. The honest resolution is to badge the EFE number, state its provenance, and either retire it or log the Chae comparison as refutation #5. Note the direction of the correction: the site is *under*-refuted here, not over-refuted.

*(3) Wide binaries are the only place the framework can genuinely lose, and Gaia DR4 lands on 2 December 2026.* At kAU separations the local stellar density is effectively constant across the sample, so C(ρ) is a constant multiplicative factor — degenerate with a rescaled G and therefore unobservable. The framework predicts exactly Newtonian wide-binary dynamics at all separations. `/tier-1-existing` labels TEST-02 "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie / awaits DR4," which is the correct adjudication (a Newtonian result also matches ΛCDM, so it cannot win) — but "cannot win" is not "not worth running." DR4 has a firm public date four months out and roughly 2.8× better proper motions than DR3. Registering a numeric threshold on the gravity-boost parameter **before 2 December 2026** would give this program the first genuinely prospective test in its history, against a site that currently states none of its 24 tests were pre-registered before data availability. That is the highest-leverage action available and it has a deadline.

**On A2ACW: the instrument is measured against the wrong controls.** `/a2acw` reports sensitivity 6/6 and specificity 0/6 → Youden's J = 0, "zero discriminating information." I don't think that number means what the page says.

The specificity arm uses COBE anisotropy, the Higgs detection, and the first gravitational-wave observation as negative controls. Those are **experimental detections**. The items being screened are theoretical claims under suspicion of being known physics in new notation. A reparametrization detector fed detections is being scored out of class — you learn nothing about its operating point on the population it will actually see. The right negative controls are theoretical results that were historically confusable with reparametrization and turned out novel: Anderson (1963) → Higgs (1964) is literally the boundary case (the Higgs mechanism *was* the plasmon argument in relativistic notation, and was new anyway); Bekenstein–Hawking entropy; Guth's inflation against de Sitter; 't Hooft–Veltman renormalizability. Score against those and you'd learn the false-positive rate that matters.

Two further defects. Sensitivity 6/6 is **in-sample**: the three detection axes (vocabulary translation, symbol audit, null-baseline) were derived from the same six demotions they are scored on, so 6/6 is training accuracy, not a measured true-positive rate. And Youden's J = sens + spec − 1 is only meaningful when both arms are drawn from a common case mix; here one arm is in-corpus claims and the other is famous external discoveries. J = 0 is arithmetic, not an ROC point. The page's Clopper–Pearson caveat is applied to specificity (upper bound ~0.46) but not to sensitivity (6/6 → 95% lower bound ≈ 0.54), which is the asymmetry that lets the result read as tidier than it is.

The registered fix is also aimed at the wrong variable. `/open-questions` gates the control arm on *"models from vendors with maximally disjoint training pipelines."* That corpus does not exist — every frontier model has read the same arXiv. The informative control is the one the site lists second and buries: **submit pre-Planck-era results in post-2015 terminology and measure the true-positive rate on prior-art detection.** That is cheap, runnable this week, needs no vendor access, and directly estimates the quantity the null claim rests on. It should be promoted to the primary registered experiment.

**On the refutation count: the site over-refutes on galaxies.** The four executed refutations — RAR transition shape (2,807 SPARC points), BTFR slope (123 SPARC), dwarf DM fractions (153 SPARC), environment null (SPARC + CF4) — are four statistics on one 175-galaxy sample with heavily overlapping subsamples. They are not four independent refutations; they are one dataset examined four ways, and the RAR-shape and BTFR results in particular are close to the same information (a slope and a curvature of the same relation). The Bullet Cluster, cited on `/key-claims` as "independent falsification," is also not independent of the local-density no-go — it refutes *any* purely baryon-density-sourced modification, which is the same theorem. A site this careful about not over-claiming positives should apply the same discipline to negatives; "one dataset, four correlated statistics, all failing" is both more accurate and, honestly, still decisive.

Two smaller technical flags in the same direction. The BTFR comparison reports **MOND at n = 3.81 ± 0.04** against observed 3.75 ± 0.10 and Synchronism's 3.35 ± 0.07. MOND's BTFR prediction is not a fitted 3.81 — it is *exactly* 4, parameter-free, from M_b = V⁴/(Ga₀). Comparing a fitted competitor against a fixed framework prediction weakens what is otherwise a clean discriminator; run MOND at 4.0 and it deviates by 0.25, which **survives the same >0.3 kill rule that Synchronism's 0.41 fires**. That version of the result is stronger, fairer, and citable. Separately, TEST-10's kill turns on a structural ceiling of 68.5% derived from 1/Ω_m = 3.17. Why 1/Ω_m and not Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.4, the cosmic baryon fraction, which would put the ceiling at ~84%? `/parameter-derivations` derives neither. One of the four refutations rests on an underived choice between two natural cosmological ratios, and the other choice may not fire.

**On DESI:** TEST-04a is still keyed to DR1. DR2's main cosmology papers went out 19 March 2025 and prefer evolving dark energy in w₀wₐCDM when combined with CMB and SNe; DR2 full-shape growth is not yet public. The site is not wrong to cite DR1 for fσ₈, but the same pre-registration logic as Gaia applies: the DR2 full-shape fσ₈ threshold should be locked *now*, while the number is still unknown. `/honest-assessment` says of evolving dark energy "no coupling mechanism exists," which is the right disclosure — but a framework whose one famous relation ties a galactic scale to H₀ is not entitled to be silent about a result that says H(z)'s late-time behaviour is not what ΛCDM assumed.

**One logical gap worth an explicit answer.** The site holds two central findings simultaneously: (a) the free-γ compander fit collapses to MOND (γ → 0.49, ΔBIC = +184 against the γ=2 form), and (b) the framework fails because it keys on local ρ rather than non-local g_bar. But the ΔBIC run is described in RAR space — i.e. the compander was fit *in the acceleration variable*. If so, the model that "converges to MOND" is not the framework; it is a one-parameter interpolating function in g_bar, and the collapse result says nothing about C(ρ). Which variable the AIC/BIC run used should be stated on the page, because the two headline findings are only compatible if it's g_bar, and if it is g_bar then finding (a) is about a proxy.

**One synthesis the site has both halves of and never makes.** `/for-researchers` lists the CHSH substrate null and the dim-4 radiative LIV naturalness gap (CPSU 2004, c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻² at one loop against 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰ bounds) as two separate citable artifacts. They are one artifact. Any substrate that reproduces quantum correlations must be Bell-nonlocal; a Bell-nonlocal substrate with an absolute time ordering has a preferred foliation; a preferred foliation without custodial symmetry is exactly what radiatively generates the dim-4 coefficients. So the CHSH loss and the 16–28 OOM fine-tuning gap are the same constraint entering twice, and stating it as one chain — *"reproducing entanglement forces a preferred frame, and a preferred frame costs 16–28 orders of magnitude of fine-tuning"* — is a genuinely tight no-go on absolute-time substrates, and considerably more citable than either half alone. It also removes one item from a refutation count I've just argued is inflated.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked / **arithmetically exact, physically incoherent by construction**. Every entry reproduces: 2/√1 = 2.000, 2/√4 = 1.000, 2/√30 = 0.365, 2/√100 = 0.200, 2/√10⁶ = 2.00×10⁻³, 2/√10⁷ = 6.32×10⁻⁴. All ✓. The caveats are unusually honest — *"no protocol exists for converting a system's Hamiltonian into N_corr without first fitting γ to observed behavior"* is a sentence most authors would never write, and it is by itself sufficient to close the parameter. My addition is above: the six presets use four mutually inconsistent definitions of N_corr, and two of them scale differently with sample size, so γ is not an intensive property of a material. The ideal gas remains the cleanest reductio on the site — the framework's *sharpest* transition (γ = 2) is assigned to the one system with no phase transition at all.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked / **MOND implementation correct, mass model inconsistent with it, and the framework law is a halo not a gravity law**. ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) is the standard simple-ν and reduces correctly in both limits. DDO 154 at V_flat = 47 km/s matches SPARC. The toy BTFR M_b = 47·V⁴ is the observed normalization, but it implies a₀ ≈ 1.6×10⁻¹⁰ while the plotted MOND curve uses 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ — an ~8% systematic in V_flat between the two traces, which matters if anyone eyeballs agreement off this plot. The more consequential observation: "Synchronism (real)" is implemented as v² = v_b² + [V_flat·C(ρ)]², i.e. baryons plus an additive component. That is a dark-matter profile in the ΛCDM sense, and it also means ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat² takes the galaxy's own asymptotic velocity as an input — so the model has **no galaxy-level predictive content whatsoever**; it can only be tested on ensemble relations, which is exactly why TEST-09/TEST-10 are the only places it could die. The site labels V_flat a "calibrated input" but never draws that consequence.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — worked / **the right finding, undersold**. The 2-parameter polynomial-in-Z null matching the correlations to |Δr| ≤ 0.07 is the correct and sufficient result: near-monotone Z-dependence manufactures r > 0.9 by construction. What the page doesn't say is why this had to happen. C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) contains **no temperature and no energy-scale ratio** — no β, no J, no T/T_c. Every equilibrium property in that table is a thermal quantity at fixed density. Correlating a density-monotone function against density-monotone thermodynamic quantities isn't 23 tests; it's roughly one test (stiffness vs Z) repeated, run in the one variable the equation cannot resolve. The null model is the empirical proof; the missing β is the reason.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Where does the 0.3–0.4× EFE factor come from, and how is any external-field dependence obtained from a function of local density alone? If it can't be, does the framework predict EFE = 0 — and is it therefore already refuted by Chae et al. 2020 at 4σ blind / 8–11σ golden?
2. Why is H₀ = 2πa₀/c = 77.6 km/s/Mpc not in the ledger? Is there a reason the relation is treated as one-directional?
3. Was the free-γ compander fit run in ρ or in g_bar? The MOND-collapse finding and the locality no-go are only mutually consistent under one answer.
4. Where does the 1/Ω_m = 3.17 boost ceiling come from, and why not Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.4? TEST-10's kill depends on the choice.
5. What prior-art search was run before "none exists" was published on the local-density no-go, and can it be shown?
6. Will a numeric wide-binary threshold be registered before Gaia DR4 on 2 December 2026? This is the only prospective test available, and the window closes.
7. Why is the cross-vendor control arm — which requires a corpus that doesn't exist — the primary registered A2ACW experiment, rather than the vocabulary-asymmetry TPR test, which is runnable now?
8. Does the program intend to lock a DESI DR2 full-shape fσ₈ threshold before that release, given DR2 BAO has been public since 19 March 2025?

### Verdict

There is no new physics here, the site says so, and I agree with its verdict while disputing its bookkeeping in both directions: the four galaxy refutations are one dataset examined four ways and should not be counted as four, while at least two live claims — an H₀ of 77.6 km/s/Mpc that sits outside both ends of the Hubble tension, and an EFE prediction that Chae et al. 2020 already constrains at 4σ–11σ — are unbadged, unlogged, and contradict the site's own "0 discriminating tests" headline. The A2ACW null, which is the most genuinely interesting artifact on the site, is measured against a category-mismatched control set and an in-sample sensitivity, so its J = 0 is arithmetic rather than an operating point; the fix is cheap and is already the program's own second-listed experiment. What I would tell a colleague: read `/parameter-derivations` and `/for-researchers` as an unusually good specimen of a self-refuted program, register the Gaia DR4 wide-binary threshold before 2 December, and stop describing a fully-executed post-mortem as a set of open predictions.
