## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, why-synchronism, first-encounter, core-idea, galaxy-plotter, gamma-calculator, honest-assessment, what-synchronism-is-not)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A weirdly honest physics site that kept telling me its own idea failed — I trusted it more than most science sites but left unsure why I should care about an equation that loses to its rivals.

### Journey

Clicked in from Reddit. The landing page hooked me immediately — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of question PBS Space Time would open with. Then it did something I've literally never seen: it advertised "0 confirmed predictions" and "4 refutations" on its own homepage. That either means these people are very trustworthy or the whole thing doesn't work. Turns out kind of both?

The marching-band-vs-crowd analogy landed for me right away. High coherence = band in lockstep, low coherence = crowd milling around. Good. I followed the "Beginner Path →" link.

**Why Synchronism** (step 1) started well — "density is the master control lever" — but the jargon wall hit fast. "Phenomenological choice," "ansatz," "compander family" — on the *beginner* page, step 1 of 6. Also the crowd/band comparison is described in words but never shown as a picture, which felt like a missed layup. I skimmed the bottom half.

**First Encounter** (step 2) was the best page of my visit. "A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy" — that clicked. The MRH "bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" idea made sense... until the page re-defined it a second time as "minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence," which reads like it was written by a different, much scarier author.

**The Core Idea** (step 3): the dimmer-switch metaphor worked, and I appreciated that tanh got explained as "just an S-shaped curve — flat, then steep, then flat again." That's all I needed. But then it told me the special density ρcrit is NOT the middle of the S-curve (C ≈ 0.88 there?), which broke my mental picture and nothing repaired it. Also γ = "2/√Ncorr" means nothing to me because I don't know what Ncorr is either — defining one mystery symbol with another.

I skipped ahead to the toys. **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: the core visual is genuinely great — gray dashed line sagging (what gravity predicts), dots staying flat (what telescopes see), and the gap is literally the dark matter mystery. I got that in ten seconds. But there are FIVE curves, and two of them are violet — one is "the real equation (fails)" and one is "hand-tuned (fits)" — and I honestly could not keep straight which violet line was the actual theory. Also acronyms like BTFR and RAR float around unexplained, and there are no sliders, so it felt more like a museum display than a toy.

**γ Calculator**: I clicked the preset buttons (Ideal gas, Liquid water, Ferromagnet...) and watched γ change, which was mildly fun, but I could not tell you what γ *is*. Is it a percentage? A steepness? The page says γ ≈ 1 marks "the regime boundary where chemistry and collective/correlated behavior happen" — those are words, but they didn't make a picture in my head. There's no graph on the page, which is strange for a tool about a curve.

Then I went to **Honest Assessment** because the failure thing kept nagging at me. First five minutes: "wow, they timestamp their own mistakes and print corrections in the body." Next ten minutes: drowning. ΔBIC=+184, 3.3σ, r²=0.14, TEST-09, badge taxonomy where "Every refutation carries a Failed badge; not every Failed badge counts as a refutation." I appreciated the italic plain-language boxes before each failure — those saved me — but I fatigued out about a third of the way down. The one translation that stuck: "a high-school algebra function explains their chemistry data better than their fancy equation." Ouch, and also, thank you for saying it that plainly.

Finished on **What Synchronism Is Not**, which was clarifying — "not a Theory of Everything, not a replacement for MOND, not journal-reviewed, not proven." But even this expectation-setting page threw "Abrikosov-Gor'kov pair-breaking" and "EFE-degenerate" at me. And it left me with the visit's core puzzle: if it's not proven, not confirmed, and loses to something called MOND everywhere... the site never quite told me, in my language, why I should still be here. I *think* the answer is "watching honest science fail in public is the point," and honestly that's kind of compelling, but the site makes me infer it.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — half-intuitive — The sagging-line-vs-flat-dots setup teaches the dark matter problem brilliantly; the five-curve overlay (especially two violet Synchronism curves) then un-teaches it. No sliders, so "interactive" oversells it.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is MOND, actually? It beats this framework on every page and I still couldn't explain it to a friend.
2. What does γ *measure*, in one plain sentence?
3. If the equation already failed the galaxy test, what would success even look like from here — what's the site hoping happens next?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've ever stumbled into — it timestamps its own mistakes and leads with its failures, which paradoxically made me trust it far more than a slick "we solved physics" site. But as a casual reader I hit a jargon wall by step 1 of the beginner path, and the site never quite says in plain words why I should care about an equation it admits keeps losing. I'd come back if a friend asked "is this legit?" — I'd say "legit-ly unfinished, and unusually upfront about it" — but I probably wouldn't return on my own without a gentler on-ramp.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, learning-paths, glossary, interactive-tools, coherence-explorer, equation-walkthrough, honest-assessment, why-synchronism, mrh, tier-1-existing, key-claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Unusually well-scaffolded docs with a real glossary and honest badge taxonomy, undermined by an undocumented status-vocabulary sprawl and acronym-expansion failures on exactly the pages that need them most.

### Journey

I audited this as I would any product doc set: nav first, then terminology, then whether the cross-references hold up.

**Navigation skeleton is genuinely good.** The landing page segments by audience ("No physics background?" / "Physicist?" / "Researcher?"), the Beginner Path is a real numbered sequence, and — this is rarer than it should be — the step indicators actually work. Why Synchronism shows "Beginner Path — Step 1 of 6," Honest Assessment shows "Step 5 of 6," Glossary shows "Step 6 of 6" with a "← Prev: Honest Assessment" link. Someone maintains this. The one hub that breaks the pattern is **/learning-paths itself**: it offers two parallel taxonomies (difficulty-based AND topic-based) claiming to cover the same content, with no mapping between them and no breadcrumbs on the hub page. If "The Hard Problem" is step 6 of one track and also appears in another, say whether it's the same page. Also, every breadcrumb routes through an intermediate crumb called "Getting Started" that isn't a link and doesn't exist as a page anywhere I could find — a phantom hierarchy level.

**The glossary is the best page on the site** from a docs standpoint: alphabetical with Greek symbols sorted after Latin (and it *tells you* that's the sort rule), validation badges clustered as a group, "Learn more →" cross-links, physicist-warning flags on the terms that collide with standard usage. My complaints are inventory gaps (Bullet Cluster, entity criterion, Tsirelson's bound are used elsewhere but absent) and register whiplash — "Baryon: protons, neutrons, and everything made of them" sits next to a BIC entry with full parameter notation.

**Terminology consistency is a two-tier story.** The *defined* vocabulary is impressively consistent: "coherence = density-driven collective ordering (0=sparse, 1=dense)" is restated nearly verbatim on key-claims (three times — twice too many; make it a shared callout component), coherence-explorer, and equation-walkthrough, always with the "not quantum coherence" warning. Refutation counts sync across pages ("4 refutations executed on external data," recount-dated). This is disciplined.

The *operational* status vocabulary, though, has sprawled without documentation. On /tier-1-existing alone I counted: FAILED, RETIRED, WITHDRAWN, CLOSED, RECLASSIFIED, ADJUDICATED, "Kill Criterion Fired," "Ceiling Exceeded," plus bracket conventions like "[Withdrawn]" in prediction text. These are clearly used *deliberately* — Withdrawn and Retired are not interchangeable here — but the badge-definitions section only covers the two badge families. There is no page documenting the lifecycle: what states exist, what transitions between them mean, which appear in headings vs. status notes. A reader who learns the badge taxonomy still can't parse "MOND-shared / RETIRED." One test note even says "the 'MOND-shared' class is retired" — a status label about a status label, with no legend.

**Badge system: well-defined, badly distributed.** The definitions at /honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions are excellent — the Failed-badge-vs-refutation distinction ("a refutation is the narrower subset of Failed results executed on external data against a registered criterion") is exactly the kind of precision most sites lack. But key-claims and tier-1-existing both use badges with only a link-away to definitions; the landing page glosses only Reparametrization inline. The canonical fix is boring and standard: title-attribute or tooltip gloss at first use per page, definitions page as backing reference.

**Acronym audit — the clearest failure.** On /tier-1-existing, RAR, BTFR, MOND, EFE, DESI, and DM are all used without expansion at first mention; only SPARC and ALFALFA-SDSS get contextual gloss. This is the page the landing page routes *researchers* to, so some assumed knowledge is defensible — but MOND going unexpanded site-wide (also flagged by Pass 1 from the casual side) means both ends of the audience spectrum hit the same wall. Meanwhile /key-claims expands nearly everything correctly (even MRH at first use), proving the site knows how to do this — it's inconsistent application, not a missing convention.

**Tools.** I audited Coherence Explorer and Equation Anatomy (Pass 1 covered the plotter and calculator). Both follow a docs pattern I'd hold up as a model: plain-language definitions *before* the controls, epistemic framing box ("What this shows / what it gets wrong"), breadcrumbs. Equation Anatomy has real prev/next step navigation (Step 1 of 6) and glosses every symbol including ln and tanh with numeric examples. Two issues: "saturation knee" is used in a slider label on coherence-explorer before it's defined, and Equation Anatomy stacks *three* caveat layers (pre-tour warning, red honesty box, physicist note) before the reader reaches Step 1 content — the honesty apparatus is starting to compete with the content for the reader's first minute. The tools index also mixes level scales: Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced / "Conceptual" — one of these is not a level.

**A structural observation about the corrections culture.** Dated corrections are embedded inline everywhere: "recounted 2026-07-17," "CORRECTED 2026-07-15," glossary entries noting "rewritten 2026-07-17." As transparency, this is admirable and I wouldn't remove it. As information architecture, it's changelog content living inside reference content, and it's accumulating. At current density, every page slowly becomes its own revision history. The standard resolution: a site changelog page, with inline corrections reduced to a marker linking into it.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, parameter-derivations, galaxy-rotation, key-claims, interactive-tools, phase-boundary-visualizer, chemistry-correlation-explorer, core-idea, honest-assessment, tier-1-existing)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The physics is a bounded fitting function that collapses to MOND when you free its one shape parameter — and the site knows this and says so; the derivation pages read like a referee report the authors wrote against themselves.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project." I skipped the on-ramp and went straight for the derivations, because that's where frameworks like this usually die quietly. Here they die loudly, with timestamps.

**Landing page.** C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)), advertised alongside "0 confirmed predictions, 4 refutations, 6 of 6 'Validated' claims failed audit." I've never seen a homepage volunteer its own obituary. Skepticism recalibrated from "debunk this" to "check whether the self-audit is real."

**Parameter derivations.** This page opens by disclaiming itself: "this is not a derivations page in the mathematical sense," self-audited to "zero parameters with independent first-principles derivations." I checked the individual entries:

- **γ = 2/√N_corr** — labeled a motivated ansatz, and the page itself makes the two objections I would have made: the 1/√N scaling is CLT logic applied to *correlated* degrees of freedom (exactly where iid fails), and the sign is inverted relative to real physics — larger correlated systems get *flatter* transitions under this formula, whereas in actual condensed matter the most collective systems (BCS, BEC) have among the sharpest transitions in nature. The site states this inversion explicitly. I have nothing to add; that's the kill shot and they fired it at themselves.
- **A ≈ 0.029** — the stated formula A = 4π/(βJ²GR₀²) evaluates 600× off from the used value, and the page documents that the ρ_crit ∝ V² exponent has "three mutually incompatible provenances" (+0.5 from the stated Jeans argument, +2 in the code, −2 required to match MOND). A constant whose derivation gives a different number, used with an exponent of the wrong sign, is not a derivation. Page agrees: "Audited-Negative — Chain-of-Custody Failure."
- **a₀ = cH₀/(2π)** — I checked the arithmetic: cH₀ ≈ 6.5×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² at H₀ = 67.4, over 2π gives 1.04×10⁻¹⁰. Correct, and correctly labeled: this coincidence has been on the books since Milgrom (1983), and it's 13% under the observed 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. Reparametrization badge is the right call.
- **Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G)** — algebraically Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) exactly, so it is the *same* coincidence in different units, and the page says so ("not a second derivation"). Good — most frameworks would count this twice.
- **φ** — "fitted-then-named," with the claimed derivation being the golden ratio's defining identity x + x² = 1 postulated as physics. Circular, flagged as circular.

**Galaxy rotation.** Here's the structural finding of my visit: **C(ρ) is never algebraically connected to rotation curves.** The actual fits use McGaugh's RAR function g_obs = g_bar/(1 − e^(−√(g_bar/a₀))) — i.e., MOND phenomenology — with Synchronism contributing only a scatter ansatz on top. When the compander μ = tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) was tested head-to-head on 2,807 SPARC points: ΔBIC = +184 at the framework's pinned γ = 2; freeing γ finds 0.49 and reproduces McGaugh's RMS to four digits (0.1437 dex), with the residual +7.1 ΔBIC being almost exactly one parameter's ln(N) penalty — an internal consistency check that actually passes, which tells me the statistics were done properly. Their own conclusion: "There is no γ for which the compander is both (a) distinct from MOND and (b) consistent with SPARC." That's the whole ballgame, stated in their own words. The deeper reason is also on the page and is correct physics: C(ρ) is a function of *local* density while the RAR is organized by g_bar, a *non-local* enclosed-mass quantity — a pointwise map can't reproduce an acceleration-space relation across different mass geometries without per-system calibration. The Galaxy Plotter's "hand-tuned radius stand-in" (which confused Pass 1) is this no-go showing through.

**Core idea.** I specifically checked the tanh motivation against Landau/mean-field theory. The page pre-empts the obvious trap: "tanh's form recalls the Ising model self-consistency equation... the resemblance is only visual: C(ρ) has no feedback loop (ρ goes in, C comes out), so the Ising analogy carries no motivating force." That is exactly right — Weiss mean field gives m = tanh(βJzm) *because* of self-consistency; a one-way sigmoid shares only the letterforms. Also honest: C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88, so ρ_crit is a saturation knee, not a critical point (midpoint is near 0.32·ρ_crit). One quibble: the dismissal of critical exponents ("β_eff = 1 is a tautology for any analytic function") is technically correct but undersold — abandoning criticality means abandoning universality classes entirely, so every "phase transition" phrasing elsewhere on the site is decorative vocabulary, not physics. The compander reframing handles this, but the cost should be stated where the dismissal is.

**Key claims + honest assessment + tier-1.** Badge audit: **I found zero "Validated" or "Supported" badges on any page I visited.** The quantum sector is correctly identified as reparametrization — the decoherence formula Γ = γ²(1−c) is flagged as the special case of Palma–Suominen–Ekert (1996) correlated dephasing, "resynchronization" maps to dynamical decoupling, and the Bell simulations honestly report S = 1.98–2.00, i.e., the classical bound, with the framework needing signaling to exceed it. The consciousness claim is labeled "unrunnable" rather than untested — a distinction I appreciate. On tier-1: after TEST-05/09/10 kills, the page's own summary is that no remaining test can favorably distinguish the framework — TEST-01's predicted lever is ~50× below MOND's EFE, and TEST-02 is "self-eliminating-or-tie." The page even documents its own criterion slippage on TEST-09 (the 0.3 threshold ported across a changed variable definition). This is more procedural self-criticism than most published papers survive.

**Tools.** Both tools I tested carry correct caveats but one has a deeper problem — see below.

The page volunteers its own null-model defeat: a 2-parameter polynomial in Z "matches these correlations (\|Δr\| ≤ 0.07) and sometimes beats them," and states plainly "High r here is evidence of density-monotonicity (known chemistry), not of C(ρ)-specific physics." That's the correct conclusion and most authors would not print it. Remaining gap is curation opacity (23 of 1,703 shown) and the unaudited 47.

### Unanswered Questions
1. After free-γ fitting collapses the compander onto MOND's interpolating behavior, what work is tanh still doing anywhere on the site? Every downstream page still writes C(ρ) = tanh(...) as *the* equation — but the site's own model comparison found it's the last-place member of a four-way-tied sigmoid family. Why not present the family and demote the specific form?
2. Is the quoted "MOND n = 3.81 ± 0.04" a fit, and how does the comparison read against MOND's parameter-free n = 4? (Observed 3.75 ± 0.10 is in mild tension with *that* too — which would be worth a sentence.)
3. The RAR misfit of the γ=2 compander leaves a "≈0.05–0.10 dex signature at the transition, significant at ~8σ per bin" — is that residual structure real in SPARC independent of any framework? If so, that's the most interesting number on the site and nobody flags it as such.
4. Has anyone computed N_corr *prospectively* for any system? Every value I saw was either asserted (N_corr = 1 for both ideal gases and galaxies — different microphysics, same number, flagged by the site as an artifact) or back-solved from a fitted γ.
5. Is there even a sketch of a dynamical principle — an action, a field equation, a transport equation — from which C(ρ) would emerge as a steady state? Without one, this is a regression exercise with an ontology attached, and the site comes within one sentence of saying so.

### Verdict
The self-audit is real. I came to catch reparametrizations and found every one I identified already flagged on the page where it lives — a₀ as Milgrom's coincidence, Σ₀ as a₀ in different units, the decoherence formula as Palma–Suominen–Ekert, the tanh/Ising resemblance correctly dismissed as visual-only, and the free-γ collapse to MOND stated in the authors' own words with correct BIC accounting. The physics itself does not survive: zero derived parameters, a sign-inverted core scaling relation, no dynamical equations, and a flagship application where the flagship equation is never algebraically connected to the observable. What's left is genuinely interesting as an artifact — the most rigorous public autopsy of a failed framework I've seen, apparently conducted by the same AI system that produced the framework — but I'd hand a fellow student the parameter-derivations page as a masterclass in how to audit an ansatz, not the equation as something to build on. My one real complaint: the Phase Boundary Visualizer still teaches the inverted γ–N_corr relation more fluently than its caveats retract it.

### Journey

I came in with three questions: what's actually new, does anything discriminate from MOND+ΛCDM, and is the A2ACW methodology sound. The site answers all three itself, mostly correctly, which is disorienting for a referee.

**What's actually new.** The for-researchers page is admirably blunt: "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, and 0 tests currently discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." The one artifact it stakes as citable is a **local-density no-go**: any gravity modification keyed on local volumetric density ρ(r) fails jointly against SPARC RAR, cluster scaling (10⁴–10⁶× density mismatch), and cross-system offsets, because the RAR is organized by g_bar — a non-local, enclosed-mass quantity. The scoping is correct and careful: the page explicitly exempts Verlinde, MOG/STVG, and surface-density MOND formulations, and credits Milgrom's modified-inertia non-locality argument as the core obstruction ("the core obstruction is *not* ours"). My assessment: this is a legitimate but modest negative result — a quantified corollary of an argument the MOND community has understood since the 1980s–90s (Milgrom's own surface-density discussions are adjacent prior art the page doesn't search). Citable in the sense that a footnote is citable. It is the only thing on the site I might reference in a paper, and only in a review's landscape section.

**Does anything discriminate.** By execution, no — and the site's accounting here is the best part. The two structural differences from MOND (bounded boost B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17; local coupling variable) each forced disagreement on exactly one observable, and the framework lost both (TEST-09 BTFR slope: 3.35±0.07 predicted vs 3.75±0.10 observed; TEST-10: 69% of SPARC galaxies exceed the DM-fraction ceiling). The free-γ compander collapses onto MOND at γ≈0.49. Of the 24 catalog tests, the site's own line is "0 currently discriminate," and every Tier 2–4 test is honestly marked unrunnable because "no protocol maps any laboratory or astronomical observable to the coherence value C" — a categorical admission most frameworks never make. TEST-02 (wide binaries) predicts 0.05–0.4% velocity deviation vs MOND's ~18%, below Gaia DR3 systematics — correctly labeled "self-eliminating-or-tie."

But here's what I can add that the site can't see from inside: **the TEST-02 fork is written against an idealized Gaia, not the actual literature.** The page presents "Gaia confirms MOND-scale anomalies" vs "Gaia confirms Newtonian null" as a clean future branch. The field's live state is that Chae (2023–24) claims a detected low-acceleration boost while Banik et al. (2024) claim a ~19σ Newtonian preference from a differently-selected sample — the branch condition is *itself* contested on selection and systematics, and neither camp is cited anywhere I looked. Similarly, the landscape section still adjudicates relativistic MOND at the TeVeS-2006 level ("Failed: requires dark matter for Bullet Cluster") with no mention of AeST/Skordis–Złośnik, which passes CMB and is the current benchmark completion; there is no EFT-of-LSS engagement; and the site itself concedes it has nothing to say about evolving dark energy — the *actual* dominant anomaly DESI has put on the table — or H₀. The refutations are real, but the rival lineup they're scored against is a museum exhibit. "0 discriminating tests vs MOND+ΛCDM" is doing load-bearing work against 2023-frozen rivals.

**One accounting inconsistency worth flagging.** The preferred-frame sector carries the most lethal numbers on the site — dim-4 LIV coefficients c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻² against bounds of 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰ (16–28 OOM), and a predicted threshold-dipole anisotropy ~10⁻³ sitting 11–19 OOM above cavity/clock bounds — yet these appear as "constraints" while the headline count stays at "4 refutations." The definitional defense (refutation = executed on external data against a registered criterion) is internally consistent, but a physicist reads 19 orders of magnitude as more conclusive than any ΔBIC. The absolute-time substrate is excluded at face value; a plain sentence saying so near the scoreboard would be more honest than letting the "4" carry the summary.

**Is A2ACW sound.** For generating physics: no, and the a2acw page measures its own failure — specificity 0/6 on held-out genuine discoveries (COBE, Higgs, GW), Youden J = 1.0 − 1.0 = 0, verdict "a reparametrization detector, not a discovery engine," with the structural diagnosis correctly identified: the Challenger "is another sample from the same corpus... novelty-vs-rederivation is precisely the question the corpus cannot answer about itself." That diagnosis is right and matches what we're seeing across AI-for-science efforts. My referee notes on the *null itself*: N=6 retrospective audits from one corpus and one vendor, no preregistration, no human baseline, no cross-vendor arm — and a 0/6 specificity carries a 95% binomial CI reaching ~0.46, so even "catches everything" is under-powered as stated. The page concedes nearly all of this ("'Controlled' in the experimental-design sense would overstate it") and the CCH health-metric weights are admitted numerology. Current status — "registered null with a stated path to becoming a citable artifact" — is the correct status. It should not migrate to "citable" without the cross-vendor and preregistered held-out arms actually running. The one component I'd watch: vocabulary-asymmetry as a rediscovery detector (4/4 retrospective) is a cute, testable idea that someone in the AI-evaluation community could pick up independently.

**Quantum sector.** Nothing new, correctly labeled: Γ = γ²(1−c) is the equal-coupling special case of Palma–Suominen–Ekert 1996 (badge says exactly this), "resynchronization" is dynamical decoupling, the c(d) form is imported from waveguide QED, and the CHSH work honestly reports hitting the classical bound S ≤ 2. The ontological reframe ("collapse = resonant selection at an MRH crossing") is an interpretation, and the page itself states the test: "If no prediction differs, this is classification as an interpretation, not as novel ontology." No prediction differs. Case closed by the defendant.
