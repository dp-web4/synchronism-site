# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-11-11

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /key-claims, /interactive-tools, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /honest-assessment (top part only), /glossary (top part only), /dark-matter)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understood the story in about two minutes (one equation for "how much things act together", tested on galaxies, it lost), and it is refreshingly upfront about losing, but almost every page past the first screen turns into a wall of numbers and dated correction notes that I skimmed past.

*Method note: pages were read through a text fetch, so I could not physically drag sliders or click buttons. Tool observations are about the default state, the controls listed and the instructions. Items marked "(fetch?)" may be artifacts of the text conversion and need a browser check before anyone fixes them.*

### Journey

**Landing page.** The headline question ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") is the kind of thing I click on. The sub-headline "Not a Theory of Everything" and the plain statement that the idea was tested and "did not hold as stated" surprised me, in a good way; I have never seen a fringe-physics site lead with that. The marching band versus crowd picture worked immediately. The "alive or dead" answer (dead as tested physics, alive as a public record) is the single most useful sentence on the page for me.

Then the page keeps going, for a long time. There is a block labelled "For physicists: which C the galaxy numbers used" with three versions of C, and I did not know there was more than one. The "Scientific Outcomes" section threw a lot at me: ΔBIC +184 and +2843, 17.95σ and 8.7σ, r² = 0.0001, Youden's J = 0. I get that "0 confirmed" and "6 refutations" are the headline, but then it says 6 is "4–8 under rules on file" and "at most 5 independent", and that whether two tests count as one "is pending the project lead". I don't know who the project lead is or why I'm being told about a pending decision. As a reader I just want one number and a sentence on why it's fuzzy.

One thing I noticed: the top of the page calls the Honest Assessment a "~25,000-word page", while a note lower down says that figure was corrected to "~18,000". I don't know which is right.

**Why Synchronism? (step 1).** This is the best page I saw. Short, three lines of "what happened", and a box that says "Beginner path: you can stop here." I liked being told I could stop. The folded physicist section I opened out of curiosity and closed fast (Hill/Naka–Rushton, Langevin/Curie–Weiss, M☉/pc³). There's a line saying the old "89%" figure is explained in the Glossary, but I never saw an 89% on this page, so I didn't know what it was pointing at.

**First Encounter (step 2).** Seven lettered sections, "about 10 minutes". Sections A and B were good: the lone electron versus the neutron star, the "dimmer switch", and the explanation that ln turns 10×, 100×, 1000× into steps of 2.3, 4.6, 6.9 is the first time anyone explained a logarithm to me that way. Section C is where I started to lose it: γ = 2/√N_corr, "a crystal of 10²⁴ atoms gives γ ≈ 10⁻¹²", BCS. I did get the punchline (the original guess for γ failed, and the value that fits galaxies just reproduces an older theory called MOND). The "predicted vs fitted" explanation was helpful. Section G "Where It Fails" was clear: 607 K predicted, 93 K actual. After Section G there seemed to be a button still labelled "Continue ↓ Section B", which looked like a leftover (fetch?).

**Key Claims.** I went here because the landing page teaser made it sound like the heart of the thing. I bounced. From the first claim it is "phase field", "Kochen–Specker", "Lüders projection rule", "reset strength s ≥ 0.975". The one part I enjoyed was the consciousness claim saying, in so many words, "Plainly: this formula is a placeholder". That honesty is appealing, but I couldn't tell you what Claim 1 actually says beyond "quantum weirdness might be things getting in sync". The page also doesn't tell me where to go next.

**Interactive Tools → Galaxy Curve Plotter.** The tools index is clear: it says which is the "Best first tool" and gives an order. On the plotter, "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" is exactly the hand-holding I wanted. Dots are measurements, the gray dashed line is what visible matter alone would give, green is MOND, violet is this site's equation, and the page says straight out that violet "does not follow the dots". A site whose own flagship tool shows its own curve missing: I found that more convincing than anything else here. What I didn't get: the "How heavy are the stars? Υdisk = 0.50" slider (the question is plain, the symbol is not, and I don't know what moving it is supposed to teach me), and the statement that the boost is "a flat 3.17× in gravity (1.78× in speed)". There are two checkboxes for extra curves ("old hand-drawn stand-in", "alternative way of plugging C in") that I would never tick. Below the chart is a very long physicist block.

**Coherence Explorer.** Described as the best first tool. It has a γ slider (opens at 0.49), a ρcrit slider, a log/linear toggle, and four presets. The "One thing to try first" instruction (drag γ up to 2 and back to 0.5) is good. But the badge right under the title says "Reparametrization — At γ = ½ this is a formula physics already had", and the text says "The default is dead on two fronts". So the first tool a beginner is sent to opens with: this curve is a known formula and the default setting is dead. I understand they're being honest, but I wasn't sure why I was playing with it. The read-out "10–90% width 1.93 dex" meant nothing to me. The page says the tool shows "three parameters" but I only found two sliders.

**Honest Assessment.** I only read the top. The "Not a physicist?" box, the "If you read nothing else" box and "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" are what I needed, and "As a theory of galaxies: fatal" is admirably blunt. But the no-jargon paragraph comes after the "who did this work" section, a 22-link table of contents and revision notes. The page says it is about 25,000 words and it is step 5 of my 5-step beginner path. I am not reading 25,000 words. Also the breadcrumb says "Beginner Path, Step 5 of 5" and "Methodology Track, Step 6 of 6" at once, which made me wonder which path I was on.

**Glossary.** "New here? Start with these ten" is a great idea, and the Coherence and Dark matter entries are written for me. Then the very first alphabetical entry (a₀) dives into cH₀/(2π). I had come to look up ΔBIC because it was everywhere. The glossary says above 10 is very strong evidence; the Dark Matter page's key says "above ~10 is decisive" and then describes a +11 as "not decisive". I can't tell if +11 is a big deal or not.

**Dark Matter Reframed.** Opens well with "The idea in plain words" and "it failed both ways it was written". The "two stories" framing is interesting, but the page says which story the framework adopts is pending the project lead, so I left not knowing what the site's position on dark matter actually is, other than "our equation didn't work".

Across everything: there are dated correction notes in the middle of the reading text on nearly every page ("Until 2026-10-06 this read…", "Flagged by a simulated-visitor persona…", "Rewritten 2026-10-19 after…"). The first one made me trust the site more. By the twentieth I felt like I was reading the edit history of a document instead of the document.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — loaded, default state readable (DDO 154, Vflat = 47 km/s, Υdisk = 0.50); could not drag the slider through a text fetch — intuitive at the top, confusing below — the "look at three things" intro and the plain statement that the violet curve misses the dots are the clearest thing on the site. Five galaxy buttons, one slider, two checkboxes I didn't understand.
- **Coherence Explorer** — loaded, default state readable (γ = 0.49, C at the reference density 0.3272, rising to 0.8259 at 10× and 0.9785 at 100×); could not drag — half intuitive — the "try this first" instruction is good and the presets have plain labels ("the framework's original guess", "same curve as MOND"), but "dex", the badge line and "dead on two fronts" left me unsure what I was supposed to learn.
- (Looked at the Interactive Tools index: 7 tools, clear ordering advice, each with a level label. Did not open the γ Calculator.)

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the only version of the equation that fits galaxies is MOND written differently, what is left that is Synchronism's own? The landing page says "an untested picture", but I couldn't say in one sentence what that picture predicts.
2. Is 6 the number of failures or not? Why would it be 4 or 8?
3. Who is "the project lead", and what are they deciding?
4. What does the Υdisk slider on the plotter show me when I move it?
5. Is a ΔBIC of +11 strong evidence or not?
6. What happens on 2 December 2026 with "Gaia DR4 wide binaries", in plain words, and what result would be good or bad news for this idea?
7. The work is done by AI agents with one human. Has any working physicist looked at it? (The site says no; will that change?)
8. Why is consciousness in here at all if the formula is, by the site's own word, a placeholder?

### Verdict
The first two minutes are excellent: a clear question, a memorable analogy, and an upfront admission that the idea lost its tests, which made me trust the site more than I expected to. Past that, the site is written for someone checking its bookkeeping, not for someone like me. Counts with ranges, pending internal decisions and correction notes crowd out the story, and the beginner path ends on a 25,000-word page. I would come back in December to see what the Gaia result does, but only if there is a short plain-language page telling me.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /glossary, /key-claims, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /honest-assessment, Test Roadmap (reached at /predictions), Interactive Tools index (reached at /tools), /gamma-calculator, /equation-walkthrough, Research Philosophy (reached at /methodology)). /about returned 404.
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The site defines more of its own vocabulary than most research sites I audit, and its headline counts agree on every page I checked, but its core parameter γ is described two incompatible ways, several pages have a different name in the nav, the breadcrumb and the H1, and the label system (eight kinds of label, nine badges) is heavier than the content it labels.

*Method note: pages were read through a text fetch (HTML converted to markdown), so I could not operate controls, and anything that depends on rendering is marked "(fetch?)". /glossary was read verbatim only for its first ~40% (through the D entries) and /honest-assessment had a ~20,000-character span (TEST-04a, Quantum Arc, start of Bell/CHSH) that I saw only in summary. Three of the URLs I used (/predictions, /tools, /methodology) were guesses, not links from the nav; they served pages whose nav links point elsewhere (/test-catalog, /interactive-tools, /research-philosophy). I did not check whether they redirect.*

### Journey

**Landing page.** I started by inventorying the header: nine items, with useful tooltips ("125 terms defined", "26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)"). The footer repeats them and adds a one-line status ("0 confirmed predictions · 6 refutations executed under the current counting rule (4–8 under rules on file…)") which then appears identically on every page I visited. As a consistency device that is excellent: I never found a page that disagreed with it.

The page has a "Scientific Outcomes" key that defines most abbreviations (ΛCDM, BAO, DESI, CMB, dex, Youden's J and others) and an "Abbreviations ahead" pointer. But the key sits after several of the terms it defines. RAR, BTFR, Ωm and ΔBIC are all used in the "which C" block before the key. Υ ("γ = 0.49 at Υ = 0.5") and M/L are used and never defined on the page. "Gaia DR4" is the site's one forward-looking date and neither word is explained. TEST-09, TEST-10 and TEST-25 appear as bare IDs with no sentence saying what the TEST-nn series is.

Two "Scientific Outcomes" and "Research Activity" labels read as section titles but did not come through as headings, so they may be missing from the document outline (fetch?). The Key Claims teaser contained a link with an empty label followed by a second link inside it, which usually means a link nested inside a clickable card (fetch?, but worth a look because nested anchors are invalid HTML).

The session count is shown three ways within one page: "~3,300 sessions" in prose, "3,308" on a stat tile, and a caution that the count is "self-reported… the archive's own tallies disagree by hundreds". A four-digit figure on a tile next to a statement that it is uncertain by hundreds is a precision mismatch.

**Glossary.** The best-structured page on the site. "How to read labels on this site" states up front that there are "eight kinds of label" and that "only the first two are verdicts on a claim". That sentence answered my main question about badges. But it is also where I realised how much apparatus there is: nine formal badges in two families, plus operational states (seven), lifecycle verbs (eleven or so), descriptors, Tool Kind and Tool Level. I could hold the nine badges in my head. I could not hold the rest, and I am not sure a reader can tell on sight whether "Ceiling Exceeded" or "Artifact Lesson" is a badge, a state or a verb.

The intro lists the deprecated badges as "Validated, Strongly Supported"; the table directly below lists the retired labels as "Validated, Strongly Supported, MOND-shared". Two lists of the same thing, a few lines apart, with different members.

Entries are mostly self-contained, with exceptions: "CRT Analogy" never says cathode-ray tube (Key Claims does), the C(ρ) entry uses γ and ρ_crit without a pointer to their entries, and "PR-box" appears undefined in the CHSH entry. In the "start with these ten" list, three anchors contain spaces and parentheses ("Presence (ρ)", "Refutation (executed)", "σ (statistical)"); I could not test whether they resolve.

**Key Claims.** Acronym discipline here is the best of any page: QM, CRT, MRH, PRL, DD, ENAQT, CHSH, IIT, EEG/fMRI, ΛCDM, MOND, SPARC and AQUAL are all expanded at first use. The misses are the second-tier ones (UDD, GRW/CSL, NMR, DFS, SNARC, BEC/BCS, H₀, g†, Bmax, BTFR), and ALFALFA–SDSS is used in the claim 3 Evidence section and only expanded two sections later.

The page explains its badges inline, which I wanted. It introduces them as "The three badges on this page, in three words each", and then none of the three definitions is three words ("known result, new notation"; "conceptual proposal without quantitative test"; "tested, did not hold").

The badge descriptors for the same claims differ between this page and the landing-page cards. Claim 1 is "Speculative — No Rate Parameter" on the landing card and "untestable as stated without a scan period" here. Claim 2 is "Untestable as Stated" on the landing card and "unfalsifiable as stated" here. Untestable and unfalsifiable are not synonyms, and a reader comparing the two pages will wonder which is meant.

The claim headings came through as "1.Is Quantum Mechanics…" with no space after the number, and one badge descriptor showed a stray trailing underscore, as if an italic marker lost its partner (both fetch?).

**Why Synchronism? and First Encounter.** Both have working prev/next links and a step label ("Beginner Path — Step 1 of 5", "Step 2 of 5"), and the sequence is correct. Why Synchronism has a labelled three-line summary. First Encounter does not, and its Section E has no heading of its own (one sentence, then "Scale Up: Galaxies"), so the promised "7 short sections, lettered A–G" is six headings and an orphan.

This is where I found the terminology problem I would rate highest. γ, the site's central parameter, is described as:
- "how sudden the switch is" (landing)
- "how sharply the dial turns" (First Encounter)
- "the dial that sets how sharp its S-curve is" (Honest Assessment, opening box)
- "a setting… for how early that change starts", and explicitly "It was meant as a sharpness, but it sets how early C rises" (Why Synchronism)
- "γ sets how early the coherence S-curve starts to rise" (γ Calculator)

So step 1 of the Beginner Path tells me the "sharpness" reading is the old one, and step 2 and the landing page then use it. I cannot evaluate which is physically right, but two of these pages are telling me the other three are out of date.

Smaller items: the same quantity is typeset "Ncorr" and "N_corr" within single pages. MOND is expanded on Why Synchronism at first use, but on First Encounter it is "an older rival theory" in Section C and only expanded several sections later. SPARC is never expanded on Why Synchronism. "audited-negative" appears twice in lowercase running text on Why Synchronism with a link to /gamma-regimes, not to the badge definition, while the page's own badge legend lists only "Failed · Reparametrization". On First Encounter a "Reparametrization" label sits on Section D with no explanation or link, on a page for beginners.

**Honest Assessment.** The canonical badge definitions live here and are clear. The summary boxes at the top are good. Structurally:
- The table of contents has labels that do not match the headings they point to ("Density adds nothing beyond gbar" for a heading that begins "Audited-Negative — Form-free: local density adds nothing beyond g_bar"; "Inherited MOND Problems (clusters, CMB, GW speed)" for what is a bold lead-in, not a heading).
- The page says it is "about 25,000 words"; the landing page says "~25,000" in the body and "~18,000" in a revision note; the text I fetched was about 187,000 characters, which is nearer 27,000 words.
- The canonical badge definitions block came through as a plain line, not a heading (fetch?). If so it cannot be reached from a screen reader's heading list, and it is the most-linked anchor on the site.
- BTFR, SPARC, TFR, RAR, RSD, LLR and PPN were not expanded in the parts I read verbatim. MOND is glossed in the opening box but its name is not spelled out until a later H3.
- SPARC sample sizes appear as 175, 166, 153, 149 and 123 galaxies, and 2,807 and 2,622 points, in different sentences. I assume these are different cuts, but only some say so.
- One heading is in capitals with an editing instruction in it: "TEST-03: ALFALFA-SDSS TFR Scatter — REMOVED FROM 'WHAT FAILED' (2026-07-09)", sitting under the H2 "What Failed".

**Test Roadmap.** This page has three names. The nav and H1 say "Test Roadmap", the nav href is /test-catalog, and the breadcrumb reads "Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap" where "Predictions & Experiments" is not a link and is not a section I can find in the nav. The page does define its terms carefully, including a distinction I found useful: "'Registered' means the kill criterion was written down before the test was run; 'pre-registered' means before the data existed." Having learned that, I noticed the Honest Assessment's badge definitions describe a kill criterion as "a pre-registered falsification threshold", and the Roadmap's own status tile says "0 Pre-registered & Completed". If the distinction matters enough to define, the canonical definition should use the right one of the two words.

The arithmetic the page shows adds up (10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24 registry tests; 2 + 2 + 11 + 11 = 26 criteria). The same point count is "2807" here and "2,807" on Honest Assessment. The revision-notes list at the bottom has bullets that begin mid-thought ("explorer, 2026-10-22; until then this sentence said…") where the sentence they refer to is not identifiable.

**Interactive Tools index.** Seven tools, each with Kind, Level, Status and "Badge on its page". Clear, and the only place the tool badges are tabulated. Six of the seven carry the status "Artifact Lesson", which makes the column nearly constant, and the one tool without it (Coherence Explorer) is unexplained. The card "Equation Anatomy" links to /equation-walkthrough.

**γ Calculator.** The H1 states the purpose before I can misuse it: "a γ formula kept to show how it fails". Input and output are explained in plain terms ("Ncorr is the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units"). I checked the defaults by hand: Ncorr = 4 gives γ = 2/√4 = 1.0000 as displayed, and the chart annotation C(ρcrit) = 0.60 matches. The seven-row Quick Reference table is fully populated and the rows I checked agree with the formula. Chart axes are labelled ("coherence C", "density ρ/ρcrit (log scale: 0.01 → 100)"). Problems: the caveats header says "Three caveats before using this tool" while a note refers to a fourth; BCS and BEC are used before the presets block expands them; CLT is never expanded here; and the badge "Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems" has an ⓘ icon whose content I could not see, with no visible definition of Audited-Negative on the page.

**Equation Anatomy.** An eight-step tour; I could read step 1 only. It opens with "Variables in this equation — defined before we start", which is exactly right and is the pattern I would copy to the landing page. It also labels its stepper "Step 1 of 8 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)", which pre-empts the confusion Pass 1 had with double step labels. The numbers quoted in step 1 check (C at ρcrit is 0.88 at γ = 2 and 0.33 at γ ≈ 0.49). CLT is used in a heading and expanded in the following sentence; BCS is not expanded.

**Research Philosophy.** Breadcrumb "Home / Methodology / Research Philosophy", track label "Methodology Track Step 1 of 6", next link to the A2ACW Protocol. A2ACW, MRH, LLM and OOD are all expanded at first use. The tallies match the other pages (3,308 sessions, about 47 survivors, 1.4%, 0 of 9). It contains a "Validation Badge Taxonomy" section, so the badge taxonomy is now stated in at least four places (here, the glossary, Honest Assessment, Test Roadmap). They agreed where I compared them, apart from the deprecated-list mismatch inside the glossary, but four copies is four things to keep in sync.

**Breadcrumbs overall.** Present on every interior page and correct in what they show. They are inconsistent in depth: Glossary, Key Claims, Why Synchronism, First Encounter and Honest Assessment are "Home / Page", tools are "Home / Interactive Tools / Tool", and two pages have a middle crumb that is not a destination ("Predictions & Experiments", "Methodology"). The separator "/" came through as its own list item (fetch?), which a screen reader would read aloud.

### Journey

**Landing → straight to /parameter-derivations.** I skipped the story and went looking for the page where a grand framework usually hides its fits. It opens by saying it is "not a derivations page in the mathematical sense" and that zero parameters have first-principles derivations. That is the correct description of what follows, which makes the page title the only inaccurate thing on it.

I checked each item:
- **tanh.** The page says outright that the Ising m = tanh(βJzm) is a self-consistency equation and C(ρ) has no such loop, so the tanh is chosen. That is exactly the objection I came to make. It also says there is no critical point and no exponents, so no Landau reading. Agreed.
- **The identity.** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1] is right, and at γ = ½ it is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). So the fitted galaxy law is Milgrom's simple μ with a₀ = 2a₀′. The badge says "Reparametrization — exactly MOND's µ_simple at γ = ½". Correct and honest.
- **Limits.** C ≈ γx at small x (so the deep-MOND scale is a₀′/γ, which the coherence page uses: 5.33×10⁻¹¹/0.489 = 1.09×10⁻¹⁰, checks). 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ} at large x. Both right.
- **γ = 2/√N_corr.** The sign argument is sound: Curie–Weiss finite-size rounding goes as N^{−1/2}, so a sharpness should grow as √N, the reciprocal of the ansatz. The Ginzburg numbers on the Crossover tool (Gi ~ (T_c/E_F)⁴ ~ (k_Fξ₀)^{−4} ∝ N_ξ^{−4/3}) are the textbook clean-3D-BCS result. The page labels this "recalled, not re-derived", which is fair.
- **ρ_crit = A·V².** I redid the stated formula A = 4π/(βJ²·G·R₀²) with G = 4.301×10⁻³, R₀ = 8 kpc, βJ = 1 and got 4.6×10⁻⁵ against the used 0.029, a factor ~635. The page says the same, and says its earlier "644× unit conversion" is not a unit conversion. The spot values (652 M☉/pc³ at 150 km/s, 1.52×10³ at 229 km/s, 64 for DDO 154) check. With disc midplane densities of 0.03–0.1 M☉/pc³ every disc sits four orders below the knee, so the density-keyed law is on its linear branch everywhere and cannot make a flat curve. The site says so.
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π.** 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ at H₀ = 67.4, checks; 13% below 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. Labelled dimensional analysis. /mond-unification goes further and shows the literal Hubble-sphere calculation gives cH₀/2 (I get 3.27×10⁻¹⁰, same as the page), so the 2π is not derived by anyone here. The prefactor table (Milgrom, Sanders & McGaugh, Famaey & McGaugh, McCulloch, Verlinde, Smolin) is a fair statement of the prior art.
- **Σ₀ = a₀/2πG.** 119 M☉/pc² at H₀ = 67.4, checks; this is the a₀ row again, as the page says.

Problems on this page are of presentation: R₀ means three different things (a radius V²/3a₀ in item 6; 8 kpc and "0.07 kpc/(km/s)^0.75" in item 3); item 3 says "A = 0.028 was derived (Session 53)" two lines before saying that derivation uses a fitted R₀; and the Σ₀ badge reads "Milgrom's Σ_M Re-derived" over text saying nothing is being derived.

The three-C table (C_ρ, C_g, C_a) is the thing I needed and the landing page should point here sooner: C_a = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)x/(1+x) has no tanh and no γ at all, so two of the six refutations (BTFR slope, dwarf dark-matter fractions) test a function that is not the headline equation. That is stated, but you have to find this table to see it. Three wording problems: the compression step is called "information-theoretically necessary" and, a few lines later, "not uniquely forced"; γ is "a transition sharpness" on a page that also says "no value is sharp" and "sharp but smooth saturation"; and the Padé remark that γx/(1+γx) matches C "only through second order at γ = ½" undersells its own result, since at γ = ½ the two are identical for all x (the page says so elsewhere).

The N_eff bookkeeping uses three scalings at once. +184 → +10.9 is N = 166 (factor 16.9). "4.3× the expectation" is N_eff ≈ 150 (factor 18.7, would give +9.8). +2843 is quoted as +142 "at 20×" and as +168 in the table. **/mond-unification ("Relation to MOND").** The warning box is the best physics sentence on the site: the density-keyed equation ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ "contains no acceleration scale, so it cannot produce MOND." Right: a coefficient that depends on ρ is a position-dependent G, and MOND needs |∇Φ|/a₀ in the coefficient. The page identifies the equation as Refracted Gravity's (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016) and notes momentum conservation needs the striction term from the variational form. I was going to raise both.

Two things here. The symbol ρ_crit is the coherence knee everywhere else and the cosmological critical density 3H₀²/8πG in Step 2 of this page. And the tidal-dwarf card: the isolated deep-MOND value σ = (4GMa₀/81)^{1/4} = 9.4 km/s for 10⁷ M☉ checks, but the card then gives MOND 10.9–40.9 km/s and Synchronism 10.5–14.5 km/s at g_ext = 1.0 a₀. **/galaxy-rotation.** Opening verdict "Does Synchronism replace dark matter? No, not on current evidence." I recomputed the fit statistics: 2807 × ln(0.1485²/0.1437²) = +184; 166 × 0.0657 = +10.9; per-law Υ, 2807 × ln(0.1443²/0.1431²) = +47 (page: +46); free γ at k = 2, ln 2807 − 0.84 = +7.1. 1. "Tight RAR confirmed (σ_int ≈ 0.057 dex) — McGaugh et al. 2016 measured value, restated." I do not think that is McGaugh et al. 2016. That paper reports 0.13 dex observed scatter (0.11 in Lelli et al. 2017) and argues the intrinsic part is small. 0.057 dex is the residual in Li, Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2018, after fitting Υ, distance and inclination per galaxy. It is also not comparable to the page's own 0.1437 dex RMS without saying so. And "confirmed" is an odd word on a label for a literature value.

**/dark-energy.** This was the page I expected to catch out, and could not. Substituting G_eff = G/C into Friedmann and defining ρ_DE ≡ ρ_m(1−C)/C makes C = ρ_m/(ρ_m+ρ_DE) = Ω_m(a) identically, so "C₀ = Ω_m" is a definition and not a result; the page calls the calibration "forced". I checked the limits: γ = ½ gives ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit, exactly Λ; small x gives ρ_DE → ρ_crit/γ; large x gives ρ_DE ∝ ρ_m^{1−2γ}, so w → −2γ, a Cardassian form with n = 1−2γ (Freese & Lewis 2002, cited). γ = 1 gives ρ_DE ∝ 1/(x+2). Ġ/G = 3H₀·d ln C/d ln ρ = 3H₀(1−C) ≈ 1.4×10⁻¹⁰/yr at γ = ½, about 2000× the lunar-laser-ranging bound; the page has the same number. What was done is a substitution into the Friedmann equation that, by the page's own account, "is not a solution of any covariant theory". "Derived" is carrying more than it should in that sentence and in the matching one on /honest-assessment.

A structural observation the site does not quite make in one place: the *same* function at the *same* γ = ½ is exactly Λ when keyed on mean density and exactly MOND-simple when keyed on acceleration. That is not a unification; it is the statement that x/(x+2) is the simplest saturating rational function and both Λ and simple-μ are "add a constant" laws (ρ_m + 2ρ_crit; g_bar + 2a₀′ in the division wiring). I would say that sentence somewhere, because a reader who meets "γ ≈ ½ in both cosmology and galaxies" on /honest-assessment#is-gamma-universal could take the coincidence for evidence.

Across elements that is the observed trend (soft lattices, larger λ: Pb vs Al), but at fixed coupling BCS gives T_c ∝ θ_D (the isotope effect), so "expected sign is negative" needs the qualifier "across materials".

**/cdm-discrimination.** The history is messy but the endpoint is right: the 0.086 dex is the residual of a five-predictor mass model and shuffling V moves it from 0.0864 to 0.0870, so it never was a Tully–Fisher scatter. **What is actually new.** Sorting the six "citable artifacts" by whether I would cite them:

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — loaded; controls not operable through a text fetch — scientifically sound in its current state — observed points are SPARC rows (since 10-26) and the Newtonian baseline is SPARC's own mass model (since 11-10) with Υ_disk = 0.5, Υ_bul = 1.4Υ_disk. The MOND curve is simple-ν (Famaey & Binney 2005) with one global a₀ and no per-galaxy tuning, i.e. a prediction, and the page discloses that its previous MOND curve was circular (BTFR mass with the flat level fixed by construction). The framework's curve is the division wiring with the Ω_m floor; with C_ρ ≲ 10⁻² on every disc it is the Newtonian curve times √(1/0.315) = 1.78 at all radii, which cannot follow a rising-then-flat dwarf. The arithmetic I checked is right (1/0.315 = 3.17; ρ_crit = 64 M☉/pc³ at 47 km/s; a₀ implied by 47·V⁴ is 1.6×10⁻¹⁰, hence the old curve's 0.93). Limits not stated on the page: no EFE, no distance or inclination uncertainty, and φ in the C_a formula is undefined. NGC 2403 / UGC 128 is the classic high/low surface-brightness pair at matched V_flat and is a good choice.
- **Coherence Explorer** — loaded; controls not operable — numbers correct, prose inconsistent — read-outs at the default γ = 0.49 check (0.3272, 0.8259, 0.9785; 10–90% width 1.93 dex; C = ½ at 2.07 ρ_crit), the γ = ½ identity C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) is right, and the peak log-slopes (0.25 at γ = ½, 0.375 at γ = 2) are right. ### Verdict
The site's own accounting is right: there is no prediction here that differs from MOND plus ΛCDM and survives, Gaia DR4 and DESI DR3 cannot change that, and the one refutation of the framework's own content that holds under every counting rule (the boost cap against dwarf dark-matter fractions) needs tighter wording than it has. A2ACW is not a sound way to generate physics and the pages demonstrate this more rigorously than a critic would; the working part of the method is executing registered criteria against public data. What I would take away is the solar-motion Ġ/G argument against ambient-density-keyed gravity, with its smoothing assumption stated. What worries me is that the program's remaining output is increasingly a set of unreviewed assertions about other researchers' code and fits, published with commit hashes but without the authors' knowledge, alongside the words "expert review" for what appears to be AI review. Those two things should be fixed before anything else.
