Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /interactive-tools, /glossary, /key-claims, /honest-assessment top)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've seen. It told me in the first paragraph that its idea failed, and I'm still not sure what I'm supposed to get out of reading the rest.

### Journey

I came from a Reddit link. The browser tab says "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" That's exactly the kind of hook that gets me to click. The line above the headline is "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything", which lowered my guard in a good way.

The first paragraph is very good. "Every system, from an atom to a galaxy, gets one number between 0 (a crowd of strangers) and 1 (a marching band)." I got that immediately. Then, two sentences later: "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." I literally sat back. I've never seen a site do that. A bit further on is "So is it alive or dead? As tested physics, dead so far. As a public record of finding that out, alive." I respect it. My Reddit-brain also asked: okay, then why am I here? The answer seems to be "to watch how science checks an idea". I like that, but I had to work it out myself.

The landing page is *long*. Once I scrolled past the opening I hit a table with "ΔBIC +2,843", "Excluded at its own cap", "TEST-09, TEST-10", "S = 1.85" and "1.98 ≤ 2", and then a big abbreviations block (SPARC, BTFR, RAR, Youden's J…). I skimmed all of it. I appreciated the abbreviation key, but by then I'd stopped reading closely. The "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)" button was easy to find, and I took it.

**/why-synchronism (Beginner Step 1 of 5).** This is good for me. "Physics has a fragmentation problem", then a three-line summary: "0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations." Then "Beginner path: that is all you need from this page", and everything else is folded under "For physicists". That's the right design and I felt respected. I peeked into the fold anyway. It says "Some predictions held up. Others failed." That confused me, because every page footer says "0 confirmed predictions". Which is it? (I checked: that sentence is really on the live page, just above "What We Tested — and How It Went".) I'm guessing it means "some fit the old data", but nothing says so.

**/first-encounter (Step 2, "about 10 minutes", sections A–G).** The dimmer-switch picture for the S-curve worked, and so did "a single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy". Then section C opens with "the formula for γ failed. The γ the framework chose loses to MOND." So I'm being taught a formula and told in the same breath that it's wrong. I didn't know whether to bother learning it. Section B has an "Expert note (physicist terminology)" box about "BEC/superconductors" and "γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ for BCS". It's labelled as expert, so I skipped it, but it sits in the middle of the beginner path. Sections D and G mention YBCO, "spin-orbit coupling" and "Milgrom" with no explanation. The superconductor miss ("607 K predicted for YBCO, 93 K actual") was clear and kind of fun, even though I have no idea what YBCO is.

**/galaxy-plotter (Step 3).** This was the best moment on the site for me. The "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" box finally explained the dark-matter puzzle in a way I *got*. "The dots are what telescopes measure… The dashed gray line is what the visible matter alone would give. It sags below the dots. That gap is the dark-matter puzzle." That's the clearest explanation of dark matter I've read anywhere, YouTube included. Green (MOND) follows the dots, violet (this framework) doesn't. Lesson received.

But the beginner box keeps going. "At the archive's own calibration (the default) violet hugs gray near the centre… 1.78× stretch at the edge… at the old units-hybrid setting it is a 1.78× stretch everywhere." Then: "You can ignore the other lines (an old hand-drawn stand-in and a second violet variant)." If I can ignore them, why are they on my chart? Below the box, the "For physicists" text about "C ρ (γ = 2) in the division wiring g = g bar /C, floored at Ω m" comes *before* the "What this tool is for" paragraph. So the tool's purpose is buried under the physicist spec.

**/interactive-tools.** Clean. The hub labels Coherence Explorer "Best first tool" and Level: Beginner, and that helped. "Kind / Level / State" plus a badge on every card is a lot of labels for a list of seven tools.

**/coherence-explorer.** "What this toy is for… seeing the shape of the S-curve" and "One thing to try first: drag γ from 2 down to 0.5 and watch the S flatten" are exactly the instructions I needed. The readouts at the default (γ = 1) say C at the knee is 0.6000, at 10× the knee 0.9836 and at 100× 0.9998. So the curve really does climb to 1 and stop, which matched the dimmer-switch picture. The preset buttons lost me. "γ = 0.046, the density version's free fit to galaxies… ΔBIC +2843" and "10–90% width: 1.50 dex" mean nothing to me. Nothing tells me what "dex" is.

**/glossary.** "New here? Start with these ten" is a great idea. The tanh entry ("Think of it as a dimmer switch") and the dark-matter entry are both readable. Right after the ten words is a table called "How to read labels on this site: The site uses eight kinds of label." Eight! I closed that part. Then I read the Reparametrization entry. It says "Not a failure — reparametrizations confirm the framework is consistent with known physics… The novelty is in unification." But the Galaxy Plotter had just told me "Why matching MOND still counts as Failed: fitting the same curves as an existing theory with a renamed formula adds nothing new." So is matching known physics good or bad? Two pages gave me opposite feelings about the same word. The Coherence entry also warns that "the letter C names at least four different functions on this site". I appreciate the honesty, but that one sentence made me feel I'd never really understand this.

**/key-claims.** The landing page told me to "Read the list bottom-up: the most exciting-sounding claims… are the least tested". Key Claims itself says "The first claim is the foundational one — the others follow from it", and doesn't mention reading bottom-up. Not a big deal, but the two reading orders pull against each other. The "In plain words: the three claims, one sentence each" block is perfect, and the badge key comes before the badges, which is good. Then "The rest of this page is written for readers with some physics", and I stopped.

**/honest-assessment (top only).** "As a theory of galaxies: fatal, by its own scoreboard." The "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" box mostly lives up to its title, and "about 4 minutes, stop at the green box" is a thoughtful escape hatch. The table of contents has 22 sections, and the page calls itself "about 30,000 words". It's Step 5 of the *Beginner* path. I didn't go further.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked (page served; five SPARC galaxy buttons DDO 154 / NGC 2403 / UGC 128 / NGC 3198 / NGC 7331, Υ_disk slider at 0.50, calibration switch). The beginner box is intuitive and the extra lines and physicist text are confusing. This is where I finally *understood* the dark-matter puzzle: gray line sags below the dots. (Note: I browse by fetch, so I read the server-rendered state and couldn't actually drag the slider.)
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked. The intro and the "One thing to try first" instruction are intuitive; the presets and the "dex" readout are confusing. Default readouts are C(ρ_crit) = 0.6000, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.9836, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.9998, consistent with "rises then levels off at 1". The page says it opens at γ = 1 since 2026-11-16 and that this is a display value, not a fitted one. Good to know it isn't pretending.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the idea failed, what am I supposed to take away? "How science checks itself" is the implied answer, but nobody says it to me plainly as the reason to keep reading.
2. "Some predictions held up" vs "0 confirmed predictions": which one is true?
3. Is matching MOND a good thing (the glossary says it shows consistency) or a bad thing (the plotter calls it Failed)?
4. What would it take for this idea to come back to life? The landing page says Gaia DR4 on 2 Dec 2026 "can remove options but cannot confirm anything new". So is there *any* result that could make it a winner?
5. Is MOND right, then? The plotter says green fits these galaxies but "fails a Solar-System test", so it sounds like nobody has the answer. That's actually interesting, and I'd click a page that explained just that.

### Verdict
As a casual visitor I've never seen a science site this upfront about its own failure, and the Galaxy Plotter's three-things box gave me the clearest picture of dark matter I've had. The beginner on-ramp (Why Synchronism → First Encounter → Plotter) is well built, but expert material keeps leaking into it. Two pages also give me opposite verdicts on "matching known physics". I'd come back for the plotter and the honesty, and I'd stop reading at every "For physicists" fold.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /interactive-tools, /learning-paths, /research-philosophy, /glossary, /honest-assessment (badge section + TOC), /for-researchers, /test-catalog, /tier-1-existing, /mrh, /gamma-calculator, /crossover-regime-visualizer), plus one lookup on /coherence-explorer to cross-check a preset
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structurally); partial (on which label system applies where)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an auditor. As a reader, maybe.
- **Overall impression**: Unusually self-documenting. Almost every rename, retirement and miscount carries a dated revision note. The cost is that the site now has more label systems than a reader can hold, and a few renames are only half-propagated.

### Journey

**Landing.** The top nav is consistent on every page I opened: Start Here / Learning Paths / Tier 1: Existing Data / Test Roadmap / Interactive Tools / Glossary / Research Philosophy / For Researchers / Honest Assessment. The landing page has no breadcrumb, which is fine for a home page. Acronym hygiene above the fold is good. MOND is expanded at first use ("Modified Newtonian Dynamics — a 40-year-old idea"), "MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)" is spelled out, SPARC is glossed as "a catalogue of 175 measured rotation curves", and DR4 is glossed in the same paragraph. Weak spots: γ, ρ_crit and N_corr appear in prose ("the formula γ = 2/√Ncorr") *before* the equation block that explains them. "Bell's limit (S = 1.85…" is used with no gloss; CHSH is only defined in the abbreviation key further down. The status table uses seven status words ("Excluded", "Is MOND", "Is Λ", "Survives; does not discriminate", "Inside Bell's limit", "No number to test", "Excluded at its own cap"). The page links to their meaning *before* the table, which is correct practice, but the meanings live on another page.

**Learning Paths.** This is solid information architecture. The landing button "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min…)" matches Beginner step 1 exactly, and the Beginner step times add up to the stated ~37 min (8+10+6+9+4). Step 5 deep-links to `/honest-assessment#if-you-read-nothing-else`, which answers Pass 1's worry about the 30,000-word page. Weak spot: step and blurb titles use undefined jargon with no glossary links: "Ising/Hill-function", "CDM Discrimination", "A2ACW Protocol", "Superconductivity (η)". The Chemistry blurb says "The γ ≈ 1 boundary → correlations → limitations", but step 3 of the same path is titled "The γ ≈ 1 Band (boundary reading retracted)". The blurb still uses the retracted word.

**The badge system (Research Philosophy → Glossary → Honest Assessment).** This is the heart of my audit. The good news: all three pages agree on the **nine** live status badges (5 MRH-relationship: Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative; 4 descriptive: Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed). Research Philosophy no longer carries its own definitions. Each chip says "see canonical legend" and points to Honest Assessment. That's the right single-source move, and the page's own note says why ("this page was a second copy, and it had already drifted"). The rough edges:

1. **"Validation badge" → "status badge" rename is half-done.** Research Philosophy and Honest Assessment both announce "called 'validation badge' until 2026-11-16". But the Interactive Tools hub still says "the site's validation badge taxonomy" and "where a tool's own page carries a validation badge" (3 occurrences). The Glossary's label-map table still names its first two rows "Validation badge: MRH-relationship" / "Validation badge: descriptive". Honest Assessment itself keeps two later "not a validation badge" phrasings, and the landing still links to `#validation-badge-definitions`. The anchor id is fine to keep, but the visible prose is two names for one thing.
2. **Retired set: two or three?** Research Philosophy says "two families, plus two deprecated labels kept for old pages". The Glossary and Honest Assessment list *three* retired labels ("Validated, Strongly Supported, MOND-shared"). Research Philosophy itself files "MOND-shared" under **operational states** ("Terms like 'Kill Criterion Triggered,' 'Withdrawn,' and 'MOND-shared' are operational states"), while marking it "(RETIRED 2026-07-15)" in the same paragraph. So one term sits in two categories on one page and in a third on another.
3. **"Post-diction"** is a "sub-status" on Research Philosophy and a "Descriptor / qualifier" in the Glossary's label map.
4. **A tenth badge string in the wild.** Tier 1's TEST-04a card reads badge "Untested at the registered power". That isn't one of the nine; it's Untested plus a qualifier. The State slot beside it ("Underpowered as registered") already says the same thing.
5. **The Glossary's "eight kinds of label"** (badge-MRH, badge-descriptive, retired, operational state with 7 members, lifecycle verb with 11 members, descriptor, tool Kind, tool Level) is an honest map. It is also evidence that the label system has outgrown a reader's working memory: about 40 controlled terms across 8 systems. "Underpowered as registered" is a *card State* on Tier 1 but a *lifecycle verb* in the Glossary map.

**Naming collision: "MRH".** The MRH page says MRH is "a term proposed by Dennis Palatov, inspired by Markov blankets". Its own "Open Gap" section concedes "The 'Markov' label sets an expectation that is not currently met" and floats "rename to Relevance Horizon". Meanwhile the *badge family* is called "MRH-relationship", and the Glossary has to warn: "These badges say nothing about the Markov Relevancy Horizon as a physics concept… The borrowing is a metaphor." When a glossary needs a disclaimer that a label doesn't mean its name, the label is the defect. A reader who sees "Active-MRH" on a claim will reasonably read it as physics.

**Breadcrumbs.** They are present and working on every non-home page I opened, but the middle crumbs are section names that no page uses as its title:
- /for-researchers → "Home / **Methodology** / For Researchers: What's Citable". "Methodology" links to /research-philosophy, whose nav label is "Research Philosophy".
- /tier-1-existing → "Home / **Predictions & Experiments** / Tier 1: Existing Data". "Predictions & Experiments" links to /test-catalog, whose nav label and H1 are "Test Roadmap".
- /mrh → "Home / Core Theory / MRH…".
So clicking a breadcrumb lands you on a page with a different name than the crumb. Also, "For Researchers" is a top-level nav item, but its breadcrumb files it under Methodology.

**Test identifiers (Test Roadmap / Tier 1 / For Researchers).** The "How the three test pages relate" line at the top of both Test Roadmap and Tier 1 is excellent: "the Test Roadmap is the full 26-test registry; Tier 1 is its zero-cost slice with verdict cards; Decisive Tests is the May 2026 ranked shortlist, now empty." I wish every multi-page cluster had one. The ID scheme is TEST-01…26 plus suffixes (TEST-04a, TEST-03s), plus a second scheme for bets, B1–B7, which the Roadmap explicitly heads "a second ID scheme". The friction is that one test carries several names:
- The RAR-shape test is "the unnumbered RAR-shape test", "the γ=2 pin", "row #3 of the refutation ledger" (Tier 1), "B2 RAR transition shape at γ = 2" (Roadmap) and "γ = 2 pin (ledger row #3)" (For Researchers).
- Bell/CHSH is "B1" on the Roadmap and "unnumbered; not a Tier 1 test" on Tier 1.
- Tier 1 lists "13 cards… TEST-07 sits here but is not a Tier 1 test by its own heading", while the Roadmap counts TEST-01–10 as the Tier 1 ten.
These are all disclosed, but a reader cross-referencing needs a name ↔ ID concordance table.

**A stale number on /mrh.** /mrh says "TEST-10 needs B ≥ 13.7 and no cosmic ratio supplies it". Tier 1, Honest Assessment and For Researchers all say the opposite framing explicitly. For example: "The 13.7 is a sample maximum, not a bound… B_max ≈ 7.5 at 3σ… ≈ 5.7 after a trials correction" (Tier 1), and "the often-quoted 13.7 is a sample maximum" (For Researchers). I can't judge the physics, but as an editor I can see that /mrh uses 13.7 the way the other three pages say it must not be used. (Verified with curl: "13.7" appears 0 times on /test-catalog, and 6, 7 and 1 times on Tier 1, HA and FR, each with the "not a bound" qualifier.)

**/mrh cross-link.** The phrase "local-density no-go" links to bare `/for-researchers`, a long page, although that page defines `id="locality-no-go"`. That's a missed deep link.

**For Researchers.** It opens with the strongest first sentence on the site for its audience: "No dataset on the horizon can confirm a novel prediction of this framework." But the first screen uses MOND, ΛCDM, ΔBIC, BIG-SPARC, RC100, DESI DR3, Υ and CHSH ("§CHSH", "S = 2") before its own Acronyms block. CHSH, Υ, RC100 and DESI DR3 never get expanded on the page. "Bucket 0" is handled well: it's expanded inline ("the confirmed-novel-predictions count, 0").

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (/gamma-calculator): worked. Clear framing, with the title stating the point up front ("a γ formula kept to show how it fails"). Input N_corr defaults to 4 → readout "γ = 1.0000", regime "Crossover band (γ ≈ 1; the 'boundary' reading is retracted)", marker "C(ρcrit) = 0.60". These are consistent with the text, and the quick-reference table checks out arithmetically (2/√17 ≈ 0.485). Labelling gaps: ρ and ρ_crit appear on the axis with no definition on the page; "dex" is used once, undefined; "Ncorr" and "N_corr" alternate. The Linear (1–1000) range toggle can't show the BEC (10⁶) and BCS (10⁷) presets, so a first-timer who clicks "BCS superconductor" as the "Try it" paragraph instructs must also know to switch to Log. Badges: "Claim status: Audited-Negative", "Tool type: Model Explainer · Artifact Lesson". Clean and consistent with the hub's Kind/State split.
- **Crossover Regime Visualizer** (/crossover-regime-visualizer): worked. One γ slider (0–4, default 1.0) and three named bands ("High-N_corr" < 0.6, "Crossover band" 0.6–1.4, "Single-particle" > 1.4) with band-edge labels N≈11.1 and N≈2.04. Each has a "Reality check" line, which is a good teaching device for a refuted map. The rename note ("Formerly 'Phase Boundary Visualizer'… renamed 2026-10-23") is exemplary change-logging. Gaps: N_corr is never defined on this page (the calculator defines it); Υ appears ("Υ frozen", "Υ free") undefined; ρ_crit is defined only by pointer ("see Equation Anatomy"). It places enzymes at N_corr ≈ 5.5 while the calculator uses 30. Both pages acknowledge the different estimates, but two tools in the same Artifact-Lesson pair disagreeing on a preset is confusing.

4. *dSphs exclude saturating boosts up to B_max ≈ 14.7.* I recomputed Draco (σ = 9.1 km/s, R_e = 221 pc, L_V ≈ 2.8×10⁵ L☉). Walker+2009's 2.5σ²R_e/G gives M/L ≈ 76 within R_e, so the escape M/L for B = 6.39 is ≈ 12, which matches the page's "8–12". Wolf+2010's estimator (4σ²R_e/G) gives M/L ≈ 120 and an escape M/L ≈ 19, so the page's choice is the *conservative* one. The headline "boosts of about 100 and 60" is quoted with no M/L or estimator attached, and the page should name both. Novelty is low: this is the long-known dSph baryon deficit (dynamical M/L ~10², far above any cosmic ratio) restated as a cap bound. It's useful as a one-line citation, not as a result.

**A2ACW (/a2acw).** The page doesn't oversell. It credits Irving–Christiano–Amodei debate, CAMEL and MetaGPT as prior art and calls the protocol "assembled prior art". It lands on the right thesis: oracle-free self-play converged on internal consistency, and every world-facing elimination came from an external oracle (5 executed, 6 read from published measurements, 2 unclear). It also concedes the thesis is close to definitional if recall from training counts as an oracle. I checked its statistics: 0/9 → Clopper–Pearson upper 0.336 ✓, 3/3 lower 0.292 ✓, 0/6 upper 0.459 ✓, J ∈ [−0.71, +0.46] ✓. I found two things more interesting than the page makes them:
- *The reinvention dataset.* Four documented cases where same-corpus adversarial pairs rederived published prior art (Milgrom's simple μ / Famaey & Binney 2005, Cardassian / Freese & Lewis 2002, Refracted Gravity / Matsakos & Diaferio 2016, Collins+2004 LIV) and **never cited it in 3,308 sessions**. Every match came from a lane with literature retrieval. That is a genuine AI-for-science datum, and it argues for a retrieval role more strongly than any of the health metrics do.
- *The missing ablation.* There is no comparison arm: a single agent with the same code execution and retrieval, no adversarial roles, same budget. Without it, "A2ACW is not a generator of physics" can't be separated from "LLM sessions without retrieval aren't". The page proposes a post-cutoff arm and a correction-trail coding. An ablation is cheaper than both, and the retrieval-enabled site tracks already supply half of it.
- (The page's own finding that CCH = 0.25·AFR + … can't reach "Healthy" from inside its own target ranges (max 0.49) is a nice catch and should stay.)
