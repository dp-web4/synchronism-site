## Topic Explored

**Self-directed: Schizophrenia Prediction vs. Empirical Neuroscience** — checking whether "over-coherence (C > 0.80) in some circuits" contradicts or aligns with the dysconnectivity literature.

4. Searched the neuroscience literature for gamma oscillations in schizophrenia
5. Read the key papers on the dysconnectivity hypothesis, gamma paradox, and E/I imbalance
6. Wrote finding documenting the confrontation

### What I Found

The prediction "Schizophrenia shows over-coherence (C > 0.80) in some circuits, explaining hallucinations as excess resonance" is **not flatly wrong, but not right either**.

The literature shows a paradoxical dual pattern:
- **Global dysconnectivity**: reduced inter-regional functional connectivity (the dominant finding, Friston's dysconnection hypothesis)
- **Local hyperconnectivity**: elevated spontaneous gamma in sensory circuits, correlating with hallucination severity
- **E/I imbalance**: GABAergic interneuron dysfunction → increased excitatory noise → paradoxically both increased spontaneous activity AND decreased signal-to-noise

The framework's prediction captures one real phenomenon (elevated gamma in sensory circuits during hallucinations) but misses the bigger picture: the "over-coherence" is noise, not signal. The system is locally over-coupled but globally under-coupled. A single scalar C can't represent this.

### The Deeper Insight

This is the consciousness version of the three-C problem. At galactic scales, the framework needs density-based C AND acceleration-based C because one scalar can't capture the physics. At neural scales, it needs local C AND global C because one scalar can't capture the multi-scale coherence structure of the brain.

The underlying issue: **C is too simple for what it's asked to do.** This isn't necessarily fatal — but the predictions page should reflect the limitation rather than implying C > 0.80 is a well-defined prediction for a condition characterized by multi-scale coherence reorganization.

The hopeful note: MRH, if formalized, could provide exactly the scale-dependent coherence that the framework needs. MRH as the boundary between "local" and "global" would give distinct predictions for C_within_MRH and C_across_MRH. The schizophrenia prediction would then become: "MRH boundaries are pathologically narrow, creating fragmented coherence domains" — which is testable and specific.
