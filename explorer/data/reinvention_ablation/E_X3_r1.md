**Assessment: not correct as a physical theory in its current form. It is a phenomenological fit with serious structural and empirical problems.**

**What checks out**
- The numbers are consistent: 10⁻⁶ × 2π/H₀ ≈ 9×10⁴ yr.
- For typical wide binaries (a ~ 10⁴ AU, P ~ 10⁶ yr), P/τ ~ 10 and the acceleration boost is 1+κ ln(1+P/τ) ≈ 1.7. That gives a velocity boost of ~1.3, the same ballpark as the contested claims of ~1.2–1.4 in wide-binary gravity tests.

**Main weaknesses**

1. **Ill-defined concept.** Inertial mass is a local property, but P is a global, historical property of a two-body orbit. The construction is undefined for unbound, flyby, hierarchical-triple, or perturbed systems, and for single particles. There is no action, Lagrangian, or conservation law, so energy and momentum conservation and Lorentz covariance are unaddressed.

2. **Solar-system constraints are likely violated.** For P ≪ τ, ln(1+P/τ) ≈ P/τ, so the effective GM scales as 1+κP/τ. For Neptune (P = 165 yr) the effect is ~6×10⁻⁴, versus ~10⁻⁶ for Mercury. This breaks Kepler's third law at a level that planetary ephemerides, which are sensitive to roughly 10⁻⁸ or better, should exclude. It also implies a period-dependent equivalence-principle violation, which is a problem for lunar laser ranging. Long-period comets (P ~ 10⁶ yr) would be strongly affected.

3. **Wrong functional dependence.** Observed anomalies, if real, track acceleration, as in MOND. At fixed separation, P depends on the total mass. The boost is therefore not a function of acceleration, so the radial acceleration relation and the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation (v⁴ ∝ M) are not reproduced.

4. **No saturation.** The logarithm grows without bound. For galaxies, P ~ 10⁸–10¹⁰ yr gives boosts of ~3–5 that rise with radius, rather than flat rotation curves. Cluster and CMB phenomenology are not addressed.

5. **Fine-tuning.** The 10⁻⁶ factor and κ = 0.31 are unmotivated. Tying τ to H₀ implies time dependence of τ and local physics, and no mechanism is offered.

6. **Empirical status.** The wide-binary anomaly itself is disputed, with independent analyses (e.g. Banik et al.) finding Newtonian behavior. Fitting κ to one contested dataset is premature.

**What the authors should do next**
- Write down a covariant action or effective field theory in which an effective inertia depends on a local, well-defined quantity (e.g. acceleration or a field gradient) rather than P.
- Compute the planetary ephemeris, lunar laser ranging, and Oort-cloud and long-period-comet constraints explicitly. These are quick and likely decisive.
- Show how the model handles eccentric, perturbed, and hierarchical systems, and demonstrate that gravitational and inertial mass can differ without breaking conservation laws.
- Test it against rotation curves, the radial acceleration relation, and lensing, or state clearly that it applies only to wide binaries.
- Fit κ and τ jointly on the wide-binary data with proper treatment of hidden companions, projection effects, and selection, and report the likelihood against Newtonian and MOND alternatives.

Until the solar-system tests are passed and a local formulation exists, the proposal should be regarded as falsified or at best incomplete.
