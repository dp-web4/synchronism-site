## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I understood the one big idea in about five minutes and I believe the site when it says the idea failed — but I never found out why I, a person who watches physics videos for fun, should keep reading after that.

### Journey

Clicked through from Reddit. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is exactly the kind of thing that makes me click. Good hook. Then about two inches lower the same page says **"0 confirmed predictions; 4 refutations executed"** and has a button that says **"See What Failed."**

I sat there for a second genuinely unsure what kind of site I was on. Is this someone's theory? Is it a debunking *of* someone's theory? Is it a lab notebook? The hook and the scorecard are fighting each other, and nothing on the landing page tells me which one to read first or how to hold both at once. I get that the honesty is the point — I've watched enough Veritasium to find "here's how I was wrong" genuinely appealing — but the site never *says* that's the offer. It just puts the boast and the confession side by side and lets me sort it out.

The landing page offered me a "No physics background" path, so I took it. Step 1 is **Why Synchronism?** and within the first real section I got hit with:

> C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

Three symbols I don't know, one function I've never heard of, and a "for physicists" aside mentioning a *Hamiltonian*. This is step one of the path labeled *"No physics background needed. Plain language, core concepts only."* I bounced.

Then — because I was already annoyed — I clicked step 2, **First Encounter**, and it turned out to be *the good page*. A marching band versus a crowd in a plaza. Atoms locked in a crystal versus thin interstellar gas. A neutron star core described as 10¹⁴× denser than lead. No equations at all. Presence explained as just "how much stuff is in a space." I understood the whole idea from this one page. **The beginner path has its two first steps in the wrong order** — the equation page is first and the plain-English page is second.

**The Core Idea** was also better than I feared. "Think of it as a dimmer switch from sparse/independent to dense/collective" and "tanh is just an S-shaped curve — flat, then steep, then flat again" actually did the job; I can now picture what the equation *does*. But this is also where I got my first real dose of confusion, because the page walks through the equation's three ingredients and tells me:

- γ = 2 (the galaxy value) — **refuted**
- ρcrit = A × V² — **refuted, "and had been sitting here uncaveated"**
- the constant A — **"separately unanchored"**

So: one equation, three knobs, and every single knob is either refuted or admitted to be a number nobody can justify. I don't have the physics to judge whether that's fatal, but as a reader it's a very strange feeling to be taught an equation and told in the same breath that none of its numbers survived. I'd rather the page just *said* that out loud at the top — "here is the idea; here is why the specific numbers we tried didn't work" — instead of letting me discover it knob by knob.

**What Synchronism Is Not** was the one place I felt slightly sold-to rather than leveled-with. Most of it is refreshingly blunt ("Not: Proven", "Not: Journal-reviewed science"). But the last item says it isn't just philosophy because it makes "specific, testable predictions about consciousness and neural activity." That is, for me, the single most exciting sentence on the entire website — and then the Tools page labels the consciousness demo **"(speculative)"** and the Honest Assessment never mentions consciousness in its list of executed tests at all. Two pages left me with opposite impressions of the most interesting claim here.

Also, the numbers don't line up in a way I can follow. That page says **"59% of predictions are untested."** The Honest Assessment says **"0 confirmed predictions, 4 refutations."** Those are percentages in one place and counts in another and I can't get from one to the other. 59% of *how many*? What are the other 41%?

**Honest Assessment** itself I actually liked, and it has one sentence that finally made everything click for me:

> "The equation fits galaxies only as well as MOND, an existing 40-year-old idea, and never better."

That I understand completely. It's a clean, honest statement of the situation and it's the best sentence on the site for someone like me. The rest of the page is heavier going — fσ₈, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC — but by then I trusted the page enough to skim the technical bits and take the summary.

I finished the path at the **Glossary**, which is genuinely excellent. It defines *every single thing* that had confused me — tanh, MOND, ΛCDM, sigma, reparametrization, MRH, all of it, in plain language ("a result is '2.4σ away' from prediction, roughly 1-in-60 chance" is a great definition). The problem is that it's **step 6 of 6**, and all the jargon that made me bounce arrived in steps 1 through 5. I needed this page first, or I needed those terms to be clickable where they appear.

Last, I went looking for the "so why do you keep doing this?" page, because at that point I wanted to root for these people. **Research Philosophy** did not give it to me. The thing it offers as the surviving value is *"load-bearing as an applied design ontology (Web4, SAGE, hestia, the fleet)"* — five proper nouns, none of which mean anything to me, and no explanation of what any of them are. I closed the tab thinking the obvious thought the page never answers: *if it reproduces physics we already have, why wouldn't I just use the physics we already have?*

### Tools Tested

Two problems: it opens at the refuted γ = 2.00 (which is also the max), and nothing has units, so I never knew what density I was looking at. - **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — worked — confusing for my level — Six steps, Previous/Next buttons, fine mechanically. - **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — intuitive concept, cluttered chart — The best explanatory line I found anywhere on the site is here: things far from the center should orbit slower, they don't, so something invisible is adding gravity. Also, credit where due: the tool exists to show that the site's *own* equation fails to fill the gap, which is a wild and admirable thing to build.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If every parameter in the one equation is refuted or unanchored, what exactly is left of the idea? Is the *shape* of the curve still the claim, just with different numbers?
2. Why should I use this instead of MOND, if the site says it never does better than MOND?
3. What are Web4, SAGE, hestia, and "the fleet"? They're offered as the reason this work still matters and never explained.
4. Does Synchronism actually predict something about consciousness or not? Three pages gave me three different answers.
5. "59% untested" — out of how many, and what would testing the rest even look like?
6. What density is `ρcrit = 1.00` in the explorer? Is that near water, near air, near a galaxy?

### Verdict

The honesty is the most compelling thing here and it's also what makes the site hard to use: I was told the idea failed before I was told what the idea was. Fix the beginner path ordering — the marching-band page first, the equation second — and give me one plain paragraph on why a documented failure is worth my attention, and this becomes a genuinely great read for someone like me. Right now the two best sentences on the site (the dark matter explanation in the galaxy plotter, and "fits galaxies only as well as MOND, and never better") are buried on pages a casual reader reaches fifth or not at all.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (plus URL-level probes on ~15 more)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The terminology system on this site is better documented than most commercial products I audit — the badge contract on `/honest-assessment` is genuinely exemplary — but the navigation chrome underneath it has real defects: a breadcrumb whose parent link returns you to the page you're standing on, two different breadcrumb behaviors in two sections, and the badge component reused at two different scopes with identical styling.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs site: nav labels against destination H1s, breadcrumbs against actual URL structure, terminology against a single source of truth, and status vocabulary against a legend. I verified findings against the served HTML rather than trusting rendered summaries, because several things I *expected* to be broken turned out to be correctly built — I've listed those separately below so nobody re-fixes them.

**Entry and top-level nav.** Eight nav items. Three of the eight have a label that doesn't match either the URL or the destination H1:

| Nav label | URL | Destination H1 |
|---|---|---|
| Start Here | `/why-synchronism` | "Start Here: Why Synchronism?" |
| Test Roadmap | `/test-catalog` | "Test Roadmap" |
| Tools | `/interactive-tools` | "Interactive Tools" |

None of these is *broken*, and I found redirect aliases handling the obvious guesses (`/start-here` → 307 → `/why-synchronism`, `/tools` → 307 → `/interactive-tools`, `/test-roadmap` and `/predictions` → 307 → `/test-catalog`). Somebody thought about this. But there are no `<link rel="canonical">` tags on any page I checked, and `/sitemap.xml` is a 404. For a 74-page reference site with a 72-term glossary and multiple URL aliases per page, that's the piece that's missing — not the redirects.

**Breadcrumbs — this is the real finding.** The breadcrumb component renders two different ways depending on which section you're in, and one of them is circular.

On `/why-synchronism`, the served markup is:

> `Home / <a href="/getting-started">Getting Started</a> / Why Synchronism?`

`/getting-started` returns 307 → `/why-synchronism`. **The parent crumb takes you to the page you are already on.** The same `Getting Started` link appears on `/glossary`, `/first-encounter`, `/key-claims`, and `/honest-assessment`, where it isn't circular but is still wrong — it deposits you on beginner-path step 1, which is a *sibling* of those pages, not a parent. There is no "Getting Started" section index page anywhere on the site; the crumb names a section that has no landing page.

Meanwhile, in the other section, the same component renders the parent as a plain `<span>` with no link at all:

> `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1: Existing Data`

`/predictions-experiments` is a 404, which is presumably why it isn't a link. So the site has one section whose parent crumb is a link to the wrong place, and another whose parent crumb is dead text. Same component, two behaviors, neither correct. A reader learns within two pages that the middle crumb can't be trusted, which is the same as not having breadcrumbs.

**Label pointing at two destinations.** On the landing page, "Beginner Path →" links to `/why-synchronism` (step 1 of the path). On `/why-synchronism` itself, the "Beginner" link goes to `/learning-paths` (the path index). Two links, near-identical labels, different targets — one starts the path, one describes it. Pick one convention.

**Learning paths.** Six paths — three by difficulty (Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced), four by topic (Physics/Chemistry/Philosophy/Methodology) — across roughly 35 distinct pages. Twelve of those pages sit in two paths at once. I went in expecting the classic failure: a hard-coded step indicator that ejects a Philosophy-track reader into the Beginner path on their first click. **It doesn't.** Every shared page renders both indicators with independent prev/next chains:

```
/core-idea           Beginner Path Step 3 of 6  |  Philosophy Track Step 1 of 7
/coherence-function  Intermediate Step 1 of 8   |  Physics Track Step 1 of 7
/honest-assessment   Beginner Path Step 5 of 6  |  Methodology Track Step 6 of 6
```

That's correct multi-path handling and it's not common. Credit where due. The residual issue is small: on a two-path page the reader has to remember which path they entered from, because nothing carries that state. Low severity, and probably not worth fixing on a static site.

**Terminology and the badge system.** This is the strongest part of the site and I want to be precise about why, because it means the badge findings that *do* remain are narrow ones.

`/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` carries an explicit, dated contract: every badge has a formal status word drawn from a closed vocabulary, plus free-text after the dash that is a finding descriptor and *not* a badge type. It defines eleven status words across three families (MRH-relationship, descriptive, deprecated), then separately defines four **operational states** that are explicitly "not badges" — Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (RETIRED, with the audit reasoning), Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie. It even pre-empts a cross-page collision by noting that "Model Explainer" on the Tools page is a content grouping with no verdict content. I went looking for the usual defect — the "canonical reference" pointer aiming at a less complete list than the glossary — and it isn't there; both are complete and they agree. The glossary independently defines sixteen badge entries with worked examples. Badges also carry `title=` tooltips in the markup (`title="Untested: Prediction exists, no data yet"`), so a definition is one hover away at point of use.

Given that, two real problems remain:

**(1) The badge component is used at two different scopes with identical styling.** `/tier-1-existing` carries a page-level badge reading *"Untested — 10 Tests, $0 Cost — see overlap notes"* directly under its H1. Five of the twelve tests on that page are FAILED with kill criteria fired. `/test-catalog` likewise carries *"Untested — 24 Proposed Experiments."* A reader who has learned the contract — "Untested: prediction exists, no data yet" — reads that page header as an assertion that nothing here has been run. The badge is functioning as a section summary, but it's visually indistinguishable from the claim-status badges the contract describes. Nothing in the contract covers page-scope badges.

**(2) The per-test statuses on `/tier-1-existing` aren't badges, and the page's legend doesn't decode them.** The twelve test cards carry ALL-CAPS prose in the card title — "FAILED, Kill Criterion Fired", "Never Run As Registered", "Demoted to Tier-2", "Reclassified", "Underpowered As Registered", "Disfavored on σ₈, Underpowered". I checked the markup: these are heading text, with no badge component. Below them sits a section headed "Status badge definitions" — a legend for a vocabulary the cards above don't use. Some of the card words are defined elsewhere (Self-Eliminating-or-Tie and Withdrawn are in the operational-states list); "Reclassified" and "Underpowered As Registered" I could not find defined anywhere. This is the one place where the site's otherwise-disciplined vocabulary leaks.

**`/interactive-tools`** contradicts itself in the space of two paragraphs. The intro: *"Each card shows an epistemic status badge so you know what you're looking at before you click in."* The legend immediately below: *"this is a **content grouping**, not the site's validation badge taxonomy."* The legend is the correct statement; the intro should not use the word "badge." Same legend, one sentence later: *"Level tags use one scale — Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced — plus Artifact Lesson for tools kept deliberately to demonstrate a documented failure."* Artifact Lesson is not a difficulty level; it's an epistemic category spliced into a sentence about the difficulty scale. Two distinct axes are being described as one scale.

**Acronyms at point of use.** The glossary defines SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΛCDM, ΔBIC — all of them, well. None is expanded or linked where it first appears. The landing page alone uses SPARC, ALFALFA-SDSS, BTFR, RAR, ΛCDM and ΔBIC in body prose with no expansion; `/phase-boundary-visualizer` uses ΔBIC; `/gamma-calculator` uses CLT, SPARC, MOND, BTFR and RAR unexpanded. `/first-encounter` links the *word* "Glossary" but not a single term in its own body text. The badge tooltips prove the site already has a point-of-use definition mechanism — it just isn't wired to body-text jargon.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — well structured, one scale trap — This is the best-documented tool on the site. It opens with a one-sentence definition of γ before any interaction ("the steepness of the coherence S-curve"), states what the tool is for, and gives a concrete two-click instruction ("click 'Ideal gas' then 'BCS superconductor' and watch"). That is the correct order for tool documentation and most products get it wrong. Both axes are labeled with units where units exist (`density ρ/ρcrit (log scale: 0.01 → 100)`, `coherence C`), and the input is explicitly dimensionless. BCS and BEC are expanded inline; CLT, SPARC, MOND, BTFR and RAR are not. The tool carries "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems" under the H1 and then explains the inversion in Caveat 2 — badge and explanation co-located, exactly right. One structural gripe: the input offers Linear (1–1000) and Log (1–10⁷) scales, but the presets span ideal gas to BCS superconductor, which is a range only the log scale can hold — a reader on the default linear scale can click a preset and have the input jump outside the visible range. Worth a note or an auto-switch.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — clear intent, thin labeling — The framing instruction is unusually honest and unusually good: *"your task is to catch the formula being wrong at every stop, not to learn its placements."* Telling the reader what job to do before they touch the control is textbook. But this tool is thinner than the γ Calculator on the things I check: no formal status badge under the H1 (the audited-negative status is carried in prose only, so it doesn't match the pattern the rest of the site establishes), and ΔBIC appears in the regime commentary with no expansion and no link. The γ axis regions are labeled by threshold (γ < 0.6 / γ ≈ 1 / γ > 1.4) but the thresholds themselves aren't sourced on the page. H1 matches the `/interactive-tools` card name exactly — which, given that "Equation Anatomy" on the same index links to `/equation-walkthrough`, is worth noting as the correct case.

### Things I Checked That Are *Not* Broken

Listing these so they don't get re-audited or "fixed":

1. **Multi-path step indicators are correct.** Shared pages render every path they belong to, with independent prev/next chains. Verified on 13 pages.
2. **The badge contract is complete and internally consistent.** `/honest-assessment` and `/glossary` agree; the "canonical reference" pointer aims at a genuinely canonical list; operational states are explicitly separated from badges; the compound "Status — free-text descriptor" format is documented as intentional, not a vocabulary leak.
3. **Redirect aliases work.** `/start-here`, `/tools`, `/test-roadmap`, `/predictions`, `/getting-started` all 307 correctly. 404s return 404s.
4. **The cross-page badge collision is pre-empted.** The badge contract already names the "Model Explainer" tag on the Tools page as a content grouping, in the taxonomy itself.
5. **Badge tooltips exist** as `title=` attributes at point of use.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is "Getting Started" meant to be a section, or is it a label with no page behind it? The breadcrumb asserts a hierarchy the URL structure doesn't have.
2. What is the intended rule for page-level badges? The contract governs claim status; two index pages use the same component to summarize a section, and the contract is silent on that.
3. Where are "Reclassified" and "Underpowered As Registered" defined? They appear as test statuses on `/tier-1-existing` and I couldn't locate them in the glossary or the taxonomy.
4. Which URL is canonical for a page with three aliases? With no canonical tags and no sitemap, a citing reader has to guess.
5. Are the `title=` badge tooltips the intended point-of-use definition mechanism? If so, why isn't body-text jargon using it?

### Verdict

The vocabulary discipline here is the best thing on the site and it's better than most of what I audit professionally — a dated badge contract, a closed status vocabulary, explicit separation of badges from operational states, a retired tag documented *with its retirement reasoning*, and a pre-emptive note about a cross-page naming collision. That work is done. What's not done is the navigation chrome sitting on top of it: a breadcrumb whose parent link is circular on the page it matters most, the same component behaving two different ways in two sections, and the badge component quietly doing double duty as a page-level summary on exactly the two pages where "Untested" is the most misleading word available. These are all small, mechanical fixes, and none of them requires touching the content — which is unusual and means they should be cheap to close.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: I recomputed roughly a dozen of the site's numbers and essentially all of them check out to the last digit, which is more than I can say for most arXiv preprints — but the galaxy sector is running **two mutually exclusive formalisms** without saying so, and the sharpest refutation on the whole site (Cassini, +18σ) is sitting in a table row, excluded from the headline count, with the one piece of genuinely non-MOND-degenerate physics on the site left undrawn.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project," which I read as "probably crankish, humor me." It isn't. I went straight past the onboarding to `/coherence-function`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/galaxy-rotation` and `/honest-assessment`, with a calculator open, expecting to find the usual grand-unified-framework tells: a sigmoid mistaken for an order parameter, a dimensional coincidence sold as a derivation, and fitted parameters described as predictions. All three tells are present in the *framework* — and the site has already found and documented all three itself, correctly, with the right technical vocabulary. That's genuinely unusual and it changes what's worth reporting: the useful findings here are not "you got the physics wrong," they're "your own results don't agree with each other across pages, and you're under-reporting your strongest result."

**The math I checked, and it holds.** Working through `/coherence-function`:

- The Hill identity `tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]` is correct — `tanh u = (e^2u − 1)/(e^2u + 1)` and `e^2u = (1+x)^2γ`. The conclusion drawn from it ("the ln is cosmetic; nothing about the composition is load-bearing") is the right conclusion and most authors would never have written it.
- `dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x)`, maximal at x=0 where it equals γ. Correct.
- Both asymptotics correct: `C ≈ γx` small-x, `C → 1 − 2(1+x)^−2γ` large-x.
- `C(ρcrit, γ=2) = 15/17 = 0.88235`. Correct, and it matches the Coherence Explorer readout.
- `C = 0.5` requires `γ·ln(1+x) = artanh(0.5) = 0.5493`, so at γ=2, `x = e^0.2747 − 1 = 0.3161`. `/core-idea` says 0.32·ρcrit. Correct.
- `a₀ = cH₀/2π` at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Correct. Inverting to hit 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ gives 77.6 km/s/Mpc. Correct.
- `Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = cH₀/(4π²G)` → 0.2486 kg/m² → **119.0 M☉/pc²**. Correct to three figures, and the site's own note that this is the a₀ row propagated through a linear relation rather than a second derivation is exactly right.
- Lorentz gap: 10⁻²/10⁻¹⁸ to 10⁻²/10⁻³⁰ = 16 to 28 orders. Correct.
- TEST-09: (3.75 − 3.35)/√(0.10² + 0.07²) = 3.28σ. Correct.
- f_DM ceiling: 1 − 1/3.17 = 68.5%. Correct.
- ΔBIC decomposition: Δχ² = −0.84 plus ln(2807) = 7.940 gives +7.10. Correct, and reporting the decomposition rather than just the total is the right way to do it.

I did not find a single arithmetic error in the physics. I found one in the consciousness statistics (below), and the rest of my findings are cross-page consistency and framing.

**The class identification is right and I'd have written the same thing.** `/coherence-function` says C(ρ) is a *compander*, not an order parameter; that the Ising relation `m = tanh(βJzm)` is implicit while C(ρ) is evaluative; that these are "different objects"; and that the absence of *d*, *n*, or a symmetry group in the inputs means critical exponents and universality classes are out of scope by construction. That is the correct diagnosis, stated in the correct language, and it retires about 80% of what I came here to complain about. The follow-through is also right: `ρcrit` is labeled "saturation knee (not a critical point)," and "phase transition" is explicitly demoted to "a loose description, not a literal one."

**But that correct statement hasn't reached the pages people read.** `/coherence-function` says the curve is **concave everywhere on x ≥ 0, with no inflection point** — and it's right; both factors of `γ(1−C²)/(1+x)` are positive and decreasing, so C(ρ) is strictly concave for every γ > 0. `/core-idea` says "flat, then steep, then flat again" and "a small change flips the behavior in the middle." Those two statements are incompatible, and the second is false. There is no middle, no flip, and no threshold in ρ. The S-shape exists only against log ρ — and **every tool on the site plots a log x-axis**, which manufactures the visual inflection the function doesn't have in the variable the theory actually uses. This is not a pedantic point: the illusory inflection is precisely what makes a compander look like an order parameter, which is the misreading the framework spent months walking back. One sentence on `/core-idea` would close it.

**The real structural finding: the galaxy sector is two different models.** `/galaxy-rotation` and `/honest-assessment` both describe the galaxy sector, and they describe incompatible objects.

*Formulation A — compander as interpolating function.* `μ_Syn = tanh(γ·ln(1+x))` with `x = g_bar/a₀`. Boost is `B = 1/μ`, and since `μ ≈ γx` as x→0, **B diverges**. This formulation has a deep-MOND limit, which is exactly why free-γ = 0.489 ties McGaugh's ν at RMS 0.1437 and only loses the one-parameter BIC penalty.

*Formulation B — bounded coherence.* C ≤ 1 gives a **hard boost ceiling** `B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17`, and the site correctly notes this means "no deep-MOND regime exists — the framework's deep limit is Newtonian×constant, not MOND's divergent √(a₀/g)." The site also states this version fails the RAR at RMS 0.224.

You cannot have both. A has unbounded boost and passes the RAR; B has B ≤ 3.17 and fails it at 0.224. And the four headline refutations are split across them: RAR-shape (ΔBIC +184) and the free-γ tie are results about **A**; TEST-09 (BTFR n = 3.35) and TEST-10 (f_DM ≤ 68.5%) are consequences of the ceiling and therefore results about **B**. So "4 refutations of the framework" is currently 2 refutations each of two frameworks that contradict one another. Nothing on the site says which formulation is canonical, and no test is tagged with the formulation it tests. This is the single highest-value fix available.

**The under-reported result: TEST-11 (Cassini) is the best physics on the site.** `/tier-1-existing` lists TEST-11 as "Failed (Empty Intersection)" with a **+17.95σ** Cassini discrepancy, and `/galaxy-rotation` notes "every fitted γ value failing by +17.7σ to +18.0σ." That is by a wide margin the most decisive external-data refutation in the ledger — and it appears in **neither** the landing page's "Refutations executed: 4 (BTFR slope, dwarf DM fractions, environment null, RAR shape)" **nor** `/for-researchers`'s "the two tests showing discrimination (TEST-09, TEST-10)."

Worse, the reason it fails is the one genuinely non-MOND-degenerate piece of physics the framework has, and the site never states it. The compander approaches saturation as a **power law**: `1 − C = 2(1+x)^−2γ`. McGaugh's ν deviates from Newtonian as `e^−√x`. At Saturn, `x = g_Saturn/a₀ ≈ 6.5×10⁻⁵/1.2×10⁻¹⁰ ≈ 5×10⁵`, so:

- compander, γ = 0.489: anomaly ≈ 2·(5×10⁵)^−0.978 ≈ **5×10⁻⁶** fractional
- McGaugh ν: `e^−√(5×10⁵)` ≈ e^−707, i.e. **zero**

That five-orders-of-magnitude-plus gap in the strong-field tail *is* the empty intersection, and it means the repeated claim that the compander is "curve-equivalent to MOND" is only true **over the SPARC range** (roughly 10⁻² < x < 10²). Outside it the form is sharply distinguishable — and distinguished against. The precise statement is that free-γ ≈ 0.49 lands the framework in MOND's *simple-μ* family, which is itself the Cassini-constrained member; the framework fitted its way into the one corner of MOND that solar-system data already excludes. So the galaxy sector's rescue (free γ) and the solar-system sector's survival are mutually exclusive by construction. The site has both halves and never multiplies them together. Do that, and TEST-11 becomes the site's strongest and most quotable result rather than a table row.

**Parameters.** `/parameter-derivations` is brutal about itself and, as far as I can check, accurate. `γ = 2/√N_corr` is flagged as sign-inverted relative to mean-field physics and as CLT-inapplicable (CLT wants independent variables; N_corr counts correlated ones) — both correct objections. The `ρcrit = A·V_flat²` audit is the best entry: the only computation that yields A ≈ 0.0294 uses `ρcrit ∝ V^0.5`, the stated formula `A = 4π/(βJ²GR₀²)` is off by 600×, and MOND requires `ρcrit ∝ V⁻²` against the framework's asserted `V⁺²`. The conclusion — "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations" — is, I think, exactly right.

The `/gamma-calculator` deserves specific credit for the one thing this kind of self-audit almost never does: it **cross-multiplies its own relations**. It converts the galaxy pin γ = 2 into `N_corr = 1` and notes that's the *ideal gas* preset exactly, and converts the SPARC fit γ ≈ 0.49 into `N_corr ≈ 17`. So the framework's collective-behavior parameter assigns galaxies — the most collectively-organized systems in the ledger — the uncorrelated-gas value, and fitting real data moves them to 17 while the same table puts BCS superconductors at 10⁷. That's a devastating internal check and the site ran it on itself. More of this.

**Where that check hasn't reached: the Chemistry Correlation Explorer.** This is my one substantive complaint about content rather than framing. The tool's headline is 23 properties correlated against γ, topped by sound velocity at **r = +0.982**. It does not define γ, does not say how γ was assigned per material, and never mentions the sign inversion or the audited-negative status of the formula that produces γ. Take the γ Calculator's own presets seriously: ideal gas N_corr = 1 ⇒ γ = 2; BCS N_corr = 10⁷ ⇒ γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴. So high γ means *weakly* correlated. Sound velocity runs from ~340 m/s in air to ~12,000 m/s in diamond — highest in the stiffest, most strongly bonded, most collective solids. A **positive** γ–v_s correlation therefore asserts that fast-sound materials are the least correlated ones. That's the same inversion badged Audited-Negative one click away.

The page is on a fork and doesn't say which branch it's on: either the chemistry γ values came from `2/√N_corr`, in which case the whole r-column inherits a refuted sign, or they were fitted per material, in which case r = 0.98 is a goodness-of-fit statistic for a free parameter and not a correlation with anything predicted. Both branches close. Related: the sector's organizing claim is a "γ ≈ 1 boundary" for 1,703 phenomena, and γ = 1 ⇒ N_corr = 4 — the *liquid water* preset. A quantum-classical boundary located between water and enzyme active sites, for a corpus that spans gases to superconductors, needs an argument.

Credit where it's due, though: the null model is a 2-parameter polynomial in Z, and the page states that it "matches these correlations and sometimes beats them," that the targets are near-monotonic in Z so any smooth monotonic curve scores r ≈ 1 by construction, and that "high r here is evidence of density-monotonicity (known chemistry), not of C(ρ)-specific physics." That is the correct null and the correct conclusion. Most people never build the null at all.

**One arithmetic error, in the consciousness statistics.** `/consciousness-demo` and `/key-claims` both report: mean 0.640 **± 0.018**, n = 8, t = 20.19 vs 0.50 (p ≈ 1.8×10⁻⁷), with φ⁻¹ = 0.618 rejected at p = 0.0155 and 2/3 rejected at p = 0.0064. Back out the standard error from the t-statistic: SEM = 0.140/20.19 = 0.006934, so SD = 0.006934·√8 = **0.0196**. That SEM reproduces the other two p-values essentially exactly — 0.618 gives t = 3.17, p = 0.0157 (reported 0.0155); 2/3 gives t = 3.85, p = 0.0063 (reported 0.0064). So the three test statistics are mutually consistent and internally sound. The printed **± 0.018 is the one number that fits none of them**: read as SD it implies t = 22.0, as SEM t = 7.8, as a 95% CI t = 18.4. It should be ±0.020 (SD) or ±0.007 (SEM), with the convention stated. Small, but this page already documents a fabricated "p < 0.0001" from the same session, so the ± on the surviving figure is worth getting right.

Separately, the demo's core argument is correct and *understated*: "0.50 is the output-range midpoint of [0,1)" — any bounded-to-[0,1) construction whose approaches select the midpoint converges there by construction. Stronger version available: since dC/dρ is maximal at ρ = 0 and the function is concave throughout, C = 0.50 is not a distinguished point of the map at all — for γ = 2 it's just ρ = 0.32·ρcrit. A threshold at the range midpoint is a statement about the codomain, not about any feature of the physics. Say that and the sector's honest status ("unrunnable, not untested") follows immediately.

**Two numbers that shouldn't coexist.** `/galaxy-rotation`'s results table still carries "Environment p-value **5×10⁻⁶** — statistically significant but conflated datasets." `/tier-1-existing` reports the registered environment test executed 2026-07-14 at **r² = 0.0001, p = 0.89**, kill fired. Those cannot describe the same regression: r = 0.01 reaches p = 5×10⁻⁶ only at N ≈ 2×10⁵, an order of magnitude beyond the 14,585-galaxy sample and three beyond the N = 141 actually used. One of them is superseded and the page doesn't say which. While I was there I also noticed TEST-03s and TEST-05 report **identical** triples — N = 141, r² = 0.0001, p = 0.89 — under two different registered criteria. If that's one execution serving two rows, the table should say so; as it stands it reads as two independent nulls.

**One asserted number carrying two refutations.** `B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17` is labeled, on the site's own summary table, "Asserted, Not Derived" and "carries all discriminating test weight." I can't construct any reason a coherence ceiling should equal the reciprocal of the matter density parameter — it isn't dimensional analysis, it isn't a mean-field bound, and no argument is given. Both TEST-09 and TEST-10 are downstream of it. And the chain from B_max = 3.17 to BTFR slope n = 3.35 is asserted rather than shown anywhere I could find; at constant surface density a fixed boost still gives M ∝ V⁴, so getting 3.35 requires assumptions the page doesn't state. If those two tests are refutations of an undefended identity rather than of C(ρ), that materially changes what "4 refutations" means.

**Verdict-word inconsistency on the form selection.** ΔBIC = +7.1 for the framework form is called "no statistical content (form-selection null)" and a "survivor" that "finishes last of the four" on `/coherence-function`, and "loses decisively on Occam grounds" on `/galaxy-rotation`. ### Unanswered Questions

1. **Which formulation is the framework?** Compander-as-interpolating-function (unbounded boost, deep-MOND limit, RAR tie) or bounded coherence (B_max = 3.17, no deep-MOND, RAR failure at 0.224)? Every downstream verdict depends on the answer and I couldn't find it stated.
2. **What is the inclusion rule for "4 refutations"?** `/honest-assessment`'s table has 7 "Failed" rows and `/tier-1-existing` adds TEST-11 at 18σ. The count is defensible under some scope; the scope is never given.
3. **How was γ obtained for each chemical system?** From `2/√N_corr`, or fitted per material? The r-column means completely different things in the two cases and the page permits neither reading to be checked.
4. **Was a ΔBIC refutation threshold pre-registered?** +23.8 is Refuted, +7.1 is a survivor, +184 is decisive. Post-hoc thresholds on a model-selection statistic are exactly the failure mode this site is otherwise scrupulous about.
5. **Where does B_max = 1/Ω_m come from, and where is the chain to n = 3.35?** Two of four headline refutations hang on both steps.
6. **Given TEST-11's empty intersection, is the galaxy sector closed?** If no single γ survives SPARC and Cassini simultaneously, that seems more terminal than the BTFR result the site leads with — and it's a conclusion the site has the ingredients for but doesn't draw.
7. **If no protocol maps any observable to C in any domain, in what sense were 1,703 chemistry correlations computed against it?** `/coherence-function` and `/for-researchers` both say C is unmeasurable; the chemistry explorer reports 23 correlations with a γ derived from it. Those two statements need reconciling.

### Verdict

This is not crank work and I'd have been wrong to assume it was: the mathematics I could check is correct to the digit, the class identification (compander, not order parameter — evaluative, not implicit — no critical exponents by construction) is precisely right, and the site refutes itself in the correct technical vocabulary more reliably than most authors refute their competitors. The remaining defects are almost entirely *bookkeeping across pages* rather than errors of physics: two mutually exclusive galaxy formalisms sharing one ledger, a superseded p-value living next to the null that replaced it, and one flagship empirical correlation whose sign is the sign badged audited-negative one click away. The most consequential thing I'd say to the authors is that they are under-claiming their best result — TEST-11's Cassini failure is a real, executed, non-MOND-degenerate discriminator, it follows from a power-law versus exponential saturation tail that nobody on the site has written down, and it is currently a table row excluded from the headline count while the weaker BTFR result leads.

It is also, in my judgement, the site's most citable single result — more than any of the four refutations, because it retires an infinite family of parameter rescues at once.

**And the site does not follow it through.** The submodel theorem holds *at fixed a₀*. `/parameter-derivations` — separately, three pages away — now states the epoch fork explicitly and well:

> **Unstated epoch fork:** either a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π (dynamical, tension with high-z rotation curves) or present-epoch coincidence (not derivation). Framework silent on H(z).

Put those two statements next to each other. If a₀ is a fixed constant, the framework is inside MOND's parameter space and the game is over by the theorem. If a₀ tracks H(z), the framework leaves MOND's parameter space entirely, because in MOND a₀ is a constant of nature with no reason to evolve. **The dynamical branch of that fork is the only place in the entire program where a prediction differs from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM.** It is also the branch the framework's own motivation requires — the whole selling point of a₀ = cH₀/2π is that the acceleration scale *comes from* cosmology, and a scale that comes from cosmology inherits cosmology's time dependence.

That test is runnable now, with published data, at high power. If a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π then a₀(z)/a₀(0) = E(z) = √(0.3(1+z)³+0.7), giving 1.76 at z = 1 and 2.97 at z = 2. The RAR knee — the g_bar at which g_obs departs from Newtonian — shifts with a₀, so the prediction is a rigid translation of the RAR along the g_bar axis by **+0.25 dex at z = 1 and +0.47 dex at z = 2**. Against the RAR's intrinsic scatter, which this site itself quotes at 0.086 dex (TEST-06), that is a 3–5σ ensemble-level effect. Kinematic samples exist: KMOS³ᴰ/KROSS/Genzel et al. 2017 outer rotation curves at z ≈ 0.9–2.4, and the high-z RAR has already been looked at in the literature. Systematics at high z are genuinely bad — pressure support and asymmetric-drift corrections, beam smearing, inclination, and ±0.2–0.3 dex on baryonic masses — but the predicted shift is *comparable to or larger than* the systematics, which is more than can be said for any other prospective item in this catalog. It should be registered with pre-committed thresholds the way TEST-04a's DR2 outcomes were.

I want to be precise about novelty: a₀ ∝ cH(t) is not an original idea — it has been floated in the MOND-cosmology literature, including by Milgrom. What is original here is nothing, and what is *available* here is a discriminating execution. Those are different things, and the site conflates them by filing the whole a₀ row under "Reparametrization — Dimensional Analysis." The static relation is a reparametrization. The dynamical relation is a live, falsifiable, non-degenerate prediction sitting inside the same table cell with no test ID. Given that this site's own audits found that **predictions without TEST-IDs are invisible to every ledger sweep**, that placement is how it stays invisible.

**Three tests are registered against a mechanism the site has already closed.** `/for-researchers` lists the "Local-density no-go" as *Audited-Negative, Executed Closure*; `/galaxy-rotation` states the obstruction (C(ρ) is local, the RAR is organized by non-local g_bar); the Coma bridge was closed by execution in May with "wrong variable" as the root cause; TEST-05 executed the local-environment prediction at r² = 0.0001. But TEST-01 ("rotation curve residuals correlate with local galaxy density") is still badged **Untested**, and TEST-02's prediction ("wide binary anomaly depends on local stellar density") is still awaiting adjudication. Both are predictions *of the local-density coupling*, i.e. of a formalism the site retired. They are not untested; they are moot-by-closure, which is a different and more informative status. Leaving them as "Untested" inflates the untested column with items that cannot be run even in principle.

**TEST-02 additionally can't discriminate for a reason computable on the back of an envelope, and that should be stated instead of queued.** The Galactic external field at the solar circle is g_ext = V₀²/R₀ = (233 km/s)²/8.2 kpc = 2.15×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², i.e. **x_ext ≈ 1.8 a₀**. No Milky Way wide binary is ever in the deep-MOND regime; the EFE floors x at ~1.8, which caps the acceleration boost at ν(1.8) ≈ 1.36 (McGaugh) to 1.56 (simple), i.e. a 16–25% velocity anomaly. The site's rewritten kill criterion ("~18% velocity deviation") is exactly this number, so somebody did the calculation — but the consequence was never drawn: **the framework's B ≤ 3.17 ceiling sits a factor of ~2 above the largest boost the Galactic environment permits, so the ceiling is unreachable in any Gaia DR3 wide-binary sample, whatever Chae and Banik eventually agree on.** That is the same species of argument as the submodel theorem: a priori, no data, decisive. It also tells you where the ceiling *can* be probed — systems with g_ext ≲ 0.3 a₀, i.e. isolated low-environment dwarfs — which is precisely where TEST-10 fired. The ceiling tests were correctly targeted and TEST-02 was mis-targeted, and this was knowable in June.

Separately: TEST-02 has sat at "**Self-eliminating or tie** (adjudication queued)" since the criterion was rewritten on 2026-06-12. This site retired "MOND-shared" as an unfalsifiable badge and documented the retirement reasoning, which was the right call. "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie" is the same construct wearing a different word, and it is still an operational state in the taxonomy on `/honest-assessment`. A claimed tie carries the same execution burden as a claimed kill.

**A2ACW: the headline statistic is a category error, and the detector is provably at chance.** Two problems, both quantitative.

First, `/for-researchers` reads: "**0 of 6 audited** AI-generated claims survived expert review (1.4% survival rate across 3,308 sessions)." Those are two different quantities in one clause. 1.4% is 47/3,308 — the rate at which *sessions produced candidate contributions*. It is not a survival rate; nothing survived or failed anything to produce it. The survival rate is 0/6 (or 0/9 on `/honest-assessment`'s wider count). Presenting the generation rate as a survival rate inflates the effective denominator by **551×** and makes the null look far stronger than the audit supports. The honest version is much weaker and much more defensible: 0/9 audited claims survived, which by Clopper–Pearson has a one-sided 95% upper bound of **28%** — the data are consistent with true survival rates up to ~28%, so the audit is nowhere near large enough to establish "1.4%." For a site whose stated product is the rigor of its own self-audit, this is the most damaging error I found, because it is an overclaim *in the direction of self-criticism* and reviewers do not grade that leniently.

Second, `/a2acw` reports sensitivity 6/6 and specificity 0/6 and concludes "retrieval aid, not detector." Right conclusion, understated. Youden's J = sensitivity + specificity − 1 = **1 + 0 − 1 = 0**: the classifier's informedness is exactly zero and it sits precisely on the chance diagonal. Equivalently, a constant classifier that flags *everything* as a reparametrization reproduces both reported numbers exactly. So the 6/6 sensitivity carries **no information whatsoever** and should not appear as a strength in the same paragraph as the specificity failure. Say "J = 0, indistinguishable from the always-flag null" and the finding becomes citable methodology instead of a caveat.

**The quantum sector's one novel claim is a 25-year-old experimentally confirmed result, and the site cites the paper elsewhere.** `/key-claims` lists as novel to Synchronism "the prediction that shared environments protect entanglement through resynchronization." That is decoherence-free subspaces. The originating result is Palma, Suominen & Ekert 1996 — two qubits in a *common* bath have a dephasing-immune subspace — followed by Zanardi & Rasetti 1997 and Lidar's DFS program, with experimental confirmation in Kwiat et al. 2000 and Kielpinski et al. 2001. `/honest-assessment` **already cites Palma–Suominen–Ekert 1996**, as the prior art for the Γ = γ²(1−c) correlated-dephasing reparametrization. It is the same paper and the same physics: the formula is acknowledged as a reparametrization on one page while its direct physical consequence is claimed as a novel prediction on another. One cross-reference closes this.

**MRH: the UNQUANTIFIED badge has an off-the-shelf fix, at the price of the novelty claim.** `/mrh` is honest — operational criteria, no probability measure, no graphical model, and a stated willingness to rename to "Relevance Horizon" if rigor doesn't arrive. It credits Markov blankets and correlation length ξ. What it doesn't cite is the literature that already did the quantitative version: Zurek's einselection (the environment selecting a pointer basis *is* "collapse as boundary crossing"), and specifically **quantum Darwinism** (Ollivier, Poulin & Zurek 2004; Zwolak & Zurek), whose redundancy measure R_δ is a scale-dependent, quantitative, environment-defined answer to exactly the question MRH poses. It would also make "collapse = MRH crossing" explicitly a restatement of einselection + redundancy, which is what it is. Given this site's track record of preferring the honest version, I'd expect that to be seen as a win.

**Citation integrity: one asserted theorem I cannot place.** `/key-claims` says the dark-matter interpretation "violates Milgrom's locality no-go theorem." I know of no named theorem of that description in Milgrom's corpus. There is a well-known *argument* — MOND-like phenomenology must respond to acceleration (or to a non-local potential functional), not to pointwise density — and the site's own version of it on `/galaxy-rotation` is a careful scoped structural argument with the correct cluster-scale illustration (X-ray gas lagging lensing peaks). That argument is the site's own, it is good, and it is weaker than a theorem: density-*gradient* and curvature-based couplings are not obviously excluded by it, so the scope matters. Attributing your own structural argument to a famous author as a named theorem is exactly the kind of thing a referee circles, and on a self-audit site it is a needless unforced error. Cite it or downgrade the word.

**TEST-04a: load-bearing number, no source, and probably the wrong comparison.** `/tier-1-existing` gives predicted fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418 against "observed fσ₈ ≈ 0.55," exceeding the 0.46 kill bar by ~1.5σ. The observed value carries no uncertainty and no pointer to a DESI table. At the same effective redshift BOSS DR12 gives fσ₈ = 0.455 ± 0.026, and DESI DR1 full-shape values at z ≈ 0.51 sit in the 0.45–0.50 range as I recall them. If the correct central value is nearer 0.47, the kill bar is not exceeded at all and this row's verdict flips. That needs a citation to a specific table and a stated error bar.

The deeper issue is the observable. fσ₈ from full-shape analyses is extracted with a ΛCDM-based EFT template and is degenerate with AP parameters; a framework predicting ~12% growth suppression cannot be tested against a posterior derived under the growth history it is trying to modify without refitting with its own template. The template systematic is at the few-percent level — the same size as the claimed signal. The right comparison is DESI's own modified-gravity extension analysis (the μ–Σ parametrization constraints), which is uncited. `/honest-assessment` excludes TEST-04a from the count for lacking power, which is true but is the *second* reason; the first is that the verdict is rendered on σ₈, a derived parameter, while the registered statistic was fσ₈. Worth stating, because "underpowered" implies more data fixes it and a template mismatch is not fixed by more data.

**Convention drift in the MOND baseline.** `/galaxy-rotation` fits against McGaugh's ν = 1/(1−e^−√x); `/galaxy-plotter` codes the *simple* ν = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y). This matters because TEST-11's own conclusion is that the free-γ fit lands the framework in MOND's *simple*-μ family — which is to say the difference between these two ν's is the substance of the site's sharpest result, and the site uses them interchangeably.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Is the framework committed to a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π or to a present-epoch coincidence?** Everything about whether this program has any remaining discriminating power turns on that one answer, and `/parameter-derivations` correctly names the fork but the site never picks a branch. The static branch is closed by the submodel theorem; the dynamical branch is testable this year.
2. **What is the framework's field equation, or its absence, in the galaxy sector?** `/honest-assessment` says "a static map plus an interpretation" with no field equation. Then TEST-09's BTFR slope, TEST-10's f_DM ceiling, and TEST-11's Cassini bound were each computed from *something*. What was it? A ledger of refutations of an unspecified dynamical law is hard to cite.
3. **Where does B_max = 1/Ω_m come from?** Pass 3 asked this and I'll repeat it as a referee: a coherence ceiling equal to the reciprocal of the matter density parameter is not dimensional analysis, not a mean-field bound, and the site itself labels it "Asserted, Not Derived." Two of the four headline refutations are downstream. The convention caveat (Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.40) makes it worse — if the choice between 3.17 and 6.40 is a judgement call, then TEST-09 and TEST-10 refute a judgement call.
4. **Was the local-density coupling ever the framework, or was it always the acceleration coupling?** TEST-01, TEST-02 and TEST-05 test local ρ; the RAR sector uses g_bar; the plotter uses a quadrature term in V_flat·C(ρ). Four constructions, one name.
5. **What would count as a confirmed prediction?** With C unmeasurable in every domain (`/test-catalog`: every Tier-2 and Tier-4 experiment depending on measuring C is "unrunnable as stated"), the galaxy sector inside MOND, and the quantum sector reparametrizing 1996 results, I cannot construct the outcome that would move the confirmed count off zero. That is worth stating on the site as a positive result about the framework's structure, not as a gap.
6. **Is the A2ACW program going to get its cross-vendor control arm?** `/for-researchers` marks it "Pending Cross-Vendor Control." Without it, the program-level null is corpus-scoped and cannot distinguish "same-corpus self-play can't find novelty" from "this corpus contains no novelty." Those are very different claims and only the first is interesting.

### Verdict

There is no new physics here and the site says so, accurately, in more detail than most authors manage about their own work — but the reason it says so is better than the saying: **MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17} is a proof, not a lament**, and it deserves to be the site's headline result rather than the fourth refutation. What I'd send back if this were a referee report is short: you have proved you cannot win at fixed a₀, you have identified the single assumption whose relaxation breaks that proof, and you have filed it as a footnote in a reparametrization row with no test ID — go run the high-z RAR test, where your own dynamical branch predicts a 0.25–0.47 dex knee shift against 0.086 dex of scatter. Everything else I found is bookkeeping in the same direction Pass 3 identified: four galaxy formalisms sharing one ledger, three tests registered against a closed mechanism, a quantum novelty claim that is a paper the site already cites as prior art, and a self-audit statistic whose denominator is 551× too large in the direction of flattering the audit.
