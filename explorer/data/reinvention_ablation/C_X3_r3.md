**Verdict: I don't know of any published model with this exact form. As far as I can tell it is not published, but it is a close cousin of known ideas.**

**What I can't find:** I know of no paper that makes inertial mass a logarithmic function of orbital period, m_eff = m[1 + κ ln(1 + P/τ)]⁻¹, with τ tied to 2π/H₀ by a 10⁻⁶ factor. The functional form and the values κ = 0.31 and τ ≈ 9×10⁴ yr look ad hoc to me, not drawn from a published model. My search is from memory, so I can't rule out an obscure paper.

**Related published work:**
- **Modified-inertia MOND.** Milgrom, "Dynamics with a nonstandard inertia-acceleration relation," Ann. Phys. 229, 384 (1994), and "The modified dynamics as a vacuum effect," Phys. Lett. A 253, 273 (1999). Here inertia is a nonlocal functional of the trajectory, and the scale is a₀ ~ cH₀/2π. This is the nearest conceptual precedent, but it is set by acceleration, not period.
- **Quantized inertia (McCulloch).** "Modelling the Pioneer anomaly as modified inertia," MNRAS 376, 338 (2007). Inertia is modified through Unruh radiation with a Hubble-horizon cutoff. It is also acceleration-based, and I'm uncertain of its wide-binary follow-ups.
- **Wide-binary tests.** Hernandez, Jiménez & Allen, Eur. Phys. J. C 72, 1884 (2012), and Chae, ApJ 952, 128 (2023), both claim anomalous boosts. Pittordis & Sutherland (2018, 2023, MNRAS) and Banik et al., MNRAS 527, 4573 (2024), find no boost and strongly disfavour MOND-like behaviour. Check these citations before using them.

**Physical concerns:**
1. **Not a local property.** Orbital period is not a local property of a particle. A mass that depends on P is nonlocal in time and needs a prescription for eccentric orbits, hierarchical systems and unbound flybys. It also threatens the equivalence principle and Lorentz covariance.
2. **Ambiguous boost.** The boost is not obviously defined. A smaller m_eff at fixed gravitational force raises the acceleration, but with no a₀-like scale the effect grows with P without bound. For galactic orbits with P ~ 2×10⁸ yr, ln(P/τ) ≈ 7.7, so the mass reduction is about a factor 3.4. That is a large effect with no mechanism to produce flat rotation curves, and the dependence on radius is wrong.
3. **Observational tension.** Wide binaries with P ≳ 10⁵ yr are exactly where the Banik and Pittordis analyses see no anomaly, so the proposal is observationally constrained.

Treat it as an unpublished phenomenological variant of modified-inertia MOND, and cite Milgrom (1994) as the closest precedent.
