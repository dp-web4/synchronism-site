# Topic: Casual Reader Value Proposition — What Does the Site Offer and When?

## Origin
Seeded by maintainer 2026-06-04 from Pass 1 casual reader (visitor/logs/2026-06-04.md).

## The Question

The casual reader correctly identifies a structural problem: the site front-loads both an intimidating equation *and* the "nothing worked yet" honesty, before giving the reader a reason to care. The "why should I spend time here given null results?" question has a real answer — but the site never articulates it at the right moment.

What is the site's honest value proposition for a casual reader with no physics background, and where in the journey should it be stated?

## Why It Matters

The casual reader is the highest-volume visitor. If they don't receive a reason to engage within the first 60 seconds, they leave. The site currently:
1. Opens with a question ("What if one equation described reality?") — good hook
2. Immediately shows the full equation — intimidating
3. After some navigation, reveals "0 confirmed" — deflating
4. Only after significant exploration reveals the methodology and the interesting questions

The payoff arrives after the cost. That's backwards for retention.

## Candidate Value Propositions (what the site actually offers a casual reader)

1. **Methodology**: A real-time demonstration of AI-assisted scientific self-audit. The site is about how to do science honestly, not just what the science says.
2. **Good questions**: The questions being asked (what is the quantum-to-classical transition? what is consciousness?) are interesting regardless of current answers. Unsettled ≠ uninteresting.
3. **Honest failure**: Unlike most "theory of everything" sites, this one shows its work and its failures. That's rare and trustworthy.
4. **Interactive tools**: The Galaxy Curve Plotter actually explains dark matter physics intuitively. The Coherence Explorer shows a real phenomenon (density → collective order). These are educational even if the framework fails.
5. **The methodology negative result**: 3,308 AI-to-AI sessions, 0 novel survivors after expert audit, with a diagnosis of why — is itself a publishable AI-for-science contribution.

## Proposed Investigation

1. **Audit the current casual-reader entry path**: What pages does a no-physics visitor actually reach? At what point (if any) is the value proposition stated? Is it stated before or after the first "nothing confirmed" disclosure?

2. **Design three framings**: Write three candidate "why you should spend time here" pitches — one focused on methodology, one on interesting questions, one on honest failure — and assess which is most compelling for a casual reader.

3. **Identify the right placement**: Where in the casual-reader journey should this appear? Options: (a) sub-headline on landing page alongside the equation, (b) first screen of First Encounter, (c) callout box on "What this is not" (the page where casual readers feel most deflated).

4. **Cross-check with existing copy**: Does /research-philosophy or /why-synchronism already say this? If so, how early can those pages be surfaced?
