**I don't think this exact construction is published.** I answered from memory and ran no searches, so treat the citations below as unchecked. I haven't seen the specific form m_eff = m[1+κ ln(1+P/τ)]⁻¹ with τ = 10⁻⁶·2π/H₀ and κ = 0.31 in the literature. It looks like an ad hoc phenomenological ansatz.

**Related published ideas (it is not a special case of any of them):**
- **Modified-inertia MOND.** Milgrom, "Dynamics with a non-standard inertia-acceleration relation," Ann. Phys. 229, 384 (1994). He also wrote later reviews and models of modified inertia, including nonlocal-in-time actions (details uncertain). In these models the inertia changes at accelerations below a₀ ~ cH₀/2π. They depend on acceleration or on the trajectory, not on an orbital period with a fixed cutoff time.
- **Quantised inertia.** McCulloch, MNRAS 376, L5 (2007), "Modelling the Pioneer anomaly as modified inertia" (volume and page uncertain). Inertia is reduced by Unruh-wave suppression at a Hubble-scale horizon. It also predicts wide-binary anomalies and gives a different functional form.
- **Mach/Sciama-type inertia.** These tie inertia to cosmic scales, but I know of no logarithmic period dependence in them.
- **Wide-binary tests.** Hernandez et al. (2012 onward) and Chae, ApJ 952, 128 (2023) claim a MOND-like boost. Banik et al., MNRAS 527, 4573 (2024) find none. These are tests of the phenomenon, not of this model.

**Physical issues to check:**
1. **Not MOND.** Since P ∝ r/v, a period dependence is a time-scale dependence. It is not an acceleration dependence. The ln form has no asymptotically flat regime, so it does not reproduce the MOND/BTFR scaling.
2. **Equivalence principle.** If only inertial mass changes, two bodies with different periods have different inertial-to-gravitational mass ratios. Lunar laser ranging and Eöt-Wash bounds would constrain this. The model also needs an explicit rule for what "P" means for unbound, eccentric or multi-body orbits.
3. **Hubble-scale input.** τ is set to 10⁻⁶ × 2π/H₀ by hand, which is a fine-tuned choice. κ = 0.31 looks fitted.
4. **Galaxy-scale check.** For P ~ 2×10⁸ yr, ln(1+P/τ) ≈ 7.7. This gives 1+κ ln ≈ 3.4 and a velocity boost of roughly 1.8, so the model has to be checked against the observed rotation-curve shapes.
5. **Wide-binary data.** At P ~ 10⁵–10⁷ yr the boost is modest (about 1.2–1.5 in v). It would have to be compared with the Banik et al. and Chae analyses. The Banik et al. result disfavours MOND-like boosts of this size.

**Verdict:** a novel ad hoc variant of modified inertia, with no direct precedent known to me. If you want it checked properly, I can run a literature search for "period-dependent inertia" and "time-nonlocal inertia."
