# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-09-17

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /galaxy-rotation, /honest-assessment, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've ever seen, and it tells me in the second sentence that the big idea failed. I'm not sure whether I just got hooked or talked out of it.

### Journey
I clicked through from Reddit expecting a "physicist says dark matter isn't real" kind of site. The landing page asks a great question: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* Almost immediately it answers itself: *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* That's refreshing, and also a bit deflating. The equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` means nothing to me. The translation, "a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are," is funny, but it didn't help me understand anything. Then there's the scoreboard: **0** prospective predictions confirmed, **6** refutations executed, "5 independent roots," "only 2 of them specific to this framework." I'd have bounced here, except the landing page asks my exact question: *"Why keep reading a theory with zero confirmed predictions?"* The answer, *"The autopsy is the product,"* is honestly a great pitch, the kind of thing Veritasium would make a video about. But it sits in the bottom third of the page, well below the scoreboard, which is where I was about to leave.

I followed the Beginner Path. **Why Synchronism?** gave me the best analogy on the site: a crowd milling around a plaza vs. a marching band, where "how densely packed the parts are" decides which one you get. I got that right away. It also explains MOND in one line ("a 1983 rival idea… tweaking gravity itself instead of adding invisible dark matter"), which I needed. The page also mentions Bose–Einstein condensates, YBCO, Abrikosov–Gor'kov, Bell/CHSH and Tully–Fisher. I skimmed all of those.

**First Encounter** (Step 2) opens nicely: a lone electron in a vacuum vs. a neutron star core. It introduces "presence" and the MRH, "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter." That was clear. But I only got Part A of the "7 short parts, lettered A–G"; the page shows a "Next: Part B of 7 →" button. (My browsing tool may not run the click-through, so this could be on my end. If Parts B–G only load through a JavaScript stepper, though, readers without JS and search engines won't see them either.)

**The Core Idea** (Step 3) has another good analogy: a dimmer switch from 0 (sparse) to 1 (collective). The tanh explanation actually worked for me: "flat, then steep, then flat again." Then it got strange. A section called "Why log? (Honest answer: it's cosmetic)" says the log is "notation, not mechanism." So why is the "compressing function" in the headline equation on the front page? Then came a wall of badges I hadn't been taught yet: "Reparametrization — Null-Class (null model run 2026-05-10)", "audited-negative", "⚠ This scaling is refuted", "6 of 6 badges previously labeled 'Validated'… demoted." One line, *"Its own best fit sets cooperativity to zero,"* sounds like it matters a lot, but I have no idea what it means. The badge legend is on Step 5 (Honest Assessment) and the definitions are on Step 6 (Glossary), so the Beginner Path teaches the vocabulary after it uses it.

**Coherence Explorer**: I liked seeing an S-curve with a "sharpness" slider and presets (γ≈0.49 "SPARC galaxy data", γ=½ "MOND's simple function", γ=2 "framework's original estimate"). The readouts at the defaults go up with density the way the dimmer story says (C = 0.33 at the knee, 0.83 at 10×, 0.98 at 100×). But the page says the tool *"does not model any real material, and the density axis has no units."* It also says this "coherence" is *"anti-correlated with quantum phase coherence as used in condensed-matter physics."* That one stopped me cold. The only "coherence" I know is from quantum videos (decoherence, Schrödinger's cat). If the site's coherence is roughly the *opposite* of that, I needed to hear it on the landing page, not in a tool caveat.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter**: this is where things finally clicked. You pick a real galaxy (DDO 154, NGC 2403, NGC 3198, UGC 128, NGC 7331). Blue dots are what telescopes see, a gray dashed line is what visible matter predicts, and a violet line is "Synchronism's real C(ρ)," which *"barely moves off the dashed gray Newtonian line"* ("max C on this disk: 0.001 — inert by construction"). A green MOND line matches the dots. That's a picture of the failure I can actually *see*, and it's the clearest thing on the site. Two things confused me: the dotted amber "hand-tuned tanh(radius) stand-in" (why show a fake curve next to the real ones?), and the "quadrature wiring vs. division wiring" fork, which is never explained for a beginner. There are no beginner instructions on the page.

Dark matter is why I clicked in the first place, so I opened **Galaxy Rotation**. The first paragraph is perfect: outer stars orbit faster than visible mass predicts. After that it gets into compander variants, algebraic chains, RAR, μ-functions, TeVeS/Horndeski and GW170817, and I was lost. As far as I can tell the bottom line is that Synchronism doesn't replace dark matter, and the galaxy fits are really MOND's machinery. It took me a while to dig that out.

**Honest Assessment** has exactly the paragraph I was hoping for: *"What a non-physicist should take away: The equation driven by density — the actual idea — does not fit galaxy rotation. A version with its input switched to acceleration and one dial fitted to the data does fit — but at that setting it becomes MOND… and never does better."* I understood that! But the same page also says *"After 3,308 sessions + 13 adversarial stress tests: 0 confirmed predictions, 0 prospective predictions tested, 6 refutations executed."* Zero predictions *tested*, but six refutations? The landing page defines a refutation as a "test that could have killed the idea." So were there tests or weren't there? It also says *"Every audit, correction and computation on this page was made by AI agents."* That was a surprise to me. I didn't catch it being said up front.

The **Glossary** is actually good: short, plain entries ("MRH: an 'event horizon for influence'"; "Null-Class: a match to data that a structurally simpler, framework-free model matches just as well or better"). I wish I'd been sent there before Step 3 instead of at Step 6.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked (rendered with defaults γ=0.49, ρcrit=1.00, log-scale toggle, three γ presets, live key-value readouts 0.3272 / 0.8259 / 0.9785) — partly intuitive. The S-curve plus "sharpness" slider is easy to grasp, but it has no stated purpose ("no units," "no real material"), and the note that this coherence is the opposite of quantum coherence undercut what I thought I'd learned. *(Browsed with a fetch tool, so I saw the default render; I couldn't physically drag the sliders.)*
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked (dropdown of 5 SPARC galaxies, default DDO 154, Vflat = 47 km/s, ρcrit = 64 M☉/pc³, max C = 0.001) — intuitive for the core message (violet line sits on the gray line = the equation does nothing; green MOND line hits the dots) but confusing on the extras (amber stand-in, wiring fork). The best "aha" moment of my visit. *(Same caveat: default render only.)*

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is the site's "coherence" related at all to the quantum coherence/decoherence from YouTube, or is it just the same word?
2. If the density version failed and the acceleration version *is* MOND, what's left that Synchronism still claims? Is anything alive, or is the whole site a post-mortem now?
3. How can there be 0 prospective predictions *tested* but 6 refutations?
4. Who is "we"? The honest assessment says AI agents did every audit. Did a human come up with the original idea?
5. Does Synchronism say anything about dark matter being real or not, in one sentence?
6. What happens in First Encounter Parts B–G?

### Verdict
As a curious non-physicist, I respect this site enormously. I've never seen a theory site put "it failed" in its second sentence and then show me the failure in a chart I can understand (the Galaxy Plotter). But the Beginner Path uses badge jargon before teaching it, the dark-matter page loses a pop-science reader after one paragraph, and "coherence" apparently means close to the opposite of what I thought. The "autopsy is the product" framing could win over people like me, but right now it's buried below the point where I'd have left.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 read in full (landing, /learning-paths, /glossary, /honest-assessment, /interactive-tools, /gamma-calculator, /equation-walkthrough, /first-encounter, /what-synchronism-is-not, /key-claims, /test-catalog), plus a breadcrumb/H1/`<title>` check of 17 pages
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes (professionally, as an audit target)
- **Overall impression**: The site's vocabulary is better defined than on most science sites I've audited: 90 glossary terms, badge tooltips, a written badge contract. But the pages read like their own changelogs, and a few site-wide counts and labels disagree from one page to the next.

### Journey
**Landing.** The header nav has 8 items. "Start Here" goes to /why-synchronism, and its tooltip says it's step 1 of the Beginner Path. Good. I checked first use of each acronym. **MRH** ("Markov Relevancy Horizon") and **A2ACW** ("AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop") are expanded inline. **SPARC, BTFR, RAR, CHSH, ΔBIC, Ω_m and ALFALFA–SDSS** appear in the scoreboard without expansion. They are all in the Glossary, but the landing page doesn't link them. Badges have `title` tooltips (e.g. Failed: "Prediction contradicted by data — kill criterion fired or claim demoted on audit"). That helps with a mouse and does nothing on touch screens. The landing page's badge key link covers only the descriptive family ("Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed"). The MRH-relationship family (Active-MRH etc.) is used on Beginner Path pages but isn't in that key. The scoreboard has an inline history note ("The cell as it read before: 'Validated' claims surviving audit: 0 of 6…") right next to the current value ("0 of 9"). A newcomer sees two different denominators for the same stat.

**Learning Paths.** The page is clean: three difficulty paths and four topic tracks, each with a step count and time estimate. The Beginner Path here matches the one on the landing page exactly. But I checked the estimate "~40 min–1 h 55 m" against the static text word counts: Why Synchronism 1,717, First Encounter ~617 visible, Core Idea 2,100, What Synchronism Is Not 1,756, **Honest Assessment 18,268**, **Glossary 9,759**. That's about 34k words, or roughly 2.5 h at normal reading speed. Most of it is Steps 5–6. Honest Assessment says so itself ("this page is ~18,000 words"). The top of the range is only a little low. The bigger problem is that a "Beginner" step is a 79-minute document.

**Glossary.** This is a strong glossary: alphabetized, Greek symbols after Latin, badge families grouped, "Learn more →" links, and "Null-Class (finding descriptor, not a badge)" is explicit. Some weaknesses:
- Entries carry edit history ("Entry corrected 2026-07-17; the previous text was a fossil of a retracted claim," "Entry added 2026-09-15").
- The guidance for replacing deprecated badges differs from Honest Assessment. Glossary: Strongly Supported → "Use Reparametrization, Untested, or Active-MRH." Honest Assessment: Validated → "Use Active-MRH or Reparametrization."
- MRH has at least four definitions across the site:
  - Landing: "the bubble of nearest neighbors that actually influence a system"
  - Honest Assessment: "the bounded context something is currently relevant within"
  - First Encounter formal version: "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence"
  - Equation Anatomy: "relevancy boundary," a third term for the same thing

  They're compatible, but a docs style guide would pick one canonical sentence.

**Honest Assessment (badge contract).** I liked the "How to read a badge" contract: formal status = color + leading word; "any text after the dash is a free-text finding descriptor." Very clear. But the taxonomy keeps growing new layers beyond the two families:
- deprecated tags
- descriptors
- "Operational states (not badges)", e.g. Kill Criterion Triggered
- a "correction provenance marker" ("Not a verdict badge")
- a retired "MOND-shared" tag
- the Tools page's "Model Explainer" content grouping

That's six kinds of label-like chips a reader must tell apart. The contract also gets bent in practice:
- Landing "Reparametrization — **Chemistry**" puts a *topic* where the contract says a *finding descriptor* goes.
- What Synchronism Is Not is badged **"Active-MRH — Scope Boundaries — Not a Claim (corrected 2026-07-09; was deprecated 'Validated')"**. Active-MRH is defined as "in active research focus; being extended or revised," so applying it to a scope page is a category mismatch. The badge text also carries its own revision history.
- Some descriptors run long: "Parallel-Paths — Fork — universal γ excluded; registered per-cluster γ = 2 marginal; count unchanged (gates on operator)". That's no longer a label; it's a sentence with three dashes.

The page is 18,268 words with **180 ISO date stamps**. Tier 1 has 140 on ~10k words. The same pattern is everywhere. Examples from Test Roadmap's top boxes: "('could select' until 2026-09-14 — false for TEST-09/10…)", "The blunter form this box used to carry — '0 discriminate' — was false", "Until 2026-09-14 this box named only Tiers 2 and 4, so Tier 3 fell through; explorer finding 2026-09-12", "after a visitor Pass 3 found TEST-11 denoting two unrelated experiments and Pass 2 found the suffix convention undocumented." Before learning the current state, the reader has to parse what the page *used to* say and which internal track noticed. As a docs professional, I'd move this into a per-page "Revision notes" section. The honesty record survives, and the body reads as current state.

**Interactive Tools index.** The index opens with a legend explaining that "Model Explainer" is a content grouping and not a verdict. Good. Problems:
- Level tags claim "one scale — Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced — plus Artifact Lesson." Artifact Lesson is a purpose, not a level. Three of the seven tools carry it in place of a level, so the scale isn't really unified.
- Names and URLs disagree. **"Crossover Regime Visualizer" lives at `/phase-boundary-visualizer`**, and the card says it was "Formerly 'Phase Boundary Visualizer' — C(ρ) has no phase transition". **"Equation Anatomy" lives at `/equation-walkthrough`.**
- The rename from phase transition to crossover hasn't propagated. The γ Calculator's regime readout still says "Transition zone. **Phase transitions**, chemistry, consciousness threshold sit near this boundary." Its Related Concepts still link "Phase Transitions — γ < 1, γ ≈ 1, γ > 1 regimes". Those cut points don't match the calculator's own regime bands (γ < 0.2 / 0.2–0.6 / ≈1 / 2).

**Tool 1: γ Calculator.** It works. The default N_corr = 4 gives γ = 1.0000 and "C(ρ_crit) = 0.60", which matches tanh(ln 2) = 3/5. The live S-curve ghosts γ = 2 in gray. It has 7 presets and a linear (1–1000) / log (1–10⁷) toggle. The quick-reference table reproduces 2/√N correctly for every row I spot-checked (17 → 0.485, 10⁷ → 6.32e-4). The page explains its purpose better than most tools I've reviewed: "What this tool is for / What to conclude / Why is a formula we know is wrong still interactive?" Issues:
- (a) At the default load, a box titled **"What just changed, and why it matters"** narrates the water preset even though the user hasn't changed anything.
- (b) "Until today this page never applied its map…" sits in a paragraph stamped "added 2026-07-27", so "today" is two months stale.
- (c) It cites "research proposal `gamma_ncorr_sign_inversion_sharpness.md`" as a bare `<code>` filename with no link.
- (d) The header says "Four caveats before using this tool," and the caveats are interleaved with a fifth, unnumbered block ("Run the framework's own galaxy parameters through this tool…").
- (e) It says "The Crossover Regime Visualizer uses different Ncorr estimates for some systems." Two sibling tools disagree on preset inputs, the disagreement is stated, and it's left unresolved.
- (f) A tool-type caption notes "(Added 2026-09-15: the index and this page showed different words with no key between them.)" The fix is visible, and so is the scaffolding.

**Tool 2: Equation Anatomy.** It works as a 6-step Prev/Next tour, with "Step 1 of 6 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)". That's a good disambiguation from the Beginner Path's "Step N of 6". The "Read this before Step 1" warning that γ = 2/√N_corr is sign-inverted is exactly the right placement for a known-bad term. Issues:
- The variable legend at the top defines **ρ as "Presence — the density of compatible elements within the system's relevancy boundary."** Step 1 directly below redefines **ρ as "the local mass/energy density"**, with a note that the broader form "has never been operationalized." Two definitions of the same symbol on one screen.
- The legend calls **ρ_crit "a characteristic scale parameter"**. The Glossary calls it "Reference density — saturation knee, not critical point." The *name* still says "crit" while both pages say it isn't critical.
- (A fetch-tool summary first told me the "ln is cosmetic" identity was self-contradictory. The raw text reads [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1], which is fine. The summary lost the superscripts. Not a site defect.)

**First Encounter.** I confirmed Pass 1's hunch from the static HTML. Only **Part A (~617 words)** is in the server-rendered page. Parts B–G ("The Coherence Function" … **"Part G: Where It Fails"**) exist only as progress-bar button titles, and their bodies load client-side. For a site whose thesis is visible failure, the failure part is the one no-JS readers, search engines and fetch-based tools never see. The page does explain its structure well: "7 short parts on this one page, lettered A–G… the letters are only for the parts inside this page."

**What Synchronism Is Not / Key Claims.** "In plain words, before the detail" is excellent. It lists the upcoming acronyms (QFT, GR, ΛCDM, CMB, EFE, A2ACW, "compander") and says you can skip them. More pages should do this. Problems:
- The page text mentions a "**Status Dashboard**" as plain text with no link. The page exists (/status-dashboard returns 200) but it isn't in the header, the footer, or this sentence.
- "Session #616", "Session #581" appear without saying what a session number points to or where to read it.
- Key Claims reuses **γ** for a noise coupling rate "[units 1/√time] — distinct from the regime parameter γ = 2/√N_corr." Honest to flag, but in a site where γ is *the* parameter, a different symbol would save everyone.

**Cross-page count/label consistency.** I checked every one of these in the raw page text, not the fetch summaries:
- **Reparametrization count:** Honest Assessment scoreboard says "**5** reparametrizations (recounted 2026-07-30)." What Synchronism Is Not says "Site-wide audit total: **6** reparametrizations (4 quantum + Born rule + entity criterion)" and, in its classification box, "0 prospective predictions confirmed, **6** reparametrizations."
- **Discrimination:** Test Roadmap's top box says the old "0 discriminate" was false: "2 executed tests did discriminate, and both selected MOND (TEST-09… TEST-10)." Further down the *same page* is "**Net discriminating galaxy tests vs MOND: 0**." What Synchronism Is Not says "**0 galaxy-scale discriminators vs MOND**." A reader can't tell whether "net" / "discriminators" means "in Synchronism's favour" or repeats the retracted wording.
- **Test Roadmap page badge** reads "**Untested** — 26 tests", but the page reports executed Failed results (TEST-09, -10, -25) and a Status tile "0 Pre-registered & Completed." Its prose still refers to the tile by an older label ("The '0 Completed' count below").
- **Tier numbering:** "four tiers… (Tier 1 = 01–10, Tier 2 = 11–14, Tier 3+ = 15–24)". The Tier 3/Tier 4 split isn't given, though the page lists them separately.

**Breadcrumbs / titles.** The breadcrumb trail exists on every page I checked (Home / Section / Page), plus a path stepper ("Beginner Path Step 5 of 6 ← Prev / Next →"). Pages on two paths show both steppers (Honest Assessment also shows "Methodology Track Step 6 of 6"), which is handled cleanly. But:
- (1) **Middle breadcrumb segments are not links**: "Start Here", "Predictions & Experiments", "Methodology", "Core Theory", "Cosmology" are plain text. Only "Home" and, on tool pages, "Interactive Tools" are clickable.
- (2) Those section names ("Core Theory", "Predictions & Experiments") appear nowhere in the header nav, so the breadcrumb describes a hierarchy the reader can't browse.
- (3) Key Claims sits under "Start Here" in its breadcrumb, while the landing page labels it "for physicists" and it isn't on the Beginner Path.
- (4) **16 of 17 sampled pages share the identical `<title>`** "Synchronism | What If One Equation Described Every Scale?" (only /for-researchers has its own). Tabs, bookmarks, history and search results are indistinguishable. The landing H1 ("…from quantum to cosmic?") also differs from that title ("…Every Scale?").

This is actually the one *genuinely distinct* EFE prediction in the RG family (linear, not MOND's nonlinear EFE). It's worth computing, not denying.
3. *"Neither [refraction nor striction] touches this framework's own parameters, whose knee never enters a disc."* That holds for the **floored** form (C ≈ Ω_m below the knee, so ∇C is small). For the unfloored form the site also uses (equations.ts, "C = γx"), C = γρ/ρ_c below the knee. Then ∇ln C = ∇ln ρ *exactly*, independent of knee and γ, and the field equation becomes **∇²Φ + ∇ln ρ·∇Φ = 4πGρ_c/γ**. The source is a uniform density, and matter enters only through its log-gradient. Refraction is O(R/h) in a thin disc wherever the knee is. It's another reason to retire the unfloored form outright.

**Globular-cluster fork.** This is the most sophisticated test on the site, and it has two problems a GC dynamicist would raise at once:
- **Forking paths.** P611.2 (registered 2026-02-17) "registers a γ, not a knee" and fixes no floor. The "marginal" verdict uses a knee "measured 2026-08-27" and the floored form. With RG's knee it passes; unfloored it's excluded at 7.5×. With two unregistered choices spanning pass → excluded, this isn't a prospective test of the registration. That's presumably why the scoreboard still says 0 prospective tests, but no page says so.
- **Two unmodelled outskirt effects.**
  - (a) Potential escapers, which are energetically unbound but inside r_t. They are the standard Newtonian explanation for flattened outer dispersion profiles (Küpper et al. 2010; Claydon, Gieles & Zocchi 2017). The page mentions "unbound extra-tidal stars inside r_t" without citing that this is the known MOND-mimic.
  - (b) Striction. In spherical symmetry the algebraic g = g_N/C is exact only for the non-variational theory. My back-of-envelope for the floored form below the knee is |f_striction|/|g| ~ (1−Ω_m)γ·ρ̄(<r)/(6Ω_m ρ_c), up to O(1) log-slope factors. That's ≫ 1 whenever the enclosed mean density exceeds the knee, which is the entire excluded window. The statistic is the *outer* slope, where ρ̄/ρ is largest.

  The window's 2026-09-16 caveats list refraction and smoothing but not striction.

### Verdict
The site answers the question I came with, honestly and correctly: no prediction that differs from MOND + ΛCDM has survived, and the two tests that discriminated both selected MOND. What I didn't expect is that the framework is now the least interesting thing here. Three things are worth an expert's time:
- the dark-energy page's matter-slaved-DE lemma, a clean and transferable one-page note pending a Cardassian literature check
- the RG-family field equation's linear EFE and striction physics. These are real, distinct and uncomputed, but they need momentum conservation done properly before any "exclusion" is quoted.
- the methodology question, which is better answered by coding the site's own correction trail than by the famous-item benchmark

The credibility risk has moved entirely to the refutation side. The Wide Binaries page misstates MOND and inverts its own regime. "Conserves momentum" and "EFE = 0 exactly" are wrong for the equation actually used. The methodology page cites the retracted version of its own null.
