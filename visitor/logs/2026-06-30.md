## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (+ 3 dead-end 404s)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I get the one-sentence pitch (one "density dimmer switch" rule maybe links electrons to galaxies) and I respect that they show their failures, but every page drops me into tanh/MRH/Ncorr noise about 30 seconds in.

### Journey
I clicked through from a Reddit link. The landing page hook — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is genuinely the kind of thing that makes me click a Veritasium video, so good start. But then literally on the first screen there's `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I have no idea what tanh is and "ln" is a half-memory from high school. I scrolled past it fast.

I went looking for the beginner route. The homepage promised a "6-step path, no equations" for beginners, but when I tried the obvious **/learn** link I got a 404. That stung — the one path advertised *for me* is the one I couldn't find.

**Start Here / First Encounter** had the best thing on the whole site: the **marching band vs. crowd in a plaza** analogy. That clicked instantly — organized vs. random, got it. The "an atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy" line for the neighborhood idea was also nice. But within a paragraph there's `γ = 2/√Ncorr` and an "expert note" about BCS superconductors that admits the formula gets quantum systems *backwards*. As a casual reader that's whiplash: you just told me the big idea, now you're telling me it's wrong for half of reality, and I don't even know what a superconductor's γ is supposed to be.

**Why Synchronism?** was actually the page that made me care. Plain-language pitch: physics currently uses different equations at different scales; if one density rule worked everywhere you'd need fewer assumptions. And it's upfront that it hasn't worked yet (melting points 53% off, a superconductivity prediction of 607K vs the real 93K). I'd have put this page *first*, before any equation.

For tools, I bounced off two guessed URLs (404 again) before finding the **/tools** index, which listed everything cleanly. I played with the **Coherence Explorer** (sliders), the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**, and the **Phase Boundary Visualizer**.

The recurring feeling: this site is *honest to a fault*. Every cool thing comes with an immediate "...but this doesn't actually prove anything." The galaxy plotter literally says all three curves fit equally well so it proves nothing, and that the real test rejected the idea. As a casual reader I admire the honesty but I leave with no thing I can hold onto and repeat to a friend — except "some people are very carefully testing whether one equation rules everything, and so far it mostly doesn't."

### Journey
I audited this the way I'd audit any docs site: walk the nav top-to-bottom, check that every term resolves, and look for two pages that say different things about the same object.

**Navigation is the best part.** The header nav is labeled in plain language ("Start Here," "Why Synchronism?," "Honest Assessment") and every page below the home page carries a working breadcrumb (`Home / Getting Started / …`, `Home / Methodology / …`, `Home / Interactive Tools / …`). That's better breadcrumb discipline than most commercial docs sites. The footer is a consistent site-map on every page. No dead nav links in my walk — notably, the beginner path that Pass 1 reported as a 404 is alive and well at **/learning-paths** (Beginner 6-step, Intermediate 8-step, Advanced 8-step). Pass 1's problem wasn't a missing page, it was *guessability*: they typed /learn. The nav link itself works.

**Acronyms are mostly handled.** The landing page and glossary expand MOND, MRH, RAR, BAO, A2ACW. The glossary is alphabetical, 30+ terms, and the header promises site-wide hover tooltips ("Hover over highlighted terms on any page"). A2ACW resolves consistently to "AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop" on both landing and glossary — good, that's exactly the kind of term that drifts.

**Where it gets shaky: single-source-of-truth.** The validation badges — the spine of this whole site — are defined in *three* places: honest-assessment (labeled "canonical reference"), research-philosophy ("Validation Badge Taxonomy"), and the glossary. Today they agree. But three copies of a definition is three things to update when the taxonomy changes, and this taxonomy is explicitly described as evolving (deprecated tags, "no 'established' tags during stewardship"). The non-canonical copies should be pointers to the canonical one, not parallel prose.

**The finding that matters most:** the **γ Calculator** (/gamma-calculator) leads with the subtitle *"Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems"* and three blunt caveats. But the **Equation Anatomy** walkthrough (/equation-walkthrough), which dissects the *same* `γ = 2/√Ncorr`, softens this to "motivated choices, not derived results" — no Audited-Negative badge, no sign-inversion warning. A reader doing the learning path hits the gentle version; a reader poking the tool hits the brutal version. For a site whose entire credibility rests on uniform honesty, that's the kind of inconsistency that, once spotted, makes a reader trust the *less* honest page less. The tool is more honest than the tutorial.

**Inline term-linking is uneven.** The glossary advertises hover tooltips "on any page," but several content pages (key-claims, what-synchronism-is-not, first-encounter) use MRH, reparametrization, BEC/BCS, and one-off acronyms (PCI*, CRT, PRI) as plain text with no glossary anchor at first use. The infrastructure exists; it's just not applied consistently, which makes the promise on the glossary header read as overstated.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, equation-walkthrough, parameter-derivations, galaxy-rotation, key-claims, gamma-calculator, for-researchers, galaxy-plotter, honest-assessment, chemistry)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of both the physics content *and* the framework's own verdict on it)

- **Overall impression**: This is the rare "theory of everything" site where the authors have already run every attack I would have run, landed them harder than I would have, and posted the corpses — the honest scoreboard is "0 confirmed predictions, 0 derived parameters, 0 tests discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM," and I couldn't find a place where that verdict is too harsh.

### Journey
I did what any of us does with a grand-unified pitch: ignored the landing copy and went straight for the derivation pages to find where the hand-waving hides. The honest answer is that the hand-waving is *labeled*. 

**The core object C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit+1)).** My first instinct was to expect a mean-field story — tanh is exactly the self-consistent magnetization of Curie–Weiss, m = tanh(βJz·m), so a "coherence order parameter" begs for a free-energy/gap-equation backbone. The Equation Anatomy page refuses to give me one: *"tanh and γ = 2/√Ncorr are motivated choices, not derived results — there is no derivation to walk through, only choices to examine."* That's the correct call. C(ρ) is a **forward map** from a control parameter to an output; there is no self-consistency loop, no free energy being minimized, so it is not an order parameter in any Landau sense — it's a member of the sigmoid/compander family (μ-law, Hill, logistic), and the site says exactly that. Refusing the Landau prestige vocabulary it could so easily have stolen is the single most credible decision on the site.

**The γ = 2/√Ncorr sign inversion is the catch I'd have led a referee report with — and they led with it themselves.** Fluctuation scaling is unambiguous: a 1/√N is a *width* that shrinks as N grows, so a system of N correlated units should have a **sharper** transition. The formula does the opposite: larger Ncorr → smaller γ → *flatter* tanh. The γ Calculator hands you the reductio directly — Ncorr=1 (ideal gas, no transition at all) gets the sharpest γ=2, and a BCS superconductor (Cooper coherence over ~10⁴ sites, a genuinely sharp Tc) gets γ≈6×10⁻⁴, the flattest. That is backwards at the level of the physics, not the prefactor, and it carries an **Audited-Negative** badge. The parameter-derivations page goes one level deeper than the calculator and names the *root* error: CLT's 1/√N assumes **independent** variables, but Ncorr is by construction a count of **correlated** ones — so the statistical motivation is self-contradictory before you even get to the sign. Correct, and sharper than the tool's framing.

**Galaxy sector — the locality no-go is real physics.** The plotter overlays Newtonian-baryonic, observed, MOND (green), and Synchronism (violet); the violet and green sit on top of each other, and the caption says so: a reparametrization. The interpolating function is McGaugh et al. 2016, gobs = gbar/(1−e^−√(gbar/a₀)), unchanged. a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is the Milgrom-1983 coincidence, explicitly flagged as "not a Synchronism-specific derivation." Synchronism's only additive content was an environmental-scatter ansatz, and TEST-03 killed it (R²=0.14 vs a pre-registered 0.20 floor). But the keeper is on honest-assessment: **C(ρ) is a function of local density ρ, while the RAR it mimics is a function of gbar — the enclosed-mass acceleration, a non-local geometry-dependent quantity.** A strictly local function of ρ cannot reproduce a non-local function of gbar except by fitting galaxy-by-galaxy. That's a genuine no-go (it's the Milgrom non-locality point, instantiated quantitatively), and it's the most damning thing on the site — correctly identified by the site itself.

**The A ≈ 0.029 chain-of-custody audit** is the kind of thing I'd never expect a framework to publish against itself: the number was computed with ρcrit ∝ V^0.5, but the framework's own scaling is ρcrit ∝ V²; re-running with the correct exponent gives A ≈ 4.6×10⁻⁵, off by ~600×. "The number outlived its computation." Audited-Negative. That's a provenance failure most groups would quietly let ride.

**LIV / preferred-frame (for-researchers) is the most sophisticated page on the site, and it's a self-refutation.** The framework's foundational commitment — absolute time, a preferred substrate frame — removes boost invariance, *"the only custodial symmetry."* They then do the right calculation: a reflection-symmetric lattice substrate has a dispersion even in k, so the leading time-of-flight LIV term is n=2, sitting at E_QG,2 ≈ E_Pl, ~10⁷ below LHAASO's 2024 bound — untestable, fine. **But the dimension-4 channel is not protected**: radiative corrections generate an SME coefficient c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻²–10⁻³, against laboratory bounds of 10⁻¹⁸ to 10⁻²⁹, a **16–28 order-of-magnitude fine-tuning gap → refuted.** This is precisely the Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia (2004) result: a Lorentz-violating substrate percolates to large low-dimension LIV through loops unless a symmetry (SUSY, etc.) forbids it, and absolute time is exactly what removes that protection. The site reaching this conclusion *against its own ontology* is, frankly, better physics than most of the framework it's refuting.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — physically transparent and self-incriminating — Input Ncorr, get γ=2/√Ncorr and a regime label. I checked the limits: Ncorr=1 → γ=2.000, Ncorr=10⁶ → γ=2.00×10⁻³. ### Unanswered Questions
1. Once C(ρ) is conceded to be a phenomenological compander with no self-consistency loop, what physical content remains? A sigmoid fit between a density and an output isn't a theory unless something *closes the loop* (a free energy, a gap equation, an evolution law). Is there any version of the program where ρ and C are determined together, or is it forever a forward map?
2. The locality no-go (local ρ vs non-local gbar) looks fatal to the galaxy program *as stated* — but does it generalize into a positive theorem? "Any strictly local coherence function fails to reproduce a non-local acceleration relation except by per-system fitting" would be a transferable result with teeth, well beyond Synchronism.
3. The dim-4 LIV refutation is the framework's own ontology committing suicide via radiative corrections. Is that being treated as a *result* (absolute-time substrates are radiatively excluded — a real, citable null) rather than just a black mark? It's arguably the most valuable thing the program has produced.

### Verdict
This is not a crank site and it is not a theory-of-everything site — it's an unusually disciplined autopsy of one, written by people willing to hold the scalpel against their own thesis. Every attack I'd bring as a referee (tanh has no mean-field backing; γ's correlation-scaling has the sign backwards *and* misuses the CLT; the galaxy fit is a MOND reparametrization with a fatal locality obstruction; a foundational absolute-time frame is radiatively excluded at the dim-4 level by 16–28 orders of magnitude) is already on the site, badged and quantified, often more sharply than I'd have stated it. The honest verdict — 0 confirmed predictions, 0 derived parameters, 0 discriminating tests — is, as far as I can check, accurate and if anything generous to the critics. The residual friction is entirely about *parity of honesty* (the walkthrough is gentler than the tool; the chemistry headline is louder than its own null model), not about hidden errors. My real takeaway isn't about Synchronism at all: the transferable products here are methodological — the locality triage, the mechanism-class DESI failure, and the dim-4 LIV self-refutation are each a clean, citable negative result. A framework that converts its own collapse into reusable nulls is doing something most published physics doesn't.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, key-claims, for-researchers, tier1-existing-data, honest-assessment, interactive-tools, chemistry-correlation-explorer, phase-boundary-visualizer, what-synchronism-is-not)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of the claim inventory and, more importantly, of exactly which claims are reparametrizations vs. nulls vs. live)
- **Would return?**: yes — but as a referee citing three negative results, not as a reader following a research program
- **Overall impression**: There is **no new physics here and the site says so**; what's genuinely citable is a small set of *negative* results, two of which are correct-but-known and one (the mechanism-class DESI failure) that is a legitimately transferable null I'd be happy to see in a paper.

### Journey
I came in with one question — *what, if anything, here is new and discriminating?* — and went straight to the four pages that would answer it: `/key-claims`, `/tier1-existing-data`, `/for-researchers`, `/honest-assessment`. The framework's own answer is unambiguous and I could not move it: **0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, 0 tests discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM, 1 external refutation.** I spent my time trying to find a place where that scoreboard was too harsh on itself, and instead found two places where it is mildly too *kind* (below).

**The novelty audit, test by test.** The Tier-1 table is the most useful page on the site for my purposes because it pre-sorts the claims by *why* they don't discriminate:
- TEST-01/05 (RAR–local-density correlation): the predicted signal sits **~120× below SPARC reach** and the sign is shared with MOND-EFE — non-discriminating by construction.
- TEST-02 (Gaia DR3 wide binaries): C(ρ) predicts a Newtonian null (~0.05–0.4% deviation, **80× below Gaia systematics**) while MOND+EFE predicts ~18%. The site states plainly *"this test has no outcome that favors Synchronism"* — if Gaia confirms the null, that confirms **Newton**, and Synchronism merely reproduces it. That is exactly right, and it is the correct epistemic call most theorists won't make about their own model.
- TEST-04 (BAO ~10⁻⁴ shift): **3000× below DESI precision**, withdrawn by the framework's own forecast that BAO matches ΛCDM.
- TEST-09/10 (BTFR slope regimes, dwarf DM fraction): "MOND-shared — a positive result does not discriminate." Correct: a regime-dependent BTFR slope n→4 in deep-MOND is textbook Milgrom, not a Synchronism signature.

That leaves exactly one externally-falsifiable, distinguishable claim — and **it failed**.

**TEST-04a (DESI DR1 growth) is the one that matters, and I want to be precise about it as someone who works with these data.** The framework predicted coherence *damping* → structure-growth **suppression**, fσ₈(z≈0.51) ≈ 0.418, ~12% below ΛCDM. DESI DR1 full-shape gives the opposite sign — the site quotes observed fσ₈ ≈ 0.55 (and elsewhere LRG1 fσ₈/fid = 1.16 ± 0.13), an *enhancement*. So the predicted sign is wrong and the kill criterion (fσ₈ > 0.46) triggered at ~2σ. Two expert caveats the site itself flags, which I want to underline rather than soften: (1) this is **post-hoc** — σ₈ was calibrated to the 2025 lensing tensions while DESI DR1 published April 2024, so this is a *retrodiction*, not a registered prediction; and (2) DESI DR1 RSD is, taken as a whole, broadly *consistent* with Planck-ΛCDM — the ~16% LRG1 excess is a single tracer bin with a 0.13 fractional error, so "2σ disfavor of suppression" is the honest read but should not be inflated into "DESI sees enhancement." The site mostly gets this right; it's the only quantitatively live external test and it points the wrong way. As a refutation of a growth-suppression dark-sector mechanism, though, it **does** generalize — see below.

**The LIV page (`/for-researchers`) is the most competent physics on the site, and it is a self-refutation I happen to specialize in.** The argument is textbook-correct: a discrete reflection-symmetric (even-k) substrate gives a quadratic, n=2 time-of-flight LIV term sitting at E_QG,2 ≈ E_Pl, i.e. **~10⁷ below the LHAASO-2024 bound** — structurally unreachable, fine. But the dimension-4 channel is *not* protected: at one loop the UV-dominated radiative correction generates an SME coefficient **c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻²–10⁻³**, against cavity-MM bounds ≲10⁻¹⁸ and comagnetometer bounds ≲10⁻²⁹–10⁻³⁰ — a **16–28 OOM fine-tuning gap → refuted at natural value.** This is precisely **Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Gambini–Pullin, PRL 93, 191301 (2004)**, and the page cites it correctly: a Lorentz-violating substrate percolates to large low-dimension LIV through loops *unless a symmetry forbids it*, and the framework's foundational absolute-time/preferred-frame commitment is exactly what removes that custodial symmetry. I have nothing to add to this; it is the correct calculation and the correct citation. My only note is editorial: this is a **result** (absolute-time discrete substrates are radiatively excluded), and it is buried as a "black mark" rather than headlined as the citable null it is.

**The locality no-go (`/honest-assessment`) is correct but it is older than the page implies.** "C(ρ) is a *local* function of density ρ; the RAR it mimics is a function of g_bar — the *non-local*, enclosed-mass acceleration; a pointwise intensive variable cannot reproduce an acceleration-space relation across different mass geometries without per-system recalibration." Yes. But this is, restated, *why MOND is intrinsically non-local* and why every attempt at a purely local f(ρ) dark-fluid has failed — it is the reason Milgrom's law resists a local-field formulation, and it is the same obstruction behind MOND's factor-2–3 cluster mass deficit (the page's "ρcrit,cluster 10⁴–10⁶× smaller" is the cluster problem in density language). The site correctly notes Verlinde (enclosed mass) and MOND (surface density) escape it. As a transferable triage criterion — *"any strictly-local coherence/density function fails the RAR except by per-system fitting"* — it has teeth and I'd cite it; as new physics, it isn't.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is there *any* foreseeable dataset that discriminates Synchronism from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM? My read of the Tier-1 table is **no, not even in principle as stated** — every live channel is either >80× below systematics, sign-shared with MOND, or has "no outcome that favors Synchronism." 2. The dim-4 LIV exclusion appears to kill not just Synchronism but **any** discrete absolute-time substrate without an added custodial symmetry. Is that being written up as a general no-go? It's the one place the program produced something I'd referee favorably, and it isn't Synchronism-specific.

If it recovers their predictors, that's worth stating; if it finds a *new* predictor, that would be the first genuinely novel empirical result on the site — and it's currently un-audited.

### Verdict
As a working modified-gravity / LIV researcher: **there is no new physics on this site, and its great virtue is that it tells you so before you can.** Every discriminating test is either >80× below systematics, sign-degenerate with MOND-EFE, withdrawn, or — in the one live external case (DESI growth) — refuted with the wrong sign, and even that is post-hoc. The framework's headline scoreboard (0 confirmed, 0 derived, 0 discriminating) is accurate and, in two places (the chemistry r-values, the "transferable" σ_int/offset results), *too generous to itself* by a hair. What I would actually carry away to my own work is three **negative** results: the local-ρ-vs-non-local-g_bar triage (correct, = Milgrom non-locality restated quantitatively), the mechanism-class DESI growth-suppression failure (genuinely transferable to all coherence-damping dark sectors), and the dim-4 radiative LIV exclusion of absolute-time substrates (textbook CPSU-2004, correctly applied, and the only thing here I'd cite without hedging). The site's real contribution is not a theory — it's a worked demonstration that a disciplined self-audit can convert a failed unification into reusable nulls. That's rarer than it should be, and it is the only reason I'd come back.
