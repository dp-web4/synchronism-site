## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (1 was a 404 — the homepage's own "Start Here" link)
- **Understanding achieved**: minimal-to-partial
- **Would return?**: maybe — the honesty hooked me more than the physics did
- **Overall impression**: A really intriguing question buried under math symbols, and people who seem refreshingly willing to admit when their idea isn't working.

### Journey

Clicked the Reddit link. Landed on a big question — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — and I liked that. That's the kind of thing Veritasium would open with. Right under it, though, there's a formula: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I don't know what `tanh` is. I don't know what `ρ` is or why it has a "crit" subscript. I read past it.

The nav had a bunch of options — Start Here, Why Synchronism, Tier 1, Tools, Glossary, Honest Assessment. "Start Here" sounded like exactly what I wanted, so I clicked it… and got a **404 page not found**. That's a really bad first move for a casual visitor — the literal "start" button is broken. I had to guess that "First Encounter" on the homepage was the same thing, and that one worked.

The First Encounter page was the best part of my visit. The marching-band-vs-crowd analogy for "coherence" actually clicked for me. The idea that "a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy" gave me the gist of MRH without making me feel dumb. But then phrases like "degrees of freedom" and "superconductor" showed up and I started skimming.

"Why Synchronism?" was where I started getting nervous. The page told me the framework had failures — melting points off by 53%, a superconductivity prediction off by factor of 6.5. I appreciated the honesty but I also wasn't sure if I should keep reading something that doesn't seem to work.

I clicked into Tools out of curiosity. Tried the **Coherence Explorer** — I could drag γ up and down and watch a curve get steeper or gentler. Cool. But I didn't know what I was *learning* by doing it, because I don't know what coherence is supposed to mean physically. Then I tried the **γ Calculator**. The preset buttons (Ideal gas, Liquid water, Ferromagnet, BCS superconductor) were helpful — I clicked them and watched γ change — but I'd never heard of "BCS" or "BEC" and the regime labels ("Weakly Correlated," "Boundary," "Collective") were words without meaning to me.

I ended on **Honest Assessment**. This is where the site won some points back. "Zero confirmed predictions, one refuted by external data, six reparametrizations." That's wild to put on your own site. As a casual reader I had two simultaneous reactions: *(1) wow, integrity*, and *(2) wait, so… this whole thing might just not work?* The page doesn't quite tell me what I'm supposed to do with that. Am I reading a failed theory? An ongoing research project? A philosophical exercise? The "47 internal methodology contributions" line is meaningless to me — I don't know what those are.

Tier 1 — same pattern. Tests failed. Honestly stated. But I'm a casual reader and I want a thread to pull on. The site gives me the receipts but doesn't really give me a hopeful "here's what's still being looked at" moment that isn't smothered in jargon.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — *intuitive in mechanics, confusing in meaning* — sliders responded smoothly, but I couldn't translate "moving γ from 2 to 0.5" into anything I cared about. Felt like driving a car with no destination.
- **γ Calculator** — worked — *partially intuitive* — the preset buttons saved me; without them I'd have no idea what number to type. The output regime labels need plain-English subtitles.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What does "coherence" actually *predict* or *let me do* that's different from regular physics? (I get the marching-band metaphor but not the consequence.)
2. If the framework has "0 confirmed predictions" and a refutation, why is the site still up — is this a research-in-progress project, a postmortem, or a philosophy site? I genuinely couldn't tell.
3. Is `tanh` important enough that I need to learn what it is, or can I keep skimming past it?
4. What's the "single equation describing reality" actually *for*? What problem in my newspaper or YouTube feed would it solve?
5. Why does density matter so much? The whole framework hinges on it but I never got a clear "here's why density is the magic input."

### Verdict

The site has a genuinely captivating question and a level of self-honesty I rarely see on physics sites — that combination almost works. But the broken "Start Here" link is a punch in the face on arrival, and the casual-reader on-ramp is too short before the jargon takes over. A curious non-scientist will leave admiring the integrity but not understanding the idea, which is the opposite of what a public-facing site should achieve. The bones are good; the first 90 seconds need rescuing.

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 (`/`, `/glossary`, `/first-encounter`, `/why-synchronism`, `/honest-assessment`, `/research-philosophy`, `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/a2acw`, `/what-synchronism-is-not`, `/interactive-tools`, `/galaxy-plotter`, `/equation-walkthrough`, `/start-here` → 404)

### Journey

I walked the nav top-to-bottom, then sampled cross-links, then opened two tools I could find from `/interactive-tools` that Pass 1 had not touched. The information architecture is unusually substantive for a research site — there's a real glossary, a real philosophy page, real test IDs (TEST-01..TEST-10 with sub-variants), and breadcrumbs on at least some pages. That's all rare and good.

What's missing is a **single source of truth for the validation badge taxonomy**, and the absence is visible everywhere. The badge vocabulary is the entire site's epistemic spine — it's how the page tells you what to trust — and I counted four different versions of it on four different pages:

| Page | Badges listed | Count |

| `/test-catalog` (Test Roadmap) | Validated, Reparametrization, Untested, Speculative, Failed | **5** |
| `/research-philosophy` (canonical?) | Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed + Post-diction sub-label + operational states (Kill Triggered, Withdrawn, MOND-shared) | **6 primary** |
| `/honest-assessment` | Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed, Post-hoc retrodiction (implicit) | **7** |

A reader hitting Test Roadmap first will not know "Strongly Supported" exists. A reader hitting Honest Assessment first cannot tell whether "Post-diction" is a badge or a sub-status. The Honest Assessment page even tells the reader "Full definitions: Research Philosophy" while the Test Roadmap tells the reader "Full definitions → [Glossary]." Two canonical destinations for the same vocabulary is one too many.

The **glossary itself** is the next-biggest structural issue I'd flag. It's not alphabetized — it's organized thematically (core physics → observational parameters → validation badges), which is fine for a primer but bad for a glossary. A glossary's affordance is lookup. There's no search, no filter, no A-Z index, and no client-side jump-to. If I saw "ALFALFA-SDSS" on `/why-synchronism` and went to find it, I would have to scroll through ~45 unrelated entries. Several common acronyms used elsewhere on the site never appear in the glossary at all: **EFE, AIC, BCS, BEC, EFT, LSS, CSR, YBCO** (some surface in tool presets, others in research-philosophy prose).

**Navigation flow** revealed one confirmed broken endpoint. `https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/start-here` returns HTTP 404. Pass 1 saw the same thing by clicking. The header nav appears to route "Start Here" → `/first-encounter` (good), but Pass 1 reached `/start-here` by clicking *something* on the homepage — which means a body-level CTA, footer link, or stale sitemap entry still points at the dead URL. Either create the route (redirect to `/first-encounter`) or grep the codebase for `/start-here` hrefs.

Breadcrumbs are present on `/first-encounter` (Home / Getting Started / First Encounter) and on `/what-synchronism-is-not` — but I couldn't confirm them on `/honest-assessment`, `/tier-1-existing`, or `/research-philosophy`. With ~74 pages, breadcrumb coverage is worth a site-wide audit. Tech-writer rule: if breadcrumbs exist on any page, they must exist on every page, or readers conclude the absent ones are "shallower" (which is rarely the intent).

**Terminology inconsistencies within Honest Assessment** were the most surprising find — surprising because this is exactly the page where the site has the most to gain from precision:

- "Kill criterion **triggered**" vs "Kill criterion **met**" — used in adjacent paragraphs.
- "Sole survivor" vs "zero survivors" — both phrases appear about the same novel-prediction count, suggesting an earlier sentence wasn't updated when the count moved from 1 to 0.
- The DESI/TEST-04a result is labeled both as a "**mechanism-class failure**" AND as a "**post-hoc retrodiction, not a prospective prediction**" in close proximity. These mean different things (the formula's *origin* vs the test's *outcome*), and both can be true, but the page never bridges them — a casual reader will read this as the site hedging.
- "RAR Transition Shape: the only non-degenerate galaxy-scale **discriminating** test" appears within the same page that classifies it as a kill-criterion failure (ΔBIC = +184). A reader can't tell whether "discriminating" means "would have discriminated" (counterfactual) or "does discriminate" (current claim).
- `/what-synchronism-is-not` says "4 reparametrizations, 1 refutation"; `/honest-assessment` says "6 reparametrizations identified." Either the counts are stale on one page or two scopes are being mixed without labels.

**Acronyms on the landing page** are the friction Pass 1 felt and I can name: MRH, RAR, A2ACW, MOND, DESI, BAO all appear without first-use expansion. The site has a glossary with hover-link affordances on internal pages — that pattern should extend to the landing.

The **Equation Walkthrough** tool is, surprisingly, where I'd push hardest as a tech writer. It's six steps. It defines C, ρ, ρ_crit, γ, ln, tanh in plain language (the dimmer-switch analogy for tanh is the single best sentence on the site). What it **does not do** is answer the question every reader brings: *why this functional form and not another*? A walkthrough that derives an equation without comparing it to alternatives is a presentation, not a derivation. A Hill function, a logistic, an erf, a Naka–Rushton all sit in the same family — the page asks me to accept that tanh(γ·ln(...+1)) is privileged without saying why. That's the docstring an honest derivation walkthrough is supposed to write.

The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the cleanest tool I tested. Axes labeled (Radius kpc, V km/s), legend identifies four curves by color, preamble "What You're Seeing" defines the dark-matter problem qualitatively. The single best touch on the entire site is the inline note: *"Related test result: this curve's underlying assumption was triggered as Failed against the kill threshold."* — that is honest documentation in a single line. The friction: `V_flat` is labeled "calibrated input" without explaining what "calibrated" means, and `a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²` appears without a sentence saying "this is Milgrom's constant, the only free parameter in MOND."

1. Which page is the **canonical** badge-definition page — `/research-philosophy` or `/glossary`? Two pages claim it.
2. Why does the glossary not include `EFE`, `AIC`, `BCS`, `BEC`, `EFT`, `LSS`, `YBCO`, `CSR` when they appear elsewhere on the site? Is there a curation criterion?

### Verdict

Four pages list four different versions of the same badge system; the glossary is not lookup-shaped; "Start Here" still points to a 404 from somewhere; and the Equation Walkthrough is missing the comparison-to-alternatives step that would make it a walkthrough instead of a presentation. None of these are content failures — they're maintenance debt. A focused two-day pass to (a) extract the badge taxonomy into a single component, (b) alphabetize and search-enable the glossary, (c) fix the `/start-here` link, and (d) add the "why this functional form" step to the Equation Walkthrough would raise the site's perceived rigor by a full tier without changing a single scientific claim.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (`/equation-walkthrough`, `/parameter-derivation`, `/honest-assessment`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/research-philosophy`, `/coherence-explorer`, `/a2acw`, `/what-synchronism-is-not`, `/test-catalog`, `/gamma-calculator`)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (the framework is exactly as advertised — and as self-criticized)
- **Would return?**: yes, but for the meta-methodology, not the physics
- **Overall impression**: A framework whose central equation is misclassified by its own front-of-site framing, salvaged by a back-of-site self-audit that is more honest than 95% of academic-physics sites I read. The site's actual contribution is its self-audit infrastructure; the physics is the substrate that contribution was forged on.

### Journey

I came in skeptical and went straight to `/equation-walkthrough` and `/parameter-derivation`. The walkthrough page is client-side stepped, so deep-linking to `?step=4` doesn't bypass the state and a non-JS fetcher only sees step 1 — friction I'd flag for any colleague trying to share the page. What I could see of the walkthrough defined symbols cleanly (dimmer-switch for tanh, log-compression for ln) but never asked the obvious question for a grad student: *why this functional form rather than a Hill function, a logistic, a Naka–Rushton, or an erf?* These all live in the same compander family. Without that comparison, "walkthrough" is a misnomer.

The `/parameter-derivation` page was the most surprising read. It explicitly disavows the central derivation:

> "The 1/√Ncorr scaling borrows from central-limit-theorem (CLT) statistics… but is not rigorously derived. Ncorr is by construction the count of _correlated_ degrees of freedom — exactly the regime where the iid hypothesis fails and 1/√N does not apply."

The factor of 2 is "phase-space dimensionality arguments (6D to 3 effective) [that] introduce temperature- and mass-dependent factors, not a clean factor of 2." `ρ_crit = A·V²flat` with `A ≈ 0.029`, calibrated to the Jeans criterion ratio. So γ is a back-fitted ansatz, ρ_crit is a calibration to observable V_flat, and the framework knows it. Fine — but that means the framing "parameter derivation" overclaims what the page actually does, which is **smooth empirical γ values into a memorable form**. Pedagogically valuable; not a derivation.

The Coherence Explorer was the most physically diagnostic visit. I asked where C = 0.5 sits for γ = 2 and the page itself replies: ρ ≈ 0.32·ρ_crit, with C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88. The page volunteers that *"ρ_crit is a saturation knee, not a critical density in the phase-transition sense,"* and that the "+1" regulator breaks sigmoid symmetry. This is the diagnostic crux of the whole framework for me. **If ρ_crit is not where the order parameter is half-saturated, then C(ρ) is not an order parameter — it is a compander (μ-law / Hill / Naka–Rushton / Kubo-susceptibility class).** The "Critical Exponents: 2× off — undermines Landau framing" finding on `/honest-assessment` is therefore not a calibration miss but a **category error**: you can't get Landau critical exponents from a sensory-compression curve. The site has correctly diagnosed both pieces but has not (front-of-site) connected them. Once you see C(ρ) as a compander, the cross-scale "unification" thins out — it's *use the same compander shape and back-fit γ per regime*, which is a function family choice, not a unification.

`/gamma-calculator` confirmed the back-fit reading. Preset Ncorr values: ideal gas 1, liquid water 4, enzyme site 30, ferromagnet 100, BCS superconductor 10⁷, BEC 10⁶. The page explicitly says these are *"back-fits, not measurements"* — i.e. the N_corr value is whatever number you need to put under the square root to land at the empirically plausible γ. Liquid water at N_corr = 4? Per hydrogen-bond shell? BCS at 10⁷? Per Cooper-pair coherence volume? The framework owes a definition of "what counts as N_corr in system X" before the calculator teaches a fictional mapping; what it currently teaches is inverse-fitting wearing the costume of forward-prediction.

`/honest-assessment` and `/tier-1-existing` were where I started writing fewer notes. The numbers:

- 0 Validated, 0 Strongly Supported, 6 Reparametrization, 6 Failed.
- "6 of 6 'Validated' badges audited to date have been demoted to Reparametrization on closer review." That's a falsification rate of 1.0 for the site's own initial classification — extraordinary for any research site to publish about itself.
- Tier 1: TEST-03 failed (R² = 0.14), TEST-04 withdrawn (contradicts Session 107), TEST-04a failed by **sign reversal** (predicted suppression, observed enhancement), TEST-07 ejected from Tier 1 (no amplitude, no mechanism, not falsifiable), TEST-09 MOND-shared, TEST-10 MOND-shared. The RAR transition-shape test — the *one* non-degenerate galaxy-scale discriminator — was executed against SPARC and got ΔBIC = +184 against γ = 2; free-γ collapses to γ ≈ 0.49 which **is MOND's μ-function under change of variable**.
- "Net discriminating galaxy tests vs MOND: 0, by execution." The site states this in plain prose. That is the only honest verdict.

TEST-04a is the most physically interesting result on the site, and ironically it is the one labeled Failed. A **sign-reversed mechanism-class failure** is a publishable result: it rules out the entire family of *coherence-modulated growth-suppression* mechanisms (anything that adds a suppression term to fσ₈), not just Synchronism's specific parametrization. The site notes this internally; it should claim it externally. This is the kind of transferable falsification that arXiv readers actually want.

`/a2acw` was where I stopped being able to suspend disbelief about the methodology and started taking it seriously. The page admits: *"A2ACW is a reparametrization detector, not a discovery engine. Two AI models trained on the same physics corpus share the same blind spots."* Temporal-asymmetry test: 0/6 catches. Vocabulary-asymmetry test: 4/6 catches when claims were pre-translated to modern notation. So what they have is a **vocabulary-mismatch retrieval aid**, not a novelty detector. That is still useful — a tool that surfaces "this looks like Breit-Wigner under a different name" or "this is Abrikosov–Gor'kov efficiency rebadged as η" earns its place in any research pipeline that uses LLMs as collaborators. But calling it a "detector" was the wrong word.

`/what-synchronism-is-not` does the work I'd want it to do *philosophically* — it acknowledges MOND's 40-year track record, doesn't claim to displace ΛCDM, and concedes that quantum results are "reparametrizations: the decoherence formula… is the textbook correlated-dephasing variance." The positioning is then "ontological reframe, not relabeling." For a grad student this is the page that needs the most work: **an ontology that doesn't change any equation is interpretation, not physics.** Either there is some equation, regime, or measurable that Synchronism predicts and MOND+ΛCDM+decoherence doesn't (in which case show it), or the contribution is methodological (in which case lead with that). The current middle position — "different ontology, same equations, no novel prediction" — is the position of an interpretation of quantum mechanics, and those are evaluated by parsimony and explanatory taste rather than empirical test. Fine to be there, but the site should know it's there.

### Unanswered Questions
