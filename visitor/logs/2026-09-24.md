## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /start-here → /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-curve-plotter)

### Journey
I came over from Reddit. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", is exactly the kind of thing I click on. The next line is "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." My honest first reaction was "...so why am I here?" The page does answer that ("the autopsy is the product"; "a theory that publishes its losses in a form you can cite has given you something; one that publishes only wins has given you a press release"). I liked that line a lot. It's the most Veritasium-ish moment on the site.

Scrolling down the landing page, I kept running into text that seems written for the site's editors, not for me. Right after the orientation box there's a visible note: "(moved up here 2026-09-18 — a first-time visitor reached both only on their eleventh page, and said they changed the meaning of everything before them)". Further down, the "Refutations executed: 6" line comes with a long explanation of "5 roots" versus "5 on external data + 1 Bell/CHSH construction check", plus a note that "the two '5's are different groupings of the same tests". The page itself says "in plain words: we ran six tests that could have shown it wrong, and it lost all six". That's all I needed, and I skimmed everything after it. The raw page has about 14 dated notes that are visible outside collapsed boxes. The collapsed "Revision notes for this cell" boxes are fine because I can ignore them. The inline ones aren't.

The refutations table (BTFR slope, ΔBIC +184, r² = 0.0001, 8.7σ–17.95σ, Bell/CHSH) meant nothing to me. I only understood the word "Failed".

I clicked "Start Here", which took me to /why-synchronism, labelled "Step 1 of 6". It was a bit odd that the button says Start Here but the page is called "Why Synchronism?". "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a good hook. But on the first screen I already met "density function", "MOND" and "compander" before any of them were explained.

**First Encounter** (step 2) was the best page for me. The marching band vs. wandering crowd picture made sense right away, and so did "presence = how packed things are" and the MRH as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter". The page explains tanh in plain words: "an S-shaped curve, like a dimmer switch — flat at first, then rising, then levelling off near 1". I appreciated that; otherwise the equation would have scared me off. "ln" is never explained. I just treated it as "some math that squishes big numbers". Section G ("Melting points: 53% error… Superconductor Tc: 6.5× wrong") was refreshingly blunt.

**Core Idea** (step 3) starts well ("In plain words. This page is about one equation…"). After that it gets dense fast: γ, N_corr, SPARC, BTFR, and corrections dropped in mid-text. It felt about twice as long as I wanted. The sidebar/nav still lists "The γ Parameter — γ = 2/√N_corr: why 2, why √N", as if it were current, but First Encounter had just told me that formula "did not survive testing". I couldn't tell whether γ = 2/√N_corr is still the theory or a dead idea.

**Honest Assessment**: the first sentence is clear ("The one equation did not hold as stated… 0 predictions confirmed"). Straight after it comes "(a count under review: two of the six are convention-dependent, and not all six are independent)". So even the scoreboard is under review? Past the opening I was lost: ΔBIC, RAR, kill criteria. I bounced off it.

**Glossary**: I looked up MOND, tanh and dark matter. All three definitions are short and clear. The MOND entry ("gravity behaves differently at very low accelerations") finally told me what MOND is. It gets mentioned on almost every page, so a hover-definition on first use would have saved me a trip. The 93 terms have alphabet links but no search box.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked. Intuitive: two sliders (sharpness γ, default 0.49; reference density ρ_crit, default 1.00) and a log/linear toggle. Readouts: C(ρ_crit) = 0.3272, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.8259, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.9785. Dragging γ up makes the switch steeper. The "pan control" description of ρ_crit was helpful. I didn't know what γ = 0.49 means physically, or why this isn't the γ = 2 from the galaxy tool.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked. Mostly intuitive, thanks to the legend. The gray line (visible matter) sits below the dots (what stars actually do), which finally showed me the dark-matter puzzle visually. As a layperson, five curves (gray, dots, violet, amber, green MOND) is a lot to take in. I'd like the option to turn curves on one at a time.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation failed, is anything from it still "alive"? Is there one thing it still might be right about?
2. Which γ is the real one: 2, 0.49, or 2/√N_corr?
3. Who is dp, and what does "AI agents arguing with each other" actually look like? Can I read one of those arguments?
4. Why is a single density number supposed to matter for both atoms and galaxies? I followed the analogy, but not why anyone would expect it.

### Verdict
I came for "one equation for everything" and stayed a bit longer because the site is unusually honest about losing. First Encounter and the Galaxy Curve Plotter are genuinely good for someone like me. They're where I learned what dark matter is supposed to explain, and why this idea didn't manage it. The landing page, though, is cluttered with the site's own edit history and count-bookkeeping. A casual reader needs one clear sentence per number, not the audit trail.

### Journey
**Landing (~4,500 words of visible text).** The header nav has 9 items. "Start Here" points to /why-synchronism, and the footer adds Key Claims and First Encounter, which are not in the header. The badge legend line is well built: "Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed · Audited-Negative — text after a dash inside a badge … is a finding descriptor, not a separate badge". It links to the canonical definitions. Only 5 of the 9 formal badges are listed, but it says "used below", so that's fair. Acronym discipline is uneven. MOND, MRH, A2ACW and BAO are expanded on first use. SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, CHSH, ΛCDM, LLM, ALFALFA/SDSS, fσ₈, g_bar/g_obs/a₀ and Ω_m/Ω_b all appear in the refutation table and scorecard with no expansion and no hover. The landing page carries **zero** glossary hover-terms (confirmed in the raw HTML: no `tooltip-` targets). There are 7 plain `/glossary#…` links.

The landing page also contradicts itself on the equation. The "No physics background?" box says "the one equation does appear, but every step explains it in plain words". About 2,000 characters later, "the Beginner Path never asks you to touch it". Both can be true in spirit, but a reader deciding whether to start the path gets two different promises.

**Breadcrumbs.** Every Beginner Path page shows `Home / Start Here / <Page>`. The middle "Start Here" is deliberately unlinked; its title attribute reads "Site section: 'Start Here' (no index page; see Learning Paths)". Yet "Start Here" in the header nav *is* a link, to /why-synchronism. So one label means a page in the nav and a non-page section in the breadcrumb. /honest-assessment and /key-claims also sit under "Start Here" in the breadcrumb, even though Honest Assessment is equally Methodology Track step 6 (its path box shows both memberships, which is nice). The prev/next learning-path nav works on every step I checked: Step 1 → Next only, Steps 4–5 → Prev and Next, Step 6 → "Final step".

**Learning Paths.** There are 7 paths: 3 by difficulty and 4 by topic. It says outright "Advanced is the only path that covers the derivation chain; topic tracks are entry points". That's a good sentence. Per-step times exist, but they are optimistic against the visible text. Core Idea is "~8 min" for ~2,700 visible words (≈11–12 min at 230 wpm), and What Synchronism Is Not is "~5 min" for ~2,100 (≈9 min). **Honest Assessment: the badge legend.** It's collapsed in a `<details>` titled "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)", and it's genuinely good. It defines the two-part badge contract (formal status + free-text descriptor), two families (5 MRH-relationship + 4 descriptive = the "nine formal badges" the glossary mentions, a count that checks out), deprecated labels, and operational states. Every badge on every page I checked has a `title` tooltip and click-through to this legend. No deprecated "Validated"/"Supported" badge rendered on any of the 11 pages. **Glossary.** It has 93 terms with alphabet jump-links, explains the two badge families at the top, and marks the nine badges "(badge)". ### Verdict

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "an interesting AI project". I expected a crank ToE and got a failure log. The landing page gives the central object up front: **C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))**, then "0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations executed". Fine. I went straight to the math.

**/coherence-function: I checked every identity on the page.**
- The key identity tanh(γ ln u) = (u^{2γ} − 1)/(u^{2γ} + 1), with u = 1 + x, is correct (write tanh via exponentials). So the "ln" really is cosmetic: this is a Hill-type rational function in (1+x). I appreciated that the page says so rather than dressing up the log.
- Small-x: C ≈ γx ✓. Large-x: C ≈ 1 − 2(1+x)^{−2γ} ✓.
- dC/dx = γ(1 − C²)/(1 + x) ✓. It's maximal at x = 0 and decreases monotonically ✓.
- "Concave on x ≥ 0, no inflection" ✓. The Hill inflection sits at u^{2γ} = (2γ−1)/(2γ+1) < 1, which is x < 0, even at γ = 2.
- So there is no "transition" in x at all. It's only sigmoidal in log x. The page says exactly this, and it also retracts the old "maximum curvature at γ ≈ 1" story on /gamma-boundary. Good.
- The γ = ½ collapse is exact: C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), so a₀ → 2a₀′. It's consistent with TEST-25's profiled a₀′ = 5.33×10⁻¹¹ (2a₀′ = 1.07×10⁻¹⁰).
- The page also gets the division wiring at γ = ½ right: g_bar/C = g_bar + 2a₀, a constant added acceleration, not MOND. That's a nice catch that most people would miss. The site correctly says MOND equivalence holds only in the μ-form keyed on g_obs.

**Form-selection table.** The raw rows are ν-δ −1.8, Hill n=1 pinned −0.7, erf-log +1.5, Hill free n +5.9, tanh-log free γ +7.1, and γ = 2 pinned +184. My checks:
- tanh-log (+7.1) minus Hill n=1 (−0.7) = 7.8, against ln 2807 = 7.94 ✓. That's what you'd expect, since they're the same curve at γ = ½.
- The +7.1 vs McGaugh with Δχ² ≈ −0.84: −0.84 + 7.94 = 7.1 ✓. The Honest Assessment confirms a₀ is floated in both arms.

So "tanh finishes last of the four" is literally true for free γ. But the same function at its γ = ½ point is the *second-best row on the table*. The framework's shape isn't disfavored; freeing γ just buys nothing. I'd word that differently (see friction).

Something bigger bothered me: every ΔBIC here uses N = 2807 points, as if they were independent. Two other places on the site admit they aren't, and they use two *different* effective-N conventions on the same SPARC points:
- /galaxy-rotation: "effective N ≈ 500–1000: ΔBIC ≈ 33" for the +184 row, a factor of ~5.6.
- /core-idea: "+2843 (+142 after allowing for neighbouring points … not being independent)", a factor of 20, i.e. N_eff ≈ 140, roughly one per galaxy.

Both still reject decisively, so no verdict changes. But the form-selection ranking (a spread of ≤ 8.9) is within noise under either convention, and the penalty term ln N changes too. Pick one N_eff and apply it to the whole table.
