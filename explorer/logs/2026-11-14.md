# Explorer Session — 2026-11-14

## WAKE
1. **Inherited assumptions.** The maintainer's two new topics both take the 11-13 result as given: the galaxy sector is
   MOND in shape, and the framework's content lies in its commitments (floor/cap, a₀ lock, knee). The census topic
   assumes "baryon census" is one kind of measurement. It is not. For galaxies it is a rotation velocity extrapolated to
   R₅₀₀ through an NFW factor. For groups and clusters it is X-ray gas in hydrostatic balance, measured inside the radius
   it is quoted at. The persona and the maintainer each treated the census as a single object, one to accept and one to
   doubt.
2. **If the frame is wrong.** The site's joint sentence says "at the galaxy scale the cap family is excluded below about
   5.7 by rotation curves, and above that only conditionally (lensing, light rule)". It confines the cap test to
   galaxies. The cap B ≤ Ω_m/Ω_b is a claim about every bound system. Galaxies may be the wrong scale to ask at, because
   rotation curves end before the boost saturates. In X-ray groups the hot gas traces the potential that moves matter,
   so no light rule enters. If groups are more baryon-poor than cosmic *inside the measured radius*, the conditional
   becomes unconditional at a different scale.
3. **Most information per hour.** Split the census into its two legs. (a) Show with a positive control that the galaxy
   leg cannot see the cap. (b) Read the per-system hydrostatic baryon fractions of groups and clusters off the papers,
   convert each to B = 1/f_b(<r), and see whether any class clears 6.39 at 3σ after its escapes. The topic asks a yes/no
   question, and the answer is likely "the persona's citation is the wrong one, but the persona's conclusion holds".
4. **What would falsify the posture.** (a) Group hydrostatic f_b(<r₅₀₀) ≥ 0.157 within errors, so no class is more
   baryon-poor than cosmic: then the cap at 6.39 survives everything except lensing, and the site's conditional sentence
   is exactly right. (b) The group leg turns out to be extrapolated beyond detected emission too: then the census leg is
   conditional for groups as well.

## Key Findings
- `baryon-census-galaxy-leg-cannot-see-the-cap-cluster-cores-reach-6p39-without-a-light-rule.md`
  - The galaxy census extrapolates V_c to R₅₀₀ with an NFW factor. A universe that obeys the 6.39 cap reproduces it
    (f_d 0.12–0.50), so it cannot test the cap.
  - Hydrostatic X-ray needs no light rule. Massive clusters sit on the cap at R₅₀₀ (X-COP 6.41 ± 0.29). Groups give
    6.4–10.5, depending on the stellar census.
  - Cluster cores at R₂₅₀₀ give B = 7.0–11.2 (Eckert+22) and 7.8 (Allen+08). That is above the cap at face value; the
    ensemble is 1.4σ after the calibration systematic, and A644 (11.2) survives stacked systematics.
  - The registered group kill was not met (P5 failed). The R₂₅₀₀ reading is post-hoc.
- `striction-at-the-archive-knee-radial-is-percent-level-vertical-is-order-gravity-and-compressive.md`
  - Radial striction is 0.5–1.6% of gravity at the median, at most 26–51%. It is never outward-dominant (S1–S4 held).
    The identity control reproduces 10-13 exactly.
  - Post-hoc: vertical striction is 9–27% of vertical gravity at the median, compressive (99% weighted), and above half
    of gravity somewhere in 40–110 of 153 discs. So "L2 verdicts stand under L3" holds only at fixed disc thickness.

## Self-Seeded Questions
- Is disc thickness the density-keyed-only observable the program has been looking for? Compressive L3 striction of
  order gravity predicts thinner discs at fixed σ_z than any acceleration-keyed law.
- A cap at exactly Ω_m/Ω_b is saturated by the cosmic fair sample (massive clusters at R₅₀₀). Is any cap below the
  cosmic ratio compatible with ordinary radial baryon structure? Gas less concentrated than total mass makes cores
  exceed the outskirts' B in every model, ΛCDM included.

## So what?
Both topics changed what the site can say, and one opened a new question.
- **The census.** The persona's kill had the right conclusion and the wrong evidence. The galaxy census cannot see the
  cap. Matter dynamics, though, reaches the most permissive cap without the light rule the lensing leg needs. The cap's
  status therefore moves from "only conditionally reached" to "exceeded at face value, systematics-limited".
- **The striction.** "Untested at V^0.5" becomes "small radially, order-unity vertically". The vertical result turns a
  long-carried rabbit hole into a concrete density-keyed prediction: thinner discs. That may be the first observable
  where density keying says something acceleration keying cannot.
- Both are discovery, not documentation. The post-hoc labels mean neither is yet a ledger entry.
