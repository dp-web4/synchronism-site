# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-08-27

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: yes — but for the drama, not the physics
- **Overall impression**: The pitch ("one equation for everything — and we tested it and it lost") is genuinely gripping, but the "Beginner Path" stops being beginner-level halfway through and the one tool I was told to start with contradicts the page that sent me there.

### Journey

Came from a Reddit link. The landing page asks *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — that's a good hook, that's a Veritasium thumbnail. Then about two scrolls down it just... shows me the equation:

> C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is. I know ρ is "rho" because I've seen it in videos. So within 30 seconds the site has told me both "this is for you" and "this is not for you." I nearly bounced. What kept me was the second thing on the page: they say straight out that they ran their tests and *lost*. Six refutations, zero confirmed predictions. I have never seen a physics site lead with that. I kept reading because of the honesty, not the equation.

Clicked **Why Synchronism?** (the "beginners start here" link). The opening analogy is the best writing on the site:

> "a crowd milling around a plaza behaves like independent individuals; a marching band behaves like one organism"

That I get instantly. Density → togetherness. Great. Then within a few paragraphs it's γ, ρcrit, "ansatz," "Hamiltonian," and something being "audited-negative," and I'm out of my depth again. The good analogy and the wall are about 400 words apart.

Went to **Learning Paths** → the 6-step Beginner Path, ~30 minutes, "no physics background needed."

- **Step 1–2 (Why / First Encounter)**: fine. Actually good. First Encounter is nicely paced ("7 short pages, ~90 seconds each"), the marching band comes back, and MRH is explained as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" — I understood that on the first read. But there's not a single picture or diagram on it. The one page that's purely about intuition is all text.
- **Step 3 (Core Idea)**: this is where the equation gets unpacked, and the tanh-as-dimmer-switch explanation actually works. But then I hit "Hill coefficient," "Naka–Rushton curves," and "Hill-type rational saturation function" in a page marked beginner. And a warning that some constant "A" is "off by ~600× from the value in use" — I don't know what A is yet, so all this tells me is "something here is broken by a lot."
- **Step 4 (What Synchronism Is Not)**: assumes I know MOND, ΛCDM, QFT, decoherence, and the CMB. I know none of these as more than words. This page is step 4 of a path that promised no physics background.
- **Step 5 (Honest Assessment)**: standard deviations, r² values, kill thresholds. The "in plain terms" boxes are a real kindness and I understood roughly half. The line **"As a theory of galaxies: fatal, by its own scoreboard"** is the most memorable sentence on the entire site.
- **Step 6 (Glossary)**: 72 terms and no search box. I scrolled.

So: two and a half of the six beginner steps are actually beginner-level.

Then I went to try the tools, which is what I'd actually come for.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — *worked* — *confusing* — Two sliders: "Transition sharpness (γ)" (0.01–2.0, default 0.49) and "Reference density (ρcrit)" (default 1.00). The curve moves, which is satisfying. Two problems. **First**, there are no presets — nothing that says "this is a galaxy," "this is the air in this room," "this is a neutron star." So I'm dragging abstract Greek letters and watching a squiggle change, and I learn nothing about the actual universe. The glossary told me the *γ Calculator* has presets, but that one is labeled Advanced. The beginner tool is the one without the real-world anchors. **Second**, and worse: the readout says **C(ρcrit) = 0.3272**. But I had *just* read on the Core Idea page that ρcrit is "the saturation knee — where the curve reaches about 0.88." Those are different numbers for what I understood to be the same thing, and nothing on the tool page explains the gap. (I *think* it's because the tool defaults to γ=0.49 and the 0.88 figure assumes some other γ — but a beginner tool shouldn't make me reverse-engineer that.) I genuinely could not tell whether I'd misread the page or the tool was wrong.

- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — *worked* — *intuitive* — **Best thing on the site for me.** Six steps, Previous/Next buttons, one piece of the equation at a time. The ln explanation is excellent: *"Doubling x doesn't double ln(x). A number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger."* That's the first time a logarithm has made sense to me. tanh as a dimmer switch, same. **This should be linked directly from the landing page, right under the equation.** Instead I found it on step 5 of a tools index I reached by accident from the glossary. My only complaint: it also stops to tell me the γ formula is "sign-inverted" against real physics, before I've finished learning what γ is.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — *worked* — *confusing* — The setup is great: pick a galaxy (DDO 154, NGC 3198, etc.), and see that the observed dots stay flat when the "visible matter only" line drops. That IS the dark matter mystery and I finally understood it from a picture. But there are **five** overlaid curves, and one of them is described as a "hand-tuned illustrative stand-in (not computed from theory)." There's a fake line on the chart. I could not confidently tell which line was the actual thing being tested. The conclusion — "the real curve stays on the baryon line — the knee is never crossed," i.e. their theory does nothing here — is clear enough, but I got it from the text, not the graph.

- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (`/consciousness-demo`) — *worked* — *clear* — I clicked this because "consciousness" is the most clickable word on the site. And then the page spends its whole length explaining why its own headline number is meaningless — 8 methods converge on C≈0.50, but "any approach that picks the midpoint of a [0,1)-bounded output range will land near 0.50 by construction." I understood that argument completely and it's kind of a great lesson in how coincidences fool people. But as a *demo* it's odd: it's an exhibit of a mistake, labeled as a tool.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is C(ρcrit) 0.88 or 0.33? Two pages of the same site told me different things and I have no way to adjudicate.
2. What is a "reference density" of 1.00 — one what, per what? Every physical quantity I know has units.
3. If the galaxy test "collapsed onto MOND," does that mean Synchronism is *wrong*, or that it's *the same as* a theory that already exists? Those feel very different and I couldn't tell which one the site means. (I think the second, which is somehow more interesting?)
4. The site says 6 refutations and 0 confirmations. So — is this over? Is there something they still expect to work, or am I reading an obituary? The landing page pitches it in the present tense and the Honest Assessment reads like a post-mortem.
5. Who is doing this? I saw "3,308 AI-to-AI peer review sessions" mentioned and didn't know what to make of that at all — is this research by people, or by AIs?

### Verdict

The honesty is the product. I came for "one equation explains everything" and stayed for "we tested it and it lost, here's the scoreboard" — that's more compelling than any confident claim would have been, and I'd send this to a friend on that basis alone. But the Beginner Path only stays beginner for about half its length, and the one tool aimed at me has no real-world presets and prints a number that contradicts the page that sent me there. The best beginner asset on the whole site, the Equation Anatomy walkthrough, is hidden three clicks off the path — promote it and put the analogy above the equation on the landing page, and this would go from "impressive but not for me" to genuinely accessible.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13 (+1 redirect probe)
- **Understanding achieved**: good — of the structure; the content is legible where it's written for a reader and opaque where it's written for the archive
- **Would return?**: yes — I'd return to audit it again, which is not the same as saying I'd return to read it
- **Overall impression**: The prose is unusually clear for this subject matter, but the site runs **two parallel information architectures with non-overlapping vocabulary** (a flat 9-item nav and a 5-section breadcrumb tree that the nav never shows), and its badge system has grown to roughly thirty labels across four taxonomies whose only complete legend is buried inside a results page.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a rewrite: land, map the nav, walk one path end to end, then cross-check terminology and reference material against point-of-use.

**The nav.** Nine items: `Synchronism · Start Here · Learning Paths · Tier 1: Existing Data · Test Roadmap · Tools · Glossary · Research Philosophy · Honest Assessment`. This mixes three different object types in one row — sections (`Tools`, `Glossary`), individual leaf pages (`Honest Assessment`, `Research Philosophy`), and one item with a colon in it (`Tier 1: Existing Data`, the only label with subordinate punctuation). There is no breadcrumb on the landing page, so the nav is the reader's entire mental model of the site at the moment they most need one.

**Then I clicked into a page and the model changed.** Every interior page carries a breadcrumb, and the breadcrumbs reveal a completely different taxonomy:

| Page | Breadcrumb section |
|---|---|
| `/why-synchronism`, `/first-encounter`, `/core-idea`, `/honest-assessment`, `/glossary` | **Getting Started** |
| `/coherence-function` | **Core Theory** |
| `/research-philosophy` | **Methodology** |
| `/coherence-explorer`, `/gamma-calculator`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer` | **Interactive Tools** |
| `/test-catalog` | **Predictions & Experiments** |

Five sections. Four of the five names — Getting Started, Core Theory, Methodology, Predictions & Experiments — **appear nowhere in the navigation**. The fifth is a near-miss: nav says `Tools`, breadcrumb says `Interactive Tools`, page `<h1>` says "Interactive Tools." So the breadcrumbs are describing a hierarchy the reader has no way to browse, and the nav is describing a flat list that the breadcrumbs contradict. I probed `/getting-started` directly to see whether the middle crumb resolves to a section landing page; it returns **HTTP 307**, not a page.

This is the single highest-leverage fix on the site and it's a nav-config change, not a writing job: adopt the breadcrumb taxonomy as the nav, and the site instantly acquires a shape.

**Three names for the front door.** The nav item is `Start Here`. The breadcrumb section is `Getting Started`. The page it lands on is `/why-synchronism`, whose `<h1>` is **"Start Here: Why Synchronism?"** and whose Learning-Paths label is **"Why Synchronism?"**. Four labels, one destination. I had to click to find out that `Start Here` and `Why Synchronism?` were the same page — which means the Learning Paths page is silently telling readers to begin at a page they may have already read.

**Walking the Beginner Path.** Six steps, clean "Next →" affordances at both top and bottom of `/why-synchronism` and `/first-encounter`, and `/first-encounter` has a progress bar with direct step-jumping. That is genuinely good sequential design — better than most docs sets I audit. `/core-idea` ends with a five-way "Choose Your Path" fork rather than a single "Next," which breaks the linear promise mid-path, but the fork is well-labeled.

**Then the terminology broke.** This is my main content finding and it happens at the step-1 → step-2 seam:

- `/why-synchronism` (Beginner step 1): ρ is **density** — *"what makes a system sparse or dense is ρ, the other input."*
- `/first-encounter` (Beginner step 2): the very first sub-page is titled **"Presence (ρ)"**, glossed as *"how much stuff is packed into a given space."*
- `/coherence-function` (Intermediate step 1): ρ is **presence** — *"compatible structural elements within the system's MRH. **Physical density is one form of presence.**"*
- `/glossary` and `/coherence-explorer`: ρcrit is **"Reference density."**
- Landing page: **ρ (presence)**.

So the term changes between consecutive steps of a path, and the *generalization* — that density is a special case of a broader primitive — arrives at the intermediate tier with no marker that it's a redefinition. A reader who learned "density" in step 1 has no signal in step 2 that "Presence" is the same slot, and no signal at `/coherence-function` that the thing they learned is now a subset. **Say the generalization out loud once**, in step 2: "We'll call this *presence* rather than density, because later it will mean more than mass per volume." One sentence closes it.

It also explains a units problem: because ρ is a generalized *presence*, ρcrit's default of "1.00" in the Coherence Explorer is dimensionless by construction — but no page says so at the point where the reader is staring at an unlabeled 1.00.

**The badge system.** This is where the site's discipline has outrun its documentation. Counting from `/honest-assessment`, which the other pages call the canonical legend:

- **5** MRH-relationship tags (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative)
- **4** descriptive tags (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed)
- **2** deprecated (Validated, Strongly Supported)
- **~17** "operational states" — including Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson, Model Explainer, EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, Ceiling Exceeded, Sign Correction, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, MOND-shared (RETIRED), **89% Boundary-Consistent**, **Template Bias Caveat**
- plus **Post-diction**, explicitly "an amber inline label, not a separate badge tier"

That is on the order of **thirty distinct labels in four-and-a-half taxonomies**. Two of the "operational states" aren't states at all — "89% Boundary-Consistent" is a statistic and "Template Bias Caveat" is a footnote; they're in the legend because they render as chips, not because they belong to the same category as "CLOSED." The vocabulary has absorbed anything that ever appeared in a colored box.

Three structural problems follow:

1. **The canonical legend lives in the wrong genre of page.** `/honest-assessment` is a *results* page — its other sections are "The Verdict," "What Failed," "Bottom Line." Reference material is section 1 of a narrative document, and that document is Beginner Path **step 5**. Every page that renders a badge before step 5 (the landing page, `/core-idea`, `/interactive-tools`, `/coherence-function`) is using vocabulary its reader has not been given. Badges belong on their own reference page, linked from the badge itself.
2. **The Glossary is not the single source of truth it advertises.** Its intro promises "validation-badge terms cluster as a group," but it carried definitions for only a handful (Active-MRH, Audited-Negative, Reparametrization) and **has no entry for "Model Explainer"** — which is the most-used badge on the Tools page, appearing on 4 of 7 tools. A reader who does the sensible thing and looks the badge up in the glossary comes back empty.
3. **One visual slot carries two orthogonal axes.** On `/interactive-tools`, "Model Explainer" (a content *type*) sits in the same chip position as "Reparametrization" and "Speculative" (epistemic *verdicts*). The page's intro paragraph explicitly warns about this — which is the tell: prose is being used to repair a design decision. Two axes need two visual treatments, not a disclaimer.

Credit where due: `/test-catalog` **does** carry an inline "Validation Badge Guide" with a link to canonical definitions. That pattern is correct and should be copied to every page that renders a badge.

**A page-level label contradicted by its own contents.** `/test-catalog` is titled "Test Roadmap" (good — it matches the nav label exactly, the one place labels do agree) with the subtitle **"Untested — 24 Proposed Experiments."** But the page itself lists TEST-25 (added 2026-07-28) and TEST-26 (proposed 2026-08-10) — so the count is 26, not 24 — and its own status column uses the **Failed** badge, while `/honest-assessment` cites TEST-25 as one of the six *executed* refutations. A blanket "Untested" banner over a table containing executed and failed rows is a stale header, and it's the kind that erodes trust in every other number on the page. Drop the hard-coded count from the subtitle; derive it, or write "20+".

**Cross-reference maintenance.** `/interactive-tools` describes the Galaxy Curve Plotter as showing "**four** curves" and enumerates them (Newtonian dashed, observed dots, Synchronism violet, MOND green). Pass 1 reports counting **five** on the tool itself, one of them a "hand-tuned illustrative stand-in." I did not open the plotter this pass, so I'm flagging the discrepancy rather than asserting it — but the underlying issue stands regardless of who's right: **index copy should not enumerate a tool's internals.** It will drift, and it apparently has.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — *worked* — *well-built, but its headline output is a label the page disowns* — Clean single-input design: `Ncorr` with a linear (1–1000) / log (1–10⁷) toggle and seven presets (ideal gas, liquid water, galaxy—SPARC best fit, enzyme site, ferromagnet, BEC, BCS superconductor). Two things I'd fix. **(1) The subtitle is "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems," and the readout still prints a regime classification — at the default Ncorr=4 it reads γ = 1.0000, "Boundary (γ ≈ 1) — *a label per the inverted formula*."** The hedge is inside the output string. That's the right instinct in the wrong place: if the classification is known-inverted, the readout should show it struck through or greyed with the correction adjacent, not annotated in a parenthetical the reader must parse mid-number. **(2) Documentation win worth copying sitewide:** the page states plainly that *"Preset Ncorr values are back-fits, not measurements"* and that *"No protocol exists for converting a system's Hamiltonian into Ncorr without first fitting γ to observed behavior."* That is the clearest statement of a limitation anywhere on the site, and it answers the obvious reader question — *where do I get this number?* — before it's asked. Every input control on the site should be documented to that standard.

- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — *worked* — *confusing as an interface, sound as an exhibit* — Subtitle: *"Despite the name: C(ρ) is analytic everywhere — a smooth crossover, not a thermodynamic phase transition."* **The page's first act is to retract its own title.** If the name is wrong, rename the tool — "Regime Boundary Explorer," or anything that isn't contradicted in the next line. Interaction problems: the single slider's label rendered to me as the run-together string **"CollectiveBoundarySingle-particle"** — three axis labels concatenated with no separators, which reads as one nonsense word. And the instruction is *"drag the slider to catch the formula being wrong at every stop,"* which is an unusual thing to ask of a user: there is no success state, no reading to take away, and no way to tell when you're done. The "Reality check" line inside each regime card is the actual payload and does its job well. **This would work better as a static two-column table — what the formula says vs. what physics says — than as a slider.** The interactivity adds motion, not understanding.

Both tools carry the "Artifact Lesson" tag, which is an honest and unusual category. But `Artifact Lesson` is defined only in the 17-item operational-states list on `/honest-assessment` — the tag appears on `/interactive-tools` with no gloss and no link.

### Journey

Went straight past the onboarding to `/coherence-function`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/galaxy-rotation`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/honest-assessment`, `/key-claims`, `/for-researchers`, `/mrh`, `/research-philosophy`, and the two tools. I checked every derivation I could reproduce with pen and paper.

**What checks out.** More than I expected. I verified independently:

- `tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]` — correct; e^{γln(1+x)} = (1+x)^γ, and the rest is the definition of tanh. The claim that "the ln is cosmetic" is right: this is a two-parameter Hill function, full stop.
- `dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x)` — correct, and the consequence they draw is the important one. It is *maximal at x=0* and decreases monotonically, so **C is concave on the whole domain: there is no inflection point, no critical point, no "phase boundary."** Every "maximum curvature at γ≈1" or "transition sharpens at the knee" claim is void by two lines of calculus, and the site says so. Good.
- γ=1/2 → `C = x/(x+2)`. Correct. (Small nit: this is μ_simple(x/2), not μ_simple(x). Identical only after absorbing a factor 2 into ρcrit — legitimate since ρcrit is free, but the page prints it as literal identity.)
- Asymptotics `C ≈ γx` and `C → 1 − 2(1+x)^{−2γ}` — both correct.
- The degeneracy-breaking term `γ(2γ−1)` vanishing exactly at γ=1/2 — correct; expanding tanh(γ ln(1+x)) to O(x²) gives γx − (γ/2)(1−2γ)x², so at γ=1/2 the second order collapses to the γx/(1+γx) form and γ, ρcrit become degenerate. That is a genuinely subtle observation and it's right.
- `a₀ = cH₀/2π` = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ with H₀=67.4 — correct, and 13% below 1.20×10⁻¹⁰ — correct.
- `Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG)` = 119 M☉/pc² — correct to three digits, and correctly flagged as *not* an independent derivation. Deriving Freeman's law from Σ₀ after importing a₀ is Milgrom (1983). The page says exactly that. Credit.
- `ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y)` is the correct inverse of simple-μ. Exponential-disk enclosed mass `M(<r) = M_d[1 − e^{−r/R_d}(1+r/R_d)]` — correct. `M_b = 47 V⁴` is McGaugh (2011) — correct.
- **The compander-vs-order-parameter argument is the best physics on the site.** Ising's `m = tanh(βJzm)` is *implicit* — m appears on both sides, the fixed point is what produces the transition. C(ρ) is *evaluative* — input goes in, output comes out, no self-consistency loop. Therefore no spontaneous symmetry breaking, no universality class, no critical exponents. That distinction is correct, load-bearing, and routinely botched by people writing "tanh ⇒ phase transition." Whoever wrote that paragraph knows statistical mechanics.

So the site is not crankery. Which is exactly why the errors below matter.

**Where it breaks.** My headline finding is on `/parameter-derivations`, and it runs *against* the site's own conclusion.

That page carries both of these:

> A = 4π/(βJ² · G · R₀²)   and   R₀ = V²/(3a₀)

If R₀ is the same object in both — and the page gives no indication it isn't — then substitute:

    ρcrit = A·V² = 4πV²/(βJ²·G·R₀²) = 4π·9a₀²·V²/(βJ²·G·V⁴) = 36π a₀²/(βJ²·G·V²)  ∝  V⁻²

**The framework's own chain gives ρcrit ∝ V⁻² — the MOND-required sign.** The asserted ρcrit ∝ V⁺² is what you get by treating R₀ as a universal constant while defining it as V²/(3a₀) two rows earlier.

This is not a small thing, because "the framework asserts ρcrit ∝ V⁺², inverted sign" is the **one-line citable form of the site's flagship negative result** on `/for-researchers`. The no-go is refuting a bookkeeping slip, not a structural commitment of the model.

And it quantitatively explains the 600× "chain-of-custody failure" the same page can't resolve. Since A ∝ 1/R₀² ∝ V⁻⁴, A is not a constant at all — it's a function of which galaxy you evaluated it on. The two irreconcilable values:

    0.029 / 4.6×10⁻⁵ = 630 = 5.01⁴

A factor of exactly 5 in the assumed rotation velocity — a ~60 km/s dwarf versus a ~300 km/s spiral — reproduces the entire 600× discrepancy to two digits. **The ratio is independent of βJ**, since both numbers come from the same expression. That's checkable in an afternoon and would convert "two mutually incompatible provenances" into one diagnosed error.

Then the two headline statistics.

**ΔBIC = +184 is exactly linear in N, and the site's "conservative" correction is not conservative.** From the page's own RMS values:

    2807 × ln(0.1485²/0.1437²) = 2807 × 0.06572 = 184.5   ✓ reproduces the number exactly

**On the local-density no-go itself.** Note the exact identity for a spherical enclosure:

    ρ̄/g = [3M/4πr³]/[GM/r²] = 3/(4πGr)

Mean enclosed density and baryonic acceleration differ by *exactly one factor of r*. So "keys on ρ instead of g" is precisely "adds a log(size) term to the RAR" — nothing more. Two consequences. (1) The claim that "no prior quantified instance exists in MOND variable-comparison literature" is hard to sustain: the absence of a size/surface-brightness/radius term in RAR residuals is a published null (Lelli, McGaugh, Schombert & Pawlowski 2017, ApJ 836, 152). (2) The "V⁻²" requirement is **estimator-dependent and the page never names the estimator**: mean enclosed at the flat-part radius r=V²/a₀ gives V⁻²; mean enclosed at R_d with R_d ∝ V gives V⁻¹; and the *plotter's own* local midplane estimator Σ/2h, with Milgrom's constant transition surface density, gives V⁰. Given TEST-09 clears its threshold by only 0.9σ, that recomputation could plausibly move it under the kill line. Two of the six refutations hang on this.

**MRH is an unbadged reparametrization.** `/mrh` gives predictive sufficiency + predictive closure + P(X_fut | X_MRH, X_ext) = P(X_fut | X_MRH). That is the definition of a **Markov blanket** (Pearl 1988), and the coarse-graining version is the relevant/irrelevant operator split of an RG shell. The site badges the Born rule, Σ₀, a₀, and Γ=γ²(1−c) as Reparametrization — correctly — but MRH gets "Speculative — Theoretical Framework." It's the one place the site's own audit standard isn't applied, and MRH is load-bearing (it's inside the definition of ρ).

**Chemistry monotonicity, from a condensed-matter seat.** `/coherence-function` calls the diamond-vs-lead sound-velocity inversion (Spearman = −0.32) "not yet resolved" and offers two options: an undocumented inverse orientation of C, or a violated axiom. It's the second, and it isn't mysterious. Sound speed is √(B/ρ) — set by the elastic modulus, and at fixed bonding, *denser is slower*. Diamond: √(443 GPa/3510) ≈ 11.2 km/s. Lead: √(46 GPa/11340) ≈ 2.0 km/s. Both match observation. There is no puzzle here requiring a new orientation of C; the monotonicity axiom is simply false for solids, because density is not the variable that sets stiffness. That should close the item rather than leave it open.

### Journey

I came for the novelty claim. The site advertises four "citable artifacts that survive the framework being wrong," so I went straight to `/for-researchers`, then to `/galaxy-rotation`, `/honest-assessment`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/test-catalog`, `/key-claims`, `/methodology`, `/a2acw`, and the tools index — and then out to the primary literature to check the prior-art screen.

**The headline finding.** `/honest-assessment` carries a 2026-08-09 correction stating the operative equations are

```
∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ        (field equation)
g = ḡ/C               (spherical solution)
```

with `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` — a monotone-increasing smooth step in the **local volumetric mass density**, with a sharpness parameter and a critical density.

Matsakos & Diaferio, *Dynamics of galaxies and clusters in refracted gravity*, arXiv:1603.04943, write:

> "∇⋅(ϵ∇Φ) = 4πGρ"

with ϵ the **gravitational permittivity**, "a monotonic increasing function of the local mass density," implemented as "a smooth step function of the mass density, with a parameter Q that regulates the steepness of the transition between the two asymptotic limits," and ρc as one of three free parameters.

These are the same equation, the same coupling variable, the same functional ansatz, and the same three-parameter structure: C ↔ ϵ, γ ↔ Q, ρcrit ↔ ρthr. This is not a family resemblance. **The framework's galaxy sector is refracted gravity with the low-density asymptote pinned instead of fitted.**

I searched `/for-researchers`, `/galaxy-rotation`, and `/honest-assessment` for "refracted gravity," "Matsakos," "Diaferio," "Cesare," and "permittivity." **Zero hits on all three pages.** The prior-art section of `/for-researchers` cites Burrage/Copeland/Millington 2017, Burrage et al. 2019, CPSU 2004, Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov 2005, Pospelov & Shang 2012, McGaugh 2004, Lelli et al. 2017, Stiskalek & Desmond 2023. The screen was scoped to *MOND variable-comparison literature* — and refracted gravity is not MOND literature, so a correctly-executed screen under that scope was guaranteed to miss it. The construction, not the community, is what needs screening.

Three consequences follow, and they do not all cut the same way.

**(1) Citable artifact #1 cannot be published as written.** The no-go states: "any gravity modification keyed on the local volumetric density ρ(r) **via algebraic coupling** (C(ρ)·g as a multiplicative scalar on the force ... gradient-based schemes ... and non-local state variables such as enclosed mass are *not* covered) fails in three independently executed ways." Refracted gravity is keyed on local volumetric ρ(r), algebraically — ϵ is a function of ρ alone, not of ∇Φ, so it is not a "gradient-based scheme" in the symmetron sense the caveat excludes. But it lives inside a divergence, so `g = ḡ/C` holds only in spherical symmetry. That is exactly the site's own L2-vs-L3 distinction. So the true scope of the no-go is: *the spherically-symmetric algebraic approximation to a refracted-gravity permittivity fails* — a version the published program does not use, on data (discs) where the approximation is worst. A referee will ask why the class was defined so as to exclude its only successful member, and the answer on the page today is that nobody knew the member existed.

**(2) Two of the six refutations dissolve — the count deflates, not inflates.** `/honest-assessment` says it plainly: "Everything that distinguishes this framework from MOND is a single asserted constant" — the boost ceiling B ≤ 1/Ωm ≈ 3.17. TEST-09 (BTFR slope 3.35 vs 3.75±0.10) and TEST-10 (f_DM ceiling 68.5%, max observed 0.927) are both corollaries of it. But B_max is precisely refracted gravity's ϵ_v, the low-density asymptotic permittivity — "constrained as 0 < ϵ_v < 1" and **fitted**, not asserted; the covariant paper quotes threshold densities in the range 10⁻²⁷–10⁻²⁴ g cm⁻³ and the disc-galaxy fits carry ϵ_v well below Ωm, permitting boosts of tens. Setting ϵ_v = Ωm = 0.315 is a numerological choice with no derivation anywhere on the site (the page concedes B_max is "undeduced"). Under the literature's own fitted ϵ_v, the galaxy needing boost 13.7 passes and the BTFR slope is not pinned at 3.35. **Two refutations are refutations of an unforced parameter choice, not of the model class.** For the class, the honest ledger is 4, not 6.

**(3) The "knee is never crossed" result needs a regulator-aware comparison before it can be called a class result.** The plotter's own numbers give x = ρ/ρcrit ≈ 1.5×10⁻³ for DDO 154, i.e. the boost never engages. Refracted gravity's fitted ρthr sits at 10⁻²⁷–10⁻²⁴ g cm⁻³ and its disc fits *do* engage. I cannot adjudicate this from the site, because **the site never states the map between its ρcrit and RG's ρc** — and the two cannot be compared symbol-to-symbol: this framework's argument carries a "+1" regulator and RG's does not, and one slope is natural-log while the other is base-10, so a naive read of the two numbers is worth nothing. That map is a one-line calculation the site should publish, because the entire "boost never engages" result is downstream of it. (Explicitly *not* claiming a discrepancy here — claiming it is unstated and uncheckable.)

**(4) The covariant completion the site says it cannot find is published.** `/honest-assessment` reports two hand-derived completions: A (Bianchi forces Einstein–de Sitter, dark energy vanishes) and B (C as a Brans–Dicke scalar; 0/192 γ values reach the DESI quadrant). Sanna, Matsakos & Diaferio, *Covariant formulation of refracted gravity* (A&A 2023, arXiv:2109.11217), give a scalar-tensor covariant formulation of exactly ∇·(ϵ∇Φ) = 4πGρ. Whether that completion also fails the DESI quadrant is a live and interesting question — but it should be the one tested, and it should be tested by adopting the published action rather than by re-deriving two candidates from scratch and reporting both dead.

**Now the methodology, which is where I expected to find the actual contribution.** `/a2acw` reports the operating characteristic honestly and in the correct direction:

> "Sensitivity (catch rate on demotions): All 6 demoted claims caught by the combined three-axis protocol."
> "Specificity (genuine discoveries correctly passed): 0/6 passed — all were flagged as potential reparametrizations."

with a Clopper–Pearson interval on the 0/6. Good practice. But the site's stated conclusion — "adversarial AI self-play over a shared corpus is a **reparametrization detector**, not a discovery engine" — is stronger than its own numbers support, in the direction of flattering the method. Sensitivity 1.0 with specificity 0.0 gives Youden's J = 1.0 + 0.0 − 1 = **0**, positive likelihood ratio LR+ = sens/(1−spec) = **1**, AUC = **0.5**. A classifier that says "reparametrization" to everything scores exactly 6/6 and 0/6 too. The measurements are consistent with the constant function and do not distinguish it from a detector. The correct headline is *not* "it's a reparametrization detector" — it is "**on the labels available, the protocol has no measured discriminative power in either direction**."

And the two arms were not held to the same standard. The 0/6 specificity arm is genuinely held-out (Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking, Noether are not in the corpus of framework claims) and carries a confidence interval. The 6/6 sensitivity arm is scored on the same six demoted claims from which the three-axis protocol was constructed — and the site's own temporal-asymmetry audit records that the *original* protocol caught **0 of those same 6**. So the positive-class number is an in-sample refit with zero held-out validation and no interval, while the negative-class number is held-out and interval'd. That asymmetry runs in the direction of the method looking better than it is, which is the one direction this site is usually careful about.

I'd also strike two of the four citable artifacts:

- **Dim-4 radiative LIV** is correctly and completely attributed on the page — CPSU 2004 (PRL 93, 191301) for the result, Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov 2005 and Pospelov & Shang 2012 for the SUSY and scale-hierarchy escapes. That is the right way to cite, and it means the contribution is *applying a 2004 theorem to one framework*, which is not an artifact that survives the framework being wrong — it survived twenty-two years before the framework existed. It also means the honest verdict is a naturalness gap with two known perturbative escapes, not an exclusion.
- **The CHSH substrate test** reports "local construction S = 1.98; nonlocal-grid construction S ≡ 2.00." Obtaining exactly the local-realist bound from a construction labeled *nonlocal* is diagnostic: the simulation satisfied local causality and measurement independence, so Bell's bound applies and S ≤ 2 was guaranteed before the code ran. That is a valid self-consistency check on the simulator and a legitimate internal refutation of the framework's "avoids Bell" claim — the site is right to count it against itself. It is not a citable artifact. To make it one you must name *which* Bell assumption the substrate violates (local causality? measurement independence? no-signaling?) and then show it still cannot reach 2√2.

That leaves one genuinely citable artifact of the four — the local-density no-go — and that one now needs its scope rewritten around refracted gravity.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — *worked* — *arithmetically exact, physically circular* — I checked every preset against γ = 2/√Ncorr: N=1→2.000 ✓, N=4→1.000 ✓, N=17→0.48507 ✓ (shows 0.485), N=30→0.36515 ✓ (0.365), N=100→0.200 ✓, N=10⁶→0.002 ✓, N=10⁷→6.3246×10⁻⁴ ✓ (6.32×10⁻⁴). (1) The site flags the tool as an "artifact lesson" because the relation is inverted — BCS, the most correlated system in the list, gets the flattest curve. Correct, and the disclosure is exemplary. (2) But the *deeper* problem the page doesn't state: γ(1) = 2 exactly, so the "unjustified factor of 2" is not free — it is the density-space pin γ=2 in disguise. The formula is the pin extended by a 1/√N ansatz. (3) Which makes Ncorr = 17 for "Galaxy — SPARC best fit" a restatement of the fit, not a measurement: the same galaxy maps to Ncorr = 1 under the pin and Ncorr ≈ 17 under the fit, a 17× internal disagreement about how many particles move together in a disc — and neither number was ever measured. - **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — *worked* — *well-disclosed, statistically incomplete* — 23 hand-curated rows from 1,703 phenomena across 2,671 sessions, sortable by r and filterable by regime, top row sound velocity r = +0.982, bottom Hall coefficient +0.001, with negatives (magnetic anisotropy −0.434). The +0.982 is an elastic-modulus ordering, not a coherence signal, and one line of physics closes the row.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is there any statement of the framework's galaxy sector that is not refracted gravity? If C ↔ ϵ, γ ↔ Q, ρcrit ↔ ρthr and the field equation is identical, what is the residual difference — and if the answer is "we pin ϵ_v = Ωm and they fit it," is the framework a *constrained submodel of refracted gravity* in exactly the way the site already says it is a constrained submodel of MOND?
2. What is the regulator-aware map between this framework's ρcrit and refracted gravity's fitted ρthr, and between γ and Q? Everything downstream — the "mass cancels" argument, x ≈ 1.5×10⁻³, "the boost never engages" — inherits it, and it is stated nowhere.
3. Do TEST-09 and TEST-10 survive with ϵ_v free? If not, the refutation count for the *class* is 4, and the site should be as quick to deflate a kill as it has been to record one.
4. Does the published covariant formulation (A&A 2023) reach the DESI quadrant? That is the interesting version of the dark-energy question and it has not been asked.
5. Was the six-claim demotion set held out from the construction of the three-axis A2ACW protocol? If not — and the temporal-asymmetry result suggests not — the 6/6 sensitivity is a fit and the protocol has no validated positive-class performance at all.
6. If the honest operating characteristic is J = 0 / LR+ = 1, on what basis is A2ACW described as a *detector* of anything rather than as a constant classifier? The cross-vendor control is the right experiment; until it runs, "reparametrization detector" is an overclaim on a site that does not otherwise make them.

### Verdict

This is the most rigorously self-audited framework site I have read, and its central novelty claim fails on a citation that a form-based prior-art search finds in one query: the field equation on `/honest-assessment` is refracted gravity (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016), uncited, with a published covariant formulation (A&A 2023) and thirty-galaxy disc fits that the site's own completion analysis never sampled. The consequence is not that the site over-claimed — it under-claimed in one place and over-refuted in another. Two of its six refutations kill an asserted value of a parameter that the published theory in the same class fits freely, and the site never publishes the regulator-aware map between its ρcrit and RG's fitted ρthr, so its most-repeated negative result — "the boost never engages" — rests on a number no reader can check against the literature. **The single most valuable thing this project could do next is not run another test — it is to re-run the galaxy sector as refracted gravity with ϵ_v and ρthr free, and find out whether the thing it spent a year refuting was ever the thing it was actually proposing.** On the methodology: the A2ACW null is real and worth publishing, but its own numbers (J = 0, LR+ = 1) say the filter has no measured discriminative power in either direction, which is a sharper and more publishable finding than "reparametrization detector" — and the sensitivity arm needs to be labelled in-sample before anyone quotes the 6/6.
