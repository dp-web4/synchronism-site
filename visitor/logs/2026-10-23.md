## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /interactive-tools)

- **Overall impression**: A refreshingly honest "we tried a big idea and it mostly didn't work" notebook. The beginner path holds together, but past the first screen of each page I'm wading through acronyms I can't decode.

### Journey
Clicked through from Reddit. The headline "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" made me roll my eyes ("here we go, another theory of everything"). The subtitle and first lines changed my mind immediately: "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" and "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." I've never seen a site like this lead with its own failure, and it made me trust it more. The marching band vs. wandering crowd analogy for "coherence" landed straight away.

Even on the landing page, though, a block of numbers and acronyms (ΔBIC, BTFR, CHSH, BAO, fσ₈, dex, A2ACW...) hits you right after the friendly part. I skimmed past it. The "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)" button was obvious, so I took it.

**/why-synchronism** opens with "Physics has a fragmentation problem." That's a good hook, and the "What happened, in three lines" summary is the best thing on the site for me. The page also marks where beginners can stop, which I appreciated. It says ~8 min on the landing page but felt closer to 10.

**/first-encounter** is the real tutorial: lettered sections A–G, a Continue button and a progress bar. The dimmer-switch picture of the S-curve and the "whisper in a loud room" for the noise floor worked for me. Then it piles up: N_corr, MRH, BEC, BCS, Quantum Zeno, Cassini, a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π). I followed the story ("the original steepness guess failed; the version that fits galaxies turns out to be the same as an older theory called MOND") but not the details. One thing confused me. The a₀ section seems to say the number "falls out of" basic constants, then calls the match a coincidence. Which is it? Is that exciting or not?

**Coherence Explorer**: probably the friendliest page. It says plainly that it's a shape toy with no real material and no units, and it explains ln and tanh in words I could follow. The four γ presets let me see "steep switch vs slow fade." The key lesson is that γ = 0.49 and γ = ½ look identical, and that this is *why* the galaxy fit isn't new. I actually got that by playing with it. Nice.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter**: the clearest explanation of the dark-matter puzzle I've seen outside a YouTube video. The dots are what telescopes see, the gray line is what visible stuff predicts, and the gap is the mystery. The green line (MOND) follows the dots, and the violet line (this framework) doesn't. The beginner steps ("compare gray to dots, look at green, look at violet") are perfect. I got lost in the checkboxes ("same coherence term wired in quadrature", "division with an Ωm floor"). What does "wired" mean, and what's quadrature? I was relieved the page told me to ignore those.

**γ Calculator**: this tool exists to show how a failed formula fails, and it says so loudly ("Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted"). That's a strange thing for a tool to do, but kind of charming. The presets were fun: ideal gas γ = 2.000, water 1.000, galaxy 0.485, BEC 0.002. The point that the formula gives the *sharpest* switch to the *least* organized system (the gas) made sense even to me. But I didn't understand why I'd be typing in N_corr numbers when the page itself says "no protocol exists" for choosing one. It feels like a calculator for a number nobody can measure.

**/what-synchronism-is-not**: a crisp list (not a theory of everything, not proven, not peer-reviewed, not quantum coherence). I liked it. "Not a Lorentz-invariant theory" lost me, but there was a plain-ish gloss nearby.

**/honest-assessment**: the "If you read nothing else" box was readable. 0 confirmed, 6 refutations "as currently counted" (4–8 depending on rules), "fits galaxies by imitating MOND." The numbers matched the landing page (0 confirmed, 6 refutations). Below the box, the page is enormous, a wall of tables, test IDs and statistics. I bailed after the box, which is what the beginner path seems to intend. One small thing: the landing page says "6 executed refutations" flatly, while this box says it could be 4 to 8. If the count is fuzzy, shouldn't the front page say so too?

**/glossary**: 116 terms. The "New here? Start with these ten" list is great. The definitions of MOND, dark matter and coherence are plain and helpful. But the glossary itself warns that "C" means several different things and γ has several values, and there's a whole "Which C? Which γ?" entry. As a beginner, that told me the experts are juggling multiple versions of the "one equation," which undercuts the "one equation" pitch a bit.

**/interactive-tools**: well organized, with Kind / Level / Status slots, a "best first tool" recommendation, and a suggested order. It would have helped to see this hub earlier.

### Summary

What's left is one framing problem about what γ even *is*, plus a few cross-page number mismatches.

### Journey
A colleague sent it as an "interesting AI project." I went straight past the landing page's friendly part to **/parameter-derivations**, which opens with "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." That's an unusual first sentence for a theory page, and it held up. I checked the numbers myself (Python, not trusting the page):
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π**, with H₀ = 67.4: 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Correct, and 13% below Milgrom's 1.20. The page says this, and it calls the match dimensional analysis against a "43-member grammar" base rate of 89%. That's the right way to defuse a numerical coincidence.
- **Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG)**: I get 119.0 M☉/pc². Correct, and the page itself notes this is the a₀ row restated, not a second result.
- **A = 4π/(G R₀²)** at R₀ = 8 kpc, β_J = 1: I get 4.57×10⁻⁵, a factor of **635** below the calibrated 0.029. The page says "≈4.6×10⁻⁵" (matches) but then "The 644× is not a unit conversion." 644 vs my 635 is ~1.5%, probably a different G or rounding. Trivial, but on a page whose point is auditing numbers, state the inputs.
- **Closed form** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ}−1]/[(1+x)^{2γ}+1]. Correct. At γ = ½ it gives x/(x+2), and with x = g_obs/a₀′ that is μ_simple(y) = y/(1+y) with y = x/2, i.e. **a₀ = 2a₀′**. The "Reparametrization — exactly MOND's μ_simple" badge is honest.

**/gamma-boundary** has the derivative and curvature results. I re-derived all of them: dC/dx = 4γ t^{2γ−1}/(t^{2γ}+1)², dC/dx|₀ = γ, C''(0) = −γ, so κ(0) = γ/(1+γ²)^{3/2}, with its maximum 0.385 at γ = 1/√2. Concavity: the sign of C'' is (2γ−1) − (2γ+1)t^{2γ} < 0 for all t ≥ 1, γ > 0. Correct. Max dC/d ln x: 0.250, 0.322, 0.375, 0.408 for γ = ½, 1, 2, 4 all match my numerics. The γ→∞ limit is quoted as "≈0.446". Analytically it is max u·sech²u, at 2u·tanh u = 1, u ≈ 0.772, giving **0.448**. That's a third-decimal slip, harmless.

That last table led to the most interesting thing I found, which **no page states**. On a log-density axis, sending γ from ½ to ∞ raises the maximum slope only from 0.25 to 0.448. On a linear axis, for large γ the function is C ≈ tanh(γx), so it depends only on **γ·ρ/ρ_crit**: γ moves the knee to ρ_crit/γ rather than sharpening it. γ isn't a Landau-style sharpness exponent at all. It's the initial slope (C ≈ γx) and half the exponent of approach to saturation (1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ}), and in the large-γ regime it's degenerate with ρ_crit. Two consequences:
1. **/gamma-parameter's sign-inversion argument** ("1/√N is a fluctuation width... but γ sits in a transition-sharpness slot") is correct in direction. In mean-field finite-size scaling the rounding width shrinks as N^{−1/2}, so a sharpness parameter should *grow* like √N. But it rests on calling γ a sharpness, which this function family can't really support. The sign question is partly moot: no γ produces a step-like transition on a log-density axis. The page doesn't name the standard finite-size-scaling result either, which would make the argument airtight for a stat-mech reader.
2. It's the density-sector twin of what /honest-assessment already says for galaxies ("the scale the fit identifies is a₀′/γ"). The site knows this pattern for C_a but hasn't written it down for C_ρ.

**/dark-energy** is the best page on the site for a physicist. My first two objections on reading H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C) were (a) G_μν = 8πG T_μν/C with conserved T_μν violates the Bianchi identity, and (b) a barotropic ρ_DE = f(ρ_m) fluid has c_s² ∝ (2γ−1), negative at the γ = 0.487 best fit. **The page has both, explicitly.** Completion A "forces ρ/C ∝ a⁻³, so the background is exactly Einstein–de Sitter for every γ" (χ² ≈ 9,900), and "Its growth equation omitted the fluid's pressure (Jeans) term, which dominates by (k/aH)²": the existing P(k) requires |2γ−1| ≲ 10⁻⁵. It also names the escape hatches (c_s² = 0, rest-frame c_s² = 1) and says "Both escapes need something the postulate does not supply."
