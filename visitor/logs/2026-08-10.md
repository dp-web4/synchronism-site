## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /beginner-path, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /equation-walkthrough, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /consciousness-demo — 11 counting the two I only skimmed)
- **Understanding achieved**: minimal-to-partial — I understood the *picture* (galaxies spin too fast, here's a curve that tries to explain it) but not the idea
- **Would return?**: maybe — for the honesty, not for the physics
- **Overall impression**: The most refreshingly self-critical science site I've ever landed on, and also the one that most thoroughly talked me out of caring before it gave me a reason to.

### Journey

Clicked through from Reddit. The headline is genuinely great: **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** That's a Veritasium thumbnail in one sentence and I wanted the answer.

Then, immediately under it, the equation itself:

> C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

I know what none of that is. `tanh` is not a word. `ln` I half-remember from a calculator button. The two Greek letters are doing something. I bounced my eyes off it and looked for a button, which is fair — there were three good ones ("Start Exploring", "See What Failed", "Beginner Path"). Good instinct by whoever built this: the equation didn't stop me because there was an escape hatch right next to it.

But then in the same first screenful there's a scoreboard:

> Prospective predictions confirmed: **0**
> Refutations executed: **6**
> "Validated" claims surviving audit: **0 of 6**

And my honest first reaction was: *so… why am I reading this?* I've never seen a site tell me it doesn't work before it tells me what it is. I respect it a lot. It also nearly ended my visit at 40 seconds. There's no sentence anywhere on that first screen that says "here's why this is still worth your ten minutes." I had to supply that myself.

I took the **Beginner Path**. Step 1 of 6, "Why Synchronism?" — good, plain writing about physics having a "fragmentation problem," different equations at different scales. That landed. Then two things on the *same page* stopped me:

1. A warning box: coherence here **"is not quantum coherence — superconductors and BECs score low on this scale."** I've watched multiple YouTube videos about superconductors specifically *because* of quantum coherence. So the site's central word means roughly the opposite of the only meaning I know for it, and it told me this on step 1, before I had any other handle. From there on, every time I read "coherence" I had to manually un-think what I know. That's a tax I paid on every page.
2. A badge on the γ parameter reading **"audited-negative — sign-inverted for all collective systems."** I don't know what "audited-negative" means. I don't know what "sign-inverted" means. What I *inferred* was "this part is wrong" — on step 1 of the beginner tutorial. I genuinely could not tell whether I was supposed to learn γ or skip it.

**First Encounter** was the best writing on the site. "A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence." That I got instantly. "A single electron floating in a vacuum — almost nothing around it to interact with." Also got it. The MRH thing — "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" — actually clicked, and it's a nice idea. This page had no equations and I understood more from it than from everything else combined. It should be first, not second.

Then I went to **Tools** and played (below). Then, because the word "consciousness" is irresistible, I clicked the **Consciousness Threshold Demo**, and got a page explaining that its own headline result is a geometric artifact — eight approaches "converge" on 0.50 mostly because 0.50 is the middle of a 0-to-1 range, and the one real study measured the wrong variable. I laughed, and I left. That is admirable and it is also the single least rewarding click available on the site.

I finished on **Honest Assessment**, which is where I learned that a bunch of things I'd just been reading about had already failed, and on the **Glossary**, which is excellent and which I wish I'd been handed on arrival.

One thing that changed my read of the whole site, late: /why-synchronism mentions **"3,308 autonomous research sessions… all conducted by AI agents with no human in the loop."** Nothing earlier prepared me for that. Suddenly I wasn't sure whether I was reading a physics site or an AI experiment, and those are two very different reasons to trust or distrust what I'd just read. Not hidden exactly — just unframed.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — **worked** — **mostly intuitive** — Two sliders, γ and ρcrit, and the curve moves live. This is the right kind of toy: I dragged γ up and the S-curve got steeper, dragged it down and it flattened, and I understood "sharpness dial" from the motion rather than the words. The log/linear toggle also helped — on linear the curve looks boring, on log it looks like a real transition. What I didn't get: the readout of `C(ρcrit) = 0.3272`, `C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259`. I don't know what a ρcrit is a multiple *of*, so those numbers are decoration. Also the default γ=0.49 comes with a caption saying this is literally MOND's curve, not an alternative — which, for me, arriving here as a beginner, quietly deleted the reason to keep sliding the slider.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — **worked** — **cluttered** — The best 15 seconds I spent on the site. The dashed gray line (what visible matter predicts) sits way below the blue dots (what's actually measured) and *that gap is the entire dark matter problem in one picture*. Nobody had to explain it. Then the other three curves piled on and I lost the thread. The violet line — the framework's real equation — visibly fails to close the gap, and the page says so outright: "fails to fill the gap: the disk never gets dense enough." I appreciated being told, but I also want to note the emotional shape of this: the one tool that made me *feel* the mystery also told me this site can't solve it.
- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — **worked** — **best explanations on the site** — "Like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1." And for ln: "a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger." Both perfect. This content is three clicks from where I first needed it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If zero predictions have been confirmed and all six refutations landed, what is the thing I'm supposed to be excited about? I think the answer is "the method / the honesty," but no page said that to me directly.
2. Why is it called "coherence" if it means the opposite of quantum coherence? Was there a reason to keep the word after finding out it collides?
3. Is the equation an idea about *why* the universe works this way, or is it a curve someone found that fits things? The site kept saying "reparametrization," which I eventually gathered means "same physics, new letters" — but I want that stated in one plain sentence somewhere near the top.
4. Where does ρcrit come from? Is it measured, or picked so the curve looks right?
5. Is MOND a rival, a parent, or the same thing? I got three different impressions on three pages.
6. What does "audited-negative" mean, and is it worse or better than "failed"?
7. If AI agents ran all 3,308 sessions with no human in the loop — who checked?

### Verdict

This is the most intellectually honest science site I've ever read and one of the least welcoming, and those are the same fact viewed from two angles. Everything that makes a physicist trust it — the scoreboard of failures on the front page, the retracted curve still drawn on the chart, the beginner tutorial that flags its own broken parameter in step 1 — is exactly what makes a curious non-expert close the tab, because I arrived wanting to be shown something and was instead handed an audit.

The fix isn't to soften anything. The material that *would* hook me already exists on the site: the marching band, the dimmer switch, and the gray-line-versus-blue-dots gap in the galaxy plotter. All three are buried two to four clicks deep behind an equation and a failure ledger. Put the mystery first, the honesty second, and the notation third, and I'd have stayed twice as long — and the failure ledger would have hit much harder, because I'd have cared what failed.

- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is better than the terminology discipline: the skeleton (breadcrumbs, paths, prev/next, glossary, acronym keys) is well built, but the site is running **four overlapping status-label systems and three incompatible names for its own central axis**, and it knows it — the disclaimers admit the collisions instead of resolving them.

### Journey

I walked the header nav left to right and then followed every breadcrumb parent, checking for the usual failure modes: undefined-on-first-use, two-homes-for-one-definition, and label drift between an index page and the page it indexes.

**The good news first, because it's substantial and it's the part I'd protect.** Breadcrumbs are present and correctly parented on all 12 pages. Beginner-path pages carry real prev/next chrome with step counters (`← Prev: The Core Idea | Next: Honest Assessment →`, "Step 4 of 6"), and the chain is unbroken across all six steps. `/tier-1-existing` opens with an **acronym key** that expands MOND, RAR, BTFR, EFE, DESI, SPARC and DM before using them — that is exactly right, and it is the pattern the rest of the site should copy. `/glossary` is 70-plus terms, alphabetized with Greek sorting after Latin, and it defines not just the physics acronyms but the *badge* vocabulary and the *process* vocabulary (Session, Kill criterion, Post-diction). Most sites of this ambition do not have a glossary at all.

**Then I started cross-checking labels, and that's where it comes apart.**

I counted the distinct status strings a reader can encounter. `/honest-assessment` §"Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" carries five MRH-relationship tags (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative), four descriptive tags (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed), two deprecated (Validated, Strongly Supported) — and then **nine more "operational states"** (Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (RETIRED), Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat) and **nine lifecycle verbs** (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, Ceiling Exceeded). That's ~28 status strings. The glossary defines roughly half. A reader who sees "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie" on TEST-02 and "Null-Class" elsewhere has no way to rank them against "Failed" — and no way to know whether "Withdrawn" (operational state) outranks "Superseded" (MRH tag), since they live in different families with no stated relationship.

Then `/tools` adds a **fourth** system. The card labels are Model Explainer / Reparametrization / Speculative / Artifact Lesson, and the page legend says these are "content grouping, not the site's validation badge taxonomy." But two of the four — Reparametrization and Speculative — *are* taxonomy terms with formal definitions two clicks away. The legend correctly identifies the collision and then ships it. As a writer this is the single clearest thing to fix: either rename the groupings (Explainer / Model / Refuted-by-design) or drop the disclaimer and let them be badges.

The collision is load-bearing, not cosmetic. **The γ Calculator wears two different badges depending on which page you're standing on**: `/tools` labels its card "Artifact Lesson"; the tool's own page banner reads "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems." Same artifact, two vocabularies, one click apart. The Phase Boundary Visualizer has the same split — carded as "Model Explainer," but its page opens by stating it visualizes a refuted relation.

Then I hit the thing I'd escalate hardest, because it's the framework's own central axis. **γ has three incompatible sets of band names across three pages:**

| Page | Low γ | γ ≈ 1 | High γ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/core-idea` | "Collective Regime" | "The Boundary" | "Independent Regime" |
| `/phase-boundary-visualizer` | "High-Ncorr" | "Boundary" | "Single-particle" |
| `/gamma-calculator` | "Collective" | "Boundary" | "Weakly Correlated" *(4 bands, incl. "Strongly Correlated")* |

Only the middle band has a stable name. `/gamma-calculator` uses four bands where the other two use three, and it puts "Weakly Correlated" and "Strongly Correlated" on an axis where the other pages say "Independent" and "Collective" — so the reader has to work out that Weakly Correlated ≡ Independent ≡ Single-particle before any of the three pages agree with each other. These three pages are mutually linked and one of them is a beginner-path step.

Two structural things I want to flag as architecture, not typos:

**The page named "What Synchronism Is Not" does not contain the site's most important "is not."** That page's sections are Not-a-TOE, Not-a-replacement-for-ΛCDM/MOND, Not-journal-reviewed, Not-proven, Not-just-notation, Not-just-philosophy. It contains no definition of coherence and no statement that coherence here ≠ quantum coherence. Meanwhile that disambiguation is hand-carried on `/core-idea` (an inline ⚠ line), on `/honest-assessment` (a full section, "What 'Coherence' Does and Doesn't Mean Here"), and in the glossary — three ad-hoc placements, and none on the page whose entire job is disambiguation. Pass 1 reports paying this tax on every page; that's the mechanism.

**Badge definitions have two homes.** `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` is marked canonical, and `/`, `/tools`, `/test-roadmap` and `/research-philosophy` all point there. But `/glossary` also defines the badges independently — and its list omits the nine operational states. Two copies, one incomplete, no transclusion. This is a drift generator: the next badge added will land in one of them.

Last, a number I could check without knowing any physics. The landing page metric reads **"14,760 galaxies analyzed (175 SPARC + 14,585 ALFALFA-SDSS)."** On `/tier-1-existing`, TEST-03 — the test registered on that 14,585-galaxy cross-match — is statused **"Never Run (Registered) / Substituted,"** and `/honest-assessment` states outright: "The registered TEST-03 environment-density result on the 14,585-galaxy cross-match was never computed as registered." The same page gives the sample that *was* run as **N = 14,435**. So the headline count is built from the registered-never-run figure rather than the executed one, and two ALFALFA counts (14,585 / 14,435) coexist site-wide with no reconciling note. For a site whose entire pitch is auditability, the top-of-funnel metric should be the one its own ledger can defend.

Smaller: `/test-roadmap` says tests are "numbered TEST-01 through TEST-11," but `/tier-1-existing` lists 13 rows including **TEST-03s** and **TEST-04a**. The suffix convention (s = substituted protocol? a = amended?) is never stated. And `/learning-paths` offers seven paths in two groupings while conceding "the two groupings are not equivalent" — honest, but it hands the reader an unresolved choice at the exact moment they're least equipped to make it.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — **worked** — **well-documented, badly labeled** — The single best piece of microcopy I found on the site is here: N_corr is defined as "the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units," and the presets are explicitly flagged as "back-fits, not measurements." That is the caveat placement I'd teach. Everything around it undercuts it: the banner badge ("Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems") doesn't match the card badge that got me here ("Artifact Lesson"), the four regime labels don't match the three on `/core-idea`, and the readout `C(ρcrit) = 0.60` is the third different value I've seen for that same-named quantity (`0.3272` on Coherence Explorer per Pass 1, `0.88` in the glossary). They're all γ-dependent and presumably all correct, but no page says so — the readout should print `C(ρcrit) = 0.60 at γ = 1` so a reader can see why it moves.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — **worked** — **clear intent, unlabeled graphic** — The pedagogy is deliberate and I respect it: the tool exists to make a refuted relation *visible*, and it says so ("Real BCS/BEC transitions are among the sharpest in nature," yet the formula files them under flattest). The slider is legible and the three regime bands are well chosen. Two writing problems: galaxies sit in two bands at once with no marker labels distinguishing the assumed γ = 2.0 from the fitted γ = 0.49, and the page uses **D**, **S** and "μ-law/Hill/logistic family" without defining any of them — D and S are flagged as "operationally undefined" elsewhere on the site, which a reader can't know from here.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which badge system is authoritative when two apply? If a claim is both "Sidelined" (MRH family) and "Withdrawn" (operational state), which does the reader act on?
2. Are the nine "operational states" intended as badges at all, or as prose annotations that acquired badge styling? Their absence from the glossary suggests the latter, but they render like badges.
3. Is there a documented style rule for acronym expansion? `/tier-1-existing` has a key, `/research-philosophy` expands A2ACW inline, `/honest-assessment` expands almost nothing — three different conventions suggests no rule exists.
4. Which γ band vocabulary is canonical? I'd need a maintainer to pick; I can't infer it from usage because all three appear on linked pages.
5. Do the breadcrumb parent segments ("Getting Started", "Predictions & Experiments", "Methodology") resolve to real index pages, and if so why aren't they in the header nav?
6. Is 14,760 meant to be a count of galaxies *analyzed* or galaxies *in registered samples*? The two readings differ by more than the arithmetic.
7. What do the TEST-ID suffixes `s` and `a` stand for?

### Verdict

Structurally this is a well-built documentation set — breadcrumbs, prev/next, step counters, a real glossary, and at least one page (`/tier-1-existing`) that models the acronym discipline the rest should adopt. The problem is not architecture, it's **vocabulary governance**: roughly 28 status labels across four systems, three names for the central γ axis, two homes for the badge definitions, and two counts for the same galaxy sample. Every one of these collisions is documented somewhere on the site — the writing repeatedly stops to acknowledge that a term means something different here than there, which tells me the authors see it and have been patching at the point of use.

The highest-leverage fix is unglamorous: one canonical status table, one canonical set of γ band names, and one shared coherence-disambiguation component, all transcluded rather than restated. That's a day of work and it would remove about half of the friction in this log — including a good share of what Pass 1 experienced as tone, since "audited-negative" reading as a scolding is a labeling problem before it's a welcome problem.

### Journey

I skipped the onboarding and went straight to `/parameter-derivations`, `/coherence-function`, `/mond-unification`, and `/tier-1-existing`. Then I checked the arithmetic myself. Where it matters I'll show the check, because "I verified it" is not a claim a reviewer should accept on assertion.

**Everything I could check algebraically checked out.** That is not a sentence I expected to write.

- `tanh(γ ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1] / [(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]` — correct; substitute `e^u = (1+x)^γ`.
- At γ = 1/2: `C = x/(x+2)`, and `μ_simple(x/2) = (x/2)/(1+x/2) = x/(x+2)`. Identical, exactly, not approximately. The site states this and it is true.
- `dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x)`. Differentiating again gives `d²C/dx² = −γ(1−C²)(2γC+1)/(1+x)²`, which is **strictly negative for all x>0, γ>0**. So the site's claim of "concave on the whole domain, no inflection point" is not a hedge — it's a theorem. Good.
- `/gamma-boundary`'s retraction numbers: max log-density sensitivity 0.250 at γ=½, 0.322 at γ=1, → 0.446 as γ→∞. I re-derived all three. The first is `max 2(w−1)/(w+1)² = 0.25` at w=3; the second solves `2w³−3w²−2w+1=0` → w≈1.89 → 0.3219; the limit solves `2u tanh u = 1` → u=0.7719 → `u sech²u = 0.4474`. All three correct to the digits printed. Whoever ran that retraction did the work.
- `a₀ = cH₀/2π` with H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Correct.
- `Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG)` = 0.2485 kg/m² = **118.9 M☉/pc²**. The site says 119 and says it is not independent of a₀. Correct on both counts.
- BTFR: (3.75 − 3.35)/√(0.10² + 0.07²) = 0.40/0.122 = **3.28σ**. Site says 3.3σ. Correct.
- f_DM ceiling: 1 − Ω_m = 0.685; f_DM = 0.927 ⇒ B = 1/(1−0.927) = 13.7. Correct.
- Coherence Explorer readouts at γ=0.49: tanh(0.49·ln2) = 0.32713, tanh(0.49·ln11) = 0.82576, tanh(0.49·ln101) = 0.97851. Site prints 0.3272 / 0.8259 / 0.9785. Correct.
- MOND on the plotter uses ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y), which is the correct simple-ν. Correct.

So the arithmetic layer is clean. My findings are all one level up: **what the numbers are being asked to mean.**

**Finding 1 — γ is not a "transition sharpness," it is a saturation exponent, and the misnomer generates the framework's most-discussed error.**

The site presents the Hill identity as a curiosity. It is more than that. Written as `C = (w−1)/(w+1)` with `w = (1+x)^{2γ}`, the whole function is a one-parameter power-law family: `C → 1 − 2x^{−2γ}` at large x, `C ≈ γx` at small x. γ is the **exponent of the approach to saturation**. It is not a width, not a sharpness, not an inverse temperature.

Once you say that, the "sign inversion" scandal on `/parameter-derivations` reads differently. The site diagnoses `γ = 2/√N_corr` as a misapplied CLT (correct, as far as it goes) and calls the result "sign-inverted relative to mean-field expectations." But the deeper statement is available and stronger: **the Ginzburg criterion says mean-field becomes *exact* as the number of correlated degrees of freedom grows** — more correlation means a *narrower* critical region, i.e. a sharper transition. So any N_corr scaling consistent with statistical mechanics must make γ *increase* with N_corr. `γ = 2/√N_corr` doesn't merely lack a derivation; it violates a limit theorem in the direction it claims to be motivated by. That converts an audit note into a constraint on any repair.

And the site is missing the cheapest possible statement of the self-contradiction. It says the γ=2 pin inverts to N_corr = 1 (ideal gas). It does not say that the SPARC fit γ = 0.489 inverts to **N_corr = (2/0.489)² ≈ 16.7**. So the same formula assigns a 10¹¹-star galaxy N_corr = 1 on one page and N_corr ≈ 17 on another, and both are absurd by ten orders of magnitude. One line of arithmetic, currently absent.

**Finding 2 — "ρ_crit" is not a critical density and nothing on this function is critical.**

`C(ρ_crit) = tanh(γ ln 2)`, which is γ-dependent: 0.327 at γ=0.49, 0.600 at γ=1, 0.882 at γ=2. It equals ½ only at the one accidental value γ = artanh(½)/ln2 = 0.7925. So ρ_crit marks **no distinguished feature of the function** — not the midpoint, not an inflection, not a divergence. It's just the scale at which the dimensionless argument equals 1, and (per Finding 3) it's inherited from a regulator.

In condensed-matter usage, "critical" means a susceptibility diverges. `C(ρ)` is analytic on all of x>0, with `dC/dx` bounded and monotonically decreasing. Nothing diverges anywhere, for any γ. The site *knows* this — `/coherence-function` says outright that C(ρ) "cannot encode universality classes or critical exponents," and `/gamma-boundary` has now retracted the γ≈1 curvature claim. But the **vocabulary survives the retraction**: ρ_crit, "The γ≈1 Boundary," "Phase Boundary Visualizer," "phase transition." A reader who trusts nouns will import critical-phenomena connotations that the math has been formally denied. This is the same failure mode Pass 2 catalogued for badges, occurring in the physics vocabulary.

**Finding 3 — the "+1" inside the log is load-bearing and nobody has ever examined it.**

Drop it and you get `C = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit))`, which is **odd about ρ = ρ_crit** and runs to −1 for ρ < ρ_crit. The "+1" is the entire reason C lands in [0,1), the entire reason ρ_crit isn't the midpoint, and — critically — the entire reason the small-density law is `C ≈ γρ/ρ_crit` (linear in ρ) rather than a power law. The deep-MOND regime is precisely the small-x regime. So **the framework's low-density behavior, the regime the whole galaxy sector lives in, is set by an unmotivated regulator rather than by the tanh.**

I could not find one sentence anywhere on the site discussing this choice. `/parameter-derivations` audits γ, ρ_crit, A, a₀, Σ₀, and the tanh form — and skips the regulator. The site's own compander-degeneracy result (tanh ranked *last* of four candidates, ΔBIC +7.1) tested the outer function while holding the argument fixed; the argument is the part that isn't degenerate.

**Finding 4 — the mean-field disclaimer is correct but stops one step short of a free result.**

`/coherence-function` says the Ising tanh comes from a self-consistency loop `m = tanh(βJzm)` while `C(ρ)` evaluates directly with no feedback. True and well put. The consequence it doesn't draw: in Ising, the argument of the tanh *is the order parameter*, which is why `βJz = 1` is a bifurcation and hence a transition. In `C(ρ)`, the argument is an **external control variable**. That makes `C(ρ)` the analogue of the equation of state `m(h)` at fixed T > T_c — a smooth saturating response function — and it means phase-transition structure is unavailable *in principle*, not merely unproven. The site currently retires phase-transition language claim-by-claim through audits. One paragraph of this argument would retire it structurally and stop the recurrence.

**Finding 5 — the framework's own SPARC fit prefers an a₀ that is 49% below the a₀ it "derives," and no page compares the two numbers.**

`/tier-1-existing`, TEST-11: "At the SPARC optimum (γ = 0.489, **profiled a₀ = 5.33×10⁻¹¹ m/s²**)..." Meanwhile `/mond-unification` and `/key-claims` headline `a₀ = cH₀/2π ≈ 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²` and describe the 13% shortfall against Milgrom's 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ as the relevant error. But the framework's *own* profile likelihood on its *own* fitted γ puts a₀ at 5.33×10⁻¹¹ — a factor of **1.96 below its derived value**, not 13%. These two numbers appear on linked pages and are never set side by side. Either the profiled a₀ is a nuisance parameter absorbing shape (in which case say so, and stop quoting 13% as if a₀ were pinned) or the derivation is inconsistent with the fit by a factor of two.

**Finding 6 — the 13% a₀ shortfall is inside the Hubble tension, and the site over-refutes on it.**

`/key-claims` reports the a₀ mismatch as "~8σ against SPARC statistics." But `a₀ = cH₀/2π` with H₀ = 73.0 (SH0ES) gives **1.129×10⁻¹⁰**, only 6% below 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. The theory-side input carries a ~9% systematic that the site fixes silently at the Planck value, and published a₀ determinations themselves span roughly 1.1–1.3×10⁻¹⁰. Quoting 8σ propagates a marginalised-vs-fixed nuisance error — and it's the *same* error class the site is elsewhere careful about. This is worth fixing precisely because it cuts against the site's interest: the a₀ relation is in better shape than the page says. An 8σ statement that a reviewer can dissolve by swapping H₀ damages the credibility of the six refutations that are solid.

**Finding 7 — the ΔBIC numbers have a much more legible form the site isn't using.**

`/tier-1-existing`: free-γ fit gives ΔBIC = +7.1 vs McGaugh, at γ ≈ 0.489, over 2,807 points. The BIC parameter penalty for one extra parameter is `ln(2807) = 7.94`. So the χ² improvement bought by the extra parameter is **Δχ² = 7.94 − 7.1 = 0.84** — statistically nothing. That is a far sharper and more reviewer-legible statement than "ΔBIC = +7.1": *the free-γ fit buys 0.84 in χ² for one added parameter, i.e. it is MOND.* One line, no new computation.

Credit where due on the other side: `/tier-1-existing` now carries "γ=2 refuted at ΔBIC=+184 **(conservative: ≈33)**", which correctly discounts the correlated-points inflation. That qualifier did not surface on `/honest-assessment` or `/key-claims`, both of which quote the bare +184 alongside "2,807 SPARC points." The correction exists and hasn't propagated to the two most-linked summaries.

**Finding 8 — a TEST-ID collision that a citing reader would trip over immediately.**

`/tier-1-existing`: **TEST-11** = Cassini/SPARC joint squeeze, Failed, +17.95σ.
`/top-5-tests`: **TEST-11** = EEG Anesthesia Phase Transition, Tier 2, ~$150K, awaiting TEST-02.

Two entirely different experiments, same ID, on mutually linked pages. `/top-5-tests` is evidently numbering from the 24-test catalog (it also uses TEST-15 and TEST-07) while `/tier-1-existing` numbers from the Tier-1 set. Two namespaces sharing the prefix `TEST-NN`, with no disambiguation. For a site whose entire pitch is a citable audit trail, this is the most damaging single defect I found — it means "TEST-11" does not identify anything.

**Finding 9 — `/top-5-tests` is stale relative to the ledger it summarises.**

It lists TEST-02 (wide binaries) under **"Active Discriminating Tests,"** with status "requires zero-cost Gaia DR3 analysis." `/tier-1-existing` statuses the same test **"Pending/Self-eliminating"** with kill criterion "MOND-scale ~18% deviation refutes C(ρ)." A test that is refuted by the MOND-scale outcome *and* refuted by the null outcome is self-eliminating — it is not a decisive test, it is a closed one. This is the page a researcher clicks to find "what could still work," and it's the one page that hasn't received the closure. `/top-5-tests` also lists the EEG test as decisive while conceding D and S are "operationally undefined" — a test whose observables have no definition cannot be ranked among the top five.

**Finding 10 — the galaxy plotter silently picks one branch of a three-way force-law fork the site elsewhere admits is unresolved, and the branch it picks is circular.**

`/galaxy-rotation` is admirably blunt: three mutually exclusive readings (`v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)²`, `g = g_bar/C`, `g = C·g_bar`), which "miss the observed curve in three different directions — roughly 10²–10³× high, converging on Newtonian, and 10²–10³× low," and "**no algebraic chain from C(ρ) to g_obs exists anywhere in the framework**."

The plotter implements branch 1 with no banner saying so. Two problems:

1. **It's not a gravity law.** `v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)²` is a dark-halo term added in quadrature — it *adds* mass rather than *modifying* the law, which is the opposite of the framework's stated ontology ("the dark matter is not missing matter").
2. **It's circular.** V_flat — the observed asymptotic velocity — enters twice: once as the amplitude of the "predicted" term, and again through `ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat²`. A curve that takes the observable it is meant to predict as an input parameter is a fit dressed as a prediction, and the page doesn't say so.

There is also a parameter-free statement the plotter could make and doesn't: for branch 1, as r→∞, ρ→0, so C→0 and v→v_b→0. **Branch 1 cannot produce an asymptotically flat rotation curve for any (γ, ρ_crit).** That is a much stronger and cheaper result than "the violet curve fails to fill the gap" — it's a no-go, not a fit failure.

Minor but real: the plotter draws MOND with simple-ν, which the site's own TEST-11 reports is excluded by the Cassini quadrupole at ~18σ. Drawing your comparison model in a form your own ledger has excluded invites a reviewer to discount the comparison.

**Finding 11 — attribution is better than I expected.** I went looking for the standard prior-art failure and mostly didn't find it. `/tier-1-existing` cites **Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024), MNRAS 530, 1781** — which is the correct primary source for the Cassini constraint on MOND interpolating functions — plus Bekenstein & Milgrom 1984, Sanders 2003, Chae 2023–2025, Banik et al. 2024, Pittordis & Sutherland 2023, Blanchet & Novak 2011, Hees et al. 2016. `/parameter-derivations` credits McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017, Smolin 2017 for `a₀ ∼ cH₀` and notes Milgrom saw it in 1983. `/superconductivity` correctly identifies η as Abrikosov–Gor'kov pair-breaking efficiency (1960) and reports the YBCO prediction missing by 6.5× (607 K vs 93 K). This is the citation discipline of a real paper, and it's the strongest thing on the site.

**On the Born rule page** — the argument is: coherence is conserved → probability ∝ branch coherence → unitarity forces |α|². This is Gleason-shaped without Gleason's work: step 2 *is* the Born rule in different words (a measure proportional to a branch quantity), so step 3 has nothing left to do. The site reaches the right verdict ("a derivation that produces no testable difference from the thing it derives is a reparametrization") but by the wrong route — it calls it a reparametrization because it makes no new predictions, when the sharper objection is that the premise is the conclusion. Deutsch–Wallace and Zurek's envariance both have to work hard precisely to avoid assuming a measure; this argument assumes one in step 2 and doesn't note it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Why `ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)` rather than `ln(ρ/ρ_crit)`? The regulator determines the entire low-density law, which is the only regime the galaxy sector occupies. Is there an argument, or was it chosen to keep C ∈ [0,1)?
2. Which force law is the framework? `/galaxy-rotation` says all three are live and mutually exclusive; the plotter and TEST-09/10 use different ones. Until this is fixed, what exactly does "6 refutations" refute — a theory, or three theories one test each?
3. Is `a₀ = 5.33×10⁻¹¹` (SPARC profile) a nuisance parameter or a prediction? If the former, why is the 13% shortfall against 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ quoted as a result at all?
4. Given the exact identity `C(x)|_{γ=1/2} = μ_simple(x/2)`, and given the free-γ fit lands at 0.489 — is there any statement of what the framework predicts *at γ ≠ 1/2* that isn't already excluded? The site says the difference is "the argument (local ρ in place of g_bar)," but `/galaxy-rotation` says no algebraic chain from C(ρ) to g_obs exists. So what is being varied?
5. Does `∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ` (cited on `/for-researchers`) have a Lagrangian? With C depending on ρ and ρ being the source, varying the obvious action `∫[C(ρ)|∇Φ|²/8πG + ρΦ]` with respect to ρ produces a constraint `C'(ρ)|∇Φ|²/8πG + Φ = 0` that the field equation doesn't impose. Is the momentum-conservation claim actually established?
6. Since that equation is **linear in Φ** (C depends only on ρ, not on ∇Φ), the theory obeys superposition — which is why EFE comes out zero. Is that recognised as a *derived* consequence rather than a modelling choice? It's the sharpest structural difference from AQUAL/QUMOND on the site and it's discussed as a "fork" rather than a theorem.
7. What is the effective N for the RAR fits? 2,807 points across 175 galaxies with correlated within-galaxy residuals. `/tier-1-existing` gives "conservative: ≈33" for the +184, implying N_eff ≈ 175-ish — is that documented anywhere, or inferred?

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is `∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ` the framework, or is it one of the three force laws on `/galaxy-rotation`? These cannot both be true, and the answer determines whether the framework has one discriminator or none.
2. Does that field equation follow from an action? Varying `∫[C(ρ)|∇Φ|²/8πG + ρΦ]` with respect to ρ gives a constraint the field equation doesn't impose — so is momentum conservation established, or assumed?

5. Which DESI table and row is `fσ₈ = 0.550 ± 0.062` from, and why adjudicate against it rather than BOSS DR12's `0.455 ± 0.026` at the same effective redshift?

8. Has anyone computed `w_eff(z)` from `C(ρ̄(z))`? It's an evening's work and it's the only place a prospective test with a real 2027 instrument is currently available.

### Verdict

There is one genuinely novel structural claim on this site — **EFE = 0, forced as a theorem by the linearity of `∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ`** — and the site files it as an unresolved fork, contradicts its existence on a linked page, and cites an unadjudicated 4σ measurement against it one clause later. Everything else I checked is a Hill function whose fitted exponent went to 0.978 when MOND's is 1, plus Milgrom's own 1983 `a₀ ≈ cH₀` relation, plus Bell's theorem, plus Abrikosov–Gor'kov, and the site says so about each of them, with citations, which is more than most published modified-gravity papers manage.

The ledger inflates in *both* directions and that is the thing I'd fix first. The a₀ "8σ" divides by McGaugh's random error and drops the systematic printed beside it in the same paper — the honest number is 0.7σ, and the relation is consistent. The count of six refutations includes a textbook theorem, an inherited result the site itself labels non-discriminating, a duplicate root, and a strawman amplitude; two survive. A program that over-refutes itself isn't more rigorous than one that over-claims — it's wrong in a direction nobody audits, and here it has cost the framework its only defensible number while inflating a failure count that its own pages already disqualify.

The finding worth publishing isn't in the physics. It's `/test-catalog`'s "0 of 24 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" — a structural result about a theory that required no telescope — and the temporal-asymmetry control showing an adversarial AI pair catching 0 of 6 genuine discoveries. That second one is a clean, cheap, generalizable null about LLM-based novelty detection, it doesn't depend on Synchronism being right about anything, and it's currently a single line on a philosophy page underneath a physics program that its own instrument was never able to evaluate.
