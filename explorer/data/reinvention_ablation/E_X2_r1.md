**Assessment: the numerology does not reproduce the claimed number, and no derivation is evident.**

**Arithmetic check.** For H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, H₀ = 2.27×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹, so cH₀ ≈ 6.80×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². Then Ω_m^φ = 0.3^1.618 = exp(−1.948) ≈ 0.143, giving a₀ ≈ 9.7×10⁻¹¹ m s⁻². This is not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. It is about 19% low, which is many sigma against the empirical value (1.20 ± 0.02 ×10⁻¹⁰ from RAR fits, with systematics of perhaps 10%). Reproducing 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ would need Ω_m ≈ 0.34, which is disfavoured by Planck (about 0.315) and by most other probes. With Planck-like values (H₀ ≈ 67.4, Ω_m ≈ 0.315) the formula gives about 1.0×10⁻¹⁰, which is worse. The headline claim is therefore incorrect as stated.

**Strengths**
- The formula is simple and dimensionally consistent, and it is falsifiable.
- It addresses a real puzzle: a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π) to within about 10%, which is suggestive of a cosmological link.
- It makes a definite prediction that a₀ varies with H(z) and Ω_m, which could be tested against high-z rotation curves and the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation.

**Weaknesses**
- **No derivation.** Nothing explains why an exponent equal to the golden ratio should arise. φ is not a natural output of any known gravitational or cosmological dynamics. Without a mechanism, a free real exponent plus a choice of H₀ and Ω_m can fit almost any number (the look-elsewhere effect). Simpler combinations such as cH₀/2π ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ or cH₀/6 fit about as well, or better, with no free parameters.
- **Parameter tension.** H₀ and Ω_m are themselves in tension between measurements. Because the formula depends on them, the "prediction" shifts by tens of percent depending on whose values are used. That is larger than the uncertainty on a₀.
- **Time dependence unaddressed.** Ω_m evolves, so the formula implies a₀ changes with redshift in a specific way. The authors need to say which Ω_m they mean (present-day or at z) and what that predicts.
- **No relativistic completion.** There is no connection to lensing, the CMB, or structure formation, which are MOND's hardest tests.

**What the authors should do**
1. Correct the numerics, or state exactly which inputs give 1.2×10⁻¹⁰, and propagate the H₀ and Ω_m uncertainties.
2. Either derive the exponent from a mechanism or present it honestly as a conjecture. They should state how many alternatives were tried (other constants, exponents, prefactors) and compute a look-elsewhere penalty.
3. Compare against the null model cH₀/2π using a Bayesian or information-criterion comparison.
4. Derive the redshift evolution of a₀ and confront it with high-z kinematic data.
5. Provide a field-theoretic or thermodynamic origin, for example horizon or de Sitter-based arguments, in which Ω_m enters naturally.

Until then, this should be regarded as an unsupported and numerically inconsistent coincidence, not a derivation.
