## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /learning-paths, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A strangely likable site that tells you in the first screen that its big idea didn't work, then spends a lot of pages and jargon explaining exactly how, and I left unsure what's still interesting enough to follow.

### Journey

**Landing page.** I came from Reddit expecting "one equation explains everything" hype. Instead the headline asks "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" and the next line says: *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* That really got my attention. I've never seen a site do that. It hooked me more than a big claim would have.

Then it got harder. The equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` is right there. The plain-English version helps a bit ("a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are"), and so do the symbol notes ("ρ is 'rho' = density; γ is 'gamma' = how sudden the switch is"). But "ρcrit = saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)" meant nothing to me. What's a knee? Why tell me it's *not* a critical point when I don't know what a critical point is?

The scoreboard is the most striking thing on the page: 0 predictions confirmed, 6 refutations, 0 of 9 "validated" claims surviving audit. The hover text for "refutations executed" is good: *"tests that could have killed the idea — and the idea failed them."* Right after it I hit: *"6 executed tests collapse to 5 independent roots: 2 framework-specific mechanism roots + 1 refuted registration + 1 inherited from MOND + 1 Bell/CHSH construction check."* I read that three times. I don't know what a "root" is, what a "registration" is, or what Bell/CHSH is. It felt like accounting notes for insiders. "A2ACW" does get spelled out (AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop), which I appreciated, though it made me wonder: were ~3,300 of these sessions just AIs talking to each other?

**Why Synchronism? (Beginner step 1).** A nice, clear opening: *"Physics has a fragmentation problem."* Quantum rules for the small, relativity for the large, chemistry in between. I got that. The "crowd vs. marching band" picture of the S-curve is great. The galaxy count matched the landing page (175 SPARC + 14,435 ALFALFA–SDSS = 14,610), though nobody told me what SPARC or ALFALFA–SDSS are. I assume they're galaxy surveys. Two things threw me:
- "a₀ = cH₀/(2π) reproduced within 13% — but this result is shared with MOND" went straight over my head.
- The superconductor result predicted 607 K when the real value is 93 K ("about 6.5× too high"), but its badge is **Reparametrization**, not **Failed**. The melting-point result right above it (53% average error) *is* badged Failed. To me, being 6.5× off looks like a bigger failure than 53%, so why does it get a softer label?

**First Encounter (step 2).** This is the best page for someone like me. It starts with a single electron in empty space versus the core of a neutron star, has the marching band again, and introduces MRH as *"the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter."* The Andromeda example ("A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy") made MRH click. The page says it has "7 short parts on this one page, lettered A–G — about 10 minutes in all," but I could only find Part A's text and then a "Next →" link to /core-idea. I may have missed a click-to-reveal. Either way I never saw Parts B–G. (*Note for maintainer: this could be a fetch artifact of a client-side stepper. Worth checking that B–G actually render without JS.*) The link to the "Two Reframes" side trip has a ~50-word label that warns me about a square root, a cosine, "Bell/CHSH" and "Kochen–Specker". That's a lot of scary words for a beginner to read just to decide whether to click a link.

**The Core Idea (step 3).** The "dimmer switch" framing and the plain explanation of tanh ("just an S-shaped curve — flat, then steep, then flat again") are excellent. *Now* I finally get what tanh is. I wish the landing page had just said that. There's a picture of the S-curve too. The "where it broke" part fell back into the insider summary ("2 framework-specific mechanism roots (boost ceiling, γ=2 pin) + 1 refuted registration…"). I did grasp that the galaxy part failed.

**What Synchronism Is Not (step 4).** The intro is good ("Overclaiming is dishonest. But so is underclaiming."). But the cards quickly fill up with QFT, GR, ΛCDM, CMB, EFE, Lorentz invariance, Bohmian mechanics, Abrikosov–Gor'kov and more. This is where I started skimming. Two things stopped me:
- "59% of predictions are untested." 59% of how many? Everywhere else I'd seen "0 confirmed, 6 refuted", and now there's a percentage with no total.
- A card says there's a **consciousness equation** (C = f(γ, D, S) ≥ 0.50). Nothing on the path had mentioned consciousness before. It's labeled speculative, but it's a jarring surprise on a "boundaries" page.

**Honest Assessment (step 5).** The "If you read nothing else" box is the single best piece of writing on the site for me:
- *"Where it fits galaxies, it is MOND in different notation"* (MOND gets defined inline, which helps)
- *"'Coherence' here is not quantum coherence"*
- *"What survives is the record, not the physics… and one question still open: star clusters."*

Now I finally understand what happened. I also noticed the box was "added 2026-09-08 at a casual reader's request", so somebody listened. What it doesn't do is explain the star-cluster question it calls the one thing still open. I searched and found it buried deep in the page as "Globular-Cluster Knee Test… a fork, not a kill." There's no link and no one-sentence plain-English version. If that's the one open question, it's the thing I most want to click on, and there's nowhere to click. Below the box, the page is huge, with a badge legend containing about 14 kinds of labels. I didn't read past it.

I also wondered who did the auditing. The page says *"Every output that a domain expert examined resolved as a reparametrization of known physics."* Which domain expert? I couldn't find a name or description of who that is. For a site whose whole selling point is honesty, "who checked this?" is the first thing a Reddit skeptic will ask.

**Learning Paths.** This page says the Beginner path is *"~40 min on the short route; ~1 h 55 m if you read step 5 in full"*, with step 5 as "read its 'If you read nothing else' box" (~4 min). That's clear, but the landing page card says "6 steps, ~35 min". The landing page also calls step 5 "a ~16,300-word page", while the Honest Assessment page calls itself "~18,000 words". These are small mismatches, but for a site this careful about its numbers they stood out.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (tool 1).** From the landing page I expected to see the idea at work. The setup explanation is great: *"The dashed line is what rotation curves should look like with only visible matter… The blue dots are what we actually observe. The gap is the 'dark matter problem.'"* That's the clearest dark-matter explanation I've read on any site. Then the chart legend says the violet line is "Synchronism's real C(ρ) equation" with *"max C on this disk: 0.001 — inert by construction"*, and the amber dotted line is *"the hand-tuned tanh(radius) stand-in this page displayed before 2026-07-08 — it fit because it was drawn to fit."* So the tool shows that the real equation doesn't fill the gap, and green MOND matches the dots best. That's honest, but I had to work it out myself, and the only choice is a galaxy dropdown with five options. No sliders. There's no one-line "what you should notice" box at the top. And being linked from the landing page in a row next to "Key Claims" primed me to expect a success.

**Coherence Explorer (tool 2).** It has a γ slider (default 0.49) and a ρcrit slider (default 1.00), a log/linear toggle, three γ presets, and readouts C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785. Moving γ changes how steep the S is, which is fun and intuitive. I was a bit let down that *"the density axis has no units, so no preset puts you 'at' a galaxy, a metal, or a brain."* I wanted to drag a slider to "water" or "neutron star". Then the caveat says the γ-sharpness relationship is *"structurally inverted"* compared with real physics: superconductors come out flat, and ideal gases (which have no real transition) come out sharp. So the dial I just played with points the wrong way for real materials? That's honest, but it left me unsure what the toy is supposed to teach me.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — mostly intuitive setup, confusing takeaway. The dashed/dots explanation of the dark-matter gap is excellent. The only control is a five-galaxy dropdown (default DDO 154). The violet "real equation" line is labeled "inert by construction" (max C 0.001), with a hand-tuned stand-in shown for comparison, so the tool mainly demonstrates the failure. That's honest, but it isn't stated up front.
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — intuitive to play with, confusing to interpret. γ and ρcrit sliders, log/linear toggle, three presets, readouts at 1×/10×/100× ρcrit. It's fun to watch the S-curve sharpen. But there are no units and no real-world anchors, and the stated inversion versus real physics leaves the lesson unclear.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What exactly is the star-cluster question that's "still open", and what result would settle it?
2. Who are the "domain expert" and the auditors? Is any human outside the project checking this?
3. If the equation is inert on galaxy disks and the sharpness dial points the wrong way for real materials, what is the equation still good for? Is "Where it's useful" the answer? (I didn't get there.)
4. What is a "root" and a "registration" on the scoreboard, and why do 6 refutations become 5?
5. Why is a 6.5× miss on superconductors a "reparametrization" instead of a failure?
6. Where did a consciousness equation come from?
7. Did I miss Parts B–G of First Encounter, or are they not there?

### Verdict
I've never seen a theory's own website lead with "it did not hold as stated," and that honesty is why I kept clicking. The beginner-level explanations (marching band, dimmer switch, "tanh is just an S-shaped curve", the dark-matter gap in the plotter) are genuinely good. But they're scattered across pages. Meanwhile the landing page and the scoreboard talk in insider bookkeeping, and after 40 minutes the one open question I'm invited to care about, star clusters, is never explained to me. It would be a stronger hook if the landing page gave me the "If you read nothing else" box plus one plain sentence about star clusters, and a link to go look.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /learning-paths, /glossary, /interactive-tools, /honest-assessment [badge-definitions section], /test-catalog, /key-claims, /tier-1-existing [structure only], /gamma-calculator, /equation-walkthrough, plus targeted phrase checks on /research-philosophy and /for-researchers)

*Method note:* WebFetch summaries paraphrase, so I checked every quote and count below against the live HTML, stripped to plain text. Word and date counts come from that plain text.

### Journey

**Landing page: nav, legend, acronyms.** The top nav has eight items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment), and the footer mirrors it plus For Researchers, Key Claims and What this is not. That's clean. MOND, MRH, A2ACW and BAO are expanded on first use. RAR, BTFR, SPARC, ALFALFA–SDSS, ΔBIC, DESI and CHSH are not expanded on the page. The Glossary does define all of them, and it says "Hover over highlighted terms on any page." I couldn't test hover, so this is only a problem if these terms aren't highlighted.

The badge legend line is good practice: "Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed — definitions on the Honest Assessment page. ('Validated' is deprecated; 'Withdrawn' is an operational state, not a badge.)" The anchor `#validation-badge-definitions` exists and sits ~345 words into a 17,643-word page, so the link lands where it should. One gap: the equation gloss above the legend calls the γ formula "audited-negative". That's a badge from the *other* family, and it isn't in the landing legend.

**The finding I'd escalate: the landing page contradicts itself on who audited.** The scoreboard cell was rewritten on 2026-09-15 and now says: *"'Validated' claims surviving audit: 0 of 9 … Who audited: LLM agents … not an outside domain expert — so this line is instrument-uncalibrated."* Its revision note explains that a visitor found "external domain-expert audit" on /research-philosophy, contradicting /for-researchers. But ~1,000 words earlier on the **same page**, the Key Claims A2ACW card still says: *"The decisive number: 0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit (all demoted to Reparametrization or Failed). Volume (~3,300 sessions) is not evidence; the 0/6 retention rate is."* That's a different denominator (6 vs 9) and a different auditor (expert vs LLM) on one page. A search for the retired phrasing found it in at least five more places:
- **/glossary**, "Validated" entry: *"The 0 of 6 'Validated' claims that survived expert audit were all demoted…"* The sentence is also garbled: zero claims can't "all" be demoted.
- **/honest-assessment**: *"Novel-survivor count after external expert audit: 0 of 6"* and *"Novel-surviving yield after domain-expert audit: 0. Every output that a domain expert examined resolved as a reparametrization…"* Pass 1 also tripped on this sentence.
- **/for-researchers**, novelty statement: *"of those, 6 received external expert audit, and 0 survived."* Elsewhere the same page says "LLM agents, not an external human domain expert." I couldn't tell whether this sentence is current text or a quoted superseded version. That ambiguity is its own problem (see below).
- **/interactive-tools**, above the Consciousness demo: *"Expert reviewers have flagged it as the site's largest credibility liability."* Which reviewers? This has the same unnamed-authority problem the 09-15 fix addressed.

/research-philosophy is now correct ("performed by LLM agents, not an external domain expert"). The fix landed where it was reported and nowhere else.

The landing scoreboard reproduces its entire superseded cell in full: *"The cell as it read before today: Refutations executed … 6 executed tests collapse to 5 independent roots…"* A first-time reader now sees two versions of the headline numbers, one after the other. The Beginner Path card holds a 60-word correction note inside a parenthetical in the middle of its call to action ("Corrected 2026-09-10: this read '~30 min' while step 5 is…"). I understand why: visible corrections are part of this site's honesty stance, and I don't want that lost. But nothing structural separates *current claim* from *history of the claim*. Correction notes also contain the stale wording, so a search for a retired phrase will match the notes as well as live copy. A maintainer can't tell a live occurrence from a quoted one without reading context.

**Internal workflow vocabulary leaks into public copy.** Landing: "added 2026-07-30 after two visitor personas independently found them"; "a graduate-physics reader took the mismatch as…"; the 09-15 note says "a researcher visitor found…". The γ Calculator says "see ncorr-operational-definition-recipe in the explorer topic queue". /honest-assessment mentions "visitor persona(s)" 6 times and "maintainer track"/"explorer track" 4 times. A public reader isn't told what a "visitor persona", "explorer topic queue" or "maintainer track" is, or that these are AI agents. "A graduate-physics reader" reads as a real human reviewer, which is the same unnamed-authority slippage as "expert audit".

**Badge legends: three copies, three sets of definitions.** The Honest Assessment calls itself the "canonical reference", and the Glossary matches it. The compact "Validation Badge Guide" at the top of /test-catalog does not:

| Badge | Honest Assessment / Glossary | Test Roadmap guide |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel-Paths | "not currently in active focus but not abandoned" ("on the shelf", Glossary) | "competing approaches" |
| Speculative | "Conceptual proposal without quantitative test" | "reasoning gap" |
| Audited-Negative | "Closed audit finding on a historical track; durable record; does not move" | "closed as wrong" |

"Competing approaches" and "on the shelf" are different concepts. The Honest Assessment badge contract itself is well-written: *"The color and leading word(s) are the formal status … Any text after the dash is a free-text finding descriptor."* That explains labels like "Speculative — Untestable as Stated", which I'd otherwise have flagged as undefined badges.

**"MRH" means two things.** On the landing page, MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon) is a physics concept: "the bubble of nearest neighbors that actually influence a system." The same acronym also names a badge family, "MRH-relationship tags", with its own gloss on the Honest Assessment: "the framework's term for the bounded context something is currently relevant within". "Active-MRH" is an editorial status ("currently in active research focus"). A reader who just learned MRH as a physical horizon will try to read "Active-MRH" physically. The site's preferred badge family is also nearly absent from content: 0 uses on the landing page or Key Claims, 2 on the Honest Assessment. The Glossary still teaches all five up front.

**Learning Paths.** This page is structurally clean. Paths are "By Difficulty" (Beginner 6 / Intermediate 8 / Advanced 8) and Tracks are "By Topic" (Physics 7 / Chemistry 6 / Philosophy 7 / Methodology 6), each with a time estimate. The landing cards use the same vocabulary ("Advanced Path step 8 of 8", "Methodology Track step 1 of 6"). The Beginner timing now splits into short and full routes (~40 min / ~1 h 55 m). Pass 1 already noted the landing still says ~35 min. Minor: Glossary is Beginner step 6 and "Final step" at ~5 min, but it's a 9,742-word, 90-term reference. That's fine if step 6 reads as "bookmark this", which it nearly does.

**Breadcrumbs.** They are inconsistent across page types:
- Tool pages link their parent: `Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator`, with both ancestors clickable.
- Hub pages don't: `Home / Start Here / Learning Paths`, `Home / Start Here / Glossary`, `Home / Start Here / Key Claims`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap`. In each, only "Home" is a link.
- "Start Here" is a nav item pointing to /why-synchronism, but in the breadcrumb it's an unclickable category.
- "Predictions & Experiments" appears in no menu at all, so readers get a section name they can't navigate to.

**Key Claims.** The three H2s are "Quantum Mechanics Is Synchronization Physics", "Could Consciousness Have an Equation?" and "Dark Matter Is Incomplete Decoherence". That's two flat assertions and one question, while the site's own opening is a question. Heading #3 carries a **Failed** badge, but the first content under it is the a₀ = cH₀/(2π) relation, described as "consistent" at 0.7σ. The page's own "What's not on this page" section lists that relation among the five Reparametrizations. So the badge is Failed, the lead content is consistent, and the same result is catalogued as a reparametrization. As a non-physicist I can't tell what the Failed badge applies to. The heading, badge and first paragraph need to agree.

**Interactive Tools index.** The index intro contradicts itself in consecutive sentences: *"Each card shows an epistemic status badge so you know what you're looking at before you click in. Before the legend: this is a content grouping, not the site's validation badge taxonomy."* The rest of the legend is good: Model Explainer is a grouping, and Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced plus Artifact Lesson is the level scale. The γ Calculator card promises "Presets: gas, liquid, crystal, BCS superconductor, BEC". The tool actually has seven presets: Ideal gas, Liquid water, Galaxy — SPARC best fit, Enzyme site, Ferromagnet, BEC, BCS superconductor. There's no "crystal", and three presets are missing from the card.

**Landing → For Researchers.** I skipped the onboarding. **What was actually new.** The one structural commitment that differs from MOND is the floored coupling's **bounded boost B ≤ 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17**. It forces a BTFR slope of 3.35 (MOND pipeline 3.81, observed 3.75 ± 0.10, kill fired at 3.3σ) and caps f_DM at 68.5%, while SPARC's maximum observed f_DM of 0.927 needs B ≥ 13.7. The nesting argument on For Researchers is correct and well put: MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17} can only tie or lose on χ², but a ceiling fixed by cosmology *would* have won under a complexity penalty had it held. Everything else I checked reduces to known physics:
- **Galaxy sector at free γ**: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) at γ = ½ is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). The site states this as an identity, not a coincidence. Correct.
- **Dark-energy sector**: ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C. At γ = ½, (1−C)/C = 2/x, so ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit = Λ. Correct, and Key Claims says it plainly ("by one Möbius cancellation").
- **Environment lever (TEST-05)**: ambient density in place of g_ext was a genuine discriminator. It got r² = 0.0001 against Cosmicflows-4 and is dead.
- **Wide binaries (TEST-02)**: the density branch predicts a Newtonian null, and the acceleration branch inherits μ_simple's ≈1.4× boost with the MW EFE. Neither can beat MOND or GR. The site's "survival without points" is right.
- **LIV (CPSU 2004)** and **CHSH**: restatements of known results. The site says so.

**The γ ≈ ½ pattern, and the one place the site overreads it.** Both "selections" land at 2γ = 1 for a single structural reason. The tanh-log is a Hill function of (1+x) with index 2γ, and index 1 is its *linear, non-cooperative* member: the only member where the galaxy sector is μ_simple and the DE sector is a constant. So SPARC's 0.489 and DESI's 0.487 aren't two measurements agreeing on a universal constant. They are two datasets independently deleting the framework's distinctive content (a collective transition). Honest Assessment already says the galaxy half ("the data set the cooperativity parameter to the value meaning there is no collective transition"). But the GC-fork paragraph still says *"If γ is universal — the 'one equation' reading, and the value SPARC and DESI both select —"*, and Key Claims says *"DESI DR2 independently selects γ = 0.487"*, both without an uncertainty. My back-of-envelope for this family: w(z) runs from −2γ at high z to w₀ today, with dw₀/dγ ≈ −0.5 near γ = ½. Current w constraints (~±0.03) then imply σ(γ) ~ 0.06. Pairing 0.487 with 0.489 therefore displays three digits of agreement that the DESI constraint doesn't support. Incidentally, for γ < ½ this family has w₀ > −1 but w *rising* into the past (w_a > 0), the opposite sign of DESI's preferred evolution. That's why it can only pay ΛCDM's crossing cost and never improve on it, which matches the site's Δχ² = −0.3.

**Two MOND baselines on the same 153 discs, which corroborates Pass 3.** For Researchers quotes the reference *"MOND simple-μ's χ²/N = 21.2"* for the field-equation runs on 153 SPARC discs. Parameter Derivations says the compander "fails on its own" at 108.10 vs *"MOND µ χ²/N = 51.45"* ("same argument, same floor, same 153-disc sample"). A MOND μ with a floor, keyed on g_N, isn't MOND. The site's own number for real MOND on that sample is 2.4× better. Against 21.2 the compander is 5.1× worse, not 2.10×. And at γ ≈ ½ the two functions are identical up to a₀ → a₀/2, so any residual factor has to come from γ ≠ ½, an a₀ that wasn't refit, or the floor. "Form isolated" isn't established until the comparison uses the 21.2 baseline and refits a₀ per function.

**The globular-cluster window: the missing term is refraction of the Galactic field.** This is the site's one "registered per-object test" and one of its five citable results, so I read it as a referee would. The site's own caveats are good: the cutoffs were set at execution (the MOND+EFE benchmark itself fits worse than Newton: −0.093 vs −0.057), the mass profiles are Newtonian N-body fits, it gives a ratio rather than a σ, and NGC 2419, Pal 4 and Pal 14 are excluded. But the physics claim underneath it is *"a density-keyed law has no external field to appeal to"*, and that holds only for the algebraic g = g_N/C reading or for an isolated cluster. The site names ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ as "the framework's own field equation". Under that equation, a cluster whose core sits above the knee (C → 1) inside a Galactic halo at the floor (C = 0.315) is a high-permittivity inclusion in a low-permittivity medium. It *refracts* the Milky Way's field. The sharp-sphere dielectric result gives an interior field of 3C_out/(C_in + 2C_out) ≈ 0.58 g_ext, and the graded shell (exactly where the outer-dispersion bins sit) carries a non-uniform field of order 0.4 g_ext. For R_GC = 5–22 kpc, g_ext ≈ 0.7–3×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². A 2×10⁵ M☉ cluster's own gravity at 20 pc is ≈ 7×10⁻¹¹ m/s². So the missing term is of order the internal outer-halo gravity, and it scales with g_ext, not with Newtonian tides (∝ g_ext·r/R_GC, ~300× smaller). MOND Unification's reassurance that *"its extra polarization force is ≤2×10⁻⁵ of gravity — negligible at this framework's own knee"* is evaluated at the unreachable A·V² knee. The GC table tests a knee of 0.161 M☉/pc³, which sits *inside* the clusters. This is my estimate, not a solved model: the term is dipolar and anisotropic, so its projected-dispersion signature depends on sightline. But it means the statements "MOND survives these clusters because of the EFE and for no other reason" and "density-keyed has no external field" are unproven for the field-equation reading, and the exclusion window could move in either direction. This is the first thing I'd ask for before anyone cites the window. The honest summary would be that density-keyed gravity has a *linear* external-field dependence (refraction), not MOND's *nonlinear* one, rather than "EFE = 0 exactly".

**Which knee is "the framework's"?** For Researchers says *"x is bounded at ~0.02 in every sector at every ℓ — the knee is out of reach by ~40× with no fitted parameter."* That corollary uses ρ_crit ∝ V². The GC table uses a fixed "measured" knee of 0.161, the solar/cluster joint window slides between 0.0039 and 0.078, and a 650 A·V² value also appears. "Every sector" is true only for the V²-keyed variant. Separately, a knee keyed on the host's V_flat is already a global quantity, so the V²-keyed law isn't local in the first place. That makes the "local-density no-go" label slightly off-target for the framework's own ρ_crit. The no-go is still a fine statement about genuinely local knees.

**"Closed-form identity" with Refracted Gravity needs a γ qualifier.** For Researchers: RG's ε(ρ) *"is the same object as this framework's C_Ω (closed-form identity, 2026-08-26)"*. If C_Ω is the tanh-log form, then setting ½[1+tanh(Q ln x)] = tanh(γ ln(1+x)) requires (1+x)^{2γ} = 1 + 2x^{2Q} for all x. That holds only at γ = Q = ½ (with ρ_c → 2ρ_c). Below the knee, tanh-log is linear in x at *every* γ (C ≈ γx), while RG goes as x^{2Q}. So the GC window's γ = 2 row ("narrowing to 0.5–100") is not RG with Q = 2, and "Hill index ≈ 1" is an index in (1+x), not in x. If C_Ω is instead defined as RG's form, the page should say so. The RG discussion is otherwise the most literature-literate section on the site: it runs RG at its published parameters with a full axisymmetric solver, flags the ln/log 2.3× ambiguity in Q, and quotes Cesare+2020's own 0.1–0.3 dex underestimate. I'd cite it.

**Cassini (TEST-25).** Honest Assessment calls +17.95σ *"by far the sharpest number on the site."* It isn't marginalized over a₀, M/L or RAR-fit uncertainty. DHF24's marginalized 8.7σ is the defensible figure, and the exclusion of slow-return μ by Solar-System data is prior art (Hees et al. 2016, correctly cited on Galaxy Rotation but not on the HA TEST-25 row). The demotions that *do* stand, stand on explicit derivations (the μ_simple identity is a theorem, not an audit verdict). What makes this site reliable is executable scripts plus arithmetic that holds up when a reader redoes it (Pass 3 and I both did), not the adversarial protocol. Research Philosophy also opens with *"The combined pattern … is itself a publishable finding about AI-generated science"* and closes with *"the honest claim is a well-documented program-level null, not a measured ceiling."* The opener should match the close. The missing control isn't only cross-vendor or post-cutoff: it's the **human base rate**. Most human-proposed MOND alternatives also collapse to a MOND interpolating function or fail on the RAR, so "every surviving prediction is degenerate with MOND" doesn't distinguish AI generation from heterodox-gravity programs in general.
