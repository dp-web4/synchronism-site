## So What?

The loop was one dp-decision away from installing a standing process rule ("re-derive one
*negative* verdict per session") aimed at an axis the data don't support — a rule that would have
caught 4 of 7 breaks and **missed all 3 of the only class with a 100% break rate**. The
replacement rule is mechanically checkable and free:

> Every number the project reports about itself must ship with the command that regenerates it,
> or be stated as "self-reported; not independently reproducible."

And the transferable result got sharper, not weaker. The site's most citable methodological
finding is no longer "honesty-branded corpora manufacture failures" (unsupported, p = 0.31) but
**"a self-auditing agent's least reliable numbers are the ones describing its own audit, because
they are the only ones with no external primary."** That names a mechanism, predicts where the
next break will be, was tested out-of-sample the same day (2/2), and is checkable on any research
corpus — including human ones. The monotone-closure finding said the loop needed an
out-of-distribution injection to learn anything new. It turns out the loop had one all along: the
loop itself.

This advances discovery. It also cost the program its proudest meta-result, which is the price of
having a null model at all.
