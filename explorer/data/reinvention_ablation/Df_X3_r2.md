**Short answer:** I found no published model with this exact form. I think it is probably unpublished as stated. It does sit in a family of known ideas, and I could only partly verify the links.

**What I found (searches done, details from snippets only):**
- **Closest published relative:** McCulloch & Lucio (2019), "Testing Newton/GR, MoND and quantised inertia on wide binaries," Astrophys. Space Sci. (arXiv:1908.01434). Quantised inertia (QI) uses a modified inertial mass, m_I = m_g(1 − 2c²/(AΘ)). Here A is the acceleration and Θ is the horizon distance, which is a Hubble-scale quantity. They applied it to Gaia DR2 wide binaries. The mass correction depends on acceleration, not on orbital period. The logarithmic form, κ = 0.31, and τ = 10⁻⁶·2π/H₀ do not appear in what I saw.
- **Other modified-inertia work:** Milgrom (2023, arXiv:2310.14334, per the search snippet) treats MOND as modified inertia. This is an acceleration-based and nonlocal-in-time framework, and it has no period-dependent mass. I could not verify the details.
- **Observational context:** Chae, Hernandez, and Banik et al. disagree on whether wide binaries show a velocity boost. Examples are arXiv:2312.03162, arXiv:2601.21728, and arXiv:2410.17178. A period-dependent inertia would need to be tested against these analyses.

**Assessment:**
- Both QI and MOND-as-modified-inertia change inertia at low acceleration, and the proposal can be seen as a variant of that idea.
- Wide-binary acceleration falls with separation, and P ∝ a^(-3/4) at fixed mass. A period cutoff therefore acts roughly like an acceleration threshold. This is my own inference, not a result from the sources.
- A mass that depends on a system's orbital period is not a local quantity. It also needs a prescription for non-orbiting or non-periodic motion, and the proposal as given lacks one. I did not find this addressed in the literature I saw.
- The scale τ = 10⁻⁶ × 2π/H₀ and the value κ = 0.31 look like fitted or tuned parameters. I found no precedent for them.
- A log-in-period inertia could conflict with Solar System and planetary tests only if the effect is not suppressed for P ≪ τ. Here 1 + κ ln(1 + P/τ) is close to 1 for short periods, so that is plausible, but it still needs checking.

**Conclusion:** I believe it is not published in this form, but I am not certain. My search was shallow. I did not read the full papers, and I did not check Milgrom's nonlocal-inertia work or older "Mach-type" proposals in depth. Treat any link to QI or Milgrom as uncertain.
