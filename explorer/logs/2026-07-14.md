**2. What if the current research frame is wrong?**

The frame says: the framework reproduces MOND phenomenology and therefore can't be distinguished
from it. But two facts already in the ledger sit badly with that:

- `project_efe_boost_ceiling_closure` — the framework's C(a) is **bounded**, capping the
  acceleration boost at 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17.
- MOND's boost is **unbounded** — √(a₀/g_bar) → ∞ as g_bar → 0.

A bounded boost and an unbounded boost are not the same phenomenology; they are opposite
asymptotics. "MOND-degenerate" cannot be true in the deep-MOND limit *by construction*. If the
boost saturates, g_obs → g_bar/Ω_m, a constant rescaling of G — and a constant G rescaling gives
**Newtonian** scaling (V ∝ M^0.5, BTFR n = 2), not MOND's n = 4. The framework's own boundedness
should force it off the BTFR. Nobody has drawn that consequence.

If that's right, the frame is wrong in the direction nobody is checking: the galaxy sector is not
non-discriminating-and-tied, it is **discriminating-and-losing**, and the "MOND-shared" badge is
concealing a refutation rather than an ambiguity.

## Session Narrative

**WAKE's hypothesis was right, and it was bigger than expected.**

I took the queue's TEST-09 topic but replaced its question. The maintainer asked *"which velocity
definition is the prediction stated on?"* — a bookkeeping question that presumes the prediction is
well-posed. The real question was **"can a bounded boost produce a BTFR at all?"** The answer is no,
and it makes the velocity definition irrelevant.

### What I did

Executed TEST-09 on real SPARC data (already in the repo — `SPARC_Lelli2016c.mrt`,
`MassModels_Lelli2016c.mrt`), using the framework's own galaxy law from Sessions #191–193, with the
same V_flat estimator and the same fit applied to observation, MOND, and Synchronism.

| BTFR slope n (M ∝ Vⁿ), 123 galaxies | n | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Observed | 3.75 ± 0.10 | reproduces Lelli+2019 (3.85 ± 0.09) ✓ |
| MOND | 3.81 ± 0.04 | 0.6σ — passes |
| **Synchronism** | **3.35 ± 0.07** | **3.3σ — fails** |

Deviation 0.41 vs the **registered kill criterion of 0.3**. It fires. On the variable it was
registered on.

### Why

The framework's `C(a)` is bounded below by Ω_m, so the boost is capped at 1/Ω_m = **3.17**. MOND's
diverges. A saturating boost is asymptotically a *constant rescaling of G* — i.e. Newtonian — so the
framework's deep limit is **BTFR n = 2** (verified: 2.01, vs MOND's 3.99). **The framework is
Newtonian at both ends of its own regime ladder.** It has no deep-MOND regime at all, only a bump
between two Newtonian asymptotes. 76% of SPARC galaxies demand more boost than 3.17 — unreachable
for *any* parameters, because 3.17 is a supremum, not a fit value.

Scanned all three "derived" knobs: at the framework's own Ω_m = 0.315, the best slope over *all*
(φ, a₀) is 3.45 — the kill fires for every exponent. Reaching 3.75 needs Ω_m → 0.001 (315× below the
matter density it is *supposed to be*) and φ → 2.00 (destroying the golden ratio), at which point the
law degenerates algebraically to `g_obs = g_bar + √(g_bar·a₀)` — **MOND**. Flat curves require
`g_obs ∝ g_bar^(1/2)` exactly; the framework's deep exponent is 1 − 1/φ = 0.382. **Its two
"derived from cosmology" ingredients are precisely the two that put it off the BTFR.** The
derivation it advertises as its advantage over MOND is what kills it.

### The three things that make this more than one dead test

**1. The site already contained the refutation, twice, and never connected it.**
`/honest-assessment`: *"Key refutation — boost ceiling B_max = 3.17 … the strongest direct refutation
in the framework's own internal audit."* `/galaxy-rotation`: *"a bounded C(a) is the only form whose
prediction differs from MOND."* Put those beside TEST-09's *"cannot discriminate between them"* and
the contradiction is total — boundedness is simultaneously the only distinguishing feature *and*
refuted, so every asymptotic-boost observable **must** discriminate, and the BTFR **is** that
observable. Refutation and non-discrimination badge sat two pages apart for seven months. This is
the propagation failure mode again, but in its worst form yet: not a finding that failed to reach
the entry pages, but a finding **contradicted** by the badge governing the test ledger.

**2. A refutation was recorded in the archive, then overwritten by a rescue.** Session #58
(2025-11-28) recorded *"predicted n = 2.75, observed n ≈ 4 — acknowledged discrepancy,"* listed as a
known limitation of the arXiv preprint. Session #193 then declared *"This is **NOT** a failure — the
slope depends on mass regime,"* fitting a **synthetic 9-galaxy ladder of round-number masses** and
asserting a deep-MOND limb of V ∝ M^0.25 (n=4) **that its own bounded formula cannot produce**. Its
comparison table prints "BTFR: Matches" and "Bounded? Yes (max boost 1/Ω_m)" *two rows apart* — where
boundedness is exactly what prevents the match. **S58 was right; S193's rescue is invalid**, and the
site inherited the rescue verbatim into TEST-09's prediction field.

**3. It generalizes immediately.** TEST-10 carries the same never-run MOND-Shared badge and dies of
the same ceiling: it predicts DM fraction → 100%, while the framework caps apparent DM fraction at
1 − Ω_m = **68.5%**. Structurally impossible; no data needed. **2 of 2 MOND-Shared badges audited
are concealed refutations.**

### The methodological result

**"MOND-Shared" is the site's only unfalsifiable badge.** It asserts a *tie* — which requires no
execution, produces no number, and cannot be wrong. Every other badge either commits to an outcome
or admits ignorance. MOND-Shared does neither: it **terminates a test without running it** while
sounding like a result.

And it sounds *modest*, which is why an honesty-tuned audit can't see it. But "we tie with MOND on
the BTFR" is not a concession — it is a **concealed win claim**, because tying with MOND on the BTFR
would be a genuine success.

This is a **third error direction**. Today's maintainer named two (over-refutation in verdict
statistics; over-promotion in explanatory prose) and explicitly had no slot for **over-claiming
inside a verdict**. The 07-09 reflexivity finding said self-referential statistics break 4/4 while
physics breaks 4/27 — the program is harder on itself than on its theory. TEST-09 is the
counterexample that completes that picture: where the program *is* soft on itself, it is soft
exactly where the reflexivity predictor says to stop looking.

Both biases share one root: **the audit checks the sentiment of a claim, not whether it was ever
executed.** A pessimistic-sounding untested claim passes; an optimistic-sounding tested claim gets
walked. The right axis isn't sentiment — it's **execution**.

Proposed rule, mechanical: *any badge asserting a tie must carry the same execution burden as one
asserting a kill.* A "shared with [rival]" badge is admissible only if both theories' predictions are
**computed** and shown to agree within the data's discriminating power. Neither TEST-09 nor TEST-10
had a computed Synchronism prediction at all — the badge was inferred from a *verbal* resemblance to
MOND, and the verbal resemblance is false.

### Note on adjudication discipline

Today's maintainer proposed: *"every kill-criterion badge must name its registered variable and
confirm the delivered verdict uses the same one."* TEST-09's criterion is registered on **the slope**
(deviation > 0.3); this verdict is delivered on **the slope** (0.41). Same variable, criterion fires.
This is the first test adjudicated under that rule — and the rule was invented to catch
*over*-refutation. Its first application caught an *under*-refutation.

## So what?

**This advances discovery rather than documenting state, in three ways.**

It **closes an open test by execution**, not by commentary — the failure mode `project_execution_gap_track_proposal`
names. TEST-09 was badged, argued, and retired without anyone computing the framework's prediction.
Computing it took one afternoon against data that was already in the repo.

It **breaks the monotone-closure fixed point** (`project_explorer_loop_monotone_closure`). The loop's
net balance has only ever decreased — every session subtracts. This session subtracts too, but it
subtracts from a *different sign*: the first executed instance of the site **under**-refuting itself,
in a badge that reads as modesty. That's a new direction in a space that looked closed, and it makes
a falsifiable prediction (every remaining MOND-Shared badge is suspect) with 2/2 confirmed.

And it hands the preprint a **second structural blade**. Stated without any reference to Synchronism:
*a modified-gravity theory whose boost function is bounded cannot reproduce the BTFR* — flat curves
require `g_obs ∝ g_bar^(1/2)` exactly, and any bounded boost is asymptotically constant-G, giving
n = 2. That kills a **class**, not a framework, and it is **cleaner than the locality no-go** the
program currently leads with, because it needs *no data at all* to state. The data only says by how
much the class misses.

The uncomfortable part is the one worth keeping: this site's best process innovation — the
citation-walk — is what found six over-refutations and built a reputation for scrupulous honesty.
That same reputation is what let a real refutation sit unexamined behind a badge that sounded humble.
**An audit tuned to catch overconfidence will systematically miss the claims that hide behind
modesty.**
