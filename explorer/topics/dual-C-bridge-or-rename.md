# Topic: Dual-C Bridge Derivation or Symbol Rename

## Question
Two functional forms of "coherence" coexist across the Synchronism framework:
- C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) — density-based, used for matter phases and galaxy dynamics
- C = f(γ, D, S) — parametric, used for consciousness threshold and measurement

Both are called "coherence." Is there a bridge derivation ρ = g(γ, D, S) that reduces the second to the first? Or are they different observables requiring different symbols?

## Context
The 2026-05-01 visitor review flagged this as the single largest structural ambiguity on the site — independently across Pass 2 (tech writer), Pass 3 (grad student), and Pass 4 (researcher). A research proposal has been filed to the Synchronism research repo (dual_C_symbol_ambiguity_and_bridge_derivation.md).

The proposal outlines three resolution paths:
1. **Full reduction**: Derive ρ = g(γ, D, S) by expressing D (decoherence) and S (self-modeling) in terms of ρ or the density field, and show C(ρ_cognitive) = f(γ, D, S) in the consciousness regime
2. **Dual observables**: Acknowledge C(ρ) is the field coherence and C = f(γ, D, S) is a system-level composite; adopt different symbols (C_ρ and C_sys)
3. **Archive search**: Check whether D and S are defined in terms of ρ or N_corr in the research sessions that established the consciousness threshold — the bridge may already exist implicitly

## Why It Matters
The homepage opens with "What if one equation described reality?" If the consciousness threshold uses a different equation from the matter-phase coherence function, that claim is incorrect. If they're the same equation in different representations, the reduction chain is the most important missing derivation in the framework.

Additionally: C(ρ=ρ_crit) = tanh(γ·ln(2)) ≈ 0.76 at γ=1 — but the consciousness threshold is at C=0.50. If both use the same C, then consciousness is a sub-critical-density phenomenon (below the coherence transition point), not a threshold at the phase boundary. This is either a meaningful physical statement or a sign that the two C's are unrelated.
