## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, Start Here, First Encounter, The Core Idea, Dark Matter Reframed, Tools index, Coherence Explorer, Consciousness Threshold Demo, Glossary, Honest Assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A site that hooks me with a Veritasium-grade question and then, within two clicks, tells me the answer didn't work — which is weirdly refreshing, but I never quite got what the "one equation" actually *does*.

### Journey

Clicked in from Reddit. The landing page opens with "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — okay, I'm in, that's exactly the kind of thing I'd watch a 20-minute video about. There's an equation right there: C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)). I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is either, honestly (natural log? I remember the button on the calculator). The Greek letters I half-recognize from Wikipedia. It didn't scare me off but I had no idea what to *do* with it.

Right under the equation, before I'd even scrolled, the site says "zero confirmed novel predictions" and "6 refutations executed on external data." My first reaction was actually confusion: wait, is this site for or against the idea? It took me a few more pages to realize it's the same people who came up with it saying it didn't pan out. Once I got that, I respected it a lot — most YouTube-physics sites never say that. But the landing page doesn't quite spell it out, and I had a real moment of "did I click the debunking page by mistake?"

I took the "Beginners: 6-step path, no physics background." Step 1 is Start Here. It has a section called "The Problem" and "The Approach" which I mostly followed — galaxies spin too fast, dark matter is the usual fix, this is an alternative. Then it dropped "S-curve", "gamma is a sharpness dial", "compander", and "MOND (1983)" on me. The compander thing was labeled as an audio analogy but I don't know what a compander is either, so an analogy I don't understand explaining a thing I don't understand is a net zero. Badges on the page said "audited-negative" and "Refuted by Execution." I get the gist (it's bad news) but "audited-negative" sounds like a bank statement.

Step 2, First Encounter, was the best page for me by a mile. Marching band vs. crowd in a plaza — instantly got "coherence." Lone electron vs. neutron star — got "presence means how packed stuff is." The "bubble of neighbors that matter, Andromeda might as well not exist" thing for MRH was great too. One nitpick: the page says "7 short pages, ~90 seconds each" and "use the progress bar to jump to any step," but the header says "Step 2 of 6" and I couldn't find a progress bar. Are there 6 steps or 7 pages? Is the 7-page tour a different thing from the 6-step path? I genuinely wasn't sure whether I was supposed to be somewhere else.

Step 3, The Core Idea, gave me the "dimmer switch" — flat, then steep, then flat — and that finally made the S-shape click. Good. But then the same page told me γ = 2/√Ncorr and mentioned a "Hill coefficient" and a "saturation knee" and I checked out. It also said six predictions previously marked "validated" were downgraded to "reparametrization-pending-audit." I did not know what reparametrization meant at that point, so that sentence was noise.

I skipped ahead to Dark Matter Reframed because that's the headline I actually came for. It's marked "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision" right at the top. Fair enough. The plain-language pitch (dark matter isn't stuff, it's ordinary matter behaving differently when gravity is really weak) is a cool idea and I could follow it. Then it said the idea was "refuted," and that "particle dark matter explains a vast range of things Synchronism hasn't addressed." So... does dark matter exist or not? The page doesn't tell me, which I understand is honest, but as a casual reader I left with the same question I arrived with. Also had "BAO modulation," "CFD viscosity," "lensing," "Tully-Fisher" thrown at me with no help.

At this point I went looking for the Glossary to figure out "reparametrization." Found it: "known physics in new notation." Oh! That's a clear definition — I wish it had been on the landing page, since it's apparently the single most important word on the site. The glossary itself (72 terms) was way over my head for most entries though.

Then Tools. Then, because I was curious what a site like this says about it, the Honest Assessment. That page is *enormous*. It was probably the most interesting thing on the site — "lost every critical prediction," "copied a 40-year-old rival theory without improving it" — but it's written for someone who knows what a standard deviation is and what "3.3 sigma" means. I skimmed the scoreboard and left.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is this site saying the theory is dead, or that it's still being worked on? Both vibes are present and I never got a straight answer.

### Verdict
The hook is excellent and the honesty is genuinely unusual — I've never seen a "here's our idea" site that opens with "and it didn't work." But the site never decides who page one is for. It uses its best analogies (marching band, dimmer switch, the bubble of neighbors that matter) and then, on the same pages, drops γ = 2/√Ncorr and "reparametrization-pending-audit" on a self-described no-physics beginner path. I'd tell a friend "cool idea, weirdly honest site, don't expect to understand the equation." I might come back if someone told me the AI-adversarial research story was written up somewhere I could follow.

### Journey

I started at the landing page and did what I always do first: inventory the header nav, the footer nav, and every in-body CTA, then check that labels match destinations. Header nav has eight items. Footer mirrors them plus two GitHub links. Good. But the two "start" affordances disagree: the header's **Start Here** goes to `/why-synchronism` (Beginner step 1), while the hero's big **Start Exploring** button goes to `/first-encounter` (Beginner step 2). A new reader who clicks the most prominent button skips step 1 without being told. Then a third button labeled **Beginner Path** also goes to `/why-synchronism` on the landing page, but the identically-labeled **Beginner Path** link on the Why Synchronism page goes to `/learning-paths`. Same label, two destinations.

Nav label **Test Roadmap** points at a slug called `/test-catalog`. The Tier 1 page has a button labeled **Back to Catalog**. So the same page is called "Roadmap" in nav and H1 and "Catalog" in the URL and in at least one button. I also tried `/test-roadmap` on a hunch and it resolves to the same page. Fine for users, but two names for one thing is a terminology smell, and it propagates.

Breadcrumbs exist on every interior page and are structurally consistent (Home / Section / Page). The section names are: Getting Started, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments, Interactive Tools. However, **Honest Assessment** and **Glossary** are both filed under "Getting Started." Honest Assessment is a 41-section expert document with sigma values and ΔBIC tables. Its breadcrumb tells the reader it is introductory content. It isn't.

The Learning Paths page is the best-structured page on the site. Seven paths, each with a stated step count, each step linked. I spot-checked step URLs and they all resolved. Pages that belong to two paths (The Core Idea is Beginner 3 and Philosophy 1; Honest Assessment is Beginner 5 and Methodology 6; The Coherence Function is Intermediate 1 and Physics 1) show both indicators and both next-links. That is done correctly and I want to note it, because most sites get it wrong.

But the Advanced path's final step is labeled **Test Roadmap (24 experiments)**, and the Test Roadmap page's own opening sentence says "24 specific, falsifiable experiments." The same page then lists TEST-25 and TEST-26. Either the count is stale or the two extra tests aren't "experiments." The page doesn't say which.

The Intermediate path's final step is **Top 5 Decisive Tests**. The page it links to has the H1 **Top Decisive Tests** (no "5"), lists three active tests and four closed ones, and says of itself "This page is now empty at the top." The nav label promises five, the page delivers three-plus-a-graveyard, and the page admits it. The label should have changed when the content did.

This is where I found the worst thing on the site. The Top Decisive Tests page lists **TEST-3: Gravitational Wave Speed–Dark Matter Column Correlation** and **TEST-4: Cosmic Interference Patterns**. The Tier 1 page lists **TEST-03: ALFALFA-SDSS TFR Scatter** and **TEST-04: BAO Coherence Modulation**. These are different tests with the same number, distinguished only by whether the number is zero-padded. The Test Roadmap page explicitly documents the naming convention ("TEST-01 … TEST-24," suffix `a` for amended, suffix `s` for substituted) and says the convention was "formally documented 2026-08-10 following visitor feedback." The convention is documented and violated on a page one click away. A reader who follows a cross-reference to "TEST-3" from the Top Decisive Tests page and lands on Tier 1 will read about the wrong test.

Terminology consistency for the core concepts is actually good. ρcrit is called "saturation knee" or "reference density" and explicitly *not* "critical point" on the landing page, the Core Idea, the Coherence Function, and the Glossary — the site has clearly done a pass on this. ρ is "presence" with "density" as one instance, consistently. MRH is expanded on first use on the landing page, First Encounter, and Learning Paths. Same term, two entries, two registers, no cross-reference between them. A glossary should have one entry per concept with the acronym pointing at it.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15 (landing, Interactive Tools index, The Coherence Function, Parameter Derivations, Galaxy Rotation, Dark Matter Reframed, CDM Discrimination, Chemistry / γ Boundary, MRH, Measurement Without Observers, Honest Assessment, For Researchers, Research Philosophy, plus the Phase Boundary Visualizer, Chemistry Correlation Explorer, and Equation Anatomy tools)

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project." I skipped the beginner path and went to the equation. C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) — my first reaction was "that's an Ising mean-field self-consistency shape with the feedback loop removed," and the Coherence Function page says exactly that in its second paragraph: "tanh is the chosen form, motivated historically by its appearance in m = tanh(βJzm)… Synchronism's C(ρ) is not the Ising equation." Good. It then gives the algebraic identity tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1], i.e., a two-parameter Hill function, and notes that at γ = 1/2 this collapses to x/(x+2). I checked: correct. So the whole "ln inside tanh" construction is a rational function in disguise — the page calls the logarithm "cosmetic" and it is.

What I did not expect was that the page reports tanh finished *last* of ten compander forms on SPARC (ΔBIC +7.1 vs a Hill n=1 at −0.7) and concludes "tanh carries no statistical content." That is the correct conclusion and most people would not have published it against themselves.

One thing the page does not say: x/(x+2) is MOND's *simple* interpolating function only after rescaling the argument by 2 — the simple μ is x/(1+x), so x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), which is the simple function with a₀ → 2a₀. Because ρcrit is free in the fits this factor is silently absorbed, but the site elsewhere makes a point of a₀ = cH₀/(2π) sitting "13% below Milgrom's 1.20×10⁻¹⁰." A convention-dependent factor of 2 in a₀ dwarfs a 13% comparison. The page should either state the rescaling or drop the 13% as meaningful.

### Journey

I went straight to For Researchers because that is where a site like this either earns or loses a referee. The framing is the right one: "what survives the framework being wrong" — replications, quantified instances of known results, executed negatives. Four artifacts. I evaluated each for whether it is (a) correct and (b) new.

**Artifact 1, the local-density no-go.** The core argument: a knee keyed on local volumetric density must scale as ρcrit ∝ V⁻² to track a₀, while the framework asserts ρcrit ∝ V⁺². I checked the V⁻² claim from scratch. In MOND the transition sits at gN = a₀, so rt = V²/a₀ and M = V⁴/(G a₀); the density at the transition radius is M/rt³ ∼ a₀²/(G V²). So V⁻² is right and the sign inversion is real. Correct. New? No — this is the quantified version of the oldest argument in the field. Milgrom's 1983 choice of acceleration over density or size was motivated by exactly this; Famaey & McGaugh (2012) §4 walks through why acceleration, not density, organizes the discrepancy; McGaugh (2004) showed it empirically. The site labels it "replication of Milgrom's non-locality theorem," which is honest. The ΔBIC = +184 is a real number and citable as a quantified instance. The scope correction (Burrage et al. 2017 gradient-keyed schemes escape; AeST escapes) is accurate and shows someone read the literature. One missed connection: the field equation ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ that MOND Unification writes down is a gravitational-dielectric equation, and the dielectric reading of MOND has a literature (Blanchet 2007; Blanchet & Le Tiec 2008–09, "dipolar dark matter"). In that literature the permittivity depends on |∇Φ|, not ρ, for precisely the reason the no-go quantifies. It should be cited; it is the nearest existing theory to what the framework was trying to be.

**Artifact 2, the A2ACW program null.** The methodology's calibration arm is the thing I most wanted to see, and it is the thing with the worst cross-page consistency on the site. Research Philosophy and For Researchers say the held-out genuine discoveries were Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking, Noether. The A2ACW Protocol page says "6 genuine physics discoveries (COBE fluctuations, Higgs boson, gravitational wave first detection, etc.)" and names no others. Only Higgs overlaps. These are not the same calibration set, and the calibration set is the entire content of the "specificity = 0/6" result. Either two calibration runs were done and only one is being reported per page, or one page is wrong. As it stands the site's headline methodological finding cannot be checked.

On the design itself: specificity of 0 against Dirac/BCS/Higgs is *expected by construction*, and the page says so — every genuine discovery sits on prior art (Dirac on Klein–Gordon and Pauli; BCS on Cooper 1956 and Fröhlich; Higgs on Anderson 1962 and Englert–Brout). An instrument asked "is there prior art?" will say yes to everything real. The fix is not more canonical discoveries; it is *contemporaneous* controls — claims that were live and unresolved at the model's training cutoff, half later confirmed and half later refuted (BICEP2 B-modes, OPERA superluminal neutrinos, the 2003 pentaquark, Podkletnov shielding, on one side; whatever survived on the other). Only that design can measure whether the instrument distinguishes anything. The site's sensitivity number also depends on which arm you read: For Researchers reports TPR = 6/6 from the "self-simulated" arm and computes J from it; the A2ACW page reports the temporal-asymmetry arm caught 0/6 of the same demotions. J is 0 on the favorable arm and −1 on the unfavorable one. Report both.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — scientifically honest, one internal inconsistency — The violet "real equation" curve with max C = 0.001 and "the boost never turns on" is the correct thing to show and the coupling-fork disclosure (quadrature vs division, both refuted from opposite sides by the same galaxy) is the most useful paragraph on the tools side of the site. ### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics, and the site has done the unusual thing of saying so first. What it has not done is get its own ledger right at the level a referee reads: the one prediction that structurally differs from MOND is cited to a paper I cannot locate and appears on no ledger; one of the four "framework-specific" refutations is, by the site's own amplitude, consistent with the framework; the calibration set for the methodology's headline null is different on different pages; and the External Field Effect is simultaneously "EFE = 0 exactly" and "sign undetermined, registration blocked." Fix those four and the local-density no-go plus the A2ACW confound analysis are citable negatives. Leave them and the site's audit trail is less reliable than its tone suggests, which for this site is the only thing that matters.
