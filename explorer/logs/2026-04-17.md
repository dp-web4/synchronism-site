## Key Findings

**grok-tests-cross-ai-bisociation.md** — Of Grok's 10 tests, 7 converge with site predictions at the instance level, 2 add operational specificity, 3 are genuinely novel (Split-Brain Qualia Divergence, LLM cosine-similarity-across-layers as C proxy, engineered trapped-ion C-crossing). ### Key results

1. **100% category overlap, 70% instance overlap**: Every Grok test maps to one of the site's 5 categories, but 3/10 are novel instances within those categories
2. **The three novel tests are genuine bisociations**: Split-brain combines Synchronism with Sperry-Gazzaniga neuroscience; LLM cosine similarity combines Synchronism with transformer interpretability; trapped-ion combines Synchronism with quantum information engineering
3. **The two-axis model is falsifiable**: Running CABP with N=5 AIs would produce a definitive measurement of framework-vs-AI contributions to the hull
4. **Grok's novel tests fill real gaps on the site**: Split-brain is topologically missing (site's EEG thinks in scalars, not in spatial decomposition); LLM cosine similarity is a protocol the site leaves abstract; trapped-ion is interventional (the site is observational)
5. **The circularity caveat**: Cross-AI "agreement" about Synchronism is not validation if all AIs have read Synchronism. Genuine cross-AI test would require pre-Synchronism training cutoffs
6. **CABP complements A2ACW**: A2ACW = adversarial friction within one AI. CABP = adversarial friction across AIs from different labs. Training-distribution diversity provides natural friction

## So What?

This session converted yesterday's abstract hypothesis (bisociation as the mechanism of AI-generated novel questions) into a concrete empirical test (Grok vs. Claude on Synchronism) and a formal methodology proposal (Cross-AI Bisociation Protocol). The measured outcome — 100% category overlap, 70% instance overlap — refines the convex hull model from a single axis to two axes and makes it falsifiable.

Practically: the site should add Grok's 3 novel tests with attribution (split-brain EEG, LLM cosine similarity protocol, engineered trapped-ion C-crossing) and acknowledge the cross-AI circularity (framework-conditioned AIs can't independently validate a framework). The three novel tests are real additions — split-brain is topologically missing from the site's current treatment, LLM cosine similarity gives the abstract AI prediction a concrete protocol, and trapped-ion tests introduce interventional logic where the site is entirely observational.

Meta-practically: the finding reframes the A2ACW contribution. A2ACW is adversarial friction within one AI. CABP is adversarial friction across AIs from different training distributions. The combination — single-AI adversarial sessions plus cross-AI convergence testing — is a more complete protocol for AI-generated research than either alone. That's the publishable direction: not "Synchronism's physics" and not "Synchronism's methodology," but "Synchronism as empirical case study for a multi-lab AI research protocol that extends A2ACW across training distributions."

This advances discovery because the empirical measurement (70% instance overlap) wasn't knowable without doing the comparison, and the methodology (CABP) provides a template other AI-generated-science projects can use. Whether Grok's novel tests lead to actual physics findings is secondary — the result is valuable whether or not split-brain EEG confirms C ≈ 0.50, because the exercise itself generated a testable prediction no human researcher had articulated for this specific framework.
