## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment, /core-idea)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A surprisingly honest "we tried this and it didn't work" site. I got the core crowd-vs-marching-band idea within a minute, but most pages after Step 2 turn into jargon I can't follow.

### Journey
I clicked through from Reddit expecting yet another "one equation explains everything" site. The headline asks "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" and then, in the very next line, says "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." I didn't expect that. It's disarming, and it's the reason I kept reading. The scoreboard ("Prospective predictions confirmed: 0 / Refutations executed: 6") is clear. The line under it ("6 executed tests collapse to 5 independent roots: 2 framework-specific mechanism roots + 1 refuted registration + 1 inherited from MOND + 1 Bell/CHSH construction check") lost me completely. I don't know what a "root", a "registration" or "Bell/CHSH" is. I also read that AI agents did the research (~3,300 sessions) and that no outside physicist has reviewed it. That's refreshing to see, and a bit alarming.

I clicked **Start Here** and landed on Step 1 of 6 (/why-synchronism). The marching-band vs. plaza-crowd analogy with density as a "dimmer switch" is the best thing on the site. I got it right away. MOND gets a short explanation here as a 1983 rival gravity theory, which helped.

**Step 2 (/first-encounter)** has its own "Part A–G", and the bottom says "Next: Part B of 7". So I'm on Step 2 of 6 *and* Part A of 7 at once, which is a bit disorienting. Parts A and B work well: ρ is "presence," tanh is "an S-shaped curve, like a dimmer switch," and 0 means independent while 1 means collective. After that it speeds up. Part C says the γ idea is "the original idea, and what happened to it." Part E (galaxies) says the stars are "individual uncorrelated classical particles (N_corr = 1, γ = 2)" and then talks about "a dimensional coincidence (the only scale buildable from c and H₀)… the 2π is unexplained and the match is off by ~13%". I don't know what a dimensional coincidence is, what H₀ is, or which 2π they mean. I skimmed the rest. Part F, "What if 'wave function collapse' isn't a special event?", is the Veritasium-style hook I came for, but the answer is "crossing the MRH… where correlations decay below the noise floor," and that went over my head.

I tried the **Coherence Explorer**. It has two sliders (γ and ρ_crit), a log/linear toggle, and presets. The marching-band text next to it helps. Dragging γ makes the S-curve steeper or flatter, and that part is intuitive. What confused me is that the default γ is 0.49 ("SPARC fit"), while one preset says γ = 2 is "the framework's registered value." Which one is the real theory? Nobody tells a beginner which slider setting to look at.

Next I tried the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**, which I think is the best tool for someone like me. You pick a real galaxy and watch the gray "what visible matter predicts" line sag below the dots of "what telescopes measure." For the first time I actually *saw* the dark matter problem. Then the page says the violet curve (the framework's real equation) "fails to fix it," while a hand-drawn amber curve fits "because it was drawn to fit." That honesty is great. It's also odd to have a tool whose main job is showing the idea not working, sitting next to a leftover curve kept "for comparison" from "before 2026-07-08." Baryon, SPARC, V_flat, a₀, ν and kpc all show up in the captions without being explained. There are no sliders, only a galaxy dropdown.

The **γ Calculator** is simple: enter N_corr and get γ = 2/√N_corr. The presets include ideal gas, water, galaxy, BEC and superconductor. The caveat says the formula is backwards: it gives the ideal gas the sharpest switch and superconductors the flattest, "the opposite of real condensed-matter transitions." Honest again. But the Galaxy (SPARC) preset uses N_corr = 17 and is labelled "Strongly Correlated," while Step 2 had just told me galaxy stars are "uncorrelated… N_corr = 1, γ = 2." Further down, the calculator does explain that 17 comes from back-fitting γ ≈ 0.49 and that 1 is the "galaxy pin quoted on Core Idea." A casual reader who only looks at the preset table sees two contradictory numbers for the same thing.

From the landing page I clicked "See What Failed" (/honest-assessment). It opens with a clear plain-English framing: AI agents did all of this, and the visitor track simulates readers like me (a slightly meta moment). The failure list is readable even for me: galaxy slope missed by 3.3σ, 69% of dwarf galaxies over the ceiling, Cassini test 17.95σ off, superconductor temperatures off by 6.5×, melting points off by 53% on average. The page is very long, though, with 10+ major sections, and I skimmed most of it. Its count line reads "0 confirmed predictions, 0 prospective predictions tested, 6 refutations executed (5 on external data + 1 construction check)." The landing page splits the same 6 into "5 independent roots" a different way (2 + 1 + 1 + 1). To me, "5 on external data + 1" and "5 roots" look like two different 5s. I can't tell whether they're the same thing.

Last, **Step 3 (/core-idea)**. It starts with a badge key (Failed / Reparametrization / Audited-Negative / Speculative / Untested), which I appreciated. After that it's tanh, SPARC, BTFR, ALFALFA-SDSS, Hill coefficient, compander, μ-law encoders… I bailed. The page is also "Step 1 of 7 (Philosophy Track)" at the same time as "Step 3 of 6 (Beginner Path)."

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Journey
I started at the landing page and mapped the IA first. The top nav has 10 items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment) and the footer mirrors it, adding Key Claims, the research archive and "Report an Issue". The hover titles are useful ("Glossary — 93 terms defined", "Test Roadmap — 26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)"). The landing page has no breadcrumb, which is correct for a home page.

**Acronyms on the landing page.** MOND, MRH, tanh, ln and ρ_crit are all glossed inline on first use, which is good practice. SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, DESI, CHSH and f_DM are not glossed. BAO and A2ACW are expanded in parentheses, but not at their first occurrence. The landing page is the one place a reader can't be assumed to have visited the glossary.

**Badge legend.** Right under the scoreboard, the landing page gives a legend: "Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed — definitions on the Honest Assessment page… ('Validated' is deprecated; 'Withdrawn' is an operational state, not a badge)." That's a good pattern. But the same page also uses **Audited-Negative** ("demoted to Reparametrization, Failed, Audited-Negative or Speculative") and shows a badge reading **"Speculative — Untestable as Stated"**, and neither is in the four-item legend. The first is a missing legend entry. The second is actually explained on /honest-assessment: text after the dash is a "free-text finding descriptor" and not a badge type. That explanation is good, but it lives only on the canonical definitions block. A first-time reader seeing "Untestable as Stated" styled like a badge will assume it's a sixth badge.

**Canonical definitions (/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions).** This is the best-written structural content on the site. It spells out the badge "contract" (formal status plus descriptor) and the two families (MRH-relationship vs descriptive), and it has a paragraph that explicitly maps **Failed / refutation / "Refuted by Execution"**: "Every refutation carries a Failed badge; not every Failed badge counts as a refutation." The glossary adds **Killed** as "the lifecycle verb for the same event" and separates **Withdrawn** from **Audited-Negative**. So the vocabulary *is* defined. The trouble is that it takes five words (Failed, Refuted, Killed, Kill Fired, Audited-Negative) plus Withdrawn to describe negative outcomes, and a reader has to find two separate pages to learn how they relate.

**The word "MRH" is doing two jobs.** In the physics, MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon) is glossed on the landing page and in the glossary as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter." In the badge taxonomy, "MRH-relationship tags" (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined…) are research-inventory metadata, and /honest-assessment glosses MRH there as "the framework's term for the bounded context something is currently relevant within." First Encounter adds a third gloss ("where correlations decay below the noise floor"). They may be one idea at different scales, but for a documentation reader, the same acronym naming both a physical horizon and a project-management status field is a real collision. When I first saw an "Active-MRH" badge, I read it as "this claim is about the MRH."

**Learning Paths.** This is clear and well organized: 3 difficulty paths (Beginner 6, Intermediate 8, Advanced 8) and 4 topic tracks (Physics 7, Chemistry 6, Philosophy 7, Methodology 6), each with time estimates. There's also an explicit and honest note that the two groupings are "not equivalent" (10 pages in one and not the other, 14 the other way). About 12 pages belong to two paths. The content pages handle this well: /coherence-function shows "Intermediate Path Step 1 of 8 · Next: The γ Parameter →" *and* "Physics Track Step 1 of 7 · Next: MRH →", with each counter labelled by path name. So the "Step 3 of 6 / Step 1 of 7" double counter on /core-idea that Pass 1 flagged is labelled. It isn't ambiguous, just visually busy. First Encounter additionally has "Part A–G of 7" inside "Step 2 of 6", and the page acknowledges this ("'Step 2 of 6' above is this page's place in the Beginner Path"). Equation Anatomy does the same kind of disambiguation well: "Step 1 of 6 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)". The site knows about the counter-collision problem and has patched it page by page instead of with one convention.

**Breadcrumbs.** Every content page I checked has an `aria-label="Breadcrumb"` trail: "Home / Core Theory / The Coherence Function", "Home / Start Here / The Core Idea", "Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1: Existing Data", "Home / Interactive Tools / Equation Anatomy". Only "Home" links anywhere. The middle segments ("Core Theory", "Predictions & Experiments") are unlinked text, and /core-theory returns **404**. "Interactive Tools" does map to a real index page. So the breadcrumbs describe a section hierarchy that the site doesn't have landing pages for.

**Tools index (/interactive-tools).** There are 7 tools, grouped under **Model Explainer**, **Reparametrization** and **Speculative**. The page says the grouping "is a content grouping, not the site's validation badge taxonomy." That's honest, but two of the three group names *are* validation badge names, so the disclaimer is fixing a collision the site created. The γ Calculator and Crossover Visualizer are grouped as "Model Explainer" while their own pages carry an **Audited-Negative** badge, so the label on the index and the badge on the page disagree in kind.

**Tier 1 page.** The opening is clear: "Tier 1 is zero-cost reanalysis of existing public data… The testing hierarchy (Tiers 1–4) is defined on the Test Roadmap." It defers to /test-catalog for the tier definitions, which is acceptable since it's linked. The status legend box links back to the canonical definitions. Three issues:
1. The summary banner reads "Failed — all 5 executed tests: 4 with the kill fired (TEST-01/03s/05 (one environment test, three IDs); TEST-09; TEST-10; TEST-25) + 1 … underpowered (TEST-04a)". The same page later traces the site-wide "6 refutations" and includes an **unnumbered "RAR shape"** test ("closed 2026-05-21… card under Recommended Start below"). So an executed test with a card on this page is outside this page's own count of executed tests, because it has no TEST-ID.
2. "One environment test, three IDs" is an identifier-hygiene problem the page describes instead of fixing.
3. Card statuses include "Pending", "Never Run (Registered)", "Underpowered", "Demoted/Exploratory" and "Convention-Dependent", none of which are in either badge family. Under the descriptor contract they are presumably descriptors, but some appear without a formal status in front of them.

**Inline revision history.** This problem runs across the whole site. The landing page includes "The cell as it read before: 'Validated' claims surviving audit: 0 of 6…". The Tier 1 page has "Count corrected from '10' 2026-08-08", "TEST-ID collision resolved 2026-08-10", and italic per-card revision footnotes. The scoreboard sentence itself contains "recounted 2026-07-30, roots split 2026-09-05". I understand the motive (the audit trail is part of the honesty). But mixing changelog into body copy doubles reading length, and it makes the current claim hard to find amid its past versions. It's an audit log and a document interleaved.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /coherence-function, /galaxy-rotation, /dark-matter, /key-claims, /measurement-without-observers, /cdm-discrimination, /interactive-tools, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment). For the key numbers I checked the raw page text, not only the rendered summary.
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes, as a worked example of auditing a framework, not as physics
- **Overall impression**: Every derivation I checked is algebraically correct, and the site's own audit is harsher than I would have been. The remaining problems sit in bookkeeping: which function "C" means on which page, and which function each refutation actually tested.

### Journey
A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project." I skipped the Beginner Path and went straight to **/parameter-derivations**. It surprised me. It reaches its own conclusion ("Zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations") and badges every rung.

**Math I checked by hand:**
- **Hill identity.** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1] is correct. At γ = ½ this gives x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). So when the page says "Milgrom's simple μ with a₀ = 2a₀′," the factor of 2 is right.
- **Derivative and limits.** dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x) is correct. The curve is concave on x ≥ 0 with no inflection point. For small x, C ≈ γx. For large x, C → 1 − 2(1+x)^{−2γ}. The claim that "phase transition is a loose description" is right: this is a compander, not an order parameter.
- **Mean-field.** The Ising comparison is handled correctly. m = tanh(βJzm) is a fixed-point equation, while C(ρ) is evaluated directly, so it has no symmetry breaking and no critical exponents. "The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen" is exactly the objection I came to raise. The page makes it first.
- **Numbers.** tanh(2 ln 11) = 0.99986 ✓. tanh(0.49 ln 2) = 0.327 ✓. cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ✓ (13% under 1.2). Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc² ✓. ln 2807 = 7.94 ✓. A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²) is dimensionally consistent: 0.029 in M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻² at V = 150 gives 6.5×10² M☉/pc³ ✓.
- **MOND-matching scaling.** Setting the knee at the MOND radius R ~ V²/a₀ gives ρ ~ V²/(GR²) ~ a₀²/(GV²) ∝ V⁻². The site's ρ_crit ∝ V⁺² really is sign-inverted ✓.
- **Deep-MOND check on the plotter.** With M = 47 V⁴, V⁴ = GMa₀ gives V = 0.93 V_flat, which matches what the plotter says ✓.
- **ΔBIC bookkeeping.** ΔBIC = +7.1 with Δk = 1 implies Δχ² ≈ −0.84, i.e. the compander fits *marginally better* and loses on the complexity penalty. The raw text says this correctly. (My WebFetch summary had flipped it to "favoring McGaugh", which is a fetch artifact, not a site error.)
- **Field equation.** ∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ reduces to g = g_N/C in spherical symmetry ✓. Attributing it to Refracted Gravity is correct.

**/coherence-function** runs a form-selection table over 10 compander families on 2,807 SPARC points. tanh-log comes *last* among the non-refuted forms, and the ΔBIC gap to Hill n=1 (7.8) equals the one-parameter penalty. I'm convinced: the form carries no statistical content.

**Where it went wrong for me: three objects called "C".** The two framework-specific kills (TEST-09 BTFR slope, TEST-10 dwarf f_DM) are the ones that count as refuting *this* framework rather than MOND. Only deep in the galaxy plotter page did I learn what they evaluate: **C_a = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)·x/(1+x) with x = (g_bar/a₀)^{1/φ}**. That function contains no tanh, no γ and no ρ. ### Journey
I came in hunting for the one prediction that differs from MOND + ΛCDM. The landing scoreboard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, 5 roots) and **/for-researchers** answer the question in the first paragraph. γ = ½ is where (1+x)^{2γ} becomes linear. At that value the galaxy sector *is* Milgrom's simple μ and the dark-energy sector *is* Λ. Both fits go there when γ is left free (SPARC 0.489 ± 0.11; DESI DR2+CMB+SN 0.487 +0.024/−0.021). The page calls this "a deflation, not a concordance." That's the correct reading, and a referee would have had to force most authors to write it.

**Candidate discriminators, one by one:**

1. **Boost ceiling B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17** (/honest-assessment: "the framework's only structural difference from MOND"). It predicts a BTFR slope of 3.35 against the observed 3.75 ± 0.10, and dwarfs with f_DM = 0.927 need B ≥ 13.7. It's killed, and the site admits the kill is convention-dependent. What's missing is the *generalization*. Any ceiling tied to a cosmic abundance ratio fails the same way, because dwarfs are baryon-deficient relative to the cosmic mean. Even the physically better-motivated cap Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.4 dies on B ≥ 13.7. Saying so would turn one convention-dependent kill into a class-level null: no cosmic-fraction ceiling survives SPARC dwarfs. That's more useful than the specific result.

2. **Dark-energy sign lock** (/dark-energy). The substituted background approaches Λ from one side, so sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ). This is a genuine, sharp, structural prediction, the only one on the site I'd call interesting. But the page says DESI prefers "w₀ > −1 with wₐ < 0, **a crossing of w = −1**", and that's imprecise. A crossing needs w₀ + wₐ < −1. The quadrant w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0 with w₀ + wₐ > −1 is ordinary **thawing quintessence**, which never crosses. So the lock forbids *all thawing* dark energy, not only phantom crossing. The allowed branch (w₀ > −1, wₐ > 0) is the **freezing/tracker** class. The Caldwell & Linder (2005) thawing/freezing classification is the standard language here, and the site never uses it. The lock is a stronger and more testable statement than the page gives it credit for: it's excluded as soon as DESI/Euclid put thawing ahead of freezing, whether or not a crossing is confirmed. It's also good that the page reports the substituted family's data-level price is exactly ΛCDM's (Δχ² = +11.0 vs w₀wₐCDM), and that it retracted the earlier "3.4–6.3σ excluded" figures as forced-w₀ artifacts.

3. **Perturbations pin γ.** Including the fluid's pressure (Jeans) term, P(k) forces |2γ−1| ≲ 10⁻⁵ on the local-density reading. That makes the sector ΛCDM at the perturbation level too, which is correct in spirit. I'd want the sound-speed assumption written out next to it: rest-frame c_s² = 1 vs adiabatic c_s² = w vs c_s² = 0 give very different Jeans behaviour, and EFT-of-DE people will ask straight away.

4. **Gaia DR3 wide binaries (TEST-02).** This is the best-argued card I read. Under ambient-density keying, the boost 1/C is common to the wide and close bins of the same volume, so it cancels in γ_g. The prediction is γ_g = 1 identically, "no Gaia release can move that." Either outcome of Chae vs Banik is covered by Newton or MOND. That's correct and non-obvious, and it's a better answer than most MOND-adjacent papers give on wide binaries. The missed connection: ambient-density keying is operationally an **environment-dependent G_eff**. Wide binaries can't see it, but stellar structure can (L ∝ G^~7 on the main sequence; helioseismology; WD cooling; red-clump luminosities), and so can comparisons of field vs globular-cluster vs bulge populations at different ambient densities. Those are real $0 constraints the test catalogue doesn't list.

5. **a₀(z) = cH(z)/2π.** The site correctly lists it as non-discriminating: anchor-dominated, and ΛCDM + baryons predict the same trend. Milgrom's own cosmic-coincidence papers raise it, and the page credits that. Fine.

6. **RAR intrinsic scatter (/cdm-discrimination).** 0.086 ± 0.003 dex is CDM-consistent. It's honest that the "CDM prediction" (≈0.085) is an *internal* figure and that an external benchmark "is queued" (since 2026-07-10). That check would take an afternoon: Ludlow+2017 (EAGLE/APOSTLE, PRL 118, 161103), Keller & Wadsley 2017, Desmond 2017 (abundance matching), Dutton+2019 (NIHAO). Also, "RAR" built from ALFALFA single-dish W50 plus SDSS stellar masses is really a BTFR-like one-point-per-galaxy relation, not a resolved RAR. The page should say which one it is before comparing to RAR scatter literature (Li+2018's SPARC ~0.057 dex observed, intrinsic consistent with ~0).
