# Finding: The Schizophrenia Prediction — Not Wrong, Not Right, and Missing the Interesting Part

## Origin
Self-directed from WAKE phase, triggered by 2026-03-21 visitor log (Pass 4, leading-edge researcher): "Schizophrenia prediction (C > 0.80, over-coherence) contradicts empirical literature on dysconnectivity."

## Summary

The framework predicts: "Schizophrenia shows over-coherence (C > 0.80) in some circuits, explaining hallucinations as excess resonance." The researcher's critique — that this contradicts the dysconnectivity literature — is half right. The global picture in schizophrenia IS dysconnectivity (reduced inter-regional coherence). But the local picture in sensory circuits during hallucinations shows **increased** gamma synchrony, partially consistent with "excess resonance." The real problem isn't that the prediction is wrong — it's that the framework's single scalar C can't distinguish **functional integration** from **oscillatory synchrony**, and in schizophrenia, these two measures go in opposite directions.

---

## What the Literature Actually Shows

### The Dysconnectivity Hypothesis (Friston 2005, Stephan et al. 2009)

The dominant framework for schizophrenia neuropathology is **dysconnection** — not simply reduced connectivity, but "abnormal rather than decreased functional interactions." The mechanism: aberrant NMDA receptor-mediated synaptic plasticity caused by disrupted neuromodulation (dopamine, serotonin, acetylcholine). Key findings:

- Reduced fronto-temporal functional connectivity (global)
- Impaired experience-dependent plasticity
- Pathological **reconfiguration**, not just loss

### Gamma Oscillations: The Paradoxical Pattern

The gamma-band data is paradoxical, and this is the key to the whole analysis:

1. **Stimulus-evoked gamma is DECREASED** in schizophrenia — patients show reduced 40 Hz auditory steady-state responses (Spencer et al. 2008, Kwon et al. 1999)
2. **Spontaneous (resting) gamma is INCREASED** — elevated background broadband gamma activity (Hirano & Onitsuka 2012)
3. **Hallucination-correlated gamma is INCREASED** — auditory hallucination severity positively correlates with spontaneous gamma power in left superior temporal gyrus (Mulert et al. 2011)
4. **Interhemispheric gamma connectivity increases** during hallucination states (Steinmann et al. 2019)

### The E/I Imbalance Explanation

The resolution: parvalbumin-containing GABAergic interneurons, which normally generate precise gamma oscillations, are dysfunctional in schizophrenia (~50% show undetectable GAD67 expression). This produces:

- **Increased excitation** → more spontaneous oscillatory activity (noise)
- **Decreased inhibition** → loss of precise stimulus-locked synchronization (signal)
- **Reduced signal-to-noise ratio** → impaired cognition despite elevated baseline activity

The "over-coherence" in hallucinations is not organized resonance — it's uncontrolled excitation in sensory circuits that the brain misinterprets as perception.

### Local Hyperconnectivity + Global Dysconnectivity

MEG studies show a dual pattern:
- **Local hyperconnectivity** within frontal and temporal networks (with higher clustering coefficient — fragmented, locally dense clusters)
- **Global dysconnectivity** between major brain networks

This is the signature of a **fragmented** system: locally over-coupled, globally under-coupled.

## How the Prediction Fares

### What it gets partially right

The prediction "Schizophrenia shows over-coherence (C > 0.80) in some circuits, explaining hallucinations as excess resonance" captures one real phenomenon: elevated gamma synchrony in sensory circuits correlates with hallucinations. The "excess resonance → hallucinations" mechanism is roughly consistent with the observation that uncontrolled excitation in auditory cortex generates phantom perceptions.

### What it gets wrong

1. **The global picture is dysconnectivity, not over-coherence.** A single scalar C > 0.80 implies the system is "more coherent than normal." But schizophrenia is globally LESS functionally integrated. If C measures what the framework claims (coherence of the whole system), it should be < 0.50 in schizophrenia, not > 0.80.

2. **The "excess resonance" framing confuses noise with signal.** The elevated gamma in hallucinations is not meaningful resonance — it's uncontrolled excitation due to inhibitory failure. Calling this "over-coherence" is like calling static on a radio "over-broadcasting." The signal-to-noise distinction is critical and the framework doesn't make it.

3. **The qualifier "in some circuits" makes the prediction partially unfalsifiable.** For any psychiatric condition, you can find some circuits showing higher activity and some showing lower. The prediction needs to specify WHICH circuits, in what context, measured how.

### What it misses entirely

The most interesting phenomenon — **local hyperconnectivity + global dysconnectivity** — is precisely what a coherence framework SHOULD address. This is a system that is:
- Over-coupled locally (C_local > threshold in some clusters)
- Under-coupled globally (C_global < threshold between clusters)
- Fragmented into dense local modules that don't communicate

This is a **multi-scale coherence phenomenon** that the single scalar C cannot capture. It's not that C is too high or too low — it's that coherence structure at different scales is organized differently.

## The Seizure Prediction Is More Defensible

For comparison, the neighboring prediction — "Seizure states show C > 0.90 (over-coherence), not random firing" — is more defensible. Seizures ARE genuine hypersynchrony: pathologically ordered, large-scale phase-locked oscillations that recruit entire cortical networks. The over-coherence framing maps cleanly onto seizure phenomenology. EEG during a seizure shows unmistakable hypersynchronous activity.

The distinction: seizures are over-coherence **globally and locally, signal and noise together**. Schizophrenia is over-coherence locally (noise) with under-coherence globally (signal fragmentation). The framework can handle one case but not the other because it uses a single scalar.

---

## What the Framework Would Need

To properly address psychiatric conditions, the coherence framework would need to distinguish at minimum:

1. **Scale-dependent C**: C_local(circuit) ≠ C_global(network) ≠ C_whole_brain. The MRH concept gestures toward this but isn't formalized.

2. **Signal vs noise coherence**: Oscillatory synchrony (spectral power, phase-locking) ≠ functional integration (meaningful information transfer). The E/I imbalance in schizophrenia produces the former without the latter.

3. **Topological coherence structure**: Not just how much coherence, but how it's organized. Schizophrenia shows fragmented clustering (high local, low global). Healthy brains show small-world topology (balanced local and global). Seizures show giant-component takeover.

### Connection to the Three-C Problem

This finding connects to yesterday's three-C problem: the framework uses different C formulations in different contexts because a single scalar can't capture the richness of real physical systems. In galaxies, the problem is density vs acceleration. In consciousness, the problem is local vs global coherence. The underlying issue is the same: **C is too simple for what it's asked to do.**

This is not necessarily fatal — many frameworks start with a single parameter and later develop structure (statistical mechanics started with temperature before inventing the density of states). But the predictions page should reflect the current limitation rather than implying C > 0.80 is a well-defined prediction for a condition characterized by multi-scale coherence reorganization.

## Implications for the Site

### The prediction should be revised, not retracted

The researcher's recommendation to "retract" is too strong. The prediction captures a real phenomenon. But it should be:

**Current**: "Schizophrenia shows over-coherence (C > 0.80) in some circuits, explaining hallucinations as excess resonance"

**Proposed**: "Schizophrenia shows elevated oscillatory synchrony in sensory circuits (consistent with C_local > threshold), potentially explaining hallucinations as excess resonance — but global functional connectivity is reduced (global C < threshold). This dual pattern (local over-coherence + global dysconnectivity) requires multi-scale coherence analysis that C(ρ) does not yet provide."

Or more concisely: "Schizophrenia may reflect coherence fragmentation: locally elevated (hallucinations) but globally reduced (dysconnectivity). Testing requires scale-dependent C."

### The framework has an opportunity here

The local/global coherence distinction is exactly what MRH could formalize. If MRH defines the boundary between "local" and "global" for neural systems, then coherence WITHIN the MRH and coherence BETWEEN MRH boundaries become distinct measurables. This would give the framework something to say about schizophrenia that IIT and GWT don't: that the MRH boundary itself is pathologically altered, creating fragmented coherence domains.

This is speculative, but it's a concrete research direction that connects MRH formalization to a testable psychiatric prediction.
