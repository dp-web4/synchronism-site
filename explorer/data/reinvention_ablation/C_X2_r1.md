**Short answer:** I don't know of any published source for a₀ = c·H₀·Ω_m^φ, and I believe it is not in the literature. The ingredients, however, are known, and the numerical claim does not check out.

**Arithmetic check.**
- c·H₀ with H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc is about 6.8×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻².
- 0.3^1.618 ≈ 0.1425.
- The product is about 9.7×10⁻¹¹ m s⁻², not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰.
- That is roughly 20% low relative to the stated value and to the empirical a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016, PRL 117, 201101). The discrepancy is large compared with the quoted precision of a₀, so the "derivation" would need to be re-examined.

**Known related results (all of which I'm fairly confident of):**
- **Milgrom 1983** (ApJ 270, 365, 371, 384) noted that a₀ ~ cH₀ is a numerical coincidence.
- **Milgrom 1999**, Phys. Lett. A 253, 273 (arXiv:astro-ph/9805346), proposed a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π from vacuum effects. That gives about 1.08×10⁻¹⁰.
- **Verlinde 2017**, SciPost Phys. 2, 016 (arXiv:1611.02269), emergent gravity, gets a₀ ~ cH₀/6 from de Sitter entropy and dark-energy-linked volume-law terms.
- **Cosmological-constant relations** of the form a₀ ~ c²√(Λ/3)/(2π), or a₀ ~ c√Λ, are discussed in many places. Examples are Milgrom 1983 and later reviews such as Famaey & McGaugh 2012, Living Rev. Relativ. 15, 10, and Sanders & McGaugh 2002, ARA&A 40, 263.
- **Ω-dependent variants** (a₀ ∝ cH₀Ω_Λ^{1/2} or similar) appear in scattered papers. I can't cite a specific one with confidence (uncertain).

**Golden ratio.** I know of no peer-reviewed MOND derivation involving φ. There are speculative golden-ratio cosmology papers (for example El Naschie's E-infinity work), but I'm not aware that any gives a₀. This is uncertain, since the fringe literature is hard to search exhaustively.

**Assessment.** The formula looks like a numerological fit. A non-integer exponent such as φ on Ω_m has no known dynamical origin, and Ω_m is a time-dependent quantity. Without a mechanism, it would be hard to distinguish from the many cH₀-type coincidences. Before claiming novelty:
1. Fix the numerics, since the result is about 0.97×10⁻¹⁰, not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰.
2. Compare it against cH₀/2π, which gives 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ with no free exponent.
3. Search arXiv and ADS for "golden ratio MOND" and "a0 Omega_m".
4. Propagate the uncertainties in H₀ and Ω_m. The Hubble tension alone moves the prediction by about 10%.
