**Short answer:** I did not search, and I know of no published derivation of a₀ = c·H₀·Ω_m^φ. It looks like numerology, not a known model. This answer comes from memory.

**Arithmetic check:** For H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, cH₀ ≈ 6.8×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². Also 0.3^1.618 ≈ 0.143. That gives a₀ ≈ 9.7×10⁻¹¹ m s⁻², not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. The result is about 20% below the stated value and about 20% below the empirical a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ (McGaugh, Lelli and Schombert 2016, PRL 117, 201101). The exponent is also sensitive to the inputs. Ω_m = 0.315 and H₀ = 67.4 give about 9×10⁻¹¹.

**Known related results** (all from memory, so treat the citations as moderately certain):
- Milgrom (1983, ApJ 270, 365, and the companion papers) noted that a₀ ~ cH₀ numerically. He later noted a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π and a₀ ~ c²√(Λ/3) (for example Milgrom 1999, Phys. Lett. A 253, 273, on MOND and the de Sitter vacuum). Both are coincidences of order cH₀ and have no derivation from first principles.
- Other published cosmological-coincidence proposals include a₀ ~ c√Λ, a₀ ~ cH₀/(2π) from horizon or Unruh-type arguments, and Verlinde's emergent gravity (2016/2017, SciPost Phys. 2, 016). In Verlinde's model a₀ = cH₀ up to an O(1) factor from dark-energy elasticity. Also relevant are Pazy (2013, PRD 87, 084063) and Klinkhamer and Kopp (2011). Details of the last two are uncertain.
- Scaling a₀ with a power of Ω_m or Ω_Λ appears in some entropic and holographic variants, for example a₀ ∝ cH₀√Ω_Λ. I can't name a specific paper.

**Golden ratio:** Golden-ratio exponents or fine-structure-constant numerology appear in fringe or arXiv-only work. I know of no peer-reviewed paper that sets a₀ = cH₀Ω_m^φ.

**Assessment:** The formula doesn't reduce to any established model. It is a one-parameter numerical fit with an arbitrary exponent. The ~20% mismatch, plus the freedom to pick other exponents, means the formula has no real predictive weight unless the program supplies a dynamical derivation of the exponent φ. The formula would also make a₀ time-dependent through H(z) and Ω_m(z). That could be tested against high-z rotation curves, and the tests so far favour a₀ constant or only weakly evolving.

If you want a firm novelty claim, search arXiv and ADS for "golden ratio MOND acceleration" and "a0 Omega_m exponent" before asserting priority.
