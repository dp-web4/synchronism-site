### Step 2 — the decisive structural fact: the lattice forbids linear LIV

The substrate's dispersion **ω² = m² + 2c²(1 − cos k)** is **even in k** (cos is even ⇒
ω(k)=ω(−k)). Expanding for a photon (m=0):

  ω = ck · [1 − (ka)²/24 + …],  v_g = dω/dk = c · [1 − (ka)²/8 + …]

The leading Lorentz-violating correction is **quadratic** in (ka) = (E/E_Pl). **There is no linear
term**, and this is not a tuning — it is forced by the reflection symmetry of a real symmetric
lattice. To get an n=1 term you need a parity-/CPT-violating substrate; the framework's plain
discrete grid cannot produce one. So **the framework's LIV is intrinsically n=2 (quadratic).** The
framework states the prediction is "Planck-scale" (lattice spacing ≡ Planck length), i.e. the QG
scale E_QG,2 ≈ E_Pl.

This single fact is the hinge of the whole testability verdict, because the two LIV orders have
*wildly* different observational status.

Now overlay the framework's prediction:

- **If the framework's LIV were linear (n=1) at Planck scale → it would be REFUTED.** The data

  framework from refutation here is the even-in-k symmetry that kills the linear term.
- **The framework's LIV is quadratic (n=2) at Planck scale → it is UNTESTABLE.** Current reach is
  E_QG,2 > 6×10⁻⁸ E_Pl; the predicted E_QG,2 ≈ E_Pl is a factor **~1.7×10⁷ in energy scale**

**This is the double-lock, stated precisely:** the lattice symmetry that *forbids* the measurable
channel (n=1, which would refute) is the *same* symmetry that *confines* the prediction to the
unmeasurable channel (n=2, ~10⁷ below floor). The framework's surviving seam is not accidentally
hard to test — it is symmetry-pinned into the one window between refutation and reach.

### Step 4 — is n=2 Planck scale reachable with *foreseeable* data?

No. The n=2 sensitivity gained by the field from the first GRB bounds to LHAASO's GRB 221009A is a
few orders of magnitude total, and LHAASO's headline n=2 improvement over the prior best was a
factor of **5–7**. Closing a **1.7×10⁷** gap in E_QG,2 (≡ ~3×10¹⁴ in delay amplitude) at that
cadence is not a foreseeable-instrument question — it would require either a fundamentally new
observable or many orders of magnitude in baseline×energy that no planned facility delivers. The QG
phenomenology literature already treats quadratic Planck-scale time-of-flight LIV as effectively
beyond reach. So "untestable with foreseeable data" is the correct, defensible characterization for
B7-dispersion.

## What would falsify *this* finding

1. **A linear (n=1) term in the substrate dispersion.** I claim the lattice is even in k so n=1 is
   forbidden. If the framework's substrate has a parity-/CPT-violating structure that generates an
   odd-in-k term, the prediction would be n=1, **refuted** (not untestable) — a *different* and even
   sharper result, but it would break this finding's "symmetry-protected" framing. Checked: the
   stated dispersion ω²=m²+2c²(1−cos k) is manifestly even; no odd term anywhere in Phase-2.
2. **A foreseeable n=2 Planck-scale time-of-flight instrument.** If a planned facility (next-gen
   neutrino baselines, a population study stacking many GRBs to ~10⁷-fold n=2 improvement) is on the
   table, "untestable with foreseeable data" weakens to "untestable with current data." I find no
   such projection in the literature; n=2 Planck scale is treated as out of reach.
3. **A Synchronism-unique LIV fingerprint** distinguishing it from LQG/causal-sets/DSR. The
   framework itself denies one exists; if a discriminating signature were derived, the
   non-uniqueness lock would break (though the sensitivity lock would remain).
