## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Why Synchronism?, Learning Paths, First Encounter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Interactive Tools index, Coherence Explorer, Consciousness Threshold Demo, What Synchronism Is Not, plus the summary box of Honest Assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've ever clicked on. I came for "one equation for everything," though, and within ten seconds I was told it didn't work, so the thing pulling me forward is the honesty, not the physics.

### Journey
I clicked through from Reddit. The headline, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, is a great hook, very PBS Space Time. Then the next line says *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* That gave me whiplash. I respected it, but my first thought was "so why am I here?" The landing page does eventually answer that: the counts box (0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations) and the line that this is an open notebook told me the experiment is the point. It took me a while to get there, though.

The marching-band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza analogy for "coherence" worked for me straight away. Then the equation appeared on the landing page: C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)). The plain-English version, "a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are," made me smile and helped a bit. I still have no idea what tanh or ln are, but I got "S-curve that depends on crowding." The landing page also threw a wall of acronyms at me (MOND, MRH, SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, CHSH, ΛCDM, A2ACW). I skimmed past all of them.

The big button says "Jump to First Encounter (Step 2)." Why does the button send me to Step 2? Am I skipping something important? I went to Learning Paths to find out. Step 1 is "Why Synchronism?" and it's about 8 minutes. I read that first. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a clear, relatable opener, and so is the question of whether one function could map density to behaviour at every scale. It told me beginners can stop after the first part, which I liked. Words like "compander," "phenomenological," "kill criterion," and "null model" floated past without explanation.

First Encounter (Step 2) is the best page for me. One section appears at a time with a Continue button, and sections A and B were easy to follow. Then Section C's heading reads *"formula failed · γ = 2 loses to MOND · fitted γ ≈ 0.5 is MOND itself."* I didn't know what MOND was yet; it only gets explained two sections later, in E. So the heading felt like reading the spoiler for a movie I hadn't seen. Section E has "ρ_crit = A × V_flat²" and never tells me what A is. Section G lists failures in words I don't know: critical exponents, Tc, spin-orbit coupling. I believed it failed. I just couldn't tell *how*.

The Galaxy Curve Plotter was the moment it clicked. You pick a galaxy, the gray line (normal gravity from what we can see) sags below the blue dots (what stars actually do), and that gap is the dark-matter problem. I'd heard of it on YouTube but had never seen it drawn for a real galaxy. Then you see the framework's own line barely move off the gray line, while MOND's green line follows the dots. Brutal, clear, memorable. My complaint is that there are five or six lines. I needed someone to say "watch just these two first." SPARC and V_flat aren't explained.

The Coherence Explorer lets me drag sliders and watch the S-curve change. That was fun for about 30 seconds. But the density axis has no units, and the presets are labelled things like "density-keyed fit to galaxies" and "MOND's simple interpolating function." I didn't know what I was looking at in real-world terms. I *think* the label for low γ said something like "many particles moving together," yet the curve got lower when I turned γ down. That seemed backwards to me, but I'm not sure I read it right.

The Consciousness Threshold Demo got my attention: "Watch 8 independent approaches converge on C ≈ 0.50, because they share one assumption." It turns out to be a static chart with no controls, even though it's listed among the interactive tools. The lesson is actually clever: anything that picks the middle of a 0-to-1 scale lands on 0.5. I learned something about how researchers can fool themselves. But I clicked expecting a consciousness thing and got a lesson about a mistake.

"What Synchronism Is Not" has seven headings in a row that start with "Not:". By the fourth I felt a bit lectured. The "What Synchronism *Is*" section at the bottom ("an interpretation… plus an openly kept record of what failed") should be at the top. I opened Honest Assessment and read only the "If you read nothing else" box, as instructed. It's clear and quotable: *"What survives is the record, not the physics."* The line *"'Untested' means nobody has looked yet; 'Failed' means someone looked"* is genuinely useful. The rest of the page is enormous. I scrolled and left.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer**: worked. Intuitive mechanically (sliders move the curve) but confusing physically: no units, and presets reference things I don't know. Fun, but I learned "it's an S-curve" and not much more.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation failed, what is the project actually trying next? Is there a "what we're testing now" for normal people?
2. What exactly is MOND, and if it already fits galaxies, why isn't it the accepted answer instead of dark matter? (The site hints at "inherited MOND problems" but I didn't get there.)
3. The landing page says 14,610 galaxies were analyzed, but the plotter has 5. Where are the others, and what did they show?
4. Is "coherence" here related to anything I'd recognise from quantum videos? The site keeps saying "not quantum coherence," but then what is it, physically?
5. AI agents did the research and the auditing. Did any human physicist look at it?

### Verdict
As a curious Reddit visitor I didn't learn a new theory of physics. I learned what the dark-matter problem looks like on a real galaxy, and how researchers can fool themselves when "independent" estimates share an assumption. Those are real takeaways, and the honesty made me trust the site. But the opening gives away the ending before it builds any stakes, and jargon like MOND keeps appearing before it's explained. I'd come back if someone showed me a live question still being tested, something I could root for.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, Glossary, Honest Assessment, Learning Paths, Interactive Tools index, γ Calculator, Equation Anatomy, Test Roadmap, Tier 1: Existing Data, For Researchers, Research Philosophy)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I didn't try to evaluate the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes, professionally. This is an unusually self-aware information architecture with fixable seams.
- **Overall impression**: The skeleton is solid: one canonical badge table, a 107-term glossary, "On this page" jump links, and counts that agree across footers. The friction sits in the seams: page-local definitions that lag the glossary, a status vocabulary that has outgrown its own taxonomy, and a few counts and descriptions that contradict the page they're on.

### Journey
I started at the landing page with a checklist: nav, acronyms, badges, CTAs. The top nav has 9 items and is sensibly ordered (Start Here → Learning Paths → tests → tools → reference → assessment). The landing page expands MOND, MRH, A2ACW, BAO and DESI at first use. That's good. But SPARC, RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, CHSH, CFD and LLM first appear bare and are expanded only in an abbreviation list further down. CMB, DR2, ALFALFA and SDSS aren't expanded anywhere on the page. The badge list on the landing page explains only Reparametrization inline and sends the reader to Honest Assessment for the rest. That's acceptable because the link exists. "Validated" is shown as deprecated with no definition, which is odd: why show a retired label on the front page at all? Two Key Claims teaser controls ("Explore All", "Introduction") came back with no visible target. That may be a fetch artifact (client-side tabs?), but a heading-less "Introduction" button is worth a look.

**Glossary.** It's strong: 107 entries, every badge has an entry, and the jargon Pass 1 tripped on is all here (compander, kill criterion, phenomenological, MOND, SPARC, V_flat, Root). Entries cross-link with "Learn more". Gaps I found: **LLM** has no entry even though the landing page uses "LLM agents". **"Null model"** has no entry; the Null-Class entry uses the phrase without defining it. One structural note: the glossary has both "Reparametrization" and "Reparametrization (badge)", and both "Validated" and "Strongly Supported" as entries. Readers need to be told which entries are *badges*, which are *concepts*, and which are *retired*. The entry names partly do this, but not consistently: "Validated" carries no "(retired)" marker in its name.

**Honest Assessment.** This is the canonical badge reference, and it does its job well. "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" gives nine badges in one table with one-line definitions. Retired labels are explicitly marked ("read it as not yet re-badged… not as an endorsement"). A separate "Status lifecycle verbs" section is clearly labelled as *not* badges. The "On this page" jump list with 14 anchors is exactly what an ~18,000-word page needs. This is the best-structured reference page on the site.

**Learning Paths.** It's clear: seven paths, each a numbered list with targets, and the Beginner path has time estimates that add up (8+10+6+9+4 = 37 min). Intermediate, Advanced and topic paths give only a total time, not per-step times. The page says *"none of the 7 paths currently include an interactive tool"*, yet Beginner step 3 is the Galaxy Curve Plotter, which the Interactive Tools index lists as a tool. A few lines later the page offers the Coherence Explorer and the Galaxy Curve Plotter as *"optional, not a path step."* So the Plotter is simultaneously step 3 and "not a path step". This directly contradicts what's on the page.

**Interactive Tools index.** Seven cards, each with kind, level, status and badge. The Consciousness Threshold Demo is now described as static on its card (Pass 1 flagged this; it looks resolved, or Pass 1 missed it). The index shows the badge each tool carries on its own page, which is good practice. The Galaxy Curve Plotter shows two badges run together ("Failed" + "Reparametrization"), and it isn't explained which badge applies to what.

**Test Roadmap and Tier 1.** Here the vocabulary sprawls. The Test Roadmap page uses the nine badges, *plus* four operational states (Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Unrunnable as stated, Withdrawn, Underpowered as registered), *plus* prose verdicts (Executed, Refuted, Pending, Pre-registered, "Not counted as a refutation", "Not falsifiable as stated"). Tier 1 adds MOND-shared (retired), Kill-or-Tie, Kill Criterion Triggered, Artifact Lesson, Post-hoc, Pending dp, Disfavored and Underpowered. That's three label families (badge / operational state / lifecycle verb) plus free-text verdicts. Research Philosophy itself admits the site uses "40+ ad-hoc status strings". The three-family model is a good idea and the pages do try to sort labels into it, but Tier 1 files "Underpowered as Registered" as an operational state in its reading guide and links to it from the lifecycle-verbs side of its legend. If the taxonomy owners can't keep it straight, readers won't.

The Test Roadmap intro calls the 24 registry experiments "specific, falsifiable". The same page tallies their kill criteria: *"Counted 2026-09-18 across all 26 rows: 2 are well-formed …; 3 state a significance at all, 11 contain no number, and TEST-07's reads 'not falsifiable as stated'."* As written, those numbers don't partition 26. Either the 2 well-formed rows are a subset of the 3 with a significance, or 9–10 rows fall into an unnamed bucket (presumably "a number but no significance"). I verified the sentence verbatim; this isn't a summarizer artifact. The page also says "Tier 1: 10 tests" while showing 13 cards. The page explains why (04a and the two later additions sit outside the tile total), but a tile that disagrees with the cards beneath it will be read as an error by anyone skimming.

**"Root".** Tier 1 uses "5 roots" in its headline count and never defines the term on the page. The glossary does have an entry, but nothing on Tier 1 points to it. A root is the backbone of the site's headline count ("6 refutations from 5 roots"), so it needs an inline gloss where the count first appears.

**For Researchers.** The page offers five numbered citable artifacts, a secondary negative and an open question. The nav tooltip says "The citable negative results". The **footer link tooltip** says *"What's citable - the two defensible negative results."* The footer is stale relative to the page. The H1 is "For Researchers: What's Citable", which is a good, specific title.

**Research Philosophy.** The first paragraph opens with a stance ("Every claim in this framework is provisional.") but never says what the page covers or who it's for. Badge spelling is consistent with the canonical nine. Minor cross-page inconsistency: Honest Assessment describes "0 of 9" as *"an audit of the site's former 'Validated' badges"*, while Research Philosophy says *"the 6 former 'Validated' badges plus 3 swept later."* The Research Philosophy version is the precise one. Honest Assessment's shorthand says all 9 were Validated badges, which is wrong by three.

**Breadcrumbs.** Breadcrumbs exist on every page I checked, which is good, but they're inconsistent in two ways:
1. *Parent section varies or vanishes.* Test Roadmap and Tier 1 sit under "Predictions & Experiments". For Researchers and Research Philosophy sit under "Methodology". Glossary, Honest Assessment and Learning Paths go straight from Home to the page with no section. Equation Anatomy and γ Calculator sit under Interactive Tools, which is correct. None of the top-nav items is called "Methodology" or "Predictions & Experiments", so the breadcrumb sections are an IA layer the nav doesn't expose.
2. *Separator rendering.* On Glossary, Honest Assessment, Learning Paths, Equation Anatomy and For Researchers, the breadcrumb came back as a list where "/" is its own item (Home → "/" → Learning Paths). For Researchers came back as "Home / / Methodology / / For Researchers". On Interactive Tools, Test Roadmap, Tier 1 and Research Philosophy it rendered cleanly as "Home / X". This may be a fetch-conversion artifact. But if the separator is a real list item in the markup on some pages and not others, screen readers will announce "slash" as a breadcrumb step. Two breadcrumb implementations seem likely.

**Naming mismatches.** The nav label "Test Roadmap" points to `/test-catalog`. The tool "Equation Anatomy" lives at `/equation-walkthrough`. The "Crossover Regime Visualizer" lives at `/phase-boundary-visualizer`, which its card admits was its old name. Readers rarely see slugs, but anyone sharing or citing links does, and "phase-boundary" in the URL contradicts the tool's main message ("no phase transition, only a smooth crossover").

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, Parameter Derivations, Galaxy Rotation, γ Boundary, Tier 1: Existing Data, Coherence Explorer, Gamma Parameter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, γ Calculator, Coherence Function, Dark Energy, Honest Assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes. I'd send it to a methods reading group as a worked example of auditing a phenomenological ansatz in public, not as physics.
- **Overall impression**: Every closed-form identity I checked by hand is correct, and the site has already found the deepest structural fact about its own equation: γ = ½ turns tanh(γ ln(1+x)) into the Möbius map x/(x+2), which is both MOND's simple μ and an exact cosmological constant. What's left is that the framework still hasn't committed to a single force law. The flagship tool contradicts itself about which wiring it plots, and the site's own text says three mutually exclusive galaxy force laws are "live".

### Journey
A colleague sent the link with "interesting AI project". I skipped the narrative and went straight to **Parameter Derivations**, because that's where frameworks like this usually hide their free parameters. The page beat me to the punchline. Its own summary says "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations," and it says the page title should be "Parameter Calibration & Honest Ansätze." I checked the arithmetic anyway:
- **tanh identity.** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. At γ = ½ this is x/(x+2). Correct.
- **MOND correspondence.** With x = g_obs/a₀′, C = g_obs/(g_obs + 2a₀′), which is Milgrom's simple μ with a₀ = 2a₀′. Correct. It also explains why the Galaxy Rotation page says the identified combination is a₀′/γ: at γ = ½ that is exactly a₀.
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π.** At H₀ = 67.4 I get 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², about 13% below 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. Correct, and correctly badged Reparametrization (Milgrom 1983 noticed the coincidence).
- **Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG).** I get ≈ 119 M☉/pc². Correct, and correctly flagged as not independent of a₀.
- **Boost ceiling and DM fraction.** B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.175 gives f_DM ≤ 0.685. f_DM = 0.927 gives B = 13.7. Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.39. All correct.
- **Coherence Explorer defaults.** At γ = 0.49: C(ρ_crit) = tanh(0.49 ln 2) = 0.3272, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.8259, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.9785, and the midpoint sits at x = e^{atanh(½)/γ} − 1 = 2.07. All four match the tool.
- **Concavity claim (γ Boundary).** C″(x) = −γ sech²(γu)(1+x)⁻²[1 + 2γ tanh(γu)] < 0 for γ > 0, with u = ln(1+x). So C is concave on x ≥ 0 and nothing singles out γ = 1. The retraction of the "maximum curvature at γ ≈ 1" sentence is correct. One nuance worth adding: in log-ρ, which is what the explorer plots by default, the curve *is* sigmoidal and has an inflection. A reader who sees an S-curve on the log axis will be confused by "concave, no inflection" unless the page says which variable it means.

**γ = 2/√N_corr.** Both the Gamma Parameter page and the γ Calculator say what I'd say. The CLT applies to *independent* variables, while N_corr counts *correlated* ones. A 1/√N fluctuation width is being used as a sharpness parameter, so the sign is inverted relative to finite-size mean-field rounding: larger systems should switch more sharply, not more smoothly. The calculator still assigns BCS the flattest curve and the ideal gas the steepest, and it says outright that these are "what the inverted formula predicts, not physical estimates." The preset arithmetic all checks: 2/√17 = 0.485, 2/√30 = 0.365, 2/√10⁷ = 6.32×10⁻⁴. I couldn't find a mean-field or Landau comparison anywhere, and neither could the γ Boundary page ("Not Landau. No critical point."). That's the right answer, but a condensed-matter reader would like one paragraph that does the comparison explicitly: what Landau theory would predict for the width, and what γ would have to be.

**Galaxy Rotation.** Here the site does good physics and says so plainly: every quantitative galaxy fit is "a fit in MOND's own variable." The free-γ fit lands on 0.49, which the site reads as the tanh family's encoding of the simple μ through the Newtonian-return exponent q = 2γ. I checked this: at large x, 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ}. It's also exactly why TEST-25 (Cassini) bites. The simple μ returns to Newton too slowly, so the Solar-System EFE quadrupole is too large. That's a known MOND problem, correctly labelled as inherited. The +7.1 ΔBIC for free γ against McGaugh's ν is consistent with Δχ² ≈ −0.8 plus a ln(2807) ≈ 7.9 penalty, so "entirely the BIC penalty" is right. **Dark Energy** has the best result on the site, and I checked it by hand. With G_eff = G/C(ρ̄) in Friedmann, ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C. At γ = ½, (1−C)/C = 2ρ_crit/ρ_m, so **ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit = const**, an exact Λ. For general γ, w runs from −2γ (x ≫ 1, ρ_DE ∝ ρ_m^{1−2γ}) to −1 (x → 0). Both limits are correct. **Coherence Function** is where I got uneasy. The proposed field equation is ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ. In spherical symmetry the flux C·g·r² = GM(<r) is conserved, so g(r) = GM/(r²·C(ρ(r))) is set by the *local* density at the field point. With C keyed on local ρ and floored at Ω_m in vacuum, that predicts a factor-1/Ω_m ≈ 3.2 jump in g across any boundary where ρ crosses ρ_crit. The obvious example is Earth's atmosphere: surface gravimetry (air, presumably C ≈ 1) against satellite/LAGEOS GM⊕ (vacuum, C = Ω_m). These agree to ~10⁻⁹. Whether this fires depends on which ρ_crit applies in the Solar System, and ρ_crit = A·V_flat² has no V_flat there. I couldn't find the site addressing it. The Tier 1 card says LLR excludes the density branch via a rate test, which is a different argument. Without its floor, C(0) = tanh(0) = 0, so the vacuum equation is degenerate, and the floor is doing structural work, not just setting a cap.

The same page says that "three mutually exclusive galaxy force laws are live" (C·g vs g/C vs quadrature), and the **Galaxy Curve Plotter** shows the consequence. Its formula box codes v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)², while its legend and body text describe the violet curve as g = g_bar/C with the 3.17× floor. Those are opposite orientations. In one, small C means no boost; in the other, small C means maximal boost. The plotter's own fetch summary says "the page is inconsistent on which wiring this curve uses." For a tool whose badge is "Failed — published equation", the reader needs to know *which* equation failed. The physics elsewhere in the plotter is fine. MOND uses the simple ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y), which is correct. M_b = 47·V⁴ with a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ gives V_∞ = (47·G·a₀)^{1/4}·V_flat ≈ 0.93·V_flat, which I checked: 1/(G·a₀) ≈ 63 M☉ in these units.
