## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Interactive Tools, Coherence Explorer, What Synchronism Is Not, Glossary, Honest Assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A website that tells me on the first screen, in plain words, that its own big idea didn't work. That's disarming and kind of refreshing, but once I scroll past the opening I'm buried in Greek letters, test codes and statistics I can't read.

### Journey
I came from a Reddit link expecting "one equation for everything" hype. What I got first was a **founder's note** saying there's "no physics and no math, only magic", and that our equations are "spells" we've learned to cast. I liked it. It reads like the opening of a Veritasium video, and the line "We still don't know how gravity actually works" is a hook I'd click on. For a second I wondered if this was going to be a mystical site, but the next part cleared that up quickly.

The **hero** is the most surprising thing I've seen on a science site: *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* Then comes a bold "So is it alive or dead?", answered as "dead as tested physics, alive as a public record". I understood that, and it made me trust the site more. Then the same box mentions a Bell test, MOND, and "Gaia DR4, 2 December 2026". I know none of those, and they're crammed into the part that's supposed to be for me. The page tells me "**New here?** That is everything you need from this page." I appreciated the permission to stop. But I kept scrolling anyway (everyone does). Past "Below this line: for people checking the work" there's a table with status words like **"Is MOND"** and **"Is Λ"**. A status of "Is Λ" means nothing to me. There's a tile saying **1,703** is "the archive's chemistry 'phenomenon type' counter, read at session #1,840 (not a corpus size: the chemistry corpus is 2,671 sessions)". I read that three times and still don't know what was counted.

The marching band vs. crowd in a plaza analogy for "coherence" clicked right away. "One number from 0 to 1 for how much a system's parts act together" is a sentence I could repeat to a friend. The warning that this is "Not the quantum coherence you've heard of" was useful too. I *had* assumed it was the quantum thing.

**Why Synchronism? (step 1).** The "What happened, in three lines" box is the best thing on the site for me: the proposal, the test, what's left. "Where the equation fits galaxies, it turns into MOND, a formula published in 1983", plus "Why does matching an existing theory count as losing?", finally made the MOND thing make sense. If your answer just equals someone else's 40-year-old answer, you haven't added anything. Got it. The rest is folded away as optional, which is good. The one section heading I saw outside a fold was "Chemistry: γ ≈ 1 Band (boundary reading retracted; the correlations are Debye-model restatements)", which is word soup to me.

**First Encounter (step 2).** It calls itself 10 minutes, in 7 sections. I liked "Start with Density" (an electron alone vs. a neutron star core) and the "dimmer switch" image for the curve. Section C was where I got disoriented. It's titled "The γ Parameter — the original idea, and what happened to it" and opens by telling me the γ formula failed. So I'm being taught a formula and told in the same breath that it's broken. That's honest, but I didn't know whether to learn it or skip it. The "ln ratios 2.3, 4.6, 6.9" and "ρ_crit = 0.028 × V_flat^0.5" lost me completely. "Superconductor predicted 607 K, actual 93 K, 6.5× wrong" was the one failure I could actually feel.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (step 3), tool 1.** The top of the page is good: "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" and "(A 'rotation curve' is just how fast things orbit, plotted against distance from the galaxy's centre.)" That's exactly the help I needed. The story I took away: the dots (real stars) stay flat far out, the grey line (visible stuff only) drops, and that gap is "the puzzle", which is dark matter, the thing I'd heard of. The violet line is this site's idea and doesn't fit. The green line is MOND and does. Lesson learned. The controls are where it fell apart for me (see Tools Tested).

**Interactive Tools → Coherence Explorer, tool 2.** This is the "Best first tool" and the most fun. Dragging γ makes the S-curve fatter or skinnier, and the knee slider slides it sideways. The preset buttons tell a little story by themselves: "original guess" (γ = 2) → "start" (1) → "galaxy fit" (0.49) → "same as MOND" (½) → "density fit (lost)" (0.05). Seeing "galaxy fit" sit right next to "same as MOND" got the MOND point across better than any paragraph. On the Interactive Tools page itself, almost every tool is tagged "Artifact Lesson" and "Audited-Negative", and neither label is explained on the cards. So most of the toys are demonstrations of something that failed? That's unusual and I'm not sure how to feel about it.

**What Synchronism Is Not (step 4).** The "In plain words" box is excellent: not a theory of everything, not proven, not journal-reviewed, the galaxy formula is MOND in disguise, the simplest version breaks a relativity rule, the consciousness part is untested. If I'd read this first I'd have saved myself some confusion. Below the box, the page runs to about 3,300 words and goes jargon-heavy (QFT, GR, ΛCDM, CHSH, comagnetometer…).

**Honest Assessment (step 5).** It warns me that "this page is about 30,000 words." That made me laugh, but the "If you read nothing else" box and the closing line, "**On the Beginner Path? You're done.** … Everything below is the audit trail, written for physicists, and you can leave it", are a really clean ending. I like being told I'm allowed to stop. "The one equation failed its galaxy tests. Where it fits, it is MOND in different notation. As a theory of galaxies: fatal." That's clear.

**Glossary.** "New here? Start with these ten" is a good idea, and the ten are the right ten (coherence, density, γ, tanh, MOND, dark matter, reparametrization, refutation, σ, MRH). The MRH definition, "an 'event horizon for influence' — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter", is the first time that acronym made sense to me. (Note: a fetch summary claimed the `#start-with-ten` anchor was missing. A curl of the live HTML shows `id="start-with-ten"` is present, so that was a fetch artifact and isn't logged as friction.)

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked (per the page text; I could not see the chart pixels through the fetch) — half intuitive. The "look at three things" intro and plain legend ("dots = real speeds, grey = visible stuff only, green = MOND, violet = this idea") are intuitive. The star-weight slider ("How heavy are the stars?", default 0.50) is a nice touch. The knee dropdown and numeric readout are confusing. Galaxy buttons: DDO 154 (default), NGC 2403, UGC 128, NGC 3198, NGC 7331. The "twin pair" hint (UGC 128 vs NGC 2403) made me want to click both, but I didn't understand what I was supposed to see differ.
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — intuitive. Two sliders (γ default 1.00, knee default 1.00), five story-telling presets, log/linear toggle. The readout "C = 0.60 at this γ" and the key values (0.6000 / 0.9836 / 0.9998) update with γ. "The density axis has no units" and "does not model any real material" are honest framings. What I noticed: the "galaxy fit" and "same as MOND" buttons are almost the same number, and that one pair of buttons explained the site's whole verdict to me. Below the controls, though, the "For physicists" fold-out is thousands of words on a page billed as the first tool.

### Unanswered Questions
1. The site calls itself an "AI-run research notebook" with "3,308 sessions" of AIs arguing with each other. How much did humans check? What does the founder actually do day to day? This is the most interesting thing on the site to me, and I couldn't find a plain answer on the beginner path.
2. If the idea failed, why does the site keep being updated? What would make it "alive" again? Is there anything coming that could make it *win* rather than just lose less?
3. Gaia DR4 is four days away (2 December). Should I come back to see what happens? What Synchronism Is Not says it "settles a question about MOND, not about this framework", so I'm not sure whether it matters for *this* idea.
4. The founder's "spells and magic" note: is that a serious philosophy the site goes on to use, or a framing that stays at the top? I didn't see it again after the first screen.
5. "Consciousness equation" was in the key claims teaser. What would that even mean, and why is it on a physics site?

### Verdict
For a casual reader, the first screen and the five-step Beginner Path work. I came away able to say what was proposed (one 0-to-1 "act-together" number driven by crowding), how it was tested (galaxies) and why it lost (the version that fits is just MOND). The "you can stop here" signposts are a genuine kindness. The friction is everything around that path. Stat tiles, status tables and control labels still talk in the researchers' shorthand. And for a newcomer, every page carrying a long technical tail makes the site feel bigger and harder than the story it's telling.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, Learning Paths, Test Roadmap (`/test-catalog`), Interactive Tools, γ Calculator, Equation Anatomy (`/equation-walkthrough`), MRH, Glossary, For Researchers, Key Claims, Research Philosophy, Wide Binaries (anchor check only))

### Method note
There is no WebFetch tool in this session, so I browsed the live site with `curl` plus an HTML-to-text strip. That also let me check things a summarising fetch can't: anchor ids, breadcrumb markup, and the client-rendered Equation Anatomy steps, which I read from the page's JS chunk. Halfway through I found another agent writing into the same `/tmp` scratch directory and moved to my own. A stale `/wide-binaries` copy in the shared directory briefly made `#efe-free-corner` look missing; a fresh fetch shows it is there. That one is not logged as friction.

### Journey
**Landing, structure first.** Top nav: Start Here · Learning Paths · Tier 1: Existing Data · Test Roadmap · Interactive Tools · Glossary · Research Philosophy · For Researchers · Honest Assessment. "Start Here" goes to `/why-synchronism`, which is good: the label promises an entry point and delivers one. All 7 in-page anchors on the landing resolve. Acronym discipline is strong. MOND is expanded inline at first use in the hero. A2ACW is expanded at first use ("AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop"). CFD is expanded and explicitly disambiguated from "counterfactual definiteness", which is a nice touch. MRH is expanded identically ("Markov Relevancy Horizon") on all 7 pages I grepped, with no "Relevance"/"Relevancy" drift. An "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC): short key" pointer sits above the equation.

Two structural problems on the landing. (1) The abbreviation key (`#terms-on-this-page`) introduces itself as "Abbreviations used in the table below", but it sits about halfway down the page (byte ~52k of ~106k). The **"Where each piece stands"** table (~37k) comes before it and uses ΔBIC, SPARC, σ, Ωm, Cassini, CHSH and Λ freely. So the first dense table a reader meets has its key after it. (2) The landing introduces ρ twice with different definitions. Near the top: "ρ is 'rho' = density". Further down: a paragraph opening "**ρ is presence** — the compatible structural elements available within a system's relevancy boundary". The Equation Anatomy page's revision note says it *removed* exactly that "Presence" definition from its own legend because it "disagreed with Step 1 directly below it". The landing still leads with it. The paragraph qualifies itself afterwards ("has never been operationalized"), but the topic sentence is the definition the rest of the site retired.

**Learning Paths.** Clear and honest about itself. It corrects its own earlier claim that difficulty paths and topic tracks "cover the same pages" and says which pages each misses. The Beginner path's time is stated two ways ("~37 min on the short route… ~2 h reading every page in full"), which is the right way to handle a long step 5. One mismatch: the Physics Track subtitle is "Quantum mechanics → cosmology → predictions", but none of its 7 steps (Coherence Function, MRH, Measurement Without Observers, Dark Matter, Galaxy Rotation, Relation to MOND, Quantum Predictions) is the cosmology page. The landing's own revision note says step 5 "is a ~18,000-word page", while Learning Paths says "about 30,000 words". The landing note is marked as history, but a reader has no way to tell which figure is current.

**Test Roadmap.** The nav label is "Test Roadmap", the URL is `/test-catalog`, and `/test-roadmap` is a 307 redirect to it. The opening line "How the three test pages relate" (Roadmap = full registry; Tier 1 = zero-cost slice; Decisive Tests = an empty May shortlist) is exactly the orientation sentence a documentation reviewer asks for. The **Status Badge Guide** is where I counted the label systems. There are two badge families (5 MRH-relationship + 4 descriptive), 7 "Operational states (not badges)", 11 "Lifecycle verbs (not badges)", and 3 retired labels. Add the scoreboard words (8) and ledger classes (4) from the Glossary, and the tool Kind (3) and Level (3) from Interactive Tools. That makes **eight distinct label vocabularies**, roughly 50 terms. The Glossary's "How to read labels on this site" table is a genuinely good mitigation ("Four kinds of label are verdicts… everything after it is metadata"). But the need for a label-of-labels map is itself the finding.

**Breadcrumbs.** Inconsistent hierarchy, and the parent crumbs are not links:
- MRH: Home / *Core Theory* / MRH (Core Theory is plain text; `/core-theory` 307s to `/learning-paths`)
- Test Roadmap: Home / *Predictions & Experiments* / Test Roadmap (plain text; `/predictions` 307s to `/test-catalog`)
- Tier 1: Home / **Test Roadmap** / Tier 1. This skips the "Predictions & Experiments" level that Test Roadmap itself claims.
- Research Philosophy: Home / *Methodology* / Research Philosophy (plain text)
- For Researchers: Home / **Research Philosophy** / For Researchers. A citable-results page filed under a methodology page.
- Key Claims, Glossary, Learning Paths: Home / page (flat)
- γ Calculator, Equation Anatomy: Home / Interactive Tools (link) / page. These are correct and clickable.

The breadcrumbs mix three depths and two parenting schemes. The section redirects already exist, so making the middle crumbs links would cost nothing.

**Interactive Tools.** Much better than I expected from Pass 1's note. The intro defines all four card slots (Kind, Level, State, "Badge on its page") and says explicitly that "Artifact Lesson is not a level — it is an operational state". The explanation is in the page intro rather than on each card, which is where a skimmer misses it. Three naming mismatches: card "Equation Anatomy" → URL `/equation-walkthrough`; "Crossover Regime Visualizer" (formerly "Phase Boundary Visualizer", per the card); and the **Consciousness Threshold Demo** is listed on the Interactive Tools hub while its own card says "A static chart with hover notes, not an interactive tool: no controls."

**MRH.** This page has the disambiguation I was going to ask for: "Not to be confused with the MRH-relationship badges… named by analogy… and say nothing about physics." Good. Still, overloading the site's one physics-concept acronym onto its status-badge family is a naming choice that needs that disclaimer on the Test Roadmap, Glossary *and* MRH pages. That suggests the name is costing more than it buys. Related oddity: **Audited-Negative** is filed in the MRH-relationship family ("where a claim sits relative to research focus"), yet its definition ("closed audit finding; durable; does not move") is a verdict on the claim, not a statement about research focus.

**Glossary.** 142 terms, an A–Z + Greek jump bar, and a "New here? Start with these ten" line. The label map table is the best single piece of information architecture on the site. Naming drift inside the Glossary itself: the label map's row is "Test history (the State slot)" and links to "history verbs", while the definitions below and the Test Roadmap call the same items "lifecycle verbs". The slot has three names across the site: **State** (Interactive Tools, "called 'Status' until 2026-11-16"), **Test history** (Glossary row heading), and **Operational states + Lifecycle verbs** (Test Roadmap).

**For Researchers.** About 20,750 words, 19 section anchors, six numbered citable results, and every fragment link resolves. The lead has been shortened, but the second paragraph still packs Cρ, ΔBIC +2843, L2, L3, AQUAL and "door #3" into one answer. L2/L3 are glossed only inside the fold ("L2/L3: the two candidate field equations, see the glossary"), after first use in the visible lead. The "which dynamics?" box ("Choosing one is a project-lead decision, not a computation") is clear and important. It is the single most useful paragraph for an outside reader and deserves to sit higher.

**Key Claims.** This page has a "how to read this page" block and the three badge definitions inline. Two problems. (1) Its reading order contradicts the landing's: Key Claims says "The first claim is the foundational one — the others follow from it", while the landing teaser says "Read the list bottom-up: the most exciting-sounding claims… are the least tested". Both can be true, but a reader is given two opposite instructions for the same list. (2) Claim 1's badge reads **Speculative** with the descriptor "as a literal sequential scan, **excluded** by single-photon interference". A badge says "no quantitative test" and its own descriptor reports an exclusion. The page's rule that "text after a dash… is a finding descriptor" is explained, but this particular descriptor contradicts the badge it qualifies.

**Research Philosophy.** It is open about its own drift: the "Single-sourced 2026-08-08" note says the status vocabulary had grown to "40+ ad-hoc strings across five pages". My count today (~50 terms across 8 vocabularies, now documented) suggests the strings were consolidated and documented, not reduced. The session count is written "~3,308 sessions" here, "3,308" elsewhere on the same page, and "~3,300" on the landing. A tilde on a four-significant-figure number reads as a typo.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** — worked — instructive but text-heavy. Default Ncorr = 4 (the "Liquid water" preset is highlighted) gives "γ = 1.0000" and "C(ρcrit) = 0.60". I checked these by hand: 2/√4 = 1, tanh(ln 2) = 0.6. The regime label "Crossover zone (0.6 ≤ γ ≤ 1.4, i.e. N_corr ≈ 2.04–11.1, whole counts 3–11)" is arithmetically right too. It offers 7 presets, matching the hub card's list exactly, and a Linear (1–1000) / Log (1–10⁷) toggle that is needed to reach the BCS preset at 10⁷. The "Try it: click Ideal gas then BCS superconductor" instruction is the right kind of task-oriented microcopy. Structural issue: in page source order, the readout and curve come about a dozen paragraphs and three caveats after the H1, and the slider and presets come *after* the readout. I couldn't confirm the visual layout (it may be two-column), but in a linear read the reader meets the output before the input.
- **Equation Anatomy** — worked — clear stepper. The server HTML shows the variable legend and Step 1. Steps 2–8 are client-rendered: I read them from the route's JS chunk, with Previous / Next → controls. Step titles form a clean procedural outline (normalize → log → ×γ → tanh → interpret → couple to gravity → γ = ½). "Step 1 of 8 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)" heads off a real confusion with the learning-path step counters. The "Read this before Step 1: one of the terms you are about to tour is known-broken" box is excellent documentation practice: you get the caveat before you learn the thing, not after.
