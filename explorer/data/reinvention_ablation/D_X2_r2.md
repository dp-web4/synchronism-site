**Short answer:** I don't know of any published result that matches this. I did no literature search, so this comes from memory. I think the specific formula a₀ = c·H₀·Ω_m^φ is not in the literature, but I haven't verified that.

**What is known**
- Milgrom (1983, ApJ 270, 365–389) noted the coincidence a₀ ~ cH₀. He also noted a₀ ~ c²√(Λ/3), which is of order cH₀. Other work since then has tried to explain this.
- Milgrom (1999, Phys. Lett. A 253, 273; uncertain on the details) linked a₀ to the de Sitter or Unruh temperature. That gives a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π ≈ 1.1×10⁻¹⁰ m/s².
- Several papers relate a₀ to cosmological parameters. Examples are a₀ ∝ cH₀√Ω_Λ, horizon-thermodynamics and entropic-gravity versions (Verlinde 2016/2017, arXiv:1611.02269), and Famaey & McGaugh's Living Reviews (2012) discussion of the coincidence. None of these uses a golden-ratio exponent.
- Golden-ratio numerology in cosmology exists, for example in Ω_m or Ω_Λ fits. I can't cite a specific MOND-related one.

**Numerical check**
- cH₀ = 3×10⁸ × 70/3.086×10¹⁹ ≈ 6.8×10⁻¹⁰ m/s².
- 0.3^1.618 = exp(1.618 × ln 0.3) ≈ exp(−1.948) ≈ 0.143.
- The product is a₀ ≈ 9.7×10⁻¹¹ m/s², not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰.

So the stated value is off by about 20%. That is roughly 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ only if you accept a loose match. The measured a₀ is about 1.2×10⁻¹⁰, with uncertainty of order 10%. Ω_m^φ gives a value about 2σ low or worse. Standard Ω_m ≈ 0.315 and H₀ ≈ 67.4 give an even lower value (about 0.315^1.618 ≈ 0.155, times cH₀ ≈ 6.5×10⁻¹⁰, so about 1.0×10⁻¹⁰). H₀ = 73 would help slightly.

**Concerns**
- The exponent φ looks like numerology unless there is a derivation. Many simple expressions, such as cH₀/2π and cH₀Ω_m^{3/2}, fit about as well. Ω_m^{3/2} = 0.164 gives 1.1×10⁻¹⁰, which is actually closer to 1.2×10⁻¹⁰.
- Ω_m varies with epoch, so a₀ would drift with redshift. High-z rotation curves and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation constrain this.

**Verdict:** probably not published in this form (not verified). The cH₀ scaling itself is Milgrom's well-known coincidence, and the Ω_m^φ factor appears to be the new, unmotivated part. Treat any literature claim here as uncertain until you do a proper search on arXiv or ADS for "golden ratio" with MOND or a₀.
