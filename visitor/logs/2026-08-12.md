## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Start Here, First Encounter, Tools index, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Coherence Explorer, Consciousness Threshold Demo, Honest Assessment, Glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Refreshingly honest science site that hooks you with a big question, then buries you in its own autopsy report before you're sure what the patient was.

### Journey

Clicked in from Reddit. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is exactly the kind of thing I click on. Then the very next thing I read is "Some predictions work. Some fail" and "0 confirmed predictions." Huh. I've never seen a science site open by telling me it doesn't work. Honestly, that made me keep reading — it felt like the opposite of every crank site I've seen.

The marching band vs. wandering crowd analogy landed immediately. I get it: lockstep = coherent, scattered = not. There's an equation with tanh in it — no idea what tanh is, but the site seemed to know I wouldn't, and there was a "No physics background?" door, so I went to **Start Here**.

Start Here was mostly readable but already dropping names at me: MOND (some 1983 galaxy theory), γ "the crowd→band dial", something about the dial being "inverted for real systems" — no idea what that means, skimmed past it. The test results table was blunt: melting points failed by 53%, superconductivity predicted 607K vs actual 93K. I don't know if 607 vs 93 is embarrassing or close-ish for physics, but the site clearly thinks it failed, so, failed.

**First Encounter** was the best page for me. The two analogies (band/crowd, crystal/gas) carried me most of the way. Then it said **"Markov Relevancy Horizon"** and my eyes glazed. The plain version right after ("the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter") is actually great — but the formal definition in parentheses ("minimal set of degrees of freedom…") read like the page switched languages mid-sentence. I expected something to click or play with on this page and there wasn't anything — just Next buttons.

**Tools** — this is where I perked up. The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** is the single best thing on the site for someone like me. "Physics predicts galaxies should rotate more slowly at their outer edges… They don't." — that's a Veritasium-video-worthy hook, in one picture. Five curves though, and one of them is the theory's own curve *visibly failing* (it "hugs the baryon line"), one is a "hand-tuned illustration" of what a fit *would* need to look like, and one is MOND doing great. It took me a while to work out that the violet line failing was the point being made. Also: which line am I supposed to be rooting for?

The **Coherence Explorer** sliders made sense at a feel level — γ makes the S-curve snap harder, ρcrit slides it left/right. Fun to fiddle with for a minute. But the text around it kept mentioning Ncorr, BEC, BCS, "off-diagonal-long-range-order" — none of which I know — and kept telling me the formula "runs backwards against real condensed-matter physics," which… okay? I don't have the background to feel the weight of that.

Clicked **Consciousness Threshold Demo** because, come on, consciousness. It claims eight approaches converge on C ≈ 0.50, then immediately says the convergence is "geometrically forced" and probably meaningless. So the page's own answer to "is this exciting?" is "no." I respect it but I left deflated.

**Honest Assessment**: first few paragraphs genuinely clear ("it failed every decisive test"). Then it turns into sigma-this, ΔBIC-that. Weirdly, reading a site tear itself apart made me trust it *more* — but I skimmed the second half entirely.

Ended at the **Glossary** to look up the words that had been bouncing off me. Mixed bag: tanh's entry starts great ("smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1") then pivots to "self-consistency loop m = tanh(βJz·m)". MRH's definition is jargon defining jargon ("minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom…"). The plain-English version from First Encounter ("bubble of neighbors that matter") is better than the glossary's own entry — why isn't that the first line?

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the theory fails everywhere, why does the site exist? (I *think* the answer is "the honest map of failure is the product" — the Honest Assessment says something like that — but the landing page never quite says it in plain words.)
2. What is MOND actually, and why does it keep "winning"? It's mentioned everywhere but I never found a "MOND for people who've never heard of MOND" explainer on my path.

But as a casual reader I was handed the obituary before I'd met the person: the site is so busy refuting itself that the simple story (one dial from crowd to marching band, tried at every scale, beaten by a 40-year-old rival) has to be assembled by the visitor from fragments. Great bones, best-in-class transparency, needs a plain-English narrator.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, Learning Paths, Start Here/Why Synchronism, Tools index, Honest Assessment, γ Calculator, Phase Boundary Visualizer, Glossary, Key Claims, First Encounter)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure and terminology system; I did not evaluate the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Exceptionally well-scaffolded site whose status vocabulary has outgrown its own legend — the documentation of corrections is now competing with the documentation of content.

### Journey

I audited methodically: landing → Learning Paths → Start Here → Tools index → Honest Assessment (badge legend) → two tools → Glossary → Key Claims → First Encounter, cross-checking terminology at each hop.

**Navigation is genuinely good.** Header nav is consistent across every page I visited. Breadcrumbs exist everywhere (`Home / Getting Started / First Encounter`, `Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator`) and match the section structure. The Beginner Path has real step indicators ("Beginner Path — Step 2 of 6") with labeled prev/next links ("← Prev: Why Synchronism?" / "Next: The Core Idea →"). Learning Paths offers 3 difficulty tracks and 4 topic tracks with step counts and time estimates. This is better wayfinding than most commercial documentation I review.

**The badge system is where the architecture strains.** The site's core UX promise is that every claim carries a validation badge, with definitions centralized at `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`. I went there and counted the vocabulary: two badge families (5 MRH-relationship + 4 descriptive), 2 deprecated badges, roughly 9 more "operational" labels (Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (retired), Withdrawn, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson…), plus a status lifecycle (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED…). That is 20+ distinct status terms for what the same page says reduces to ~6 refutations from 3–4 independent roots. Worse, the headline count's own inclusion rule cites labels — "Empty Intersection" among them — that the legend never defines; the page *admits this itself* ("Three of those four labels are absent from the badge legend readers are pointed to") and then doesn't fix it. Verdict words like "Disfavored," "post-hoc retrodiction," and "collapsed onto" are used as if they were formal statuses but appear in no legend. As a tech writer this is the classic failure mode of a living spec: every correction minted a new label, and nobody owns the controlled vocabulary.

**Acronym hygiene is inconsistent by page, which tells me there's no house style rule.** MRH is exemplary — expanded and analogized at first use on every page I saw ("the MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon): the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"). But MOND appears 5+ times on the landing page undefined, and on Start Here it's used twice before the definition surfaces mid-paragraph. SPARC, ΛCDM, BTFR, DESI, BAO, BEC/BCS are undefined on the pages where a newcomer first meets them. On Key Claims, "QM" appears ~20 times with no expansion anywhere. The pattern: terms the *framework invented* get careful first-use treatment; terms *imported from physics* get none. That's exactly backwards for the stated audience mix.

**The Glossary is strong but its delivery mechanism may be aspirational.** 72 entries, alphabetized, all ten of my spot-check terms present, with plain-language first sentences (MOND: "An alternative to dark matter: gravity behaves differently at very low accelerations") and honest "⚠ Physicist terminology note" flags. But the glossary promises "Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline," and on First Encounter I found no evidence of inline term links or hover markup in the body text — MRH's definition there is hand-written into the prose, not pulled from the glossary. Either the hover system isn't deployed on the content pages I checked, or it's invisible to non-JS readers; either way the promise and the pages disagree. (Caveat: my browsing tool doesn't execute scripts, so I can report absence of markup, not absence of behavior.)

**Counts don't reconcile without archaeology.** The footer says "0 confirmed predictions; 6 refutations executed." Honest Assessment says 3–4 independent roots. Key Claims reports "1 refutation" inside Claim 1 — an arc-local count with no flag telling you its scope differs from the site-wide footer two inches below it. Each number is individually defended somewhere, but the reader has to reconstruct the reconciliation across three pages. A single canonical scoreboard component with one footnote would end this.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** — worked — confusing status sequencing — Inputs and outputs are labeled (default N_corr = 4, linear/log range toggle), and the four caveats are unusually candid ("Every γ 'prediction' is therefore a consistency check on a back-fitted parameter"). But the interaction invites you to compute before the page tells you the formula is a documented failure, and there's no visual badge on the tool. Related links and breadcrumbs are fine.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Who owns the badge vocabulary? Is there a single source-of-truth file for status labels, or does each page's author coin labels ad hoc? (The legend's own gaps suggest the latter.)
2. Is the glossary hover system actually live on content pages, or only on some? I couldn't verify without scripts — but the beginner-path prose doesn't even link glossary terms as plain anchors.
3. What is the intended scope convention for counts — when a page says "1 refutation," how is a reader supposed to know it's arc-local rather than site-wide?
4. Why do the learning paths and the tools live in disjoint structures when the tools index already defines a recommended pedagogical order?
5. Is there a style guide? The MRH-vs-MOND first-use asymmetry looks like the output of careful writers with no shared rule rather than careless ones.

### Verdict
Structurally, this is one of the best-organized science sites I've audited: real breadcrumbs, real step indicators, a real glossary, and a self-correcting ledger most documentation teams could learn from. But the correction process has produced vocabulary debt — 20+ status terms, a headline count whose inclusion rule uses undefined labels, and three refutation tallies that only reconcile if you read three pages — and the site knows it, because it annotates the gaps instead of closing them. The fix is editorial, not scientific: one controlled vocabulary, one scoreboard component, one first-use rule for acronyms.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, Parameter Derivations, MOND Unification, Tools index, Equation Anatomy, Chemistry Correlation Explorer, Honest Assessment, Key Claims, For Researchers, Open Questions; also tried /dark-energy — 404)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — not for the physics, which is dead on arrival, but as a case study in adversarial self-auditing
- **Overall impression**: A MOND reparametrization that audited itself to death and published the autopsy — the framework fails honestly, but its headline statistics still over-refute in ways a referee would bounce.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project." I skipped the beginner path and went straight for the equations.

**Landing page** shows one equation: C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)), plus a scoreboard I've never seen on a framework site: 0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations, 0 of 6 "validated" claims surviving audit. My crank alarm, primed by "one equation from quantum to cosmic," partially stood down. Partially.

**Parameter Derivations** is where these frameworks usually hide the bodies, and this one instead exhibits them. Every parameter in the chain is tagged: γ = 2/√N_corr is a "Motivated Ansatz" that invokes the central limit theorem for *correlated* degrees of freedom (self-contradictory, and the page says so); the tanh is a "Motivated Choice" from the sigmoid family, explicitly *not* the mean-field tanh — the page correctly notes that the Ising tanh comes from the self-consistency loop m = tanh(βJz·m) while C(ρ) is evaluated directly, no fixed point, no critical point, no diverging correlation length. That distinction is exactly right and most physics-adjacent sites would have fudged it. The A-from-Jeans coefficient is documented as a "Chain-of-Custody Failure" — the stated formula gives 4.6×10⁻⁵, not the quoted 0.029, a 600× discrepancy the page itself computes. The golden-ratio exponent gets a forensic history: fitted at 1.62, ruled "coincidence" by their own session 45, resurrected without citation at session 170, with every claimed derivation dismantled (one candidly "gives exponent 1, not 1/φ"). Conclusion, verbatim: zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations. I have never seen a framework site conclude that about itself.

**The sign inversion is real and correctly diagnosed.** γ = 2/√N_corr makes the most collective systems flattest: BCS with N_corr ~ 10⁶–10⁷ gets γ ~ 10⁻³, i.e., the *sharpest* real transitions get the *smoothest* predicted curves. In mean-field land, fluctuations scale like 1/√N and transitions *sharpen* with correlated size. Any first-principles derivation must produce the opposite sign, and the site says exactly that. Correct physics, refuting their own formula.

**MOND Unification** confirms what I suspected three pages earlier: the modification is g_obs = g_bar/C(ρ), a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is Milgrom's own 1983 coincidence (properly credited, with McCulloch and Verlinde cited), and the framework's one genuinely distinct claim — coupling to local density ρ rather than acceleration — is what the data killed. The page admits its tidal-dwarf "prediction" interval [10.5, 14.5] nests almost entirely inside MOND's [10.9, 40.9]: a submodel that can only tie or lose. There's also a telling correction: momentum conservation required a post-hoc field equation ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ, meaning the original algebraic form wasn't even a consistent force law.

**Honest Assessment** is where my one serious complaint crystallized. The refutations are real, but the *statistics quoted for them* are inconsistent between headline and fine print, always in the over-refuting direction. The RAR transition-shape kill headlines ΔBIC = +184, computed on 2,807 SPARC points treated as independent when they're ~175 galaxies of correlated radial samples; the page's own "conservative intra-galaxy correlation correction" gives ≈33, yet +184 is what appears in summaries, and the effective sample size is never actually computed. TEST-04a registered a kill criterion on fσ₈ but headlines a "2.4σ tension" on σ₈ — a different, GR-conditioned parameter; the page eventually flags the swap but the amplitude number still leads. The BTFR kill quotes 123 of 175 SPARC galaxies with no stated selection. None of this rescues the framework — free-γ fitting converges to 0.489, which is just MOND's simple μ, so the kills stand — but a referee would strike every one of those headline numbers. Refuting yourself with inflated statistics is still inflated statistics.

**Chemistry Correlation Explorer** does something I want to praise loudly: next to its flagship r = +0.982 (γ vs sound velocity, 1,703 phenomena), it displays a 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z that matches or *beats* every correlation, and states plainly that "high r here is evidence of density-monotonicity (known chemistry), not of C(ρ)-specific physics." That's the null model every correlation-mongering paper omits. Two quibbles: the sortable table still leads with the 0.98s before the null caveat, and if the null *outperforms* the model on the model's own targets, the honest badge is "Failed," not "Null-Class" — the page is inconsistent with its own taxonomy here. Also unresolved: the correlation runs positive with γ in materials where the formula assigns *low* γ (high N_corr), i.e., the flagship number uses the flipped orientation of their own parameter.

**Equation Anatomy** was the disappointment. It walks C(ρ) component-by-component but never makes the Ising/self-consistency distinction the Parameter Derivations page handles so well — a condensed-matter reader who sees tanh in a coherence context will assume the mean-field connection, and this tool leaves that door open on the very page whose job is anatomy. Worse, the "+1" inside the logarithm is never discussed at all. That regulator is not cosmetic: it sets the entire small-ρ behavior (C ≈ γρ/ρcrit, linear, instead of the log divergence you'd get from ln(ρ/ρcrit)), which is precisely the deep-MOND regime where all the galaxy phenomenology lives. An anatomy tool that skips an organ.

**Key Claims / For Researchers / Open Questions**: the badge audit mostly holds up. "Validated" is deprecated and nothing carries it; the six formerly-validated claims are individually demoted with correct diagnoses (Breit-Wigner for the "entity criterion," dimensional analysis for a₀). One badge is too generous: "Speculative" on the consciousness claim, which the page itself calls "unfalsifiable as stated" with "no calibration procedure" — unfalsifiable is a different category than untested, and their own taxonomy has no badge for it. The A2ACW audit-calibration numbers on For Researchers are startlingly honest: their expert-audit instrument false-flagged 6/6 canonical genuine discoveries as reparametrizations, so specificity is 0, Youden's J = 0 ± 0.46, and the site says the headline 0/6 "should not be quoted without an interval." An instrument with AUC ≈ 0.5 undermines their own demotions as much as their claims, and they say so.

**What I could not find**: any statement about the cosmological background. I tried /dark-energy (404) and Open Questions (DESI appears only via the fσ₈ growth test). A framework whose entire content is a density-dependent modification of gravity *must* say something about Friedmann-level expansion — what does C(ρ̄(z)) do to the background? With DESI's w₀–wₐ result being the live topic in cosmology, the absence of any w(z) statement on the visitor-facing pages is the biggest content hole I hit. Either the framework has no background sector (say so — that's a refutation-shaped gap) or it has one (show it — that's the one place left where it could differ from MOND, which famously has no cosmology).

### Tools Tested
- **Equation Anatomy** (/equation-walkthrough) — worked — clear stepping, physics-incomplete — Step-by-step structure is good pedagogy and the sign-inversion honesty box is prominent. But it omits the two things a physicist most needs: the Ising-vs-direct-evaluation distinction (handled correctly elsewhere on the site) and any discussion of the "+1" regulator that controls the deep-MOND limit. (Caveat: text-level access; I evaluated content, not scripted behavior.)
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (/chemistry-correlation-explorer) — worked — best statistical practice on the site — Displaying the Z-polynomial null beside the headline correlations is exactly what every correlation table should do, and it's fatal to the r = 0.98 claims in the correct way. Table ordering still leads with the impressive numbers, and the Null-Class/Failed boundary is applied inconsistently.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What does the framework predict for the cosmological background — w(z), or even just whether C(ρ̄) enters the Friedmann equations? This is the one sector where a density-coupled theory could genuinely differ from MOND, and the site is silent.
2. Is there a covariant action behind ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ, and does it satisfy the Bianchi identities / stress-energy conservation? The patch is stated; its consistency is not.
3. Which ΔBIC is the live verdict for the RAR kill — +184 or the correlation-corrected ≈33 — and what is N_eff for 2,807 points across 175 galaxies?
4. Was the argument of the compander ever swept? All the sigmoid-family robustness checks vary the outer function; the "+1" regulator inside the log seems never to have been audited, and it owns the deep-MOND regime.
5. If the audit instrument false-flags 6/6 genuine discoveries (specificity 0), how much of the 0/6 demotion verdict survives — and why does the headline still say "0 of 6" without the interval the site itself computed?

### Verdict
As physics, this is a nested submodel of MOND with one underived constant, and it cannot win by its own admission — the interesting question isn't whether it's right (it isn't) but whether anything was learned, and the parameter-derivations autopsy plus the chemistry null model are genuinely instructive artifacts. As practice, the self-refutation is real but statistically sloppy in the over-refuting direction: inflated ΔBIC headlines, criterion-swapped tensions, and uncomputed effective sample sizes would all get flagged in review regardless of which side they cut against. The site's rigor asymmetry — forensic about its parameters, casual about its kill statistics — is the thing I'd fix first, and the missing cosmological background sector is the thing I'd write next.

**Honest Assessment** contained the one thing I didn't expect: a dark-energy sector. "Evolving dark energy (DESI DR2 w₀wₐ) — removed from the silence list: the framework has a dark-energy sector, and it forbids the quadrant DESI prefers." The construction is H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C) with ρ_DE ≡ ρ_m(1−C)/C, a one-parameter family with C₀ = Ω_m forced. I checked the claimed asymptotics by hand and they're right: in the far past 1−C ≈ 2(ρ/ρ_crit)^(−2γ) gives ρ_DE ∝ a^(−3(1−2γ)), i.e. w = −2γ; in the far future C ≈ γρ/ρ_crit gives ρ_DE → ρ_crit/γ = constant, i.e. w → −1. Monotone, no phantom crossing, hence sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) — the opposite quadrant from DESI DR2's preferred (w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0) crossing in all four data combinations, which the page quotes as 3.4–5.4σ offsets. And the elegant, damning corollary is stated: at γ = 1/2 the background is *algebraically identical to ΛCDM* — the same γ = 1/2 that is exactly MOND's simple-μ in the galaxy sector, and within noise of the SPARC-preferred 0.489. The framework's own best-fit parameter is the point where it becomes indistinguishable from both incumbents simultaneously. Both sectors independently pin the theory to its own disappearance. That is the single most informative structural fact on the site and it's presented almost in passing.

**Top Decisive Tests** carries TEST-26 (DESI DR3 sign-lock, "drafted, not yet adopted") with framing I want to quote because it's rarer than it should be: "this test can only kill the framework or tie — recorded now so a later tie is not read as a success." The same page concedes the structural result — 0 of 24 cataloged tests could select the framework over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM.

But my expert objections to the DE sector as executed are real. First, the comparison to DESI is **contour-level in CPL space**. The framework's w(z) is a specific monotone one-parameter family; projecting a non-CPL w(z) onto (w₀, wₐ) is known to bias the recovered point, and there is an active literature (Shlivko & Steinhardt 2024; Wolf, García-García & Ferreira 2024–25; Cortês & Liddle) arguing DESI's crossing preference is partly a CPL-projection artifact. The honest move — fit the actual γ-family to the public DESI+CMB+SN chains — has not been done, and, strikingly, the projection-artifact literature is the framework's *best available defense* and goes uncited. A site this compulsive about steelmanning its executioners has missed the one live debate that cuts in its favor. Second, the sector is **background-only**: G_eff = G/C substituted into the Friedmann equation, with the caveat ("a covariant completion would generate Ċ-terms that could in principle move the locus — that derivation is open") doing enormous load-bearing work. Without a covariant action there is no perturbation sector, so the CMB-anchored contours being borrowed for the 3.4–5.4σ figures assume a perturbation model the framework doesn't have. Third, nobody connects the new sector to the site's own force-law fork: galaxies run on algebraic g = g_bar/C (with a post-hoc ∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ patch), cosmology on G/C in H² — which one generates which, and are they the same theory? The site invented the fork-amplitude diagnostic; it should apply it to its newest sector. Fourth, ρ_crit is recalibrated per sector (0.029·V_flat² in galaxies; C₀ = Ω_m forced in cosmology) — the "one equation" quietly carries sector-dependent constants, and that should be said where the sector is introduced.

**MOND Unification** confirmed the galaxy sector is a strict submodel — nested tidal-dwarf intervals, McGaugh's own interpolating function, Milgrom's own a₀ coincidence, all now properly credited. One framing error survives: "EFE = 0 exactly" is presented as the structural contrast with MOND, in tension with Chae et al. 2020's ~4σ detection. Two problems an expert catches immediately: (a) EFE = 0 is just the strong equivalence principle — it is *also* ΛCDM's prediction for internal dynamics, so this channel can only refute the framework (EFE detected → framework and ΛCDM both wounded, MOND wins) and can never select it; (b) the Chae 2020 detection is contested in the literature (environmental-systematics rebuttals by Freundlich et al. and Paranjape & Sheth), which the page doesn't note — this one actually *softens* the tension against the framework, and it's again a missed citation that would have helped them.

**Tier 1 / attribution check**: I went in expecting the classic AI-framework failure — presenting known results as discoveries. It largely passes now.
