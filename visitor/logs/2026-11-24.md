## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Refreshingly honest. The first ten minutes on the Beginner Path work, and the Galaxy Plotter gave me a real "aha", but outside that path every page piles internal bookkeeping (counting rules, ΔBIC, "Is Λ", calibration history) onto someone who just wanted the idea.

### Journey
I clicked through from Reddit. The headline, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, is exactly the Veritasium-style hook that gets me. The very next bold line is *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* I've never seen a science site lead with that. My first reaction was "huh, respect". My second was "wait, then why am I here?" The page answers that with "Dead as tested physics so far… alive as a public record of testing it." I sort of get it, but "a public record of testing" is not obviously interesting to me yet. The Gaia DR4 date (2 December 2026, eight days away) was the most concrete hook on the page: something is about to happen. Then it says the data "can remove options but cannot confirm anything new", and the air goes out a bit.

Below the hero the landing page turns into a wall. There's a "Where each piece stands" table with rows like "Cosmology: **Is Λ**", "Local substrate on Bell's test: Excluded", "Bell escape plus absolute clock: One joint requirement (open)". Stat tiles say things like "1,703 chemistry 'phenomenon type' counter reading" and "15–27 orders of magnitude gap for the preferred-frame sector". There's an equation strip with ΔBIC = +184, S = 1.85, α/π ≈ 2×10⁻³. There's also a line saying the framework is "a family, not one theory," with four functions sharing the letter C. I skimmed all of it and understood none of it, so I clicked the big "Start here: Why Synchronism?" button.

**Why Synchronism** was the best page of the visit for me. It has a short top section (about 500 words): physics has a "fragmentation problem", the proposal in one line (crowding makes parts act as one), what happened in three bullets (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, and the word "refutation" is defined right there). Then the section **"Why does matching an existing theory count as losing?"** taught me something about how science works that I didn't know. Then it says *"Beginner path: that is all you need from this page"* and folds the rest. That's exactly what I needed. I peeked into the fold and bounced off "Debye model" and "Abrikosov–Gor'kov".

**First Encounter** (Step 2) opened with the marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza analogy, which landed. The neutron star being "10¹⁴× denser than lead" is a fun fact I'll repeat. "Markov Relevancy Horizon" is a scary name, but the explanation (an atom doesn't "feel" the Andromeda galaxy) clicked. The page kept pulling the rug out early, though. The intro, before I even knew what C was, says the formula scores superconductors *low*, "the opposite of this picture". In Section C I learned the γ idea and in the same breath that "the formula for γ failed" with the sign backwards (+0.55 predicted vs −0.55 measured). I appreciate the honesty, but I never got to hold the idea before watching it die. Section D (1,700 chemistry sessions, a "template-bias caveat") and Section E (ρ_crit = 0.028 × V_flat^0.5, "a calibrated constant") lost me completely. Section G ("Where It Fails") was oddly satisfying: 607 K predicted vs 93 K real for a superconductor, 6.5× wrong. Concrete numbers I can actually understand.

The **Interactive Tools** index is clear: 7 tools, each with a Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced level and a suggested start order (Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment). Five of the seven carry a state called "Artifact Lesson", which isn't explained on the card. Most of the badges say "Audited-Negative" or "Failed", so the toolbox is mostly a museum of what went wrong. That's fine, but it's not what I expected from "interactive tools".

The **Galaxy Plotter** was the highlight. *"New here? Look at three things on the chart below"*: the dots are what telescopes see, the gray dashed line is visible matter alone, and the gap between them is "the dark-matter puzzle". I finally *saw* what dark matter is a puzzle about. The legend is in plain English ("Newton, visible matter only (no dark matter, no change to gravity)"). Seeing the violet "this framework's equation (tested reading, failed)" curve miss while green MOND follows the dots made the whole "it collapsed onto MOND" story land in a way the landing page's text didn't. The controls under the chart drop back into insider language, though. The slider is labelled "mass per unit of 3.6 μm light". The radio buttons choose between "0.028·Vflat^0.5" and a "Units hybrid: 0.029·Vflat² (this page's only curve until 2026-11-14)". There's also a checkbox to "Show the old hand-drawn stand-in… kept for the record". Why is a hand-drawn line on a science chart at all? That made me trust the plot less, not more.

The **Coherence Explorer** is labelled "best first tool", and its first box is honest: it's "a shape toy", the density axis "has no units", and "no setting here is one the framework predicts". The instruction *"drag γ up to 2… then down to 0.5 and watch the S flatten"* is the kind of guidance I want. The preset buttons, though, have whole paragraphs as labels: "γ = 0.046 — the density version's free fit to galaxies (its fitted knee is off this axis, so the drawn curve is not that fit; it lost, ΔBIC +2843)". I left knowing what a dimmer-switch curve looks like and nothing about the universe.

**Honest Assessment**: the page itself says it's ~30,000 words, and it kindly tells beginners they can stop after ~4 minutes. The "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" section and the "Who is doing this work" line (*"No outside human domain expert has audited these claims"*) are both valuable. The fact that it's all AI agents plus one human called "dp" is genuinely surprising and should probably be more prominent. But a section called "Decisions queued on the project lead (12)" and a "How firm is the 6?" box left me feeling the scoreboard itself is still being argued over. The landing page also says "6, range 4 to 8 depending on the counting rule". As a casual reader I'd rather have one number and a footnote.

The **Glossary** (134 terms) has a "New here? Start with these ten" list, which is good. The tanh entry ("an S-shaped curve… rises steeply at first, then levels off at 1") is the plain version I needed. I looked for "Intent" because the CFD entry says "a field (Intent) flows", but there's no Intent entry.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the main equation failed and the galaxy part turned out to be MOND, what is the "bigger question" that Why Synchronism says is "still open"? I couldn't find it stated plainly.
2. What exactly should I look for on 2 December when Gaia DR4 comes out, and what result would change anything here?
3. Who is "dp", and why is a physics project being run mostly by AI agents? Is that the actual experiment?
4. The Key Claims teaser lists "Quantum mechanics as synchronization" and a "Consciousness equation" as Speculative. What would those even mean, and are they connected to the failed density equation or separate?
5. What does "Is Λ" mean in the landing table? Is that good or bad for the theory?
6. Why is the number of refutations a range (4–8) that depends on a decision someone hasn't made yet?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science website I've ever stumbled on. The Beginner Path (Why Synchronism → First Encounter → Galaxy Plotter) mostly works, and the Galaxy Plotter's "look at three things" intro taught me what the dark-matter puzzle actually is. Outside that path, nearly every page puts the project's internal bookkeeping in front of a newcomer: counting rules, calibration dates, ΔBIC values, pending decisions. I came away understanding *that* the idea failed and roughly *why*, but not why I should keep following it. Make that "why" as clear as the honesty, and I'd come back for the Gaia DR4 update.

### Journey
I started on the landing page and mapped the information architecture first. The header has ten flat entries with no dropdowns, and each one has a hover description ("134 terms defined", "26 test IDs by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)"). Acronyms are handled better than on most science sites. MOND, MRH, CFD and A2ACW are expanded at first use. For the rest, an "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC): short key" link points forward to a key. The problem is placement. ## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /coherence-function, /parameter-derivations, /mond-unification, /galaxy-rotation, /dark-energy, /tier-1-existing, /equation-walkthrough, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /galaxy-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes, mostly as a case study in how an ansatz gets taken apart. There's nothing here I'd cite as physics.
- **Overall impression**: The algebra is right nearly everywhere I checked it, and the reparametrizations are identified correctly and without spin. The errors I found are second-order bookkeeping: a survey statistic computed under one calibration and displayed beside a number from another, a dimensionful ratio quoted as a discrepancy, and one stale "refuted at +184".

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as an "interesting AI project", so I expected a grand-unified-theory crank site with an LLM gloss. It isn't one. The landing page gives the equation, C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)), and then says straight away that "in ρ itself the function is concave everywhere". That's the first thing I would have objected to, since the "switch" only exists on a log axis, and they got there first. The scoreboard sorts each sector into "Excluded", "Is MOND", "Is Λ" or "No number to test". As a summary of a framework that hasn't made it, that's about as compressed and honest as you can get.

**/coherence-function** was where I sat down with a pencil. I checked the following, and they're all correct:
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1].
- dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x). It's maximal at x = 0 and decreasing, so C is concave in x, as stated.
- Small x gives C ≈ γx, and large x gives 1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ}.
- At γ = ½, C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), i.e. Milgrom's simple μ with a₀ = 2a₀′.
- The log-axis inflection satisfies u·sech²u·(1 − 2u tanh u) = 0. That gives 2u tanh u = 1, u ≈ 0.772, so the "0.77 ρ_crit/γ" knee for large γ is right.

As physics there's nothing here: every sector reduces to MOND's simple μ, Λ, Milgrom's Σ_M or a textbook identity, and the site says so in its own badges. ### Verdict
As physics there's nothing new. Every surviving piece is MOND's μ, Λ, or prior art, and everything that differs is excluded, the density-keyed law on four independent legs. The site states all of this before I can. A2ACW is not a sound discovery method, and the site now says so with numbers. What remains of value is a short list of side results about other people's theories (wide-binary companions, a Draco cap bound, Cassini on a new interpolation family, a₀(z) bookkeeping) and the "frame-hiding" argument. The side results are less tested, less consistent with the published literature, and less exposed to outside eyes than the self-critique around them. The wide-binary crux box contradicts both groups' own framing, the Draco bound probably uses a 2009 error bar, and nobody outside has been asked about any of it. The site has become very good at auditing its own framework. The next useful step is sending these results to the people whose theories they concern.
