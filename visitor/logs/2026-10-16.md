## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment, /what-synchronism-is-not)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Refreshingly upfront that the idea failed, and the marching-band picture plus the galaxy plotter got me somewhere real, but I kept meeting different "γ" values and could never tell which one was "the" number.

### Journey
I came from Reddit expecting a crackpot "theory of everything" site. The headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") fit that expectation, and the subheading ("An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything") plus "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." immediately broke it. That got my attention more than a big claim would have. I've never seen a site lead with its own failure. The routes for "no physics background", "physicist" and "researcher" were helpful, and I took the beginner one.

The marching band vs. plaza crowd analogy landed instantly. The equation right below it (tanh, ln, ρ/ρcrit) did not. I skimmed past it, and the page does say it's okay to.

**/why-synchronism**: "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a good hook. The dimmer-switch picture worked. "Beginner path: you can stop here" after the first section felt kind, though it made me wonder whether I'd missed the point. The "What We Tested" section quickly fills up with a₀, cH₀, SPARC, ΔBIC, BTFR, YBCO and p-values. I understood "predicted 607 K, real answer 93 K" (way off!). I didn't understand "reproduced within 13% but shared with MOND" until a later page explained MOND.

**/first-encounter**: This is the friendliest page. It finally explains MOND in plain words: gravity gets stronger than Newton says in very weak pull, instead of adding invisible stuff. It never says what dark matter actually *is*, though. Section headings are lettered (A–G) with a progress bar, which made it feel like a course. The line "this is exactly MOND's own formula, so it matches MOND by construction" was the moment I understood the main problem: their equation turned into someone else's older idea.

**/interactive-tools**: This was a clear menu with Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced labels. I liked the note that "Beginner" means easy, not true. Without it I'd have assumed the beginner tools were the ones that work.

**/galaxy-plotter**: This was the best page for me. Picking DDO 154 and seeing the gray Newton line sag below the blue dots made "the dark matter puzzle" click visually. The "start here" guidance (compare gray to dots, then green) was exactly what I needed. The page actually *does* define dark matter ("a proposed invisible substance that has never been directly detected"). It just does so here, not on First Encounter, where I first needed it. Then the twist: their own violet line is wrong and MOND's green line is right. That's honest, but it made me ask why I'm on this site instead of a MOND site. The long revision notes under the chart buried the takeaway.

**/coherence-explorer**: The sliders were fun. Sharpness changes cliff vs. ramp, and the knee shifts left and right. But there are four preset γ buttons (0.046, 0.49, ½, 2), each attached to a phrase I didn't understand ("density-keyed free fit", "acceleration version", "MOND's simple formula", "original guess"). The default, 0.49, is described as the value galaxies prefer but which "fails a Solar System test (Cassini) at 8.7σ". So the tool opens on a number the page says fails? I don't know what σ means, but 8.7 of them sounds bad.

**/gamma-calculator**: You type how many particles move together (N_corr) and get γ. The default is 4 → γ = 1.000, "Boundary". I liked the page saying plainly that "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails." But I got really confused here. The **"Galaxy" preset (N_corr = 17) gives γ ≈ 0.49**, yet the gray ghost curve on the same chart is labelled the **"γ=2 galaxy pin"**, and γ=2 is the **ideal-gas** preset. So on one screen, "galaxy" is both 0.49 and 2. Deeper in the text there's an explanation ("the galaxy pin γ = 2 quoted on Core Idea gives N_corr = 1 — the ideal gas preset, exactly; and the SPARC best fit γ ≈ 0.49…"), but it's buried and assumes I know what "pin" and "SPARC best fit" mean. Counting across the two tools, I've now seen "galaxy γ" = 0.046, 0.49, ½ and 2.

**/honest-assessment**: The "Not a physicist?" box is excellent: "The one equation did not hold as stated. It lost every test it executed — 6 refutations, 0 predictions confirmed." The definitions "Untested means nobody has looked yet; Failed means someone looked" were the clearest sentences on the site. Then the page goes on and on (it says ~18,000 words), and I bailed after the first table.

**/what-synchronism-is-not**: "In plain words" was readable, and "Its galaxy formula turned out to be an existing idea (MOND) in disguise" is a great one-liner. The "Not:" cards beneath (Lorentz, Hořava–Lifshitz, comagnetometer) were way over my head, but the page said I could skip them.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Which single γ value, if any, does the framework currently stand behind for galaxies?
2. If their equation just becomes MOND for galaxies, is anything left that's *theirs*?
3. What is dark matter, in one sentence, on the beginner path? (I only found it on the plotter.)

### Verdict
This is the most honest "one equation for everything" site I've ever seen. It tells you up front it didn't work, and the galaxy plotter actually taught me what the dark matter puzzle is. But the beginner tools show a pile of γ numbers whose meanings change between pages, and "galaxy = 0.49" and "galaxy = 2" on the same calculator chart lost me. I might come back to show someone the plotter, but I left knowing *that* it failed better than *what* it was.

The landing page also says up front that the text after a badge's dash is a "finding descriptor, not a separate badge". That rule is good, and I wish more products wrote rules like it.

**Step numbering.** The Beginner Path is "5 steps" on the landing, on /learning-paths, and in every page's step bar ("Step 1 of 5" on Why Synchronism, "Step 2 of 5" on First Encounter, "Step 5 of 5 … Final step" on Honest Assessment). **Glossary.** This is the strongest asset. There are 112 alphabetised terms, Greek sorted separately, the nine badges marked "(badge)", and the two badge families (MRH-relationship vs Descriptive) explained in the intro. A "Variables in this equation — defined before we start" legend defines all six symbols (C, ρ, ρcrit, γ, tanh, ln) in plain words, which is textbook-good. Two nits. The opening warning uses γ = 2/√Ncorr *before* the legend that defines γ. In the server-rendered HTML I found exactly one ("no shared transition physics is known to connect the systems grouped here…"), for the active γ≈1 band. ### Unanswered Questions
1. What *is* the 1,703? A table of phenomena, a set of fitted γ values, or a session counter? Which page is current?
2. Is there a single source for the galaxy-sample numbers (14,585 / 14,435 / 14,610), or is each page hand-maintained? The pattern of fixes landing on one page but not its siblings suggests the latter.
3. Does the site run any link checker for `#fragment` targets?
4. Why does "Predictions & Experiments" appear as a breadcrumb section if it has no index page? Should it have one, or should the crumb go?
5. Who owns step-count strings? The "5 of 6" survives while the step bars are correct, so are the cards and the step bars fed from different sources?

### Verdict
From a docs-architecture standpoint this site is unusually well-instrumented. It has a real glossary, defined badge families, explicit "this is not a verdict" disclaimers on content categories, and accessible breadcrumbs. Most of my findings are **propagation failures**: a correction lands carefully on one page (the 14,585/14,435 reconciliation, the "1,703 is a counter" note, the H₀ contradiction callout) while the sibling sentence it corrects stays live elsewhere. The fix isn't more careful writing. It's rendering repeated numbers and labels from one source and adding a fragment-link check, so the next correction can only be made once.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /galaxy-rotation, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /coherence-explorer, /cdm-discrimination, /tier-1-existing, /dark-matter)

- **Overall impression**: The arithmetic is right everywhere I checked, and the reparametrizations are named as such. The remaining problems are a few un-hedged lead sentences that still claim derivations the rest of the site has withdrawn.

### Journey
A colleague said "interesting AI project", so I skipped the onboarding and went straight to **/parameter-derivations**, expecting to find where the hand-waving lives. The page beat me to it: it calls itself a calibration page and says "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations." So I checked the algebra instead of the rhetoric:

- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]: correct. At γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2): correct. It is MOND's simple μ with the scale doubled, as the page says.
- a₀ = cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4: I get 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², 13% below 1.2. Correct.
- Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG): I get 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc². Correct. The page also anticipated my convention quibble: Milgrom's Σ_M is usually a₀/G ≈ 861, and a note explains the 2π normalization.
- A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²) with G = 4.30×10⁻³ pc (km/s)²/M☉ and R₀ = 8 kpc: I get 4.6×10⁻⁵, the page's number. So the "600× off" audit of the 0.029 calibration is itself correct.

**/galaxy-rotation**: the compander g_bar = g_obs·tanh(γ ln(1+g_obs/a₀′)) at γ = ½ is μ_simple with a₀ = 2a₀′. I verified that. The γ-free row reports ΔBIC +7.1 with Δχ² ≈ −0.84, and ln(2807) = 7.94 gives exactly that, so the "entirely BIC penalty" claim is right. The statement "There is no γ for which the compander is both distinct from MOND and consistent with SPARC" is the honest conclusion, and the page reaches it on its own.

**/tier-1-existing** holds the cleanest physics on the site. A boost capped at 1/Ω_m has a Keplerian deep limit, so it can produce neither n = 4 nor flat outer curves. TEST-10's convention-free form ("B_max ≲ 14 excluded by SPARC dwarfs", with NGC 2915 needing B = 13.7, i.e. 1/(1−0.927)) is a real, model-independent kill, and the KiDS lensing figure of B ≈ 11 at 10⁻¹⁴ m/s² backs it. I checked f_DM ≤ 1 − Ω_m = 68.5%. TEST-09 is honestly flagged as convention-dependent. It also turns out to depend on a₀: with a₀ free, exponent 1 reaches n = 3.50, which does not fire.

The site anticipated my sharpest objection: γ = 2/√N_corr runs backwards relative to mean-field finite-size scaling. In Curie–Weiss the rounding width scales as N^{-1/2}, so a sharpness parameter should go as √N, the exact reciprocal. The site acknowledges the inversion. I have one quibble about the stated mechanism (see friction).

Then I went looking for overclaims and found them on **/dark-matter**. Its body says, with no hedge: "What Synchronism adds is a mechanism: the coherence function predicts where and why the transition occurs. a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.04 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² **The MOND acceleration scale emerges from cosmology**." Twenty lines later, the same page's Related Concepts box says "the a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π coincidence (known since 1983) … the density equation has no a₀." The a₀ card on /parameter-derivations has the same problem. Its badge says "Reparametrization — Dimensional Analysis", but its first sentence reads "a₀ = cH₀/(2π) **follows from Synchronism's coherence function**." It can't both follow from C and be a dimensional coincidence absent from C. (Verified in raw HTML on both pages.)

**/cdm-discrimination** has a propagation failure with physics content. The "Honest Caveat" box says "Net verdict: σ_int = 0.086 dex **is consistent with CDM**." Further down, the Desmond 2017 paragraph says the "sits inside the CDM range" verdict "is not supported by the literature as it stands" and is suspended. Desmond's abundance-matching scatter is ~0.25 dex, about 3× the observed figure. Against that external benchmark, 0.086 dex would be *below* CDM, which is the very reading the page retracted. But that is the well-known BTFR-tightness problem for ΛCDM, and MOND explains it with zero intrinsic scatter. So even the restored reading would select the MOND class, not Synchronism. The page should say this so the reader doesn't read the suspension as a hidden win.

**/honest-assessment** has two stale sentences that contradict its own scope statement.
