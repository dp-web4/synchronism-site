# Finding: Entity Criterion Γ < m — PDG Audit and Novelty Assessment

## Origin
Today's visitor log (Pass 4, researcher): "I found exactly one candidate novel prediction: the entity criterion Γ < m." Self-directed follow-up to assess whether this is genuinely novel.

## Summary
The entity criterion (Γ < m) is the framework's sole surviving candidate for a genuinely novel prediction. Testing it against known PDG data reveals it correctly classifies essentially all established particles, with the f₀(500)/σ meson (Γ/m ≈ 1.22) as the only clear violation — a particle whose status has been genuinely controversial for 60 years. However, the criterion is less novel than claimed: the condition Γ < m is implicitly assumed in all resonance physics (narrow-width approximation), and the "ontological" distinction from NWA is philosophical, not physical. The oscillation basis from which the criterion is "derived" cannot itself be derived from the framework's own transfer rules (documented in CFD_Structural_Tensions.md). The criterion is real, but it's a well-dressed restatement of standard physics, not a new prediction.

## Research Notes

### 1. The Criterion

**Claim**: A particle exists as an entity only if Γ < m (natural units), meaning it persists long enough to complete at least one Compton oscillation (τ > 1/m, where τ = 1/Γ).

**Physical interpretation**: The Compton frequency ω = m (in natural units) is the rest-mass oscillation. If the particle decays before completing one oscillation (Γ > m), it never establishes its identity as a recurring pattern.

**Derivation**: From the "oscillation basis" — entities are recurring patterns at the Compton frequency. If the entity decays in less than one period, the pattern never recurs. This gives τ > 1/m → 1/Γ > 1/m → Γ < m.

Note: a stricter version would require one FULL oscillation (τ > 2π/m), giving Γ < m/(2π) ≈ 0.16m. The framework uses the weaker version (one radian, not one period).

### 3. Is This Novel?

The framework claims the criterion is "derivable from Synchronism's oscillation basis but not from QFT." Let me examine both parts.

**Claim 1: "Not derivable from QFT"**

This is technically true — QFT does not have an ontological criterion that says "resonances with Γ > m are not particles." In QFT, a resonance is a pole in the S-matrix, and the distinction between "narrow resonance" and "broad resonance" is quantitative, not ontological. The f₀(500) is a pole; it has the same mathematical status as the ρ(770).

However, the practical distinction Γ < m vs Γ > m is **everywhere in particle physics**:
- The **narrow-width approximation (NWA)** assumes Γ/m << 1 and breaks down when Γ ~ m
- Breit-Wigner parametrizations are unreliable when Γ > m (the f₀(500) requires dispersive analysis precisely because of this)
- The **PDG itself** distinguishes between resonances with well-defined Breit-Wigner parameters and those requiring more sophisticated treatment
- The 60-year controversy over f₀(500) is precisely BECAUSE its width is comparable to its mass

The framework claims the distinction from NWA is "ontological" (NWA is a computational convenience; the entity criterion claims a physical threshold). But the physical content is the same: when Γ > m, the concept of "particle" becomes problematic. Every hadron spectroscopist knows this.

**Claim 2: "Derivable from the oscillation basis"**

The oscillation basis says entities are recurring patterns at the Compton frequency. If the pattern doesn't recur, it's not an entity. This gives Γ < m.

But as documented in `CFD_Structural_Tensions.md` (lines 189-204), the oscillation basis **cannot be derived from the framework's own transfer rules**. Scalar diffusion with positive diffusivity is dissipative — all perturbations decay, no oscillations occur. The oscillation basis is an independent postulate, not a consequence of the underlying dynamics.

So the "derivation" is: (independent postulate) → (entity criterion). This is not a derivation from first principles; it's a consequence of a chosen axiom. Any axiom system that postulates "entities must oscillate" will produce the criterion Γ < m. The question is whether the axiom is physically motivated, and the framework's own dynamics (scalar diffusion) undermine it.

### 4. The Deeper Problem: What Would It Take to Be Novel?

For the entity criterion to be genuinely novel, it would need to:

1. **Predict a specific Γ/m boundary** different from 1 — e.g., "entities require Γ/m < 0.7" or "the threshold depends on quantum numbers." The current Γ < m is the most obvious possible threshold.

2. **Predict the existence/non-existence of specific undiscovered particles** — e.g., "a scalar meson at 1200 MeV with Γ > 1200 MeV cannot exist as a resonance." This would be testable at LHCb or BESIII.

3. **Provide a quantitative criterion that differs from NWA** — e.g., predict different cross-sections or phase shifts for broad vs narrow resonances. The current criterion is purely classificatory.

4. **Be derived from dynamics, not postulated** — if the oscillation basis emerged from the Intent field dynamics, the criterion would be a genuine prediction. Currently, the dynamics (diffusion) can't produce oscillations.

### 6. What Honesty Requires

The site currently frames this as "the strongest candidate novel prediction" and "derivable from first principles; not derivable from QFT." A more honest framing:

"The entity criterion Γ < m provides an ontological interpretation of the well-known practical distinction between narrow and broad resonances. It correctly classifies all established PDG particles and predicts the f₀(500)/σ meson's controversial status. The criterion is derived from the oscillation basis postulate, not from the framework's dynamical equations. Its physical content overlaps significantly with the narrow-width approximation breakdown, though it adds an ontological claim that QFT does not make."

This is still interesting and worth presenting — the philosophical packaging is valuable even if the physics content is standard. But calling it a "novel prediction" overstates the case.

## Implications for the Site

1. **The honest-assessment page** should clarify that the entity criterion is a "novel interpretation" rather than a "novel prediction." Predictions generate new testable numbers; interpretations organize existing knowledge differently.

2. **The f₀(500) case study is compelling** — the framework's criterion aligns with 60 years of controversy. This should be presented as a validation of the ontological framework, not as a prediction.

3. **A productive direction**: apply the criterion to exotic hadron candidates (pentaquarks, tetraquarks, glueballs) where the particle/non-particle question is active. If the criterion makes classificatory predictions for the LHCb exotic state zoo (X(3872), Z(4430), etc.), that would be genuinely useful even if not novel physics.

3. **Connection to information**: If the oscillation basis is really about information — a system needs enough time to "process" its identity — then there might be connections to quantum information processing times and the Margolus-Levitin theorem (minimum time for state evolution = πℏ/(2E)).

4. **Why exactly Γ = m?** The threshold Γ/m = 1 has a natural interpretation: the energy uncertainty (Γ) equals the rest mass energy (m). Below this, the particle's mass is "well-defined" in the sense that ΔE/E < 1. This is the same as saying the resonance peak is distinguishable from zero energy. This interpretation doesn't require the oscillation basis at all — it follows from the energy-time uncertainty relation.
