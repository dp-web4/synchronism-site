# PRIMARY, Round 1

## 1. Label: **(A) Interpretive analogy only**

The whitepaper's rhetoric ("perfectly demonstrates," "no quantum mystery," "superposition doesn't exist") reads like (B). I won't defend that. A replacement theory needs its own dynamics that reproduce QM's quantitative predictions, and §5.1-5.5 supply no such derivation. The Ψ = Σαᵢψᵢ notation is explicitly "illustrative," and §5.3 says Synchronism "doesn't dispute results, it reframes their interpretation." I take that self-description at face value and defend the claim at that scope.

## 2. Bounded claim (one sentence)

The CRT analogy illustrates that a fixed, autonomously cycling process can yield qualitatively different recorded outcomes (smooth image, banding, single dot) purely from the sampling window and phase of the recorder, which makes it a useful intuition pump for why *time-integrated vs. single-shot detection* can look wave-like vs. particle-like without the source changing.

## 3. What it does NOT claim (one sentence)

It does not claim to derive the Born rule, to reproduce Bell/CHSH violations, or to show that "measurement problem" is dissolved in the formal sense. The analogy motivates a *hypothesis class* (sampling-dependent readout of a deterministic cycling process) and does not establish that quantum mechanics is that class.

## 4. Experiment class it must address to avoid being "just metaphor"

**Primary: Bell/CHSH-type tests, plus Kochen-Specker contextuality.** This is where the analogy is most exposed. A deterministic, pre-synchronized "two CRT screens" model is a local hidden-variable model, and those are bounded by |S| ≤ 2. Experiments, including loophole-free ones, observe S ≈ 2.4-2.8. So I concede upfront:

- The "raster entanglement" picture (§5.4), as written, **fails** unless Synchronism specifies a mechanism that is nonlocal, or violates measurement independence, or else gives up the claim "nothing travels / pre-synchronized from the start."
- To be more than metaphor, Synchronism must supply a **formal mapping**: the sampling rule (window, phase, detector-coupling) → a probability or outcome function that yields cos(2θ)-type correlations. Without that mapping, §5.4's "Bell's theorem makes perfect sense" is an assertion, not a result.

**Secondary tests the mapping should address:**

- **Delayed-choice / which-path:** the claim "path detection = resampling, not decoherence" must predict the visibility-vs-distinguishability tradeoff, V² + D² ≤ 1, quantitatively.
- **Quantum Zeno:** §5.5 says witnessing "influences system behavior," which contradicts its own "no observer effect" claim. This internal tension needs resolving.

## 5. Where I expect the Challenger to press, and my stance

- **Where the analogy breaks:** a CRT's output is classical and sampling-limited, whereas quantum statistics are not recoverable from a classical sampled process with local fixed phases (Bell). I accept this as a real limit of the analogy.
- **Formal mapping:** the "Technical Implementation" list (Planck-rate scan, Planck-length pixels) is a *label table*, not a mapping. It contains no equation connecting sampling parameters to outcome statistics. I accept this too.

## Status entering Round 2

**Narrowed**, already. The defensible residue is "pedagogical illustration of sampling-dependent readout." Claims about entanglement, Bell, and "no observer effect" in the strong sense are not defended without a formal mapping.

**Would settle:** a explicit model, with equations, in which a cycling-pattern plus a sampling rule reproduces the Malus law and the CHSH value 2√2 under stated assumptions (and states which of locality, determinism, or measurement independence it gives up).
