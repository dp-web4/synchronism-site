## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The honesty is genuinely refreshing and the CRT analogy made one thing click for me, but the front-of-site mixes confident-looking confidence badges with a back-of-site admission of "0 confirmed predictions" and I can't reconcile them.

### Journey

I clicked through from a Reddit link and the first thing I saw was *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — that got me. Two big buttons: "Start Exploring" and "See What Failed." A site with a "See What Failed" button is interesting enough that I wanted to poke around. I also noticed four big domain tiles (quantum, cosmology, chemistry, consciousness) each with a confidence-looking badge: "Strongly Supported," "89% Validated," "34 Predictions." That looked impressive.

I went to **Why Synchronism?** first because that's where I thought the elevator pitch would be. The pitch landed: physics is fragmented, what if one function spans all scales? Then the equation showed up — `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` — and even though there was a "dimmer switch" explanation underneath, my eyes glazed over the symbols. I don't know what tanh is and ρ is just a Greek letter to me.

I followed the "Need an analogy first?" link to **Two Reframes**. The CRT analogy is fantastic — *"only your synchronization timing with the ongoing process changed."* That's the kind of thing I'd repeat to a friend. The pendulum/centrifuge one for relativity I just bounced off; I don't know enough about time dilation to know what's being reframed.

Then I went to **First Encounter** expecting to be eased in. It started concretely (electron in vacuum vs. neutron star core) but pretty quickly hit "Markov Relevancy Horizon" with no on-page explanation. **Core Idea** has the same equation as Why Synchronism with the dimmer-switch reframe — that helped, but it's still the same wall of symbols.

I clicked the landing's **"See What Failed"** button — **404**. That's the page I most wanted to read. I had to find **Honest Assessment** through the menu instead. And honestly, *that* page is the one that made me trust the site. *"0 confirmed predictions"* across 3,302 sessions, with a list of specific failure magnitudes (53% melting-point error, 6.5× off on a superconductor). That's brave. But it also short-circuited my brain: the landing page told me "89% Validated" and "Strongly Supported" — and the honest page is telling me nothing has been confirmed? Which is it? I'm not enough of a scientist to know what to do with that contradiction, and nothing on the page tied the two framings together for me.

I then dipped into **Galaxy Rotation** (handsome statistics, "remaining 86% is unexplained" — appreciated the candor), **Measurement Without Observers** (lost me at "γ is high, its Ncorr is low"), and the **Gamma Boundary** chemistry page (good caveat about "sessions 134-2660 may have inflated results through repetitive AI analysis patterns" — that admission gave me whiplash; AI is doing the analysis?).

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the honest assessment says "0 confirmed predictions," why do the landing tiles say "Strongly Supported" and "89% Validated"? Are those different things? Both? Marketing vs. reality?
2. What is a "session"? The chemistry page says some sessions may have been inflated by AI analysis patterns — does that mean an AI did the experiments, or analyzed pre-existing experiments? The casual reader has no context for the methodology.

Fix the broken `/failures` link, reconcile the badge language with the honest assessment, and lead with the dimmer-switch metaphor instead of the equation, and this becomes a site I'd actively recommend rather than one I bookmarked but didn't quite trust.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 (12 with content, 2 confirmed 404s, plus 2 tool pages)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structurally — I can map the site)
- **Would return?**: yes (to use the glossary as a working reference)
- **Overall impression**: The glossary, validation taxonomy, and breadcrumbs show real IA discipline, but the site has not propagated that discipline outward — first-use definitions, sequential navigation, and badge conventions are applied inconsistently across the surface, and several front-of-site links lead to 404s.

### Journey

I worked methodically: landing → header nav (Glossary, Honest Assessment) → footer nav (Research Philosophy, Predictions) → the "Getting Started" sequence (Why Synchronism → First Encounter → Core Idea → Two Reframes → MRH) → a sample claim page (Galaxy Rotation) → publication roadmap → two tools → and I checked the URLs that prior friction reports flagged.

**The good news first.** The glossary at `/glossary` is genuinely well done — ~30 entries, a six-category validation badge taxonomy at the bottom, and consistent symbol definitions for ρ, γ, N_corr, ρ_crit, and tanh. The MRH definition on `/glossary` matches the formal definition on `/mrh` *verbatim* — that kind of consistency between a reference page and its glossary stub is rare and worth preserving. Breadcrumbs are present on every interior page I visited and follow a clean "Home / Section / Page" pattern. The validation badge taxonomy is documented in three places (`/glossary`, `/honest-assessment`, `/research-philosophy`) with matching definitions.

**Then the structural problems start showing up.**

*Sequential navigation is inconsistent.* `/first-encounter` has both Previous and Next buttons. `/two-reframes` has only a "Next: Measurement Without Observers →" — no Previous. `/core-idea` has no sequential nav at all, only a lateral "Choose Your Path" with five thematic branches. `/galaxy-rotation` has forward links only. There is no continuous ribbon connecting the "Getting Started" sequence, so a reader who lands mid-path can't easily go back to the beginning, and a reader on the linear path hits dead ends.

*The "previous" link on `/first-encounter` crosses section boundaries.* `/first-encounter` lives under "Getting Started"; its Previous button points to `/two-reframes`, which lives under "Quantum Physics" per its own breadcrumb. That's a cross-section "previous" — it implies a reading order that the breadcrumb hierarchy contradicts. Either the breadcrumbs are wrong or the prev/next is wrong.

*Two of the URLs the site itself advertises are broken.* The landing page button "See What Failed" still goes to `/failures`, which returns a hard 404 (Pass 1 flagged this; I confirmed). And `/mrh-calculator` — which fits the same naming pattern as `/coherence-explorer`, `/galaxy-plotter`, and `/chemistry-correlation-explorer`, and is the kind of URL a reader might type or guess from MRH content — also 404s. If this is intentional (no calculator exists), nothing on the site indicates that; if it's unintentional, it's a missing route.

*"Predictions" is two different things at once.* The footer link labeled "Predictions" routes to `/test-catalog` (URL slug). The breadcrumb on that page reads "Predictions & Experiments / Test Catalog." But the page itself is a catalog of 24 *experiments*, not a list of *predictions* with validation badges. Meanwhile `/honest-assessment` discusses confirmed/unconfirmed predictions but is a separate page. There is no single page titled "Predictions" that lists, for each prediction, the claim + status badge + evidence. Three labels (`Predictions`, `Test Catalog`, `Honest Assessment`) fragment what is conceptually one inventory, and a tech writer would consolidate them or at minimum align the link label with the URL slug and page title.

*The equation precedes the metaphor, on the page where the metaphor is most needed.* `/why-synchronism` (the elevator-pitch page) presents `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` in "The Approach" section without the dimmer-switch reframe. The dimmer-switch metaphor lives on `/core-idea`, which sits *deeper* in the path. Pass 1 hit this from the casual angle; structurally, the IA has the explanatory scaffolding placed *after* the load-bearing claim, which is backwards.

*First-use definitions are not enforced as a rule.* MRH is correctly expanded on `/first-encounter` and `/mrh`, but used without expansion on `/two-reframes`. γ is fully defined on `/core-idea` but appears on `/two-reframes` ("shared γ") with no inline gloss. On `/galaxy-rotation`, RAR/SPARC/SDSS/ALFALFA/CDM/MOND/BIC are defined inline (good), but "HI" in "ALFALFA HI survey" is not. On `/publication-roadmap`, "circularity percentages" and "readiness scores" are used as if defined, but I never saw them defined. There is no style rule being enforced: each page seems to make its own choice.

*The methodology vocabulary is undefined site-wide.* "Sessions" are referenced on multiple pages — the chemistry page mentions "sessions 134-2660 may have inflated results through repetitive AI analysis patterns," and `/research-philosophy` says "Session #616 audited all 4 research tracks." Yet `/research-philosophy` does not define what a session is, who/what runs it, or what "AI-assisted" means in this context. A reader cannot resolve "AI is doing the analysis?" from anything I read on the public site. This is the single biggest documentation gap I noticed: the methodology that produced the results is not documented on the page that purports to be about methodology.

*Validation badges work as a taxonomy but not as a UI convention.* The taxonomy is defined and consistent — Validated / Strongly Supported / Untested / Speculative / Reparametrization / Failed. But the badges are applied unevenly: the landing page tiles use badge-flavored language ("Strongly Supported," "89% Validated," "34 Predictions") with **no legend on the landing page itself** and no link to the taxonomy. `/galaxy-rotation`, a major claim page, carries no badge at all on its central claim — only a generic "experimental research framework" disclaimer. `/test-catalog` lists 24 experiments with no badges. So the taxonomy exists, but the surface where casual readers actually live (landing tiles, claim pages, prediction inventory) doesn't use it consistently. A landing-page reader sees badge-shaped marketing language without the taxonomy that would let them calibrate it.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (10 loaded, 2 confirmed 404s)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I can map the math; that is what makes the problems visible)
- **Would return?**: yes — but as a critical reader, not a believer. The honest-assessment page is doing real intellectual work; the theory pages are doing less than they advertise.
- **Overall impression**: The site is unusually self-aware about its own gaps — but a careful reader can show, page-by-page, that the central equation is a sigmoid with no derivation, the "mean-field" framing is cosmetic, the "derived" parameter γ is admitted-inconsistent, and the flagship novel prediction (500 Mpc) is publicly labeled "not a prediction in the scientific sense." The site is honest enough that the case against its own strong claims can be assembled from its own pages.

### Journey

I went straight to the math. I started on `/honest-assessment` to set the bar (six-category taxonomy, "0 confirmed predictions" after 3,302 sessions, named failed predictions with magnitudes — fine, this is the right framing) and then walked the derivation chain: `/core-idea` → `/coherence-function` → `/gamma-parameter` → `/parameter-derivations` → `/critical-density-derivation` (404) → `/galaxy-rotation` → `/mrh` → `/top-5-tests` → `/cosmic-interference` → `/wide-binaries`. Two interactive tools: the **γ Calculator** (new this pass) and the **Coherence Explorer** (Pass 2 tested it as UI; I went back to check the math).

**What I expected to find on the derivation chain.** A condensed-matter mean-field model gets you tanh through one specific construction: write a Hamiltonian, minimize free energy variationally, get a self-consistency equation `m = tanh(βJzm)`. The tanh isn't decorative — it comes out of the partition function for an Ising spin in a self-consistent mean field. Solving the loop is what gives you spontaneous symmetry breaking, a critical temperature, mean-field critical exponents (β=1/2, γ=1, δ=3, etc.), and the Landau expansion near criticality. That's how you go from "tanh appears" to "this model predicts something."

**What the site actually has.** `/coherence-function` says, candidly, "C(ρ) is not the Ising equation … the tanh result is a self-consistency loop where m appears on both sides. C(ρ) evaluates directly with no feedback loop." That single sentence is the whole story for a stat-mech reader: dropping the self-consistency loop strips the mean-field machinery of everything that makes it a *theory* rather than a *fitting form*. There is no order parameter (C is just a number you read off given ρ), no spontaneous symmetry breaking, no derivable critical exponents, no Landau expansion. The page goes further and admits "any sigmoid satisfying the four constraints would have been equally valid." That is correct, and it tells me the predictive content of the framework is in those four boundary conditions, not in `tanh` per se. Calling tanh "natural from mean-field" on the elevator-pitch pages while admitting on the technical page that the mean-field connection is only a shape-similarity is doing rhetorical work the math doesn't support. To the site's credit, the technical page says so. To the site's discredit, the front pages don't.

**γ = 2/√N_corr is the same problem in miniature.** `/gamma-parameter` derives the 1/√N from CLT (correct for fluctuations of an i.i.d. ensemble) and the factor of 2 from "phase space has 6 dimensions, contraction to 3 effective." That's numerology. `/parameter-derivations` is admirably honest about it: "integrating out momenta introduces temperature- and mass-dependent factors, not a clean factor of 2." That's the right concession — it means γ has the *form* of a derived quantity but the *content* of a fitted constant, with the inconsistency acknowledged. CLT scaling for ensemble averages is also a different object from a coupling constant in a free-energy expansion; using one to set the other is a category move that needs argument, not just dimensional matching. The site half-acknowledges this; it doesn't resolve it.

**`/critical-density-derivation` is a 404.** This is the page that matters most: ρ_crit = A·V²_flat with A ≈ 0.029 is the load-bearing parameter, and `/parameter-derivations` points to it for the "Jeans criterion ρ_crit = V²/(Gα²R²_half) → A ≈ 0.029" chain. I cannot verify how A is determined from the live site. If A is fitted (which the small numerical value strongly suggests), that's a free parameter, and the chemistry/galaxy "agreements" downstream are re-fits, not derivations. The prior log already noted the broken `/failures` and `/mrh-calculator`; this is a third broken URL on a more critical page.

**Galaxy rotation is the most honestly broken claim on the site.** `/galaxy-rotation` uses the McGaugh interpolating function `g_obs = g_bar / (1 − exp(−√(g_bar/a₀)))` — this is RAR/MOND verbatim. The a₀ "emerges from cosmology" as cH₀/(2π), which `/honest-assessment` correctly flags as a reparametrization (McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017, Smolin 2017 all derive this same dimensional combination). What Synchronism adds is an environmental scatter ansatz: σint = 0.086 ± 0.003 dex, environment effect at p = 5×10⁻⁶ over N = 14,585 galaxies, **R² = 0.14**. That's the number that matters. p = 5×10⁻⁶ is what you get for a tiny effect with large N; 86% of the scatter is unexplained. And the page explicitly states **"ΔBIC vs MOND: not calculated."** Without ΔBIC (or any model-selection statistic that penalizes the added parameters), you cannot claim Synchronism's environmental term is preferred over MOND. The page knows this — "ΔBIC would determine whether the fit improvement exceeds the penalty" — and lists it as missing. Fine. Then the landing tile shouldn't carry a "Strongly Supported" badge for galaxy dynamics.

**`/cosmic-interference` is the cleanest case of the site arguing against itself.** `/top-5-tests` lists "Cosmic Interference Patterns at λ ~ 500 Mpc" as **"Unique to Synchronism"** and **"Novel: Yes — explicitly unpredicted by other frameworks."** The `/cosmic-interference` page itself says: "the derivation of the ~500 Mpc scale does not yet exist on this site. This page is a placeholder … this is not a prediction in the scientific sense — it is an exploratory hypothesis." Both statements are on the public site at the same time. As a reviewer I would note the more pessimistic statement is the correct one — without a derived scale you cannot symmetrically falsify both amplitude *and* wavelength, so a null result rules nothing out. The top-5 page should not list it among genuine novel tests until the derivation page exists.

**`/wide-binaries` has the same problem at smaller scale.** Top-5 page calls it differing from MOND ("density-dependent vs. density-independent"); the page itself gives no formula, no slope, no threshold, no N_corr for stellar pairs. "The wide binary anomaly should depend on local environment density" is a direction, not a prediction. Gaia DR3 analyses are already in active dispute (Chae 2023 vs. Pittordis-Sutherland 2023 at the time of this writing); without a numerical prediction, Synchronism is not in the conversation either way.

**MRH is not Markov.** `/mrh` admits the Markov-blanket connection is invocation-by-analogy ("extends this idea") rather than a derivation from Markov-process formalism. There's no formal definition, no equation, no computational procedure. As a candidate replacement for "wave function collapse" (which the page openly labels untested), this currently has the same epistemic status as decoherence-without-decoherence or Penrose's OR — a research program, not yet a model. That is fine if framed that way; the site mostly does, with one exception: the "framework spans quantum to cosmic" tagline implicitly relies on MRH being the same object across scales, which the math doesn't yet establish.

**The chemistry r = 0.982 needs a footnote it doesn't have.** Sound velocity in a solid is `v = √(K/ρ)` — it is *defined* in terms of bulk modulus and density. If γ is constructed using density and any elastic-modulus information, a high correlation with sound velocity is partially tautological. `/honest-assessment` does flag the chemistry results as "non-independent variables" — that's the right hedge — but the chemistry pages and landing tiles do not carry that hedge alongside the r-value. A reader who sees "r = 0.982" on the front of the site and "non-independent variables" buried in the honest-assessment page will not connect the two.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — clean UI — Computes `γ = 2/√N_corr` live from a numeric N_corr (linear 1–1000 or log 1–10⁷). Six presets (ideal gas, water, enzyme site, ferromagnet, BCS superconductor, BEC) auto-fill N_corr, and a regime label snaps among Quantum / Boundary / Strongly Correlated / Macroscopic Coherence. **What works**: the math is what it claims (just one formula), the presets are useful intuition pumps. **What's wrong from a stat-mech angle**: no units anywhere on the page, the regime boundaries (γ ≈ 0.6, 1.0, 1.4) are hardcoded with no derivation visible, and the "BCS superconductor" preset places you in a regime where the framework's documented YBCO Tc failure (6.5× off) and Abrikosov-Gor'kov reparametrization both live, with no signposting from the tool to those known issues. The preset reads like a success story; it's actually pointing into the known-failure region.
- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — the math angle Pass 2 didn't push on — Plots `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` analytically. Critically, it **does not** iterate any self-consistency equation. A standard mean-field demo would show `m = tanh(βJzm)` converging from an initial guess; this tool can't do that because the framework's C(ρ) is by construction a closed-form evaluation, not a self-consistency loop (`/coherence-function` admits this). For a reader who knows what mean-field looks like, the absence of the loop in the "explore mean-field coherence" tool is exactly the structural admission the technical page makes in words.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Where does A ≈ 0.029 in `ρ_crit = A·V²_flat` come from quantitatively? The page that should answer this (`/critical-density-derivation`) is a 404. If A is fitted, every downstream "agreement" is a re-fit, and the badge taxonomy needs to reflect that.
2. What are the four constraints that uniquely pick the *family* of valid sigmoids (per `/coherence-function`)? They are referenced repeatedly but I never saw them enumerated on a page. They are the actual predictive content of the framework — they should be the front-of-site claim, not the tanh form.
3. What is the formal definition of MRH? Without a Markov-process formalism (transition kernel, conditional independence structure), "Markov" is being used as a metaphor. Either upgrade the formalism or relabel.
4. How are the regime boundaries on the γ Calculator (γ ≈ 0.6, 1.0, 1.4) derived? They feel chosen to make the presets land in expected categories — which would make them post-hoc bin edges, not predictions.
5. Why do `/cosmic-interference` and `/top-5-tests` make contradictory public claims about the 500 Mpc prediction? Either the test is "Unique to Synchronism, Novel: Yes" or it is "not a prediction in the scientific sense." Both cannot be true.
6. Has anyone computed ΔBIC for Synchronism's environmental-scatter term vs. MOND-only baseline on the ALFALFA-SDSS sample? This is a single-day analysis given the data are already in hand. Until it's done, the "Synchronism adds detectable environmental dependence on top of MOND" claim is structurally unfalsifiable.
7. On the chemistry side, what is the correlation between γ and sound velocity *after* partialling out density and bulk modulus? r = 0.982 with non-independent variables is the kind of number that should always be reported alongside its partial-correlation residual.

### Verdict

The site has done the rare thing of building its own steelman against itself — the honest-assessment page, the explicit "0 confirmed predictions" tally, the reparametrization admissions for a₀ and η, and the public placeholder on `/cosmic-interference` are all the right moves and most physics outreach sites don't make any of them. Where it falls down is propagation: those concessions live on dedicated honesty pages while the front-of-site tiles, the top-5 list, the chemistry r-values, and the galaxy-rotation framing still read as if the framework has predictive successes it does not have. The single most useful thing the site could do for a critical reader is to apply its own validation taxonomy uniformly: ρ_crit and γ get downgraded to "speculative / partially fitted" until the missing derivation page is built, the 500 Mpc test is removed from the top-5 list until it has a derivation, ΔBIC is computed for galaxy rotation, and the landing tiles inherit from `/honest-assessment` rather than fight it. The framework is then a coherent — and interesting — research program with one clean candidate prediction (Γ < m for entity status), several known failure modes, several reparametrizations, and a derivation backlog. That's a more honest and (to a grad-student reader) far more compelling story than the current front-page version.

**What I found, in order of severity.**

*The most quantitative test on the top-5 list has no page on the site.* `/top-5-tests` advertises "BAO Coherence Modulation" with a "10⁻⁴ density-dependent shift vs. zero (ΛCDM)" and calls it a "clean discriminator with no ambiguity." *`/gw-dark-matter` is also a 404, and this matters because of GW170817.* The top-5 list cites an existing bound "α < 3.0 × 10⁻¹⁵" for a GW-speed/dark-matter coupling and asks for "20+ events" to push to 10⁻¹⁶. *The `f₀(500) is "not a particle"` claim is the only candidate "novel prediction surviving stress tests" — and it's not novel.* `/honest-assessment` calls Γ < m for entity status the "strongest candidate novel prediction" and points to f₀(500) (Γ/m ≈ 1.16) as the example. "Synchronism predicts f₀(500) is not a particle" therefore aligns with one of two existing positions on a known controversy. *The "shared-environment decoherence protection" / 10× T₂ at c≈0.90 result is admitted post-diction.* The page references a PRL 2024 paper without a citation. Whether the paper exists or what it claims, the framework's own /key-claims labels this a post-diction. A referee reading "Synchronism is consistent with PRL 2024" learns nothing about whether the framework's machinery would have produced the result without seeing it.

*The Bell freezing/revival formula |S(t)| = S_max · e^(−Γt) on `/key-claims` doesn't appear on `/measurement-without-observers`.* And as written, this is just exponential decay of a Bell parameter — every decoherence model produces that. The "revival" half is what would be novel; no formula is given for revival, no experimental signature, no comparison with existing Bell tests under engineered shared environments.

*The methodology — A2ACW — is defined in the glossary as "AI agents stress-test each other's claims; one defends, another demands operational definitions and falsification criteria."* This is meaningful — it's an adversarial protocol between LLM agents, not free-form generation. The disclaimer page (`/what-synchronism-is-not`) describes it as "AI-to-AI validation sessions with human oversight" and reports 3,308 such sessions. The methodology page (`/research-philosophy`) does not describe A2ACW at all. As a reviewer, I would want to see: how the agents are prompted (do they have prior conditioning on correct physics, which would bias toward known reparametrizations); what the human oversight loop actually catches; and most importantly, *is there any case in which an A2ACW session has rejected a claim that the human authors would have kept, or accepted a claim that was later falsified by stress tests?* The answer to that last question is the only one that would tell me whether the protocol is calibrated. The site offers session totals as if quantity were calibration. It is not.

5. For Γ < m / Compton-oscillation entity criterion: what is one PDG state where this criterion makes a call that no existing analysis has made? f₀(500) is consistent with one of two existing camps; that's not novel.
6. With the March 2026 Bullet Cluster sign-error on the books, is the dark-matter-as-incomplete-decoherence claim retracted, modified, or under revision? `/key-claims` calls it a "structural failure" but `/galaxy-rotation` is unchanged. Which is the current state?
7. What numerical density-dependent slope does Synchronism predict for the Gaia DR3 wide-binary anomaly (dlog(anomaly)/dlog(local stellar density))? Without this, the framework cannot be cited by either the Chae or the Banik camp.
8. Are the chemistry r-values raw correlations or partial correlations controlling for density and elastic moduli? The sound-velocity correlation r = 0.982 is the most exposed example because v = √(K/ρ) is definitionally entangled with both.

### Verdict

The site is doing the right epistemic things at the meta-level — A2ACW as adversarial AI protocol is a legitimate research instrument; cataloging failures is the correct posture; the `/honest-assessment` page is more disciplined than 95% of physics-adjacent outreach. But for an expert reviewer the test isn't the meta-level, it's the inventory: which of the claimed novel predictions can I actually weigh against the current literature? The answer today is essentially none. The most quantitative cosmological test (BAO 10⁻⁴) has no page. The most experimentally tractable QM test (consciousness EEG) has no page. The GW test sits on top of an existing GW170817 bound that the site doesn't address. The flagship dark-matter mechanism has been internally falsified by March 2026 stress testing on Bullet Cluster but the falsification hasn't propagated to its own page or downgraded its tile. The strongest candidate "novel prediction" (Γ < m for f₀(500)) is consistent with one of two existing PDG camps. The wide-binary test, which could genuinely discriminate from MOND, has no number. The site is not in a state where I would recommend it to a colleague as containing testable new physics — it is in a state where I would recommend it to a colleague as a methodology study (does adversarial AI-to-AI session work generate falsifiable physics?), and the answer to that is interesting and currently negative. Building the four 404 pages and propagating the Bullet Cluster falsification to the dark-matter page would change my read substantially. Until then, this is a coherent research-program *index* without the research-program *content* at the locations the index points to.
