## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (`/`, `/first-encounter`, `/why-synchronism`, `/core-idea`, `/interactive-tools`, `/coherence-explorer`, `/galaxy-plotter`, `/equation-walkthrough`, `/honest-assessment`, `/two-reframes`, `/glossary`)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The analogies and the galaxy tool are genuinely great, but nobody ever told me why I should keep reading a theory whose own front page says it has failed everything.

### Journey

I got here from a Reddit link. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a good hook. That's exactly the kind of thing PBS Space Time does an episode about.

Then, immediately under it:

```
C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))
```

Five symbols, four of which mean nothing to me. `tanh` I have never seen in my life. `ln` I vaguely remember from a calculator button. Two Greek letters. My honest reaction in the first five seconds was "oh, this isn't for me." I nearly bounced right there. The only reason I didn't is that the buttons underneath were friendly: **Start Exploring**, **See What Failed**, **Beginner Path**.

Then I read the line near the bottom of the landing page: *"0 prospective predictions confirmed; 6 refutations executed."* And I stopped again — for a different reason. Wait, so… it doesn't work? Six things it predicted turned out wrong and zero turned out right? Why is this a website?

I want to be clear that I *respect* that. It's obviously more honest than the usual "SCIENTIST DISCOVERS THEORY OF EVERYTHING" stuff. But respect isn't the same as motivation. Nobody on this page told me what I get out of reading further. If a theory has a 0–6 record, I need a sentence explaining why the losses are worth my ten minutes — "here's what we learned by being wrong," or "here's why this is a better way to do research," or *something*. That sentence isn't there. I found the philosophy later, buried, but by then I'd already almost left twice.

I clicked **Beginner Path** and got "Why Synchronism?" — Step 1 of 6. The framing of the problem was the clearest writing on the whole site: physics has one set of equations for tiny things, a different set for huge things, another for chemistry, and nothing at all for consciousness. That I understood completely, and it made me want the answer.

Then **First Encounter** (Step 2 of 6) delivered the best moment of the visit. *Coherence is how collectively a group of things behaves. A marching band is high coherence. A crowd wandering a plaza is low coherence.* That's it — that's a real explanation. I got it instantly and I'd be able to repeat it to a friend. Same with "presence" being basically how crowded the neighborhood is: one electron in empty space vs. the inside of a neutron star.

And then, two paragraphs later, this simple idea got a name: **Markov Relevancy Horizon**. The idea behind it is easy — only the neighbors close enough to matter count, an atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy. That's a lovely image! But "Markov" means nothing to me and the three-word name arrives before the easy idea has had time to land. Then it got a "formal version" involving *degrees of freedom*, which is another thing I don't know. From that point the page shifted from "explaining" to "naming," and my attention dropped.

Confusing detail: this page says "Step 2 of 6" at the top and then "7 short pages — ~90 seconds each" a bit further down, with its own internal "Section 1 of 7." Two different counters on one screen. I genuinely did not know how much reading I had signed up for.

I bailed to the **Tools** page because playing beats reading. More on those below — the Galaxy Curve Plotter is the best thing here by a wide margin.

I then took the **Two Reframes** side trip, which First Encounter offers to people who "prefer analogies to equations." That's me! But it turned out to be a very long read, and the main analogy is an old CRT television scanning a beam across a screen. I know what a CRT is from photos. I have never owned one. My parents' generation would get it instantly; I had to reconstruct the picture before the analogy could do any work.

Last I checked the **Glossary**, half expecting to be defeated. I wasn't — every single word I'd tripped over was in there, in plain English, with the crowd analogy for coherence and everything. That was a real relief. But I only found it because it's in the top nav and I went looking. Nothing in the middle of First Encounter said "confused? every term links to a definition."

Running under all of this: the footer, which appears on **every single page**, reads *"6 refutations executed on external data (RAR shape, BTFR slope, dwarf DM fractions, environment null, Cassini/SPARC squeeze, Bell/CHSH substrate)."* That's seven acronyms I can't read, repeated at the bottom of every page I visit, including the beginner ones. It's the most intimidating text on the site and it follows me around.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — *worked* — *mostly intuitive* — Two sliders, live curve, and it opens with the crowd/marching-band line so I knew what I was looking at. Dragging γ down and watching the S-curve flatten out genuinely taught me something: the parameter controls how *sudden* the switch from "crowd" to "band" is. That clicked. What didn't: the horizontal line at C = 0.50 doesn't sit where the curve reaches the reference density, which made me think I'd broken it, and the table of four-decimal numbers underneath had no takeaway attached. The x-axis label `log₁₀(ρ)` is also unreadable to me — I know it means "very spread out numbers" and that's all.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — *worked* — *intuitive, best thing on the site* — This is the page I'd send to a friend. "The dashed line is what rotation curves *should* look like with only visible matter. The blue dots are what we actually observe. The gap is the dark matter problem." I have watched multiple YouTube videos about dark matter and this one paragraph plus one chart explained the actual problem better than any of them. And then — this is the part that impressed me — the site's *own* purple line visibly fails to close the gap, and the page says so out loud. Watching a theory's homepage show you its own theory losing is a genuinely novel experience. My only complaint is the five-curve pileup and the deliberately fake amber line, which I couldn't make sense of.

- (also poked at `/equation-walkthrough`) — The dimmer-switch description of tanh — "input near 0 → output near 0, input very large → output near 1" — is the single sentence that made the scary equation stop being scary. That explanation should be on the landing page, next to the equation itself, not six clicks in.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If zero predictions have been confirmed and six have been refuted, why is this still worth reading? I believe there's a good answer — the site clearly thinks documenting failure is the point — but nobody told me the answer up front.
2. What is a "prediction" here, and why does "prospective" matter so much? The site keeps distinguishing prospective from other kinds and I never learned why that distinction is doing so much work.
3. Is coherence a real physical thing you can measure with an instrument, or is it a number someone calculates from a formula? After eleven pages I still don't know which.
4. The galaxy plotter shows Synchronism's line failing where MOND's line works. So… is the answer just MOND? Why isn't that the conclusion of the whole site?
5. What does consciousness have to do with any of this? It's listed on the front page as one of the gaps the framework wants to address, and then I never found the thread again on the beginner path.
6. Where did the equation come from in the first place? The site says tanh was "picked because it matches the data," which was refreshingly blunt — but then why *this* equation rather than any other curve with the same shape?

### Verdict

The teaching materials here are better than most popular-science YouTube: the marching-band explanation of coherence, the dimmer-switch explanation of tanh, and above all the galaxy plotter, which taught me what the dark matter problem actually *is* in one paragraph and then showed its own theory failing to solve it. I've never seen a site do that and I liked it.

But the site is built for someone who has already decided this is worth their time. It leads with a wall of Greek letters and a 0-for-6 record, and never once tells a curious stranger why a losing record is interesting. I got there eventually by reading the philosophy, and I'd probably come back for the tools — but the version of me that clicked away in the first ten seconds is the more likely one, and it wouldn't have been that person's fault.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 (`/`, `/learning-paths`, `/first-encounter`, `/coherence-function`, `/key-claims`, `/honest-assessment`, `/research-philosophy`, `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/for-researchers`, `/glossary`, `/interactive-tools`, `/gamma-calculator`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The prose is unusually disciplined and the breadcrumbs are flawless, but the site's controlled vocabulary is duplicated in four places and then ignored by its own largest table — the documentation system is drifting away from the documentation.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs site before a release: nav coverage, term consistency, definition-before-use, and single-sourcing of anything that looks like a controlled vocabulary.

**First impressions are strong.** The landing page has a genuine information hierarchy — a question, then a one-paragraph thesis, then an explicit audience router ("No physics background? … Physicist? … Researcher?"). That router is the best structural decision on the site. Most technical sites make the reader guess which door is theirs; this one names three readers and points each somewhere specific. Breadcrumbs are present and correctly formed on **every** page I visited — Home / Section / Page, no exceptions, no orphans. That is rarer than it should be and I want it on the record before I start complaining.

**Then I checked whether the breadcrumbs and the top nav agree.** They don't. The top nav is: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. The breadcrumb sections are: Getting Started, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments, Methodology, Interactive Tools. These are two independent taxonomies over the same pages. "Start Here" lands on a page whose breadcrumb says "Getting Started." "Research Philosophy" and "Honest Assessment" are siblings in the nav but the breadcrumb files one under Methodology and the other under Getting Started. And most consequentially: **"Core Theory" has no top-nav entry at all.** `/coherence-function` — the page that explains the equation the entire site is about, step 1 of both the Intermediate and Physics learning paths — is reachable only by clicking through a learning path or a body link. The theory has no navigational home.

**The badge taxonomy is single-sourced in principle and quadruple-sourced in practice.** `/honest-assessment` carries the section labelled "canonical reference," dated 2026-07-08, defining nine badges in two families (MRH-relationship: Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative; descriptive: Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed) plus two deprecated. Good — that's exactly right, and `/key-claims` correctly links to it with a deep anchor rather than restating it. But `/research-philosophy` reproduces the *entire* nine-badge taxonomy inline while simultaneously declaring that the canonical version is elsewhere. `/test-catalog` has its own "Validation Badge Guide" legend. `/tier-1-existing` has its own "Status badge definitions" section — listing **eight** categories, not nine. Four copies of a controlled vocabulary, with the copies already disagreeing on cardinality. This is the failure mode where a term gets revised in one place and silently forks everywhere else. There should be one definitions page and three links to it.

**Then I found the bigger version of that problem.** The site tells readers that every claim carries a badge from the nine-term taxonomy. The largest, most heavily-referenced table on the site — the twelve tests on `/tier-1-existing` — mostly does not use it. Its status column reads:

> Untested · Self-Eliminating-or-Tie · Never Run As Registered · Withdrawn · Disfavored on σ₈, Underpowered on Registered fσ₈ · Re-Adjudicated · Underpowered As Registered · Demoted to Tier-2 · Reparametrization · Failed, Kill Criterion Fired · Failed, Ceiling Exceeded · Failed, Empty Intersection

Two of those twelve ("Untested", "Reparametrization") come from the defined vocabulary. One ("Withdrawn") is in the glossary but not in either badge family. The remaining nine are free text — and one of them is a 47-character sentence fragment. I understand the impulse: each verdict is genuinely different and the author wanted precision. But this is a status column, and a status column with twelve distinct values across twelve rows conveys no status at all — it's a notes field wearing a badge's clothes. The honest fix is two columns: a badge from the closed set, plus a "why" field carrying the specificity. As it stands the site documents a taxonomy it doesn't follow, which is worse than having no taxonomy.

**Terminology consistency on the core quantity is the other systemic issue.** ρ is called four different things depending on where you land:

| Page | What ρ is called |
|---|---|
| `/` (landing) | "presence" |
| `/coherence-function` | "presence" — "physical density is one form of presence" |
| `/first-encounter` | "density — how much stuff is packed into a given space" |
| `/key-claims` | "density" |
| `/glossary` | "Presence (ρ)" |
| `/coherence-explorer` | "density" |

There *is* a defensible model in here — presence is the general concept, density is the physical instance — and `/coherence-function` states it in one clause. But that clause is on a page with no nav entry, and the beginner path teaches "density" first, so most readers meet the specific case and then get hit with the general term on the landing page they already read. Pick the canonical noun, use it in every heading and every axis label, and gloss the other one on first use everywhere.

**ρ_crit is worse, because the notation contradicts the prose.** The subscript says "crit," which every technically literate reader parses as *critical* — a critical point, a phase transition, a threshold. The glossary entry is titled "ρ_crit Reference Density." `/coherence-function` calls it "the saturation knee" and then explicitly warns: *"a reference density — not a critical point."* So the site is spending prose to talk readers out of the reading its own symbol invites, on multiple pages. That's a symptom of a naming decision that should have been changed rather than annotated. `ρ_ref` or `ρ₀` would delete this entire class of correction. I recognize renaming a symbol that appears in the headline equation is expensive — but the current cost is a recurring caveat on every page that uses it, forever.

**The glossary is genuinely good and undersold.** 72 terms, alphabetized with Greek sorted after Latin and badge terms clustered — the sort order is documented in the intro, which almost nobody bothers to do. Nearly every acronym I'd flagged elsewhere is in there: SPARC, RAR, BTFR, CHSH, DESI, ΛCDM, BAO, fσ₈, A2ACW, even `ln` and `tanh`. Three gaps matter:

- **"prospective prediction"** has no entry — and the site's single most-repeated sentence is *"0 prospective predictions confirmed."* The word doing the load-bearing work in the headline number is the one word not defined.
- **"degrees of freedom"** is used in the formal MRH definition on `/first-encounter`, a beginner-path page, and isn't glossed.
- **"intent"** appears inside another entry with no entry of its own.

Also: 72 entries and no filter box. And there's no inline glossary affordance in body text — the glossary intro says *"hover over highlighted terms on any page,"* but I couldn't confirm any such highlighting exists in the page content, and `/first-encounter` links out to the glossary only in a "see the Glossary for the full definition" aside, not from the terms themselves.

**`/for-researchers` is the discoverability failure I'd escalate first.** It's the sharpest page on the site — it opens by conceding the scoreboard ("0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, 0 tests that could select it over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM") and then lists four things that survive anyway. It is precisely what its named audience needs, and it is in **neither the top nav nor the footer.** The only route I found was a body-text link in the landing page's audience router. Its title is also unstable: breadcrumb says "For Researchers: What's Citable," inbound links say "For Researchers." Same page, two names.

**Learning paths are well-constructed but leak counts into labels.** Eight paths (3 by difficulty, 5 by topic), each with step counts and time estimates — good. But: "Test Roadmap (24 experiments)" bakes a number into a link label, which will drift the moment a 25th test is added; "Superconductivity (η)" in the Advanced path is plain "Superconductivity" in the Chemistry path; and because pages appear in multiple paths, `/honest-assessment` renders step indicators for *two* paths at once (Beginner step 5, Methodology step 6). `/first-encounter` shows the collision most clearly — it displays "Beginner Path — Step 2 of 6", "7 short pages — ~90 seconds each", and "Section 1 of 7" on one screen. Three progress systems, no hierarchy between them.

**Test counts don't reconcile.** `/test-catalog` headlines "24 Proposed" but only TEST-01 through TEST-11 have IDs — the other thirteen exist as tier-level groupings with no identifiers, so they can't be cross-referenced or cited. Meanwhile `/tier-1-existing` lists twelve rows (TEST-01–11 plus TEST-04a), headlines "10 Untested Tests, $0 Cost", badges exactly one row "Untested", and then states "effective independent tests after failures and withdrawals: approximately 4." Four different numbers describing one table, on one page. I can guess the intended meanings ("10 that can be run on existing data at no cost" vs "1 with the Untested badge"), but a reader shouldn't have to guess, and "Untested" meaning two different things in the headline and the badge column is exactly the collision the controlled vocabulary was supposed to prevent.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — *worked* — *well-documented, structurally excellent* — This is the best-documented artifact on the site and the pattern the others should follow. Breadcrumb present. It defines both of its terms before using them ("γ — the steepness of the coherence S-curve"; "N_corr — the count of particles that move as a correlated unit"), carries an unambiguous per-artifact badge (**"Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems"**), and — this is the part I'd hold up as a model — it tells the reader what conclusion to draw: *"not a physical estimate, but a live demonstration of the formula's central defect,"* with an explicit instruction to watch the most-correlated systems produce the flattest curves, "which is backwards." Seven named presets (ideal gas → BCS superconductor) mean a reader who doesn't know what N_corr values are plausible can still drive it. From a docs standpoint: definition before use ✓, status declared ✓, takeaway stated ✓, worked examples ✓. My only complaint is the default of N_corr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, which is a suspiciously round landing spot presented without a note on why 4 is the default.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which page is canonical for badge definitions? `/honest-assessment` claims it, `/research-philosophy` points there while restating the whole taxonomy, and `/test-catalog`'s legend link is described as pointing at the research-philosophy section. Three pages, circular pointers.

### Verdict

The writing itself is better than most technical documentation I audit: the prose is direct, the audience router on the landing page is a genuinely good structural decision, breadcrumbs are correct on all fourteen pages I checked, and the γ Calculator is a model of definition-before-use with an explicit takeaway. The site clearly *knows* what good documentation practice looks like.

The problem is single-sourcing. A controlled vocabulary defined in four places will fork, and it already has — nine badges in one copy, eight in another, and the site's flagship test table using ten labels from neither. The same pattern shows up in the ρ/presence drift, the four irreconcilable counts on `/tier-1-existing`, and the three progress indicators stacked on `/first-encounter`. None of these are writing problems; they're all one maintenance problem wearing different costumes.

Two things I'd fix before anything else: make the badge taxonomy live in exactly one place and make `/tier-1-existing` conform to it, and put "Core Theory" and `/for-researchers` in the global nav. The first stops the vocabulary from drifting further; the second makes the two most important destinations on the site reachable without a body-text link.

**Second: the structural concession is the right one.** `/coherence-function` states that Ising mean-field gives m = tanh(βJzm) — *m on both sides* — while C(ρ) evaluates directly with no self-consistency loop. That is the correct and fatal distinction, and I did not expect to find it stated. In Landau/mean-field theory the tanh isn't the model, it's the *solution* of a self-consistency condition derived from minimizing a free energy; the transition emerges from where that condition acquires new roots. Here tanh is imposed as a shape on an explicit function of a control parameter. There is no order parameter, no free energy, no fixed point — and consequently the site's own verdict that critical exponents are a **category error** is exactly right. C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit+1)) is real-analytic on ρ > −ρ_crit; there is no singularity anywhere to host a critical exponent. Combined with "no field equation anywhere in this framework's galaxy sector — no action, no Lagrangian, no covariant formulation," the honest description of this object isn't "a theory that failed six tests." It's **a two-parameter fitting function whose functional family was tested.** That's a different kind of thing, and the site would lose nothing by saying so.

**Third — and this is my main finding — the site prints the identity and never takes the last step.** `/equation-walkthrough` Step 2 states, correctly:

> tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) is exactly [(1+x)^(2γ)−1]/[(1+x)^(2γ)+1]

Set γ = 1/2. Then 2γ = 1 and the whole thing collapses:

**C = [(1+x)−1]/[(1+x)+1] = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2)**

where μ_simple(u) = u/(1+u) is MOND's simple interpolating function. Not asymptotically. Not approximately. **Identically, for all x.** γ = 1/2 is the exact algebraic MOND point of this function family.

Now: `/tier-1-existing` reports the free-γ SPARC fit as **γ ≈ 0.489** (TEST-11), and `/honest-assessment` reports "free-γ converges to MOND" as an empirical outcome with ΔBIC = +184 against γ=2. The data pulls γ to within **2.2%** of the unique value where the framework's coherence function *is* MOND's μ function. The site owns the identity and owns the fitted value and never multiplies them.

This matters because it upgrades the verdict. "Free-γ converges to MOND" is a statistical statement about model comparison. "γ→1/2 makes C algebraically identical to μ_simple" is an identity, and it explains *why* the fit converges rather than reporting that it did. It is the sharpest possible form of the **Reparametrization** badge, it requires no new data, and it's two lines of algebra.

It gets tighter. `/tier-1-existing` defines f_DM = 1 − C, so C ≡ M_b/M_dyn ≡ 1/ν ≡ μ. **C is the interpolating function.** The framework's entire galaxy sector is therefore: *MOND, with μ's argument replaced by local density ρ/2ρ_crit instead of the enclosed-mass acceleration g_bar.* That is precisely the local-density-vs-non-local-acceleration no-go the site identifies as its structural failure — which means the no-go isn't one failure among six. **It is the whole of the difference.** Everything else is shared. `/for-researchers` lists that no-go as citable contribution #1; this framing is what makes it citable, and it's stronger than what's currently written there.

One corollary the site should also draw: since C = μ has no lower bound (C→0 as ρ→0, so the boost 1/C diverges), the "structural ceiling" B_max = 1/Ω_m is not a property of the function at all — it's an external floor asserted on C. The badge **"Speculative — Asserted, Not Derived"** is correct, but the reason it's asserted is visible from the algebra: nothing in the compander produces a ceiling.

**Fourth: identifiability.** Expanding for ρ ≪ ρ_crit (which is where galaxy outskirts live — the plotter reports max C ≈ 0.001 on a real disk):

C ≈ γx/(1+γx), x = ρ/ρ_crit

This depends on γ and ρ_crit **only through the ratio γ/ρ_crit.** The two parameters are not separately identifiable in the regime that carries all the signal. The degeneracy first breaks at O(x²) with coefficient γ(2γ−1) — which *vanishes at γ = 1/2*, i.e. essentially at the fitted value. So near γ≈0.489 the degeneracy is second-order-exact. A quoted "γ = 0.489 preferred by SPARC" is therefore not a measurement of γ unless the ρ_crit prior is stated alongside it, and I couldn't find it stated anywhere. ### Unanswered Questions

6. Is there a stated reason ρ has no units and no measurement protocol, or is that a gap? The site says "doubly unanchored" and I agree, but a compander needs *some* dimensional statement for ρ/ρ_crit to be well-posed even as a fit.

#### Finding 1 — The site's only remaining novel prediction rests on an object the site elsewhere says does not exist

and adds "no momentum conservation, no lensing prediction," and "The framework is a static map plus an interpretation." These cannot both be true. There is no nonlinear Poisson equation in this framework; MOND has one (Bekenstein–Milgrom 1984), and the EFE is *its* property, arising because μ takes the total acceleration |g_int + g_ext| as its argument. Ambient medium density adds to local ρ, raising C and suppressing the boost. #### Finding 4 — The A2ACW "program-level null" is underpowered, and the site never says so

### Unanswered Questions

1. By what mechanism does a function of local matter density respond to an external gravitational field at all? If the answer is "it doesn't," the framework predicts EFE = 0 exactly — which is a stronger and more discriminating statement than 0.3–0.4× MOND, and puts it in immediate tension with Chae et al. 2020.
2. What is the intrinsic scatter of ρ at fixed g_bar across SPARC? That single number is either the sharpest available refutation of algebraic local-density coupling or the reason the no-go is weaker than advertised. Nobody appears to have measured it.
3. Does BCM 2017's symmetron reproduce the RAR's *scatter* with universal Lagrangian parameters, or only its mean? The site withdrew a generality claim on the strength of a paper whose scatter performance it hasn't checked.
4. What radius do the tidal-dwarf σ intervals assume, and how is MOND's 40.9 km/s upper bound obtained for a 10⁷ M☉ system when the isolated deep-MOND value is 9.4 km/s?
5. Why is the program-level A2ACW null declared on n = 6 when every physics claim on the site carries a registered threshold and a σ? What would the audit sample size need to be, and why isn't finishing the existing 41-item backlog the top-priority action?
6. Is there any observable in this framework that depends on a variable MOND doesn't use, other than ρ itself? If ρ-vs-g_bar is the whole difference internally *and* environmentally, then the framework has exactly one degree of freedom relative to MOND and every test is a test of that one substitution — which would be worth saying as the site's organizing claim.
7. Does the framework have anything to say about the CMB, or is "no relativistic completion, therefore no prediction" the complete answer? Either way it should be written down, because a reader currently has to notice the absence.

### Verdict

There is nothing new here as physics, and the site is right about that — the reparametrization ledger is correctly attributed, the six refutations are real, and a₀ = cH₀/2π was Milgrom's remark in 1983. What I did not expect is that the site's errors now run predominantly in the *self-punishing* direction: 8σ quoted against a stat-only error bar when the systematic makes it 0.7σ, a MOND opponent credited with a fitted 3.81 rather than its actual exact prediction of 4, and a program-level methodology null declared on six audits that exclude nothing below a 39% survival rate. Over-refutation is harder to catch than over-claiming because no one complains about it, and it costs the same credibility with a referee.

The one thing that is genuinely underexploited is the environmental axis. The framework's boost is suppressed by ambient *density*, not by external *acceleration* — a different variable with a different scaling — and the site converted that into a scalar ratio against a field equation it elsewhere says it doesn't have. Written correctly, it is a discriminating prediction (EFE = 0 structurally), it is testable at zero cost against Chae et al. 2020, and it is the only candidate I found for the site's missing TEST-12.

My recommendation to whoever maintains this: stop running tests and run two computations. The scatter of ρ at fixed g_bar across SPARC, and the environmental refit keyed on ρ_ambient. Both use data already on disk, both are afternoons, and either one produces a citable negative sharper than anything in the current Tier-1 table. The framework is finished; the *no-go* is not, and the no-go is the part worth publishing.
