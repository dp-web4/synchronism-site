### The one-paragraph version

The site's equation writes the density ratio to the first power. That single index — call it
`p` in `C_p = tanh(γ·ln(1 + x^p))` — is what makes the galaxy sector reduce to MOND, and
nothing in the framework derives it. `C → γ·x^p` exactly as `x → 0`, so `p = 1` ⇔ flat
rotation curves ⇔ BTFR slope 4 ⇔ MOND's deep limit ⇔ Milgrom's (2009) spacetime scale
invariance. Freeing it turns the model from a **submodel** of MOND (via the bounded boost,
a restriction) into a **supermodel** (via the index, an extension) — and a supermodel is the
one object that *can* be selected over its nested rival. So the site's a-priori argument that
the galaxy sector "can only tie or lose" rests on a premise — *"the bounded boost is the
framework's only structural difference from MOND"* — that is **false as stated**. I then
measured `p`, and it came back consistent with 1.

### Where I was wrong

I registered a prediction in the WAKE and in both script headers — *"ϒ rescaling translates
the RAR horizontally and cannot change a slope, so `p̂` should be ϒ-invariant where γ̂ was
destroyed."* **It is wrong as stated.** ϒ scales only the stellar component, so it changes the
star/gas mix and hence the *shape* of g_bar; and it re-selects which windows pass the cuts.
`p̂` spans 20–23 % of its mean across ϒ ∈ [0.3, 0.8] on both routes. What survives is the
comparative form — `p` moves ≈1.2 bootstrap σ where γ runs to a bound — and `p̂ < 1` at every
ϒ on both routes, so the direction is ϒ-robust even though the value is not. Recorded here
because the prediction failed in the direction that makes my own result *weaker*.

### The estimator trap I nearly walked into

The kinematic route (`s_V = d lnV/d lnr`, no ϒ in the statistic) first returned **5.08σ**
against `s_V = 0`. It is an artifact of inverse-variance weighting with formal errors: **one
galaxy carries 30 % of the total weight, three carry 57 %.** Across five estimators the
exclusion of `p = 1` ranges from **0.80σ (median — does not exclude) to 5.13σ**, while the
central value stays `p ∈ [0.65, 0.91]` throughout. Fourth instance of the unnamed-estimator
failure mode in this program. Direction robust; significance not.

### Two robustness checks that could have dissolved the result, and did not

- **Is `p̂` just a property of `tanh`?** The 05-02 sweep moved the consciousness threshold a
  lot by swapping the outer sigmoid. Here, re-fitting with three qualitatively different
  saturations (tanh; `u/√(1+u²)`, a power-law approach; `1 − e^{−u}`) moves `p̂` by
  **0.091 = 0.62 bootstrap σ** — while γ̂ moves by a factor of 36 and â₀ by 213. The argument
  exponent is separately identifiable from the outer-sigmoid choice; the two degrees of
  freedom do not collide. (The three −lnL agree to 1.3, so the data still cannot distinguish
  the outer sigmoid at all — consistent with 07-22.)
- **Is there a universal `p` at all?** I set this up as the outcome to beat, because Persic,
  Salucci & Stel (1996) report a *tight* outer-slope–luminosity relation and the kinematic
  route's own universality χ² was 613.1/41 dof = **14.95**. The family-fit route does not
  support it: per-galaxy `p` (125 galaxies, γ and a₀ frozen) has median 0.743, IQR
  [0.591, 0.916], and correlations with acceleration regime and mass proxy of **−0.064** and
  **+0.103** — no trend. So the 14.95 is most plausibly underestimated correlated errors
  (adjacent rotation-curve points inside a beam are not independent) rather than real
  variation. It would have been easy to quote the 14.95 and stop.

### Prior art, checked before claiming anything (five sources)

- **Milgrom 2009, ApJ 698, 1630** — the deep-MOND limit *follows from* spacetime scale
  invariance under `(t,r) → (λt,λr)`. So `p = 1` is a **theorem** in MOND and a **convention**
  in this framework. That is the sharpest available statement of the difference, and it is the
  credit line any write-up must carry.
- **Desmond, Bartlett & Ferreira 2023, MNRAS 521, 1817** — exhaustive symbolic regression on
  this same SPARC data: *"the deep-MOND limit as g_bar → 0 is little evident at all"*, and
  `s_deep = 1/2` is not recovered even from MOND mocks. This set the bar my power gate had to
  clear, and explains *why* it clears: family rigidity, not dynamic range. Only 0.4 % of SPARC
  points sit below a₀/100.
- **arXiv:2006.06700** — weak lensing + dSph + outer-MW, *independent of SPARC*: *"the data
  weakly favour a break to a steeper low-acceleration slope"* — i.e. `p < 1`, same direction as
  every estimator here. Weak, and the only non-SPARC handle.
- **arXiv:2608.08945 (Aug 2026)** — identifiability audit of one-parameter structural
  corrections to the RAR in SPARC: not uniquely recoverable in the full sample; **0.106 dex
  per-galaxy nuisance floor.** My M1-vs-M2 separation is 0.038 dex, *under* that floor. An
  independent, three-week-old confirmation of my null by a different route.
- **Persic, Salucci & Stel 1996** — the outer RC slope tracks luminosity ("a tight outer
  slope–luminosity relationship"). This is the honest ceiling on the kinematic route; I measure
  corr(`s_V`, log L₃.₆) = −0.291 (perm p = 0.067) — right direction, weak.

**No source found that fits a deep-limit exponent as a free parameter to SPARC inside a rigid
