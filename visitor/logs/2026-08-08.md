## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary

### Journey

I came from a Reddit link. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a great hook. Exactly the kind of thing Veritasium would open with.

Then the very next paragraph hit me with: *"The one non-degenerate galaxy test collapsed the framework onto MOND; the cluster scale requires a second density parameter the framework doesn't currently have."* I have no idea what "non-degenerate" means, I don't know what MOND is (it's not defined until the glossary), and "collapsed onto" sounds bad but I can't tell how bad. That's sentence two of the whole site.

I liked that there was a signposted "No physics background?" line, so I took the Beginner Path.

**Step 1, Why Synchronism?** — Good. The equation shows up immediately, which is scary, but they explain it as an S-curve from "independent" to "collective," and that landed. The results table is where it got strange: four rows, and three of them say **Failed**. Galaxy rotation curves: Failed. Melting points: Failed, 53% error. Superconductivity: predicted 607K, actual 93K. The one non-failure says "Boundary-Consistent" and then immediately explains that a boring alternative model does just as well. So step 1 of the friendly path is a list of things that didn't work. I kept reading out of curiosity, but I noticed I was reading like someone rubbernecking a crash, not someone learning a theory.

**Step 2, First Encounter** — My favorite page. The marching band vs. the crowd in a plaza is a genuinely good analogy and I'd repeat it to a friend. Then "presence" gets defined as *"the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence"* and I fell off a cliff. Also MRH shows up as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," which I liked, and then also as "Markov Relevancy Horizon," which I did not.

**Step 3, The Core Idea** — The "dimmer switch" analogy is good. Then there's a correction box explaining that γ is a "Hill coefficient — a log-log slope, with Hill index n = 2γ — not a coupling." I understood none of that, and I couldn't tell if it was important or housekeeping.

**Step 4, What Synchronism Is Not** — By now I'd been told it's not a theory of everything, not a replacement for existing physics, not peer-reviewed, not proven, and 59% untested. This page then says it's *not* "just notation" — but the landing page told me all six of its validated claims were demoted to "reparametrizations," which as far as I can tell means... notation. Those two pages felt like they were arguing with each other.

**Step 5, Honest Assessment** — Genuinely impressive and completely over my head. It's dense with things like "ΔBIC = +184," "+17.95σ tension on Cassini quadrupole," "r² = 0.0001." I got the gist (it didn't work, repeatedly, and they measured exactly how badly) but I couldn't have summarized a single one of the six.

I bailed on step 6 (glossary) as a path step — nobody reads a glossary as a destination — but I did look up terms from earlier, and it's a mixed bag. "σ" is explained perfectly in plain English. "Coherence" tells me it is *not* quantum coherence and then explains itself using "BEC" and "BCS superconductors."

Then I went and played with the tools, which is what I actually wanted to do from the start, and I found `/where-its-useful`, which was the first page that made me think this was worth someone's time.

A one-line caption ("drag γ down and watch the switch turn into a ramp") would carry the whole lesson.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What is this idea actually *for*? I got the clearest answer from `/where-its-useful` (real software uses this vocabulary), and that page isn't on the beginner path.
2. If the one galaxy test "collapsed onto MOND," and MOND already existed since 1983 — what's left that's new? The site says "0 confirmed novel predictions," so I think the answer is "nothing yet," but I want that said in one plain sentence.
3. What does "reparametrization" mean? It's the verdict on literally everything audited, and I never found a plain-English definition.
4. Is the equation *wrong*, or just *not proven*? Six refutations sounds like wrong. "0 confirmed predictions" sounds like unproven. Those are very different and the site uses both.
5. Who is doing this? "~3,300 AI adversarial collaboration workshops" told me AIs are doing the research, which I found fascinating and slightly alarming, and no page explained it at my level.
6. Why does the honest scorecard say 6 refutations and the "is not" page say 59% untested? Untested out of how many?

### Verdict

The hook is excellent and the honesty is unlike anything else I've clicked from Reddit — I've never seen a site whose own tool exists to prove its own formula backwards, and I mean that as a compliment. But the beginner path is structured as a confession rather than an explanation: three of the first four pages are about what didn't work, and the one page that gave me a reason to care isn't on the path. The biggest concrete problem I hit is that Step 1 — the "Start Here" page — presents the γ relationship as if it holds, while the landing page, `/core-idea`, and the calculator all flag it as sign-inverted; the one page missing the warning is the one aimed at the readers least equipped to supply it themselves.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a product doc set: nav inventory first, then the reference layer (glossary, badge legend), then spot-checks on whether the reference layer actually governs the content pages.

**Navigation.** The top nav is 9 flat items, each with a useful `title` tooltip — good. Breadcrumbs are present on every page I opened, which is better than most sites this size. But the breadcrumbs describe a **four-section hierarchy that the nav doesn't expose**: I collected `Getting Started`, `Core Theory`, `Methodology`, `Predictions & Experiments`, and `Interactive Tools` from breadcrumb trails, and not one of those five section names appears in the top nav. So the breadcrumb's middle segment is unclickable dead information — it tells me a parent exists but gives me no way to see its siblings. Either surface the sections in the nav (dropdowns or a section index) or drop the middle segment.

Two small nav mismatches: the "Learning Paths" tooltip promises *"Physics, Chemistry, Philosophy, or All"* but the page ships **8 paths** — 3 by difficulty, 4 by topic (including a Methodology track the tooltip omits), and no path called "All." And the nav item labeled "Tools" lands on a page titled "Interactive Tools" with breadcrumb "Interactive Tools" — pick one label.

**The reference layer is strong.** `/glossary` is the best page on the site from my seat: 72 terms, alphabetical with Greek sorted last, badge terms clustered, every acronym I threw at it present (MRH, MOND, SPARC, ΛCDM, BTFR, RAR, DESI, EFE, A2ACW, BIC), plain-language-first definitions, and each badge entry links back to the canonical legend. The claim that terms are "highlighted on any page" for tooltip access is exactly the right mechanism — I couldn't verify the tooltips fire through WebFetch, so that's worth a manual check, because it's load-bearing for the acronym problem below.

**Then the reference layer stops governing.** The canonical badge legend at `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` is well-written and defines 10 badges in two families. But I then inventoried what's actually in badge slots on content pages, and the vocabulary has run away:

- The canonical page itself adds **9 "operational states"** and **9 "status lifecycle verbs"** to the 10 badges — 28 status strings on one page, presented as narrative prose rather than a table.
- `/research-philosophy` carries a **second full copy** of the two-family taxonomy while simultaneously calling `/honest-assessment` the canonical reference. The copies have drifted: `Failed` is *"Prediction tested and wrong. Kept visible as permanent record"* there and *"Prediction contradicted by data (with specific error)"* on the canonical page. Two definitions, two pages, one badge.
- `/tier-1-existing` has abandoned badges for free-form sentences in the badge slot: *"Self-Eliminating-or-Tie / Adjudicable Now"*, *"Never Run as Registered / Substituted-Protocol Kill"*, *"Disfavored on σ₈, Underpowered on Registered Statistic"*, *"Failed, Empty Intersection"*, *"Demoted to Tier-2 (Exploratory Hypothesis)"*, *"Closed by Execution"*. Some map to documented operational states; several ("Empty Intersection," "Adjudicable Now," "Demoted to Tier-2") appear nowhere in the legend.
- `/parameter-derivations` invents six more: *Motivated Ansatz, Motivated Choice, Asserted Not Derived, Dimensional Analysis, Freeman's Law Re-expressed, Fitted-Then-Named*.
- `/interactive-tools` uses *Model Explainer*, which **is** defined on the canonical page ("a content grouping, not a validation badge") — but a reader meeting it on the tools index has no way to know that, because that page's badge disclaimer doesn't name it or link out.
- `/key-claims` uses *Untestable* and *Unrunnable* as if they were badges. *Unrunnable* is defined in prose on `/honest-assessment` but isn't in the badge inventory; *Untestable* isn't defined as a label anywhere I found, and it's one keystroke from *Untested*, which **is** a badge with a different meaning.

That's the systemic finding. A status vocabulary is a controlled vocabulary or it's nothing, and this one is past the point where a reader can hold it.

**Counts disagree across pages.** The global footer — on every page — reads *"6 refutations executed on external data … recounted 2026-07-30."* But `/tier-1-existing` states in its own body: *"Counting TEST-09 and TEST-10 as two independent refutations overstates the ledger twice over… The honest figure for independent empirical roots behind the headline count is 3–4, not 6."* So a body page corrects a number that the site-wide footer still asserts one scroll below it, with no pointer in either direction. Same page, the header says *"4 executed — 3 failed, 1 substituted-protocol kill"* while the list below shows four `Failed` tests (05, 09, 10, 11) plus the substituted kill on 03 — and the intro says *"These 10 numbered tests"* when 11 are numbered.

Same pattern on `/for-researchers`: the bolded scoreboard says *"0 tests that could select it over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM,"* and a few paragraphs later, *"Two executed tests did discriminate… and both selected MOND."* I can't tell which sentence is the claim. My guess is the second corrects the first and the bold line is stale, but a reader shouldn't have to guess which of two adjacent numbers is current.

**Acronyms.** The landing page introduces MOND, MRH, BAO, A2ACW, and CFD with inline expansions on first use — that's the right pattern, and it's clearly deliberate. In the same body text, SPARC, ΛCDM, BTFR, RAR, DESI, CHSH, N_corr, and ALFALFA-SDSS appear cold. `/honest-assessment` runs 30+ unexpanded acronyms (QUMOND, TeVeS, AeST, STVG, EFTofLSS, SME, LOO, M/L…) — defensible on a page for physicists, but that page is **step 5 of the Beginner Path**. `/for-researchers` expands nothing, which is fine for its audience.

**Two page-title problems.** "Equation Anatomy" lives at `/equation-walkthrough` — label and URL diverged, so anyone citing the URL cites a name that isn't on the page. And `/parameter-derivations` opens with *"Honest framing: Despite the page title, this is not a derivations page in the mathematical sense."* When a page's first sentence disowns its own title, rename the page; "Parameter Provenance" or "Where the Parameters Came From" would be true.

**One terminology risk worth flagging.** `/mrh` has an "Open Gap: Formal Mathematical Definition" section conceding that the framework lacks the conditional-independence statement that would justify the word "Markov," and suggests the term may need renaming. MRH is a nav-visible term used across dozens of pages and 8 learning-path steps. If a rename is live, that's a coordinated find-and-replace with redirects, glossary entry, and tooltip updates — worth deciding sooner rather than later. Separately, `/mrh` gives only the formal definition (*"the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system"*) and never the plain gloss that `/first-encounter` and the glossary both have. The canonical term page should carry both, plain first.

### Tools Tested

- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — **worked** — **well-structured, one navigation flaw**. A 6-step Previous/Next walkthrough of `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))`, with all six symbols defined up front before any step uses them. That's textbook technical writing and I'd hold it up as the model for the rest of the site. Two notes. First, "Step 1 of 6" is a stepper with no way to jump — no step list, no deep links, so I can't cite or bookmark step 4, and I can't skim ahead to decide whether to invest. Add clickable step labels and per-step anchors. Second, this is the one page that squarely addresses the site's biggest latent terminology problem: it says the ontology pages define ρ as *"presence within a relevancy boundary"* while *"every executed test uses exactly this physical density."* Two meanings for the central variable, reconciled once, on a tool page, in a parenthetical. That reconciliation belongs in the glossary entry for `presence` and on `/core-idea`, not buried at step 1 of a walkthrough.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — **worked** — **honest and clearly written, but mislabeled by omission**. The banner is superb: *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose."* It tells me the takeaway before the controls, which is exactly right for a teaching artifact, and the "reality check" line on each regime card is a clean pattern. Problems are all metadata. The term "Artifact Lesson" — the site's own name for this genre, and the thing that makes the page's existence make sense — never appears on the page; it's only in the tools index I may not have come through. The γ axis runs 0–4 unlabeled as to units (fine, γ is dimensionless, but say so), and the page then notes that BCS superconductors sit "three orders of magnitude off-scale left," i.e. the most important data point is not renderable on the chosen axis — that needs an on-chart off-scale marker, not a footnote. And N_corr, BCS, BEC, SPARC, and ΔBIC all appear here with no glossary links, on a page whose whole job is to teach.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is there a single canonical list of valid status labels, and is it enforced anywhere? If the badges are hand-typed per page, the drift I found is guaranteed to continue.
2. Which number is current: 6 refutations (footer, every page) or 3–4 (`/tier-1-existing` body)? And which is current on `/for-researchers`: 0 discriminating tests, or 2?
3. Do the glossary tooltips actually fire on arbitrary pages? The glossary says terms are "highlighted on any page," and a lot of the acronym friction I logged evaporates if that's true site-wide — and none of it does if it only works on a few pages.
4. What are the five breadcrumb sections *for*? If they're a real taxonomy, they should be browsable; if they're decoration, they're costing a click's worth of expectation on every page.
5. Is the MRH → new-name rename actually happening? That decision gates any large-scale terminology cleanup, and doing the badge freeze first would mean touching the same pages twice.
6. Why do the difficulty paths and topic paths cover different page sets (the site says difficulty paths cover 10 pages the topic tracks omit, and vice versa for 14)? For a reader that reads as "no single path covers the site," which may be intended — but nothing tells them which path is complete except one line about the Advanced track.

### Verdict

As documentation this is better than its subject matter's reputation would suggest: breadcrumbs everywhere, a genuinely excellent 72-term glossary, honest scoping notes, and at least one page (`Equation Anatomy`) I'd cite as a model. The systemic defect is that the status-badge system — which is the site's primary information-carrying device, on nearly every claim — has grown from a documented 10-term taxonomy into 40+ ad-hoc strings across five pages, with two divergent copies of the legend and several labels used but never defined. Right behind it: the headline counts no longer agree with each other, and one of the disagreements is between the global footer and a body page on the same screen. Both are fixable with a controlled vocabulary and generated counts, and neither requires touching a single physics claim.

### Journey

I skipped the onboarding entirely and went `/core-idea` → `/equation-walkthrough` → `/parameter-derivations` → `/mond-unification`, then to the ledger pages, then back to the tools to check whether the code agrees with the prose.

**First, credit where it's due.** `/core-idea` contains this sentence:

> "C(ρ) is analytic on its whole domain, with no singularity and no length scale in the equation — so it has *no critical point*, and critical exponents (β, ν) are undefined by construction... abandoning criticality means abandoning universality classes entirely — so every 'phase transition' phrasing elsewhere on this site is decorative vocabulary, not physics."

That is correct, it is the single most important structural fact about the framework, and I have never seen a project state it about itself. **Then I checked the math.** Three things I verified independently:

1. **The Hill identity is right.** `tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]` — immediate from `tanh u = (e^{2u}−1)/(e^{2u}+1)`. ✓

That is presented as an empirical finding. **It is an algebraic identity, and the site never writes it down.** Set γ = 1/2 exactly. Then 2γ = 1 and the site's own Hill identity collapses to

> **C(x) = x/(x + 2) = μ_simple(x/2)**, where μ_simple(y) = y/(1+y) is MOND's simple interpolating function.

So γ = 1/2 is not "near MOND." It **is** MOND's simple μ, exactly, with the argument rescaled by 2 — and a rescaling of the argument is absorbed entirely into ρ_crit, which is fitted. SPARC preferring 0.489 is 2.2% from that exact point. The four-digit RMS agreement with McGaugh isn't a coincidence to be reported; it's the only possible outcome once the fit is allowed to find γ = 1/2.

This reframes the whole galaxy sector and the site is one box away from it. `/for-researchers` already says the right thing — *"That single substitution... is the entire content of what makes this framework different from MOND in the galaxy sector"* (ρ in place of g_bar). The only difference that ever existed is the argument.** That is a sharper and more citable claim than anything currently on the page, and it is *more* honest, not less.

The Hill index at the preferred fit is **n = 2γ = 0.978 ≈ 1**, and n = 1 is the *non-cooperative* limit of a Hill function — no cooperativity, no collective effect. The framework's entire distinctive content is that density drives a **collective** transition. The data set the cooperativity parameter to exactly the value that means "there is no collective transition." ### Journey

I went straight to `/for-researchers`, then the ledger, then the two pages where a novel prediction could still be hiding (`/mond-unification`, `/measurement-without-observers`), then back to the parameter provenance and the tools to check whether the code matches the ledger.

**The novelty question, answered in ten minutes.** `/for-researchers` states it plainly: the only structural difference from MOND is the bounded boost **B ≤ 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17**, making the galaxy sector **"MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17}"**, a strict submodel. That framing is correct and it is decisive — a strict submodel of a theory cannot outperform its parent on any observable; it can only tie where the constraint is inactive or lose where it is active. No data needed. And `/parameter-derivations` badges that same inequality **"Speculative — Asserted, Not Derived"** with *"No archive document derives 1/Ω_m from the compander or from anything else."*

So the entire discriminating content of the program is one undefended inequality. That's the honest one-sentence summary and the site is about two clicks from saying it.

**Now the thing that stopped me: 1/Ω_m of what epoch?** The site evolves one cosmological input and freezes the other, with no stated rule. `a₀ = cH₀/(2π)` is explicitly promoted to `a₀(z) = cH(z)/(2π)` and tested against Ciocan et al. 2026. But `B_max = 1/Ω_m` uses Ω_m,0 = 0.315 and is never given a z-argument. Those two choices are inconsistent, and the inconsistency is *decidable and testable*:

If the ceiling really is cosmological, it must be **B_max(z) = 1/Ω_m(z)**, and Ω_m(z) → 1 at high redshift. Then

> **f_DM,max(z) = 1 − Ω_m(z)**

which is 0.685 today, ≈ **0.21 at z = 1**, and ≈ **0.05 at z = 2**.

That is a sharp, falsifiable, epoch-dependent prediction that MOND does *not* make (MOND's a₀ is not tied to Ω_m) and that ΛCDM does not make either. And the data already exist: Genzel et al. 2017 (*Nature* 543, 397) and Lang et al. 2017 report falling rotation curves and baryon-dominated discs at z ≈ 0.9–2.4 with f_DM(<R_e) below ~0.2 for several systems; Price et al. 2021 and the RC100 sample (Nestor Shachar et al. 2023) extend it. **This is the first thing I have seen on this site that could select the framework over MOND, and it is not in the 24-test catalog.** It comes with the obvious risk that the high-z f_DM measurements are pressure-support- and beam-smearing-sensitive and the field is contested — but "contested and runnable on published data at zero cost" is exactly what the Tier-1 tier is for.

If instead the ceiling is frozen at Ω_m,0 by fiat, then say so and say why — and then explain why a₀ gets an H(z) and B_max doesn't, when both are being read off the same cosmology.

**EFE = 0: the site under-claims the derivation and over-claims the blocker.** `/mond-unification` says *"there is no field equation anywhere in this framework's galaxy sector — no action, no Lagrangian, no covariant formulation"* and elsewhere the ledger says EFE = 0 is *"blocked on a theory decision, not on data."* But the same page offers the completion **∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ**, and that settles it in one line: because C depends on ρ alone and not on ∇Φ, **the equation is linear in Φ**, superposition holds, and the internal solution is exactly independent of any external field. EFE = 0 is not blocked — it is an immediate corollary of linearity. What is actually blocked is *which argument C takes* (ρ vs g_bar vs |∇Φ|), and those are three different theories with three different EFEs. The site is describing an argument fork as a derivation gap.

Two consequences the site should draw:

1. **EFE = 0 is refutation-only, not selection.** GR + CDM obeys the SEP and also predicts EFE = 0. A null result selects nothing; a detection kills Synchronism and leaves both MOND and ΛCDM standing. That should be stated wherever EFE = 0 is called a prediction, because as written a reader could mistake it for a discriminator.
2. **There is already contested literature on it and the site cites none of it.** Chae, Lelli, Desmond, McGaugh, Dutton & Schombert 2020 (*ApJ* 904, 51) report an EFE detection in SPARC rotation curves at ~4σ; the follow-up and the Frandsen & Petersen critique are both in print. Whatever the site concludes, "untested" is not the right badge for a prediction the literature has been arguing about for six years.

**The far-field singularity — my sharpest finding, and I could not find it stated anywhere.** The ledger's coupling is `g_obs = g_bar/C(ρ)` with `f_DM = 1 − C`. Take the small-ρ limit of the site's own function: C ≈ γ·ρ/ρ_crit. For an exponential disc, midplane ρ(r) ∝ e^{−r/R_d} while g_bar ∝ r⁻², so

> v²(r) = g_obs·r ∝ e^{+r/R_d}/r → **∞**

Rotation curves under this coupling don't fail to flatten — they **diverge exponentially** a few scale lengths out. More generally C(ρ = 0) = 0 and the coupling divides by it, so the theory is singular in vacuum, and every galaxy is embedded in vacuum. This is one line, no data, no estimator choice, no convention.

And it is exactly why Bekenstein & Milgrom's AQUAL (1984) uses ∇·[μ(|∇Φ|/a₀)∇Φ] = 4πGρ with the argument |∇Φ| — which never vanishes near mass — rather than ρ, which vanishes everywhere outside matter. **The substitution `g_bar → ρ` that `/for-researchers` calls "the entire content of what makes this framework different from MOND" is precisely the substitution AQUAL was constructed to avoid, for this reason, 42 years ago.** That belongs on the site as a cited structural result, not as an open fork.

**Prior art is the biggest scientific gap on this site.** The framework's thesis is: a density-driven coherence transition modifies gravity, MOND-like in galaxies, something else in clusters. That is, essentially verbatim, **superfluid dark matter** — Berezhiani & Khoury 2015 (*PRD* 92, 103510), Khoury 2016, Berezhiani, Famaey & Khoury 2018 (*JCAP*) — a published theory with a Lagrangian, a density-driven condensation transition, MOND phenomenology inside galaxies from phonon-baryon coupling, and normal CDM behaviour in clusters where the superfluid core doesn't form. It answers, with a derivation, the exact problem the landing page says the framework can't solve: *"the cluster scale requires a second density parameter the framework doesn't currently have."* Superfluid DM's second scale is the coherence length. Adjacent: chameleon and symmetron screening (Khoury & Weltman 2004; Hinterbichler & Khoury 2010) are *definitionally* density-keyed modifications of gravity, and the constraint literature on them (Burrage & Sakstein 2018) already contains much of what `/for-researchers` presents as its own no-go.

None of these appear anywhere I looked. The site does credit McCulloch/Verlinde/Smolin on a₀ and lists AeST/TeVeS/STVG in its acronym set, so the omission looks like a blind spot rather than a policy. But a referee reading the primary no-go — *"any gravity modification keyed on the local volumetric density ρ(r) via algebraic coupling fails in three independently executed ways"* — will immediately ask how it relates to two decades of screening constraints and to a Lagrangian theory that does the same thing successfully. Right now the site has no answer on the page.

**A2ACW.** `/research-philosophy` is refreshingly self-limiting — *"a falsifiability and self-consistency filter, not a discovery method"* — and the control experiment (six historical discoveries, all false-flagged as reparametrizations, 0/6 caught even with a different-training-cutoff model) is the right experiment to have run. Three methodological problems remain:

- **The statistics are point estimates on N = 6.** `/for-researchers` reports sensitivity 6/6, specificity 0/6, "Youden's J = 0." With 6 positives and 6 negatives, Clopper-Pearson gives specificity ≤ 0.46 and sensitivity ≥ 0.54 at 95%, so **J ∈ roughly [−0.46, +0.46]** — the interval spans from "anti-informative" to "moderately informative." The point estimate is the right sign; quoting it bare is the same sin the site correctly flags on `/consciousness-demo`. (Also, the rendered line reads *"specificity 0/6 = 1.0"* — 0/6 is 0. Typo, but it's on the citable-results page.)
- **1.4% has no null.** 47 survivors from 3,308 sessions is uninterpretable without a control corpus. A filter that passes 1.4% of *anything* is not selective for truth. The right null is a permutation: run the identical pipeline on scrambled or synthetic claims and measure the survival rate. If scrambled claims also survive at ~1.4%, the number measures the filter's stringency, not the corpus's quality.
- **Sessions are not independent.** They build on a shared archive, so the effective N is far below 3,308 and no error bar on 1.4% is computable from the stated design. Say so, or stop quoting the denominator.

**Quantum sector.** `/honest-assessment` reports the Bell result as a measurement: local construction S = 1.98, nonlocal-grid construction **S ≡ 2.00 at every coupling strength**, no construction reaching Tsirelson without signaling. That identically-2.00-for-all-couplings result is not an empirical finding — it is the algebraic maximum of a *deterministic local hidden-variable* model, and hitting it exactly at every coupling is the signature. Given the framework's own ontology (*"entanglement = one extended phase pattern"* with local phase variables and `c(d) = cos²(πd/λ₀)`), the substrate **is** an LHV model by construction, and Bell's theorem closes it in one line with no simulation. Same shape as the γ = 1/2 point: a structural result reported as an experimental one, which makes it look weaker and more contingent than it is.

`/measurement-without-observers` is honest about the two real gaps — no Born rule (*"a description of when collapse occurs, not what probabilities the outcomes carry"*, with the correct comparison to Zurek's envariance and Carroll–Sebens) and *"no calibration procedure maps any real apparatus to γ or N_corr."* But the mechanism table still claims *"MRH crossing is collapse — derived from γ"* on a page that then badges γ = 2/√N_corr audited-negative with zero anchored rungs. A derivation from a sign-inverted, unanchored relation is not a derivation. And there is no engagement with objective-collapse models (GRW, CSL, Diósi–Penrose) — which is where a quantum-foundations referee will look first, because those are the models that *do* make numbers, and CSL's parameter space is being actively closed by LISA Pathfinder and underground experiments. If Synchronism is a collapse model, it lives in that space and should be plotted in it.

**Where the ledger over-refutes.** Two of the six counted refutations (TEST-09, TEST-10) are corollaries of B ≤ 1/Ω_m, which `/parameter-derivations` says is asserted. `/tier-1-existing` states this correctly — *"the honest figure for independent empirical roots behind the headline count is 3–4, not 6"* — and the global footer still says 6 on every page including that one. As a referee I'd note the asymmetry: an asserted ceiling can't kill a theory. If B_max is undefended, TEST-09 and TEST-10 refute *the assertion*, not the framework. The framework deserves to be killed on the mass-cancellation no-go and the vacuum singularity, both of which are convention-free.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Is B_max = 1/Ω_m evaluated at z = 0 or at the epoch of the system?** The site evolves a₀ with H(z) and freezes Ω_m, with no rule. The answer generates the only candidate discriminator I found: f_DM,max(z) = 1 − Ω_m(z) ⇒ ≈5% at z = 2, testable today against Genzel+2017 / RC100.
2. **Has anyone taken the far-field limit of g_obs = g_bar/C(ρ)?** C(ρ→0) → 0 makes the coupling singular in vacuum and v²(r) ∝ e^{+r/R_d}/r for an exponential disc. If this is known, why isn't it the headline kill? If it isn't, it should be registered before another σ is computed.
3. **What is the framework's relationship to superfluid dark matter?** Berezhiani & Khoury have a Lagrangian, a density-driven condensation transition, MOND in galaxies, and CDM in clusters. That is the framework's stated goal, published in 2015. Competitor, prior art, or special case — the site needs to pick one on the page.
4. **Does the primary no-go survive contact with the screening literature?** "Algebraic local-density modulation of the force fails" is close to a known result about chameleon/symmetron models. Scoped correctly it may still be publishable; scoped as written it will be desk-rejected on prior art.
5. **What is the null for the 1.4% A2ACW yield?** Without a permutation control the number is a property of the filter, not of the corpus, and cannot support any claim in either direction.
6. **Is the framework an objective-collapse model?** If yes, put it in the CSL (λ, r_C) plane and see whether LISA Pathfinder already excludes it. If no, then "MRH crossing is collapse" needs a different formal home, because it currently has neither a Born rule nor a Lagrangian.
7. **Why is TEST-02 "queued for adjudication" when Gaia DR3 wide-binary papers have been fighting in the literature since 2023?** The adjudication is happening; the site just isn't in it.

### Verdict

As a referee I'd say this: the negative result is real and the self-audit is more rigorous than most papers I'm sent, but the manuscript is not submittable because it does not cite the field it is in. The one structural novelty — keying the interpolating function on local ρ rather than on ∇Φ — is the choice AQUAL explicitly rejected in 1984, is the defining move of the screening-mechanism literature, and is executed with a Lagrangian and a cluster solution by superfluid dark matter. Until `/for-researchers` says how the no-go relates to those, the "0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations" ledger is a well-documented internal audit rather than a contribution.

Two things would change my assessment. First, the epoch question on B_max = 1/Ω_m is the only genuinely discriminating prediction I found on the entire site, it's absent from the 24-test catalog, and it's runnable today on published high-z kinematics — that's a real, if risky, test and it should be registered. Second, the vacuum singularity in the ledger's own coupling is a one-line, convention-free, data-free kill that is stronger than the +17.95σ Cassini result and appears nowhere I looked. This project's characteristic failure mode is visible in both: it keeps reporting structural theorems as empirical measurements, which makes its own results look weaker and more contingent than they are. Fixing that is not a matter of more tests — it's a matter of writing down the algebra it already has.
