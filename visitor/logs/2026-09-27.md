## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /glossary, plus tools /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-curve-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A strangely likeable site that tells me up front its big idea didn't work. I understood that it failed, but I only half understood what the idea was.

### Journey
I came from Reddit expecting a crackpot "theory of everything." The landing page says right away "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" and "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." That surprised me and made me trust it more. Then the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` appears near the top of the landing page. I have no idea what tanh, γ or ln mean, and it made me flinch a little. The scorecard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations across 5 independent roots, 0 of 9 "validated" claims survived) is easy to read, but "independent roots" means nothing to me.

The landing page also says the galaxy test "collapsed it onto MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics)". That's the first time I've seen MOND, and nothing on the landing page tells me what it is. The whole story ("it turned out to be MOND") depends on knowing MOND, so it matters.

I took the Beginner Path (6 steps, ~45 min).

**Step 1, Why Synchronism:** The marching band vs. crowd picture and the "density is a dimmer switch" idea clicked right away. The equation is marked optional. Good.

**Step 2, First Encounter:** This was the best page for me. ρ, C, γ and tanh are each explained in plain words ("S-shaped curve, like a dimmer switch"). Two things lost me:
- One paragraph says "γ = 2/√N_corr … a crystal of 10²⁴ atoms gives γ ≈ 10⁻¹²". I don't know what 10⁻¹² means for the curve.
- One paragraph says a₀ "falls out of the same constants — but this is a dimensional coincidence… the 2π is unexplained". That is written for someone else. I skimmed it.

The in-page "Next: Section B →" buttons sit next to the "Next: The Core Idea →" link. For a second I didn't know which "Next" was the path.

**Step 3, Core Idea:** The "In plain words" box and "Beginner? You can stop here" were exactly what I needed. The takeaway "density barely mattered, and the version that fits turned out to be MOND's interpolating function in other clothes" is clear except for "interpolating function". The page below that is a wall of math, which I skipped as invited.

**Step 4, What Synchronism Is Not:** This is where the path got hard. The opening ("Overclaiming is dishonest. But so is underclaiming.") is nice. After that, the page has ΛCDM, Lorentz invariance "by 16 to 28 orders of magnitude", "absolute-time substrate" and "preferred frame leaks into the matter sector". Lorentz invariance does get a one-line definition, which helped. I had no idea whether "16–28 orders of magnitude" is a small or a fatal problem. It sounds fatal, but the page moves on.

The "Not: Just philosophy" section also confused me. It first says the site's own Consciousness Threshold page badges the threshold "untestable as stated". The very next paragraph says the consciousness equation is "specific and falsifiable — 34 EEG protocols are defined… That makes them science, not philosophy." So is it testable or not? Both claims are in the same section, and I came away not knowing which to believe.

**Honest Assessment:** The top line is crystal clear ("6 refutations executed, 0 predictions confirmed"). The page says it's ~18,000 words and offers "On the Beginner Path? You can stop here." I took that offer. The concrete failure examples (melting points off by 53% on average, about two-thirds of galaxies breaking a predicted cap) made "failed" feel real rather than abstract.

**Glossary:** 98 terms with an A–Z index. The MOND entry ("gravity behaves differently at very low accelerations") finally told me what MOND is. That definition should be on the landing page, not only here.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is MOND, in one sentence, and why is "it turned out to be MOND" a failure rather than a success? If it matches MOND and MOND works, isn't that good?
2. Is the consciousness part testable or not? The same section says both.
3. If the main equation failed, what is the site *for* now? "The autopsy is the product" sort of answers it, but I'd like a plain "here's why you should still care."
4. What are the "5 independent roots"?
5. How bad is "16–28 orders of magnitude"? Is that part of the idea already dead?

### Verdict
The honesty won me over. I've never seen a "theory of everything" site lead with "it didn't work," and the Beginner Path (the Step 1–3 pages especially) is well built for someone like me. The path breaks at Step 4, where the jargon spikes with no escape hatch. The one idea the whole story depends on (MOND) is never explained where I first meet it. I'd come back for the Galaxy Curve Plotter and the failure stories, not the math.

### Journey
I started at the landing page and checked acronyms against the raw page text, not a summary. Every acronym I tested is expanded at or before first use: MOND, ΛCDM, MRH, A2ACW, SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, CHSH, BAO, CFD. There is also a symbol legend (g_bar, a₀, Ω_m, fσ₈). That's a strong result. **Cross-pass note:** Pass 1 said MOND is never explained on the landing page. It is expanded at first use: "MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics — the 40-year-old rival gravity theory it keeps losing to)". That tells you *who* MOND is, not *what* it claims. Pass 1's real complaint is that the gloss says nothing about content ("gravity changes at very low accelerations"), not that a definition is missing.

**Badges.** This is the best-documented part of the site. The landing page has a "Badges used below" legend. It says a dash introduces a finding descriptor, not a new badge, and that "Withdrawn" is an operational state, not a badge. The canonical definitions sit near the top of Honest Assessment (~6% into a ~134k-character page) under `#validation-badge-definitions`. The Test Roadmap repeats a compact legend. The glossary says "the nine formal validation badges": 5 MRH-relationship + 4 descriptive = 9, which checks out. My one structural complaint: the landing legend lists only the descriptive family (Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed · Audited-Negative). Meanwhile the canonical reference calls the MRH-relationship family "preferred". A reader meeting "Active-MRH" or "Sidelined" elsewhere won't have seen it in the first legend.

**Navigation and breadcrumbs.** Breadcrumbs exist on every inner page I checked, and learning-path steppers ("Beginner Path · Step 5 of 6", with Prev/Next) work well. Honest Assessment shows *two* steppers stacked, Beginner step 5 and Methodology step 6. That's a nice touch, since a page on two paths says so. Problems:
- The middle breadcrumb crumb names categories that don't exist in the main nav. /mrh says "Home / Core Theory / MRH", and /core-theory returns 404. /test-catalog and /tier-1-existing say "Home / Predictions & Experiments / …", which also isn't a nav item. These middle crumbs are unlinked text, so they read as locations the reader can't visit.

- Key Claims isn't in the main nav at all (footer only), yet Honest Assessment's first sentence is "This page audits the claims listed in Key Claims."
- Label/URL/title drift: nav "Test Roadmap" → `/test-catalog`; nav "Tools" → page "Interactive Tools"; tool "Equation Anatomy" → `/equation-walkthrough`; "Crossover Regime Visualizer" → `/phase-boundary-visualizer`. The visualizer page admits this openly ("the URL keeps that name"), which is honest, but four label/URL mismatches in one nav is a pattern.

**Learning Paths.** The page is clear and self-correcting (it admits difficulty and topic paths don't cover the same pages). **The status layer: same fact, different hedge per page.** This is my main finding. The headline "6 refutations from 5 roots" is stated differently across pages:
- **Landing:** "6 … lost all six", "5 independent 'roots'". It notes TEST-09/10 are convention-dependent, but **does not say the count is pending**.
- **Honest Assessment box:** "no more than five are independent. The count itself is under rev[iew]".

- **Tier 1:** "about 4 of them are effectively independent" (of the tests on that page, a different denominator that sits right next to the "5 roots" line).

The same object also has four names: "the ceiling" (landing, first use, never introduced there), "the boost ceiling" (footer, Tier 1), "cap" (Honest Assessment: "the site's choice of cap"), and "B_max = 1/Ω_m" (Tier 1, first use, undefined). A reader can't tell these are one thing without already knowing.

**ρ: density or presence?** The landing page says "ρ is presence — the compatible structural elements…", while the glossary, Equation Anatomy and every test treat ρ as mass/energy density. Equation Anatomy handles this well: "The ontology pages read ρ more broadly, as 'presence'… that broader reading has never been operationalized". The landing page should carry that sentence too, and currently leads with the unoperationalized reading.

**MRH has two glosses.** Glossary and /mrh say "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter". Honest Assessment's badge section says "the framework's term for the bounded context something is currently relevant within". The glossary *does* warn that the badge family "borrows the name", which is good. But the canonical badge reference should use the same gloss, or say explicitly that it's the borrowed sense.

**Revision notes as a reading hazard.** Collapsible "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)" blocks appear on the landing page (8), Tier 1 (17), For Researchers (8) and Key Claims (3). The label is clear and honest, but on Tier 1 there are 17 of them. Several sit inside the opening badge region, where the reader needs the current state. The Tier 1 page badge is itself a ~170-word paragraph containing TEST IDs, B_max, Ω_m, Brouwer+2021 and a governance note. A badge that long isn't working as a badge.

**Voice drift on Key Claims.** Most of the site is written as questions with stakes attached. Key Claims #1 is headed as a question ("Is Quantum Mechanics Synchronization Physics?"), but its body switches to assertion: "Quantum 'mysteries'… are not mysterious", and "Every QM interpretation… is an epicycle". It's also introduced as "the foundational one — the others follow from it", while carrying a *Speculative — untestable as stated* badge. As an editor, I'd flag that the foundation of the claims page is the one claim the page says can't be tested.

**Internal artifacts in public copy.** Honest Assessment cites `maintainer/scripts/lensing_ceiling_every_convention.py (+ _output.txt)` in running text just above the badge definitions. Key Claims cites "Session #581". These are fine as provenance but read as internal file references to an outsider. Link them, or move them into footnotes.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /core-idea, /parameter-derivations, /galaxy-rotation, /tier-1-existing, /dark-energy, /gamma-boundary, /key-claims, /interactive-tools, plus tools /galaxy-plotter, /consciousness-demo, /chemistry-correlation-explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The algebra is right almost everywhere I checked. The site's self-audit is harsher than I would have been. The remaining problems are stale positive sentences left inside otherwise-negative pages, and one page whose badge and prose still defend a result the page itself has undercut.

### Journey
A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project." I expected a crank TOE. I skipped the beginner material and went straight to /parameter-derivations and /core-idea. I checked every equation I could by hand, and I grepped the raw page text before trusting any summary.

**The core identity checks out.** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1] is correct: write tanh(u) = (e^{2u}−1)/(e^{2u}+1) and set e^{u} = (1+x)^γ. At γ = ½ this gives x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), exactly as claimed. So the "coherence function" is a Hill function with index n = 2γ, and at n = 1 it *is* Milgrom's simple μ with a₀′ = 2a₀. The site says this itself ("the tanh∘ln decomposition is notation, not mechanism"). That is the correct verdict, and the Reparametrization badge on the functional form is honest.

**The derivative on /gamma-boundary checks out.** The page gives dC/dx = 4γ·t^{2γ−1}/(t^{2γ}+1)², t = 1+x. I re-derived it by the quotient rule and got the same expression; it gives dC/dx|₀ = γ. So the retracted "maximum curvature at γ ≈ 1" claim really is false, and the retraction is correct.

**γ = 2/√N_corr.** As a condensed-matter person, this is the first thing I wanted to attack. More correlated units should make the crossover *sharper* (in the thermodynamic limit it becomes a true singularity; see the Ginzburg criterion), not flatter. And CLT scaling for correlated variables is self-contradictory. /parameter-derivations says both things in almost those words ("sign-inverted relative to mean-field physics", "Invoking CLT for correlated N_corr is self-contradictory"). No page I read claims tanh is derived from Ising/mean-field self-consistency. That's good, because m = tanh(βJzm) has nothing to do with a tanh of log-density.

**Numbers I recomputed:**
- a₀ = cH₀/2π with H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², 13% below 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. ✓
- Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc². ✓ The page correctly calls the 0.5% match to Freeman "precision theater".
- B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17 → f_DM ≤ 1 − Ω_m = 0.685. ✓ f_DM = 0.927 → B ≥ 1/(1−0.927) = 13.7. ✓ Ω_m/Ω_b = 6.39. ✓
- TEST-09: (3.75 − 3.35)/√(0.07² + 0.10²) = 3.28σ. ✓ (The page quotes |Δn| = 0.41 where the central values give 0.40. Rounding, trivial.)
- ρ_crit ∝ V² vs data V^{−0.16±0.19}: (2 + 0.16)/0.19 = 11.4σ. ✓ The "MOND-matching requires V⁻²" claim also checks out dimensionally: ρ ~ V²/(GR²) with R ~ V²/a₀ gives ρ ~ a₀²/(GV²).
- Galaxy plotter: ρ_crit = 0.029 × 47² = 64 M☉/pc³. ✓ For small x, C ≈ 2γx, so C ≈ 0.001 means ρ ≈ 0.03 M☉/pc³. That's a reasonable DDO 154 midplane density, so "never activates" is physically right.
- Environment null: r² = 0.0001, N = 141 → t ≈ 0.12, p ≈ 0.9. ✓

**Dark energy was the best page for me.** ρ_DE ≡ ρ_m(1−C)/C with the Hill form gives ρ_DE = 2ρ_m/[(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]. At γ = ½ this is exactly 2ρ_crit, i.e. a cosmological constant. For general γ: as x → ∞, ρ_DE ∝ ρ_m^{1−2γ}, so w = −2γ; as x → 0, ρ_DE → ρ_crit/γ, so w = −1. That is exactly the page's "w runs from −2γ to −1". The sign lock sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a) then follows from monotonicity, and it genuinely forbids DESI's w₀ > −1, w_a < 0 quadrant. This is the one place I saw a *structural* prediction with a sign. It's a nice result, even though the fit lands on ΛCDM (γ = 0.487). I first misread "0.011 offset" as |0.489 − 0.487|. The raw text says it is SPARC's offset from ½ (σ_γ = 0.11). That's fine, and my summary tool mangled it, not the page.

**Cassini / TEST-25.** My first instinct was "McGaugh's exponential RAR function saturates to Newton exponentially fast, so it can't fail the Solar System." The page anticipated this. The failure is the Galactic external-field *quadrupole* Q₂ at ~7,000 AU (Hees et al.-style) in a QUMOND realization, not the direct anomaly at Saturn. The page scopes it to QUMOND and says it does not reach modified inertia. That's correct, and it is honestly booked as "inherited from MOND". Small nit: one sentence says "for any simple-μ-class return, ν−1 ≈ a₀/g, so δg ≈ a₀", then quotes δg ≈ 3×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², which is 2.5a₀. The factor presumably comes from the compander's a₀′ ≈ 2a₀, but the sentence doesn't say so.

**Where it goes wrong:**
1. **/galaxy-rotation "Dataset Results" SPARC card** still lists two unbadged bullets: "Coherence function fits within observational scatter" and "Environment-dependent effects visible but sample too small for strong statistics". The same page reports ΔBIC = +184 against the γ = 2 pin. /tier-1-existing reports the environment run at r² = 0.0001, p = 0.89 against a registered r² > 0.20. The first bullet is only true for free γ ≈ 0.49, i.e. for MOND. The second is contradicted by the site's own null. These are leftover positive claims, and a skeptical reader will quote them.
2. **/gamma-boundary** is badged "Reparametrization — 1,703 Phenomena / 89% Boundary-Consistent".
