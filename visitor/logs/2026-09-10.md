## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The honesty is the hook — I stayed because a site that opens with "we tested it and it didn't hold" is more interesting than one selling me a theory, but the beginner path quietly dumps an 18,000-word page into a "~30 min" route.

### Journey

I clicked in from a Reddit link expecting the usual "one equation explains everything" crankery. The headline is *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — and then immediately underneath: **"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."** That stopped me. I've never seen a physics site lead with its own failure. I kept reading purely because of that.

The equation is right there on the landing page: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))`. I have no idea what tanh is, ln is something about logarithms from high school, and the Greek letters mean nothing. I skimmed past it. There's a "Beginner Path" button so I took that.

**Step 1, Why Synchronism?** — good. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a sentence I understand. The idea that one number (how densely packed something is) might replace a bunch of separate patches is genuinely appealing. It also tells me I'm on "Step 1 of 6", which I liked — I knew where I was.

**Step 2, First Encounter** — the crowd-vs-marching-band analogy for coherence landed immediately. Crowd in a plaza = everyone doing their own thing; marching band = one organism. Got it. "Presence" as density inside a bubble of neighbors that matter (MRH) also made sense — "a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy" is a great line.

Then I saw a callout: *"Prefer analogies to equations? Try Two Reframes (side trip — not part of this sequence)"*. Yes! That's me. I clicked it. It has `Γ = γ²(1−c)`, `dτ/dt = √(1−2GM/r)`, and `E(a,b) = −cos(a−b)`. That is *more* equations than the page I came from. I felt a bit tricked. The actual content was the best thing on the site for me though — the CRT television analogy for quantum superposition (the electron beam scans so fast you see the whole picture at once) is the first time a quantum-weirdness explanation has ever clicked for me. And the pendulum-clock-in-a-centrifuge idea, that maybe clocks are just instruments being affected rather than time itself bending — I don't know if that's right but I understood it, which is new.

**Step 3, The Core Idea** — the "dimmer switch" framing plus "an S-shaped curve — flat, then steep, then flat again. Nothing happens at low density, a small change flips the behavior in the middle, and past the knee more density changes almost nothing" is the explanation of tanh I needed *on the landing page*, three clicks earlier. Once I had that, the equation stopped being scary.

**Step 4, What Synchronism Is Not** — I appreciated this a lot, but it's where the vocabulary got away from me: ΛCDM, QFT, "reparametrizations," "ontology." I gathered "reparametrization" means roughly "same old idea in new clothes" from context.

**Step 5, Honest Assessment** — I hit a wall. The page announces it is ~18,000 words. There is a "If you read nothing else" bullet list right at the top, which is excellent and clearly written and which I did read (the mass–speed prediction failed at "3.3σ", none of the numbers come from first principles, no experiment can currently tell it apart from existing physics). But this is listed as one of six steps in a "~30 min" beginner path. Eighteen thousand words is an hour and a half by itself. I read the summary and left.

**Step 6, Glossary** — 72 terms, alphabetical, no search box. I wanted to look up "tanh" and had to scroll. It *is* defined there ("smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1"), which is more than most sites do.

I then went and played with the tools, which is where I actually enjoyed myself.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — Two sliders and a curve that moves in real time. I dragged "Transition sharpness (γ)" and watched the S-curve go from a gentle ramp to a sharp step, and I finally *saw* what the equation means: below some density nothing happens, then it flips fast, then it saturates. This did more for my understanding than any paragraph. The "Log scale / Linear scale" toggle confused me until I flipped it and saw what happened. Minor gripe: the readouts are labelled `C(ρ_crit)`, `C(10ρ_crit)`, `C(100ρ_crit)` — I'd rather see "at the knee / 10× denser / 100× denser."
- **γ Calculator** — worked — intuitive, and startlingly honest — You type in how many particles move together and it gives you γ. The preset buttons (ideal gas, liquid water, ferromagnet, BCS superconductor) let me poke around without knowing any numbers. The violet line — the framework's own prediction — sits on the drooping grey line and plainly fails, right there in public.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the equation was tested and lost every decisive test, what exactly am I being invited to believe? I think the answer is "nothing — the audit is the product," but I had to read 18,000 words deep to find that stated plainly.
2. What is MOND and why does everything collapse onto it? I gathered it's a rival idea from 40 years ago that also explains galaxy rotation, but I never learned what it actually *says*.
3. The site mentions a "consciousness equation, C = f(γ, D, S) ≥ 0.50" with EEG protocols. C means coherence everywhere else — is this the same C? Consciousness appearing next to galaxy rotation is exactly the thing that makes my crank-detector twitch, and nothing on the beginner path prepared me for it.
4. Who is doing this? It says "3,308 AI-to-AI sessions with human oversight." Does that mean AIs did the research? I genuinely couldn't tell if that's a bold experiment or a red flag, and the site doesn't say which it thinks it is.
5. If it failed, why is the site still here and still being updated daily? (I'd read a page that answered this.)

### Verdict

I came expecting to bounce and I stayed for twenty minutes, which is not what I expected. The tools are genuinely good — the galaxy plotter and the coherence slider taught me more physics than the prose did — and the refusal to hide the failures is the most compelling thing about the site. But the beginner path has a trapdoor in it: five easy steps and then an eighteen-thousand-word wall, inside a route labelled "~30 min." Fix that one thing and I'd send this to friends as the most honest science site I've read.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The status vocabulary is the most rigorously governed I've audited on a science site — one canonical definitions page, explicit deprecation notes, cross-links from every consumer page — and it fails at exactly one point: the canonical page is also an 18,000-word narrative, and the cross-links all aim at one anchor out of three.

### Journey

I came in to audit structure, not physics. I mapped the top nav, then walked the paths, then chased every status label back to its definition to see whether the chain holds.

**Nav and IA.** Nine top-nav items, each with a `title` tooltip that actually says something useful ("72 terms defined", "24 proposed experiments by tier"). The footer mirrors the nav and adds three items the nav omits — First Encounter, Key Claims, For Researchers — plus two external links. That's a reasonable split: nav for wayfinding, footer for the full index.

Breadcrumbs are consistent in shape: `Home / <Category> / <Page>`, where the category segment is plain unlinked text and the endpoints are links. I checked linkage explicitly on Test Roadmap — "Predictions & Experiments" is a label, not an anchor. That's a defensible pattern (the categories aren't pages), and I want to say so plainly because it *looks* like a bug and isn't.

**Learning Paths.** Three difficulty paths and four topic tracks. What impressed me: the page states outright that the two groupings are not alternative views of the same content — "difficulty paths reach 10 pages no topic track does" and "topic tracks reach 14 the difficulty paths don't." Most sites with dual taxonomies quietly pretend they're equivalent. This one measures the gap and publishes the number.

**The status vocabulary.** This is where I spent most of my time, and where the site is both best and worst. There are **three** parallel label families:

1. **Validation badges** — an MRH-relationship family (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative) and a descriptive family (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed), plus two deprecated (Validated, Strongly Supported) with migration notes.
2. **Operational states** — Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (RETIRED), Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson, and two chemistry-specific caveats.
3. **Status lifecycle verbs** — EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, CEILING EXCEEDED.

All three are defined. All three live on `/honest-assessment`. Research Philosophy explicitly renounces its own copy — *"The definitions now live in exactly one place; this page keeps only the rationale for having two families."* I audit documentation for a living and I rarely see a single-source-of-truth discipline stated that cleanly.

Then I tested the chain from the consumer side, and it broke.

**Tier 1: Existing Data** carries its own inline legend defining Active-MRH, Reparametrization, Untested, Speculative, Failed, MOND-shared, Kill Criterion Triggered, and Withdrawn. Below it, the test table uses **"Underpowered as registered"** (TEST-04a, TEST-06), **"Demoted to Tier-2"** (TEST-07), and **"Closed"** (CLUSTER-SCALE). None of those three are in the legend. The page's escape hatch is *"Full canonical definitions: Honest Assessment — Badge Definitions"* pointing at `#validation-badge-definitions` — and I verified that anchor contains the badges, not the lifecycle verbs. So a reader who looks up "Underpowered as registered" follows the one link offered, lands on the badge section, doesn't find it, and is now hunting an 18,000-word page by eye. The definition exists. The path to it does not.

**Four things I thought were defects and had to withdraw.** Worth recording, because the site earned them:
- "Model Explainer" and "Artifact Lesson" on the tools index looked like undocumented vocabulary. There's a legend immediately above the cards defining both, and distinguishing level tags from verdicts.
- "Withdrawn" on the landing page looked like a badge outside the taxonomy. The landing page pre-empts this: *"('Validated' is deprecated; 'Withdrawn' is an operational state, not a badge.)"*
- I expected duplicate, drifted badge definitions across Research Philosophy and Honest Assessment. There is exactly one copy.
- Pass 1 read SPARC/ΛCDM/BTFR as undefined. They are all in the glossary with correct expansions. The problem is first-use glossing on the pages that use them, not a missing glossary — a different fix with a different owner.

**Terminology.** Mostly disciplined. ρ is introduced as "presence" and immediately equated to density (*"presence is density — how much stuff is packed into a given space"*), so the dual naming is glossed rather than drifting. Equation Anatomy is careful where it counts: it flags that ρ_crit is **not** the curve's midpoint (at γ=2, C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88), which is the exact misreading the notation invites.

The one collision I couldn't resolve is **C**. Everywhere on the site, C is coherence, C(ρ), range 0→1. The Consciousness Threshold Demo puts a threshold at "C ≈ 0.50" and describes it as *"the output-range midpoint of [0, 1)"* — which strongly implies the same C — but the page never states that it is. For a reader arriving from the physics pages, an unlabelled symbol reuse across galaxy rotation and consciousness is the single highest-cost ambiguity on the site. (Pass 1 hit this independently from the opposite direction, as a credibility alarm rather than a notation question. Two personas, two ceilings, same sentence missing.)

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which of the three label families is a reader supposed to look at *first*? A test can carry a validation badge, an operational state, and a lifecycle verb simultaneously (TEST-09: "Failed, Kill Criterion Triggered"). Is there a precedence order, and where would a reader learn it?
2. The MRH-relationship family is described as preferred for in-flight work, yet the landing page legend enumerates only the four descriptive tags. Is the landing page deliberately scoped to descriptive verdicts, or is it mid-migration? A dated note either way would tell a reader which.
3. Is `/tools` a permanent alias for `/interactive-tools` or an accident? Both resolve. If it's intentional, fine; if not, it's two URLs for one page and a canonical-link question.
4. Who is the Honest Assessment page written for? It currently serves the casual reader (the "If you read nothing else" summary, added at a reader's request), the auditor (18,000 words of narrative), and the machine-readable reference (three definition tables). Those are three documents.

### Verdict

The governance of this site's vocabulary is genuinely better than most funded documentation projects: one canonical definitions location, explicit deprecation with migration owners, consumer pages that renounce their own duplicate copies, and legends that distinguish "level tag" from "verdict" so a reader can't mistake a tool label for a scientific claim. Four times I thought I'd found undocumented terminology and four times the site had already written the sentence I was about to recommend. The defect is not rigor, it's routing: three label families and a data dictionary are buried inside an 18,000-word essay, and every cross-link on the site aims at one of the three anchors. Split the taxonomy into its own reference page and add two anchors, and the chain that's already been built end-to-end would actually be walkable.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: I checked eight numerical claims against my own arithmetic and every one of them held, which is not the outcome I expected — but the site's headline scoreboard ("6 refutations executed") advertises a breadth of testing that the site's own decomposition, eighteen thousand words later, reduces to two.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," which is usually how I end up reading a preprint about consciousness and the golden ratio. I went straight past the beginner path to `/parameter-derivation`, because that's where a framework either has a spine or doesn't.

It opens by conceding it has no spine: *"zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations."* Fine — but that's cheap to write and expensive to mean, so I started checking.

**γ = 2/√N_corr.** The page says the 1/√N borrows CLT scaling and applies it to *correlated* degrees of freedom, "exactly the regime where the iid hypothesis fails." That's correct and it's the right objection. Then it makes the objection I would have made: *"In any mean-field model (Ising, Curie–Weiss), increasing the number of correlated units makes the transition sharper... Here, γ = 2/√N_corr decreases with N_corr, giving the most-correlated systems the flattest transitions."* That is the whole game. Mean field becomes *exact* as coordination number grows; the sharpness of a transition is monotone increasing in effective coupling range. This ansatz runs the arrow backwards, and the site says so and badges it Audited-Negative. I did not expect a framework to publish the sentence that kills its own scaling law.

**The tanh.** Here is where I spent the most time, because "we chose tanh from the sigmoid/compander family (μ-law, Hill, logistic)" is a sentence that hides a lot. Note the identity:

    tanh(γ·ln u) ≡ (u^{2γ} − 1)/(u^{2γ} + 1)

so with u = 1 + ρ/ρ_crit, C(ρ) is *exactly* an affine rescaling of a Hill function with Hill coefficient 2γ. And a logistic in ln ρ is a Hill function too. So tanh∘ln, logistic∘ln and Hill are not three candidates — they are one function up to an affine map on the output, and the only content in the whole "choice" is the exponent 2γ. (I verified the identity numerically to 1e-12 at γ = 0.489, 0.7, 2.0.) Only μ-law is genuinely a different animal, and μ-law isn't a sigmoid.

I went looking for whether the site knew this, expecting it didn't, and found on `/honest-assessment`:

> "set **γ = 1/2** exactly. Then 2γ = 1 and it collapses to **C(x) = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2)** — MOND's simple interpolating function, with the factor of 2 absorbed entirely into the fitted ρ_crit. SPARC's 0.489 is 2.2% from that exact point."

Checked: tanh(½·ln(1+z)) = z/(z+2) identically, for every z I tried, to machine precision. And 0.489 is 2.2% from 0.5 — which is also, independently, the max fractional deviation between the γ=0.489 curve and μ_simple across four decades in z. So the site has found the strongest statement available about its own galaxy sector, stated it exactly, and put it on a page. The free-γ SPARC fit landing at 0.489 is not a coincidence to be explained; it is the data recovering an algebraic identity. That is the single best result on this site and it is a negative one.

Twelve for twelve. The B_max chain in particular is properly closed: the ceiling, the implied f_DM cap, and the 13.7× requirement are three views of one inequality and they agree to the last digit. Whoever built that ledger was not eyeballing it.

**Where I did find something.** Two places, both structural rather than arithmetic.

First, the BTFR comparison row reads *"Observed n = 3.75 ± 0.10; MOND n = 3.81 ± 0.04; Synchronism n = 3.35 ± 0.07."* Synchronism's 3.35 is an a-priori prediction, derived from B_max = 3.17. MOND's 3.81 is an *empirical fit* — MOND's actual a-priori BTFR prediction is n = 4 exactly, since M_b = V_f⁴/(Ga₀) is a theorem in the deep-MOND limit, not a fit. Against n = 4, the observed 3.75 ± 0.10 sits at 2.5σ, and MOND is in mild tension too (this is the known M/L-systematics story; Lelli et al. get 3.85 ± 0.09). The page mentions n → 4 only as "MOND's deep limit," never as the BTFR prediction. So the row that reads "MOND passes at 0.6σ, Synchronism fails at 3.3σ" is scoring a fit against a prediction. The verdict on Synchronism doesn't change — 3.3σ is 3.3σ — but the *contrast* is manufactured, and on a site whose brand is not manufacturing contrasts, that's worth one clause.

Second, and more interesting: **the site over-refutes itself, and the over-refutation reads as breadth of testing.** The landing page scoreboard says "Refutations executed: 6 (5 on external data + 1 theorem-level)." Deep inside `/honest-assessment` is the decomposition, and it is scrupulous:

> "Read the column, not the total... two framework-specific mechanism roots... One entry refutes a *registration* the equation never implied. One is a published MOND constraint the framework inherits by being in the same IF family; it does not discriminate. One is a theorem, and a theorem is not 'external data.'"

Six becomes two. And those two are B_max and the γ=2 pin — one inequality and one fixed parameter. The *functional form* has never been refuted at all; it's been shown to be identical to MOND at γ=1/2, which is a different and much more damning result than a refutation. A reader who takes away "6 refutations" concludes the framework was comprehensively probed and lost. The truth is narrower and stranger: almost nothing framework-specific was ever exposed to data, because at free γ there was nothing framework-specific left. The honest headline isn't "6 refutations." It's "two constants tested, both excluded; the rest of the equation is MOND."

**The Bullet Cluster and the local-density problem.** `/key-claims` states it plainly — C(ρ) is keyed to volumetric density, the RAR needs non-local enclosed-mass acceleration, cluster fits demand ρ thresholds 10⁴–10⁶ below galaxy values, and Bullet Cluster lensing peaks track the collisionless component. That is the correct and fatal objection to any local-density modification, it's Milgrom's own 2005 point, and the site attributes it correctly and calls the bridge closed. `/tier-1-existing` shows all four Coma ansätze (A1–A4) dying for the same reason. Good.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the compander at γ=1/2 *is* μ_simple identically, what was ever supposed to be different? The γ=2 pin was the only distinguishing content in the galaxy sector, and the pin's justification (γ = 2/√N_corr with N_corr = 1) requires galaxies to be uncorrelated. 2. The site's own audit says ρ_crit's knee sits 2.5×10⁴ above every density in the SPARC sample. If the knee is unreachable, the model is exactly its own linearization there — and a linearization of a monotone sigmoid is a one-parameter rescaling. Is there *any* dataset at a density where the knee is reachable? The globular-cluster fork (P611.2) looks like the only live attempt; is that because it's the only resolved system dense enough?
3. Four things I expected to find and didn't, because the site got there first: the CLT misuse, the mean-field sign inversion, the a₀/Σ₀ non-independence, and the γ=1/2 identity. ### Verdict

I came in expecting to spend an hour finding the place where the arithmetic quietly stops working, and I did not find it — twelve independent checks, twelve agreements, including the non-obvious ones (Σ₀ = 119 M☉/pc² to three digits, the B_max/f_DM/13.7× chain closing exactly, the tanh–μ_simple identity holding to machine precision). The framework is dead and the site knows it is dead and has written down why with more precision than most published refutations manage. My two real objections are both about *asymmetry*, not error: MOND is scored on a fit while Synchronism is scored on a prediction, and the "6 refutations" headline advertises a testing breadth that the site's own decomposition reduces to two constants. ## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: solid

- **Overall impression**: This is the most rigorously self-audited failed framework I have read, and its remaining defects are no longer overclaims — they are *misclassified negatives*: one refutation filed as inherited that is actually framework-specific, one test filed as untestable whose own numbers are internally contradictory by seven orders of magnitude, and a pre-registered cosmology kill criterion that failed for statistical power in a data release that has since been superseded twice.

### Journey

I came for the usual reason: a colleague forwarded "AI-generated physics framework" and I wanted to know how long it would take to find the overclaim. Twenty minutes in I stopped hunting for overclaims, because there aren't any left — the site has already found them, badged them, and dated the demotions. So I changed the question to the one that's actually interesting: **given that the audit is the product, is the audit itself correctly done?** That is a different and harder question, and it is where I spent the rest of the session.

**The landing scoreboard and `/for-researchers`.** "0 prospective predictions confirmed. 6 refutations executed. 0 of 6 'validated' claims surviving audit." `/for-researchers` goes further and enumerates six *surviving negative results* offered for citation independent of the framework — the local-density no-go (SPARC ΔBIC=+184, cross-system ρ↔g_bar offset ~1.7 dex, clusters needing ρ_crit off by 10⁴–10⁶), the density-keyed unidentifiability result (Fisher correlation ρ(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000 — γ and the normalisation enter only through their product), the Lorentz naturalness gap, the Bell/CHSH corollary, the A2ACW audit null, and a globular-cluster exclusion window. That framing — "here is what survives even though we're wrong" — is the correct way to close out a dead program, and I want to say so before I start taking it apart.

The unidentifiability result is the real one. For ρ_crit above every sampled density, tanh(γ ln(1+x)) → γx, and the model collapses to a one-parameter family. A Fisher correlation of exactly +1.000000 is not a fitting pathology; it is the statement that the second parameter does not exist. That is a clean, transferable lemma about density-keyed compander models, it is framework-independent, and it is worth a short methods note in its own right. Nobody needs to believe anything about Synchronism to use it.

**Where the audit is wrong: TEST-25 is not inherited.** The site classifies the Cassini/SPARC joint squeeze (+17.95σ) as "inherited from MOND," on the grounds that Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024) already found RAR–Cassini quadrupole tension for AQUAL/QUMOND with RAR-preferred interpolating functions, and this framework "inherits it by sitting in that family."

That reasoning does not hold, and the site's own sentence two lines later is why: *"at γ = 1/2 the compander is Milgrom's simple μ identically."*

MOND is not a theory, it is a family with a free function. The Cassini bound excludes *particular* interpolating functions — those approaching unity too slowly — and a MONDian escapes by choosing another μ. That freedom is the whole reason the constraint is survivable there. Synchronism has no such freedom. γ is not a choice in this framework; it is the one number the SPARC data determines, and the free-γ fit lands on 0.489, which is 2.2% from the exact value at which the compander *is* the excluded function. The framework is pinned to the single member of the family that the Solar System rules out, and it is pinned there by its own best fit.

A refutation you can escape by reparametrization is inherited. A refutation your fit drives you into is yours. TEST-25 belongs in the framework-specific column, and the honest split is **3 mechanism roots, not 2**. I note this cuts against the direction of the over-refutation correction elsewhere on the site: in this one cell the audit under-attributes.

**Where the audit is wrong: TEST-02's own numbers contradict each other.** The wide-binary card states the mechanism as `g_eff = g_N/C(ρ); lower ρ → smaller C → larger boost above Newtonian` and, in the same card, states the predicted deviation as **0.05–0.4%**, adding *"the knee sits above any stellar-neighbourhood density, so C = γx and the boost never turns on."*

Those two sentences are reciprocals of each other, and they cannot both be right.

Take the site's own published calibration. `/galaxy-plotter` gives ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat²; `/parameter-derivation` gives the calibrated ρ_crit = 6.5×10² M☉/pc³ with SPARC median midplane density 2.6×10⁻² M☉/pc³, i.e. the knee sits 2.5×10⁴ above the sampled densities. For the solar neighbourhood, ρ ≈ 0.09 M☉/pc³ (McKee, Parravano & Hollenbach 2015) and V_flat ≈ 229 km/s gives ρ_crit ≈ 1.5×10³ M☉/pc³, so

    x = ρ/ρ_crit ≈ 5.9×10⁻⁵
    C = tanh(0.489·ln(1+x)) ≈ 0.489x ≈ 2.9×10⁻⁵
    1/C ≈ 3.5×10⁴

Under the stated mechanism the density branch predicts a **~10⁴× enhancement of gravity in the solar neighbourhood**, not a 0.4% one. (At the plotter's γ = 2 it is 8.5×10³×.) The quoted 0.05–0.4% band tracks *C itself*, not 1/C — which is to say the amplitude appears to have been computed from a coupling in which the excess is proportional to C, while the mechanism line specifies the reciprocal. I looked for the arithmetic on the page and it is not shown.

This matters because the softer number is load-bearing. It is what produces "~80× below Gaia DR3 reach," which produces "practically untestable, not just difficult," which is why TEST-02 was never executed. If the mechanism line is the correct one, the density branch is not untestable — it is excluded by four orders of magnitude by the Oort limit and by Solar System ephemerides, and has been for decades. `/key-claims` already says as much in different words (*"under g_obs = g_bar/C(ρ) the theory diverges where matter runs out — rotation curves do not fail to flatten, they blow up"*), so the site holds both readings on two pages without reconciling them.

**TEST-02 is not non-discriminating. It is a branch squeeze, and the site has already built the machinery to say so.** The card's logic is: a Chae-type ~1.4× anomaly refutes C(ρ); a Newtonian null is consistent with C(ρ) but equally with GR; therefore no outcome favours Synchronism. True as far as it goes, and it stops one step early. The framework has two branches, and the card names them both: the **density-keyed** branch (predicts a Newtonian null) and the **acceleration-keyed** branch that *actually fits SPARC*, which "is μ_simple(x/2) at γ = ½ and predicts MOND's wide-binary boost."

So run the table properly:

| Gaia DR4 outcome | Density branch | Acceleration branch (the one that fits SPARC) |
|---|---|---|
| Newtonian null | survives | **refuted** |
| Chae-type ~1.4× | **refuted** | survives (as MOND) |

Every outcome kills a branch. And the branches are not free to be selected per-test: the density branch is already dead on SPARC (ΔBIC=+184, ρ↔g_bar offset 1.7 dex, clusters off by 10⁴–10⁶), and the acceleration branch is already dead on Cassini (+17.95σ, per above, framework-specifically). **No single branch survives both the rotation curves and the local dynamics.** That is exactly the structure the site calls an "internal-consistency squeeze" and credits to TEST-25 — it applies to TEST-02 too, and it converts the site's weakest test card into a second closed one. The verdict on the framework doesn't change; the accounting does, and this site's product *is* the accounting.

**The one live, actionable item: TEST-04a is stale, and its kill criterion may now be reachable.** This is the most useful thing I can tell this site.

TEST-04a is a *pre-registered* test: the criterion `fσ₈(z=0.51) > 0.46 at >3σ` was written down in advance. It was executed 2026-05-05 against DESI DR1 (arXiv:2411.12021/12022) and reached only ~1.5σ. The site records it as "underpowered as registered" and moves on. But that is a power failure, not a verdict — and the power has since arrived. DESI DR2 was released 2025-03-19 and DR3 on 2026-07-30; DR2 full-shape analyses are published and validated (e.g. arXiv:2607.27411 for the Lyα forest full-shape validation). Two data releases have landed since the version this test was run against.

A site whose entire methodological claim is *"we register criteria in advance and we execute them"* is sitting on a registered criterion that failed only for want of statistics, in a sector where the statistics have roughly doubled. Re-running TEST-04a on DR2/DR3 full-shape is the single highest-value executable action available here, and it is cheap. Note also that the site's cosmological verdict language ("disfavored 2.4σ on σ₈," Δχ² ≥ +79 for Completion B, "inheriting ΛCDM's own ~2.9σ DESI cost") is all pinned to DR1-era numbers.

**The revival criterion is well-posed and points the wrong way — more sharply than the site says.** `/honest-assessment` states that the only empirical rescue is an **interior maximum of ρ_DE(x)**, and that no completion of C = tanh(γ ln(1+x)) produces one — the family yields monotone, zero, or minimum-type ρ_DE, never a maximum. That is a good, checkable statement of a structural obstruction, and I want to point out it is *stronger* than the page realises. For CPL,

    ln ρ_DE(a) = −3(1+w₀+w_a) ln a + 3w_a(a−1),  d/d ln a = 0  ⟹  a* = (1+w₀+w_a)/w_a

and for DESI's preferred region (w₀ ≈ −0.7, w_a ≈ −0.9) this gives a* ≈ 0.67, i.e. an interior maximum at z ≈ 0.5, with d²/d(ln a)² = 3w_a a < 0 confirming it is a maximum. So DESI's preferred dark energy *does* have exactly the feature the compander family structurally cannot produce, and DR2 strengthened that preference relative to DR1. The framework's cosmological sector is refuted harder by the current data than by the data the site cites.

**A2ACW: the methodology critique is right, and its own null is asserted from n = 6.** The site is admirably clear that A2ACW is "a falsifiability and self-consistency filter — not a discovery method," and that two agents sharing a training distribution share blind spots. The retrospective audit is genuinely good practice: run the protocol on claims that were *subsequently* demoted, using models with different training cutoffs, and see if it catches them. Result: 0 of 6.

But that is a *negative* control, and it is being over-read. `/for-researchers` lists "A2ACW AI Audit Null" as citable result #2 — while simultaneously and correctly recording Youden's J = 0 with CI [−0.46, +0.46], i.e. uninformative. The honest statement is "underpowered, direction unknown," not "null."

Six for six is consistent with "the framework had nothing," and equally consistent with "the protocol demotes everything." The site has run the negative control and not the positive one. That single control would tell you whether the 1.4% yield figure means anything at all — and it is, as far as I can see, the only genuinely novel methodological result this project is still in a position to produce.

Two caveats: the α/π one-loop result is regularization-scheme-dependent and that dependence has been contested in the literature, which the page does not mention; and listing this as citable result #3 slightly over-credits an *application* of a known naturalness theorem as a finding.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — scientifically honest in presentation, scientifically compromised in implementation — Five SPARC galaxies (DDO 154, NGC 2403, NGC 3198, UGC 128, NGC 7331), four curves. Verified: at V_flat=47, ρ_crit = 0.029×47² = 64 M☉/pc³, and with disc ρ ~ 10⁻² the curve cannot lift off the Newtonian baseline for any galaxy in the dropdown. The tool is a working demonstration that the equation is inert, which is worth keeping — it just shouldn't be called "real."
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — worked — and it is a properly executed null, which is rare — 23 curated examples from 1,703 phenomena, r up to +0.982 (sound velocity), +0.979 (electronegativity), +0.967 (bulk modulus). What matters is that the page runs the control rather than describing it: a 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z alone reproduces every correlation to |Δr| ≤ 0.07 and *sometimes beats them*. That is the correct null for a monotonic-target dataset, and it is the null almost nobody runs. The stated caveats — multiple-comparison artifact, Pearson r from exploratory fitting rather than held-out prediction, phenomena not statistically independent — are the right three, in the right order. My one addition: with 1,703 targets and near-monotonicity in Z, the informative statistic isn't max r, it's the *distribution* of r across all 1,703 against the Z-polynomial's distribution. The page shows the top 10 and one negative (−0.434); showing the two histograms overlaid would make the null visual instead of textual, and would take the argument from "we checked" to "look."

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Which coupling is the framework's?** `g_obs = g_bar/C` (singular in vacuum, 10⁴× boost locally, `/key-claims`) or an excess proportional to C (inert, 0.05–0.4%, TEST-02)? Every quantitative verdict on the density branch depends on which, and the site uses both.
2. **Has the positive control been considered and rejected, or just not run?** If A2ACW demotes genuine post-cutoff discoveries at 6/6 as well, the 1.4% yield is a property of the protocol, not the framework — and that would be a *publishable* result about AI-assisted research, independent of any physics here.
3. **What is ρ_crit for a system with no V_flat?** If ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat², the globular-cluster exclusion and the cluster-scale ansätze are using a quantity the calibration doesn't define for pressure-supported systems.
4. **Why has TEST-04a not been re-run?** Two DESI releases have landed since execution. Is this a resource constraint, a decision that the sector is closed, or simply an item nobody revisited? The site's credibility rests on the answer being the second one, stated.
5. **Is the 47-candidate pool retrievable?** `/for-researchers` says "do not cite" the 47. But if the audit protocol is suspect (Q2), the 47 are the sample on which that suspicion would be tested. Discarding them and doubting the discarder are not simultaneously coherent.
6. **Does anything in the framework survive the branch squeeze at all?** If density fails on SPARC and acceleration fails on Cassini, and the two exhaust the framework's readings of C, the correct headline is not "0 confirmed predictions" but "the coupling has no consistent reading" — a stronger and cleaner closure than the one currently posted.

### Verdict

The physics is closed and the site knows it: at free γ the compander is Milgrom's simple μ identically, and the only thing distinguishing this framework from MOND — the boost ceiling B_max ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17 — is refuted twice on SPARC. There is nothing novel in the galaxy sector, nothing testable in the quantum sector, and nothing calibratable in the consciousness sector, and every one of those sentences is already written somewhere on this site in stronger language than I would have used.

What is *not* closed is the audit, and that is where the remaining value is. Three of the site's classifications are wrong in ways that matter — TEST-25 is framework-specific, not inherited; TEST-02 is a branch squeeze, not a non-discriminator; and the A2ACW null is an underpowered statistic being cited as a finding on the same page that certifies it as uninformative. Fixing those makes the negative *stronger*, which is the direction this site has consistently been willing to move.

And there is exactly one live thread: a pre-registered kill criterion that failed for statistical power against a data release that has since been superseded twice. A project whose entire claim is "we register in advance and we execute" should not leave that sitting. Re-run TEST-04a on DESI DR2/DR3. Then run the positive control on A2ACW. The second one is the only genuinely novel result still available here — and it is about AI-assisted research, not about gravity, which may have been the real finding all along.
