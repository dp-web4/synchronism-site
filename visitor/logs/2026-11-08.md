## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Key Claims, Honest Assessment, Interactive Tools, Galaxy Curve Plotter, γ Calculator, Consciousness Demo, Glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I got the story within a minute and respected it ("we tried a big idea and it failed, here's the receipts"), but every page I clicked turned into a physics paper about two screens down.

**Method limit:** pages were read as fetched text through a fetch agent. I could not drag, click or hover, and I could not see layout or whether a block starts collapsed. Long pages (Honest Assessment, Glossary) were read in part, as noted below. Anything marked *(unverified)* may be an artifact of the fetch rather than the site.

### Journey

I clicked through from Reddit expecting a crank page, because "one equation from quantum to cosmic" is what those pages say. The second thing I read was "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." That got my attention more than any claim could have. Right after that, it tells me AI agents did the work, one human directs them, and no journal or outside physicist has reviewed it. I've never seen a site open like this. I kept reading mostly out of curiosity about what kind of thing this even is.

The marching band versus the crowd in a plaza worked immediately. I understand that picture. Then the page shows me an equation with "tanh" in it, a symbol key, and a "For physicists" block with three different versions of C. I skimmed past those. Further down it says "dead as tested physics so far, alive as an open research record." That line is the clearest thing on the page, and I wish I'd met it sooner. The row of counts (0 predictions confirmed, 6 refutations, "4 to 8 depending on counting rules," 0 of 9 audited claims surviving) made me smile and also confused me. How can you not know how many times you failed? I didn't go find out.

I took the suggested start, Why Synchronism? "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a good hook, the dimmer switch is a good picture, and "What happened, in three lines" plus "you can stop here" felt respectful of my time. Then came "What We Tested," and a badge saying "89% Boundary-Consistent | Template Bias Caveat." I don't know what any of those words mean. There's also a "real, significant effect" with an R² and a p-value in the same section that's stamped Failed. So was there an effect or not? I gave up on that part and clicked on.

First Encounter is the page I liked most. Short sections, and each opens by telling me the outcome: "In short: the formula for the dial failed." "Verdict first: probably a coincidence." I wish every page did this. The quantum measurement bit ("a whisper in a loud room is still there, but nobody can pick it out") is the first explanation of wave function collapse I've found that I could repeat to a friend. But I lost track of the dial. On the landing page the bet was "a single dial." On this page γ "sets how sharply the dial turns," and then "the formula for the dial failed." Is γ the dial, or the thing that turns the dial? And the same stuff is called crowding, density, and presence (ρ) depending on the page. Also, this is the "about 10 minutes" intro and it has ρ_crit = A × V_flat², "≈6×10⁻⁴ for BCS," and "607 K predicted for YBCO."

The Galaxy Plotter was the best tool. "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" is exactly what I needed: dots are what telescopes see, gray is visible matter alone, green is the rival theory. I get why dark matter is a question now. "The only version of this framework's equation that fits these dots is MOND's formula written differently" is a very clear sentence, and a slightly sad one. Below that, though, the page keeps going with terms like BTFR, SPARC and ΔBIC.

Then I did what anybody from Reddit would do and looked for the consciousness stuff. Key Claims told me straight: "this formula is a placeholder," "f, D and S are defined nowhere," "Evidence: None yet." I respect that. The demo page explained the trick well: "every approach picks the midpoint of the output range," so of course they all agree. I actually learned something there about how fake agreement happens. It was also the most fun I had on the site, and it's a chart with no controls, listed under *Interactive* Tools.

Honest Assessment is the last step of the Beginner Path. It says itself that it's about 18,000 words. The top summary box is great ("The one equation failed its galaxy tests... As a theory of galaxies: fatal"), and there's a "Not a physicist?" box. A few paragraphs later I'm looking at "Registered kill fires only at B_max = 1/Ω_m." The Glossary's "New here? Start with these ten" is a nice idea and helped with MOND and Reparametrization, but it sits on a page with 123 terms and talks about eight label systems. That's when I closed the tab.

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've ever stumbled onto, and that honesty is the reason I stayed; the failure is the hook, not the embarrassment. The first screen or two of each page speaks to me, and then it's written for someone else, with no clear signal that I've reached the end of the part meant for me. I'd share the First Encounter page or the Galaxy Plotter with a friend, but I wouldn't send anyone to the Beginner Path's last step.

### Journey

I started with the skeleton, because that's where my job starts. Top nav has nine items, the footer adds Key Claims and First Encounter, and the Beginner Path chains cleanly: all five steps have working prev/next blocks, including the tool page in the middle. Breadcrumbs exist on every page I checked except the landing page, which doesn't need one. All 69 internal paths linked from my 17 pages were tested, and only two return 404. Of 131 anchors, one fails. All nine badges have a canonical definition with an anchor, the ⓘ badges are marked up to open those definitions, and the glossary has a "New here? Start with these ten" list. Most sites never get this far.

Then I started tracking what things are called, and that's where it comes apart.

The central object has at least six working names: "the one equation," "the coherence function," "C(ρ)," "the compander," "the tanh-log equation" and "the density law." The Beginner Path never uses "compander" (0 on First Encounter). Tier 1 uses it 25 times and "coherence function" zero times. Tier 1's own acronym key doesn't define it either; its first use is mid-sentence ("the compander is Milgrom's simple μ"). So a reader who finishes the Beginner Path and moves to Tier 1 meets the same object under a new name, with no signpost on that page. The glossary does have "Compander" and a "Which C? Which γ?" entry, and the landing page's "for physicists" block points at C_ρ/C_a/C_g. The site knows there's a problem. The fix sits in reference material, not at the point where the name changes.

The test programme has a similar spread. The nav says "Test Roadmap," the URL says `/test-catalog`, the Tier 1 back-link says "Back to Catalog," the breadcrumb section says "Predictions & Experiments," and the content calls it "the registry." `/predictions` redirects there too. Tier 1's breadcrumb makes "Predictions & Experiments" a link to `/test-catalog`, while on `/test-catalog` the same label is plain text with a tooltip saying "no index page." As a reader I can't tell whether that section has a home.

MRH is the one that bothered me most, because the site uses it for two different kinds of thing. On First Encounter and in the glossary it's a physical idea: "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter." On the Test Roadmap and Tier 1, the badge family "MRH-relationship" means "where the claim sits relative to current research focus," glossed as "the context something is currently relevant within." So MRH is both a claim the site is testing and the filing label on its claims. /mrh explains that the badges are "named by analogy" and "say nothing about physics," which is honest, but only a reader of that page learns it. There's a further wrinkle: First Encounter explains "Markov" as the mathematician's word for "only the current neighbors matter," while /mrh says "The 'Markov' label sets an expectation that is not currently met" and floats a rename.

The label system is the most carefully documented thing on the site, and that's part of the worry. "All eight label systems are mapped side by side." I now know about badges, descriptors after a dash, operational states, lifecycle verbs, retired labels, tool kinds, levels, and test-card states. Even so it isn't quite closed. The glossary intro says "Deprecated badges," its table says "Retired label," and one entry says both ("(Deprecated) ⚠ Retired badge"). When a taxonomy needs its own eight-row map, a reader has to learn a filing system before reading the content. Inside a badge the free text can also pull against the verdict: the landing page's Cosmology card reads "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision," and the same page says "dead as far as it has been tested."

What surprised me most was the amount of editorial history in running text. The landing page gets it right: it puts this in a collapsed "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)" block, so only about 2 of 28 history markers are visible. Elsewhere it stays in the body. By my rough count, about 80 markers are visible on Tier 1, about 83 on Honest Assessment, about 64 on For Researchers and 49 on the Glossary. Examples include "(Entry added 2026-10-23 after a technical-writer persona found the term used on /for-researchers with no definition.)" inside a glossary definition, and "(added 2026-10-08; a technical-writer visitor could not find the fifth external-data one here)" in Tier 1's page introduction. Those notes are about people like me, and they got in my way. Correction history belongs on this site, but it doesn't belong mid-definition.

That also explains the length. Honest Assessment says "this page is ~18,000 words." I count about 25,000 outside its collapsed blocks and about 28,000 in total. My count is rough, but the gap is too big to be counting noise. The stated "~80 min" for that page probably inherits the same low figure.

Counts are mostly excellent. The "0 confirmed / 6 executed / 4–8 under rules on file" line matched on every page I read. One tally does disagree in its parts. Tier 1 says "11 numbered tests (TEST-01 through TEST-10, plus TEST-25), plus two variants" (04a and 03s). The Test Roadmap says Tier 1 has "10 registry cards + 1 lettered variant card (04a; 03s is discussed inside the TEST-03 card) + 2 post-registry additions (25, 26) = 13 cards." The headings on the page match the Roadmap: 13 cards, including TEST-26. Tier 1's own inventory sentence leaves TEST-26 out and counts a 03s variant that has no card. Both can reach 13, which hides the mismatch. TEST-07 is also headed "(Not a Tier 1 Test)" while sitting inside the "11 numbered tests" of a page called Tier 1.

**What I recomputed and found correct:**
- **Compander:** tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^(2γ)−1]/[(1+x)^(2γ)+1]. At γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) to machine precision, so a₀ = 2a₀′. Small x gives C ≈ γx, and hence the identified scale a₀′/γ (5.33×10⁻¹¹/0.489 = 1.09×10⁻¹⁰).
- **Cosmology:** w = dlnF/dlnx, from −2γ in the far past to −1 in the far future. w(0) = −1.256 at γ = 2. The sign lock holds at γ = 0.3, 0.487, 0.6 and 2. c_s² ≈ 0.22(2γ−1) comes out at 0.217–0.229 for the total fluid. At γ = ½, g_bar-keying gives g_bar + 2a₀′ and the Friedmann equation gives exact ΛCDM. Ġ/G = 3(1−Ω_m)H₀ = 1.417×10⁻¹⁰/yr, about 1995× the LLR central value.
- **Statistics:** ΔBIC +184.5, +10.9, +7.10 and the [1.36, 20.76] scaling. TEST-09 at 3.3σ. TEST-10 caps of 0.685 and 0.844, with B = 13.70.

As far as the text says, though, that run freezes the mass-to-light ratio, and the site itself reports that the fitted (γ, a₀′) pair moves strongly with Υ (γ from 0.27 to 0.96). The marginalized 8.7σ is published for MOND's RAR-preferred functions. It is not a number for the compander at γ ≠ ½. So "family-wide exclusion" for γ up to 3 is an unmarginalized statement. It may well survive marginalization, but none of the pages I read shows that, and the 09-29 sentence was left in place without being reconciled.

**3. γ is not a sharpness.** The landing page says "γ = sharpness of transition." The Calculator says "Big γ = hair-trigger switch." The Glossary says "big γ means a sharp snap." The Explorer says "large γ = a sharp cliff," while the same page, further down, says "for γ above ~1 it mostly moves the knee." The second statement is the correct one:

| γ | x at C=½ | 10–90% width (dex) | peak dC/dln x |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.046 | 1.5×10⁵ | 13.0 | 0.044 |
| 0.49 | 2.07 | 1.93 | 0.248 |
| 1 | 0.73 | 1.50 | 0.322 |
| 2 | 0.32 | 1.33 | 0.375 |
| 10 | 0.057 | 1.20 | 0.431 |
| 1000 | 5.5×10⁻⁴ | 1.17 | 0.448 |

The "hair-trigger" at γ = 2 spans a factor of 21 in density. Sending γ to infinity only narrows that to a factor of 15. It cannot go further, because the large-γ limit is tanh(γx), and on a log axis that has a fixed shape. This bears on the headline audit. The site's objection is that γ = 2/√N has the wrong sign. But no choice of γ(N) gives a sharp transition, since the family has no sharp member. Below γ ≈ 1 the parameter does set the width; above it the parameter moves the knee. "Sign-inverted" implies a flipped law would have been physically right. The Calculator's flip paragraph (γ = 2√17 ≈ 8.25) gets a width of about 1.2 dex for all that effort and doesn't mention it.

Two more things from my own field. The Parameter Derivations page calls γ ∝ N^(−½) "the exact reciprocal" of Curie–Weiss rounding. That holds when N is the total size of an all-to-all model. N_corr is a count of correlated units, which is closer to the number in a coherence volume, and there the relevant scaling is the Ginzburg criterion. For BCS that gives a fluctuation window of order (T_c/E_F)⁴, a much steeper dependence than N^(−½). The word "Ginzburg" appears on none of the seven pages I searched for it. The direction of the audit is right, but "exact" is too strong. Also, "real BCS transitions are among nature's sharpest" refers to sharpness in temperature, while C(ρ)'s axis is density. The comparison needs a sentence saying so.
