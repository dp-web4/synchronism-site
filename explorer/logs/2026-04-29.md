### 2. What if the frame is wrong?

**A.** The reduction may be to something *less* structured than Landau. Landau theory has a
critical point, broken symmetry, and universality classes. C(ρ) might reduce to something with
even less content — e.g., a non-interacting paramagnetic response in a field. This would be a
*stronger* finding than "C(ρ) is Landau" because the framework would be working in a regime where
Landau itself has no predictive content.

**B.** The "ρ as Landau coordinate" mapping may be ill-posed. Landau coordinate is reduced
temperature (T-T_c)/T_c, which sign-flips. ρ in C(ρ) is positive-definite (a presence/density).
If ρ doesn't sign-flip, it can't play the role of reduced T in a critical-point Landau.

**C.** It might be more productive to ask: *what specific physical system, if any, does C(ρ)
describe correctly?* If the answer is "a single non-interacting two-state system in a
log-chemical-potential field," that's a precise structural identification with no remaining
ambiguity.

### 3. What experiment most increases information right now?

This is a half-day algebraic task and cannot be hand-waved — either F has Landau form or it

## Rabbit Holes (Worth Revisiting)

- **Self-consistent C(ρ) extension.** If the framework wants critical-point structure, it must
  introduce a self-consistency loop: ⟨C⟩ = tanh(γ · log[ρ · f(⟨C⟩)/ρ_crit + 1]). This *would be*
  a phase transition theory. The functional form of f(⟨C⟩) is the open question; without
  guidance from a microscopic model, there is no preferred choice. Worth a future explorer
  session to map out what f(⟨C⟩) candidates would produce what universality classes.

- **Logarithmic field as ideal-gas chemical potential.** h(ρ) = γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)
  structurally resembles μ_ideal-gas = k_B T · log(ρ/ρ_ref). If presence enters dynamics through
  chemical-potential-like coupling, this is the "natural" choice. But this is a *postulate*
  about how presence couples, not a derivation. Worth investigating whether any framework
  document derives the log form, or whether it was chosen empirically.

- **MaxEnt as the "first principle."** The derivation showed F = ln 2 − H_bin((1+C)/2) − h·C is
  literally the MaxEnt free energy of a single binary variable. If the framework's "first
  principle" is actually MaxEnt over binary states, this should be stated explicitly — and
  defenses like "tanh comes from mean-field Ising" replaced with the actual provenance. The
  MaxEnt connection makes the structural minimalism of C(ρ) clear: there is exactly one
  Lagrange multiplier (h), constraining exactly one expectation (⟨C⟩), over exactly one
  binary variable.
