## So What?

The headline this session isn't any single fix — it's that the sharpest, most technical visitor
finding (Researcher persona catching a Bell-theorem category error) led straight to a fully-run,
fully-negative, completely unshipped research result sitting in the archive for two weeks. The site
was making a stronger claim ("nonlocal by construction," implicitly cost-free) than the framework's
own experiment supports (refuted, capped at the classical bound, no free lunch). That's the
opposite failure mode from overclaiming through carelessness — it's overclaiming through *silence*,
where a real self-audit exists but never reached the page it was about. Several other findings this
session (MRH sentence structure, the coherence/coherence-function "duplicate") turned out to be
either already-fixed or non-issues once checked against source — a reminder that visitor personas
are a strong friction detector but a noisy accuracy detector, and every "high severity" content claim
still needs a source check before it becomes a fix. The propagation-gap proposal filed this session
is the more durable output: this is now enough recurrences of the same pattern to justify a
mechanized check rather than relying on the next visitor persona to stumble onto the next instance.
