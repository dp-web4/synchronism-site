**Short answer:** I haven't searched, so this comes from memory. The substrate is not new, and the claim that Lorentz violation is Planck-suppressed is a known problem. The citations below are from memory, and I'm unsure of the page details.

**Substrate: a special case of known frameworks**
- **Quantum cellular automata and lattice models.** Bialynicki-Birula, "Weyl, Dirac, and Maxwell equations on a lattice as unitary cellular automata", PRD 49, 6920 (1994). Also Meyer's quantum lattice gases (1996), and Feynman's checkerboard. These have a fixed grid, global discrete ticks, and local transfer between neighbouring cells.
- **Deterministic cellular automata as fundamental physics.** 't Hooft, *The Cellular Automaton Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics* (Springer, 2016; arXiv:1405.1548). Also Wolfram, *A New Kind of Science* (2002) and Fredkin's digital philosophy.
- **Hamiltonian lattice field theory.** Kogut and Susskind (1975) used a continuous time variable. Your version has discrete time, as in a Euclidean or Floquet lattice.
- **Preferred-frame or absolute-time gravity and aether models.** Hořava–Lifshitz gravity (2009, PRD 79, 084008) and Einstein-aether theory (Jacobson and Mattingly, 2001). These also have a fixed foliation.
- **Lorentz invariance as emergent.** Chadha and Nielsen, Nucl. Phys. B217, 125 (1983).

"Intent patterns" is not standard terminology. I know of no published model that uses it, so any new content would be in that part. It would reduce to a CA or lattice field with some internal state.

**Result: the suppression claim is not safe as stated**
- Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia and Vucetich, PRL 93, 191301 (2004), "Lorentz invariance and quantum gravity: an additional fine-tuning problem?" They show that with a Planck-scale cutoff or lattice, radiative corrections generically give unsuppressed, O(1) Lorentz violation in dimension-4 operators. Examples are different limiting speeds for different species. The result is not suppressed by (E/E_Pl)^n. Tight bounds on these operators exist, summarized in Kostelecký and Russell's data tables (Rev. Mod. Phys. 83, 11, 2011).
- Myers and Pospelov, PRL 90, 211601 (2003). Dimension-5 operators would give linear E/E_Pl effects. Fermi GRB 090510 (Abdo et al., Nature 462, 331, 2009) pushes the bound on a linear dispersion term above the Planck scale.
- Liberati, "Tests of Lorentz invariance: a 2013 update", CQG 30, 133001. A review of these bounds.
- Dowker, Henson and Sorkin, Mod. Phys. Lett. A19, 1829 (2004). Causal sets avoid the problem, because they are Lorentz-invariant by construction. A rigid cubic grid with absolute time cannot do the same.

**Verdict:** The grid is a special case of known QCA and lattice models, and I know of no published form of "intent". "Unobservable because of E/E_Pl suppression" is not established. Unless the program has a symmetry or mechanism that protects the dimension-4 operators, such as supersymmetry or a specific fine-tuning argument, it is in tension with the Collins et al. result and with the bounds above. A bare cubic lattice also gives k⁴ anisotropy at order a²k², and a Planck-length lattice would need this to be tiny. Cosmic-ray and GRB constraints would test it.
