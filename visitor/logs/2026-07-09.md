## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The honesty is genuinely disarming and the galaxy plotter made dark matter click for me, but the "no equations required" beginner path hits me with a tanh equation on step one and the beginner tools mostly exist to explain why the equation is broken.

### Journey

I clicked in from Reddit expecting either crackpottery or something over my head. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a question, not a boast, and the subtitle immediately says "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything." That bought a lot of goodwill in about four seconds.

The landing page has three doors. Mine says: **"No physics background? Use the Beginner Path (6 steps, ~30 min, no equations required)."** Perfect. I clicked.

Step 1 of 6 opened with the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` sitting right there on the page. I was told thirty seconds earlier that no equations were required. I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln does. I have a Greek letter divided by the square root of a subscript. My first instinct was that I'd clicked the wrong link, so I went back and checked — no, that's the right destination. This is the single worst moment of the visit, and it happens at the earliest possible point.

I pushed on anyway because the prose around the equation is actually fine. Step 2, "First Encounter," was the best-written page on the site for someone like me: coherence explained as a marching band (everyone in lockstep) versus a crowd (everyone wandering). No math at all. If *that* were step 1, I'd have been hooked.

But the marching band analogy created a problem I couldn't shake. Every YouTube video I've watched uses "coherence" for the quantum stuff — lasers, superposition, the delicate thing that decoherence destroys. Here, "coherence = 1" means a neutron star and "coherence = 0" means the quantum regime. That's backwards from the only meaning of the word I had. The page does say this coherence "differs from quantum coherence," and much later the glossary has a ⚠ note about it — but the warning is aimed at physicists, and I'm the one who actually got confused. I spent the rest of the site quietly mistranslating.

I never found out what steps 3 through 6 are. The page says "Step 1 of 6" and offers one "First Encounter →" arrow. There's no visible map of the path I supposedly signed up for, no sense of how far in I am, and no reason to trust I'll get somewhere.

I detoured to the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**, which the landing page also offers beginners, and this was the highlight of the whole session. Pick a real galaxy, see the dashed line for "what visible matter predicts," see the dots for "what we actually see," and the gap between them is the dark matter problem. I have watched entire documentaries that explained this less clearly. Then the site does something I did not expect: it draws its own theory's curve, and *the curve doesn't reach the dots*. It says so plainly — the disk never gets dense enough for the effect to switch on. A site showing me its own failure, in color, on the beginner-facing tool. I actually sat back for a second.

Then I tried the **γ Calculator**, and it lost me completely. It wants a number called Ncorr, "number of correlated particles." I have no idea what that is or what I should type. I clicked a preset (liquid water) because presets are what you click when you're lost. Out came a γ value and a label like "Strongly Correlated," and then the surrounding text explained that this demonstrates "the formula's central defect" — the relationship is inverted, the most correlated systems get the flattest curves, which is the opposite of what should happen. So: I put in a number I didn't understand, got out a number I couldn't interpret, and was told the machine producing it is known to be wrong. I bounced.

I recovered on **Honest Assessment**, which is the page that will make me remember this site. Zero confirmed predictions, stated in bold. The big cosmology test predicted structure should grow ~10% slower than standard cosmology, and the DESI survey showed no such thing. The galaxy test, when they let one parameter float freely across 175 galaxies, "simply turns into MOND's curve — so it is either worse than MOND or identical to it, never better." That's their own sentence about their own theory. The "In plain terms:" boxes are exactly the right call and I wish they were on every page.

**What Synchronism Is Not** kept the streak going: not a theory of everything, not peer-reviewed, not proven, and the remarkable line that the project "currently occupies the interpretation + methodology category rather than the novel-physics category."

And that's where I ran out of road. Because after all that honesty, nobody ever told me why I should keep reading. The Honest Assessment page argues that "the real product is this audit itself" — that knowing what a density-based coherence function structurally *cannot* do is genuinely useful. I think that's a great pitch! But it's buried on page eight, in a paragraph, aimed at someone who already knows why negative results matter. The landing page sold me one equation for everything. Nothing on the way in prepared me for the answer being "it doesn't work, and that's the point."

Fix the first sixty seconds and this site is remarkable. Right now it rewards exactly the reader who didn't need the beginner path.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The self-audit is real and mostly correct, but the arithmetic on the site does not survive being checked — and every error I found makes the framework look *worse* than its own equations warrant, which is a stranger failure mode than the one this site was built to prevent.

### Journey

I came in expecting to spend an afternoon writing "this is a sigmoid with free parameters." The site said that on page one, in its own voice, with a badge. So I went hunting for the thing a self-audit can't catch: whether the numbers *in* the audit are right.

**The central equation is a Hill function, and the tanh and the ln cancel.** Start from what `/equation-walkthrough` and `/galaxy-plotter` both state:

    C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))

Write x = ρ/ρ_crit. Since tanh(z) = (e^{2z} − 1)/(e^{2z} + 1), and here e^{2z} = (1+x)^{2γ}:

    C(ρ) = [(1 + ρ/ρ_crit)^{2γ} − 1] / [(1 + ρ/ρ_crit)^{2γ} + 1]

That's an exact algebraic identity, not an approximation. No transcendental functions remain. The equation is a Hill/Michaelis–Menten form with cooperativity coefficient n = 2γ, in the shifted variable (1 + ρ/ρ_crit).

This matters for three reasons the site doesn't state. First, `/equation-walkthrough` justifies the logarithm as "this compression lets one equation span 80 orders of magnitude of physical density." It doesn't. The ln compresses and the tanh's internal exponential immediately un-compresses it; the composite is a power law, C → 1 − 2(ρ/ρ_crit)^{−2γ} for large ρ. The stated motivation for the ln is void — the ln is not doing the work attributed to it, because it isn't doing any work at all. Second, the site's own honest-assessment calls this a "compander" and hedges toward the sigmoid family (μ-law, Hill, logistic, erf); the identity above shows it isn't merely *like* a Hill function, it *is* one, so the AIC/BIC-across-the-sigmoid-family test the site proposes is partly already settled by algebra. Third, and most usefully: in the Hill form, 2γ *is* the cooperativity coefficient, and cooperativity rises with the number of cooperating units. So the sign inversion the site honestly flags for γ = 2/√N_corr isn't a subtle CLT-vs-correlated-variables subtlety. It's visible in one line once you remove the tanh.

**There is no phase transition and ρ_crit is not a critical point.** C(ρ) is smooth and analytic in ρ on the whole positive axis. Differentiating, C′(ρ) = γ·sech²(γ ln(1+x)) / (ρ_crit + ρ), which is strictly positive and strictly decreasing — C is *concave everywhere on ρ > 0*. There is no inflection, no knee, no steepest point. The Ising mean-field tanh gets its transition from *self-consistency*, m = tanh(βJz·m), with m on both sides; that fixed-point structure is what produces a critical point at βJz = 1 and the exponent β = 1/2. C(ρ) is a forward map with no fixed point. The site says this — "unlike the Ising model's tanh (which emerges from self-consistency), this tanh is selected directly without self-iteration" — and that is exactly right and to its credit. But then the site keeps the vocabulary: "ρ_crit," "critical density," a page called `/phase-boundary-visualizer`. At γ = 2, C(ρ_crit) = (2⁴ − 1)/(2⁴ + 1) = 15/17 = 0.882. ρ_crit is not where anything begins; it's where the curve is already 88% saturated. Nothing in the functional form privileges it.

**Then I checked the galaxy plotter, and found the failure is structural, not numerical.** The tool states its recipe: v(r) = √(v_b² + [V_flat·C(ρ(r))]²), with ρ(r) = Σ(r)/2h and ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat². It reports that disk densities sit "thousands of times below ρ_crit," so C ≈ 0 and the curve never leaves Newtonian. True, and honestly displayed. But watch what happens when you ask *why*. Approximate Σ ≈ M/(πR²), so ρ ≈ M/(2πR²h). And V_flat² ≈ GM/R. Then

    ρ/ρ_crit  ≈  [M/(2πR²h)] / [A·GM/R]  =  1/(2π·A·G·R·h)

**The mass cancels exactly.** With ρ_crit ∝ V_flat², the coherence factor of a disk galaxy depends only on its geometry — on the product R·h — and not at all on how much matter it contains or how fast it rotates. Plugging A = 0.029, G = 4.30×10⁻³ pc·M☉⁻¹(km/s)², R = 10 kpc, h = 300 pc gives ρ/ρ_crit ≈ 4×10⁻⁴, hence C ≈ γ·ρ/ρ_crit ≈ 8×10⁻⁴ — the "thousands of times below" the page reports. But the ratio scales as 1/(R·h). To push a galaxy to C ≈ 0.5 you would need R·h ~ 10³ pc², i.e. an object roughly 30 pc across. No galaxy, of any mass, surface brightness, or type, can cross the knee. Not a single one, ever.

This is a much stronger result than the page claims. `/galaxy-plotter` presents the failure as a magnitude problem — the disk merely happens to be too dilute. It's a scaling problem: the V² in ρ_crit exactly cancels the M in ρ, so the framework's own normalization guarantees that no galaxy is ever dense enough, independent of the value of A. Fixing A cannot help. This is the same object as the "local-density no-go" advertised on `/for-researchers` as citable artifact #1, but the one-line proof is nowhere on the site and it's sharper than the prose version.

**The statistics don't reconcile, and I can show it without leaving the site.** `/tier-1-existing` gives TEST-03 as R² = 0.14 on ALFALFA+SDSS with N = 14,585, kill criterion "<20% explains scatter," status Kill Criterion Triggered. It gives TEST-05 as R² = 0.14, p = 5×10⁻⁶, on SPARC + density catalogs. The same R² to two digits, on two different surveys with sample sizes differing by ~85×, is already a coincidence worth a footnote. Then check whether the pair (R², p) is even self-consistent. R² = 0.14 means r = 0.374, and t = r√(N−2)/√(1−r²):

- With N = 14,585: t = 0.374 × 120.8 / 0.927 = **48.7**, i.e. p of order 10⁻⁵⁰⁰, not 5×10⁻⁶.
- Inverting instead, p = 5×10⁻⁶ (two-sided, t ≈ 4.57) with r = 0.374 implies **N ≈ 130.**

So the reported N and the reported p differ by roughly two orders of magnitude in sample size and five hundred in significance. They cannot both describe the same measurement. One of these numbers has been transplanted from a different run. A grad student's read: the R² = 0.14 was almost certainly measured on a sample of order 10², and the N = 14,585 belongs to some other analysis of the ALFALFA catalog. Whichever way it resolves, **the site is currently reporting a kill criterion as "triggered" on a statistic whose provenance is internally impossible.**

**TEST-04a's kill criterion did not fire either, by the site's own registration.** `/tier-1-existing` states the criterion as fσ₈(z=0.51) "> 0.46 (rules out Synchronism at >3σ)" and the result as "disfavored 2.4σ on σ₈ amplitude." Two mismatches: the criterion is registered on fσ₈, the result is quoted on σ₈; and 2.4σ does not clear a threshold explicitly registered at >3σ. The status label reads "Kill criterion TRIGGERED." It didn't. The honest label is "disfavored, below the pre-registered kill threshold." `/honest-assessment` gets this closer to right (σ₈ = 0.841 ± 0.034 vs. predicted 0.76, "2.4σ tension") but still under a header about failed predictions. 2.4σ is a tension, not a kill, and the site knows the difference because it wrote the threshold down.

**And TEST-03's design has a dead zone nobody mentions.** The prediction is "environmental term explains 51% of scatter." The kill criterion is "<20%." A measured 35% would refute the prediction by a factor of 1.5 and fire nothing. A prediction and its kill criterion separated by a 31-point gap of unfalsifiable outcomes is not a pre-registration; it's a pre-registration and a separate, much weaker pre-registration.

**Σ₀ is a 0.5% match reported as a 12% miss.** `/parameter-derivations` gives, in adjacent rows and from the same constants:

    a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²     (vs Milgrom 1.20×10⁻¹⁰, "10% error")
    Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) ≈ 110 M☉/pc²        (vs Freeman 124, "12% error")

But Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) = [cH₀/(2π)] / (2πG) = a₀/(2πG). Take the site's own a₀ = 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ and G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹: Σ₀ = 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ / (4.193×10⁻¹⁰) = 0.2576 kg/m². Converting, 1 M☉/pc² = 2.089×10⁻³ kg/m², so **Σ₀ = 123.3 M☉/pc²** — versus Freeman's 124. That's a **0.5% agreement**, not 12%. To get 110 you would need H₀ ≈ 62 km/s/Mpc, which contradicts the H₀ ≈ 70 implied by the a₀ printed one row above, and lies outside every published measurement.

I want to be precise about what this does and doesn't mean. It does *not* rescue the framework: Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) with Freeman's law is a known MOND identity (Milgrom 1989; Brada & Milgrom), so "Reparametrization" remains the correct badge and the tight agreement is exactly what a reparametrization predicts. What it means is that the site's arithmetic is wrong in the direction of self-flagellation, and it's wrong in a way that *destroys the evidence for its own correct conclusion* — the near-exact match is the tell that this is bookkeeping, and the site has replaced it with a 12% miss that would, if real, be a mildly interesting near-hit.

**That's the pattern, and it's the finding.** Every arithmetic error I found on this site over-refutes. TEST-03 fires a kill on an impossible (N, p) pair. TEST-04a fires a kill at 2.4σ against a threshold it registered at 3σ. Σ₀ turns a 0.5% match into a 12% error. Claim 3 on `/key-claims` badges a₀ = cH₀/(2π) as **Failed** while `/parameter-derivations` badges the identical formula **Reparametrization** — and the evidence adduced for "Failed" is the TFR-scatter R², which is a test of environmental scatter and says nothing whatsoever about the value of a₀. That's not a badge inconsistency, it's a non sequitur: the claim's stated content and the kill criterion attached to it are about different physical quantities.

I have never reviewed a document that erred systematically against itself. The bias here runs opposite to the one peer review is built to catch, and the site's own quality machinery — the badges, the ledger, the kill criteria — is aimed at overclaiming, so it doesn't see this. **The self-refutation statistics are the least-audited numbers on an honesty-branded site**, precisely because nobody checks a number that makes you look bad.

**Where the physics is right, it's right.** `/two-reframes` states plainly that declaring the substrate "nonlocal by construction" chooses Bell's nonlocal horn alongside Bohm rather than evading the theorem, and reports S = 1.98 for the local construction and S ≡ 2.00 for the nonlocal-grid one, with no construction reaching Tsirelson's 2√2 without signaling. Correct, and correctly damning. The page even volunteers that the CRT temporal-scanning picture is a non-contextual hidden-variable model excluded by Kochen–Specker, and that PBR applies. That is a genuinely difficult admission, stated without hedging.

`/galaxy-plotter` uses ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) for MOND — that's the standard simple interpolating function, correctly written — with a single global a₀ and no per-galaxy tuning, against a Synchronism curve that takes V_flat *as an observed input per galaxy*. The page calls the result a reparametrization violating parsimony. It's worse than that and the page could say so: because V_flat is an input, the C → 1 limit of the Synchronism curve just hands back the measured flat velocity. The model cannot predict a rotation curve; at best it interpolates between the Newtonian curve and the answer.

The dim-4 LIV entry on `/for-researchers` is now stated correctly as "a severe fine-tuning gap rather than a falsification" — the ~10⁻² one-loop coefficient against 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰ bounds is a naturalness problem, and calling it that rather than a refutation is the technically right call. ## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The site's ledger says "0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted"; the correct statement is "1 discriminating prediction, registered post-hoc, disfavored below its own kill threshold — and four tests that never discriminated against MOND at all," which is a more interesting result and a worse-run test program than the one advertised.

### Journey

I came for the novelty claim. `/for-researchers` states it cleanly: four artifacts survive, and they are offered as citable independent of whether the framework does. That page is the only thing on this site a referee needs to read, so I read it against the literature, and then went looking for whether the physics pages support it.

**Three of the four citable artifacts are prior art, and the page mostly knows it.** Artifact 1 (local-density no-go) cites Milgrom astro-ph/0510117 and Lelli et al. 2016/2017 — correct, and it *is* Milgrom's non-locality obstruction: MOND's field equations depend on the enclosed-mass acceleration g_bar, not on local volumetric ρ, and Milgrom argued in the 2000s that no local-density modification can work. Artifact 3 (dim-4 LIV) cites Collins–Perez–Sudarsky et al. PRL 93, 191301 (2004) and both known escapes. Artifact 4 (CHSH substrate S ≤ 2) is Bell's theorem: a local-hidden-variable substrate returns S ≤ 2 because that is what the theorem *proves*. Simulating it and recovering 2 is a unit test of the simulator, not a result about the substrate. Crediting "Bell's theorem (implicit); Tsirelson bound (implicit)" while listing the outcome as a citable artifact is the one place the page has it backwards — the artifact is the *absence* of a derivation of 2√2, which the page states and which is genuinely worth writing down as a negative.

So artifact 2, the A2ACW null, is the only candidate for an original contribution. It is also the site's only overclaim, and I found it by counting denominators.

**The A2ACW null is quoted at three mutually inconsistent denominators.** The landing ticker: "0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit." `/research-philosophy`: "Novel survivors after domain-expert audit: 0 of 47," with 3,308 sessions yielding ~47 internally-consistent claims (a 1.4% yield). `/for-researchers`: "**0 novel survivors after external expert audit** across 3,308 sessions." The last formulation inflates the denominator by 70× over the methodology page and 550× over the landing page. Sessions are not trials — 3,308 sessions produced 47 candidates, and 6 have been audited. The honest null is 0/6, possibly 0/47 pending audit. Written as "0 across 3,308 sessions" it implies three thousand independent adjudicated trials, which is exactly the kind of denominator laundering the site's badge machinery exists to catch. A project whose central methodological finding is *AI systems overstate what they have established* has overstated its own null by two and a half orders of magnitude on the page written for people who cite things.

And the null has no control arm and a fatal confound. There is no base rate: what fraction of 47 claims from a *human* adversarial pair on the same corpus would survive expert audit? Plausibly also zero — most research sessions in physics produce no novelty, and 47/3308 candidates over a program is not obviously below the human rate. Worse, the corpus is Synchronism itself, which the site concedes is a reparametrization of MOND. If the seed framework contains no novelty, then "0 novel survivors" is a measurement of the seed, not of adversarial AI pairing. The two hypotheses — *AI cannot generate out-of-distribution novelty* and *this particular framework had none in it* — are perfectly degenerate in this design, and the generalization on `/for-researchers` ("relevant to AI-for-science pipelines generally") is not licensed by a single-arm, single-corpus study. Citing Irving, Christiano & Amodei 2018 for debate does not supply the missing control.

**And the detector statistics are reported without specificity.** `/honest-assessment` credits the protocol with "4/6 detection of later-demoted claims through vocabulary translation alone." `/research-philosophy` reports the temporal-asymmetry variant at 0/6. Neither page reports a false-positive rate. A flag that fires on 4 of 6 true positives is worthless unless you know how often it fires on genuine results; if the vocabulary-translation test flags most claims, 4/6 is what a constant returns. The site demands operational definitions and kill criteria of its physics claims and reports its own detector as a bare sensitivity. That is the same asymmetry Pass 3 found in the arithmetic, running the other way: **the physics is over-refuted and the methodology is over-claimed.** Both errors flatter the narrative that the audit worked.

**Now the physics. Does anything here discriminate against MOND + ΛCDM?** I put the five live Tier-1 tests through the only question that matters.

TEST-02 predicts a 0.05–0.4% velocity deviation in wide binaries — i.e., Newtonian to within a part in a thousand. ΛCDM predicts Newtonian. The kill criterion is "MOND-scale anomaly (~18%) confirmed refutes C(ρ)." So the test refutes on a MOND detection and, on a null, returns the ΛCDM answer. It cannot confirm. The site's phrase "self-eliminating or tie" is exactly right and I'll credit it. What the page should also say: 0.05–0.4% is one to two orders of magnitude below the *statistical* reach of the Gaia DR3 wide-binary analyses, whose systematics are dominated by undetected close companions, eccentricity priors, and line-of-sight contamination — the residual disagreement between Chae and Banik et al. lives at the ~10% level. Framing the status as "adjudication pending literature consensus" delegates the outcome of a pre-registered test to an unresolved dispute about *someone else's* systematics. That is not a test; it is a bet on a referee. TEST-02 is unrunnable, and "unrunnable" is a status the site has used elsewhere and should use here.

TESTS 01, 03, and 05 are all environment-dependence tests: does RAR/TFR residual scatter correlate with local galaxy density or Hubble type? **MOND already predicts that it does.** The external field effect is MOND's — not ΛCDM's — signature prediction, and Chae et al. (2020, 2021) reported an EFE detection in SPARC at ~4σ. Nowhere on `/tier-1-existing`, `/key-claims`, or `/for-researchers` does the word EFE appear in connection with these tests. Three of the framework's five tests are therefore degenerate with a published MOND result, and the site — which cites Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert, and Skordis & Złośnik — does not cite Chae. **This is the largest missed connection on the site.** A positive detection in TEST-01/03/05 would be scored by any referee as support for MOND-with-EFE, and Synchronism would need to show its environmental dependence differs *in functional form* from the EFE to claim anything. No such comparison is proposed anywhere.

Which leaves TEST-04a. Growth suppression of ~12% in fσ₈ is the one prediction on this site that differs from both ΛCDM and (classic) MOND. It is also, per its own Cost/Timeline field, a "post-hoc retrodiction (adjudicated 2026-05-05)" against DESI DR1 data that were already public. A prediction registered after the measurement is a fit. The site buries this concession in a logistics field on a test card.

So the honest ledger is: **one discriminating prediction, retro-registered, disfavored at 2.4σ; four tests that were never discriminating.** The site's "0 confirmed, 1 refuted" is not just miscounted — it is the wrong taxonomy. The framework's problem is not that its predictions failed. It is that four of five could not have distinguished it from theories that already exist, which is what "reparametrization" means when you push it all the way through to the test plan. That statement is stronger, more useful to another modified-gravity worker, and absent.

**Now the over-refutation pattern, which Pass 3 opened and which extends further than they saw.**

TEST-05's registered kill criterion is *"RAR scatter independent of Hubble type/environment."* The reported result is a significant environmental effect, p = 5×10⁻⁶. **The criterion did not fire. The framework's prediction was confirmed on its own registered terms.** The site badges it "Presumptively failed" — by importing TEST-03's *effect-size* threshold (R² < 20%), which was never TEST-05's criterion. TEST-05 registered a significance test and was adjudicated against an effect-size bar borrowed from a different test on a different survey. This is the single most consequential error I found: the one Tier-1 test the framework arguably passed is on the board as a failure. (It still wouldn't have been a *discriminating* pass — see the EFE point above — but "confirmed, non-discriminating" and "presumptively failed" are not the same entry in a ledger.)

Note also that TEST-05's Prediction field reads "RAR scatter shows NP2 environment dependence (p = 5×10⁻⁶)." The predicted p-value *is the measured p-value*. Pre-registrations do not contain their own results. That field is a retro-registration, visible on its face.

**I independently confirm Pass 3's TEST-03 statistic and can now attribute it.** With r² = 0.14, r = 0.374, and N = 14,585, t = r√(N−2)/√(1−r²) = 48.7 — p is of order 10⁻⁵⁰⁰, not the 5×10⁻⁶ quoted next door on TEST-05. ### Verdict

There is one original result on this site and it is not on the list of four. Pass 3 concluded that "refuted" is the least-audited word a self-critical project ever writes. I'd sharpen it: this project audits its physics for overclaiming and its methodology for nothing at all, and the result is a site that is too hard on its equations and too easy on its own audit. The equations deserve it. The audit hasn't earned it.
