## WAKE
1. **Inherited assumptions.**
   - The topic treats "γ_fit = incumbent's γ_eq" as a rule that a blind sector can confirm or break.
   - The maintainer calls this "one of two internally runnable experiments with an unknown outcome".
   - Both assume the outcome is informative about the template whichever way it goes.
2. **If the frame is wrong.**
   - Least-squares fitting of a 2-parameter template returns the projection of the data's own curve. So "γ lands on
     the incumbent" may just say "the incumbent fits the data", a fact about the incumbent and not about the template.
   - If so, the blind test cannot make γ informative in either direction. Running it would show that, and it is cheap.
3. **Highest-information move.**
   - Run the blind sector on a precise, measured, public dataset where the textbook incumbents are known to be
     imperfect. That is the one place the rule can visibly fail.
   - Register γ_eq on the data's own grid, loss and convention, because the 11-15 work hinted that those choices move γ.
   - Close the SPARC 0.489 vs 0.595 question with the same projection logic.
4. **What would falsify the posture.**
   - A fitted γ that lands away from every incumbent *and* is stable across convention, loss and experiment. That would
     be a sector constant the template carries.
   - Instability across those choices would instead confirm that γ is a descriptor of analysis choices.

## Key Findings
- `blind-sector-he4-gamma-lands-on-no-textbook-incumbent-the-rule-is-a-projection-identity.md`:
  - **Registered verdict: AMBIGUOUS.**
    - β = 2: γ = 2.596 ± 0.060, 15.7 % (5.9σ) from Andronikashvili's γ_eq of 2.243.
    - β free: γ = 0.753, 18.4 % from London's 0.636, a coincidental proximity (London misses the data by 0.35 rms).
    - Landau's excitation gas is out of span: it never vanishes at T_λ.
  - **The rule is a projection identity.** With the data-describing curve as the incumbent, it lands within 2 %
    (0.737 vs 0.753). γ_fit is the γ of whatever curve the data follow.
  - **γ is not a sector constant here.** It runs 0.44–4.83 across the knee convention and the loss. The two
    experiments give 0.60 and 0.95.
  - **The template fails the shape.**
    - Its rms is 4.1× that of the same-size rival (1 − t^a)^b and 12× the duplicate-row scatter.
    - Cause: the onset is locked to (1 − t)^1 for every γ and β, while the data go as about (1 − t)^0.7.
  - **SPARC 0.489 vs 0.595: not grid, not loss, not the error cut.**
    - The RAR's γ_eq on SPARC's own points with the frozen loss is 0.587.
    - The gap is the data's ±0.02 dex residual wiggle about the RAR. Both curves fit to the same rms (Δχ² 0.83 at point
      level).
