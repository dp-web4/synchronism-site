## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The marching-band explanation of coherence is genuinely great and I'd tell a friend about it, but by page three I'm being told the whole thing failed and I never found out what I was supposed to be excited about in the first place.

### Journey

I clicked this from Reddit because the headline is a good hook: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* That's a Veritasium-video-title kind of question and I wanted the answer.

Then about one scroll down, there it is:

**C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**

I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is (well — logarithm, I've heard of it, but I couldn't tell you what it does). The site does put a little key underneath — "tanh (S-curve function)", "ln (logarithmic compression)" — but "logarithmic compression" is explaining a thing I don't know with two more things I don't know. My honest reaction was to scroll past the equation, which is probably not what you want, since it's the whole point of the site.

Below that is a scoreboard:
- Prospective predictions confirmed: **0**
- Refutations executed: **6**
- "Validated" surviving audit: **0 of 6**

I want to say something careful here, because I think the site is being admirably honest and I don't want to punish that. But as a first-time visitor, the emotional arc of the landing page is: *big exciting question → equation I can't read → a scoreboard of zeros.* At no point did anyone tell me what's still cool about this. I nearly closed the tab. What kept me was curiosity about **why anyone would build a whole website to say their idea didn't work** — which is actually interesting! But that's an accident of my personality, not something the page did for me.

I clicked **Beginner Path** since it's explicitly labeled for non-physicists. Step 1 of 6. And in Step 1 — the *first page of the beginner path* — I hit:

> galaxy tests "collapse to MOND"
> Galaxy rotation curves matched a₀ = cH₀/(2π) within 13%, but this is "shared with MOND and other frameworks"

I do not know what MOND is. It is not explained here. It is mentioned four times on the beginner path's first page. Also on that same first page: "Superconductivity (YBCO): predicted 607K vs. actual 93K" and "refuted by execution with r²=0.0001". This is step **one**. Of the **beginner** path.

**Step 2, First Encounter, is the best page on the site.** Seriously. "A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence." Then: "A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy — it only responds to what's immediately around it." I got that instantly. I could explain it to someone at a bar. This is what the landing page should be doing.

But it's short, and then Step 3 (Core Idea) hands me **γ = 2/√Ncorr** where Ncorr is "the number of correlated particle units" (I don't know what a correlated particle unit is), and tells me the tanh "is mathematically equivalent to a Hill-type saturation function," which is a sentence I can only nod at.

I tried the **Coherence Explorer**, which I liked. Sliders, curve moves, that's a good way to learn something. But I got stuck on a small thing: the chart draws a labeled reference line at **C = 0.50**, and the readout right underneath says **C(ρcrit) = 0.3272**. ρcrit is described as the "saturation knee" — the special density where the interesting thing happens. So why is the special line drawn at 0.50, and the special density lands at 0.33? Those are two different "middles" and I can't tell which one is the one that matters.

Then **Equation Anatomy**, which finally cracked tanh for me: *"like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1."* That's it. That's the sentence. It should be on the landing page next to the equation instead of "S-curve function." Two problems though: it says tanh maps to **(−1, +1)** while every other page insists coherence runs **0 to 1**, and I couldn't work out whether that's a contradiction or something I'm too dumb to see. And Step 1 of this beginner walkthrough contains a parenthetical admitting that the ontology pages define ρ differently than the tests do. I appreciate the honesty but I hadn't even finished learning the *first* definition and I'm already being told there are two.

I clicked **See What Failed** (the honest-assessment page). I bounced. BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, SPARC, Ω_m, "growth suppression," "the framework's built-in ceiling on how much it can boost gravity." There are little "In plain terms" boxes which I appreciate, but one of them explains that "the heavier a galaxy, the faster its outer edge spins" — and I don't know why a galaxy's outer edge spinning at *any* particular speed would surprise anybody. **Nobody on this site ever told me what the dark matter puzzle actually is.** The whole site assumes I already find the galaxy thing mysterious.

The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** was fun to click, and I picked DDO 154. But the legend has five entries and two of them are Synchronism — one called "REAL C(ρ), γ=2" and one called "Illustration only — hand-tuned reference curve (not theory-derived)." So there's a fake line on the chart? I *think* the point is "here's what it would need to do vs. what it actually does," which is a great point, but the labels don't say that — I had to guess. And the readout says "(max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on)" and I genuinely could not tell whether 0.001 was supposed to be impressive or damning until I reasoned it out from the marching-band thing (0 = crowd in a plaza, so almost nothing is happening).

Then **Consciousness Demo**, which I clicked because of course I did. And this one I actually *got*, and it's the smartest thing on the site: eight different ways of estimating a consciousness threshold all land at C ≈ 0.50, and then the page tells you that's meaningless — because 0.50 is just the middle of a scale that runs 0 to 1, so anything that picks "the middle" gets 0.50 by definition. That's a real lesson about how science fools itself and I'll remember it. It's buried under a correction notice with "t = 20.19, p ≈ 1.8×10⁻⁷" in it, which I skipped.

The **Glossary** is good. Short, plain sentences. "MRH: an 'event horizon for influence' — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter." That's a real definition I can use. It should be linked from every scary word on every page, and as far as I could tell it isn't.

Last, I read **What Synchronism Is Not**, which is — weirdly — the most readable page on the whole site and the one that finally told me what I was looking at. It says plainly: this is an *interpretation* and a *methodology*, not new physics, and that's still a legitimate thing to be. Good! That's an answer!

And then it says this, about itself:

> "That's a real contribution — just not the one the landing page implies."

The site is telling me the landing page oversold it. I agree with the site. I just wish the site had told me on the landing page instead of on page thirteen.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — Sliders are the right idea and the curve responds immediately; this is the single best on-ramp to the equation on the site. Snagged on the C = 0.50 reference line vs. the C(ρcrit) = 0.3272 readout, which look like they should agree and don't.
- **Equation Anatomy (/equation-walkthrough)** — worked — intuitive — The "dimmer switch" line is the one thing that made `tanh` click for me. Six steps, clear pacing, "ln(1000) ≈ 6.9" was a concrete example I could hold onto. Undercut by the (−1, +1) vs 0-to-1 mismatch and an errata note inside step 1.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — confusing — Picking a real galaxy is satisfying and the curves clearly separate. But five legend entries with two Synchronism lines (one of them explicitly not from the theory) is too much for a page badged "Beginner," and the "max C = 0.001" readout needs a plain-language verdict attached.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — confusing at first, then excellent — The eight converging approaches look impressive, and then the page dismantles its own picture: 0.50 is just the midpoint of the scale, so the convergence is geometry, not evidence. Best conceptual payoff on the site. Nearly buried under a correction notice full of t-statistics.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What is dark matter, and why is a galaxy's rotation a problem in the first place? I read four pages about the framework failing to explain it and still don't know what "it" is.
2. What is MOND? It's the yardstick everything gets measured against, it's on the beginner path's first page, and I never got a sentence explaining it.
3. If the site's own page says the landing page overpromises — why is the landing page still promising that?
4. Is there anything here that's currently *working*? The chemistry r = 0.982 gets mentioned and then immediately deflated ("polynomial models perform comparably"). I couldn't find one thing I was allowed to be impressed by.
5. What actually is a "correlated particle unit," and how would anyone count them?
6. If coherence is explicitly *not* quantum coherence (superconductors score low, which surprised me), why is it called coherence?

### Verdict

There is a genuinely great popular-science explainer hiding in this site — the marching band, the Andromeda-outside-the-atom's-bubble line, the dimmer switch, the consciousness demo's "you found the middle of your own scale" lesson. Those four things are better than most of what I get from YouTube. But they're scattered across four different pages and surrounded by acronyms, session numbers, and correction notices from a self-audit that clearly isn't written for me.

The site's honesty is the reason I'd come back, not the reason I'd bounce — I've never seen a physics site tell me its own front page oversells it. But right now the front page hooks a curious person with a big question and then hands them an unreadable equation and a scoreboard of zeros, without ever explaining why the question was worth asking. A casual reader needs to be told what the mystery *is* before being told how the answer failed.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I can't judge the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The navigation skeleton is genuinely well-built — breadcrumbs work everywhere, the glossary is excellent, and the landing page links its badge legend — but the site is running **four competing status vocabularies across three legends with no single source of truth**, and the term in its most prominent headline number isn't defined anywhere.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a release: walk the nav, check every legend against its usages, verify counts agree across pages, and see whether a reader can resolve every term without leaving the page they're on.

**What works, stated up front, because it's rare.** Breadcrumbs are present and correct on all 14 pages I opened — `Home / Cosmology / MOND Unification`, `Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1: Existing Data`. The section tier is consistent. The glossary carries 72 terms with genuinely usable one-line definitions and `Learn more →` links back out to the pages that use them. And the landing page does something most sites skip: before showing any status chips it says *"Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed"* with a link to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`. That's the right pattern. Badge legend linked before first use, on the entry page. Credit where it's due.

Which is exactly why the rest of it is frustrating — the discipline is there on the front door and nowhere behind it.

**The badge system is four systems.** `/honest-assessment` presents itself as canonical: two families, nine active tags (`Active-MRH`, `Parallel-Paths`, `Sidelined`, `Superseded`, `Audited-Negative`, `Untested`, `Speculative`, `Reparametrization`, `Failed`) plus two deprecated. `/key-claims` points to it explicitly as "canonical reference." Good.

Then `/tier-1` publishes **its own legend, also titled "Status badge definitions,"** with eight entries — five overlapping the canonical set, and three that appear in no canonical list at all: `MOND-shared` (annotated "RETIRED"), `Kill Criterion Triggered`, and `Withdrawn`, the last two labelled "Operational state," a category the canonical page doesn't have.

And then the table on that same page uses labels that appear in *neither* legend:

> `Self-Eliminating-or-Tie` · `Substituted-Protocol Kill` · `Disfavored on σ₈` · `Underpowered as Registered` · `Demoted to Tier-2` · `Failed; Ceiling Exceeded` · `Failed; Empty Intersection` · `Closed by Execution`

Some are compounds of a real badge plus a free-text rider; some aren't. There's no rule stated for which is which, so a reader can't tell whether `Underpowered as Registered` is a formal verdict or an author's aside.

`/for-researchers` then introduces a fourth outcome word — *"unrunnable as stated (third category beyond refuted/untested)"* — presented as a genuine third category. It's not in any legend.

`/tools` uses a fourth vocabulary entirely: every tool carries a **Status** of `Model Explainer`, `Reparametrization`, or `Speculative`. Two of those three are canonical badges; `Model Explainer` is in no legend and — I checked — **not in the 72-term glossary either**. Neither is `Artifact Lesson`.

**`Artifact Lesson` is used as two different kinds of thing on the same card.** On `/tools`, the γ Calculator reads Status: `Model Explainer`, Level: `Artifact Lesson`. The other three levels are `Beginner`, `Beginner`, `Advanced`. So the Level column mixes an audience-difficulty scale with a content-genre label. Three tools have `Artifact Lesson` in the Level slot, meaning a reader filtering for "Beginner" tools silently loses them. This is a schema problem, not a wording one.

**A page that contradicts its own count, twice, in one scroll.** `/tier-1` opens with:

> "4 executed — 3 failed, 1 substituted-protocol kill; 1 withdrawn; rest untested."

mid-page says *"Effective independent tests after failures and withdrawals: approximately 4,"* and closes with:

> "6 refutations executed on external data (RAR shape, BTFR slope, dwarf DM fractions, environment null, Cassini/SPARC squeeze, Bell/CHSH substrate — recounted 2026-07-30)"

I assume "executed tests" and "refutations" are different units and both numbers are correct. **The page never says so.** It states 4 at the top and 6 at the bottom with nothing in between reconciling them, and 6 is the number the landing page, the footer, and `/for-researchers` all headline. A reader who scans the top of the ledger page walks away with a different number than a reader who scans the bottom of it. One sentence — "these count different things: N tests were executed; those executions plus M non-Tier-1 results yield 6 refutations" — fixes this permanently.

**The ledger's ID scheme leaks rows.** `/tier-1` lists 13 entries. Two of them break the `TEST-NN` pattern: `TEST-04a` (a sub-ID) and `CLUSTER-SCALE` (no ID at all). `/test-roadmap` then describes the inventory as *"TEST-01 through TEST-11"* — which is what you get if you count IDs rather than rows, and it drops both irregular entries. Any inventory keyed by ID where some rows have no ID will be miscounted by anyone counting IDs, and the site has already done it once. Either give every row an ID or stop describing the set by ID range.

**"Prospective prediction" is undefined.** The very first line of the Scientific Outcomes scoreboard is *"Prospective predictions confirmed: 0."* Line three is *"Post-hoc retrodictions attempted: 1 — underpowered."* The whole scoreboard turns on the prospective/post-hoc distinction — that's the difference between "we predicted it then measured" and "we fitted it after." Neither `prospective prediction` nor `post-hoc retrodiction` is in the glossary. I checked both. The site's single most-cited number depends on a term of art it never defines, and a reader who doesn't already know the distinction will read line one as "nothing works" rather than the much more specific and more defensible claim it's actually making.

**Beginner Path step 1: eleven acronyms, zero expanded.** MOND, BEC, BCS, YBCO, SPARC, ALFALFA, SDSS, BTFR, DM, RAR, CHSH — none expanded on the page, none linked to the glossary, on step 1 of 6 of the path explicitly labelled for newcomers. Pass 1 hit this too. What I'd add from the structural side: **the glossary already has almost all of them, with good definitions.** This isn't a content gap, it's a linking gap, which makes it much cheaper to fix than it looks. Auto-linking glossary terms on first use per page would close most of Pass 1's complaints and most of mine in one change.

**Learning paths route through retracted pages without warning.** The Advanced path is listed as *"1. Parameter Derivations 2. Compression Action 3. MOND Unification 4. CDM Discrimination 5. Superconductivity (η) 6. Born Rule Derivation 7. Falsifiability 8. Test Roadmap (24 experiments)."* Step 4 opens with the banner **"Superseded — Original 'Below CDM' Claim Retracted In-Archive."** Step 3, `/mond-unification`, has no top banner at all — instead its subtitle does badge work in prose: *"Reparametrization — Dimensional Analysis — 13% Error."* So within two consecutive steps of one curriculum: one page banners its status, the next encodes it in a subtitle. And the path listing itself shows no status for any step. A reader following the numbered sequence walks into a retracted page with no advance notice. Step listings should carry the destination's badge.

**Eight paths, two taxonomies, no map.** `/learning-paths` offers Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced *and* Physics/Chemistry/Philosophy/Methodology, and to its credit says outright *"The two groupings are not equivalent."* That's honest, but it's a note where a reader needs a table. Pages clearly appear in multiple paths and there's no indication of which, nor any way to tell from a page whether you've already covered it in another track. The paths also don't link to each other — you go back through the menu.

**Same page, two URLs.** `/tier-1` and `/tier-1-existing` both serve "Tier 1: Existing Data." Internal links use `/tier-1-existing`. Pick one canonical path.

**No page-level freshness date.** The landing page has no "last updated," but the body carries inline correction dates spanning 2026-07-14, 2026-07-30, and 2026-08. For a site whose entire value proposition is *"we correct ourselves in public,"* the absence of a visible revision date on each page is a strange omission — it's the one metadata field that would make the honesty legible at a glance.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — **intuitive, and unusually well-written** — Breadcrumb correct. `N_corr` is defined right where it's used: *"the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units. For a single atom, Ncorr = 1."* Stating that it's dimensionless is exactly the detail most docs skip. γ is glossed in plain language too. The instructional move is the best thing I saw on the site: *"Why is a formula we know is wrong still interactive? So you can see the flaw yourself: click the 'Ideal gas' preset, then 'BCS superconductor,' and notice the far more collective system gets the far flatter curve."* That's show-don't-tell in a docs context and it works — I don't know the physics and I could still see the inversion. The one structural gap: four numbered caveats in prose at the top, but no status badge, so the page's own vocabulary never says what `/tools` says about it.

- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary`) — worked — **intuitive** — Opens with *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose,"* which is the right first sentence. Two terms are disambiguated on the page, both of which I'd have gotten wrong: "phase boundary" is clarified as a regime boundary along the γ axis, *not* a thermodynamic phase transition; "sign inversion" is spelled out as strongly correlated systems getting the flattest curves. Defining a term by saying what it is *not* is the correct call when the name is borrowed from a field with an established meaning. Same gap as above — prose disclaimer, no badge. Also: `ρ_crit` appears in the tool but isn't defined on the page and isn't linked to the glossary entry that defines it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which legend is authoritative — `/honest-assessment` or `/tier-1`? Both call themselves "Status badge definitions" and they don't agree.
2. Are `Model Explainer`, `Artifact Lesson`, and `unrunnable as stated` formal categories or editorial prose? They're styled like badges and defined like neither.
3. Is the ledger 4 or 6? I believe both, of different things, but no page on the site says that.
4. What is a "prospective prediction"? The whole scoreboard rests on it and the glossary doesn't have it.
5. In a chip like "Speculative untestable as stated — ontological reframe New Ontology," where does the badge end and the commentary begin?
6. Is `Withdrawn` a badge? It's in `/tier-1`'s legend and on the landing scoreboard, but not in the canonical taxonomy.

### Verdict

The information architecture is better than the site gives itself credit for — breadcrumbs are consistent, the glossary is a real asset, and the landing page links its badge legend before first use, which most documentation sets never manage. The problems are almost entirely **downstream drift**: a taxonomy that was defined once, canonically, and then re-defined locally on `/tier-1`, extended informally on `/tools`, and appended to freely on `/key-claims` until four vocabularies were in play. That's the classic failure mode of a docs set with no owned single source of truth, and it's fixable without writing new content — one canonical legend, transcluded, with every local variant deleted or promoted into it.

**The tool arithmetic is exactly right.** I checked the Coherence Explorer against hand calculation at its default γ = 0.49: C(ρcrit) = tanh(0.49·ln 2) = 0.32717 vs. displayed 0.3272; C(10ρcrit) = 0.82580 vs. 0.8259; C(100ρcrit) = 0.97852 vs. 0.9785; and the stated C = 0.5 crossing at 2.07·ρcrit is 2.0684. Four for four. Whoever wrote the plotting code was not cutting corners, and the tool correctly refuses to put the knee at the midpoint.

**But the tanh is decorative, and the site doesn't say so.** Write x = ρ/ρcrit. Then

> tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^2γ − 1] / [(1+x)^2γ + 1]

identically — the hyperbolic tangent of a logarithm is algebraic. Verified: at γ = 0.49, x = 1, (2^0.98 − 1)/(2^0.98 + 1) = 0.32721, same number the tool prints. So the entire one-parameter family is a Möbius transform of a power law, controlled by nothing but the return exponent **q = 2γ**. Asymptotics: C ≈ γx for x ≪ 1, and C → 1 − 2(1+x)^−q for x ≫ 1.

This matters because `/parameter-derivations` defends the choice as *"a phenomenological choice from the sigmoid/compander family (μ-law, Hill, logistic, erf),"* implying the family is degenerate and the choice harmless. It isn't. Logistic and erf approach saturation **exponentially**; this function approaches it as a **power law**. Those are different physics, and the power is exactly what an RAR transition-shape fit measures. The defense is wrong in the one direction that would have mattered — the compander is far *more* constrained than the page admits, and SPARC is measuring q directly.

Which brings me to the best thing on the site. At γ = 1/2, q = 1 and the function collapses to C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) — MOND's simple interpolating function exactly, with the argument rescaled. **1. The DESI test is over-refuted, and the site cites the paper that shows it.**

Two problems, both standard in this subfield:

- **The σ₈ number is model-dependent in exactly the direction being tested.** DESI's full-shape σ₈ is inferred inside a ΛCDM growth history. Using it to adjudicate a modified-growth framework is circular — you have to refit, not compare. DESI's own analysis notes σ₈ is essentially unconstrained by full-shape alone without bispectrum information to break the growth–amplitude degeneracy. The registered observable, fσ₈, is the less model-dependent one, and it gives 1.5σ, which is no verdict at all.
- **The field has a currency for exactly this claim and the site doesn't use it.** A 12% growth suppression maps onto the (μ₀, Σ₀) parametrization. DESI's modified-gravity full-shape paper (arXiv:2411.12026, = Ishak et al., JCAP 09 (2025) 053) reports **μ₀ = 0.05 ± 0.22** for FS+BAO+CMB+SN. A 12% suppression is roughly μ₀ ~ −0.2 to −0.3 — *inside DESI's 1σ*. In the parametrization the field actually uses, TEST-04a is a null.

The site **cites Ishak et al. on `/for-researchers` and never uses its constraint.** That's the sharpest single fix available: convert the growth prediction to μ₀ and quote DESI's own bound. The honest verdict is "consistent with DESI in the μ₀–Σ₀ plane; the framework has no independent σ₈ chain to compare," not "Failed."

Also: everything here is keyed to DR1. DR2 BAO landed in 2025 and DR2 full-shape has followed. A ledger whose only cosmology row is a DR1 comparison is already a release behind.

**2. The growth prediction is not derivable from the framework, which is a stronger statement than "underpowered."**

`/honest-assessment` states plainly: *"no Lagrangian, no covariant formulation, no momentum conservation, and no lensing predictions derived from first principles."* `/cosmology` states fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418 and gives **no derivation**.

Growth requires a perturbation equation — δ̈ + 2Hδ̇ − 4πG μ(a,k) ρ̄ δ = 0. Without μ(a,k) you cannot produce an fσ₈, full stop. So the 0.418 did not come from the framework. `/test-catalog` calls TEST-04a *"first prospective pre-registration"* — meaning the project's single prospective registration pre-registered a number its own theory cannot generate. That's a much more informative failure than "underpowered as registered," and it belongs in the status field: **not derivable**.

**3. The Cassini kill is prior art, and it is a *consequence* of EFE = 0 rather than an independent refutation.**

`/galaxy-plotter` contains this, in its own caveats: *"MOND (simple-ν) used here is excluded by Cassini at +17.7–18.0σ; the RAR test uses McGaugh's exponential-return ν — two different functions, only one solar-system safe."*

That sentence is the whole of TEST-11, and it is a **known result about interpolating-function families**, not about Synchronism. Solar-system ephemeris exclusion of the simple/ν family is Blanchet & Novak (2011, MNRAS 412, 2530) and Hees, Famaey, Angus & Bertone (2016, MNRAS 455, 449); it is why MOND practitioners moved to functions with faster Newtonian return a decade ago. The site is scrupulous about prior art everywhere else — Milgrom 1983, Freeman 1970, Breit–Wigner, Abrikosov–Gor'kov, Palma–Suominen–Ekert. This is the one refutation with no citation, and it is the biggest σ on the board.

But there's a second, better point buried underneath. **MOND's escape from that constraint runs partly through the external field effect** — the Galactic field sets the solar system's regime. Synchronism forbids itself that escape, because `/mond-unification` says: *"The framework predicts EFE = 0 structurally because C is a function of local matter density ρ alone. A uniform external gravitational field does not change ρ."*

So TEST-11 is not independent of the EFE question. It is *downstream* of EFE = 0. The site presents them on different pages with no link. Stating the dependency turns a rediscovered constraint into a genuine structural result: **the framework inherits MOND's solar-system tension while surrendering MOND's resolution of it.** That is worth a paragraph, and it doesn't exist anywhere on the site.

**4. EFE = 0 is the only discriminating prediction the framework has, and it has no TEST ID.**

This is my headline finding. Line it up:

- It follows from the framework's *defining* move (local ρ instead of non-local g_bar) — not from a tuned parameter.
- It differs from MOND. `/mond-unification` says so itself: *"This differs from MOND's observed ~4σ EFE detection."*
- It is testable on existing data (SPARC + environment catalogues), which is the tier the project claims to specialize in.
- Its failure mode is asymmetric and clean.

And: it appears in **no ledger row**. Not in `/tier-1-existing` (11 IDs + TEST-04a + CLUSTER-SCALE), not in `/test-catalog`'s 24, not among the six refutations, not in the "0 of 24 could select Synchronism" tally.

Which means that tally is **an artifact of the ID scheme, not a property of the framework.** The site's own statement — *"0 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM"* — is computed over tests that have IDs. The one candidate that could was never assigned one, so it was never counted. Pass 2 found the same mechanism structurally (rows without IDs are invisible to ID-keyed audits); here it has eaten the single most consequential claim on the site.

I'd add that `/mond-unification` already gestures at the operationalization — *"satellites at identical external acceleration but different host gas content would behave differently here versus under MOND"* — and then drops it. That's a registrable protocol. **Register it, number it, state a threshold.** If it fires against the framework, the ledger gains its first genuinely discriminating row rather than another convention-dependent one. If it can't be run, say why in the ledger, where the "0 discriminating" claim is made.

**5. The A2ACW null is over-claimed, and the statistics say so.**

`/for-researchers`: *"3,308 sessions produced ~47 internally-consistent candidate claims (1.4% survival rate); of those, 6 received external expert audit, and 0 survived."*

Three problems:

- **Rule of three.** Zero events in 6 trials gives an upper 95% bound of 3/6 = 0.5. The 0/6 result is statistically consistent with **up to ~23 of the 47 being novel.** The site uses 0/6 to underwrite a corpus-wide "0 novel," which the sample cannot support. This is the site over-refuting itself, and it's the one place where being wrong in that direction is still being wrong.
- **The 6 were not randomly drawn.** They're the ones an external expert found tractable to audit — which correlates with being close to known physics, i.e. with being demotable. Unstated selection bias, pushing the estimate down.
- **The confusion matrix has an empty cell with a number in it.** *"Youden's J = 0 (sensitivity = 1.0, specificity = 0/6, both measured)."* J = Se + Sp − 1 is right arithmetic. But by the site's own account the audited set contains **no positive-class instances** (0 survived) — so sensitivity has no denominator and cannot have been measured there. If Se = 1.0 was measured on a *different* set of six (e.g. known historical discoveries used as controls), then two distinct 6-item sets are both being called "6" in adjacent sentences, and no page distinguishes them. Either way, "both measured" is not supportable as written.

The honest version is stronger and shorter: *"0 of 6 audited; binomial upper bound 50%; non-random sample; the remaining 41 are unaudited, not refuted."* That is a real registered null with real limits. What's there now reads as a harsher result than the data delivers.

**6. The Bell/CHSH row is a theorem, not a refutation.**

`/for-researchers` says it outright: *"This is pedagogy plus a registered bet honestly lost, not novelty. S ≤ 2 was the guaranteed outcome."* Correct — the construction was local, so S ≤ 2 was Bell's theorem, not a measurement. It is nonetheless counted as item 6 of the six refutations on the landing page, the footer, and `/honest-assessment`. **An outcome guaranteed by a theorem is not an empirical refutation** and inflating the count with it undercuts the five that are real.

Combining with Pass 3's independence point, here is what the six actually are:

**7. The scale-dependence problem outranks every empirical result on the site, and it's filed as item 3 of 8 in a parameter audit.**

`/parameter-derivations` states it: *"A ∝ 1/ℓ² means the framework never specifies the smoothing scale ℓ where ρ is defined,"* and *"the galaxy-sector knee verdict flips with ℓ — NGC 3198 ranges from 1.4×10⁻³ (A = 0.029) to 0.91 (A = 4.6×10⁻⁵)."*

Take that seriously and it is not a parameter-provenance note — it is a **well-posedness failure**. If C depends on the smoothing scale at which ρ is evaluated, and ℓ is not part of the theory, then C is not an observable, and no test of C is well-defined. In a structured ISM, ρ at ℓ = 1 pc and ρ at ℓ = 300 pc differ by four to six orders of magnitude in the same location. Every density-keyed row in the ledger — RAR shape, the boost ceiling, the environment null, the Cassini squeeze — quotes a ρ at an undeclared scale.

This also supplies the *reason* for something the site states but doesn't explain. `/galaxy-rotation` notes that screened scalar fields (Burrage, Copeland & Millington 2017) escape the no-go because they use the gradient of the density-functional rather than an algebraic factor. The deeper reason is that **a field equation supplies ℓ dynamically** — the scalar's Compton wavelength in the ambient density sets the smoothing scale, so ρ enters at a scale the theory determines rather than one the modeller picks. An algebraic C(ρ)·g has no such mechanism and cannot acquire one without becoming a field theory. That's the constructive statement, it generalizes past this framework, and it is the most citable thing on the site that isn't currently written down.

I'd also note the ℓ ≈ 317 pc figure is not an independent inference: `/galaxy-plotter` hard-codes exponential-disk scale height **h = 0.3 kpc** and computes ρ = Σ/2h. ℓ ≈ 317 pc is that input read back out. Presenting it as a derived diagnosis of the 644× is circular.

**8. The nested-submodel argument is the best result on the site and it's on one page.**

*"The galaxy sector is MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17} — a strict submodel of MOND. A nested submodel has exactly two possible outcomes: statistically indistinguishable or refuted. It cannot win."*

That is correct, it is available **a priori with no data**, and it logically supersedes TEST-09 and TEST-10 — those executions could not have produced any other class of outcome. It should be the lead of `/honest-assessment`, not a mid-page note on `/for-researchers`. As a methodological result it generalizes: *before running tests on a modification, check whether its parameter space is nested inside the incumbent's.* That's genuinely useful advice and nobody in this literature states it crisply.

**9. Wide binaries: two incompatible statuses, and the literature will not rescue either.**

`/tier-1-existing` gives TEST-02 as *"Adjudicable/Non-selecting — Awaiting published literature resolution."* `/falsifiability` says the criterion **cannot fire**: signal predicted at 0.05–0.4%, *"roughly 80× below Gaia DR3 systematic reach."* If it cannot fire, no literature resolution adjudicates it. These are contradictory statuses on the same test.

On the substance: the Gaia wide-binary dispute has not converged and shows no sign of doing so — Chae's confirmations continue through 2026 (e.g. arXiv:2607.14450), against Banik et al., Pittordis & Sutherland, Saglia et al. (2025), and a 2026 "no evidence for MOND" quality-framework analysis, with methodological rebuttals in both directions. "Awaiting resolution" is an indefinite hold, not a status. Separately, the *registered* prediction is density-dependence of the anomaly — which none of those papers measures; they all measure anomaly vs separation. The registered test has never been run by anyone and would not be settled by the dispute resolving either way. Say that.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — **the MOND implementation is correct; the parsimony annotation is not** — I checked the comparison curve rather than the framework curve, since that's what determines whether the chart is a fair test. ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) with y = g_N/a₀ is the standard simple-ν: y → ∞ gives ν → 1 (Newtonian) and y → 0 gives ν → y^(−1/2) ⇒ g → √(g_N a₀) (deep-MOND). Both limits correct, and v = v_b√ν follows properly from g = ν g_N. The single global a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ across all galaxies is the right way to run the comparison. The Newtonian baseline caveat is also correctly *signed*: a spherical enclosed-mass approximation underestimates a thin exponential disk near the peak (r ≈ 2.2 R_d), so it exaggerates the gap, which is what the page says. That's a level of self-criticism I don't often see in a demo. Three problems: (1) the parsimony note claims one free ρcrit per galaxy, which contradicts the page's own ΔBIC = +7 by ~200× (see friction log); (2) constant h = 0.3 kpc across a 5× range in V_flat is doing unflagged work in the density scaling; (3) the page's own best sentence — that the simple-ν MOND curve it plots is itself Cassini-excluded at 17.7–18.0σ — is a caveat about *MOND*, sitting one line away from the framework refutation that reuses the same number, with no note that they're the same physics.

- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — **physics correctly represented, including its own refutation** — γ = 2/√N_corr with the regime bands, and the numbers are internally consistent: BCS at N_corr = 10⁷ gives γ = 6.3×10⁻⁴ ✓; the SPARC fit γ = 0.49 gives N_corr = (2/0.49)² = 16.7 ≈ 17 ✓. The tool states the sign inversion explicitly — *"real BCS/BEC transitions are among the sharpest in nature, yet the formula files them at the flat end"* — which is the correct physics objection and the correct verdict. The best line on the page is the one about counting: *"γ = 2/√N_corr encodes the counting convention used for N_corr, not a physical invariant."* That's right and it's more general than the page realizes — N_corr for a superconductor could equally be pairs, coherence-volume electrons, or condensate modes, spanning several decades, and no convention is privileged. Worth stating that the ansatz is not merely sign-inverted but **not well-defined**, which is the same class of failure as the unspecified ℓ in the density sector. Two failures, one root: the framework's state variables are specified without the scheme that makes them numbers.

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`, spot-checked) — worked — the ideal-gas vs BCS preset contrast is a genuinely good way to make an audited-negative result visible by clicking. Same missing note: N_corr's counting convention, not just its exponent, is undefined.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Why is EFE = 0 not a numbered test?** It's structural, it differs from MOND, it's testable on existing data, and it makes the "0 of 24 could select Synchronism" claim false as stated — the candidate was never enumerated.
2. **What is the framework's μ(a,k)?** Without it, fσ₈ = 0.418 has no provenance, and TEST-04a — the project's only prospective registration — tested a number the theory cannot produce.
3. **What is ℓ?** Until the smoothing scale is part of the theory, C(ρ) isn't an observable and none of the density-keyed refutations are well-posed. What does the framework say ρ is in interplanetary space, at what scale, and does the Cassini σ survive the answer?
4. **Was the Cassini result checked against Blanchet & Novak (2011) / Hees et al. (2016)?** If not, one of the six refutations is a rediscovery; if so, the citation is missing from the ledger.
5. **How were the 6 audited claims selected from the 47?** If by auditability, the 0/6 is biased low, and the corpus-wide "0 novel" is not supported by a sample whose upper bound is 50%.
6. **Which 6 does "0 of 6" refer to?** `/honest-assessment` says 6 audited Synchronism claims; `/for-researchers` computes a sensitivity that requires a positive class the audited set doesn't contain.
7. **Is there a confidence interval on γ against 0.5?** Pass 3 asked this and it's the right question: if the SPARC posterior on γ contains 0.5, the site can state that the data cannot distinguish the framework from MOND's simple μ *at any significance* — a cleaner claim than "0 discriminating tests."

### Verdict

On the question I came to answer: **there is no new physics here, the site says so, and it is correct.** Every quantitative result I checked is a reparametrization, a known coincidence, or a failure — and the attributions are, with one exception, right. I would not send this to a journal as physics and neither would the authors, which is more than I can say for most of what crosses my desk in this genre.

What I did not expect is that the ledger is **wrong in both directions**, and that the errors are concentrated exactly where the project's credibility lives. It over-refutes on DESI while citing the paper that shows the row is a null in the field's own μ₀ parametrization. It over-reads 0/6 into a corpus-wide zero that the binomial doesn't support. It counts a Bell-theorem-guaranteed outcome among six empirical refutations. It rediscovers a decade-old Cassini constraint on the simple-ν family without citing it — and misses that the interesting part isn't the constraint but that EFE = 0 forecloses MOND's escape from it. And it under-claims the one structurally discriminating prediction it has, because that prediction never got a TEST ID and the ledger is keyed to IDs.

There are two citable artifacts here and neither is what the site leads with. The first is the **well-posedness result**: an algebraic local-density coupling has no mechanism to supply the smoothing scale at which its own state variable is defined, which is why field-theoretic screening escapes the no-go and C(ρ)·g cannot. The second is the **nested-submodel argument** — MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17} could only tie or lose, knowable a priori, no data required. Both are methodological, both generalize past this framework, and both are currently filed below results that are weaker. Promote them, fix the six numbered items above, register EFE = 0, and this becomes a short paper I'd referee favourably as a constraints-and-methodology note. As a physics program it's finished, and the site already knows that.
