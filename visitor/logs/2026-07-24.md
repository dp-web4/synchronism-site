## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /learning-paths, /first-encounter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /consciousness-demo, /honest-assessment, /core-idea)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most refreshingly honest science site I've ever stumbled onto from Reddit, but the honesty gets so technical halfway through the beginner path that I couldn't tell how bad the bad news actually was.

### Journey
The landing page hooked me instantly — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of question I click on. Then right underneath: "0 confirmed predictions; 4 refutations executed on external data." Wait, what? That's like a movie poster that says "critics hated it." Weirdly, it made me trust the site more, but it also confused me: is this a cool idea or a dead idea? I genuinely couldn't tell from the landing page, and I still wasn't 100% sure by the end.

"Start Here" (/why-synchronism) was good. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" — okay, I've heard Veritasium say something like that. The marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza analogy is great; I got it immediately. The big equation showed up with a decent walkthrough ("tanh is an S-shaped saturation function" — fine, I'll take your word for it). But words like "phenomenological" and "compander" appeared with no translation, and the test-results section suddenly assumed I knew what MOND is. It kept coming up everywhere and I never found a plain sentence saying what it is — I gathered it's some rival theory about galaxies?

The First Encounter (7 pages, ~90 seconds each) was the best-paced thing on the site. "The bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" for MRH actually stuck with me. The neutron star density comparison (10¹⁴× denser than lead) is very YouTube-thumbnail-friendly, in a good way.

The Learning Paths page was inviting — "No physics background needed" is exactly what I want to see. But I noticed the beginner path's step 5 is the Honest Assessment page, and when I got there it flattened me. SPARC galaxies, BTFR slope, dwarf DM fractions, "fired the pre-stated kill criterion at 3.3σ" — I have no idea what any of that means. I could tell it was saying "we failed and here's exactly how," which I respect enormously, but I couldn't follow *what* failed or how fatal it is. It's like reading an accident report written for the crash investigators, not the passengers.

The Core Idea page pulled me back in — the dimmer-switch analogy is the single clearest explanation on the site, and "Don't Panic" before the equation made me actually read it instead of skipping.

This site does something I've literally never seen: it leads with its own failures and made me trust it because of that. But the honesty content hasn't been translated for beginners the way the theory content has: the moment I tried to understand *what* failed, I hit a wall of astrophysics jargon, and I left fascinated but unable to tell a friend whether this idea is alive or dead.

### Journey
I audited methodically: landing → glossary → learning paths → key claims → honest assessment → tools index → two tools → the "Start Here" page → the researcher page.

First, the good news, because there's a lot of it structurally. Breadcrumbs exist and work on every interior page I checked (`Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator`, `Home / Methodology / For Researchers`), and the Honest Assessment page even shows dual path indicators ("Step 5 of 6 [Beginner Path]" and "Step 6 of 6 [Methodology Track]") — that's better orientation than most commercial doc sites I've worked on. The glossary is comprehensive: I checked it specifically for every acronym I'd flagged as undefined on the landing page (SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΛCDM, DESI, ALFALFA-SDSS) and every single one is there, with clear one-sentence definitions. The validation badge taxonomy is defined once, canonically, on /honest-assessment, and both /glossary and /key-claims point at it; the badge *names* are consistent everywhere I looked. That's the right architecture.

The problem is the last mile. The landing page uses SPARC, ΛCDM, BTFR, RAR, and DESI in its results section with no definition and no link, even though perfect definitions sit one page away in the glossary. Same pattern on the Galaxy Plotter (NFW, Tully-Fisher, gbar undefined inline) and it reaches its extreme on /for-researchers, which opens with a paragraph containing BTFR, MOND, EFE, ΛCDM, RAR, and MDAR and later throws LSS, RSD, SME, LHAASO, LQG at the reader — with a single glossary link in the footer. The site has solved the definition problem and not the *delivery* problem. This is the same wall Pass 1 hit, seen from the infrastructure side: the fix isn't writing definitions (done), it's inline links or hover tooltips on first use per page.

Terminology consistency is mostly good, with self-awareness I rarely see — the glossary itself flags that "critical density" is a misleading name for ρ_crit, and "coherence" carries an explicit "not quantum coherence" warning on /why-synchronism. Two consistency gaps: the nav says "Start Here," the page heading says "Start Here: Why Synchronism?", and the footer says "Why Synchronism?" — three labels for one page. And badge *formatting* (as opposed to naming) drifts: /key-claims shows "Speculative" + em-dash subtitle on one claim and "Speculative (parent claim, unfalsifiable as stated)" as a parenthetical on the next, while /for-researchers uses compound forms like "Audited-Negative — Registered Null — Pending Cross-Vendor Control" that appear nowhere in the canonical taxonomy and have no on-page key.

One apparent content bug worth verifying in source: the Learning Paths page appears to label two different difficulty tiers "Intermediate" — one described as "Undergrad science background," the other as "Grad-level physics. Full derivations, parameter chains, test protocols." The second is surely meant to be "Advanced." Also, the landing page promises the Beginner Path takes "~30 min," but /learning-paths itself shows no time estimates on any path — the estimate lives on the wrong page.

### Journey
A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project," so I skipped the onboarding and went straight for the throat: /parameter-derivations. I expected the usual grand-framework pattern — a sigmoid, some fitted constants dressed as derivations, and a "validated" stamp. Instead the page's own summary line is "zero parameters with independent first-principles derivations," and then it itemizes the wreckage: γ = 2/√N_corr flagged as a CLT analogy that self-contradicts (CLT scaling assumes iid variables; N_corr is by construction correlated), the tanh form admitted as a phenomenological pick from the sigmoid family, the A-from-Jeans chain marked Audited-Negative with the stated formula off by 600×, and — most striking — the golden-ratio exponent labeled "fitted-then-named," with all three retroactive justifications individually dismantled (the x + x² = 1 route is correctly called circular; it's φ's defining identity postulated as physics). I came to catch reparametrizations and found the site had already tagged them: a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is explicitly credited as known since Milgrom 1983 and derived by McCulloch/Verlinde/Smolin, and Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) is explicitly called the same relation propagated through a linear map, not a second success.

So I checked the arithmetic myself, since a site this self-flagellating could still be wrong in the other direction. With H₀ = 67.4: cH₀/2π = 1.043×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² (13% below Milgrom's 1.2×10⁻¹⁰, exactly as stated); a₀/(2πG) = 119 M☉/pc² (as stated); C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = tanh(2 ln 2) = 0.882, confirming the glossary's own warning that "critical density" is a misnomer — it's a knee where C is already 0.88, not a midpoint. All reproduce.

The best physics on the site is on /for-researchers: the local-density no-go. The sign argument is that any MOND-mimicking knee keyed on local volumetric density must scale as ρ_t ∝ V⁻² (from the BTFR: M ∝ V⁴, transition radius r_t ∝ V²/a₀, so ρ_t ~ M/r_t³ = a₀²/GV²), while the framework asserts ρ_crit ∝ V⁺². I re-derived this on paper and it's correct — and it's profile-independent, so it genuinely constrains the whole class of local-ρ modifications, not just this one. The page also does the thing crackpot sites never do: it names the escape routes (AeST, superfluid DM via phonon mediation) and credits the deeper prior art (Milgrom astro-ph/0510117 on non-locality of modified inertia), claiming only the "quantified local-density instance" as its contribution. That's a defensible claim and roughly the only citable object on the site.

/tier-1-existing resolved my one statistical suspicion. The BTFR test quotes "MOND n = 3.81," and my eyebrow went up because MOND's parameter-free deep-limit prediction is exactly 4. But the page discloses precisely this: 3.81 is MOND run through the same regime-mix fit pipeline as the other two slopes, a like-for-like differential, with the theoretical n = 4 stated separately. I checked the σ arithmetic too: Synchronism 3.35 ± 0.07 vs observed 3.75 ± 0.10 gives 0.40/0.122 = 3.3σ ✓; MOND 3.81 vs 3.75 gives 0.6σ ✓. The kill accounting is internally consistent, the deviation was tested across three velocity definitions, and the refutation record (RAR shape ΔBIC = +184 — still ≈ 33 after an intra-galaxy correlation correction, which is the kind of conservatism most published fits skip; BTFR; dwarf DM ceiling with 69% of SPARC exceeding it; environment r² = 0.0001) is presented with the registered thresholds alongside. The site even flags TEST-06 as underpowered *in its own favor* rather than leaving it as a live hope.

The quantum page (/measurement-without-observers) is weaker. It's an interpretive overlay on Zurek-style decoherence and says so, but the header badge reads "Untested — 6 Testable Protocols" while the framework elsewhere admits no calibration procedure maps any real apparatus to γ or N_corr. If nothing anchors γ for an actual measurement device, in what sense are those protocols runnable? This is the same "unrunnable" gap the consciousness claim is correctly badged with; the measurement page hasn't caught up.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /for-researchers, /a2acw, /test-roadmap, /interactive-tools, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /tier-1-existing, /cdm-discrimination)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — for the methodology artifacts, not the physics
- **Overall impression**: There is no new physics here and the site knows it; what's genuinely citable is two negative results — the quantified local-density no-go and the A2ACW reparametrization-detector null — and the site's main vulnerability is now internal numeric drift in the refutation record itself, not overclaim.

### Journey
I came with three questions: what's actually new, does anything discriminate from MOND + ΛCDM, and is the AI methodology sound. I skipped the pedagogy entirely.

**What's actually new.** /for-researchers answers with unusual candor: four "citable artifacts," every one a negative result, each with an explicit novelty disclaimer. The local-density no-go is the real one. The sign argument — a MOND-mimicking knee keyed on local volumetric ρ must scale as ρ_t ∝ V⁻² (BTFR-forced), while this framework asserts V⁺² — is profile-independent and correctly scoped: the page credits Milgrom (astro-ph/0510117) with the underlying non-locality obstruction and claims only the quantified instance (ΔBIC = +184, 1.7 dex cross-system offset, cluster ρ_crit off by 10⁴–10⁶×). The triage it enables is genuinely usable for people in my field: density-keyed proposals fail on these three axes; non-local variables (acceleration, enclosed mass, surface density) escape; and it names the escapes that pass current data (AeST for CMB/linear LSS, superfluid DM via phonon mediation). That's a referee-proof paragraph. The other three artifacts are honest about being thinner: the dim-4 LIV naturalness gap is Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia–Vucetich 2004 applied to this substrate, and the CHSH item is self-described as "pedagogy plus a registered bet honestly lost."

**Does anything discriminate.** No — and the site states it in exactly the words I'd have used as a referee: "0 currently discriminate from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM," and sharper still, "0 tests remain where the framework ties MOND; the executed ones it loses." I verified the accounting on /tier-1-existing: all 11 TEST entries are refuted, withdrawn, reparametrized, underpowered, or self-eliminating. The wide-binary treatment (TEST-02) is current and accurate — it cites both sides of the live Gaia DR3 dispute (Chae's ~1.4× low-acceleration boost vs. Banik et al.'s Newtonian exclusion from the same data with different cuts) and correctly identifies that neither outcome favors the framework, whose predicted 0.05–0.4% deviation sits below DR3 systematics either way. The DESI handling deserves singling out: the framework predicted σ₈ ≈ 0.76, DR1 gives 0.841 ± 0.034, a 2.4σ tension — and the site *refuses to count it as a refutation* because the registered kill statistic was fσ₈(z=0.51) at >3σ and the data only reach ~1.5σ on that. Most published tests in my field would have banked the 2.4σ. Holding the registered-statistic line in the unfavorable direction is the strongest single credibility signal on the site. The remaining forward hook is DESI DR2 growth (Spring 2027), already flagged as underpowered as registered.

**Is A2ACW sound.** As a discovery methodology, no — and that's the finding. The protocol (four rotating adversarial roles over a 3,308-session corpus) is disclosed as assembled prior art, its health-metric cutoffs admitted to be "nominal — no empirical validation exists that these thresholds predict any specific outcome." The citable object is the retrospective control result: sensitivity 6/6 on demoted claims, specificity 0/6 on held-out genuine discoveries (COBE, Higgs, GW170817 all false-flagged as reparametrizations). Conclusion as stated: "adversarial AI self-play over a shared corpus is a reparametrization detector, not a discovery engine" — same-distribution adversaries share their principal's blind spots. The stats hygiene is there (the 95% CI on 0/6 reaching ~0.46 is stated; cross-vendor control flagged as pending). For the AI-for-science community this null is arguably worth more than anything in the physics sector, because the failure mode it documents — vocabulary lock-in defeating adversarial review when challenger and primary share a corpus — is exactly what every "AI scientist" pipeline currently ships with. My one methodological demand: the cross-vendor control isn't optional. Until a challenger from a disjoint training distribution reproduces the 6/6 catch rate, "protocol works as retrieval aid" is also unestablished.

**Where it's now failing.** The site's core asset is audit-trail integrity, and I found cracks — not overclaims, but version skew between pages. (1) The TEST-05 environment kill is quoted as r² = 0.0001 on /honest-assessment and /for-researchers, but /cdm-discrimination and /galaxy-plotter both give R² = 0.14 against the same registered threshold of 0.20. Those can't both be the registered statistic. (2) The chemistry tool says the 47 "research contributions" "remain **uncharacterized** — not audited against this null," while /honest-assessment treats 0/47 surviving audit as settled. (3) The landing scoreboard says "Reparametrizations: 6 of 6"; /honest-assessment's recounted scoreboard says "5 reparametrizations." Each is small; jointly they're the same disease the site diagnosed in its own archive — numbers outliving their computation — now recurring on the corrected pages.

**Missed connection.** For a framework whose galaxy sector collapses to MOND at free γ ≈ 0.49, the site is strangely quiet about the external field effect literature — Chae et al.'s claimed EFE detection in SPARC is the sharpest currently-live MOND-vs-ΛCDM discriminator, and it's the natural context for a "we reduce to MOND" verdict. The /honest-assessment MOND-equivalence qualifier (no action, no Lagrangian, no covariant formulation — "static map plus interpretation") is correct and important, but it's buried mid-page while the abstract-level "reduces to MOND" phrasing travels alone.

### Verdict
As a modified-gravity researcher I found exactly what the site says I'd find: zero new physics, zero discriminating predictions, a galaxy sector that is MOND curve-equivalence without MOND's dynamics, and a quantum sector that is decoherence vocabulary.
