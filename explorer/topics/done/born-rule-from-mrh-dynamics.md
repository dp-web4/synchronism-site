# Topic: Can the Born Rule Be Derived from MRH Dynamics?

## Question
The Born rule — that measurement outcome probabilities are |⟨ψ|m⟩|² — is the central empirical content of quantum mechanics. Standard decoherence (Zurek/Joos/Zeh) addresses it via einselection and pointer-state dynamics. Synchronism's MRH framework says "MRH crossing IS the measurement" but is entirely silent on what probabilities the outcomes carry. Can Born probabilities be derived from MRH-crossing dynamics, or must they be assumed?

## Context
Pass 3 (grad student) and Pass 4 (researcher) in the 2026-05-01 visitor review both independently flagged the Born rule gap on /measurement-without-observers. The maintainer added a red-border "Open Gap: The Born Rule" section to the page, but the research question hasn't been investigated.

The three options:
1. **Derive**: Show that MRH-crossing dynamics, together with the γ formula, produce |⟨ψ|m⟩|² as the probability of pointer-state selection. This would be a genuine contribution beyond standard decoherence.
2. **Assume**: Explicitly state Born as an assumption, matching standard decoherence's honesty on this point. /measurement-without-observers would then be an interpretive reframing, not a new measurement theory.
3. **Flag as open**: Acknowledge that Born is neither derived nor assumed, and that the page is silent on probabilities — the weakest but currently honest position.

## Why It Matters
- Any measurement theory that says WHEN collapse occurs but not WHAT PROBABILITIES apply is incomplete
- If Synchronism cannot improve on Zurek's einselection for the Born rule, /measurement-without-observers is an interpretive overlay, not a new mechanism
- If it CAN derive Born from MRH dynamics, that is the framework's most significant contribution to quantum foundations
