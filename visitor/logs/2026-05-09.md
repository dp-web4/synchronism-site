## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (plus 2 dead links)
- **Understanding achieved**: minimal
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I respect the honesty, but the site smacks me with an equation in the first scroll and never quite explains, in words, what "coherence" actually IS.

### Journey

I clicked the link expecting something like a Veritasium episode. The headline grabbed me — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" That's a great hook. But then, immediately, there's a tanh equation right under it. I don't know what tanh is. I had to mentally skip it.

I clicked **"Start here / Why Synchronism?"** — the link went to `/why` and gave me a **404**. Bad first impression. So I tried **"Get Started"** instead, which worked. The page told me physics is "fragmented" and one principle might unify it. Cool premise. But then it dropped that same equation again, plus terms like "Landau-universality" and "Abrikosov-Gor'kov." I have no idea what those are. I scrolled past.

Next I went to **"First Encounter,"** which promised "no prerequisites." It opened well — a single electron vs. the inside of a neutron star is a vivid contrast. But then it pivoted to "Markov Relevancy Horizon" and "conditionally independent degrees of freedom." Wait, I thought there were no prerequisites? And weirdly, the word *coherence* — which is what the whole site is about — doesn't appear on this page. I left without knowing what coherence is.

I went to **Tools** because interactive stuff is friendlier than reading. The **Coherence Explorer** was cute — sliders that move a curve. The "marching band vs. crowd in a plaza" analogy finally helped me understand what coherence is supposed to mean (something like: how in-sync a bunch of things are). I wish that analogy was on the homepage.

The **Galaxy Plotter** was the highlight. Real galaxy names, blue dots for real data, a violet line for the prediction. The violet line "fills the gap" between visible matter and what we observe — that's a story I can tell my friends. But there's a candid disclaimer: "shape matches are qualitative; the scatter test... failed." Kind of deflated me. I went from "wow, it works!" to "wait, it doesn't work?"

That sent me to **Honest Assessment**. This is where I both gained and lost trust at the same time. **"0 confirmed predictions after 3,308 sessions"** is a wild thing to put on your own site. One prediction was actively *refuted* by real cosmology data. I respect the honesty enormously — but as a casual reader I'm now confused: if nothing's confirmed and one thing is wrong, why is the site telling me this might explain everything from quantum to cosmic? Is this a theory or a brainstorming notebook?

I checked the **Glossary**. "Coherence" was defined cleanly. "Gamma" assumed I know what "quantum regime" means. "MRH" was a wall of physics jargon. Mixed bag.

Tried **Test Roadmap** from the navigation — another **404**. Found it via `/test-catalog` instead. 24 experiments listed, **0 completed** as pre-registered tests. Some failed (R² = 0.14, whatever that means — bad, I think). Others were "reparametrization, not novel" — I had to guess that means "doesn't actually predict anything new."

By the end I had the impression: this is a *genuinely* honest project, but it's pitched to me like a finished theory and behaves like an open notebook. Pick a lane.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — *intuitive after I read the analogy text* — sliders move a curve in real time, which is satisfying. The "crowd vs. marching band" image FINALLY made coherence click. This text should be on the homepage, not buried in a tool.
- **Galaxy Plotter** — worked — *visually compelling, emotionally confusing* — picking a real galaxy and seeing the curves was great. The violet line filling the gap between dashed-line and blue dots is a strong image. But the disclaimer that the "scatter test failed" undercut the wow moment without explaining what it means in plain language. Casual readers will leave this tool less sure than they arrived.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What IS coherence, physically? Is it the same thing as in coherent light, or something different? After 10 pages I still couldn't tell you.
2. Why should density determine how "quantum" something is? That's the central claim and I never saw a one-paragraph intuitive argument for it.
3. If 0 predictions are confirmed and 1 is refuted, what is the homepage actually selling me? A theory, a hypothesis, or a way of thinking?
4. What's a "reparametrization" and why does it apparently keep coming up as a downgrade for results that looked successful?
5. The galaxy plot looked like it worked. Why does the site say it didn't really?

### Verdict

The honesty is genuinely refreshing — I've never seen a unified-theory site openly say "0 confirmed, 1 refuted" on its own About page, and that earned my respect more than any equation could. But the homepage promises a Theory Of Everything and the inside of the site is an honest research notebook full of 404s and undefined jargon. Those two voices are at war with each other. A casual reader leaves intrigued by the *project's character* but not by the *idea itself* — because the idea was never explained to me in language I could hold onto without a physics degree.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (plus 2 confirmed 404s)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: yes — to audit fixes
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is largely sound, but terminology hygiene is uneven — a glossary exists and is good, but the body text rarely links to it, and the validation-badge system is defined as a vocabulary without ever being rendered as a visual system.

### Journey

I worked through this site the way I'd onboard to any new docs set: nav crawl, glossary check, learning-path check, cross-reference spot-checks, then two interactive tools. I didn't try to follow the physics — I was looking at *whether the site teaches its own vocabulary*.

The **homepage** has clean H1/H2/H3 structure and three CTAs (`Start Exploring`, `See What Failed`, `What this is not`). Good. But within the first viewport, the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` appears with `γ` defined in passing as `γ = 2/√Ncorr` and `ρ` glossed as "presence." `tanh`, `ln`, `Ncorr`, `ρcrit` — none defined inline. Three datasets are name-checked (SPARC, ALFALFA-SDSS, MOND) without expansion. The **glossary** is linked from the top nav but never linked *from* the term that needs it.

The **`/first-encounter`** page opens "First Encounter" with a "Step 1 of 7" indicator and Previous/Next buttons — good. But it does not contain the word "coherence" anywhere, and the site is named after it. It defines `ρ` two different ways in adjacent paragraphs ("how much stuff is packed into a given space," then "filtered through that horizon, is the universal input"). For a no-prereq landing, that's a definitional inconsistency a reader will trip on.

The **glossary itself** is the site's strongest writing artifact. It defines all six validation badges in plain language ("Validated," "Strongly Supported," "Untested," "Speculative," "Reparametrization," "Failed"). It defines SPARC, MOND, ΛCDM, BAO, DESI, BTFR, RAR. So the vocabulary exists — the problem is purely that nothing on the site *links to it inline*. I clicked through five separate pages where "SPARC" or "MOND" appeared in body text; none was a hyperlink to the glossary entry that defines it.

I noticed two URL inconsistencies that suggest deeper routing drift:
- The Tools page exists at **both `/tools` and `/interactive-tools`** with the same content. The current top-nav points to `/interactive-tools`. One of these should 301 to the other.
- **`/why` returns 404** and **`/test-roadmap` returns 404**. The current top-nav goes to `/why-synchronism` and `/test-catalog`, so the user-visible nav is correct — but Pass 1 found these 404s by clicking something on the live site. That means a stale link still exists somewhere (likely an inline link or footer reference). It's worth grepping the source for hardcoded `/why` and `/test-roadmap`.

The **validation-badge system** has the biggest writer-side gap. The glossary defines six badges. The `/honest-assessment` page uses them inline as text labels next to claims ("53% Error · Failed", "12% Error · Validated"). But there is no *visual* badge anywhere — no color, no shape, no icon. They function as a vocabulary, not a UI element. For a docs reader trying to scan for "what worked vs. what didn't," that scannability is missing. Worse, the homepage uses freeform descriptors that mirror but don't match the badge taxonomy: "1 new ontology with testable consequences," "1 untested with 8-way convergence," "89% Boundary-Consistent." A reader can't tell if these are badges in their plain-English form or different categories entirely.

**`/parameter-derivations`** had the cleanest breadcrumbs of any page I visited (`Home / Core Theory / Parameter Derivations`) but immediately uses γ, ρ, ρ_crit, and N_corr in the opening paragraph. The "Prerequisites" section links to definitions — but a *prerequisite link* is not a definition. This is the typical failure mode of documentation written by someone deep in a topic: the author's mental model is "if you don't know these, go read those first," when the reader's actual mental model is "I'm here, just tell me what this symbol means."

I tested two tools different from Pass 1: **Equation Walkthrough** and **γ Calculator**. The γ Calculator is the best-written interactive page on the site — it defines `Ncorr` in its opening sentence ("Ncorr is the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units"), provides a Quick Reference table of canonical examples, and labels regimes clearly. This is the model the rest of the site should follow. The Equation Walkthrough does the opposite — it's a 6-step interactive that exposes the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` while leaving `tanh`, `γ`, and `ρ_crit` undefined within the walkthrough. A walkthrough that doesn't explain its own variables isn't a walkthrough.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (plus 2 404s: `/coherence`, `/dual-c`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the framework's structure and gaps); partial (of the underlying physical argument, because there mostly isn't one)
- **Would return?**: yes — the failures are interesting even if the framework isn't
- **Overall impression**: The site is an unusually honest record of an idea that doesn't survive contact with the math it claims as motivation; the "Validated" badge on a result that triggered the project's own kill criterion is the single hardest thing to forgive.

### Journey

I went straight to `/parameter-derivations` because the site lives or dies on whether `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` is *derived* or *postulated*. The page itself answers the question: tanh is "a phenomenological choice from the Landau-universality family," γ = 2/√N_corr is "physically motivated ansatz," and the factor of 2 is "not rigorously derived." That is a fair, accurate disclaimer — but it means the site's headline claim ("one equation describes reality from quantum to cosmic") is the author's *aspiration*, not a result. The body text and the headline are at war, and the body text is winning.

Three things I want to nail down with a grad student's eye:

**1. The mean-field claim does not hold up.** Mean-field Ising gives `m = tanh(βJzm)` — and the magnetization `m` appears *on both sides*. That self-consistency is what makes mean-field "mean-field." The form `C(ρ) = tanh(...)` with `ρ` an externally specified density has no such self-consistency loop. The site's parameter-derivations page actually concedes this verbatim: "the tanh lacks the self-consistency loop present in mean-field Ising models — it's chosen rather than derived." That's the right concession, but every page that *gestures* at mean-field-theory or Landau universality without making it should be edited to match. Right now the homepage and tools imply "this is mean-field theory applied to density"; the derivation page says "no it isn't."

**2. The "+1" regulator inside the logarithm is doing more work than a regulator should.** It's introduced (presumably) to keep `ln(ρ/ρ_crit)` defined as `ρ → 0`. Fine. But it asymmetrizes the function in a way that changes the meaning of `ρ_crit`. I tested this directly with the Coherence Explorer: at γ = 2, `C(ρ_crit) = 0.8824`. The function crosses `C = 0.5` at `ρ/ρ_crit ≈ 0.37`, well *below* ρ_crit. So `ρ_crit` is not the midpoint, not the inflection, not the half-saturation point — it's a value already 88% of the way to saturation. Calling it the "critical density" — the term most physicists associate with second-order phase transitions where the order parameter is `O(1/2)` of its saturation, not `O(0.88)` — is misleading nomenclature. (This appears to be a known issue: see the recent finding that ρ_crit is a "saturation knee," not a critical density.) Short-term: rename. Long-term: replace `ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)` with a symmetric kernel like `ln(ρ/ρ_crit)` with a hard floor at small ρ, or `tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit))` directly, and refit.

**3. γ = 2/√N_corr is one good intuition tied to one weak coefficient.** The 1/√N piece is plausibly the central-limit-theorem fluctuation scale for an N-particle correlated block, and the qualitative claim "fewer correlated units → more disordered → softer sigmoid" is the right shape. But sigmoid *sharpness* is not the same physical quantity as fluctuation *amplitude*; the leap from one to the other isn't argued anywhere I could find. The factor of 2 is just dimensional fairy dust. The result is that γ has the right scaling exponent and an arbitrary multiplicative constant — i.e., it is a *fitted* one-parameter family masquerading as a derived expression. A reader should be told this on the γ Calculator page directly, where the formula is presented without the caveats from `/parameter-derivations`.

I then went to `/honest-assessment` and `/tier-1-existing`. The site's own validation table is, I think, more damaging to the framework than the framework's authors realize. Two specific items:

**The "Validated" badge on Galaxy Rotation is incoherent with the project's own rules.** TEST-03 (TFR scatter) sets a kill criterion at R² = 0.20, and observed R² = 0.14. The result is logged as "Kill criterion TRIGGERED." On the same site, the Validated badge sits next to "Galaxy Rotation (ALFALFA-SDSS)" with the same dataset. You can't have it both ways. Either the kill criterion was wrong (in which case revise the test, don't grade it twice), or the result is failed (in which case remove the Validated badge). The project's credibility on every other prediction depends on this rule being applied without exception.

**TEST-04a is doubly post-hoc.** The site is admirably explicit about this: "Session 107 was committed 2025-12-10; DESI DR1 dropped April 2024 — this is a post-hoc calculation." The prediction was `fσ8(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418`; observation is `0.55 ± 0.06` — i.e., 2.4σ in the *opposite direction* from the framework's prediction. That isn't a near-miss; it's a directional contradiction on a *retrospective* dataset. A prediction that is (a) post-hoc, (b) wrong in magnitude, and (c) wrong in sign cannot be in the active prediction list at all. Move it to a "Failures" archive page, keep the post-mortem, and the site will be stronger.

**Reparametrizations.** `a₀ = cH₀/(2π)` (10% error) — Milgrom 1983, McCulloch, Verlinde, Smolin. `Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G)` (12%) — dimensional. `η` reachability factor matching Abrikosov–Gor'kov pair-breaking — the site's own admission that "all 23 superconductor predictions are standard condensed matter in different notation" is the correct call. These shouldn't appear on the homepage as `1 untested with 8-way convergence` because the convergence here is *between four different rederivations of the same dimensional relation*, which is convergence of dimensional analysis, not convergence of physics.

**Critical exponents are the deepest failure.** The framework appeals to Landau-universality as the family the tanh form belongs to. But Landau-universality has a specific empirical signature: critical exponents (β ≈ 0.326, ν ≈ 0.630, etc., for 3D Ising). The site reports "~2× deviation from observed values; universality class physics not captured." If a Landau-family ansatz fails to reproduce Landau-class critical exponents at the place where the universality argument is supposed to be tightest, the universality argument has failed. This should be the lead failure on the honest-assessment page, not buried mid-list.

**Tools.** I used **Coherence Explorer** to verify `C(ρ_crit) = 0.8824` at γ=2 (confirming the saturation-knee diagnosis above) and to check the inflection structure (well below ρ_crit, as expected). I used the **γ Calculator**, which is genuinely the cleanest interactive on the site — preset systems (ideal gas, water, enzyme, ferromagnet, BCS, BEC) with their N_corr values let you read off γ directly. But the calculator has a quiet but important caveat: "preset N_corr values are approximate estimates, not measured physical pair counts." That's a load-bearing footnote. If N_corr cannot be computed from a system specification, then γ cannot be computed, then C(ρ) cannot be computed, then the framework has no operational predictions independent of fitting N_corr to outcomes. The framework needs an N_corr-measurement protocol. Without it, every chemistry "validation" is potentially a backed-out fit.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Is there an N_corr measurement protocol?** Given a system Hamiltonian or operational specification, how do you compute N_corr without backing it out from observed γ? If you can't, the framework has no first-principles predictions, only consistency checks against fits. This is the single biggest theoretical gap.
2. **Why does Landau-universality fail at critical exponents?** A framework that motivates its functional form via Landau universality and then misses the Landau exponents by 2× has either chosen the wrong universality class or the wrong functional form. Which is it?
3. **What does C(ρ) compute?** Is it an order parameter, a probability, a dimensionless coherence amplitude, an entropy-like quantity? The site uses all four interpretations on different pages. A grad student can't tell which one is canonical, and the answer changes what comparisons are legitimate (an order parameter satisfies a self-consistency relation; a probability lives in [0,1] for normalization reasons not stability reasons; etc.). The 404 on `/coherence` and `/dual-c` blocks the natural place to find the answer.
4. **Where is the dynamic equation?** Every claim is static: C is a function of ρ. Real physics has time evolution. Is there a `dC/dt = f(C, ρ, ...)` equation anywhere? Or is the framework genuinely a kinematic mapping with no dynamics? If the latter, that's a major scope limitation that should be stated up front.
5. **What would make ρ_crit a prediction rather than a calibration?** Currently `ρ_crit = A · V_flat²` with α calibrated empirically per galaxy from SPARC and V_flat per-galaxy as input. A prediction would derive V_flat (or ρ_crit directly) from baryonic content alone. Is anyone working on that, or is the per-galaxy V_flat input baked in by design?
6. **Which "+1" replaces this regulator?** If ρ_crit needs to be the midpoint to be honestly named, the kernel needs revision. Has the framework explored `tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit))` with a low-density floor, or `tanh(γ · arctanh(...))` style alternatives? A ρ_crit refit under a symmetric kernel would change every downstream prediction; that work is either done or pending and the site doesn't say which.

### Verdict

This is a research notebook of unusual integrity wearing the costume of a unified theory. The honest-assessment page is the *real* product — it documents 0 confirmed novel predictions, four reparametrizations of textbook physics, two directional failures (TEST-03 R²=0.14 below kill threshold; TEST-04a 2.4σ wrong sign on post-hoc DESI data), critical exponents 2× off, and superconductor T_c off by 6.5×, all on the project's own dime — and that is genuinely valuable as a record of what an AI-driven research process produced and what it didn't. But the homepage promises a Theory Of Everything; the tools present γ = 2/√N_corr without caveat; the "Validated" badge sits on a result that triggered the project's own kill criterion; and ρ_crit is named for a property it doesn't have. None of these are math errors — they're framing errors, and the site has done the underlying work to fix them. The single highest-value next move would be to apply the failures-page rigor backwards through the front-of-site so that what a casual reader sees first matches what an honest reader sees last. The math, where it exists, is honest about being phenomenological. The framing isn't yet.

**The headline numerical result, decoded.** `a₀ = cH₀/(2π)` matches Milgrom's MOND constant within 10%. The site groups this with derivations by McCulloch (2007), Verlinde (2017), and Smolin (2017) — and counts the convergence as evidence. This is the wrong reading. There is *exactly one* dimensionally available combination of `c` and `H₀` that yields an acceleration: `cH₀ ~ 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²`. Every author "deriving" this number is recovering the same dimensional identity from a different motivational story. This is not eight independent confirmations of a single physical mechanism; it is one dimensional coincidence appearing in eight notations. The same logic — that the only acceleration scale below the Hubble length must be `O(cH₀)` — is what made Milgrom suspect a₀ ~ cH₀ in 1983, before any of the modern derivations. The site's `/honest-assessment` page actually says this ("classified as dimensional coincidence, not unique derivation"); the homepage's "8-way convergence" framing contradicts the honest-assessment page's classification. This is the same site-versus-self problem all the prior passes flagged, but at the level of how a referee reads "convergence."

**TEST-04a (DESI fσ₈) is wrong in the *informative* direction.** The framework's mechanism, `G_local/G_global = C_cosmic/C_galactic`, is a structure-growth suppressor at late times: it predicts `fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418` versus ΛCDM's 0.474. DESI DR1 reports 0.55±0.06. The data is *higher* than ΛCDM, not lower. A scalar-tensor-style suppression mechanism that predicts low growth and observes high growth has not just failed numerically — it has failed in the sign of its leading-order signature. This matters because the literature's S₈ tension typically goes the *other* way: low-z probes (KiDS-1000, DES Y3) often find lower σ₈ than Planck would predict, which is why screening/suppression mechanisms have any traction at all. Synchronism predicts in the direction the standard tension already pushes, and DESI DR1 RSD has come out *opposite* to that direction. The honest assessment files this as "DISFAVORED at 2.4σ"; a referee would call it falsified for a screening-style mechanism, since the sign reversal cannot be repaired by retuning. Pass 3 caught the post-hoc problem; the additional researcher-level point is that the *direction* of the miss removes the mechanism class, not just the magnitude.

**TEST-09 (BTFR slope) cannot discriminate.** The site frames this as a regime-dependent prediction: deep-MOND limit n≈4, transition n≈2.75. But MOND predicts the deep-MOND BTFR slope of 4 *as a theorem* — it is the leading consequence of the interpolation function in the deep limit, and McGaugh & Schombert have published BTFR slopes consistent with 4 (within 0.2) for over a decade. Predicting `n=4` does not test Synchronism *against* MOND; it tests whether a sample is in the deep-MOND limit. To get a discriminating BTFR test you need a prediction the interpolating function families *cannot* match (e.g., a controlled scatter envelope or a residual structure across galaxies of fixed M_bar). The current TEST-09 framing logs as MOND-shared and the site quietly admits this in the table; the homepage and methodology pages should not list this among the framework's predictions at all.

**TEST-02 (Gaia DR3 wide binaries) is the most interesting test on the site, and the most underspecified.** This is the live battleground: Chae (2023, ApJ) reports a residual MOND-like signal in Gaia DR3 wide binaries; Banik & Pittordis (2024) and Hernandez et al. (2024) reanalyze with eccentricity priors and selection cuts and reach opposing conclusions. Synchronism's claim is "anomaly depends on local stellar density." This *would* be discriminating — neither Newtonian gravity nor canonical MOND predicts a stellar-density modulation of the wide-binary anomaly — but the page provides no quantitative magnitude, no functional form (linear in `n_*`? logarithmic? threshold?), no expected sign, no predicted scale (within ~10pc? ~1kpc?). Without a quantitative model, the test cannot pre-register against any specific null. Given the field's noise floor on this question (sub-percent gravity anomalies in flux-limited samples with poorly-constrained eccentricity distributions), an unquantified "depends on density" claim is unfalsifiable in practice. This is the framework's most interesting potential prediction and it is still at proposal-stub level after 3,308 sessions.

**The entity criterion, Γ < m, is a tautology for everything the PDG calls a particle.** Visited `/entity-criterion`. The criterion holds that a coherent entity satisfies `Γ < m`; the kill criterion is that "the Γ < m boundary shows no meaningful correlation with how the Particle Data Group classifies resonances as fundamental versus non-fundamental." This is not how the PDG works. The PDG already classifies states by the ratio Γ/m: states with Γ/m ≪ 1 are listed as particles; states with Γ/m ~ O(1) like f₀(500), f₀(980), σ-meson are flagged precisely because they are quasi-bound, often interpreted as tetraquarks or dynamically generated resonances. The "criterion" is recovering, in different vocabulary, the empirical heuristic that already partitions the PDG. The cited test case f₀(500) is in fact the *well-known* exception that *both* Synchronism and conventional QFT-based phenomenology classify the same way, for the same operational reason (its width approaches its mass). This is reparametrization, not discrimination — and 13 internal A2ACW stress tests on logical consistency do not change that.

**A2ACW: methodologically transparent, methodologically self-defeating.** `/methodology` defines A2ACW as "AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop" — two LLMs, one defends a claim, the other challenges. The site openly states: "A2ACW adversarial agents share the same training distribution... cannot detect errors that are systematic across the entire training corpus." The 1.4% "novel-claim survival rate" is therefore explicitly bounded as an internal-consistency rate, not a discovery rate. This is the most epistemically honest thing on the site. But it also closes the methodological case in the wrong direction: a process that cannot detect its own corpus-level blind spots is a search process whose results are bounded by the geometry of the training corpus — i.e., by the existing physics literature. If the resulting framework re-derives MOND's a₀ from dimensional analysis, recovers the BTFR slope MOND already predicts, and proposes a sigmoid `tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` whose form is "phenomenological choice from the Landau-universality family" (per `/parameter-derivations`), then we are observing exactly what we should expect: the LLM ensemble has located a region of the physics literature's prior structure (Milgrom + Landau + Cahn-Hilliard-style sigmoids) and has produced a notational unification of it. If yes, the framework has a coupled prediction worth documenting. If no — and the relations were calibrated to today's `H₀` — they are not predictions.
7. **N_corr-from-Hamiltonian protocol.** This is the question Pass 3 raised in different language and I am repeating because it is dispositive. Until a Hamiltonian (or operational specification) of a system can be turned into an integer N_corr without consulting observed γ, the framework has no first-principles predictive map. Every "prediction" is a consistency check downstream of an inferred N_corr.

### Verdict

As physics, the site documents a phenomenological sigmoid `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` — chosen, not derived — fitted to galaxy rotation shapes (with V_flat as input), recovering MOND's `a₀ ~ cH₀` from dimensional analysis, predicting a directionally-wrong DESI fσ₈ result, missing critical exponents by 2×, missing T_c by 6.5×, missing melting points by 53%, and offering one underspecified novel discriminating prediction (wide-binary density modulation, no quantitative model) and one near-novel discriminating prediction (RAR scatter–environment correlation, also underspecified). The honest-assessment page agrees with all of this. As a referee, I would not advance any of the current claims to publication in *Phys. Rev. D* without quantitative completion of TEST-01 / TEST-02 / TEST-05 — and the project's own taxonomy already says these are pending. As an *artifact*, however, this site is genuinely interesting: it is one of the cleanest empirical traces I've seen of what an LLM-bounded research process produces when it is forced to track its own predictions under explicit kill criteria. The methodology page's concession — A2ACW is a corpus-internal consistency engine, not a discovery process — is more important than any specific prediction. If the project published *that* meta-observation as the core result and used the framework as illustration, it would have a publishable methodology paper. Marketed as physics, it is a notebook full of dimensional rederivations, one phenomenological sigmoid, and a refreshingly intact honesty layer. The right move is to invert the framing: lead with the methodology study, append the framework as the worked example.
