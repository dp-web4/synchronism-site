## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (plus 2 broken links)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Honest and intriguing, but the big equation on the front door and the jargon in the onboarding pages keep pushing me back out before I really get hooked.

### Journey

Landed on the homepage. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is the kind of question that makes me click. Cool. But right underneath it there's this formula, `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`, and my eyes immediately slide off. I have no idea what tanh is. There's a plain-English blurb below the equation which helps a little ("smooth S-curve from quantum to classical"), but honestly my casual-reader brain reads it as "some function goes up."

What *did* land: the big counter numbers (14,760 galaxies, 1,703 chemicals, 3,308 sessions) feel impressive and real, and a link called "Honest Assessment" with the subtitle "what failed" is unusual enough that I clicked it before the "learn more" path. That's worth noting — the honest-failure framing is probably your strongest hook for skeptical-but-curious people like me.

Went to **First Encounter** expecting to be eased in. Instead I hit "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)" and "compatible structural elements available within a system's relevancy boundary" inside the first screenful. Bounced. Tried **Why Synchronism?** next — this was better. I understood "physics uses different rules at different scales, and we claim one rule works everywhere." That's a pitch I can repeat at a dinner party.

**Honest Assessment** was the best page for me. "Zero confirmed predictions across 3,302 sessions" made me laugh out loud, and then stay. It's so counter to how fringe-physics sites usually read that I trusted the rest of the site more afterward. The "0 confirmed ≠ 0 confirmable" line landed as genuine, not spin.

**Core Idea** was a mixed bag. The "dimmer switch between quantum and classical" metaphor is gold — I want that on the homepage. But then the same scary equation appeared again and I had the same reaction.

**Glossary**: some terms (phase transition, coherence) were fine. MRH and MOND definitions were still over my head. Clicking a scary term from another page into the glossary didn't rescue me.

Interactive tools: tried **Coherence Explorer** first — pretty, I could drag sliders, but I had no idea what I was supposed to *notice*. Tried **Phase Boundary Visualizer** next — this one clicked much better. A single slider, three labeled zones (classical / boundary / quantum), and real things placed along it (galaxies, enzymes, consciousness). In 20 seconds I understood the core pitch of the whole site better than after ten minutes of reading.

Tried to click "Predictions" (footer) and "Chemistry boundary" from context — both 404'd. That's the kind of thing that makes me suspect the site is half-built.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is "presence"? The site leans on this word but I never got a concrete definition I could hold onto.
2. If there are zero confirmed predictions, what *would* count as a confirmation? Is anything being actively tested right now?
3. Is this human research, AI-driven research, or both? The "3,308 sessions" number suggests something unusual is going on behind the scenes, but the site doesn't tell me.
4. How is "consciousness" being predicted by an equation — what does that even mean as a test?
5. What does a success for this theory look like in the next 5 years? Give me something concrete to watch for.

### Verdict

The honesty is the best thing you have going, and the Phase Boundary Visualizer is the best teaching tool. The worst thing is that a casual visitor hits a scary equation before they hit either of those. Move the metaphor and the visualizer up, push the formula and MRH down, and I'd stay on the site twice as long. I'd come back if a friend linked me to the honest-assessment page directly — I wouldn't come back from a bookmark of the homepage.

### Journey

Went page by page with a documentation-audit lens: acronyms on first use, terminology drift, breadcrumbs, next/prev navigation, badge usage, and whether claim numbers reconcile across pages.

The **top-level navigation is thinner than the site deserves**. The persistent nav exposes only *Glossary* and *Honest Assessment*. Meanwhile the breadcrumbs reveal at least six site sections — *Getting Started, Core Theory, Cosmology, Methodology, Predictions & Experiments, Interactive Tools* — none of which have a menu entry. `Learning Paths`, which is the single most useful discovery page for a new reader (it's the only place that maps audiences to entry pages), is not linked from the homepage nav at all. A reader who doesn't already know to click *First Encounter* → *Learning Paths* won't find it.

**Breadcrumbs are present and well-formed on every page I checked** (Home / Section / Page), which is genuinely good. What's missing is the reverse: there is no persistent sidebar or section index. Breadcrumbs tell you where you are; they don't tell you what else is in the same section.

**Next/Previous navigation is asymmetric.** Every page I checked has a "Next →" link at the bottom but no "← Previous." This forces the browser back button as the only way to retrace a reading path, and it means the linearized reading order is implicit rather than declared.

**Terminology consistency is generally good at the sentence level but breaks at the cross-page level.** The biggest examples:

*Validation badge taxonomy — three different counts across three pages.*
- `/honest-assessment` — mentions seven badges and offers one-line definitions
- `/research-philosophy` — the "Validation Badge Taxonomy" defines **six**: Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed
- The homepage shows badges like *"Strongly Supported"*, *"89% Validated"*, *"1 untested with 8-way convergence"*, *"Candidate Prediction"* — some of which (89% Validated, 8-way convergence, Candidate Prediction) don't appear as taxonomy entries on the Research Philosophy page

This is exactly the kind of thing a technical writer is trained to notice. A reader encountering *"Strongly Supported"* on the homepage has to click to `/research-philosophy` (which the homepage does not link prominently) to find out what it means. The definitive taxonomy page should be the page the badges link to on hover — ideally they should be tooltipped in-place.

*Quantitative claims don't reconcile.*
- Homepage: **3,308 sessions · 14,760 galaxies · 1,703 chemicals · 34 Predictions**
- `/honest-assessment`: **3,302 sessions · 14,585 galaxies (ALFALFA-SDSS) · 1,703 chemicals**
- `/test-catalog`: **24 experiments** defined
- `/research-philosophy`: "47 genuine contributions / ~3,302 sessions"

So the homepage says 34 predictions; the Test Catalog (the page that should list them) says 24 experiments. Session counts differ by 6; galaxy counts differ by 175. I can't tell whether these are genuinely different metrics (e.g., "predictions" vs "tests") or the homepage numbers are stale. Either way, the site needs a single source of truth for these counters or a disambiguating caption ("24 defined experiments + 10 open conjectures" or similar).

*"Interactive tool" is used inconsistently.* The `/mond-unification` page ends with a CTA "**Try It: MOND Comparator**" which routes to `/mond-comparator`. That page turns out to be a **static two-column comparison table** with no inputs, no calculation, no interactivity. The breadcrumb on `/gamma-calculator` reads "Home / **Interactive Tools** / γ Calculator," so the site has the category — but `/mond-comparator` lives under it as well despite not being one. "Try It" is a strong affordance cue and it's being misused here.

**Acronyms on first use.** Mostly handled, with some gaps:
- `/mrh` — MRH is defined well. But "decoherence," "causal influence," "phase transition," and "correlation length ξ" all appear undefined on the page itself; only a generic Glossary link sits in the header.
- `/mond-unification` — opens with *"The MOND acceleration scale a₀ is related to cosmological parameters."* MOND is in the page title and never expanded. a₀ is defined only in the glossary. Also uses RAR, TeVeS, NP2, Gaia DR3 without on-page expansion.
- `/gamma-calculator` — presets include "BCS superconductor" and "BEC" without explanation on-page.
- `/glossary` — the γ entry is self-referentially dense: it uses λ (interaction strength), K (connectivity), D (dimensionality), and N_corr in its definition, but λ, K, and D are not themselves glossary entries. Same issue for ρ_crit which uses a constant "A" with no glossary entry for it.

**First Encounter page displays "Step 1 of 7ρ"** in what appears to be a header — the stray ρ character looks like a Unicode/encoding bug (perhaps an accidental interpolation of the rho symbol into the step-counter template). On a page whose job is to welcome new readers, a broken header character is a bad first impression.

**Hover-glossary claim vs reality.** The glossary page says *"Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline."* I did not observe any highlighted/tooltipped terms on `/mrh` or `/mond-unification` (undefined terms appear as plain text, not styled). If this feature exists it isn't discoverable; if it's planned, the claim shouldn't be in the glossary yet.

**Interactive tools tested:**

*γ Calculator (`/gamma-calculator`)* — genuinely interactive. Ncorr input + linear/log toggle, live output (γ value + regime label + descriptive text), and six preset buttons ("Ideal gas," "Liquid water," "Enzyme site," "Ferromagnet," "BCS superconductor," "BEC"). The presets are the page's best feature — they turn an abstract parameter into concrete anchors. But the calculator assumes knowledge of what Ncorr *is* and *how you'd measure it for your own system*; there's no guidance for "what number should I type?"

*MOND Comparator (`/mond-comparator`)* — labeled as a tool, is actually a static comparison table. This is the single clearest "fix this" in my pass.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/core-idea`, `/gamma-parameter`, `/gamma-calculator`, `/coherence-explorer`, `/mond-unification`, `/mond-comparator`, `/test-catalog`, `/honest-assessment`, `/mrh`; one 404 on `/phase-boundary`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (understood the claim structure; unconvinced by the derivations)
- **Would return?**: yes — specifically to check whether the γ derivation gets a real writeup, and whether TEST-02 (wide-binary density dependence) gets published analysis
- **Overall impression**: The tone is unusually honest for a grand-unified framework, but the "theorems" advertised on the homepage are either reparametrizations or unsupported ansätze, and the central equation is a fitted sigmoid dressed in mean-field vocabulary.

### Journey

Went in expecting the standard grand-unified-framework pattern (hand-waved equation, slippery validation claims, no derivations) and found something more interesting: the site is partially self-aware of those failure modes. The `/honest-assessment` page lists specific failed predictions with numbers (melting points 53% error, YBCO Tc off by 6.5×, critical exponents off by ~2×, fractal coherence bridge 0/7) — this is unusual and to the site's credit. But the core equation, the γ derivation, and the MOND link don't hold up under a second look.

**The central equation: tanh of a log-density is not a mean-field result.**
The homepage says the tanh form is "derived from binary Boltzmann statistics (a theorem)." This is the kind of claim I'd want to see spelled out. The canonical mean-field binary-Boltzmann result is the Curie-Weiss / Ising self-consistency equation:

  m = tanh(β·J·z·m + β·h)

Here the argument of tanh is *linear in the order parameter* (and the external field). It comes directly from ⟨s⟩ = tanh(βE_eff) for a binary spin in an effective field. That's the theorem.

Synchronism's equation is:

  C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

The argument of tanh is a log of a density — not a linear effective field. This is not what binary Boltzmann statistics gives you. The shape of the two curves is also genuinely different: near ρ = 0 the Synchronism form behaves like tanh(γ·ρ/ρcrit) (power law ramp-up), and for ρ ≫ ρcrit it saturates at tanh(γ·log(ρ/ρcrit)) — which is *not* 1 unless γ is large. By contrast the Ising tanh saturates cleanly at |m|→1 as β→∞ because the argument scales with m itself.

Calling this a "theorem" is overclaiming. It's a convenient sigmoid with adjustable sharpness and a log-compressed input. The Coherence Explorer tool confirms this: γ and ρcrit are independently adjustable sliders, and the curve just responds — there's no self-consistency, no coupling between C and ρ (as there would be in a true mean-field order parameter). A real mean-field order parameter is a *fixed point* of a self-consistency equation; this is a *functional form* applied to an externally-set density.

**γ = 2/√Ncorr is an ansatz, not a derivation.**
The `/gamma-parameter` page attributes the 1/√Ncorr factor to "central-limit-theorem scaling" and the factor of 2 to "phase space has 6 dimensions (3 position + 3 momentum). Through contraction to effective degrees of freedom, this yields a factor of 2."

Two problems:

1. *CLT scaling* applies to fluctuations of sums of independent or weakly dependent random variables. Getting from "fluctuations scale as 1/√N" to "effective *coupling strength* scales as 1/√N" requires an additional model of how coupling and fluctuations relate — which isn't given. For comparison, in BCS you get the coherence length from the gap equation: ξ₀ = ℏv_F/(πΔ). That's a calculation. Nothing of comparable content is shown here.

2. *The factor of 2 from "6D phase space → effective dof → 2"* is a non-sequitur. 6 doesn't reduce to 2 by any standard phase-space contraction I know. Canonical pair = 2 dof? Equipartition per quadratic dof = ½kT? Neither gives you a coupling prefactor. The page admits the factor 2 is "a motivated ansatz rather than a rigorous derivation" — so the derivation claim on the homepage ("derived from binary Boltzmann statistics") conflicts with this page's own framing.

**The γ Calculator is plug-and-chug with physically questionable presets.**
Tested all six presets. The formula γ = 2/√Ncorr is applied directly — verified (2/√4 = 1.0, 2/√100 = 0.2). The deeper issue is that Ncorr is an input, not a predicted quantity. For a BCS superconductor the tool uses Ncorr = 10,000. But Cooper-pair coherence volume for a typical type-I superconductor is ~(ξ₀)³ with ξ₀ ~ 100–1000 nm, and n_electron ~ 10²⁸–10²⁹ m⁻³, which puts the number of electrons inside a Cooper-pair coherence volume around 10⁶–10⁹. The 10⁴ choice is arbitrary — and if you change it, γ and the regime label change. This is fitting, not predicting.

Even more striking: **Ideal gas → Ncorr = 1 → γ = 2.0 → labeled "Quantum."** An ideal gas is the textbook classical system — uncorrelated and high-temperature. Labeling uncorrelated "quantum" inverts the standard use of those words. Quantum coherence typically *requires* correlations (entanglement, condensate order, long-range phase). The tool's regime labeling redefines "quantum" to mean "low-Ncorr ansatz input," which is a definitional choice that should be made explicit before the equation is presented as universal.

**MOND: a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is the 1983 Milgrom coincidence, not a novel derivation.**
Credit where due — `/mond-unification` says this outright: "this relationship is not unique to Synchronism" and cites McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017, Smolin 2017. The value 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² from H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc is ~10% off Milgrom's empirical 1.20×10⁻¹⁰ — which is within the uncertainty of which H₀ you use (the SH0ES/Planck tension covers most of this gap).

What concerns me is that the homepage then badges cosmology as "Strongly Supported." If the numerical match is dimensional analysis recovering a relation Milgrom noticed 40+ years ago, and the framework is *reproducing* MOND phenomenology rather than *deviating* from it in a testable way, then "strongly supported" should be read as "reproduces an existing empirical fit" — not as support for novel physics. The honest framing is on the page; the homepage badge doesn't match it.

**The decisive test is TEST-02 (wide-binary density dependence) — but it may not discriminate.**
This is the best claim in the framework: Synchronism predicts density-dependent anomaly, MOND predicts universal. Gaia DR3 data exists. That's a real falsifiable structure. The concern: the wide-binary debate is very active (Hernandez vs Chae vs Banik etc.), and a density-dependent signal could easily come from contamination of the wide-binary sample by unresolved hierarchical triples, which correlates with stellar density. So even a positive result would need to decisively rule out the contamination alternative before it supported Synchronism. I'd want to see the site commit to a specific statistical test (cut on orbit distribution, triple fraction model, etc.) before the data analysis, not after.

**MRH is a reparametrization.**
The `/mrh` page is relatively honest: it acknowledges the concept builds on Markov blankets from PGMs and "extends correlation length concepts into a dynamical framework." In practice, "crossing the MRH" = decoherence; "cosmological MRH" = particle horizon; "spatial MRH" = correlation length ξ. None of these are new physics. The framework gains from having one word for all of them; it loses when that lets it claim unification across 80 orders of magnitude that is really just a choice of vocabulary.

**Circularity in the definitions.**
ρ ("presence") is defined as "compatible structural elements within a system's relevancy boundary." The relevancy boundary is the MRH. The MRH is defined operationally as "the minimal set of interacting dof whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system." Coherence is C(ρ). So: C depends on ρ, ρ is defined via the MRH, the MRH is defined via coherence evolution. This is circular as stated. It's salvageable (you pick a system, coarse-grain, fit parameters), but the site presents these as first-principles definitions, which they are not.

**Chemistry 89% / melting points 53%.**
The 89% number for 1,703 chemical phenomena with r = 0.982 sound velocity correlations is, on its face, strong. But r = 0.982 for sound velocity vs any density × bulk modulus proxy is easy — sound speed scales as √(B/ρ), so *any* framework that gets the bulk modulus and density right will score high correlations. What I want to see: was this a *prediction* (computed from γ and ρ before comparing to data) or a *fit* (Ncorr tuned per substance to hit the data)? The `/honest-assessment` page flags "Era 2 chemistry identified as template-based" which hints at fitting. If so, 89% is not a validation figure — it's a goodness-of-fit. The melting-point failure (53% average error) is the more informative data point: it shows the framework does *not* generalize across observables even for fitted substances.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — **physics is misleading** — The formula γ = 2/√Ncorr is applied correctly. The problem is that Ncorr is an input per system, not derived. ### Unanswered Questions

1. **Is there an actual self-consistency equation** making C(ρ) a mean-field order parameter, or is it a chosen functional form? If the latter, the "mean-field / Ising" framing on the homepage needs to be retracted.
2. **Has any preset in the γ Calculator been derived rather than fitted?** E.g., can Synchronism compute Ncorr for liquid water from its microscopic structure, or is 4 chosen to make γ ≈ 1 land in the "chemistry and biology" regime?

### Journey

I came in looking for the answer to one question: **what does this framework predict that no existing framework does?** I gave each page the reviewer's test — skip anything I already know, zero in on claims that move the needle. Here's what I found.

**The novelty inventory is smaller than the homepage badges imply.**
Working through the Advanced learning path (`/parameter-derivations` → `/compression-action` → `/mond-unification` → `/cdm-discrimination` → `/superconductivity` → `/born-rule` → `/falsifiability` → `/test-catalog`), the genuinely distinguishing claims reduce to:

1. Environment-dependent RAR scatter (`/cdm-discrimination`): "small and environment-dependent" vs MOND's ~0, CDM's 0.11–0.16 dex. Claimed validated at p = 5×10⁻⁶ on SPARC+environment catalogs.
2. Weaker External Field Effect (`/mond-unification`): ~0.3–0.4× of MOND's EFE, discriminable via tidal dwarf galaxy kinematics.
3. Wide-binary anomaly density dependence (TEST-02): the headline falsifier.
4. BAO peak shift ~10⁻⁴ between regions (TEST-04).
5. Oscillatory modulation at λ ~ 500 Mpc in galaxy cluster separations (TEST-07).
6. Anesthesia phase transition at Φ_crit = 3.5 ± 0.2 (TEST-11).

Everything else on the advanced track is either known physics in different vocabulary (MRH → correlation length / Markov blanket / particle horizon), a reparametrization of an existing result (η-reachability ≈ Abrikosov-Gor'kov), or a reproduction of a known relation (a₀ = cH₀/(2π), noted by Milgrom 1983, derived by McCulloch 2007 and Verlinde 2017).

**Now the novelty-by-novelty reality check:**

*(1) RAR environment dependence, p = 5×10⁻⁶.* This is the only claim I'd currently rank as a genuine contribution — if the analysis holds up. But the `/galaxy-rotation` page itself admits "The remaining 86% is unexplained by the coherence model" and "Synchronism adds a small, detectable effect on top of what MOND already provides — it does not replace MOND's success." So the quantitative content is a residual correlation, not a rotation curve theory. A p-value this small on ~14,000 galaxies is suspicious without a null-model treatment: environment correlates with Hubble type, surface brightness, inclination distribution, gas content, and distance bias — any of which can induce residual-environment correlations in SPARC that have nothing to do with coherence. I'd need to see (a) the specific environment estimator, (b) the null model used to compute the p-value, (c) bootstraps over galaxy subtypes, before treating this as evidence.

*(2) EFE suppression 0.3–0.4× MOND.* This is potentially the sharpest prediction on the site. MOND's EFE (Milgrom 1983, Famaey & McGaugh 2012) predicts specific kinematic signatures in satellite galaxies in external fields, and there are recent papers (Asencio et al. 2022, Chae 2023) claiming detection consistent with MOND's full EFE. A 0.3–0.4× suppression would already be in tension with those results — but the site doesn't engage with them. Either it should claim those results are wrong, or the prediction should be reduced.

*(3) Wide-binary density dependence (TEST-02).* The honest falsifier. Current state of the art I'd want engaged: Hernandez 2022, Chae 2023, Banik 2024, Pittordis & Sutherland 2023. Banik argued the apparent anomaly could be explained by unresolved hierarchical triple contamination, which *itself correlates with stellar density*. So Synchronism's "density dependence" prediction has an identical signature to the most-cited null hypothesis. The site doesn't acknowledge this and doesn't offer a contamination model to distinguish. Until it does, TEST-02 is not a clean discriminator even when the data arrives.

*(4) BAO peak shift ~10⁻⁴ between regions.* DESI DR2 (2024) measures BAO distances to ~0.5% precision per tracer; the cross-tracer (α_iso) has internal consistency at the 10⁻³ level. A 10⁻⁴ *region-dependent* shift is below DESI DR2 discrimination by an order of magnitude. The kill criterion "BAO identical everywhere to 10⁻⁵ precision" is a decade away from reach. This is not a near-term falsifier; listing it as Tier 1 (zero-cost, existing data) overstates what DESI+SDSS can currently do. Also: there's a genuinely interesting 2–3σ DESI DR2 hint of evolving dark energy (w₀wₐCDM favored over ΛCDM) — a framework claiming to speak to BAO should engage with it. The site doesn't mention DESI's w₀wₐ tension at all.

*(5) Oscillatory modulation at λ ~ 500 Mpc (TEST-07).* This is probably the most problematic claim. The galaxy power spectrum P(k) has been measured from BOSS/eBOSS/DESI across the relevant wavenumber range (k ≈ 0.012 h/Mpc corresponds to λ ≈ 500 Mpc). At that scale there is no detected oscillation beyond the BAO feature at 147 Mpc. If Synchronism predicts an additional oscillation with *any* observable amplitude in cluster separations at 500 Mpc, it's already in tension with existing P(k) data from thousands of papers. The site should either (a) quantify an amplitude that sits safely below current sensitivity — in which case this is not a Tier 1 test on existing data, or (b) engage with the existing measurements that appear to rule this out. Neither is done.

*(6) Φ_crit = 3.5 ± 0.2 (TEST-11).* Φ is Giulio Tononi's integrated information measure. The specific threshold value is Synchronism-novel, but the underlying framework (Φ, integration, anesthesia causing phase transition in consciousness) is fully IIT. The prediction is "sharp phase transition in coherence at universal threshold Φ_crit" versus the standard-model null "variable loss-of-consciousness thresholds without clustering." This is actually a testable claim — but the site doesn't note that IIT's calculational tractability is strongly contested (Doerig et al. 2019; the calculation of Φ on real neural data requires approximations that introduce their own scale). A $150K, 12-month proposal to test a threshold on an observable whose *calculation* is under active debate is not ready to run. Also: whether propofol-induced anesthesia is a phase transition has been asked (Tagliazucchi et al. 2016, Luppi et al. 2022, Barttfeld et al. 2015) with no consensus; "sharp vs gradual" decays on what observable you pick.

**The Born rule "derivation" doesn't add content.**
`/born-rule` offers three sentences: (1) coherence is conserved, (2) probability is proportional to branch coherence, (3) therefore |α|². This is not a derivation — it's a restatement. Gleason's theorem requires frame independence, Zurek's envariance requires entangled subsystem invariance, Deutsch-Wallace requires rationality axioms; each of these *does* mathematical work. "Coherence conservation" as stated here is the Born rule renamed. To the site's credit, it admits: "Whether this is truly more fundamental than the Born rule itself, or just a reformulation at the same level, is debatable. . . may contain circular reasoning." Given the admission, the page should not be on the Advanced learning path as a derivation.

**The "theorem" claim vs the actual content.**
Pass 3 caught the mean-field issue on the homepage. What I'll add from my perspective: the `/parameter-derivations` page itself says "integrating out momenta introduces temperature- and mass-dependent factors, not a clean factor of 2." So the site *knows internally* that the factor of 2 doesn't cleanly drop out. Then the homepage still says "theorem." That inconsistency isn't honest-assessment-level friction — it's the core claim versus the author's own notes, and whichever page is right, the other needs to change.

**ρ_crit is galaxy-specific.**
`/parameter-derivations` gives ρ_crit = A·V_flat² with A = 4π/(α²GR₀²) ≈ 0.029 at "5% Error." This is the first time I've seen it explicitly: ρ_crit isn't a universal constant, it's set per-galaxy by the flat rotation velocity. That changes the framing from "one equation describes everything" to "a two-parameter functional form fits rotation curves." Which is a fine empirical statement, but it's not what the homepage promises.
