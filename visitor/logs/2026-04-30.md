### Journey

I landed on the home page and read the headline: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* That hooked me — that's the kind of question Veritasium would open with. But then directly under it sat `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` and my eyes glazed. I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ρ is. I noticed the buttons, though — "Start Exploring", "See What Failed", "Honest Assessment". The "See What Failed" button actually made me trust the site more, weirdly. Most pop-sci pages don't lead with their failures.

I clicked **Start Exploring** and landed on `/first-encounter`. The first concept was "Presence (ρ)" with an electron-vs-neutron-star example, which is the kind of thing I can grasp. But the very next sentence introduced "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)" as if I should already know what that meant. I bounced to `/learning-paths` hoping for a friendlier on-ramp.

Learning Paths offered three tiers: Beginner ("No physics background needed"), Intermediate, Advanced. Great. But when I clicked the Beginner card I got a 404, so I just typed `/why-synchronism` (one of the listed beginner steps). That page was actually the best one I read — it said honestly that physics is fragmented and asked whether one function could span it all. It even admitted melting points were 53% off and the superconductor result was "a reparametrization of Abrikosov-Gor'kov pair-breaking (known since 1960)." That's a sentence I don't understand at all, but I respect that it was there.

I clicked **Honest Assessment** because the home page kept inviting me to. This is where I got confused. The honest page told me there are **0 confirmed predictions**. But the home page had been showing me "14,760 galaxies fit." And then `/galaxy-rotation` said the framework "explains only 14% of the unexplained orbital behavior." So... did it work or didn't it? The site is being honest with me, but the three pages tell three different emotional stories.

I tried the **Coherence Explorer** tool. I dragged the γ slider. The curve flattened. There was a label that said "Quantum (γ > 1.4)" — at least that gave me a vibe. But the x-axis said `log₁₀(ρ)` and the y-axis said `C` from 0 to 1. I had no idea what density values I was actually moving through, or what "coherence" meant in everyday terms. I dragged it back and forth for about 30 seconds and gave up.

I tried the **γ Calculator** next. It wanted me to type in "Ncorr (number of correlated particles)." I have no idea how to count correlated particles in anything. There were preset examples — "ideal gas," "liquid water," "enzyme sites" — which I clicked, and they spat out numbers and labels like "Boundary (γ ≈ 1)." I felt like I was looking at a calculator someone left running for someone else.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary

Pages invent compound and ad-hoc labels that aren't in the glossary, and the same result is described with three different severity vocabularies depending on where you land.

### Journey

The glossary at `/glossary` was a pleasant surprise: 33 terms including the six validation badges (Validated, Untested, Failed, Speculative, Reparametrization, Strongly Supported), each with cross-links to the pages that use them.

I expected to confirm that the rest of the site honored the badge taxonomy. It mostly does *not*. The home page itself uses **"new ontology with testable consequences"**, **"untested with 8-way convergence"**, **"structural failure"**, **"89% Boundary-Consistent"**, and **"reparametrization"** — only one of those (the last) is in the glossary as a defined badge. `/key-claims` then uses **"Failed — Mechanism Under Revision"**, **"Consistent with PRL 2024"**, and **"Untested — 8-Way Convergence"** — none of those compound forms are in the glossary either. `/honest-assessment` adds **"Dimensional Analysis"**, **"Negative Verdict"**, and **"Candidate Prediction"**. Counting the same thing two ways was the next problem. The home page advertises "14,760 galaxies fit"; `/honest-assessment` says "14,585 galaxies (ALFALFA-SDSS)". Same result, two numbers, no bridge.

A reader with reasonable attention will read these as three different statuses. They are the same status.

`/test-catalog` introduced a fourth vocabulary: **"kill criterion"** for falsifiability requirements. That's a fine engineering term, but it sits in parallel with the validation-badge system rather than mapping onto it. A tier-by-tier overview is the right structure; the per-test detail rows below it should adopt the same six-badge taxonomy as the glossary.

`/learning-paths` clarified one issue Pass 1 raised. The path *cards* with "Start this track →" do link directly to the first lesson (`/why-synchronism` for Beginner) — they work. The 404 Pass 1 hit was almost certainly from clicking the *card heading* itself rather than the action link. That's still bad UX (people click headings) but it's a different problem from "the route doesn't exist."

I tested two interactive tools different from Pass 1's choices.

**Phase Boundary Visualizer** — slider from 0 to ~3.5 with labeled regions (Classical / Boundary / Quantum). Useful for orientation. Gap: only one example point ("γ=2 (galaxies)") has a precise position on the axis, while the prose lists six other systems (water, enzymes, neural firing, consciousness threshold, etc.) without anchoring them to specific γ values. Either anchor them or drop the list.

The MRH page (`/mrh`) was the structural weak spot. It opens with "Markov blankets" and "probabilistic graphical models" before plain language, doesn't link to the glossary's MRH entry, has a "Next" with no "Previous," and uses no validation badges despite explicitly discussing untested protocols. Compare to `/first-encounter`, which defines MRH inline ("its Markov Relevancy Horizon, or MRH") on first use — that pattern should propagate.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (home, honest-assessment, parameter-derivations, coherence-function, galaxy-rotation, key-claims, gamma-boundary, test-catalog, tier-1-existing, research-philosophy, interactive-tools, plus three tools)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I can read what's claimed; the math is mostly transparent)
- **Would return?**: yes — to monitor whether the missing ΔBIC measurement gets done and whether the chemistry circularity gets audited
- **Overall impression**: The site is **more honest than the front page implies**. Buried in `/parameter-derivations` and `/honest-assessment` is an unusually candid self-audit ("the Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen," η = Abrikosov-Gor'kov, 0 confirmed predictions). But the home page still leads with C(ρ) = tanh(γ·log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)) as if it's a discovered law. The honest assessment hasn't propagated to the front of the funnel.

### Journey

I went straight to `/parameter-derivations` because that's where a physicist looks first. What I found surprised me. The page **explicitly disclaims a derivation of its own central equation**: *"tanh is a phenomenological choice from the Landau-universality family of sigmoid functions… The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen. These are structurally different justifications."* That is the right thing for an honest paper to say. It is also exactly opposite to the impression the home page gives.

Walking through the form C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)):

- **The tanh is an ansatz.** Mean-field Ising gives tanh because of the self-consistency loop m = tanh(βJz·m); the field is the order parameter is the input. Here, ρ is exogenous — there is no self-consistency, no fixed-point equation, no order parameter being solved for. The functional form is a sigmoid that happens to look like tanh; logistic, erf, or (1 + (ρ_crit/ρ)^n)^{-1} would do equivalently. The site admits this. The home page does not.
- **The +1 in log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1) is a regularizer**, keeping the argument ≥ 0 at low density. The page calls it that. Fine — but advertising the form as "the equation that describes reality from quantum to cosmic" is then a category error. It is a smooth interpolating function with a knob.
- **γ = 2/√N_corr.** The 1/√N is honest central-limit-theorem scaling for correlated ensembles. The factor of 2 is admitted to be unjustified ("not rigorously derived — integrating out momenta introduces temperature- and mass-dependent factors, not a clean factor of 2"). With N_corr = 4 you get γ = 1, the "boundary." Why 4? Because that's where the formula was tuned to land.
- **ρ_crit is data-driven.** ρ_crit = A·V_flat². V_flat is observed. The constant A is fit. So ρ_crit is parametrized by the very velocity scale you're trying to predict. That's not a free-parameter scandal — every interpolating function does this — but it's the opposite of "derived."

Then I checked the **MOND relation** a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². The page is fully transparent: this has been noted since Milgrom (1983); McCulloch, Verlinde, and Smolin all derive it; it's dimensional analysis with a chosen factor of 2π. The 10% error vs the observed value is consistent with what you get from any cosmic-coincidence argument involving c, H₀, and a numerical factor of order unity. As a derivation of MOND's acceleration scale this contributes nothing the literature didn't already have.

`/galaxy-rotation` was the most informative page for evaluating actual content. The framework uses **the McGaugh et al. (2016) RAR interpolating function** g_obs = g_bar / (1 − exp(−√(g_bar/a₀))) — i.e., MOND's interpolating function — and adds an environmental-scatter ansatz on top. The reported σ_int values (0.057 dex on SPARC, 0.086 dex on ALFALFA-SDSS) are the **standard MOND fits**; they are not a Synchronism-specific result. The environmental scatter, the actual novel piece, explains **R² = 0.14 of total RAR variance**, with the remaining 86% accounted for by mass-to-light corrections that MOND already invokes. Most damning: the page itself notes *"Missing measurement: The incremental value of adding the environmental term has not been quantified via ΔBIC."* Without ΔBIC against MOND-only, the claim that the environmental term is real reduces to "we found a small p-value (5×10⁻⁶) on a residual that explains 14% of variance." That's a detection, not a prediction; with 14,585 galaxies, p = 5×10⁻⁶ is what you get from minor systematics in M/L, environmental selection, or beam smearing.

`/gamma-boundary` was where I got actively concerned. The page reports r = 0.982 with sound velocity and r = 0.979 with electronegativity across 1,703 chemical phenomena. Two issues:

1. **What is N_corr for "sound velocity in copper"?** γ = 2/√N_corr requires a correlated-particle count. The site never tells me how to compute N_corr from a chemical compound. If γ is being inferred *from* sound velocity (or from quantities that depend on it), then "γ correlates with sound velocity at r = 0.982" is correlating a derived quantity with its own input. The page does not foreclose this.
2. **The "template-based" admission.** Sessions 134–2660 used "similar analysis patterns across phenomena." That is exactly the failure mode — generative-AI sessions producing self-similar fits across superficially distinct cases — that would inflate r artificially. The page admits the issue but does not quantify the inflation.

A grad student verdict: r = 0.98 across 1,703 chemical phenomena would be a Nobel-level result if real. The fact that it isn't being heralded that way, combined with the "template-based" caveat and the unspecified N_corr definition, tells me the authors themselves don't fully believe it. Their prose ("89% consistent with the γ ≈ 1 boundary — a weaker but still meaningful statement") is the appropriate hedge. The home-page card "89% Boundary-Consistent" is acceptable phrasing, but the underlying claim needs an independence audit before any of those numbers should be cited externally.

`/honest-assessment` is the page I would point a colleague to. The η = Abrikosov-Gor'kov pair-breaking reduction is named explicitly (good — that's the kind of identification that takes courage). The 53% melting-point error, the 6.5× YBCO Tc failure, the dark-matter sign error, the 0/7 fractal-coherence-bridge predictions — all kept visible as Failed. The discovery rate is reported as 1.4% (47 / 3,302). For an exploratory framework this is the right denominator to be honest about.

`/key-claims` listed three top-line claims. Of these:
- **Claim 1 (QM as synchronization)** — admitted post-diction. No quantitative prediction differs from standard decoherence. Reparametrization, with one untested protocol claim ("resynchronization outperforms isolation").
- **Claim 2 (consciousness threshold C ≈ 0.50)** — the only claim that's structurally novel, but the threshold value is **the trivial midpoint of tanh(x∈[0,∞))**. Eight "independent" derivations all converging on 0.499 ± 0.012 is what you'd expect when all eight use tanh-based or ½-symmetric models. The page admits the eight aren't independent. There is no calibration to map measured neural EEG/fMRI/integrated-information quantities to the C-axis, which means the "kill criterion" cannot currently be evaluated against any dataset.
- **Claim 3 (dark matter as decoherence)** — Failed (sign error, structural). Site is honest.

`/test-catalog` and `/tier-1-existing`: the kill-criterion structure is the strongest thing the site has going for it methodologically. But:
- **TEST-04 (BAO coherence modulation, 10⁻⁵ precision out to 2000 Mpc)**: DESI's BAO peak position is currently nailed to ~0.5–1% (≈10⁻³). A 10⁻⁵ kill criterion is two orders of magnitude tighter than current observational reach. This is functionally untestable — and Maintainer's recent note about "DESI silence resolved as drift 2026-04-27" (in the project memory index) suggests this exact issue has been seen before.
- **TEST-09, TEST-10**: explicitly MOND-shared, can only discriminate via null result.
- **TEST-01 + TEST-05**: as the page notes, these are not independent. They measure the same prediction on overlapping samples.
- **TEST-03 (TFR residual explains <20% of scatter as kill criterion)**: the *current* number being reported is R² = 0.14, which is already below 20%. So depending on which way the kill criterion is read, the framework may already be in trouble on its own data.

Net: Of 10 Tier-1 tests, only 3–4 carry actual discriminating power, and one of them (BAO at 10⁻⁵) is currently inaccessible. The kill-criterion framework is the right idea; the kill criteria themselves need a calibration pass against what experiments can actually deliver.
