## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /key-claims, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've seen come out of Reddit. It tells me up front that the big idea didn't work, but once I'm past the headline I'm buried in acronyms and I struggle to find the "so what" for someone like me.

### Journey
I came from Reddit expecting a "one equation explains everything" crank site. The landing headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") seemed to confirm that. The very next lines threw me, though: this is an *open research notebook*, the proposal "was tested and did not hold as stated," and the scoreboard reads **0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations**. That got my attention, because I've never seen a theory's homepage lead with its own failure. Then the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` hit me. I don't know what tanh or ln are. There's a plain-English line under it, though, so I didn't bounce.

I followed "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)". The marching-band-versus-crowd analogy finally made "coherence" click for me: how much a bunch of things act together. The page then turns into a wall of names I don't know (ALFALFA, SDSS, Tully–Fisher, BTFR, RAR, Cassini, YBCO, Bell/CHSH, Curie–Weiss, Naka–Rushton) and a "p=5×10⁻⁶" with no explanation. I skimmed. I did pick up the gist from lines like "Where it could be told apart from existing physics, it lost," and the superconductor example ("predicted 607 K; the real value is 93 K") was the clearest failure for a non-expert. The "(~8 min)" estimate felt about right if you skim. Reading properly took me longer.

/first-encounter was the best page for me. It's broken into steps with Continue buttons and uses a dimmer switch and a dial as analogies. It even says this "coherence" is *not* quantum coherence, which I'd have assumed. Section G ("Where It Fails") is short and blunt, which I liked. Section C lost me with "γ = 2 loses to MOND on real rotation curves", because I didn't yet know what γ = 2 meant or why it was the framework's number.

Next I tried the tools (below). The Galaxy Plotter was the high point: it finally explained what a rotation curve *is* in one sentence and showed me the dark matter puzzle with my own eyes.

/honest-assessment has an "If you read nothing else" box whose first line is "The one equation did not hold as stated." That's perfect. Below the box, the page is enormous. I scrolled through a refutation ledger, badge definitions, "status lifecycle verbs" and more without reaching the end. For me, the box is the page.

/key-claims surprised me. The consciousness equation is still on the site as "Speculative." The page is frank that it is "currently unrunnable" because nothing links a brain measurement to the score. The dark matter claim is "Failed." I still couldn't tell *why* a site that says its main equation failed keeps a consciousness formula built on that same equation.

The glossary (113 terms, with a filter box and an A–Z index) defined tanh in one friendly sentence ("smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1"). I wish the landing page had linked to that right next to the equation.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — intuitive. Two sliders and four γ presets. The crowd-versus-marching-band caption is great. One thing confused me: the default readout is C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, and I assumed the "critical" density would be the halfway point. The page anticipates this ("not the curve's midpoint"; the 0.5 point is at about 2.07·ρcrit), which helped, but the word "crit" sets up the wrong expectation. The note that the axis "has no units" and models no real material told me this is a shape toy.
- **γ Calculator** — worked — confusing in an interesting way. Type in N_corr ("how many particles move together") and get γ = 2/√N_corr. The page openly says the formula is backwards and is kept "so you can watch that happen." I respect that, but without knowing what a BEC or a BCS superconductor is, I couldn't watch it happen. The ideal gas (γ = 2) versus superconductor (γ ≈ 0.0006) numbers needed a sentence telling me which one *should* be sharper.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — the most intuitive thing on the site. Pick a galaxy (DDO 154 by default), see the gray "visible matter only" line sag below the blue measured dots. That's the dark matter problem in one picture. The green MOND line fits. The violet Synchronism line "gets the shape wrong," and the add-version barely leaves the gray line. Even I could see the framework losing. The hidden amber "drawn to fit" curve behind a checkbox is a nice honesty touch.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the one equation failed, what is the project still trying to find out? The landing page says "the broader question of the frame is still open." What *is* that broader question, in one sentence?
2. Why does the consciousness formula survive when the dark matter version failed? Do they rely on the same curve?
3. What is ρcrit physically for a galaxy or a glass of water? The glossary says "a saturation knee." Knee of what, measured how?
4. "3,308 autonomous research sessions": autonomous by whom? Is this AI-run research? That alone would interest a Reddit audience, and I couldn't find it explained on the pages I read.
5. Is there anything left to test that a non-scientist could watch for in the news (e.g., "DESI DR3" was mentioned)? When, and what would it mean?

### Verdict
I came expecting a crank site and found the opposite: a project that leads with "it didn't work" and lets me watch it fail in the Galaxy Plotter. That's a rare and genuinely compelling hook. The beginner path doesn't stay beginner for long, though. By step 1's testing section I was skimming acronym soup, and the deep pages (Honest Assessment) are written for experts. The tools and the "If you read nothing else" box carried me. The story I'm missing is *why I should keep caring now that the answer is no*.

### Journey
**Landing.** The top nav has 10 items, and the footer repeats them plus First Encounter, Key Claims, What Synchronism Is Not, the GitHub archive and "Report an Issue". That's consistent. The hero then offers **three competing entry points** in about 60 words: "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)", "Or skip to step 2: First Encounter →" and "See What Failed". Below them is a parenthetical routing paragraph for "No physics background? / Physicist? / Researcher?". The Beginner Path parenthetical is a single 70-word sentence that includes "so the cliff is visible before you walk off it". It's honest, but it reads as an internal note.

Acronym hygiene on the landing page is mixed. MOND, MRH, A2ACW, DESI and BAO are expanded at first use, which is good. SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, Ωm, N_eff, V_flat and dex all appear in the scoreboard *before* the abbreviations key that defines them. The tanh gloss contradicts itself on one page: the "Never seen these symbols?" box says "**tanh squashes any number into the range 0–1**", and the symbol list three lines later says "tanh = S-shaped saturation function: maps any real number to **(−1,+1)**". Both are defensible in context, since C stays in [0,1) because the argument is ≥ 0. But a reader comparing the two lines sees a contradiction, and the glossary agrees with the second ("between −1 and +1").

The landing page is about 35,000 characters of text, and a large share of it is inline "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)". One of them reads "The cell as it read before **today**:" inside a note dated 2026-09-15. A relative date in permanent text goes stale on the day it's published.

**Learning Paths.** This is well built: 7 paths, per-step times, and an honest correction note about the two groupings not covering the same pages. Three small defects:
1. "Separately, 1 of the 7 paths (Beginner) **include** an interactive tool": the verb should be "includes".
2. "If you want the equation explained rather than stated, start at **Equation Anatomy**" is a recommendation for a page that appears in **none** of the seven paths. The tool's own page agrees: "this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step."
3. The Advanced path's last step is labelled "Test Roadmap (26 tests: 24 in the original registry + 2 added later)". The Test Roadmap's own Discrimination box then says "**Of these 24** proposed tests, 0 selected Synchronism…". The denominator changes between paragraphs of the same page, with no sentence saying whether the 2 later tests are included.

**Badges.** The canonical table on /honest-assessment ("The nine badges in one table", about 11k characters into a 158k-character page) is exactly what I'd want: one line per badge, the two families, deprecated tags named. The anchor `#validation-badge-definitions` is linked from the landing page and the Tools page, which is good. The problem is that **the same definitions are restated in at least three places, in different words**:
- Sidelined, canonical: "was in focus, now parked; reactivation condition stated". Test Roadmap guide: "not currently pursued; reasons and reactivation condition documented".
- Audited-Negative, canonical: "closed audit finding; durable; does not move". Test Roadmap: "closed audit finding on a historical track; durable record".

The meanings match today, but copies drift. Single-source the strings (the CLAUDE.md conventions suggest `terms.ts` exists for this).

**Operational states are classified inconsistently. This is the sharpest terminology defect I found.**
- Test Roadmap: "Operational states (not badges): Self-Eliminating-or-Tie | Unrunnable as stated | Withdrawn | **Underpowered as registered**".
- Tier 1: "operational states (Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, **Artifact Lesson** …) · status lifecycle verbs (**Underpowered as registered**, Demoted to Tier-2, Closed …)".

So "Underpowered as registered" is an operational state on one page and a lifecycle verb on another. "Artifact Lesson" is an operational state on Tier 1 and on the Tools page ("the site's own term, per Tier 1") but is missing from the Test Roadmap's list. A reader can't tell whether the list is exhaustive or which axis a label sits on, and the Honest Assessment note "Operational states and lifecycle verbs are not badges" assumes the reader can tell them apart.

**Tier 1 page badge vs Test Roadmap.** Tier 1's page-level badge reads "**Failed** — all 5 numbered executed tests … + 1 disfavored but underpowered as registered, not counted as a refutation (TEST-04a)". The Test Roadmap says "Underpowered as Registered (**not Failed**, not counted as a refutation): TEST-04a." One page puts TEST-04a under a Failed badge and the other says it is not Failed. The badge contract on Honest Assessment says text after the dash is "a free-text finding descriptor". This descriptor runs to about 600 characters, which makes it a paragraph wearing a badge.

**Breadcrumbs.** They are present on every page I checked, which is good. The intermediate crumbs point at pages, not hubs:
- "Predictions & Experiments" → `/test-catalog` (titled *Test Roadmap*). On the Test Roadmap page itself the breadcrumb reads "Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap", so the parent crumb links to the current page. `/predictions` redirects (307) to `/test-catalog` as well.
- "Methodology" → `/research-philosophy`, with the same self-loop on Research Philosophy ("Home / Methodology / Research Philosophy"). `/methodology` 307s there too.

There are no section hub pages, so two crumbs are effectively synonyms for leaf pages. Honest Assessment handles membership in two paths well: it shows both "Beginner Path Step 5 of 5" and "Methodology Track Step 6 of 6". That's the right pattern.

**Tools page.** The labelling scheme is overbuilt but self-aware. Each card has Kind, Level and Status, plus "Badge on its page", and the intro spends about 250 words explaining that the four labels are different axes, followed by a revision note about two personas misreading them. When a legend needs a legend, there are too many axes. URL slugs also lag renamed titles: "Equation Anatomy" lives at `/equation-walkthrough`, and "Crossover Regime Visualizer" lives at `/phase-boundary-visualizer`. The second page says so itself, which is good, but the slug still bakes in the "phase boundary" framing the rename was meant to remove. That isn't beginner copy.

**Glossary.** This is the site's best asset. I checked 18 terms the other pages left undefined (BCS, BEC, SPARC, ΔBIC, BTFR, RAR, Cassini, CHSH, dex, Retrodiction, Compander, Artifact Lesson, Withdrawn, Underpowered, QUMOND, EFE, L2/L3) and **all 18 have entries**. That's well caught, but it means one acronym names both a physics concept and a badge family. The glossary says "Some pages highlight terms with a dotted underline … Most pages link here instead." From what I saw, most pages do neither at the point of first use. The definitions exist; they aren't wired in.

*Method note: I pulled the raw page text and recomputed every number I quote below in Python. I didn't log anything from a fetch summary without checking it against the raw text.*

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "interesting AI project", so I expected numerology. I went straight to **/parameter-derivations**. Its first box retitles the page "Parameter Calibration & Honest Ansätze" and states "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations". That pre-empts the paper I'd have written about it.

**What I checked and found correct** (all recomputed):
- **Closed form**: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. At γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), so the "MOND's simple μ with the scale doubled" identity is exact. The small-x limit C ≈ γx and the large-x limit 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ} are right. The site badges this Reparametrization and calls it "the strongest reparametrization on the site". I agree. The R0–R5 control table (the function-only swap ties MOND at 0.988×, and the 2.10× loss came from changing the knee and the floor form) is exactly the control I'd have demanded.
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π** at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² (13% below 1.20). **Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG)** = 119 M☉/pc². The site correctly notes these are one number, not two, and correctly renames the Σ₀ row as Milgrom's Σ_M rather than "Freeman's law re-expressed".
- **A**: 4π/(G·R₀²) with β_J = 1 and R₀ = 8 kpc is 4.57×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻², so the ratio to 0.029 is 635×. Inverting, β_J·R₀ = 317 pc, which matches the stated 4.5 × 70 pc = 315 pc. ρ_crit = 0.029·150² = 652 M☉/pc³ = 4.4×10⁻²⁰ g/cm³, and the 2.5×10⁴ gap to the SPARC midplane median is right.
- **Curie–Weiss argument**: the finite-size rounding width does scale as N^{−1/2}, so a mean-field sharpness grows as √N. The sign-inversion critique of γ = 2/√N_corr is correct. The CLT-for-correlated-variables objection is also correct.
- **Ceiling arithmetic**: 1/Ω_m = 3.17. Ω_m/Ω_b = 6.39 gives an f_DM ceiling of 0.844. f_DM = 0.927 requires B ≥ 13.7. Under ΛCDM, B_max(z) = E²/[Ω_m(1+z)³] = 1.27 at z = 1 and 1.08 at z = 2. Capping B at 3.17 under the division wiring needs x ≥ 0.177 at γ = 2. All correct.
- **/galaxy-rotation μ-form vs g_bar-keyed division** at γ = ½ and g_bar/a₀ = 0.01: solving g_bar = y²/(y+2) gives y = 0.1465 (deep MOND gives 0.141), while g_bar/C(g_bar) = g_bar + 2 = 2.01. That is a correct and genuinely useful point: the "identity" only holds for the μ-form keying.
- **/gamma-boundary retraction**: dC/dt = 4γt^{2γ−1}/(t^{2γ}+1)², so C′(0) = γ and C″(0) = −γ. I checked concavity myself: the numerator −(2γ+1)t^{2γ} + (2γ−1) ≤ −2 < 0 for all x ≥ 0, so there's no inflection point. κ(0) = γ/(1+γ²)^{3/2} peaks at 1/√2 with κ = 0.385. The large-γ limit of max dC/d ln x is 0.448 (u·sech²u at u ≈ 0.77), and the page's 0.446 is close enough. The retraction is right, and the unit-dependence remark about κ is the correct reason to dismiss it.

**What I found wrong.** The main one is the Ciocan level table on /parameter-derivations, item 4. The page states Ciocan's z ≈ 1 value as 2.38 (+0.12/−0.10, 95% CI, "1σ ≈ 0.06") and the anchor as 1.00 ± 0.02 (1σ). Branch (A) then gives 1.00 × E(1) = 1.79 and 1.04 × 1.79 = 1.86. The deficits are 0.59 and 0.52. With 1σ ≈ 0.051–0.06 (plus the anchor's 0.036 in quadrature for row 1), that comes to **≈ 8.4–9.5σ and ≈ 8.7–10.2σ**. The table prints **"~19σ low"** and **"~18σ low"**, and the correction note says the previous "+9.4σ" and "+9.8σ" had "divided [95% CIs] as 1σ; on the measurement error alone they are about twice that". The arithmetic says otherwise. 9.4σ and 9.8σ were *already* the 1σ values. The genuinely 95%-as-1σ numbers were the 08-01 "2.3–5.9σ" (0.59/0.10 = 5.9). The 2026-10-12 correction looks like it converted a second time. The same section's *other* numbers support this. "Constant a₀ … roughly 21σ (1σ ≈ 0.06)" is 1.18/0.06 ≈ 20σ. Branch (A)'s "~4σ" on measurement error alone for the McGaugh anchor is 0.23/0.06 = 3.8σ. Both are consistent with 1σ ≈ 0.06, while the top two table rows are not. Separately, the Vărăşteanu row prints 2.91, but 1.69 × 1.79 = 3.03, which gives −2.7σ rather than −2.3σ. That row may evaluate E(z) relative to the MIGHTEE-HI sample redshift (that would make it 1.72), but the page doesn't say so. None of this changes the verdict, since the row is declared level-non-discriminating and the slope is what matters. It is, however, a correction that introduced an error into a table about correcting errors.
