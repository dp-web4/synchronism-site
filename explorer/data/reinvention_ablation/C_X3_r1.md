**Short answer:** I don't know of any publication with this exact construction, a period-dependent inertial mass m_eff = m[1 + κ ln(1 + P/τ)]⁻¹. I'm fairly confident it is not a published model, but I can't prove a negative.

**Nearest published work**
- **Milgrom's modified inertia (MOND).** Milgrom, Ann. Phys. 229, 384 (1994), "Dynamics with a nonstandard inertia-acceleration relation" (also his 1983 ApJ papers). Inertia there depends on acceleration, not period. In the deep-MOND regime it also makes orbital velocities larger than Newtonian. The scale a₀ ~ cH₀/2π is the same cosmological coincidence that your τ ∝ 2π/H₀ invokes. Your 10⁻⁶ prefactor is a free fit parameter, not a derived number.
- **McCulloch's "quantised inertia".** MNRAS 376, L6 (2007), on the Pioneer anomaly, and later papers, including wide-binary claims (uncertain on exact citations). It ties modified inertia to a Hubble-horizon or Unruh cutoff, but the dependence is on acceleration, not period.
- **Mach/Sciama-type inertia.** Sciama, MNRAS 113, 34 (1953). Inertia comes from cosmic matter, with no period dependence.
- **Wide-binary tests.** Hernandez et al., MNRAS 2012 (uncertain on the volume). Chae, ApJ 952, 128 (2023), found a boost. Banik et al., MNRAS 527, 4573 (2024), found none. Both used Gaia data. They constrain any model with enhanced velocities at wide separations, including yours, but they don't propose this one.

**Physical concerns (my own assessment)**
1. **Non-locality.** P is defined only for bound, closed orbits, so there is no covariant local rule for unbound, eccentric, or perturbed motion. The dynamics are not derivable from a standard Lagrangian without extra structure.
2. **Equivalence principle.** Inertia changes but gravitational mass does not, so m_i ≠ m_g in an orbit-dependent way. Lunar laser ranging and Eöt-Wash tests probe composition dependence, not this, but the framework is non-standard.
3. **Large-scale behavior.** Galactic orbits have P ~ 10⁸ yr, so the factor is 1 + 0.31 ln(10³) ≈ 3.1. A logarithm doesn't give asymptotically flat rotation curves or the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation, which MOND reproduces naturally.
4. **Intermediate scales.** Oort-cloud comets (P ~ 10⁶ yr) would be affected. Open clusters and Kuiper-belt dynamics would need checking.
5. **Fine-tuning.** The values τ = 10⁻⁶ × 2π/H₀ and κ = 0.31 appear to be fitted rather than derived.

If a source exists, it would most likely be in the fringe modified-inertia or time-dependent-gravity literature. I can't recall one, so treat any such match as uncertain. I'd search arXiv for "period-dependent inertia" and "scale-dependent inertial mass" before claiming novelty.
