## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Interactive Tools hub, Coherence Explorer, What Synchronism Is Not, Dark Matter Reframed, Honest Assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest "theory of everything" site I've ever seen, so honest that it took me a while to figure out what the theory was before learning it had lost.

### Journey
I came from Reddit expecting either a crank site or a big claim. The headline "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" hooked me. The next line, "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated.", hooked me harder. I've never seen a site lead with its own failure. Within 30 seconds I knew three things: it's an AI-run notebook, no outside physicist has reviewed it, and the galaxy idea "collapsed onto MOND." I didn't know what MOND was. The landing page is long, though. After the intro I hit "Scientific Outcomes" with "6 executed refutations (3 to 8 depending on counting rules)", "1 underpowered retrodiction", a refutation table with formulas like `Ca = Ωm + (1 − Ωm)·x/(1+x)`, and a "1.4% internal-consistency survival rate". That's where I started skimming. The "Where to Start" cards at the bottom are what I wanted, but they're near the end. The "Start Here" button in the top nav is what actually saved me.

**Why Synchronism? (Start Here).** "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a good opener. The crowd vs. marching band picture is the best thing on the site; I got it instantly. The dimmer-switch image for the S-curve worked too. The "What happened, in three lines" box was great: one crowding equation, tested on galaxies, lost. The page also told me I could stop here as a beginner, which I appreciated. Everything below that was cards full of numbers (R²=0.14, p=5×10⁻⁶, r=0.982, 607 K vs 93 K), YBCO, Debye model and Tully–Fisher. I skimmed all of it. One small thing: the landing page told me this page takes "about 8 min", but the page itself showed no reading time, and the whole page clearly takes much longer than 8 minutes. I wasn't sure whether "8 min" meant the beginner part only.

**First Encounter.** I liked the Continue-button, one-section-at-a-time format. It felt like a guided tour, and the marching band came back, which made it feel connected. "Presence = density, but only counted within your neighbourhood (MRH)" made sense: an atom doesn't "feel" the Andromeda galaxy. Then Section C told me the main formula for γ "failed" because the sign came out backwards, and that the fitted value "just reproduces MOND." That was the third time MOND came up and I still didn't know what it was. Section E gave me `a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π)` and said it's "likely a coincidence". I don't know what H₀ is, so I can't tell why a coincidence is interesting. Section G was clear and memorable: the superconductor prediction was 607 K versus a real 93 K. I understood that one. The "predicted vs fitted" explanation (a number fixed in advance vs a knob turned until it matches) was genuinely useful and taught me something about how science works.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (Beginner step 3).** The opening, "New here? Look at three things on the chart below", was exactly right. Dots = what telescopes measure, gray = visible matter only (it sags), green = MOND (follows the dots), violet = this site's equation (doesn't). *Now* I finally understood the dark matter puzzle and what MOND is ("gravity changes in weak fields"). That should have come much earlier in my journey. Between that intro and the chart, though, came a long stretch: "For physicists: the calibration, the two wirings, parsimony…", "Which MOND, and why it matters", a formula box with Bessel-function-looking stuff (`I₀(y)K₀(y)`), a "Parsimony note", a "γ note" and "Why the stand-in misled". The beginner bits and the physicist bits are interleaved, and I had to scroll past a lot of "beginners can skip this" to reach the actual chart. The legend in plain words ("Observed (what telescopes measure)", "Newton, visible matter only") is excellent. The "kpc ≈ 3,260 light-years" note on the axis was a nice touch.

**Interactive Tools hub.** It was clear and well labeled. The Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced levels help, and "Best first tool" on the Coherence Explorer told me where to click. Badges like "Audited-Negative" and "Artifact Lesson" meant nothing to me, though, and the hub didn't explain them beyond saying they aren't the validation badges. I didn't know what the validation badges were either.

**Coherence Explorer.** The "One thing to try first" instruction (drag γ up to 2, then back down to 0.5) is the right idea: give me one action. The Key Values readout (C at 1×, 10× and 100× the reference density: 0.33, 0.83, 0.98) helped me see that the curve fills up toward 1. I checked the first two by plugging them into a calculator using the formula on the page (my phone has tanh and ln), and they matched. The badge right under the title confused me: "Reparametrization — At γ = ½ this is a formula physics already had (MOND's, fed acceleration; ΛCDM in cosmology)." I'm on the beginner tool and the first thing it says is a word I can't pronounce plus two acronyms. Lower down there's a pile of formulas (`2u tanh u = 1`, `C(x) = x/(x+2)`) that I skipped.

**What Synchronism Is Not.** The "In plain words — beginner? You can stop here" box is the clearest page on the site: "Not proven", "Not checked by science journals", "Not a replacement for today's physics". I'd show this one to a friend. The cards below it went fully technical (QFT, GR, Hořava–Lifshitz, one-loop) and I bailed.

**Dark Matter Reframed.** This was the topic I actually came for. It defines dark matter for a newcomer ("Unknown particles with mass but no electromagnetic charge"), it has an acronym key at the top, and "the boost is capped at about 3×, while galaxy edges need up to 14×" is a failure I could understand. But "ΔBIC +2843" appears in the opening before the key defines it. And "two different framings, and they are not compatible" left me wondering: if the theory can't decide what dark matter is, what *is* the claim? The page says choosing between the stories is "gated on the project lead", which made it feel unfinished rather than failed.

**Honest Assessment.** It's huge (the page says ~18,000 words). "Not a physicist?" points to a green stop-here box, which is good. "As a theory of galaxies: fatal" is about as clear as it gets. "6 refutations as currently counted… the rule range 3 to 8" confused me, because I don't understand why the number of failures depends on rules nobody has chosen. That sounds like bookkeeping, not science. I stopped after the plain summary.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked (default readouts present; the values C(ρcrit)=0.3272 and C(10ρcrit)=0.8259 match the page's formula when I computed them by hand) — mostly intuitive. The "one thing to try first" instruction is great. The badge and formula wall around it are intimidating. Something I noticed: the curve is S-shaped on the log axis, and γ changes how fast it climbs. It's like the dimmer switch from Why Synchronism, which tied the pages together nicely.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked (galaxy dropdown with DDO 154 default, plus 4 others; two optional checkboxes) — intuitive *once you reach the chart*. The four-colour plain-words legend is the single best teaching device on the site. I finally understood dark matter vs MOND here. Something I noticed: the violet line is labeled "failed" right in the legend, which was surprising but clear.
- (Also looked at the Interactive Tools hub, which is clear structure but has unexplained badge words.)

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is MOND, in one sentence? (I eventually figured it out on the plotter, but not where it was first used.)
2. If the galaxy version failed and the quantum version "has no distinct prediction", what part of Synchronism is still alive? Is it just a way of talking?
3. Why does the number of refutations depend on "counting rules" nobody has picked? Who decides?
4. Why is it interesting that `a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π)` if it's a coincidence?
5. What is the "consciousness equation" on the landing page, and why does a physics framework have one? (The landing card marks it Speculative; I didn't dare click.)
6. Which γ is "the" γ: 2, 0.49, ½ or 0.046? Every page seems to use a different one.

### Verdict
As a Reddit drive-by, I was won over by the honesty: a site that says "we tested it and it didn't hold" in its second line earns my trust, and the crowd/marching-band picture, the "predicted vs fitted" explanation and the galaxy plotter's legend taught me real things about how science works. My main problem was finding the beginner material. It's there (Start Here, the stop-here boxes, the plotter's "New here?") but it sits below or between dense physicist content, and "MOND", the word every failure is measured against, isn't explained until step 3. I'd come back to read the plotter and the "What It Is Not" box again, but I'd never make it through the Honest Assessment.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, Learning Paths, Glossary, Honest Assessment, Test Roadmap, Key Claims, Tier 1: Existing Data, Interactive Tools hub, γ Calculator, Chemistry Correlation Explorer, MRH)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; not evaluating the physics)
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is mature: breadcrumbs, path step indicators, a 123-term glossary with a jump bar, a nine-badge legend and acronym keys. But the *vocabulary* has outgrown the architecture. There are three overlapping label systems (badges, operational states, lifecycle verbs), "Path" and "Track" are used interchangeably, and a single test (TEST-09) is described differently on the three pages that discuss it.

*Method note: pages were read via WebFetch, and every structural defect reported below was then spot-checked against the live HTML. Three candidate defects turned out to be fetch artifacts and are excluded: an "empty middle breadcrumb item", a BCS preset reading "Ncorr = 107" (the HTML uses `10<sup>7</sup>`), and "glossary missing MOND/SPARC/γ entries" (all present).*

### Journey
**Landing.** The top nav has nine items plus the site title, and the footer adds Key Claims, First Encounter, the research archive and "Report an Issue". The acronym handling is better than on most science sites. MOND, A2ACW and MRH are expanded inline at first use. An "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC): short key" line points to an on-page key, and I confirmed that `#terms-on-this-page` resolves. Gaps remain. "LLM" (in the methodology note), "dex", "CFD-like model" and `TEST-09/10/25` appear before the key, and "MNRAS" is never defined. The badge legend is linked, not shown ("the full set of nine is at the link"), which is reasonable. The "Where to Start" block labels its cards across three systems at once: "Beginner Path · Step 1 of 5", "Deep dive · Advanced Path step 8 of 8", "Methodology Track step 1 of 6" and "Shortcut · also Beginner Path step 5 of 5". Step 4 of the Beginner Path has no card in this block, so the visible sequence is 1, 2, 3, 5.

**Learning Paths.** This is clear and well built. Seven paths, each with a step list, and the Beginner per-step times (8+10+6+9+4) do sum to the stated ~37 min. Naming is inconsistent, though. The difficulty paths are headed "Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced" with no suffix. The topic ones are "Physics Track", "Chemistry Track" and so on. The prose says "the 7 paths", and every button says "Start this track →". The landing calls them "Beginner Path" and "Advanced Path", but the Learning Paths page itself never uses the word "Path" in a heading. There's also a typo: "Separately,1 of the 7 paths" (no space; confirmed in the HTML). Only the Beginner path gives per-step times. The others give totals I can't check.

**Breadcrumbs and step navigation.** Breadcrumbs work on every subpage I checked ("Home / Core Theory / MRH: Markov Relevancy Horizon", "Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator", "Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1"). Each has a "Learning path navigation" bar with Prev/Next. Two inconsistencies: the "Predictions & Experiments" crumb is a link on Tier 1 but plain text on the Test Roadmap, and Key Claims has no parent crumb at all ("Home / Key Claims"). Honest Assessment carries **two** step indicators at once ("Beginner Path — Step 5 of 5 … Final step" and "Methodology Track — Step 6 of 6"). Meanwhile its own stop-here box says "Your next step is the Glossary →". It can't be both the final step and point to a next step.

**Glossary.** It has 123 terms and an A–Z + Greek jump bar (J/X/Z are unlinked, which is fine). There are "Learn more →" links to the canonical pages, and all nine badges are defined in two families that match the Honest Assessment. I spot-checked ids for MOND, MRH, SPARC, RAR, Retrodiction, Reparametrization, Tier, γ, ρ_crit and the five MRH-relationship badges, and all exist. Two internal contradictions:
- The **AIC** entry says the compander-family comparison "has not yet been run". The **BIC** entry says it "has now been run twice", and another entry carries a note that "an earlier version said this comparison 'has not yet been run'". So the AIC entry is a stale copy of a sentence that was corrected elsewhere.
- The **Coherence** entry says high C = collective. The **C(ρ)** entry says that through γ = 2/√N_corr "it ranks the most collective systems lowest", which it calls the formula's audited defect. Both are probably true, but a reader who looks up "Coherence" gets only the first and has no pointer to the second.

The label taxonomy also leaks: "Unrunnable as stated", "Sign Correction" and "Null-Class" are listed as operational states, but I couldn't find a definition for each.

**Honest Assessment.** It has a good "On this page" list of ~23 jump links. The anchors the landing footer uses (`#what-was-tested`, `#count-rule`, `#validation-badge-definitions`) all resolve. But the footer link *labelled* "ledger" goes to `#what-was-tested` ("What Was Tested — Analyses run with results"), not to `#refutation-ledger` ("Which six? The refutation ledger"). Those are different sections. The page says "this page is ~18,000 words". Text extracted from `<main>` comes to ~28,000 words (that count may include collapsed `<details>`, but the stated figure is the only size cue a reader gets). The "3 to 8 under rules already on file" range is introduced in the first bullet, inside a note "for readers checking the bookkeeping". The plain reason (some failures are close calls; some share a cause) only arrives later, under "How firm is the 6?". Also, "Not a physicist? Read the box below…" relies on a colour ("green box") and on "below" when the summary block it means is partly above.

**The three test pages (Test Roadmap, Tier 1, Key Claims).** Both Test Roadmap and Tier 1 open with an identical "How the three test pages relate" box. It's a good idea, but the three pages it names are the Test Roadmap, Tier 1, and *Decisive Tests*, which it describes as "the May 2026 ranked shortlist, now empty". Key Claims, the page a reader is most likely to open first and the one carrying test verdicts, is not one of the three. And at the bottom of the same Test Roadmap page, the Related Concepts card still says "Decisive Tests: Status — BAO, wide binary, anesthesia, GW-DM, cosmic interference", which contradicts "now empty" a screen above it.

The test-ID scheme is explained well on the Test Roadmap: one flat namespace, Tier 1 = 01–10, Tier 2 = 11–14, and so on, with suffixes `a`/`s`. Key Claims never explains it and uses "TEST-09", "TEST-10" and "ledger row B1" bare.

**TEST-09 is described three ways.** Key Claims: "the registered |Δn| > 0.3 criterion fired" (no caveat). Tier 1: "TEST-09's kill does not survive its own sweep". Test Roadmap: "so it does not survive its own pre-fixed rule". I can't judge which is correct, but as a documentation reviewer I can say a reader who reads only Key Claims comes away with a different status than one who reads only Tier 1. The same test also carries four significance figures across pages (3.3σ, ~4σ, ~0.9σ beyond threshold, 3.27σ unrounded), with "3.3σ is the separation…, not the kill margin" explained in only one place.

**Counting terms.** The site defines "refutation" as "the narrower subset of Failed results", which is helpful. But the vocabulary in circulation includes refuted, failed, killed, kill fired, kill line, Kill Criterion Triggered, falsification threshold, REFUTED by execution, and Executed and Failed. The headline number is "6 refutations" while the Failed badge says "4 numbered tests with the kill fired" and then lists six IDs: "TEST-01/03s/05 (one environment test, three IDs); TEST-09; TEST-10; TEST-25". Readers will count IDs, get 6 or 7, and not 4.

**Label categories don't agree across pages.** Test Roadmap lists "Underpowered as registered" and "Never run as registered" as *lifecycle verbs (not badges)*. Tier 1 treats "Underpowered as Registered" as an *operational state*, and "Never Run as Registered" as a verb in one paragraph and a card state in its legend. Tier 1 defines Untested as "prediction exists, no data yet", while its TEST-02 card is Untested with "Data: Gaia DR3". Near-synonyms "unfalsifiable", "untestable" and "unrunnable" are all used, and only Key Claims distinguishes them.

**Process text in user-facing labels.** Directly under the Tier 1 Failed badge, inside the badge's explanatory toggle: "(folded 2026-10-23: a technical-writer persona read the whole paragraph as the badge)". The Test Roadmap also has "researcher simulated-visitor persona", "explorer finding", "maintainer estimate", "added 2026-10-27" and "project lead (dp)". These are change-log and internal-process terms. A first-time reader doesn't know what a "persona", "explorer" or "maintainer" is (nothing on the landing explains the three-track setup), and the dated parentheticals make labels longer and harder to scan. (I note the irony that one of them is about my own persona.)

**MRH page.** The breadcrumb and "Physics Track Step 2 of 7" Prev/Next both work. The definition is consistent with the landing ("Markov Relevancy Horizon", "the bubble of neighbors that matter"). It openly flags that "Markov" overpromises and proposes renaming it "Relevance Horizon", which is commendable. The page also exposes a real naming collision: "Not to be confused with the MRH-relationship badges (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, …)". One acronym names both a physics concept and a badge family. The page itself admits the collision; the hub and the badge legends don't warn about it. There's a symbol collision too: ρ is "presence" here, "density" on the γ Calculator, and a correlation coefficient in "ρ(lnγ, lnA) = +1.000000" on this same page.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** — worked (default Ncorr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, C(ρcrit) = 0.60; a quick-reference row 2/√17 = 0.485 checks out arithmetically) — mostly intuitive. The "Try it: click 'Ideal gas' … then 'BCS superconductor' and watch the curve collapse" instruction is the right kind of guidance, though it is inline rather than in a headed "Try this first" box like the Coherence Explorer's. Good: the regime label is self-qualifying ("a label per the inverted formula (Caveat 2), not a physical classification"), and the page openly lists the competing γ values (2, ≈0.49, 0.046, 0.485), so the inconsistency is documented rather than hidden. Weak: ρcrit is undefined, the page doesn't state its Level (the hub says Beginner, but the caveat density says otherwise), and the BCS preset (10⁷) can't be entered in "Linear (1–1000)" mode without switching the scale toggle first, which is not signposted.

### Verdict
Nothing here differs from MOND + ΛCDM and survives, and the site says so before I could. The galaxy sector is MOND's simple μ (which Cassini then excludes, just as it excludes MOND's), the dark-energy family nests Λ in a quadrant DESI disfavours, and the quantum sector is a reading with no rate. What *is* worth a researcher's time are three negative results: the algebraic-locality no-go with its escape taxonomy, the B_max class bounds, and the TEST-02 analysis showing that wide binaries cannot select this family. A2ACW's own statistics say debate didn't find the failures and execution did. To the "is it sound?" question, my answer is: as a discovery engine, no; as a self-audit harness wired to real data, surprisingly good, and the reason it's good is the data, not the debate.
