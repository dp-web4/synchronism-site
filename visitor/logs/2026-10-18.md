## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-plotter, /measurement-without-observers, /honest-assessment (top only), /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've seen. It told me in the first sentence that the idea didn't work, and then I couldn't work out why I should keep reading, or why "dense = collective" when the most collective things score low.

### Journey
I came in from Reddit expecting a "theory of everything" crank site. The headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") fit that expectation. The next lines didn't: "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" and "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." That got my attention, because I've never seen a site open by saying it was wrong. The scoreboard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, ~3,300 AI-agent sessions, no outside physicist review) is blunt, and I trusted it more because of that. The equation sits above the fold. I didn't know tanh or ln, but each symbol has a plain-English gloss, so it didn't scare me off.

I clicked "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)". The "What happened, in three lines" box was the best thing on the site: the proposal, "it lost: 0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations", and "what remains open is the bigger question." Then I hit "you can stop here", which felt odd on step 1 of 5. The crowd vs. marching band analogy clicked for me. Below that the page turned into Tully–Fisher, ALFALFA–SDSS, p = 5×10⁻⁶, Abrikosov–Gor'kov, YBCO and "compander", and I skimmed. The "What Failed" section was easy to follow: melting points off by 53%, and a superconductor predicted at 607 K when the real value is 93 K.

/first-encounter was a gentler retelling: marching band = high coherence, a crystal lattice "every atom locked in step" = high coherence. **Here I got stuck.** The whole idea is that *packing* (density) makes things act together. But a marching band isn't a denser crowd. It's a crowd with a drummer. Nothing told me what plays the drummer for atoms. Then the landing page and the coherence tool told me a **superconductor scores low**. I know from YouTube that superconductors and Bose–Einstein condensates are the famous "everything acts as one" quantum states. The glossary defines coherence as "sparse/independent (0) to dense/collective (1)", so I expected them at the top, not the bottom. /honest-assessment admits this, saying a physicist "will read several pages backwards". The reason it gives is "enormous Ncorr makes γ tiny". To me that's just symbols, not a reason.

The tools: the **Coherence Explorer** was fun to drag around, and the "Move γ from 2.0 downward and watch the curve flatten" prompt was helpful. The presets confused me, though: γ = 0.49 ("galaxy fit") and γ = ½ ("same curve as MOND"). Those are nearly the same number. Is that the point (the fit just *is* MOND), or two different things? The **γ Calculator** opens with a big "Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted" warning, and every output carries a "not a physical classification" note. I respect the honesty, but I didn't know why I was using a calculator for a formula the site says is backwards. The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the clearest page on the site. Gray line = visible matter, dots = reality, gap = the dark matter puzzle. Then the violet "Synchronism" line visibly fails and the green MOND line works. In about 30 seconds I understood what dark matter is evidence *for*, and that this theory lost. Honestly, that taught me more than any other page.

Next I went to /measurement-without-observers, because Schrödinger's-cat videos are my thing. The opening was exciting: "No observer needed. No consciousness required." Then the badge said "Speculative — No Differing Prediction". After that it was einselection, Lindblad, CSL and Diósi–Penrose, and I left. I skimmed the top of /honest-assessment. The page says it's ~18,000 words. The "What a non-physicist should take away" box was clear and quotable, especially "None of its numbers come from first principles" and "No currently proposed experiment can tell the framework… apart from existing physics." Finally, the glossary (112 terms) gave a decent MRH analogy ("event horizon for influence — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"). It then reused "MRH-relationship" as the name of a *badge family*, which threw me.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (static defaults rendered: C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785). Intuitive. The "drag γ down from 2" prompt is good, and "a shape toy, not a map of real materials" is a fair warning. The 0.49 vs ½ presets confused me.
- **γ Calculator**: worked (preset table: ideal gas 2.000, water 1.000, galaxy 0.485, BEC 2.00e-3, BCS 6.32e-4). Confusing as to *purpose*. It's a calculator for a formula declared backwards, and it never says what I'm meant to notice.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked, with five galaxy presets and clearly colour-coded lines. Very intuitive and the best page for me. I saw the dark matter gap, the Synchronism line failing and MOND succeeding, all at a glance.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If coherence means "dense and collective", why do superconductors and BECs (the famous examples of matter acting as one) score *low*? What exactly is this dial measuring that they don't have?
2. What makes packed-together atoms "march in step"? What's the drummer?
3. Is the galaxy-fit γ ≈ 0.49 being basically ½ the whole story (it just turns into MOND), or a coincidence?
4. The site says some core ideas "have never been testable with existing instruments." What are they, in one sentence, and is anyone trying to build a test?
5. ~3,300 AI-agent sessions and no outside physicist: did the AI agents find the failures themselves? That seems like the most interesting story here, and the landing page only mentions it as a number.

### Verdict
As a pop-science reader I came away with a real lesson. The Galaxy Plotter showed me in seconds what the dark matter problem is, and I watched a theory lose to its rival. The upfront "we were wrong" framing made me trust everything else. But the central word, "coherence", seems to contradict itself for anyone who's watched a superconductor video, and outside the opening boxes most pages turn into a physicist's audit within a screen. So I'd come back for the honesty and the plotter, not because I understood the idea.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /learning-paths, /glossary, /honest-assessment (badge section + TOC), /interactive-tools, /mrh, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /equation-walkthrough, /test-catalog, /key-claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; the physics I take on trust)
- **Would return?**: yes

### Journey
I started on the landing page and mapped the IA. The header has ten flat items with no dropdowns, and the footer adds Key Claims, First Encounter and two external links. The landing page has no breadcrumb, which is fine for a root. The landing page also does the abbreviation hygiene well: it carries an inline abbreviation list that defines about 25 acronyms (A2ACW, SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC, ΛCDM, DESI…). The badge strip above the claim cards reads "Badges used in the table below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed · Audited-Negative", with a link to the canonical definitions. I checked the raw page text: "Untested" appears only in that legend line and on no card, so the legend over-lists.

/learning-paths is the best page for a newcomer. It has three difficulty paths and four topic tracks, each with step counts and time estimates that add up (Beginner 8+10+6+9+4 = 37 min, with a note that full reading is ~2 h). It's also honest that "the two groupings are not equivalent". The Beginner path's last step deep-links to `#if-you-read-nothing-else` on Honest Assessment, which is the right move for an ~18,000-word page. The acronyms on the page itself are not expanded: MOND, CDM and A2ACW appear in step titles.

The breadcrumbs work but are uneven. Simple pages show `Home / Glossary`. (A fetch summary rendered this as "Home / / Glossary"; the raw HTML shows the `/` is a separator, not a doubled crumb.) /mrh shows `Home / Core Theory / MRH…` and /test-catalog shows `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap`. The middle crumbs ("Core Theory", "Predictions & Experiments") are plain text, not links, so there's no section index to go up to. The tool pages, by contrast, do link their middle crumb (`Home / Interactive Tools / …`). The path-step bar under the breadcrumb is excellent. On /honest-assessment it shows both "Beginner Path Step 5 of 5" and "Methodology Track Step 6", which is exactly how to handle a page that belongs to two sequences.

Then the badges. The glossary (112 terms, alphabetical, A–Z bar) and the Honest Assessment's "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" agree. There are nine badges in two families: **MRH-relationship** (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative), which the canonical section calls *preferred for in-flight work*, and **Descriptive** (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed). Validated and Strongly Supported are listed as deprecated. The glossary and canonical wordings match nearly word for word. The one drift: Audited-Negative is a "durable negative result" in the glossary but a "durable record" on Honest Assessment. The problem is usage. I counted badge names in the visible text of the pages a newcomer actually sees. The landing page, Key Claims, Interactive Tools and /mrh never use Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined or Superseded. The "preferred" family shows up mostly in legends (/test-catalog, /research-philosophy), not on claims. So a reader is taught a two-family taxonomy and then meets only one family, plus one member of the other.

On top of the nine badges sit more layers. There are "finding descriptors" (text after a dash, "not a separate badge") and "operational states, not badges" (Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Unrunnable as stated, Withdrawn, Underpowered as registered, Artifact Lesson). On /interactive-tools each card also gets a Kind, a Level and a Status, plus a "Badge on its page". That's four labelled slots per card, and the page needs a paragraph explaining that the Kind "Model Explainer" "is a content grouping, not the site's validation badge taxonomy". Every one of these distinctions is defended, and I could follow each. But when a page needs a legend to tell you which of its labels *aren't* badges, the labels have become the content.

/mrh opens with a clean definition ("The Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH) is a term proposed by…"). It explains Markov blankets and its own formal gap honestly. It then uses ρcrit, A ("ρcrit = A·Vflat²"), γ, x ("C ≈ γ x"), RAR and TEST-10 without defining them on the page, so it leans on its prerequisite link. It never mentions that "MRH" is also the name of a badge family, so the overload Pass 1 hit is invisible from the concept's own page.

/test-catalog is titled "Test Roadmap" everywhere visible, but the URL is `/test-catalog`. The tier counts reconcile (10+4+7+3 = 24 registry, +2 later = 26). **The kill-criterion tally does not reconcile.** It reads: "Counted 2026-09-18 across all 26 rows: 2 are well-formed …, 3 state a significance at all, 11 contain no number, and TEST-07's reads 'not falsifiable as stated'." That's 2+3+11+1 = 17. The text doesn't say what the other 9 rows contain, or whether the categories overlap (does "3 state a significance" include the 2 well-formed?). The sentence just before it complicates things further: a parenthetical says TEST-25/26 are "listed but not in this count", which suggests 24, not 26. A reader can't tell whether the base is 24 or 26, or whether the buckets are exhaustive.

/key-claims has three questions with Speculative / Speculative / Failed headline badges and Reparametrization sub-cards. Descriptor formatting varies: some are italic lines, some follow a dash, and Claim 3 has none. "QM" appears 16 times and is never expanded. A revision note says ALFALFA–SDSS is "expanded at first use", but the first occurrence of "ALFALFA" in the text is that note itself, unexpanded. The expansion (Arecibo) comes later.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /mond-unification, /gamma-boundary, /gamma-parameter, /galaxy-rotation, /honest-assessment, /dark-energy, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes, as a case study in auditing, not as physics
- **Overall impression**: Every number I recomputed checks out, and the self-audit is harsher than the review I'd have written. Two tool pages still state as fact a "γ ≈ 1 is where chemistry and biology happen" boundary that the dedicated page has retracted, and the headline "6 refutations" counts more than the record shows.

### Journey
I skipped the onboarding and went to /parameter-derivations, because a "one equation" site lives or dies on whether its parameters are derived. The page answers that itself: "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." It also does the thing I came to check, the mean-field argument: "The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen… C(ρ) has no such self-consistency loop." That is exactly the objection I'd have raised. In mean-field Ising, tanh comes from m = tanh(β(h + Jzm)) solved self-consistently. Here tanh is evaluated once on an input. I'd go further than the page. With u = 1+x, tanh(γ ln u) = (u^{2γ} − 1)/(u^{2γ} + 1) is just an algebraic, Hill-type function in (1+x), so there is no thermodynamics in it at all. The page states the γ = ½ case: x/(x+2), MOND's simple μ with the scale doubled. I verified it, as μ(x/2) = (x/2)/(1+x/2). Stating the general closed form would make "chosen, not derived" self-evident, and it would make the return exponent q = 2γ quoted on /gamma-calculator a one-line check rather than a claim.

Then I recomputed the parameter numbers myself:
- **a₀ = cH₀/2π** at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ✓. That's 13% below 1.20; at H₀ = 73 it's about 6% below, so "6–13% below, H₀-dependent" ✓. The page calls this dimensional analysis, which is the correct label.
- **Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G)** gives 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc² ✓. It's correctly described as a₀/(2πG), not a second derivation.
- **A from Jeans**: 4π/(G R₀²) at R₀ = 8 kpc is 3.09×10⁻³⁰ kg s² m⁻⁵, which is 4.6×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻² ✓. The audit is right that 0.029 is about 630× off the stated formula. Dimensionally the formula is at least consistent.
- **B_max = 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17**, and the dwarf-fraction cap 1−Ω_m = 68.5% follows from f_DM = 1−C with C ≥ Ω_m ✓.

/mond-unification calls itself "not a unification" and says "The 2π is a choice… Step 2 does not produce Step 3." /gamma-parameter is candid that γ = 2/√N_corr is an ansatz with no protocol for measuring N_corr. A text extraction showed me "N_corr = 1024 gives γ = 2×10⁻¹²", which looked like an arithmetic error. The raw HTML has 10<sup>24</sup>, and 2/√10²⁴ = 2×10⁻¹² is correct. That's a rendering artifact, not a defect, though a copy-paste will reproduce my false alarm.

The sign-inversion argument on /gamma-calculator is correct condensed-matter physics. Finite-size rounding of a real transition narrows as N grows, so more correlated systems should be sharper, and γ = 2/√N_corr makes them flatter. The calculator also inverts the map on the framework's own galaxy values: γ = 2 gives N_corr = 1, and γ = 0.49 gives N_corr ≈ 17, "about as collectively organized as a small protein pocket." That is a self-refutation done well.

/galaxy-rotation and /honest-assessment carry the statistics. The point-level ΔBIC +184 for γ = 2 is rescored as ≈ +11 at galaxy level ("disfavoured, not refuted"). That correction for correlated points within a galaxy is exactly the one I'd want, and most published RAR papers don't make it. The vacuum limit I went looking for is handled on /honest-assessment: as ρ→0, C ≈ γρ/ρ_crit, so the source ρ/C → ρ_crit/γ is a uniform density floor filling space ✓. /galaxy-rotation itself never states this limit, though it's where a reader would look.

/dark-energy: H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C) with ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C. I checked the asymptotics. At early times 1−C ≈ 2(ρ_m/ρ_crit)^{−2γ}, so ρ_DE ∝ a^{−3(1−2γ)} and w → −2γ. At late times C ≈ γρ_m/ρ_crit, so the total density tends to the constant ρ_crit/γ and w → −1 ✓. The sign lock follows ✓. At γ = ½, ρ_m/C = ρ_m + 2ρ_crit, which is exactly Λ ✓. The page also says the substitution violates the Bianchi identity and calls it "notation, not derivation," and says TEST-26 is "a test of ΛCDM with the framework attached." It even notes that ρ_DE = f(ρ_m) is background-vacuous because C ≡ Ω_m(a). Those are the right admissions. I'd only add that C = ρ/(ρ+K) gives exact Λ for *any* K, so landing on Λ at γ = ½ is a property of the simplest saturating rational function, not of tanh-log. The same is true of galaxies, where γ ≈ ½ is MOND simple-μ. Both sectors' free fits land on the point where the framework collapses into the incumbent. /honest-assessment says as much ("γ = ½ is exactly where…"), which I credit.
