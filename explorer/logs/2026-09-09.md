**1. Assumptions I am inheriting.**
(a) From the maintainer's fresh topic `argument-of-c-which-variable-puts-the-switch-outside-the-disc.md`:

**2. What if the frame is wrong?**
Assumptions (a) and (b) **contradict each other and the maintainer's topic did not notice**. My 09-08 run
reports `medReq = 5.10` — the median over 153 discs of the maximum required boost V_obs²/V_bar². That
number is **data**, not model: it does not depend on C at all, on its argument, or on its knee. It is
identical in all 32 rows of the 09-08 table. And the delivered maximum boost is `1/floor`: 3.17 at the Ω_m
floor, ~11 at 0.089 — a property of the **floor alone**, because in the L2 field equation the boost is 1/C
and C ≥ floor by construction, whatever C is a function of.

So the topic's question is malformed at the Ω_m floor: *no* argument can work there, for a reason that has
nothing to do with keying. And at a freed floor the amplitude is available and the question becomes live.
The topic is right that placement is the residual problem; it is wrong that placement is where the sector
closes, and it did not state the precondition that makes its own question answerable.

Second frame check, on the candidate list. In this code the scale height is a single number per galaxy
(`h = 0.196 Rd^0.633`), so ρ_mid(R) = Σ(R)/(2h) **exactly**. Σ-keying and ρ-keying are then the same
function of R within a galaxy, differing only by a per-galaxy constant 2h — i.e. Σ is not an independent
candidate, it is ρ with the knee rescaled by a factor that varies ~5× across the sample. Likewise an
MRH-smoothed ρ with horizon λ, smoothed along z (the direction ρ actually varies), has the closed form
Σ(R)·tanh(λ/h)/(2λ) at the midplane: it is a **one-parameter family whose λ→0 limit is ρ and whose λ≫h
limit is Σ**. Three of the topic's four candidates are one candidate.

That leaves the real dichotomy, which the topic never states: **arguments that fall exponentially with R
(ρ, Σ, ρ_MRH — anything local to the baryons) versus arguments that fall as a power law (g_N ∝ R⁻², or the
mean interior density)**. V_obs is flat, so B_req(R) grows ≈ linearly in R — a power law. An exponentially
falling argument crosses the tanh's transition in a couple of kpc and then pins at the floor; a power-law
argument can transition across the whole outer disc. That is a *slope* statement, and it is measurable
without fitting anything.

**3. Highest-information investigation.** Two things, in order.
(i) The **slope diagnostic**: measure d ln B_req/d ln R on SPARC's outer curves, and for each candidate
argument measure d ln X/d ln R, then bound the deliverable d ln B/d ln R by max_x |d ln C/d ln x| × |d ln X/d
ln R| — knee-free, floor-free, fit-free. This says which arguments are *kinematically capable* before any
solver runs.
(ii) The **head-to-head L2 run** the topic asks for, but with the precondition stated: same solver, same
likelihood, same Υ profiling as 09-08, C keyed on ρ / Σ / ρ_MRH(λ) / g_N / ρ̄(<r), at both floors. Include
**g_N at the Ω_m floor** as the decisive control: if MOND's own keying also fails at the Ω_m floor, the
sector's close is the floor, stated structurally, and the argument question is settled for every argument
at once.

**4. What would falsify today's posture?** If C keyed on g_N at floor = Ω_m fits SPARC near MOND's 21.2,
then the boost ceiling is not 1/floor and my 09-08 conclusion is wrong. If Σ-keying differs measurably from
ρ-keying at matched knee, the constant-h identity above is broken by the gas/bulge components and the three
candidates are not one candidate. If a power-law argument does *not* beat the exponential ones at the freed
floor, the slope diagnostic is the wrong diagnostic and placement is not about slope.

## Rabbit Holes (Worth Revisiting)

- **The unfloored limit is not computable with this solver as configured.** MOND's μ at floor 1e-6,
  solved self-consistently, gives 7.19× algebraic MOND and does not converge (max remaining ΔC/C = 8.8e-3
  after 12 damped iterations); at floor 0.02 it reproduces algebraic MOND to 1.4%. The suspect is the
  outer Dirichlet condition Φ → −GM/(C_min r), which presumes a constant far-field C that an unbounded μ
  does not have. Until that is fixed, **no row at floor ≲ 0.01 in any run in this archive is interpretable**,
  and "isolate the compander form by removing the floor" cannot be done.
- **One-shot vs self-consistent is model-dependent in sign**, not a correctable factor: at floor Ω_m,
  MOND's μ improves 111.69 → 71.68 while the compander at the same floor *worsens* 86.89 → 107.72. Every
  acceleration-keyed row in today's main tables is one-shot; every density-keyed row is unaffected (ρ is
  not a function of the solution). The head-to-head is therefore biased, by an amount that is not a
  constant, in an unknown direction per model.
- ρ̄(<r), the spherical mean interior density, is second-best at the Ω_m floor (58.97) and was never on the
  topic's candidate list. Its freed-floor rows are worth a proper knee scan.

## Self-Seeded Questions

- **"Is the boost-ceiling refutation χ²-invisible?" — asked, tested, and answered NO within the session.**
  The 51-disc validity control said a ceiling of 3.17 costs 5% in χ² while 77% of discs demand more than
  3.17 somewhere, which would have made the site's B_max badge, S684's fork and my own 09-08 conclusion
  all invisible to the statistic the program fits with. I wrote that up as the session's headline, then
  ran the full-153 radial decomposition it implied. **The full sample reverses it**: the ceiling costs
  1.62× overall, and the pre-registered "it hides at large radius" hypothesis is confirmed in *direction*
  (3.08× on the last two points vs 1.53× on the inner half, 14.1% median velocity deficit at R_last
  against 5.2% errors) but refuted in *degree* — it does not hide well enough to be invisible.
  **Tenth near-miss in the record, and the first caught before publication by a test the finding itself
  designed rather than by the next day's session.** The lesson generalises past this sector: a control
  run on a subsample is a control on the subsample.
- The surviving new content is the *shape* of the refutation rather than its existence: the boost ceiling
  is refuted **radius-gradedly**, and the sharpest single statement available is the 14.1% median velocity
  deficit at the outermost fitted point against a 5.2% median error — a statement S684's algebraic version
  could not make.
- Every framework-vs-MOND comparison in this archive scores a field-equation model against an *algebraic*
  MOND. Stage 3's first row shows the two agree to 1.4% for MOND itself at a working floor, which is
  reassuring — but that check had never been run before today.

## So What?

It advances discovery, and the sharpest evidence is that it contains a retraction of its own headline
made three hours after the headline was drafted. A 51-disc control said the boost ceiling was
χ²-invisible; the full-153 radial decomposition the claim implied said it costs 1.62×. I would have

Three things are new rather than restated. **The identity** — Σ, MRH-smoothed ρ and local ρ are one
argument, exactly — collapses a fix space the site and the archive have both treated as open, and it is
algebra, so it does not depend on any fit. **The floor arithmetic** answers a question that has been
outstanding since 09-08 in the only currency that cannot be argued with: SPARC needs f ≤ 0.100, the
archive's enumerated candidates stop at 0.157, and 1/Ω_m was never derived at all. **The boundary-condition
failure** is a negative result about the tooling that invalidates a class of rows across the archive and
closes a quarter of today's topic as unanswerable rather than unanswered — which is a more useful thing to
know than another number would have been.

And the lint is the first mitigation in this program that is a check rather than an exhortation. Its value
will be measurable: the next transcription error either fires it or it does not.
