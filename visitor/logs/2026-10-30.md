## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /first-encounter, /why-synchronism, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /key-claims, /two-reframes)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A site that tells me in its first screen that its big idea failed. That honesty made me trust it and keep reading, but by the third click I was skimming a wall of acronyms and dated revision notes.

### Journey
I clicked through from Reddit expecting a "theory of everything" crank page. The headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") looked like that. Then the first paragraph said the idea was *proposed, tested, and did not hold as stated*, and the scoreboard read 0 confirmed predictions and 6 refutations. That was a genuine surprise and the reason I didn't bounce. The plain-language gloss on "coherence" (sparse things like stars in a galaxy ≈ 0, tightly packed things ≈ 1) was the first thing I understood. So was the note that it is *not* quantum coherence.

On the landing page, though, MRH, MOND, ΛCDM, SPARC, RAR, BTFR, ΔBIC, CHSH and Ωm all appear before I've clicked anything. I don't know what any of them mean, apart from having heard "MOND" on PBS Space Time.

**First Encounter** says "about 10 minutes, sections A–G." Sections A and B (presence = how crowded things are; an S-curve that turns crowding into a 0-to-1 score) were really good. I got it. From Section C on, every section is mostly about how the previous idea failed, e.g. "That formula did not survive testing" and "A fitted match is not evidence." I understand *that* things failed, but often not *what* was being tested. Section E mentions "a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π), probably a coincidence." I don't know what a₀ or H₀ are, so I couldn't tell why a coincidence would even be interesting. Section G's "53% error on melting points" and "607 K predicted vs 93 K actual" for a superconductor were the most concrete, satisfying moments. I could actually see the failure.

**Why Synchronism?** opens strongly ("Physics has a fragmentation problem"). The pitch, one curve for how "crowding" makes things act as one, is the clearest one-sentence version on the site. Further down I tripped over three galaxy counts: 14,610, 14,585 and 14,760. Reading the raw page, 14,610 is 175 + 14,435, the 14,585 is a different "cross-match" sample, and 14,760 sits inside an edit-history note about an old error. Each one has a reason, but as a skimmer I just saw three numbers for "how many galaxies," and it made me wonder whether anyone was keeping track. I also noticed a section saying it was "titled 'What Worked' until 2026-07-17". It was oddly endearing, but it reads like a changelog, not an explanation.

**Coherence Explorer** was the best moment on the site. The "one thing to try first" box (push γ to 2, then down to 0.5, watch the curve flatten) gave me something to *do*. The plain explanations of `ln` ("compresses huge ranges into small ones; 1,000× denser adds only about 7") and `tanh` ("the S-curve shape," like a dimmer's sharpness) were exactly what I needed. I no longer feel locked out by "tanh." The Key Values (C at ρcrit = 0.327, at 10× = 0.826) checked out when I punched them into a phone calculator. One snag: it warns that ρcrit is "not where C = 0.5." I'd assumed a "critical" thing would be the midpoint, and I still don't know *why* it's called critical if it isn't.

**γ Calculator**: the presets table is fun (ideal gas γ = 2, water 1, galaxy 0.485, BEC 0.002). Then the page tells me the formula is "inverted": it gives the *most* collective things, like superconductors, the *flattest* curves, which is backwards from reality. I get the lesson ("models can be wrong in direction"), but it's strange to play with a calculator whose own page says its outputs mean nothing. It took me a minute to realise that's the point.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the page that finally made "dark matter" click for me. Blue dots are real star speeds. The gray line is what ordinary gravity predicts from visible stuff, and it falls off. The dots stay flat. That gap *is* the puzzle. The "start here: compare gray with dots, then green" instruction is perfect. Then the solid violet line (the site's own equation) visibly misses the dots at the edge. Seeing the failure on a chart is far more convincing than reading "Failed" on a badge. The takeaway ("the only version that fits is MOND's formula written differently") I understood only half of. I don't know why "written differently" counts as failing rather than as matching.

**Honest Assessment** says up front that it's about 18,000 words, and it has a "Not a physicist? read the box, then the plain summary, then stop" instruction. That was very kind. The plain summary worked: the equation's distinctive galaxy predictions failed, the parts that fit are a rival theory restated, and about two-thirds of real galaxies show more "missing gravity" than the equation's built-in cap allows. That last one is a great, concrete sentence. I stopped where it told me to.

**Key Claims** has three cards. Quantum (Speculative), Consciousness (Speculative), Dark matter (Failed). Inside the "Failed" dark-matter card, a sub-relation is labelled "Reparametrization," and the text says the framework "reduces exactly to MOND's simple formula at γ = ½." Same confusion as the plotter: if it equals a working theory, how is it Failed? The page may answer this, but not in words I could find. I also noticed the body says the consciousness threshold came from "7 approaches that exist." The Related Concepts list at the bottom says "8 approaches that agree because they share one calibration." I checked the raw page, and both are really there.

**Two Reframes** has lovely analogies: an old CRT TV whose single dot only looks like a picture, and a pendulum clock in a centrifuge. It says honestly that the literal CRT version failed a 1990 experiment and that the clock picture "reframes existing predictions, it does not generate new ones." After the analogies it dives into Bell/CHSH/Tsirelson/Kochen–Specker and I bailed.

### Verdict
I came expecting a crank and found the opposite: a site that leads with its own failure, and that's genuinely refreshing. The Galaxy Plotter and Coherence Explorer taught me something real (what a rotation curve is, what an S-curve does). But past the first screen of almost every page, I'm skimming caveats about things I was never told the meaning of. The site's best trick, the "not a physicist? stop here" box on Honest Assessment, should be on every page.

### Journey
A colleague called this an "interesting AI project", so I expected tanh-flavoured numerology. The landing page dropped that frame within one screen: "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." I went straight to the math. (Method note: I pulled the live pages as raw HTML with curl, stripped the tags and redid the arithmetic in Python. I also read the two tools' shipped JS chunks, because I can't drag sliders.)

**Parameter Derivations.** The page says plainly that it isn't a derivations page, which is right. I checked:
- The closed form tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = ((1+x)^{2γ}−1)/((1+x)^{2γ}+1) ✓. At γ = ½ it gives x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), so a₀ = 2a₀′ ✓. Limits C ≈ γx and 1−C ≈ 2x^{−2γ} ✓.
- The Ising remark (the tanh there comes from a self-consistency loop; this tanh has none) is exactly the objection I'd have raised. The Curie–Weiss restatement is correct: the rounding width goes as N^{−1/2}, so a mean-field sharpness would grow as √N. The γ = 2/√N_corr sign inversion is real.
- The A = 4π/(β_J²GR₀²) arithmetic: with G = 4.301×10⁻³ pc (km/s)²/M☉ and R₀ = 8 kpc I get 4.565×10⁻⁵ ✓, so 644× ✓. The 0.029 is recovered only at β_J·R₀ ≈ 317 pc ✓. The MOND-matching knee ρ ∝ a₀²/(GV²) ∝ V⁻² is a two-line BTFR argument ✓, and its magnitude is 0.08–0.3 M☉/pc³ at V = 100 km/s ✓.
