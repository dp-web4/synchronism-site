# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-11-18

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The site is honest about failing to an unusual degree, and that is what kept me reading. But by step 3 of the "beginner" path I was reading a page built for physicists, with a beginner paragraph on top.

### Journey

**Landing.** I came from a Reddit link. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", reads like a PBS Space Time thumbnail, which is why I clicked. Three lines later the page says "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." That surprised me in a good way, because I've never seen a theory site open by saying it failed. The "So is it alive or dead?" paragraph gave me the honest answer I was already asking: "dead so far" as physics, "alive" as a public record. It also gives a date, Gaia DR4 on 2 December 2026, which made it feel like a story still in progress.

The crowd-of-strangers vs. marching-band picture (0 vs. 1) worked for me immediately. The equation is shown, but each symbol is read out in plain English, so it didn't scare me off. One snag: the "Read it as" line says the equation tells you "how fast that switch flips." Two sentences later, the symbol key says γ is "how early the switch starts to turn as density rises (no setting makes it sudden)." So is it about *how fast* or *when*? I couldn't tell, and the tools later sided with "when."

"New here? This is a good place to stop scrolling" was a kind and useful signpost. I peeked below it anyway. It's a scoreboard with an abbreviation key of about 30 items and coloured status words like "Is MOND" and "Is Λ". I didn't know what "Λ" was, and I took the hint and went back up.

**Start Here: Why Synchronism? (Step 1 of 5).** "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a pitch I could follow. The "What happened, in three lines" box was great. The two failures in "What Failed" were the clearest thing on the whole site for me: melting points off by 53% on average, and a superconductor temperature predicted at 607 K when the real value is 93 K. I don't need calculus to see that 607 isn't 93. The page tells me "Beginner path: that is all you need from this page," and I believed it. The rest was folded under "For physicists."

**First Encounter (Step 2 of 5, "about 10 minutes").** I liked the electron-alone vs. neutron-star contrast, and "a single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy." That one clicked: things only respond to what's near enough to matter. The MRH idea makes sense as a "bubble of relevance," and it's nice that they explain what "Markov" means.

Section C gave me whiplash. It teaches me a formula for γ, then immediately tells me it failed with "the sign reversed." I appreciate the honesty, but I don't know why a beginner needs to learn a dead formula in step 2. Section E has "ρ_crit = 0.028 × V_flat^0.5" and then a note that an older form "0.029 × V_flat²" was a units mix-up. That is history of a bug, and as a newcomer I didn't need it. Section F (wave-function collapse as "crossing the MRH") was the part I actually wanted more of, because I've watched videos on the measurement problem. It's labelled "an interpretation, not a failed or a winning prediction," and then it stops. The end of Section G showed "Continue ↓ Section B," which looked odd after the last section. This may just be how the progress widget reads in fetched text, not a real bug.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (Step 3 of 5).** The intro "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" is exactly what I needed: the dots are measured speeds, the gray dashed line is what visible matter alone predicts, the green line is MOND (the rival), and the violet line is this framework's equation. The message landed: violet "is the gray line scaled up, still falling where the dots stay flat," while green matches. *Their own equation loses to the rival on their own plotter.* I respect that.

Then the page kept going for about 50,000 characters. There's a calibration selector with options "Archive's own form: ρcrit = 0.028·Vflat0.5 (default)" and "Units hybrid: 0.029·Vflat² (this page's only curve until 2026-11-14)." I have no idea why a beginner sees a units-bug toggle on step 3. Further down it says the green MOND line, the one that "matches," is itself excluded by a Solar System test at 15–20σ. So the winner also loses? That left me more confused than before. The jargon wall below the fold (ΔBIC, QUMOND, EFE, Cassini, simple-ν, NFW, Jeans formula, TEST-09/10/25) is for someone else. On the good side, the stellar-mass slider is labelled "How heavy are the stars?", which is the friendliest control label on the site.

**Coherence Explorer.** First Encounter promised I could "drag two sliders and watch C(ρ) change in real time." The page opens with a "Reparametrization" banner ("At γ = ½ this is a formula physics already had…") before telling me what the tool does, and I don't know that word yet. The next line is great: "What this toy is for: seeing the shape of the S-curve and what γ does to it. It does not model any real material, and the density axis has no units." That's honest, but it also means there's nothing real to anchor to. The presets are γ values (0.046, 0.49, ½, 1, 2), not things like "water" or "a galaxy," and the page says "no preset puts you 'at' a galaxy, a metal, or a brain." The defaults rendered with values (C(ρcrit) = 0.6000, C(10ρcrit) = 0.9836). The suggestion "drag γ up to 2 … then down to 0.5 and watch the S flatten" is a good first thing to try. The line "no setting makes it abrupt" is where I worked out that the landing's "how fast that switch flips" was misleading.

**γ Calculator.** The title is "a γ formula kept to show how it fails," and the first bold line reads "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails." That's clear and up front. But why would I play with a broken calculator? Presets: Ideal gas (γ = 2), Liquid water, Galaxy, Enzyme site, Ferromagnet, BEC, BCS superconductor. The default readout is "γ = 1.0000," followed by the regime label "Crossover band (γ ≈ 1; the "boundary" reading is retracted) — a label per the inverted formula (Caveat 2), not a physical classification." I couldn't parse a single clause of that after the first three words. "BEC" and "BCS" aren't defined where I meet them. The one-sentence explainer ("γ sets how early the coherence S-curve starts to rise… Big γ = the rise comes earlier, but it does not become a switch") is good and should be the first thing under the readout.

**Honest Assessment.** The "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" box is the best-written beginner text on the site. "As a theory of galaxies: fatal, by its own scoreboard," and "untested is not the same as failed," which I hadn't thought about. "On the Beginner Path? You're done." is a mercy, because the page is around 30,000 words. I stopped there.

**Glossary.** It has 130 terms. "New here? Start with these ten" is a great idea, and all ten links jump to real entries (I checked: Coherence, Presence, γ, tanh, MOND, Dark matter, Reparametrization, Refutation, σ, MRH). The tanh entry ("Think of it as a dimmer switch… large inputs get squashed toward the ceiling") and the σ entry ("2σ = 'interesting', 3σ = 'evidence', 5σ = 'discovery'") are excellent. The Rotation Curve entry finally explained the galaxy problem in a way I could picture. Right under the ten, though, is a table titled "How to read labels on this site" listing *eight kinds of label*. That's a lot of meta before my first definition. It also says MRH in the badges "borrows the word by analogy and says nothing about physics." But First Encounter had just used MRH *as* physics (the atom and Andromeda). Same word, two jobs.

**What I think I understood:**
- [x] The idea: one S-shaped curve says how "together" things act, based on how crowded they are.
- [x] It was tested and mostly failed; the site counts 0 confirmed predictions.
- [x] Where it fits galaxies, it's just MOND (an older idea) in new notation.
- [x] Concrete failures: melting points 53% off; superconductor 607 K vs 93 K.
- [~] What γ does (I eventually got "when the rise starts," but only from the tools).
- [ ] Why I should care about the parts that are "untested" rather than failed.
- [ ] What the quantum-measurement idea actually claims.
- [ ] Who runs this. It says "AI-run research notebook" and "about 3,300 AI-to-AI sessions." Is a person steering it?

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked (controls and labels render; the chart is drawn client-side and I couldn't see it in fetched text). Intuitive top, confusing below. The 3-item "look at these" intro is excellent and the violet-vs-green takeaway is clear. The calibration toggle and the Cassini aside overwhelm a beginner.
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (default readouts render: C(ρcrit) = 0.6000, width 1.50 dex). Intuitive. The "drag γ to 2, then 0.5" suggestion is a good first experiment. It's abstract by design ("density axis has no units"), so I learned the curve's shape but not what it means for anything real.
- **γ Calculator**: worked (default γ = 1.0000 at N_corr = 4; I checked that 2/√4 = 1). Confusing. It's framed up front as a demo of a failed formula, which is honest, but the regime label is unreadable for me and I didn't come away knowing what clicking the presets was meant to teach me.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If MOND matches the galaxy dots but also fails a Solar System test, what *does* explain galaxy rotation? Is dark matter still the default answer?
2. What does "wave-function collapse = crossing the MRH" actually predict, if anything? Could it ever be tested?
3. Does γ change *when* the switch happens or *how fast* it happens? The landing says one thing and the tools say another.
4. Why do the beginner tools include a calculator for a formula the site says is wrong? What am I supposed to take away from it?
5. Who is behind this? "AI-run" with ~3,300 AI sessions: is a human scientist checking any of it? (Honest Assessment says no outside expert has.)
6. What happens on 2 December 2026 with Gaia DR4? Could this idea come back to life, and what result would do that?

### Verdict
I stayed longer than I expected, because a site that says "we tested it and it didn't hold" up front is rare, and the plain-language pieces (crowd vs. marching band, 607 K vs 93 K, the no-jargon box on Honest Assessment, the glossary's "start with ten") are genuinely good. The beginner path is solid for steps 1–2. At step 3 the galaxy plotter's good three-line intro gives way to a physicist's workbench with bug-history toggles. I'd come back on 2 December to see what Gaia DR4 does, but I'd skim. Honestly, I'm still not sure whether I'm reading about physics or about how an AI does research, and I think the site could say outright which story it's telling a newcomer.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /interactive-tools, /learning-paths, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /research-philosophy, /key-claims, /test-catalog, /crossover-regime-visualizer, /equation-walkthrough)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I'm not judging the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an auditor
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is in better shape than most research sites I've audited. Paths are numbered, the counts add up, and there's one canonical badge legend. The weak spot is the label system: there are now more kinds of status word than a reader can hold in their head, and the words for "this didn't work" aren't mapped to each other.

### Journey

**Landing → nav audit.** The header has nine items, each with a tooltip that does real work ("26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)", "130 terms defined", "badge families (definitions on Honest Assessment)"). The footer repeats them in two rows. Good. The hero offers three audience shortcuts (no physics / physicist / researcher), which is the right first fork.

I checked acronyms on the landing. MOND, MRH, A2ACW and CFD are all expanded inline, which is well done. But the page also has an "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC)" line that *names* the abbreviations without defining them. The definitions sit in a key under "Scientific Outcomes", well below many first uses (RAR, ΔBIC, ΛCDM, σ, dex, LLM). Some are never defined on the page at all: **Λ** (status word "Is Λ"), **Υ**, **M/L**, **Bmax**, **a0′**, **S** (the Bell statistic), and **DR4/DR3**. DR4 matters most, because "Gaia DR4 is released on 2 December 2026" is the landing's one dated hook, and the key defines only DR2.

**Learning Paths.** I cross-checked this page against the landing, and it's the best-kept page on the site. Beginner has 5 steps whose per-step times (8+10+6+9+4) add to the stated "~37 min". Advanced is 8 steps, with step 8 = Test Roadmap as the landing says. Methodology is 6 steps, with step 1 = Research Philosophy. All agree. The step indicators on the pages match too: What Synchronism Is Not says "Step 4 of 5", Research Philosophy "Step 1 of 6", Test Roadmap "Step 8 of 8" with "Final step", and Honest Assessment carries both "Step 5 of 5" and "Step 6 of 6" because it ends two paths. Prev/next links point where they should. Step 4 → step 5 lands on `#if-you-read-nothing-else` rather than the top of a ~30,000-word page, which is a thoughtful touch.

Small nits. The landing's step-4 card has no "Step 4 of 5" label while the other four cards do. And this page uses CDM, MOND and A2ACW in path titles without expanding them.

**Badge legend.** The landing says "the full set of nine is at the link" (`/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`). I went there, and it really is nine:

- MRH-relationship family: Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative
- Descriptive family: Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed
- Deprecated: Validated, Strongly Supported

The spelling is Title Case and consistent with Key Claims, the tools index and the Test Roadmap. That's a pass on "are badges explained before use", with one asterisk: the canonical legend lives *inside* a 190,000-character page. Key Claims also links each badge to a glossary entry (`/glossary#failed-badge` etc.), so there are two places that define the same badge. That's fine if the glossary entries are short pointers, and a drift risk if they're full definitions.

Where the label system gets heavy. The same legend also lists **seven "operational states"** that are "not badges" (Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Unrunnable as stated, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson). The Test Roadmap adds **lifecycle verbs** (Executed, Closed, Retired, Never run as registered, Underpowered as registered, Empty Intersection, Closed by Execution). Research Philosophy adds **five Speculative sub-types** (Motivated Ansatz, Dimensional Analysis, …). The landing's status table uses yet another set of words ("Excluded", "Is MOND", "Is Λ", "Survives; does not discriminate", "Disfavoured", "underpowered"), and those aren't in any legend. The Test Roadmap actually says "Excluded" "is not a label", yet it's a coloured status word on the landing. I count 9 + 7 + ~7 + 5 + ~10 ≈ 38 distinct status terms. Pass 1 bounced off the glossary's "eight kinds of label" table, and this is why.

**Refutation vocabulary.** "Failed" (badge), "refutation" (the headline count), "kill" / "Kill Criterion Triggered", "excluded" and "refuted" are used near-interchangeably, and no page maps them to each other. The worst instance is the Lorentz card on What Synchronism Is Not. It calls the same result "already excluded by existing precision experiments", then "not a refutation of the whole idea", then says the ledger lists the minimal framework as "refuted at its natural value". A reader can't tell whether those are three states or one. The footer's "6 refutations executed" also includes "1 registration that could not be met" and "1 construction check". The page *explains* that, but it means "refutation" is a term of art here, not the dictionary word.

**Test Roadmap.** The H1 matches the nav ("Test Roadmap"). The slug is `/test-catalog`, which is invisible to readers, so that's fine. The 26-test count reconciles (10+4+7+3 = 24, plus TEST-25/26), and the ID ranges are contiguous. The page has its own in-page "Status Badge Guide", which is the right place for one. Two things a skimmer will trip on:
- The page says "TEST-01, TEST-03s and TEST-05 are one environment test under three IDs", so 26 IDs ≠ 26 tests. The nav tooltip says "26 tests".
- The top box names four "Executed and Failed" items (TEST-09, -10, -25, -03s), while the footer says 6 refutations. The page doesn't name the other two, and it says "Whether TEST-09 stays in the count of 6 is pending." So the count is stated with confidence in one box and as open in another.

**Research Philosophy.** It has a clear structure (Core Principles 1–4, then taxonomy, then A2ACW), and it answers Pass 1's open question about who runs this: "A human (dp) reviews borderline cases and maintains the status badge taxonomy", and "Who audited: LLM agents, not an external human domain expert." That sentence should be on the landing next to "AI-run research notebook". Three wording issues:
- The page says "The taxonomy has two families" and then lists a third (Deprecated) group.
- It says "Every scientific claim on this site carries a validation badge", but also "'Withdrawn' does not carry any badge."
- The term itself was renamed: Honest Assessment's heading says these were "called 'validation badges' until 2026-11-16", yet this page still says "validation badge", and the canonical anchor is still `#validation-badge-definitions`. Keeping the anchor stable is right; the prose should follow the rename.

I also hit lines that read like internal memo copy in public prose, e.g. "No human audit decision is on record, so Session 662's line is wrong", which assumes a reader knows what Session 662 said.

**Key Claims.** It shows three badges (Speculative, Speculative, Failed), each linking to the legend and to the glossary. The landing labels the dark-matter claim "*Failed* as a mechanism". Here it's a bare "Failed", narrowed in the body ("the Failed badge above is on the _interpretation_"). It's consistent once you read the body, but the qualifier the landing puts on the badge is missing from the page that owns the claim.

**Changelog-in-body pattern.** On nearly every page I read, there are dated revision notes inline in the copy: "formerly 'Phase Boundary Visualizer'; the URL carried that name until 2026-10-23", "called 'validation badges' until 2026-11-16", "Revision history of this paragraph (verbatim)", "corrected 2026-07-17". Nav and path titles carry errata too: "The γ ≈ 1 Band (boundary reading retracted)" and "Decisive Tests (none currently running)". I understand why (honesty is the point of this site). But from a doc-structure view these are three different kinds of text (current claim, correction notice, history) sharing one typographic register. Some pages fold them ("Revision history…"); many don't. Pass 1 hit the same thing on the galaxy plotter's units-bug toggle.

### Tools Tested
- **Crossover Regime Visualizer** (`/crossover-regime-visualizer`): worked (H1, slider spec and band labels render; the readout card is client-side, and only "γ = 1.00" shows in static text). Confusing for a non-physicist.
  - **Naming is handled well.** The H1 uses the new name, and the old "Phase Boundary Visualizer" appears exactly once, as a dated disclosure.
  - **Instructions:** one slider (γ, 0–4, default 1.0), three bands (<0.6, 0.6–1.4, >1.4). "Your task is to catch the formula being wrong at every stop" is an unusual and honest framing.
  - **Undefined symbols:** the first paragraph uses γ, ρ, ρcrit, N_corr and tanh before defining any of them. N_corr is never defined explicitly, and it's written two ways (N_corr / Ncorr). The page also uses Ginzburg criterion, Gi, ξ0, Tc, EF, BCS, BEC and SC without definition.
  - **Cross-tool inconsistency:** the page puts enzymes in the γ ≈ 1 band (N_corr 3–11), and in the same breath notes the γ Calculator's enzyme preset is N_corr = 30 (γ ≈ 0.37, a different band). It acknowledges this, but one system shows up in two bands across two tools.
- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`): worked. Its own stepper ("← Previous · 1 / 8 · Next →") renders; only step 1 is in the static text, so steps 2–8 are client-side and I couldn't audit them without a browser. Intuitive at the top.
  - **Good disambiguation:** "Step 1 of 8 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)". On a site with five different step counters, that parenthetical is exactly right.
  - **Notation drifts slightly:** "tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))" in the main block vs "tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))" in a Related Concepts description (underscore, no "·").
  - **γ's role:** the legend says γ "sets the low-density slope and how fast C approaches 1, not a sharpness", and the ρcrit legend says "_where_ it sits is set by γ". The Crossover tool says "γ sets how early the curve rises". Pass 1 found the Coherence Explorer saying "no setting makes it abrupt". So **three tools agree γ is about position/onset, not sharpness. The landing's "how fast that switch flips" is the outlier.** This independently confirms Pass 1's top friction item.
  - **Undefined acronyms:** it uses BCS, BEC, SPARC, RAR, MOND, ΔBIC, dex and "10–90% width" without expansion.
- (Context, not a full tool test) The **Interactive Tools index** lists 7 cards with Kind, Level, State and Badge for each, which is a clean, scannable schema. Card 7 (Consciousness Threshold Demo) describes itself as "a static chart with hover notes, not an interactive tool: no controls", yet it's listed under "All of Synchronism's interactive tools". The index also flags Coherence Explorer as "Best first tool", but the Beginner Path never routes through it (step 3 is the Galaxy Plotter).

- **The preferred-basis problem is never named.** For any decoherence-based account of measurement, this is *the* question: why do pointers end up localized in position? "Crossing the MRH" doesn't select a basis.
- **Door #3 decoheres the wrong basis for this job.** The page (and /for-researchers) says door #3's diffusion is "in position, not momentum", which is why the CSL (λ, r_C) plane doesn't apply. That's correct as far as it goes. But a position random walk is generated by a Lindblad term in p̂. It dephases *momentum* superpositions (Γ ∝ D·Δk²) and leaves superpositions of macroscopically distinct *positions* untouched. So even unbounded, door #3 couldn't produce position-localized pointer states. It's the x↔p dual of what a measurement mechanism needs. The page should say this, because it closes door #3 as a measurement mechanism independently of the 15–38-order bound. The bound kills its magnitude; the basis kills its role.
- **A smaller point:** the page says decoherence "does not give definite outcomes" as if that were Synchronism's distinctive contribution. Every interpretation adds outcome selection to decoherence somehow (Everett, Bohm, QBism, relational QM). The page names Zurek, Deutsch–Wallace and Carroll–Sebens but not QBism or Rovelli. "Crossing the MRH" sounds a lot like relational QM's relative facts, and that's the comparison worth making.

**/honest-assessment** (refutation ledger and Research Outputs). The ledger is right, with one omission from the site's own definition. The page states a sharper criterion: "refutes the framework's own content on outside data" requires (a) a statement the archive asserts for this framework and (b) an external dataset. Only 3 of the 6 meet it (#1, #2, #3), and only #2 is in the rule-robust floor of four. **That is the number I'd quote in a review: one rule-robust refutation of the framework's own content (TEST-10), plus two more that move with the counting rule.** The "6" is mostly the record's bookkeeping: an inherited MOND failure (Cassini), a failed registration (environment), and a theorem (Bell). I agree with Pass 3 on row 6.

Same-quantity-different-number pairs within one page are still common:
- TEST-10 at 6.39: "3 stay above it at ≥ 3σ (NGC 2915, NGC 3741, UGC 06667)" vs "2 at 3σ or more… (NGC 2915, NGC 3741)".
- TEST-09's card calls "B_max ≲ 14 excluded by SPARC dwarfs" a bound, while row 2's note says "the 13.7 once printed as an upper bound of 14 is one disc's maximum, not a bound."

**/cdm-discrimination.** It has the right banner ("Superseded — Original 'Below CDM' Claim Retracted In-Archive"), and the noise-modelling case study (z from +0.5 to +64 depending on choices) is a good cautionary tale. But:
- **The page still says 0.086 dex "sits about 3× below CDM"** (Honest Caveat) against Desmond 2017's ~0.25 dex. That ~0.25 dex is scatter in log *baryonic mass*. The 0.086 is an in-sample residual on W50 *velocity widths* from a five-predictor regression, and the page's own Desmond table quotes SPARC at 0.029 in log V. The page says a like-for-like comparison "has not been run", then quotes a ratio anyway.
- **The title promises CDM discrimination, but the page covers none of the places where MOND-class phenomenology actually struggles against CDM:** cluster residual mass, the CMB third peak, the Bullet Cluster, galaxy–galaxy lensing out to ~1 Mpc (Brouwer+ 2021), and rotation-curve diversity (Oman+ 2015). The framework is MOND in galaxies and Λ in cosmology. The referee question is what plays the role of CDM at z ≈ 1100 and in clusters. If the answer is "CDM", the "unification" is MOND + ΛCDM side by side. That's a coherent position (it's roughly where hybrid superfluid-DM models sit), but it should be stated. I didn't find this addressed on the pages I read; /dark-matter may cover it.

**/research-philosophy (A2ACW).** This is where the genuinely new material is. It isn't physics, it's measurement of a method:
- 3,308 sessions → ~47 internal-consistency survivors (itself flagged as a ~57% overcount of ~30) → **0 of 9** audited claims survived (Clopper–Pearson 95% upper bound ~28–34%).
- A calibration arm, written and scored by one Claude model, gave sensitivity 3/3, specificity 0/6, Youden's J = 0. The literal rule flagged every canonical discovery as a reparametrization.
- Claude replicates agree with each other 86% but with the cross-vendor (LV) verdicts only 41–52%, and every one of the 17 disagreements was harsher.
- "0 of 3,308 A2ACW sessions cited any" literature.

Those are real numbers on a question people care about: can LLM adversarial self-play tell discovery from rediscovery? The current answer is "the critic has no discrimination; it just shifts the threshold." But it's an n = 9 arm scored by its own author. The experiment that would make it publishable (the "modest-novelty results published after the models' training cutoff" arm) "is unrun (routed to the project lead since 2026-09-10)". In my view this is the highest-value item on the whole site, and it's blocked.

What the page doesn't acknowledge:
- How the 9 audited claims (and the "top 3 of ~47") were selected. If they were the most prominent claims rather than a random draw, 0/9 is a statement about the headline claims, not about the method's yield.
- That the harsher-than-LV direction of all 17 disagreements is itself the "over-refutation" signature. It should be connected to the 6/6 false flags in the calibration arm, because both say the current critic is miscalibrated in the *strict* direction now.

### Tools Tested

All seven tools are listed on /interactive-tools. Passes 1–3 covered five, so I tested the remaining two for scientific accuracy.

- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`): it works, as a sortable table of 23 hand-curated rows with regime filters (All 23 / Neutral 4 / Coherence helps 12 / Incoherence helps 4 / Barrier-dominated 3). It's honest but presented as evidence.
  - **Disclosure is good.** "Each r is a post-hoc fit against a γ proxy that contains the target". With γ_phonon = 2T/θ_D, a correlation with 1/γ is exactly a correlation with θ_D, which is correct because it's linear at fixed T. The page states "There is no single 23-material sample" (n = 19–33 per row, different materials per row), and it publishes the jackknife without diamond (Young's 0.60, shear 0.63, bulk 0.37).
  - **The default view is still a table sorted by r descending**, opening on "sound velocity +0.982". For a tool whose own badge says every r restates its ingredient, the default sort presents the restatement as the headline.
  - **The null shown is the wrong null.** It's a polynomial in atomic number Z (|r| ≤ 0.53, quadratic R ≤ 0.51) on 10 unnamed recoverable rows. The page itself says the right null "is the ingredient alone, not Z" (θ_D or IE) and then doesn't run it. Under the page's own identity that null would reproduce the r values. That's the point, and it would be the most persuasive thing the tool could display: a second column "r with θ_D alone", equal to the first.
  - **Physics details:**
    - "θ_D ∝ v" holds only at fixed atomic number density (θ_D ∝ v_m n^{1/3}), so across the row's materials the restatement isn't exact.
    - The electronegativity row uses Pauling χ, while the restatement argument cites Mulliken's IE-based χ. Pauling χ correlates with IE but isn't defined from it.
  - **Provenance:** printed r values have moved repeatedly (0.979→0.938/0.956; 0.955→−0.414; 0.961→0.804/0.883), and the per-material N_corr table "has not been published". The r column has no fixed provenance, and the page says so.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (`/consciousness-demo`, with /hard-problem and /consciousness-predictions): a static chart with no controls, as the index says.
  - **The physics check passes.** The site's maximum-sensitivity condition C* = 1/(2γu*) reproduces: C* = 0.500 at γ = ½, ≈ 0.60 at γ = 2, ≈ 0.497 at γ = 0.487.
  - **But that undercuts the chart.** The framework's *registered* γ = 2 puts the anchor at ~0.60, the other end of the axis, so the 0.50 line isn't the framework's own value.
  - **Seven "approaches" agree at 0.50 ± 0.011.** They're described as "Synchronism-internal" estimates with no sources, for a quantity the page says "cannot be computed for any real system". Independent estimates of an undefined quantity should scatter. Agreement at the output-range midpoint to ±0.01 is a signature of **anchoring in generation**, not convergence. That's a methodology finding the site could claim, and it pairs naturally with the A2ACW calibration results.
  - **Inconsistencies across the three pages:**
    - Two different Cs share the symbol and the threshold: the demo's C_ρ(γ, ρ) and /hard-problem's C = f(γ, D, S).
    - "8-way convergence" on /hard-problem vs "7 approaches (an eighth has no critical point)".
    - "'Dissolved' would overclaim it" sits under section headings "The Dissolution" and "Mind-Body: Dissolved".
    - The EEG prediction is marked "Testable: Yes — $150K, 12 months" on a page whose badge says the threshold is untestable as stated.
  - **No link to existing theories.** The page cites PCI* = 0.31 (Casarotto+ 2016) as the instrument a mapping would need, which is the right instrument. Global workspace, higher-order and recurrent-processing theories aren't mentioned. IIT appears only as "cf. Φ", with no reference.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **What plays CDM's role at z ≈ 1100 and in clusters?** The cosmology identity C = Ω_m(a) and the 1/Ω_m floor both use Ω_m ≈ 0.315, which includes non-baryonic matter. If the framework assumes CDM there, what does it replace in galaxies, and why doesn't that CDM also sit in galaxy halos?
2. How were the 9 audited A2ACW claims selected? Is 0/9 a statement about the method or about its most visible outputs?
3. When will the post-cutoff discrimination arm run, and who scores it? A scorer from the same model family as the generator repeats the calibration arm's main weakness.
4. Has the local-density no-go + LLR Ġ/G bound on density-keyed G_eff been compared with existing constraints on environment-dependent couplings (chameleon/symmetron screening, Ġ/G-from-environment literature)? It's the most portable negative result on the site, and its novelty depends on that comparison.
5. Is there any route left to a positive physics result, or is the honest next step to write up the density-keyed no-go and the A2ACW calibration as two short papers and stop adding sectors?
6. Why does the Chemistry Explorer still sort by r if the site's position is that r measures the ingredient?

### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics, and the site says so more precisely than most published modified-gravity papers state their own limits. The "0 confirmed novel predictions" verdict is accurate. The stronger statement, which the site's own forward table implies but doesn't say, is that **no dataset in view can change it**: the program can only lose. What *is* new is not on the physics pages:
- a framework-independent no-go for density-keyed modified gravity (via LLR Ġ/G)
- the first numbers I've seen on whether LLM adversarial self-play can distinguish discovery from rediscovery (specificity 0/6, PPV 0/9, cross-vendor agreement 41–52%)

That second result is one experiment (the post-cutoff arm, waiting since September) from being publishable. If I were advising this project, I'd stop opening physics sectors, run that arm with an out-of-family scorer, and write up the two negative results. Smaller concerns: the wide-binary page should describe the 2026 dispute as it stands rather than the 2024 one, and the measurement page should say that door #3 dephases the wrong basis.
