**Short answer:** I found no published source for a₀ = c·H₀·Ω_m^φ. I believe it is not in the peer-reviewed literature. The numerology is also weak.

**What I found**
- Milgrom (2020), "The a₀–cosmology connection in MOND", arXiv:2001.09729, is the standard reference for a₀ ~ cH₀ ~ c²√Λ. It treats this as a coincidence that may point to a deeper theory. It contains no Ω_m^φ factor.
- Milgrom's earlier work, including the pedagogical review astro-ph/0112069, gives a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π. The Unruh/de Sitter vacuum-inertia suggestion is in Milgrom 1999 (astro-ph/9805346). Both are in the results, but I did not check them in detail.
- arXiv:1106.4108 (a holographic-screen MOND model) cites a₀ ≈ a_Λ/2π with a_Λ = √(Λ/3). I did not verify the attribution.
- Bekenstein and Sagi (arXiv:0802.1526) discuss a time-varying a₀. The search snippet gave a₀(z) ∝ [Ω_m(1+z)³+Ω_Λ]^½. I did not check that expression against the paper. This is about redshift evolution, not a fixed Ω_m power.
- The only source I found with a golden ratio is "The Proslambenomenos" on nickjoven.github.io. It is an unreviewed personal website, not a journal or arXiv paper. It uses a Kuramoto-synchronization picture, a₀ = cH₀/(2π√g*(1/φ)) with g* = 0.697, and a fitted ratio. That is a different formula from yours, though it is also golden-ratio-flavored and brings in Ω_m. Treat it as uncertain and not citable physics.

**Numerical check**
- cH₀ = 3×10⁸ × 2.27×10⁻¹⁸ ≈ 6.8×10⁻¹⁰ m/s².
- 0.3^1.618 ≈ 0.143, so a₀ ≈ 0.97×10⁻¹⁰ m/s².
- That is not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. It is about 20% low, or about 1.0×10⁻¹⁰ with rounding.
- Observed a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ (McGaugh et al. 2016). The quoted value therefore doesn't follow from the formula.
- cH₀/2π ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰, which fits better than the proposed formula and needs no free exponent.

**Assessment**
- The exponent φ has no derivation I could find. With Ω_m uncertain at about ±0.01 and H₀ uncertain at about ±7%, many exponents would fit. This looks like numerology.
- Ω_m^φ would also make a₀ depend on the matter fraction. That conflicts with the usual MOND picture, in which a₀ is fixed or tied to Λ.
- I searched only a limited number of times. A narrower search of arXiv for "golden ratio" with MOND or cosmology could still turn up an obscure paper.
