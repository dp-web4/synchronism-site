## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /glossary, /honest-assessment, /galaxy-rotation, /where-its-useful)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've clicked into from Reddit. The beginner path (Why → First Encounter → Plotter) actually works. But if I leave that path, even by one click on a homepage card, I hit a wall of ΔBIC, σ and acronyms within the first screen.

### Journey

**Landing page.** The headline question ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") hooked me. Then the page said, almost right away, that the idea is "dead so far" as tested physics, with "0 confirmed novel predictions, 6 refutations executed." That's disarming, and I've never seen a theory site lead with its own failure. It also made me wonder why I'm reading about a dead theory. The "So is it alive or dead?" block answers that: it's alive as a public record of testing. I mostly bought that. The "not quantum coherence — a superconductor scores low" note was confusing. Superconductors are the textbook "everything acting together" example from YouTube, so why do they score low? That made me trust the word "coherence" less, not more.

The landing page is long, and it has a lot of links (I counted roughly 70). Further down it shows three different versions of "C", including `Ca = Ωm + (1 − Ωm)·x/(1+x)` and `x = (gbar/a0)^(1/φ)`. On a front page that's the point where I would normally bounce. "1.4% internal-consistency survival rate" and "about 3,300 AI-to-AI sessions" meant nothing to me. The "AI-run notebook with one human overseer, no outside physicist has reviewed it" line was useful to know. I'm glad it's stated up front. The "No physics background?" routing paragraph saved me: I clicked "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)".

**Why Synchronism (step 1/5).** This was good. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a pitch I understand. The crowd-in-a-plaza vs marching-band picture is the one image I left the site with. "What happened, in three lines" followed by "Beginner path: you can stop here" is a kind touch. The page keeps saying "6 refutations" without telling me what a refutation is here, though: a failed test? A disproof? Further down, "4–8 under rules on file" made me feel the score itself is up for argument. The melting-point (53% error) and superconductor (607 K predicted vs 93 K actual) failures were concrete and easy to follow. More of that, please.

**First Encounter (step 2/5).** The best page for me. "Electron vs neutron star core, 10¹⁴× denser than lead" got my attention. The "dimmer switch" for tanh and the "ln turns 10×, 100×, 1000× into evenly spaced steps" explanation are the first times anyone has made those symbols mean something to me. Sticking points:
- A correlation shown as "±0.55" when the prediction was +0.55 and the measurement was −0.55. Writing it as "±" hides the most interesting part: it came out backwards.
- The galaxy number "happens to match" cH₀/(2π), and then it's "off by ~13%." Is 13% a match or not? I couldn't tell whether to be impressed.
- The short section E teaser ("A number measured in galaxies sits suspiciously close…") sits before its own heading and looks like a stray line.

**Interactive Tools hub.** Clear. I liked that it says "Model Explainer" means "shows how the equation works," not that it's correct, and that "no tool here is certified correct." "Artifact Lesson" took me two reads, and I still only half get it: the tool deliberately shows a failure?

**Coherence Explorer.** See Tools Tested. The intro reused the marching band, which helped.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (step 3/5).** "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" is exactly what I needed: dots = telescope, gray = visible stuff only, green = MOND, violet = this theory. "That is the whole lesson" landed: the theory's own line misses the dots, and the version that hits them is just MOND rewritten. Then the readouts underneath lost me ("Max C_ρ on this disk = 0.001", "flat 3.17× boost", "quadrature vs division wiring", Ωm).

**Glossary.** The "New here? Start with these ten" list is a great idea. Several of the ten definitions then lean on words I don't have (Coherence's notes mention N_corr, BEC, BCS and "mean-field", and Dark matter names ΛCDM without spelling it out). The page is also huge. I used the ten and left.

**Honest Assessment.** It says it's "about 30,000 words," which made me laugh out loud. The "If you read nothing else" box is genuinely great, though: "failed its galaxy tests… where it fits it is MOND in different notation… As a theory of galaxies: fatal." That's the clearest sentence on the site. I stopped there.

**Galaxy Rotation (from the homepage "Cosmology" card).** This is where I bounced. The page opens with a banner "Failed — density-keyed form ΔBIC +2843; γ = 2 compander disfavoured at about 2σ". The first paragraph is about what "+2843 compares" across "2,438 SPARC points in 122 galaxies", and the second is "γ ≈ ½ twice is not a cross-sector agreement". The plain-English sentence explaining what a rotation curve even is ("outer stars orbit faster than visible mass predicts") only comes after all that. MOND is used in the banner and, as far as I could see, never spelled out on this page. Coming from the Plotter, I already knew the story, but someone clicking the homepage card first would be lost on the first screen.

**Where It's Already Useful.** The "AI agents in a lab, every command gets a rule check" example was understandable. Then `Web4 = MCP + RDF + LCT + T3/V3*MRH + ATP/ADP` appears with no term explained, and the banner badge "Active-MRH — Applied design ontology — load-bearing in running code" reads like a password. The page also says, fairly, that the software "would work the same whether or not the physics is right." That left me unsure why it's on a physics site at all.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: rendered (via WebFetch I saw the server-rendered default state; I couldn't physically drag the sliders, so I can't confirm live behavior). Fairly intuitive. Defaults γ = 0.49, ρ_crit = 1.00, with key values C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259 and C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785. The "One thing to try first" (drag γ up to 2, then back down to 0.5) and the four preset buttons (0.046 / 0.49 / ½ / 2) are good beginner scaffolding. The line "the two presets γ = 0.49 and γ = ½ are nearly equal, which is why the fit is not a new prediction" was the moment the MOND story clicked for me. The note "a galaxy disc sits ~10³ below the knee" raised a big question (see below).
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: rendered (default DDO 154 state seen; galaxy buttons, the Υdisk slider and checkboxes need JS, which I couldn't exercise via fetch). Intuitive at the top, confusing at the bottom. The legend wording ("Observed (what telescopes measure)", "This framework's equation (tested reading, failed)", "MOND, the rival theory") is perfect for me. The "Try the twin pair" exercise (NGC 2403 vs UGC 128, the same speed but 16× dimmer) sounds like the most fun thing on the site, but I didn't understand what result I should expect to see or why it matters. One more sentence on what MOND says should happen would make it a real experiment. The checkbox "Show the old hand-drawn stand-in" puzzled me. Why is a hand-drawn curve still offered?

### Unanswered Questions
1. The Coherence Explorer says a galaxy disc sits about 1,000× below the "knee", and the Plotter says the max C on DDO 154 is 0.001. So by the framework's own number, galaxies have almost no coherence. Then why was the theory expected to explain galaxies in the first place?
2. Is MOND right, then? The site keeps saying "it's just MOND", but never tells me in one sentence whether MOND itself is considered correct, wrong or contested.
3. The homepage says Gaia DR4 comes out on 2 December 2026. What exactly will it show, and could it bring the theory back to life or only finish it off?
4. Why does a superconductor score *low* on coherence?
5. Who checks the AI's work? "No outside physicist has reviewed it" is honest, but then how do I know the failures are real failures rather than the AI's own mistakes?
6. What's the consciousness card doing next to the galaxy stuff? Is that serious?

### Verdict
The beginner path is the strongest thing here: Why → First Encounter → Galaxy Plotter gave me a real story in about 20 minutes (a crowd vs a marching band, a dimmer switch, a line that misses the dots, and a rewritten MOND that hits them). The honesty is what would make me share it. Off the path the site assumes a physicist: one wrong card click from the homepage drops you into ΔBIC and SPARC point counts. Fix the first screen of the pages that homepage cards point to, and a casual visitor stays.

*Method note for the maintainer:* pages were read via WebFetch subagents. The glossary (~109k chars), Honest Assessment (~190k chars) and the tail of /galaxy-rotation came back partly as model summaries past ~40k chars. So "Presence (ρ)" and "σ" not appearing in the glossary text I read is a truncation artifact, not a finding, and I haven't logged it as friction.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /interactive-tools, /learning-paths, /what-synchronism-is-not, /glossary, /honest-assessment (top + badge section), /test-catalog, /gamma-calculator, /crossover-regime-visualizer, /core-idea, /for-researchers)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure); partial (of the terminology system)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an auditor
- **Overall impression**: The site's counts are unusually disciplined and agree across pages: 26 tests, 125 glossary terms, 9 badges, 0 / 6 (4–8). The weak layer is vocabulary. One letter (C) names three functions, γ has three definitions, sample sizes are never reconciled, and the pages narrate their own edit history to readers who don't know who the "personas" are.

### Journey

**Landing → header nav.** The header has nine items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Interactive Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment). The footer adds First Encounter and Key Claims. Key Claims is reachable from the footer and from a teaser on the landing page, but not from the header. That's odd, because the landing teaser treats it as the central "where does Synchronism move the needle?" page. The landing page handles acronyms with an "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC): [short key]" jump link, which is a sensible fix. The key itself sits far down inside Scientific Outcomes, though, and about ten abbreviations are used before it. M/L and Υ appear in the refutation table ("with distance, inclination and M/L propagated") and are defined neither in the key nor, as far as I could see, in the glossary (see below). ΛCDM is described ("the standard cosmological model") but its letters are never spelled out on any page I read. DR4 is never expanded on the landing page. The glossary has it.

**Badges.** These are explained well, and better than most science sites manage. The landing page defines "Reparametrization" inline, links to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, and says there are nine badges. Honest Assessment's section ("Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)") lists exactly nine: Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed. The count matches. /core-idea also opens with a local "Badge key (you'll meet these below)" listing only the five used on that page. I'd steal that pattern for every page. The trouble is at the edges of the system:
- The glossary's badge intro says "Deprecated badges (Validated, Strongly Supported)", and its label table says "Retired label: Validated, Strongly Supported, MOND-shared". Deprecated and retired aren't the same thing, and the lists differ (MOND-shared).
- The glossary's Refutation entry says refuted claims "carry the Failed badge", while its Outcome-words entry says "Failed or Audited-Negative".
- Null-Class is listed as an "Operational state" in the intro table, while its own entry calls it a "finding descriptor, not a badge".
- The glossary defines Audited-Negative as "the strongest closure badge", but the RAR γ = 2 entry wearing it says "disfavoured at about 2σ". To a non-physicist "strongest" and "about 2σ" read as different strengths. I can't judge the physics, but the words should not pull in opposite directions.
- On /crossover-regime-visualizer the badge reads "Audited-Negative" while the explanatory heading reads "Sharpness direction is inverted (open problem)". Is it closed or open?

**Interactive Tools hub.** This is the clearest legend on the site. It opens with "Before the legend: this is a _content grouping_, not the site's validation badge taxonomy", and it separates Kind / Level / Status / Badge with one scale per slot. It also explains that "Artifact Lesson" is "not a level — it is an operational state". That's exactly how a doc set should separate orthogonal metadata. The tool cards have no path membership, though: only the Galaxy Plotter belongs to a learning path.

**Learning Paths.** Seven paths (Beginner 5, Intermediate 9, Advanced 8, Physics 7, Chemistry 6, Philosophy 7, Methodology 6). Every number I checked matched the "Step N of M" labels on the destination pages: What-Is-Not is "Step 4 of 5", Test Catalog "Step 8 of 8", Honest Assessment "Step 5 of 5" and "Step 6 of 6", Core Idea "Step 1 of 9" and "Step 1 of 7". Prev/next links work and land on the right pages. A page that sits on two paths (Core Idea, Honest Assessment) shows both labels stacked, which is correct but visually busy. Two conflicts:
- Honest Assessment's link text says "full page about 30,000 words, about 2 hours", and the same page's body says "the full 80-minute read". The landing page says "~30,000" in one place and "~18,000" in a revision note.
- The Advanced and Methodology paths are numbered on the landing page ("Advanced Path step 8 of 8") but never introduced there.

**What Synchronism Is Not.** It's a good scope page. It explicitly says it "carries no validation badge" because it's a scope page, which is the right call, and stated rather than silently omitted. Acronym load is heavy, though: MOND, ΛCDM, QFT, GR, CMB, EFE, RAR, SPARC, CHSH, MRH, PRL and EEG are all unexpanded on the page. It's step 4 of the *Beginner* path, so this matters more here than anywhere else.

**Glossary.** It says "125 terms", and my reader's count also came to 125 (91 verbatim + 34 seen only in summary). There's a letter index plus a "Greek (γ, ρ, σ…)" jump and a "New here? Start with these ten" list. Good. It answers most of my acronym checks: MRH, MOND, BTFR, RAR, DR4, A2ACW, dex, Compander, N_corr, LCT and EFE all have entries. Gaps I could verify on the verbatim part: no entry for **M/L** (the M section reads complete: Mean-Field, Milgrom, Model Explainer, MOND, MOND-shared, MRH) and none for **Υ**, though "global Υ" is used inside the BIC entry. "Counting rule" has no heading of its own and is buried in the Refutation entry, yet it's the most-linked concept on the landing page (`#count-rule`). Web4 appears only inside the LCT heading.

**Honest Assessment.** The top of the page has a proper TOC ("On this page (full table of contents)", 22 entries) whose anchors match what the landing page links to. One TOC label is "Counting rules (why 4–8)", while the target is a bold run-in lead ("The counting rules on file, and what each does to the 6"), not a heading. That works for the anchor, but it isn't an outline entry.

**Test Roadmap.** The numbers reconcile: "26 tests (24 in the original registry + 2 added later)", and the tiers add up (10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24, plus TEST-25/26). The legend says both badge lists "are complete and rendered from one source". The status vocabulary in prose is large and inconsistently cased, though: "Underpowered as Registered", "Never run as registered", "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie", "Unrunnable as stated", "not falsifiable as stated". There's also no single table giving test ID → tier → status. Statuses are scattered through prose, so I had to assemble that table myself to check anything.

**Core Idea (Intermediate step 1).** The per-page badge key is excellent. This page also showed me the deepest terminology problem:
- **γ** gets three definitions on one page: "proposed as a transition sharpness" (first card), "a Hill coefficient … not a coupling" (correction), and a fitted "dial" (0.489 or 0.046). The slider on the Crossover Visualizer still says "labelled γ (proposed as the sharpness of the S-curve)".
- **ρ** is "Presence: local density" with number-density examples ("stars per cubic light-year, atoms per unit volume"). /for-researchers says "'ρ' means mass density in rows 3–5".
- **C** is introduced as the landing equation C(ρ). /for-researchers says "Three functions share the letter" (Cρ, Cg, Ca), adds that Tier 1 defines fDM = 1−C so that C "_is_ the interpolating function μ by definition", and warns: "A reader who takes the landing equation and tries to reproduce TEST-10 will compute the wrong function." That warning belongs at the point of definition (Core Idea, Glossary "Coherence"), not on the researcher page only.
- **ρcrit** is "A × Vflat²: saturation knee (not a critical point)", and the card then says this scaling is refuted. Vflat (a galaxy rotation speed) isn't defined here.

**For Researchers.** It's 110k characters, built mostly from bold run-in labels rather than heading tags, so there's no TOC or anchor structure to navigate by. The page opens with "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)", and a note quotes "a technical-writer simulated-visitor persona said it read like a changelog." It still opens with a changelog. In the part I could read verbatim there's no citation format and no contact route, on a page whose title is "What's Citable". Data and code are referenced by filename ("gc_slope_with_mond.py") with no links. Its headline numbers agree with the rest of the site, but it adds that "Whether TEST-09 stays in the count of 6 is pending the project lead". So the 6 is provisional, while the landing page and the footer present it without that caveat.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`). Rendered; I saw the server-rendered default state only and could not operate the controls via fetch. Fairly intuitive. Default N_corr = 4 → "γ = 1.0000", "C(ρcrit) = 0.60", labelled "Boundary (γ ≈ 1) — a label per the inverted formula (Caveat 2), not a physical classification". The Quick Reference table values are internally consistent (e.g. 1,000,000 → 2.00e-3). ### Verdict
There is nothing new here as physics, and the site says so correctly. Every surviving branch reduces to μ_simple (galaxies) or Λ (cosmology) in a γ ≈ ½ limit that I verified algebraically, and the framework-specific bets (bounded boost, ambient density, EFE = 0 via Refracted Gravity) are failed or undefined. What's useful to the field is incidental: a careful wide-binary literature audit with a potentially important code check on Saad & Ting, a clean demonstration that a tanh-log Cardassian family is sign-locked out of the DESI quadrant, and a candid record of how an AI-run program fails. The weakest instrument is the ledger. It counts shared failures and construction checks as refutations while leaving the headline equation's worst data failures out, which makes the one number on every footer the least reliable number on the site. As for A2ACW, it hasn't been calibrated. The cross-vendor result suggests it measures the model as much as the claim, and the experiment that would settle that is sitting unrun.
