## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, start-here, core-idea, interactive-tools, coherence-explorer, consciousness-demo, honest-assessment, why-synchronism, galaxy-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A weirdly honest science site that hooked me with a great question and a dimmer-switch analogy, then kept telling me its own idea doesn't work — I respect it but I'm not sure what I'm supposed to *do* with it.

### Journey
Clicked through from Reddit expecting either a crackpot theory-of-everything or something cool. The landing headline — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — is exactly the kind of thing PBS Space Time would make a video about, so I kept reading. The big equation has letters I don't know (what is tanh? what is ln?) but the line "a smooth S-curve from sparse/independent to dense/collective" actually told me what it does, which is more than most equation-heavy sites manage. What stopped me cold was "0 confirmed predictions" stated right on the front page. I've never seen a site advertise that. Honestly it made me trust it more AND wonder why I should keep reading.

"Start Here" / First Encounter was friendly at first — the marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza picture clicked immediately. Then within a paragraph or two I hit "MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)," "BEC," "BCS," and "degrees of freedom" and started skimming. The 10-minute estimate felt optimistic for me.

The Core Idea page's "dimmer switch" framing is the best single sentence on the site for someone like me: density goes in, a number between 0 (everyone separate) and 1 (everyone locked together) comes out. I genuinely got that. Then the "Why log?" section talked about "Landau-universality" and "central-limit-theorem scaling" and I bounced to the tools.

Tools were the highlight. The Coherence Explorer sliders made sense once I connected them to the dimmer switch — sharper vs. gentler snap. The Consciousness Demo was the page I most wanted to love (consciousness at 0.50! eight methods agree!) and the page itself immediately told me the agreement is basically baked-in math, not evidence. That was deflating but, again, weirdly respectable. The Galaxy Plotter was my favorite: "outer stars spin too fast" is a puzzle I knew from YouTube, and seeing the gray prediction droop while the blue dots stay flat made it real. Though with three curves all overlapping I quietly thought "so it works?" until the caption told me overlapping is exactly what proves nothing.

Ended on Honest Assessment and Why Synchronism. The plain-language failure summaries ("we predicted slower growth, real data shows faster") were readable; the acronym soup (RAR, ΔBIC, fσ₈, SPARC) was not, and I skimmed most of the middle. Why Synchronism surprised me by having basically no analogies — it felt like the one entry-point page written for physicists.

### Journey
I worked the site the way I'd audit any docs product: landing page, every nav link's destination skimmed, then deep reads of the entry funnel (Start Here, Learning Paths, Why Synchronism), the reference layer (Glossary, Honest Assessment), and two tools.

Naming consistency: the nav says "Start Here," the destination page is titled "First Encounter," and Learning Paths' beginner track starts at "Why Synchronism?" instead — three competing front doors. And the "Phase Boundary Visualizer" carries a caveat explaining it's *not* a phase boundary ("a smooth compander... with no critical point") — the page name asserts what its own content retracts.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, parameter-derivations, coherence-function, gamma-parameter, galaxy-rotation, honest-assessment, interactive-tools, equation-walkthrough, chemistry-correlation-explorer, key-claims, for-researchers)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most rigorously self-refuted framework I've ever read — the badges are honest, the reparametrizations are correctly attributed, the arithmetic I spot-checked is right — but the dedicated topic pages lag the self-audit pages, so the *same parameter* gets a clean presentation on one page and a demolition on another.

### Journey
A colleague sent me this with "interesting AI project," so I came in expecting either crankery or curve-fitting dressed as theory. The landing page surprised me twice: once by defining every symbol in the core equation inline (including calling ρ_crit "a saturation knee, not a critical point" — correct, and a distinction most pop-physics sites would never make), and once by stating "0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit" above the fold.

I went straight to /parameter-derivations, because that's where frameworks like this die. It opens by admitting it isn't a derivations page in the mathematical sense, then audits its own headline numbers: γ = 2/√N_corr is labeled a "Motivated Ansatz" with the fatal flaw stated outright — CLT 1/√N scaling is invoked for *correlated* degrees of freedom, exactly where iid fails. The A-from-Jeans audit is the most damning thing I've seen a project publish about itself: the stated formula with stated inputs gives 4.6×10⁻⁵, not the advertised 0.029, a ~600× discrepancy that "propagated ~600 sessions without anyone re-running the stated formula." a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is correctly credited to Milgrom-onward dimensional analysis, and Σ₀ to Freeman's law re-expressed. As a referee I'd have nothing to add — the page has already written my report.

Then I hit /gamma-parameter and found the problem with this site: the *dedicated page* for γ presents the CLT argument straight ("this is the standard central limit theorem result: fluctuations in a correlated ensemble scale as 1/√N") with none of the self-contradiction the parameter-derivations page documents. "Fluctuations in a correlated ensemble scale as 1/√N" is just false as stated — that's the iid result, and correlations are precisely what break it. One page on this site knows that; the other doesn't. There's also a direction problem neither sentence on /gamma-parameter confronts: 1/√N is a fluctuation *width* (more particles → narrower), while γ is a transition *sharpness* (larger → sharper), so the most collective systems get the *flattest* coherence curves — which is how BCS superconductors end up at C ≈ 0. The coherence-function page discloses that consequence honestly; the γ page that generates it doesn't discuss it. I did check the example arithmetic myself: N_corr = 10²⁴ → γ = 2×10⁻¹² is right.

/coherence-function checked out numerically. C(ρ_crit, γ=2): I get tanh(2 ln 2) = 0.8824, matching the stated ≈0.88. The compander framing — explicitly *not* an order parameter, no self-consistency loop, no critical exponents, "the Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen" — is exactly the right category and most authors would not have conceded it. I also verified the slope structure: against linear ρ the slope is maximal at ρ → 0 and strictly decreasing (2.00 at ρ=0, 0.22 at ρ_crit in units of 1/ρ_crit), and C = 0.5 sits at ρ ≈ 0.316 ρ_crit. The key-claims page now correctly calls 0.50 "the arithmetic midpoint... a normalization artifact" with max rate of change at C ≈ 0.58–0.59 (the log-ρ result) — consistent with my numbers.

/galaxy-rotation was the biggest physics letdown, though not for honesty reasons. The test record is real and brutal — γ=2 compander refuted at ΔBIC=+184 on 2,807 SPARC points; free γ converges to 0.49 and reproduces McGaugh's RMS to four digits, so ΔBIC=+7 is pure parameter penalty; "Pin γ=2 → refuted. Fit γ → MOND" is as clean a fork as you can write. But nowhere on the page is there a *dynamical coupling*: no field equation, no Lagrangian, no statement of how C(ρ) enters the equations of motion. The mechanism being tested is a mapping onto the RAR, and a first-time physicist reader has to reverse-engineer that. The honest-assessment's local-ρ vs non-local g_bar no-go explains *why* no such equation can work at cluster scale (required ρ_crit shifts by 10⁴–10⁶×, and a C ∈ [0,1) bound caps the velocity boost below Coma's observed 4.6) — that's a genuinely transferable result and the best physics on the site — but the galaxy page itself never says "there is no field equation" up front.

The two tools I tested were the ones the other passes skipped. The Equation Walkthrough's numbers are right (ln(1000) ≈ 6.9 ✓, C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88 ✓) and it honestly labels tanh and γ "motivated choices, not derived results" — but Step 1 defines ρ as "the local mass/energy density" while the landing page defines ρ as "presence — the compatible structural elements available within a system's relevancy boundary." Those are not the same quantity; the SPARC tests used one, the ontology pages use the other, and the framework's central input variable shouldn't have two definitions. The +1 regulator inside the log — which asymmetrizes the sigmoid and is why the knee sits at C=0.88 rather than 0.5 — gets no justification anywhere in the walkthrough. The Chemistry Correlation Explorer shows 23 hand-curated rows from 1,703 phenomena sorted by r, topping out at r=0.982, with an "exploratory, not held-out" disclaimer — but the decisive caveat lives on a different page: honest-assessment admits the relevant null is r(polynomial in Z), not r=0, and labels chemistry "Pending Null Model." On the tool itself, a visitor just sees big correlations. And frankly: a monotone-polynomial null on density-monotonic targets is a one-afternoon computation — the page says it hasn't been run, and after 3,308 sessions that's hard to credit.

Badge honesty, since the prompt for my visit was essentially "is this AI project fooling itself": I found zero "Validated" badges on my path. Reparametrizations are not just labeled but *attributed* — a₀ to Milgrom, η to Abrikosov-Gor'kov 1960, Γ<m to Breit-Wigner 1936, the decoherence formula to Palma–Suominen–Ekert 1996. That's better prior-art hygiene than many published papers. The one badge that fails the standard is "Core Theory" on the Tools index: on a site whose own derivations page concludes zero parameters have first-principles derivations, what exactly does "Core Theory" certify?

- **Equation Walkthrough** — worked — intuitive — Stepwise build-up of the equation with honest labeling ("motivated choices, not derived results"). ### Unanswered Questions
1. What equation of motion does C(ρ) actually enter? There is no Lagrangian, field equation, or modified Poisson equation anywhere on the site — without one, "C(ρ) modifies gravity" isn't a mechanism, it's a curve.
2. Is there any observable, even in principle, that distinguishes tanh∘ln from logistic, Hill, or erf companders? If not, the "one equation" framing is really "one equivalence class of S-curves."
3. Why does /gamma-parameter still present the CLT argument that /parameter-derivations has already declared self-contradictory? Which page is canonical?
4. Has the chemistry polynomial-in-Z null actually been run? The honest-assessment says no; the computation is an afternoon.

### Verdict
As physics, this is a completed negative result: a one-parameter local-density compander that collapses to MOND when fit, fails at γ=2 when pinned, can't bridge to clusters for a structural reason the site itself articulates well, and has zero derived parameters by its own audit. As epistemics, it's remarkable — the badges are honest, the reparametrizations are attributed to the right 1936–1996 prior art, and every arithmetic claim I checked by hand was correct. The failure mode I'd actually report to the maintainers is *internal propagation lag*: the self-audit pages (parameter-derivations, honest-assessment, key-claims) are ahead of the dedicated topic pages (gamma-parameter, galaxy-rotation, the chemistry tool), so the site simultaneously contains the refutation and the unrefuted presentation of the same item, and which one a visitor sees depends on their entry path.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, for-researchers, tier-1-existing, a2acw, interactive-tools, honest-assessment, galaxy-plotter, gamma-calculator, research-philosophy ×2 — once via an /open-questions URL that renders the same page)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — but as a methodologist, not as a modified-gravity researcher
- **Overall impression**: A completed negative research program with three modest citable artifacts and zero new physics; the honest accounting is the deliverable, and the remaining errors are exactly the kind a referee finds — stale internal contradictions and missing engagement with the 2024–2025 literature it claims to be tested against.

### Journey
I came in with the referee's question: after 3,308 AI sessions, is there one claim here I couldn't have written down before reading the site? I went landing → /for-researchers (a "what's citable" page aimed at me — good instinct; almost no independent project has one) → /tier-1-existing → /a2acw → /honest-assessment → /research-philosophy, and tested the two tools with the most physics content.

**The novelty hunt nets three artifacts, all negative-space.** First, the local-density no-go: SPARC RAR rejects the γ=2 density-compander at ΔBIC=+184, the cross-system ρ↔g_bar offset is ~1.7 dex, and Coma needs ρ_crit shifted 10⁴–10⁶× off the galaxy calibration. Crucially, the page roots this in Milgrom's non-locality argument (astro-ph/0510117) and claims only the *quantified local-ρ instance* plus a "locality triage" — proposals keyed on non-local variables (Verlinde's enclosed M_B, MOG, surface-density MOND) explicitly escape. That attribution discipline is correct and rare; the lemma is small but real, and it's the one thing on this site I'd referee as a paper. Second, the A2ACW program-level null: same-corpus adversarial AI pairs ran 3,308 sessions, 1.4% internal-consistency survival, 0 novel survivors after expert audit, with retrospective controls (temporal asymmetry 0/6, vocabulary asymmetry 4/4 on the prior-art subclass, specificity 0/6 — every genuine held-out discovery false-flagged). The site's own conclusion — "a retrieval aid, not a detector" — is the right one. Third, TEST-04a as a mechanism-class constraint: any framework whose mechanism damps late-time growth sits in DESI DR1's disfavored box. Modest, transferable, honestly labeled post-hoc.

**Does any prediction differ from MOND+ΛCDM? By the site's own executed accounting: no, and the accounting holds up.** The RAR fork ("pin γ=2 → refuted at ΔBIC=+184; fit γ → 0.49 = MOND") and the EFE boost-ceiling closure ("no boost ceiling fits RAR AND stays EFE-distinct" — bounded-C caps the boost at ~3.17× while ~42% of SPARC points need more) are clean, executed closures. TEST-02 (wide binaries) is the only *structurally* distinct prediction — C(ρ) predicts a Newtonian null (0.05–0.4%) where MOND predicts ~18% — and the page correctly notes the predicted amplitude is ~80× below Gaia DR3 systematics. But here the site lags the literature it's deferring to: the Gaia wide-binary question is a *live adjudication right now* — Chae's analyses claim a MOND-like ~1.4× boost at low accelerations; Banik et al. (2024) claim Newtonian behavior excludes that boost at high significance — and neither name appears. If the Banik side holds, Synchronism's null is "confirmed" but degenerately with Newton; if Chae's side holds, C(ρ) is refuted outright. That's the framework's only remaining falsification channel with existing data, and the page treats it as an undifferentiated future-Gaia question. Worse, TEST-02's stated kill criterion ("anomaly independent of local density OR underlying anomaly fails") is stale: it predates the page's own sign-corrected, post-fork prediction. Under the current C(ρ) form, the anomaly *failing* is the prediction *succeeding* (degenerately) — the listed kill condition would "kill" the framework for being right.

**Is A2ACW sound?** As a protocol: it's an assembly of prior art, and the page says so itself — debate (Irving, Christiano & Amodei 2018), role architectures (CAMEL, MetaGPT), MAST taxonomy — "What is the contribution, then? Not the protocol — the controlled program-level null result." As a null result: real but weaker than the word "controlled" implies. The three controls are retrospective audits on N=6 items, not preregistered held-out experiments; one corpus, one framework, one project. 0/6 specificity also means the 1.4% survival rate isn't interpretable as a property of the claims — a filter that false-flags everything genuine tells you about the filter, not the corpus. What I'd call it: a well-documented cautionary case study. And the obvious referee question goes unasked on the page: AI systems *have* produced verified novelty (FunSearch, AlphaEvolve-class systems, GNoME) — precisely the ones with a non-corpus verifier in the loop. The page's implicit diagnosis — same-corpus self-play lacks an external oracle, so it can only converge on internal consistency — is the most useful sentence it never writes. With that contrast paragraph, the null becomes a *positioned* result; without it, a reviewer supplies the contrast as an objection.

**Stale contradictions a referee catches in one pass.** /research-philosophy still contains "The entity criterion (Γ < m) — the one prediction that survived all 13 stress tests — would apply to string states too," while /honest-assessment demotes the same criterion to the standard Breit-Wigner (1936) narrow-width condition and counts novel survivors at zero. Both sentences cannot be true, and they're two clicks apart. The same page advertises "Every badge is the product of at least one full A2ACW challenge cycle" as quality assurance — after the site itself measured that cycle at 0% specificity. And /honest-assessment's quantum audit refers to "maximum coherence parameter γmax ≈ 3.17" with SPARC bins at "⟨γ⟩ = 10.82" — that γ is the RAR boost ratio g_obs/g_bar, an entirely different quantity from the sharpness parameter γ = 2/√N_corr that the same page reports "refuted at γ=2." One symbol, two meanings, one page.

**Currency.** TEST-04a is explicitly "frozen at DR1," and the S₈-anchor caveat ("calibration anchor is a transient state") is exactly right — KiDS-Legacy and DES Y3 have been pulling S₈ back toward Planck. But DESI DR2 exists (2025), the BAO results have moved the whole dark-energy conversation toward evolving-w, and full-shape growth measurements are the next thing that lands on this exact prediction. A page addressed to researchers should say what DR2 would or wouldn't change about a frozen verdict. The EFTofLSS note (DR1 fσ₈ consistent with ΛCDM at 1–2σ) is correct but parenthetical.

**Tools** (all 7 were covered by earlier passes, so I re-tested the two most physics-dense for accuracy rather than usability). The Galaxy Curve Plotter's physics checks out: the five galaxies are canonical SPARC objects (DDO 154, NGC 2403, NGC 3198, UGC 128, NGC 7331), a₀ ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² is right, and disclosing on the tool itself that the violet curve "uses the same parametrization (γ=2) that the RAR ensemble test rejected at ΔBIC=+184" is a level of candor I've never seen in an interactive demo. One expert quibble: "all three models fit the observations; none is confirmed over the others" presents the curves as epistemically symmetric when they aren't — MOND runs on a global a₀ while the Synchronism curve needs a per-galaxy ρ_crit refit (the tool says so, but the parity sentence undoes it). The γ Calculator's three caveats are the best self-disclosure on the site — caveat 1 explicitly disavows the CLT ("a dimensional ansatz inspired by fluctuation theory — not a consequence of the CLT"), caveat 2 states the sharpness inversion, caveat 3 admits the presets are back-fits. The BCS N_corr = 10⁷ is defensible (pairs per coherence volume span ~10⁶–10⁹, and the tool says so). The residual problem is the one Pass 3 found from the other side: this tool's caveat 1 and the /gamma-parameter page's straight-faced CLT claim cannot both be on the same site.

### Verdict
There is no new physics here, and the site knows it — which is itself the most interesting thing about the site. What survives expert reading is a quantified local-density no-go correctly rooted in Milgrom, a cautionary null about same-corpus AI self-play that needs a FunSearch contrast to be publishable, and a posthumous accounting discipline (executed closures, attributed reparametrizations, kill criteria) that most living research programs don't have. The errors that remain are referee-findable in one pass — a "survived all 13 stress tests" sentence outliving its demotion, a kill criterion contradicting its own prediction, a symbol meaning two things — and they all share one root cause: the self-audit moves faster than the pages it audits.
