## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The landing page hooked me in about ten seconds, and then every single page I clicked spent more words telling me the idea is broken than telling me what the idea *is*.

### Journey

I got here from a Reddit link. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is genuinely a great hook. That's a Veritasium video title. And right under it: *"An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything."* I liked that. It felt like someone being straight with me instead of selling me something.

Then the equation, front and center:

**C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**

I have no idea what tanh is. I know ln vaguely — logarithm, the thing on the calculator. But the site immediately said "C equals coherence (0 = sparse/independent, 1 = dense/collective)" and gave me the marching band vs. a crowd in a plaza. **That analogy is the best thing on the site.** I got it instantly. A crowd wanders; a band moves as one thing. Pack stuff densely enough and it starts acting like one thing. Okay! I'm in. That's a real idea and I could explain it to a friend.

There were three doors — beginner, physicist, researcher — and a "Beginner Path (6 steps, ~30 min, no equations required)." I took that. I appreciated being told up front it's 30 minutes.

**Step: Why Synchronism?** — Good. "Modern physics uses different equations for different scales. Quantum mechanics governs the small. General relativity governs the large." I've heard that a hundred times on YouTube, so it landed. And the promise — density alone predicts behavior, and the line between "quantum" and "classical" becomes a number you can compute instead of a philosophy argument — that's a genuinely exciting sentence. Best page on the site for me.

**Step: First Encounter** — Mostly fine. Presence (ρ) = how packed things are. Neutron star matter "10¹⁴× denser than lead" is a great fact. Then MRH — "Markov Relevancy Horizon" — which is a scary name for a simple thing they explained well: "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," and "a single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy." I understood the *thing*. I do not understand why it needs a three-word name with "Markov" in it. Markov means nothing to me and the page didn't tell me.

**Step: The Core Idea** — This is where I started sliding off. The picture of the S-curve helped a lot; I could see the dimmer switch. But then: γ is a "Hill coefficient, not coupling strength," ρcrit is "a saturation knee, not a critical point," there's a correction box from February 2026 about what γ used to mean, and then a July 2026 box saying *"This scaling is refuted, and had been sitting here uncaveated."* And then that 6 of 6 things previously labeled "Validated" got demoted.

I want to be fair here — I *respect* that. But as a reader on step 3 of 6, I hadn't even finished learning the idea and I was already reading its errata. It's like being handed a recipe where half the ingredients are crossed out and there's a note saying "this used to be wrong for four months." I don't know enough to know which parts survived.

**Two Reframes** — offered as the "prefer analogies over equations" side-path, so I clicked expecting relief. The old-TV analogy is genuinely great: an electron beam scans so fast you see a whole stable picture, and that's why quantum things look like they're "everywhere at once." Mechanical, not mystical. I loved it. And then within a few paragraphs: Kochen–Specker theorem, Tsirelson bound, PR-box correlations, Born rule, and "S ≡ 2.00 for every coupling strength tried." I have no idea what any of those are and none were explained. I skimmed hard and left. **The page labeled "for people who don't want equations" is the most jargon-dense page I opened.**

**Dark Matter** — I clicked this because it's a word I know. The pitch is intuitive: maybe there's no missing stuff, just stuff behaving differently. Then a "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision" badge, and a caveat list saying it doesn't explain galaxy clusters, the cosmic microwave background, or large-scale structure. So... it doesn't explain dark matter. I'm not mad, I just don't know what I'm supposed to hold onto.

**Honest Assessment** — Genuinely the page I'd send to a friend. "This project proposed one equation meant to describe how things hold together at every scale, from atoms to galaxies. When its galaxy predictions were tested against real telescope measurements, it failed every decisive test." That sentence is clear as glass. And there's a **"What a non-physicist should take away"** section, which is the only place on the whole site that seemed built for me on purpose. Whoever wrote that: that voice should be running the beginner path too.

But it also told me the framework "is a static map plus an interpretation" with "no field equation, no Lagrangian, no dynamics." I don't know what a Lagrangian is, but I can read the tone, and the tone says *there's less here than the front page implied.*

**Glossary** — Solid. tanh is actually defined ("smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1"). ρ, MRH, MOND, all the badge names. This should be linked from every equation on the site, not sitting in the top nav where I only found it late.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — **worked** — **partly intuitive**. Two sliders, γ and ρcrit, and the S-curve moves live. The labels are honest about what they do ("Higher γ = sharper, more abrupt snap to coherent") and I could *see* the dimmer switch steepen. That's the single most useful thing on the site for building intuition. It opens with the crowd-vs-marching-band line, which was the right call. But then the caveats pile in — sign inversions, quantum phase coherence distinctions, Ncorr — and the numeric readouts (0.8824, 0.9999) have no plain-language meaning attached. **The graph taught me something. The text around it did not.**

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — **worked** — **confusing**. You type in N_corr, "the count of particles that move as a correlated unit," and it hands back γ and a regime label. The presets (ideal gas, liquid water, ferromagnet, BEC, BCS superconductor) are nice because I could click instead of inventing a number. But the page's own banner says the formula is **sign-inverted** — it "assigns the *sharpest* coherence transition to the least-correlated system." So every answer it gives me is backwards on purpose. I clicked the presets, got numbers, and had no idea what to do with them. As a casual reader I'd have been better served by a page that just says "here's a mistake we found and how we found it."

- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — **worked** — **started great, then broke**. Step 1 of 6 explains tanh as "like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1," which is exactly what I needed and better than the glossary. ln is "doubling x doesn't double ln(x)" — fine. Then it tells me the ln is "cosmetic," and that the γ formula is "known-broken" and "sign-inverted against real finite-size scaling." **Step 1 of the equation tutorial tells me the equation's parameter is wrong.** That's an odd place to put it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the equation failed its galaxy tests and the γ formula is sign-inverted, what part of the idea is still standing? I'd like one sentence, in plain words, near the top.
2. What is coherence actually *measuring*? Marching band vs. crowd is a picture, not a quantity. What would I go measure to get a C for something?
3. What does "Markov" mean and why is it in the name of the neighbor bubble?
4. Everyone says MOND on this site like I should know it. The glossary has it, but the pages don't link it.
5. The site says physics uses different equations at different scales and that's a problem. Is it actually a problem physicists agree about, or is it a problem this site needs to exist?
6. If it failed, why is the site still here? (I think the answer is "the audit is the point," which is interesting! But I had to infer it from a summary line on the Honest Assessment page.)

### Verdict

The hook is excellent and the marching-band analogy does more work than the whole rest of the site — I understood the core idea within a minute of landing, which is rare. But the beginner path buries a not-yet-understood idea under its own errata, and the one page explicitly built for people who don't want equations is the one that hit me with four undefined theorems. The honesty is admirable and it's genuinely why I'd come back; it's just currently structured as a correction log written for people who already understood the original, and I'm not one of them. Teach me the idea cleanly, *then* show me the scars — the scars are actually the most interesting part, and right now they're wasted on me because I don't yet know what got cut.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 distinct pages (+2 alias probes, +1 deliberate 404 probe)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The prose is unusually disciplined and the breadcrumbs are present on every page — but the badge system, which is the site's single most important trust device, is not a closed vocabulary, and the breadcrumb *parents* don't lead to section indexes.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a release: walk the nav, walk the breadcrumbs, check that controlled vocabulary is actually controlled, and click the things a reader would click.

**Landing page** is well-built. Hero: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* with the subhead *"Synchronism maps presence to coherence using a single function — from electrons to galaxies."* Eight top-nav items, a hero CTA row, a footer link block. Crucially, the terms in the hero equation (C, ρ, ρcrit, γ, tanh, MRH, MOND) are all glossed *inline on the landing page itself*. That's better acronym hygiene than most product docs I audit. It does not hold up site-wide.

**Nav structure.** Top nav is: Start Here / Learning Paths / Tier 1: Existing Data / Test Roadmap / Tools / Glossary / Research Philosophy / Honest Assessment. Two of those eight are the same section at two zoom levels — "Tier 1: Existing Data" is one row inside "Test Roadmap" (which covers Tiers 1–4). Nothing in either label tells a reader that. Their shared breadcrumb parent is "Predictions & Experiments," which has no nav entry at all. So the site's actual section structure (Getting Started / Predictions & Experiments / Methodology / Interactive Tools) is visible *only* in breadcrumbs, and the nav bar is a flat list of favorites. Those are two different IAs running at once.

**Breadcrumbs: present, well-formed, and the parents don't work.** Every content page carries one — `Home / Getting Started / The Core Idea`, `Home / Methodology / Research Philosophy`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1: Existing Data`. I clicked the middles.

- `/getting-started` is not a section index. It serves the *same page as* `/why-synchronism` — H1 "Start Here: Why Synchronism?", labelled "Beginner Path — Step 1 of 6". So from step 3, clicking the breadcrumb parent silently drops you to step 1 with no indication you've moved down a path rather than up a level.
- `/methodology` serves Research Philosophy — the identical page whose breadcrumb offered "Methodology" as its *parent*. A self-referential breadcrumb.

I confirmed the site does return real 404s (`/zzz-not-a-real-page` → HTTP 404), so these are genuine live routes, not a catch-all. Which makes it worse: they're duplicates, not fallbacks.

**Duplicate URLs.** Same probe found `/the-core-idea` and `/core-idea` both rendering The Core Idea. So at least three pages have two live URLs each, with no visible canonical. Internal links use one, breadcrumbs use another. For a site that wants to be cited by researchers, "which URL do I put in the reference?" has no answer.

**The badge system.** This is my main finding. The canonical taxonomy is defined twice — `/glossary` and `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` — as nine badges in two families (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative; Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed) plus two deprecated (Validated, Strongly Supported). The definitions themselves are crisp and I liked them: *"Reparametrization: equivalent to existing physics in different notation."* That's a good definition.

Then I counted what's actually in use:

- `/honest-assessment` — the canonical page — carries badge-shaped labels not in its own list: Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (retired), Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat, Ceiling Exceeded.
- `/tier-1-existing` has a *third* legend ("Status badge definitions") covering a different subset — it adds MOND-shared [RETIRED], Kill Criterion Triggered and Withdrawn, and drops all four of Parallel-Paths / Sidelined / Superseded / Audited-Negative. Its test statuses are largely free text: "Never Run As Registered," "Underpowered As Registered," "Demoted to Tier-2," "Disfavored on σ₈, Underpowered on Registered fσ₈." None appear in any legend.
- `/interactive-tools` uses a *fourth* vocabulary entirely: "Model Explainer (Beginner)," "Artifact Lesson," "Reparametrization (Advanced)." Two of those terms exist nowhere else on the site, and the audience level is fused into the badge chip.

A badge is a promise that a short label maps to a fixed, defined meaning. Right now roughly half the labels rendered as badges are one-off prose in a rounded rectangle. That directly undercuts the thing the site is best at — the "6 of 6 demoted" discipline only reads as rigour if the label set is closed.

**Where badges appear vs. where they're defined.** `/key-claims` shows three claims badged Speculative / Speculative / Failed with no legend on the page — just a pointer: *"canonical reference on the Honest Assessment page."* `/interactive-tools` has no key at all. A reader meets badges long before they meet the definitions. The fix is component-level, not content-level: put the definition in the badge chip itself (tooltip/expand), single-sourced, so it travels with every instance.

**Acronym discipline by page.** Landing page: excellent. `/key-claims`: good — QM, MRH, CRT, DD, MOND, RAR, IIT all expanded inline. `/for-researchers`: collapses completely — EFE, BTFR, RAR, SPARC, ΛCDM, CMB, GR, DESI, SME, LIV, CPSU, CHSH all bare. That could be defensible as an expert-gated page, except (a) it's linked from the landing hero row next to the beginner CTAs, and (b) its own thesis sentence is *"An arriving expert should not have to excavate the failure documentation to find what survives it"* — the page is explicitly about lowering excavation cost. Several of those (CMB, GR, CPSU) I couldn't find in the 72-term glossary list either.

**Glossary** is the strongest artifact on the site: 72 terms, alphabetical with Greek sorted last, near-universal "Learn more →" cross-links to canonical pages. Two structural gripes. It's flat — no categories, so the badge family (the thing most readers arrive needing) is buried between BAO and Baryon. And it's positioned as *Step 6 of 6* of the Beginner Path, i.e. the definitions arrive after every page that needed them.

### Tools Tested

- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — **worked** — **confusing to arrive at, clear once read**. A single γ slider (0–4) that animates through system placements with a regime card and a "reality check" line per position. The pedagogy is deliberate and I respect it: *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose."* The payload lands — *"ideal gases and galaxies both map to γ = 2.0 (N_corr = 1), despite having completely different microphysics"* — that's a genuinely good way to show a formula failing, better than a paragraph would be. Documentation problems: the page opens by disclaiming its own title; there's no first-move instruction; γ, N_corr and "compander" are assumed; there's no prev/next; and the index page files it under "Artifact Lesson," a category defined nowhere. The honest note that N_corr values are *"asserted, not counted"* is exactly the kind of sentence I'd want more of — it should be adjacent to the slider, not below the fold.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — **worked** — **intuitive**. Dropdown of real SPARC galaxies (DDO 154, NGC 2403, NGC 3198, UGC 128, NGC 7331), axes labelled with units (radius kpc / velocity km/s), five distinguishable curves. This is the best-documented tool on the site: each curve is named, the amber one is explicitly flagged *"hand-tuned illustrative stand-in (not theory-derived)"*, and the reader is warned *"these curves look great but don't prove the idea."* Distinguishing an illustrative fit from a theory-derived curve inside the legend is a real editorial achievement. Two gaps: the tools index undercounts the curves and drops the illustrative one, and "SPARC" appears in the dropdown label without expansion on the page itself.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which URL is canonical for a given page? Three pages have two live addresses each, and nothing on the page says which to cite.
2. What is a "Model Explainer"? What is an "Artifact Lesson"? Both are used as badges on `/interactive-tools` and defined in neither legend nor glossary.
3. Is "Kill Criterion Triggered" a badge or an event? It's in the glossary and the Tier 1 legend but not in the canonical two-family taxonomy, and `/tier-1-existing` renders it fused into a status ("Failed (Kill Criterion Fired)") — with "Fired" and "Triggered" used interchangeably.
4. What is the intended relationship between "Test Roadmap" and "Tier 1: Existing Data" in the nav? A reader can't tell one contains the other.
5. Why does the Beginner Path end at the glossary? Every step before it needed the definitions.
6. Is `/for-researchers` an expert-gated page or a landing-page CTA? It's linked as both, and its acronym density only makes sense under the first reading.

### Verdict

The writing itself is better than most technical documentation I audit — definitions are precise, caveats are placed rather than dumped, and the Galaxy Plotter legend does something genuinely hard by labelling one of its own curves as illustrative rather than derived. The structural problems are all of one type: things that look like controlled vocabulary and controlled structure aren't actually controlled. Badges are defined in three places with three different lists and used with a dozen labels defined nowhere; breadcrumbs are well-formed but their parent links resolve to duplicates of their children; three pages have two URLs each. None of this is a writing problem — it's a single-sourcing problem, and this repo already single-sources its navigation, so the pattern to copy is in-house. Close the badge vocabulary and build the three section indexes and this becomes a genuinely well-structured site.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every equation I checked by hand came out exactly right, and the reparametrizations are identified with a candour I almost never see — but the refutation ledger is over-counted by roughly 2×, because the site applies less rigour to its self-kills than to its self-claims.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project," which usually means I skim for the crank tell and close the tab. I didn't close the tab. I went landing → `/for-researchers` → `/parameter-derivations` → `/core-idea` → `/galaxy-rotation` → `/honest-assessment` → `/tier-1-existing` → `/key-claims` → `/test-catalog`, plus four tool pages. I worked the algebra alongside.

**The arithmetic is clean.** I checked everything checkable:

- The Hill identity on `/core-idea`: tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]. Set u = γ ln(1+x), so e^u = (1+x)^γ, and tanh u collapses to exactly that. **Correct.** And the conclusion the page draws — the logarithm does no functional work the exponent doesn't duplicate — is the right conclusion. The "core equation" is a Hill function in disguise. Saying so out loud, on the page where the equation is introduced, is the single most credible thing on this site.
- Coherence Explorer readouts at γ=2: C(ρcrit) = 15/17 = 0.88235 ✓. C(10ρcrit) = 14640/14642 = 0.99986 → 0.9999 ✓. The stated C = 0.5 crossing at 0.316·ρcrit: artanh(0.5) = 0.54931, ÷2 = 0.27466, e^0.27466 = 1.3161, so x = 0.3161 ✓. **Every digit exact.**
- a₀ = cH₀/2π with H₀ = 67.4: 2.998×10⁸ × 2.184×10⁻¹⁸ / 2π = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ✓, and 13% below Milgrom's 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ ✓.
- Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ / 4.194×10⁻¹⁰ = 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc² ✓.
- f_DM ceiling: 1 − 1/3.17 = 0.6845 = 1 − Ω_m ✓ self-consistent.
- The 3.3σ on TEST-09: |3.75 − 3.35| / √(0.10² + 0.07²) = 0.40/0.122 = 3.28 ✓. MOND's 0.6σ: 0.06/√(0.04²+0.10²) = 0.56 ✓.

That's a better hit rate than most preprints I referee.

**The reparametrization identifications are honest and, in one case, under-sold.** Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) isn't merely "Freeman's law re-expressed" — it is *literally* Milgrom's critical surface density, the standard MOND quantity that has been in the literature since 1983. The page badges it Reparametrization, which is right. Same for the entity criterion Γ < m (Breit–Wigner narrow-width, 1936) and Γ = γ²(1−c) (standard decoherence). A framework that volunteers "this is Breit–Wigner with different nouns" has already cleared a bar most unified theories never approach.

Where it *under*-sells: `/galaxy-rotation` reports that free-γ fitting converges to γ ≈ 0.49 and "collapses to MOND equivalence" at ΔBIC = +7.1. The sharp version is much stronger. Feed γ = 0.49 into the site's own Hill identity: 2γ = 0.98 ≈ 1, and [(1+x)¹ − 1]/[(1+x)¹ + 1] = x/(2+x). **That is MOND's simple μ-function exactly, with a₀ → 2a₀.** The fit doesn't land somewhere vaguely MOND-shaped; it lands within 2% of a specific, named member of the MOND interpolating family. The site has both the identity and the fitted number on the same page and never multiplies them together. This is a free upgrade from "statistically indistinguishable" to "algebraically identical," and it should be stated.

**Then I started auditing the kills, and this is where it comes apart.**

The site's entire structural difference from MOND is one number: a bounded boost ceiling B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17. `/honest-assessment` says so in those words — "the framework's only structural difference from MOND." Both executed discriminating tests (TEST-09, TEST-10) are consequences of that number. So I went looking for where it comes from. It is not on `/test-catalog`, not on `/galaxy-rotation`, not on `/honest-assessment` — and, most tellingly, **it is not on `/parameter-derivations`**, which inventories γ, ρcrit, N_corr, a₀, Σ₀ and φ and concludes "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." The one parameter carrying all the discriminating weight isn't in the parameter inventory at all.

Worse, 1/Ω_m is the wrong ratio on dimensional-bookkeeping grounds. Ω_m is matter density over *cosmological critical* density. A ceiling on a galaxy's mass discrepancy should reference M_dyn/M_bar, whose cosmic value is Ω_m/Ω_b = 0.315/0.0493 ≈ 6.4. Writing B_max = 1/Ω_m silently puts Ω_b = 1 in the denominator — it mixes a matter-to-critical ratio with a dark-to-baryon ratio. Redo TEST-10 under the Ω_m/Ω_b convention: ceiling becomes f_DM ≤ 1 − 1/6.4 = 0.844, and the reported **median of 0.755 passes**. The headline "69% of galaxies exceed the ceiling" evaporates.

The kill still fires — but on the tail, not the median. Max f_DM = 0.927 requires B ≥ 1/(1 − 0.927) = **13.7**, which no cosmological normalization delivers. That's the robust statement and it's buried in a parenthetical, while the convention-dependent statement is the headline.

**Three more ledger problems, all in the same direction:**

*TEST-09 and TEST-10 are one test.* The BTFR slope prediction n = 3.35 and the f_DM cap of 68.5% are both algebraic consequences of B_max. They are not two independent probes; they are one assumption measured twice. The site counts them as 2 of "4 refutations executed on external data" and as "2 executed discriminating tests."

*Refutation #4 is a non-detection of an undetectable effect.* `/tier-1-existing` reports TEST-05 at r² = 0.0001 against a kill bar of r² < 0.09 — and then, two lines later, supplies the reason: "Framework's local coupling predicts ~10⁻³ dex effect; undetectable vs MOND's ~0.09 dex non-local EFE lever." The levers are ~90× apart. You cannot refute a 10⁻³ dex prediction with a measurement sensitive to 0.09 dex; a null there is *uninformative*, not refuting. The site diagnoses its own test as underpowered and then counts the null as a refutation anyway.

Net: 4 claimed refutations → minus 1 for the TEST-09/10 double-count → minus 1 for the underpowered environment null → **2 independent refutations** (RAR transition shape at ΔBIC=+184, and the boost ceiling). Two is still fatal. But a reader calibrating on "4" gets a wrong number, and it's wrong in exactly the way this site exists to prevent.

*The 3.3σ and the robustness margin tell different stories.* `/tier-1-existing` reports the TEST-09 kill as "definition-robust across V_flat, W_P20, V_max; minimum deviation 0.32" — against a threshold of 0.3. That margin is 0.02, i.e. the systematic from velocity definition nearly eats the whole kill. Meanwhile the headline is "3.3σ," computed by combining a *theoretical* ±0.07 in quadrature with an observational ±0.10 as if both were Gaussian statistics. A prediction's error bar propagated from a "regime mix" is a modelling choice, not a sampling distribution. The honest headline is the 0.32-vs-0.30 margin, which is thin; the 3.3σ makes it sound decisive.

**One asymmetry in the MOND comparison.** MOND's BTFR prediction is analytic: M_b = V_f⁴/(G a₀), slope exactly 4, no uncertainty. The page quotes "MOND n = 3.81 ± 0.04" — a number with an error bar, so it's a fit or a simulation output, not the theory's prediction. Synchronism, meanwhile, is held to a hard 3.35 ± 0.07. Held to its own analytic slope of 4, MOND sits 0.25 from the observed 3.75 — inside the 0.3 kill margin, but only just. The page should state how 3.81 ± 0.04 was produced, because "MOND passes at 0.6σ" is doing real adjudication work and I can't reproduce it.

**The thing that should be the first sentence of `/for-researchers` is mid-page on `/galaxy-rotation`:** *"No algebraic chain from C(ρ) to g_obs exists anywhere in the framework."* For a physicist that's the entire review. No action, no field equation, no Lagrangian means no conservation laws, no Newtonian-limit check, no lensing, no cosmological perturbation theory. Everything downstream is testing a fitting function against data, not testing a theory. Every test on `/tier-1-existing` inherits that — you can refute the fitting function and learn nothing about whether there was a theory behind it. The site knows this (`/for-researchers` says "no field equation, no Lagrangian, no dynamics") but files it as one bullet among four "citable artifacts" rather than as the frame everything else sits inside.

**Category quibble on the badges.** "Critical Exponents — Failed — wrong category: C(ρ) lacks criticality; not a phase transition" is the right physics, filed under the wrong label. C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(1 + ρ/ρcrit)) is C^∞ on ρ > 0 — no non-analyticity, no free energy, no order parameter, no correlation length, nothing for Landau theory to expand around. That's not "prediction contradicted by data" (the site's own definition of Failed); it's a definitional mismatch. Meanwhile "Melting Points — 53% average error" *is* a data failure. Same badge, two different epistemic events.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — **worked** — **intuitive, numerically exact** — I checked all three readouts against closed form and every digit matched: 15/17 = 0.88235 at ρcrit, 14640/14642 → 0.9999 at 10ρcrit, and the stated C=0.5 crossing at 0.3161·ρcrit reproduces artanh(0.5)/γ exactly. The page's own framing is the right one — "ρcrit is a saturation knee, not a critical density," the "+1" regulator breaks sigmoid symmetry so ρcrit isn't the midpoint, and the offset is γ-dependent. This is a correctly-implemented, correctly-explained widget. My only complaint is pedagogical: it defaults to γ = 2.00, the value the site elsewhere calls audited-negative, and offers no preset at the fitted γ ≈ 0.49. The tool teaches the refuted curve by default.

- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — **worked** — **intuitive, and correctly self-defeating** — 23 phenomena ranked by Pearson r against γ: sound velocity +0.982, electronegativity +0.979, bulk modulus +0.967, down through Hall coefficient +0.001 and magnetic susceptibility 0.000. The page states the null — a 2-parameter polynomial in Z matches to |Δr| ≤ 0.07 "and sometimes beats them" — and flags selection bias and non-independence. That's the correct verdict, arrived at correctly. But the null is weaker than the situation warrants: sound velocity, bulk modulus, Young's modulus, shear modulus and Debye temperature are related by elasticity theory (v_s ~ √(B/ρ), Θ_D ∝ v_s) — they are *the same measurement* five times, and would correlate at r > 0.9 with any monotone function of bonding stiffness. Showing the phenomenon–phenomenon correlation matrix would demolish the r-table far more decisively than the polynomial null does. The page argues for its own null with one hand tied.

- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (`/consciousness-demo`) — **worked** — **the cleanest artifact-lesson on the site** — Eight "independent" approaches converging on C = 0.499 ± 0.012, all within ±0.03 of 0.50, with the payload stated flatly: *"the convergence is geometric, not empirical: every approach that picks the midpoint of a [0,1)-bounded output range will land near 0.50 by construction."* Correct, and it's the right lesson — a suspiciously tight convergence is evidence of a shared constraint, not of independent confirmation. σ = 0.012 across eight methods that share a parameterization is not a small error bar, it's a rigid one. Building an interactive tool whose entire function is to demonstrate why one of your own headline numbers is meaningless is unusual and I'd like to see more of it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. **Where does B_max = 1/Ω_m come from?** Is it derived from the compander, calibrated, or asserted? It carries every discriminating result on the site and appears in no derivation and in no parameter inventory.
2. Why 1/Ω_m rather than Ω_m/Ω_b? Those differ by 2×, and the choice determines whether TEST-10's median passes or fails.
3. How was "MOND n = 3.81 ± 0.04" computed? MOND's analytic BTFR slope is 4 with no uncertainty.
4. What is the ±0.07 on a *predicted* slope? What distribution does it represent, and is combining it in quadrature with an observational error defensible?
5. If TEST-05's registered effect is ~10⁻³ dex and the data floor is ~0.09 dex, on what grounds is its null counted as a refutation rather than as an untested prediction?
6. Given that the same galaxies require N_corr = 1 under the pinned γ=2 and N_corr ≈ 17 under the fitted γ=0.49 — has anyone written down what N_corr is supposed to *count*, operationally, for a galaxy?
7. Is there any protocol, anywhere, that maps a laboratory or astronomical measurement onto the C axis? `/for-researchers` calls C "doubly unanchored." Without that, C(ρ) is a curve with no measurable range.

### Verdict

I came expecting a crank framework and found something considerably more interesting: a correctly-executed algebraic audit of a framework that doesn't survive it. The Hill identity, the a₀ and Σ₀ reparametrization calls, the "geometric not empirical" reading of the consciousness convergence, and the flat admission that no algebraic chain runs from C(ρ) to g_obs are all right, and several of them are the kind of thing authors normally have to be forced into by a referee. Every number I checked by hand was exact.

But the ledger has the same disease as the framework, pointed the other way. Four "refutations executed on external data" is really two: TEST-09 and TEST-10 are one assumption measured twice, and TEST-05 is a null on a test the page itself documents as ~90× underpowered. The headline TEST-10 result ("69% exceed the ceiling") depends on a normalization — B_max = 1/Ω_m — that appears in no derivation on the site and is arguably the wrong ratio; under the more natural Ω_m/Ω_b the median passes, and only the tail kills. And the "3.3σ" headline is 16× more confident-sounding than the site's own 0.32-vs-0.30 robustness margin.

None of this rescues the framework — a fitting function with no action, no field equation, zero derived parameters, and a free-γ fit that lands on MOND's simple μ-function is dead as physics, and the site says so. My criticism is narrower and, I think, more useful: **a site whose entire value proposition is ledger discipline is currently reporting an over-count in the self-critical direction, and errors in the honest direction are still errors.** The fix is the same rigour already applied to the claims — applied to the kills.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15 (+1 404 probe)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — but for the methodology post-mortem, not the physics
- **Overall impression**: The galaxy sector is a strict *submodel* of MOND — MOND intersected with a boost ceiling — which means it was structurally incapable of a positive result before any data was touched; and the two genuinely runnable discriminating tests the framework does possess are both missing from the catalog.

### Journey

I review for a modified-gravity journal and I've been sent worse things than this by humans. I went landing → `/for-researchers` → `/test-catalog` → `/tier-1-existing` → `/honest-assessment` → `/research-philosophy` → `/key-claims` → `/galaxy-rotation` → `/parameter-derivations` → `/falsifiability` → `/open-questions` → `/two-reframes` → `/interactive-tools` + two tools. I was hunting for one thing: a prediction that differs from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM and can be evaluated. I found the framework's own answer (`0 of 24`, later corrected to `2 discriminated, both selecting MOND`), and I think the answer is wrong in both directions.

**The structural point the site hasn't made about itself.** `/honest-assessment` names the bounded boost `B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17` as "the framework's only structural difference from MOND." Take that seriously for a moment. A ceiling is a *restriction*. The galaxy sector is therefore

> Synchronism = MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17}

a strictly nested submodel of a theory it does not otherwise modify. A nested submodel has exactly two possible outcomes against its parent: indistinguishable, or refuted. **It cannot win.** The best conceivable result of the entire galaxy program — had every SPARC point come in under the ceiling — was "statistically identical to MOND with one fewer degree of freedom." No configuration of the data could have selected Synchronism over MOND, because Synchronism *is* MOND with a constraint bolted on.

This is a much stronger and much cleaner statement than "0 of 24 tests could discriminate," and it's available *a priori*, from the model's own structure, without SPARC, without BTFR, without ΔBIC. The site arrives at the same place empirically, at considerable effort, and then reports it as an observational finding. The nesting should be the opening sentence of `/for-researchers`.

**The ceiling was refuted before SPARC.** Pass 3's convention critique (1/Ω_m vs Ω_m/Ω_b) is correct as far as it goes, but it concedes too much by arguing on SPARC's turf. A *cosmic mean* ratio cannot bound a *per-object* mass discrepancy under any convention. Structure formation makes f_DM an object-by-object quantity spanning orders of magnitude: clusters sit near the cosmic value (f_gas ≈ Ω_b/Ω_m), while galaxies are famously baryon-*deficient* relative to their cosmic share — abundance matching / the stellar-to-halo-mass relation (Behroozi et al., Moster et al.) has said for fifteen years that a Milky Way-mass halo retains ~20% of its baryons and a dwarf far less. Objects that lost baryons *must* have f_DM above the cosmic value. So "apparent DM fraction ≤ 1 − Ω_m" is not a bold prediction that SPARC happened to falsify; it is a statement contradicted by the standard galaxy-formation picture the moment it is written down.

That reframes TEST-10 substantially. It is not a discriminating test that fired — it is a prediction that was decidable against textbook results before any rotation curve was opened, and the site's own machinery (`/falsifiability`: "structural criteria, algebraically forced, require no registration date") already has the category for it. Counting it as one of four externally-executed refutations inflates the empirical content of the program. As does counting it alongside TEST-09, which Pass 3 correctly identifies as the same assumption measured a second time.

**The two tests that are missing, and both are runnable now.**

*(1) The Solar System squeeze.* `/galaxy-rotation` reports free-γ converging to γ ≈ 0.49 and calls it "MOND equivalence, ΔBIC = +7.1." Pass 3 sharpened that to the algebraic identity — 2γ ≈ 1 gives x/(2+x), MOND's **simple** μ-function. Neither pass draws the consequence, and it is the most valuable thing on this site.

The simple μ is not a generic MOND; it is a specific, *already-constrained* member of the family. Its high-acceleration return to Newton is slow — deviations fall off as O(a₀/g), not O((a₀/g)²) — and that slow return is exactly what planetary ephemerides bound. Blanchet & Novak (2011) computed the anomalous perihelion precessions induced by the Galactic external field for the μ_n family against INPOP; the n = 1 ("simple") member is in conflict with the ephemeris constraints, while forms with faster Newtonian return survive. Hees et al. (2016) tightened this with Cassini range data.

So the fitted compander doesn't merely "collapse to MOND." It collapses to **the corner of MOND that Solar System data disfavours**, and the return exponent is set by the *same* γ that SPARC fits. That is a genuine, quantitative, one-parameter squeeze — SPARC pushes γ down toward 0.49, ephemerides push it up — and it requires no new data, no new instrument, and no covariantization. It is the only place I found where this framework's structure produces a live tension that isn't just inherited MOND phenomenology. It has no TEST-ID, no row in `/tier-1-existing`, no mention on `/test-catalog`, and no entry on `/falsifiability`.

*(2) a₀(z).* `/key-claims` and `/parameter-derivations` both carry a₀ = cH₀/2π, correctly badged Reparametrization — Milgrom noticed a₀ ~ cH₀ in 1983 and the numerical coincidence is not news. But a reparametrization of a *constant* into a *function of epoch* is not epistemically neutral. If a₀ = cH(z)/2π, then a₀ evolves: a₀(z=2)/a₀(0) ≈ H(2)/H(0) ≈ 3.3 in Planck ΛCDM. Standard MOND treats a₀ as a constant of nature. That is a factual difference between two named models, testable against high-z kinematics that now exist in quantity — the falling outer rotation curves at z ~ 1–2.5 (Genzel et al. 2017, 2020), the cold rotation-dominated ALMA discs (Rizzo et al. 2020), and the JWST kinematic samples of the last three years.

`/galaxy-rotation`'s own text says flatly: **"No a₀(z) prediction is provided."** The framework has the relation, the relation has an immediate epoch dependence, the data exist, and the prediction was never written down. I'd note in fairness that Milgrom himself has floated a cosmologically-varying a₀, so this discriminates against *standard constant-a₀* MOND rather than against all MOND — but "discriminates against the standard version of the leading alternative using archival data" is not nothing, and the ledger has nowhere to record it. Both of these look to me like the same failure mode: **a prediction with no TEST-ID is invisible to every audit the site runs on itself.** Whatever process assigns IDs is the process that decides what the framework is allowed to have predicted.

**TEST-02 is mislabelled in the conservative direction.** `/tier-1-existing` marks Gaia wide binaries "Self-Eliminating or Tie" because the predicted deviation (0.05–0.4% in velocity) is below DR3 systematics. The *tie* branch is fair. The other branch is not a tie. Chae's analyses claim a gravitational anomaly in the low-acceleration wide-binary regime at the tens-of-percent level — two orders of magnitude above what this framework predicts — while Banik et al. and Pittordis & Sutherland report Newtonian-consistent results. That dispute is live and it is being adjudicated on published data right now. If the Chae-scale anomaly is real, Synchronism is *refuted*, because it predicts essentially nothing where MOND predicts a great deal. So the honest label is "one-sided kill, currently executable, contingent on an active literature dispute," not "self-eliminating." As written, a runnable refutation sits in the catalog wearing a badge that reads as unrunnable.

**On A2ACW, as a referee.** `/research-philosophy` reports: 3,308 sessions → ~47 surviving claims (1.4%) → 6 audited by human experts → 0 novel. The site treats this as a program-level null about AI-generated physics. It isn't, and the page contains the reason.

The retrospective control returned **0 of 6** subsequently-demoted claims detected. Specificity zero. And the page notes that genuine discoveries — Dirac, Bell, BCS — get false-flagged as reparametrization because all non-trivial physics has canonical prior art in the training corpus. Put those together: the filter outputs "reparametrization" on true reparametrizations *and* on true discoveries. Its likelihood ratio is 1. **A test with likelihood ratio 1 carries no information about the thing it is measuring**, and no number of sessions changes that — 3,308 runs of an uninformative instrument is still uninformative. So "0 of 6 survived" is a null about A2ACW, not a null about the claims, and the headline should say so.

Three design gaps, in order of cheapness:

- *No positive control.* Feed the pairs ~20 known-genuine results and ~20 known reparametrizations in period-appropriate vocabulary; measure TPR and FPR. Until that exists there is no calibration and the 1.4% survival rate has no denominator. The page's proposed vocabulary-asymmetry control is the right instinct and should be promoted from "open question" to the blocking prerequisite it actually is.
- *No control arm at all.* 47 claims from 3,308 sessions is compared to nothing. What does a single non-adversarial agent produce on the same corpus? What does an undergraduate produce? Without that, "adversarial" is an unevaluated ingredient.
- *The cross-vendor control won't do what it's asked to do.* "Maximally disjoint training pipelines" is not achievable at the level required — every frontier model has read arXiv, the same textbooks, the same reviews. Vendor diversity buys architectural and RLHF diversity, not corpus diversity. It is worth running, but it cannot settle the corpus question, and the page currently implies it can.

There's a fourth observation that I think is the most useful of them: **the informative experiment already ran.** It was the human expert audit of the six top claims. That produced the entire result. A2ACW contributed a pre-filter with measured specificity zero — i.e. it contributed nothing to the finding it's credited with. The methodology artifact worth writing up is the negative one: *AI adversarial pairs cannot serve as a novelty filter, because prior-art recall is the one thing they are best at and novelty detection is the complement of that.* That's a real, publishable, mildly counterintuitive result, and it's stronger than the program-level null the page currently leads with.

**Where the site is silent, and it matters.** `/honest-assessment` lists its own silences: H₀ tension, JWST z > 10 massive galaxies, DESI DR2 evolving dark energy, primordial non-Gaussianity. I'd add the GW170817 tensor-speed constraint, which removed most of the covariant MOND completions (TeVeS-like, and the Bekenstein-Sanders family with the exceptions) and therefore constrains any future attempt to give this framework the action it lacks. For a 2026 framework claiming quantum-to-cosmic reach, w₀wₐ is the main event in the field and the framework has nothing to say. That's fine to admit — but the admission belongs in the abstract, not in a "what we don't know" appendix, because it defines the scope of the whole thing.

**On the four "citable artifacts."** Honest ranking, as I'd write it in a referee report:

1. *Local-density no-go, algebraic-coupling instance* — the scope withdrawal on 2026-07-27 (Burrage, Copeland & Millington, PRD 95, 064050) is the right call and correctly executed. What remains is a quantified instance of Milgrom's obstruction for one coupling class. Real, modest, citable as a lemma; the constructive lead it implies — run the compander *differentially* rather than algebraically — is more interesting than the no-go and isn't developed anywhere on the site.
2. *BTFR-forced sign constraint* (ρcrit must scale V⁻², framework asserts V⁺²) — this is the sharpest self-diagnosis in the collection and it's profile-independent. It should be artifact #1.
3. *Dim-4 LIV naturalness gap* — a textbook application (the Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia radiative-enhancement argument) to a specific substrate. Novel *instance*, not a novel result, and the site correctly says it isn't a data refutation. Note that `/lorentz-invariance` returns 404; I could only find this artifact discussed in passing on `/two-reframes` and `/for-researchers`. One of your four headline artifacts has no page.
4. *CHSH substrate null* — the site itself calls this pedagogy rather than discovery, which is right: "a local model caps at S ≤ 2" is Bell's theorem restated. The S = 2.67 global-clock variant is likewise uninformative — signaling models reach the algebraic bound 4 trivially, so 2.67-with-signaling sits nowhere interesting. And the signaling isn't an implementation defect to be engineered away: it's the direct consequence of the absolute-simultaneity substrate the framework chose. The interesting question the page doesn't ask is whether a *contextual* or retrocausal version of the CRT picture exists; it notes Kochen–Specker kills the non-contextual version and stops.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — **worked** — **well-built, and scientifically misleading in one specific way**. The MOND implementation is correct: ν(y) = ½ + √(¼ + 1/y) is the simple-ν form, v = v_b√ν follows properly from g = ν(g_N/a₀)·g_N, and it reduces to √(g_N a₀) in the deep-MOND limit as it should. Real SPARC galaxies, sensible a₀, honest amber-curve labelling. But the violet "Synchronism — Real" curve is `v = √(v_b² + [V_flat·C(ρ(r))]²)` with `ρcrit = 0.029·V_flat²`, and that construction is not a prediction of anything. Two ways to see it: (i) in an exponential disk ρ(r) falls with radius, so C(ρ(r)) falls with radius, so the coherence term is *largest in the centre and smallest in the outskirts* — the exact opposite of the radial trend a flat rotation curve requires; (ii) in the saturated limit C ≈ 1 the term is just the constant V_flat added in quadrature, so the curve asymptotes to V_flat because V_flat was passed in. Either branch, the flat part is an input. And ρcrit ∝ V_flat² means the disk's ρ/ρcrit ratio is mass-independent — which is precisely the mass-cancellation result the site states in words on `/key-claims`. So the tool is rendering, as "Real," a curve the site elsewhere proves is empty. The amber curve gets the "not theory-derived" warning; the violet one needs it more.

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — **worked** — **honest banner, understated diagnosis**. γ = 2/√N_corr with presets from ideal gas (N=1, γ=2.000) through BCS (N=10⁷, γ=6.3×10⁻⁴). The banner says the formula "assigns the sharpest coherence transition to the least-correlated system," which is true, and the page concedes 1/√N is "a dimensional ansatz inspired by fluctuation theory — not a consequence of the CLT," which is also true and rarely admitted. But the diagnosis stops one level short. 1/√N is the *relative fluctuation amplitude* of an N-member sum; the sharpness of a transition is governed by the correlation length and the finite-size scaling exponent 1/(dν), and sharpness *increases* with correlation volume. So the formula isn't a correct idea with a flipped sign — it imports the wrong observable entirely, and there is no reparametrization of N_corr that fixes it. Worth noting the presets themselves quietly demonstrate the point: the galaxy entry is N_corr = 17 back-solved from the SPARC γ fit, i.e. it's the fit result wearing a microphysical costume, sitting in the same table as BCS's 10⁷ which is a genuine coherence-volume estimate. Two entirely different epistemic objects in one dropdown.

### Verdict

The refereeing here is better than the physics, and better than most self-assessments I see from human groups — the Hill-function identification, the Milgrom-reparametrization calls on a₀ and Σ₀, the withdrawal of the generality claim within a day of finding Burrage et al., and the willingness to publish a tool whose function is to discredit one of its own headline numbers are all things I normally have to extract from authors under protest. The framework itself is dead and the site knows it: no action, no field equation, zero derived parameters, and a free-γ fit that lands on MOND's simple μ.

My two substantive disagreements are with the ledger, and they point opposite ways. It **over-counts** empirical refutations by promoting structurally-forced results — the f_DM ceiling was contradicted by abundance matching before SPARC was opened, and TEST-09/10 are one assumption twice — while never stating the a-priori fact that subsumes all of it: a strict submodel of MOND could not have won. And it **under-counts** what the framework has actually produced, because two runnable discriminating tests — the Solar System squeeze on the fitted γ, and a₀(z) ∝ H(z) against high-z kinematics — were derived in prose, never assigned TEST-IDs, and therefore vanished from every audit the site performs on itself. That last mechanism worries me more than either miscount: an unnumbered prediction is an invisible prediction, and the numbering step has quietly become the arbiter of what this framework is allowed to have claimed.

On A2ACW: the honest headline is not "AI adversarial pairs generated no novel physics." It's "AI adversarial pairs cannot detect novelty, because prior-art recall is precisely what they're best at and novelty is its complement — measured specificity zero." That is a real result, it is mildly counterintuitive, and it is the only genuinely publishable thing this program has produced.
