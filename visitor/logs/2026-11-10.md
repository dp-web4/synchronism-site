## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Coherence Explorer, What Synchronism Is Not, Honest Assessment, Key Claims, Two Reframes)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've ever clicked on from Reddit, and that honesty is also the problem — by the third page I know in detail that the idea failed, but I still couldn't explain to a friend what the idea *was*.

### Journey

Landed on the big question: "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" Great hook. Then the very first bold line: "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." Honestly my first reaction was "...so why am I here?" The "So is it alive or dead?" bit right after helped — dead as physics, alive as a public record — but I had to scroll to find it.

The marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza picture worked for me immediately. A number from 0 (strangers) to 1 (marching band). Got it. Then the equation showed up — C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) — and the site did something nice: "read it out loud like this: coherence equals a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are." I don't know what tanh is and I still don't, but "squashing function" is enough for me. That's the best single sentence on the site for someone like me.

Then the landing page kept going. And going. Abbreviation soup — MOND, ΔBIC, BTFR, RAR, SPARC, Ωm, ΛCDM, Neff — a big "Scientific Outcomes" table, and "Revision notes" boxes everywhere saying things like "corrected 2026-10-29." I skimmed all of that. It felt like scrolling through the edit history of a Wikipedia article instead of the article. The footer line "0 confirmed predictions · 6 refutations executed" I did read, and I understood: they tried six times to break it and it broke six times.

Clicked "Start here: Why Synchronism?" The beginner part is short and good. "Physics has a fragmentation problem," one dimmer-switch curve for everything, three lines on what happened, "you can stop here." I did not stop; I peeked under the "for physicists" fold and bounced right back out (Debye model, r = 0.982, Abrikosov–Gor'kov). Also, the page says the galaxy test "collapses to MOND" like that's a bad thing, and MOND is explained in one parenthesis as gravity being slightly stronger where it's weak. I didn't understand why matching an existing theory counts as a failure — isn't matching good? That question never got answered on any page I read.

First Encounter was the page I liked most. Seven short sections with Continue buttons, ~10 minutes as advertised. The Andromeda line (an atom doesn't "feel" Andromeda) and the whisper-in-a-loud-room thing made the "bubble of neighbors that matter" idea click. The plain definitions of "predicted" (decided before looking) vs "fitted" (turned a knob until it matched) were genuinely useful — I'll remember those. Section C lost me: N_corr, a correlation that flipped sign, γ fitted to 0.5 which is "MOND's own form." I pressed Continue without understanding it.

The next step sent me to the Galaxy Curve Plotter. Picked a galaxy from a dropdown (DDO 154 was the default). "New here? Look at three things on the chart" — dots, gray dashed line, green line — was exactly the hand-holding I wanted. But the checkboxes are labeled things like "Show the second, untested wiring of the same equation (light violet dashed; it lies on the gray line, so it changes nothing here)." Wiring? If it changes nothing why is the checkbox there? I ticked it, nothing changed, as promised. Also the page only told me what a "rotation curve" actually is (speed vs. distance from the center) well below the chart, after I'd already been asked to read the chart.

Coherence Explorer: here the "What is coherence?" crowd/band paragraph is placed *before* the sliders, which is the right order. The "one thing to try first" (drag γ from 0.49 up to 2 and watch the S flatten into a ramp) is a nice concrete instruction. But the slider itself is labeled "Initial slope / saturation rate (γ; proposed as 'sharpness')" with a readout "10–90% width 1.93 dex." Dex? And the preset buttons say things like "γ = 0.046 — density version fit to real galaxies (SPARC...)" — I don't know what SPARC is. Under the sliders it says the default setting "is dead on two fronts" and mentions "Cassini ... 8.7σ." I know Cassini is the Saturn probe. No idea what it has to do with this.

Went to What Synchronism Is Not because the landing page promised "plain language, no equations, no hype." The top box delivers: not a theory of everything, not quantum coherence, not peer-reviewed, not proven, galaxy formula is "MOND in disguise." Then nine "Not:" cards below that absolutely do have equations (Γ = γ²(1−c)) and words like Lorentz-invariant and comagnetometer. The promise held for the top third.

Honest Assessment: 25,000 words. I read the "If you read nothing else" box and the badge table (nine badges: Failed, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, and five others with names like Active-MRH and Sidelined that I skipped). Noticed the page says in one spot "only row #2 refutes the framework's own content," in another that three rows do, and elsewhere that the count is "4–8" depending on rules. I'm not sure the site agrees with itself about how many times it failed, and the footer picks "6."

Key Claims: three. Quantum mechanics as synchronization (Speculative), consciousness has an equation (Speculative, and the page itself says the symbols in the equation are undefined — so why is it on the Key Claims page?), and dark matter as "incomplete decoherence" (Failed). So of the three headline claims, one failed and two are admittedly untestable. That's a weird thing to title "Key Claims."

Finished on Two Reframes because the CRT TV analogy sounded fun. It was — the electron beam scanning the screen so fast you see a whole picture, and "put a pendulum clock in a centrifuge" for relativity. Then the page says the CRT picture was tested and failed too, and the back half is Tsirelson bounds and Kochen–Specker. Bounced.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — mostly intuitive — The "look at three things" box is the right idea. Dropdown of five galaxies, real data dots with error bars. The story (gray line sags, green line follows the dots, violet line is "ours" and doesn't) came through. Checkbox labels were the confusing part; "it changes nothing here" is an odd thing to let me click.
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — half intuitive — Dragging γ and watching the S-curve flatten is satisfying and the "try this first" instruction is good. The slider labels, "dex," and the preset buttons full of SPARC/ΔBIC/Cassini are not for me. I never figured out what the ρcrit slider was for beyond sliding the curve sideways.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the idea failed, what does the site want me to take away — a cautionary tale, a method, or an idea that's still worth thinking about? Each page gives a slightly different answer.
2. Why does "it turned out to be MOND in different notation" count as a failure rather than a confirmation of MOND? Nobody told me MOND was the rival.
3. What is the "density" of a single electron? The site says ρ is density, then "presence," and First Encounter says presence is counted inside a "bubble." Density of what?
4. The landing page mentions "Gaia DR4 wide binaries, 2 December 2026." Is something going to happen on that date that could change the verdict? That's the one thing that would get me to come back and it's buried.
5. How many times did it fail — 6, 4, 8, or "only one that really counts"?

### Verdict
I came for "what if one equation described everything" and got a very well-organized confession that it doesn't. I respect that enormously and I'm not sure I've ever seen a science site do it. But the honesty is front-loaded so hard that the idea itself — crowd vs. marching band, one dimmer-switch number, a bubble of what matters to you — only reaches me in fragments between apologies. The beginner-marked bits (Why Synchronism top half, First Encounter, the "read it out loud" sentence, the plotter's "look at three things" box) are excellent and I'd point a friend at those. Everything else assumes I know what MOND, SPARC, and dex are. I'd return on December 2 if someone reminded me why.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Learning Paths, Glossary, Honest Assessment, Research Philosophy, Interactive Tools, γ Calculator, Equation Anatomy, Key Claims, Test Roadmap, For Researchers, MRH)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; partial of the content)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is better sourced than most documentation I audit — one canonical badge legend, every cross-page anchor I checked resolves, consistent test-ID format — but the terminology layer leaks: the one input variable has three names, the one parameter has six, and "Step N of M" means three different things on three pages.

### Journey

I started where the nav told me to and did what I do on every audit: read headings and first sentences, follow every link in the header, and keep a list of every term used before it is defined.

**Header nav** is nine flat items with helpful tooltips ("123 terms defined", "26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)"). The tooltips are the best labelling on the site and nobody on a phone will see them. Nav label and page H1 match everywhere except "Start Here", whose page H1 is "Start Here: Why Synchronism?" while the browser tab says "Why Synchronism?". Minor, but it is the first page.

**Breadcrumbs** exist on most pages but are not one system. Learning Paths renders "Home / / Learning Paths" with an empty middle crumb; Key Claims renders "Home / / Key Claims" the same way; MRH renders "Home / Core Theory / MRH" where "Core Theory" is a section that appears in no nav, no learning path, and no index. Interactive-tool pages use "Home / Interactive Tools / Tool", which is the right pattern.

**Step labels** are the part that would get sent back in review. "Step 2 of 5" on First Encounter is the Beginner Path. "Physics Track · Step 2 of 7" on MRH is a different path. "Step 1 of 8 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)" on Equation Anatomy is a third thing, and the parenthetical exists precisely because the author knew it collided. Test Roadmap puts "Step 8 of 8 / Final step" at the top of the page. Same visual component, three meanings. The "Start Here" tooltip adds a fourth: "step 1 of the Beginner Path", lowercase.

**Learning Paths** is clean: seven paths, every step linked, per-step times on the Beginner path that sum correctly to the header's 37 minutes. But the Honest Assessment is described three ways on one page — "nearly 2 hours" (step 5), "the full 80-minute read" (Beginner intro), "about 25,000 words" — and the landing page's revision note says "~18,000-word page". There is also a typo, "Separately,1 of the 7 paths".

**Glossary** is the strongest page for my purposes. Alphabetical, Greek after Latin as stated, a "New here? Start with these ten" block at the top, and a "How to read labels on this site" section with a "States used on test cards" sub-table. I fetched the HTML and confirmed every anchor the landing page points at exists: `#start-with-ten`, `#tanh`, `#Prospective`, `#Retrodiction`, `#ΔBIC`, `#BTFR`, `#RAR`, `#SPARC`, `#CHSH`, and the three badge anchors Key Claims uses (`#failed-badge`, `#speculative-badge`, `#reparametrization-badge`). Both capitalised and lowercase ids are present, so case cannot break a link. That is unusually careful. Problems are inside the entries: the intro lists Failed as a descriptive badge but the "Empty Intersection" entry calls it one of "four operational labels"; the lifecycle table lists Ceiling Exceeded, Null-Class and Artifact Lesson in one category while each entry's own parenthetical puts it in another; the intro names two deprecated badges and the table names three (adds MOND-shared). The environment test is TEST-03 in two entries and TEST-05 in a third. The "Prospective" entry ends "The site has completed 0." which reads like a sentence that lost its noun. LCT and DR4 have no entries; "Gaia DR4, 2 December 2026" is the one forward-looking date on the landing page and the glossary cannot tell me what DR4 is.

**Validation badges**: the single-sourcing is real. Research Philosophy lists the nine names and says "see canonical legend"; Test Roadmap repeats the lists and links `/glossary#test-card-states`; Key Claims gives one-line definitions inline and links the glossary anchors; Honest Assessment holds the canonical nine-row table at `#validation-badge-definitions`, which exists. Spelling is consistent (Active-MRH, Audited-Negative, Parallel-Paths) except lowercase "audited-negative" in running prose on the landing page, the tools index and the Artifact Lesson entry. The gap is the one badge a beginner meets first on a tool: the landing page says γ is "audited-negative — see γ Calculator", and the γ Calculator shows the badge "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems" with an ⓘ icon but never defines "audited" in its body text. Equation Anatomy does the same. Both tool pages assume the reader has been to the Honest Assessment legend first; the Beginner path sends them to tools at step 3 and the legend at step 5.

**Terminology drift on the two symbols that matter.** ρ is "density" on Why Synchronism (the word "presence" never appears there), "presence (ρ) … at its simplest, presence is density" on First Encounter, "compatible structural elements that drive coherence" on MRH, and on Equation Anatomy it is "The universal input" in the variables table while Step 1 on the same screen calls the density-fed version "the refuted version". The glossary's start-with-ten lists "Presence (ρ)". A reader who starts at step 1 learns a word that step 2 renames. γ is worse: "how early the coherence S-curve starts to rise" (γ Calculator, in one sentence, good), "sets the low-density slope and how fast C approaches 1, not a sharpness" (Equation Anatomy), "Initial slope / saturation rate (γ; proposed as 'sharpness')" (Coherence Explorer, per Pass 1), and the γ Calculator's own regime table still labels rows "γ-sharp" and "sharp transition" directly below the sentence saying it is not a sharpness. Six names, one of them explicitly retracted on the same page that still uses it. ρcrit is spelled ρcrit, ρ_crit and ρ\_crit across pages, and the log argument is ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1) in the equation but ln(1 + ρ/ρcrit) in the glossary Crossover entry.

**Acronyms.** The landing page expands MOND, MRH and A2ACW at first use and has an "Abbreviations used in the table below" key — placed below the first use of most of those abbreviations, and after an "Abbreviations ahead" warning that lists them without expanding them. Elsewhere, MOND is used unexpanded on Learning Paths ("Relation to MOND"), Interactive Tools ("an existing theory"), γ Calculator, Equation Anatomy, and in First Encounter Section C before its parenthetical expansion later in the same section. SPARC is never spelled out anywhere, only glossed as a catalogue. A2ACW is expanded on the landing page and Research Philosophy but not on Learning Paths, Key Claims, or in the body of For Researchers. BCS, BEC, EEG, fMRI, IIT and PCI are used on tool cards without expansion. "CLT self-contradiction" appears as a label before the body spells out Central Limit Theorem. "dex" is used on the landing page, γ Calculator and Equation Anatomy; its glossary entry ("1 dex = a factor of 10") is one line and excellent, and no page links to it at first use.

**Honest Assessment** has a 22-item jump list. Three of its labels do not match the headings they jump to ("Badge definitions (canonical)" vs "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)"; "A2ACW Self-Audit" vs "Self-Audit on the Self-Audit Protocol (A2ACW)"; "Modified-Gravity Landscape" vs "Where This Sits in the Modified-Gravity Landscape"), and "Inherited MOND Problems" is a bold run-in paragraph, not a heading. The sitewide footer link "ledger" points at `#what-was-tested`, whose heading is "What Was Tested", while the ledger table lives at `#refutation-ledger` under "Which six refutations?". I counted fifteen sentences stating a refutation count. The total is reconciled in one place (6; 4–8 under rules on file) and consistent in the footer, Learning Paths, Glossary and For Researchers. The qualifier is not: "Only row #2 refutes the framework's own content on outside data" (opening box), "3 rows refute content the framework itself asserts, on data" (By kind), and "Of the four, only TEST-10 is an external-data refutation of content the framework itself asserts" (counting rules) each use a different criterion and none names it. Pass 1 noticed the symptom; the structural cause is that "refutes the framework's own content" has no definition anywhere.

**For Researchers** says "the five numbered artifacts below" and then numbers 1–6. **Key Claims** H2s render "1.Is Quantum Mechanics…" and "3.Is Dark Matter…" with no space after the number, and its framing paragraph says "Status labels (Untested, Speculative, etc.)" on a page where no claim is Untested. **Interactive Tools** skips from H1 to H3 and calls the same tool "Galaxy Curve Plotter" in the card and "Galaxy Plotter" in the recommended reading order. **MRH** needs a "Not to be confused" paragraph because the badge family is named "MRH-relationship" after a physics term the badges say nothing about; that collision is a naming decision, not a typo.

The landing-page "details" link Pass 1 flagged as going to the wrong page: the sentence it sits in is about the framework's own cosmology needing cold dark matter put in by hand, so `/dark-energy` may be the intended destination. The defect is the link text, which gives no hint that it leaves the dark-matter topic.

2. Where is "Core Theory"? The MRH breadcrumb says I am inside it; no nav, path or index page has it.

### Verdict
Structurally this is a well-run documentation set: one canonical badge legend that every other page defers to, anchors that all resolve (I checked eighteen from the landing page and all three badge anchors from Key Claims), consistent zero-padded test IDs, a glossary with both-case ids so links cannot break on capitalisation. The failures are at the vocabulary layer, and they cluster on exactly the two symbols a reader must hold onto — ρ and γ — plus one overloaded UI label. Fix the ρ/γ naming and the step-label collision and the beginner path would read as one document instead of five authors.

- **Pages visited**: 16 (landing, Why Synchronism?, Parameter Derivations, Coherence Function, Galaxy Rotation Curves, Relation to MOND, Dark Matter Reframed, Dark Energy & DESI, MRH, Wide Binaries, Tier 1: Existing Data, Honest Assessment, Interactive Tools, Crossover Regime Visualizer, Chemistry Correlation Explorer, MOND-Synchronism Comparator)

### Journey

A colleague sent me this as "an interesting AI project," so I skipped the beginner track and went straight for whatever page claims to derive something. The landing page links "See derivations" → Parameter Derivations, which opens by saying zero of its parameters have an independent first-principles derivation. Good: that saved me an hour. The page then audits each one — γ = 2/√N_corr is a "motivated ansatz" with the √N borrowed from the CLT (which does not apply to correlated degrees of freedom, as the page itself notes), the tanh is a "phenomenological choice" from the compander family, a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is dimensional analysis with a 2π that a literal Hubble-sphere computation does not produce (it gives cH₀/2), and Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) is Milgrom's Σ_M read backwards. It even gives a base rate: a grammar of 43 simple prefactors on cH₀ hits 89% of targets in [0.1, 0.3]cH₀ to ±10%. That is the kind of numerology check I would normally have to do myself.

I checked the algebra on the Coherence Function and Galaxy Rotation pages. tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1] is exact; dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x) is right and shows the curve is concave for x ≥ 0 with no inflection, so "S-curve" is only true on a log axis. At γ = ½ it is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) identically, so the "converges to MOND" result is an identity with a₀ = 2a₀′, as the site says. More than that: for any γ the small-x limit is C ≈ γx and the large-x limit is C → 1, so every member of the family is an admissible MOND μ with deep-MOND scale a₀′/γ. The γ = 2 "kill" is therefore a choice among interpolating functions, and the site's own Fisher correlation ρ(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000 on the globular-cluster test confirms that deep-regime data see only that one combination. The site states all of this. What it does not say in one place is the consequence: a framework whose only identifiable galaxy parameter is a₀′/γ has exactly MOND's one parameter.

The dark-energy page is the best-written physics on the site and I spent the most time there. Substituting G/C(ρ̄) into Friedmann and *defining* ρ_DE ≡ ρ_m(1−C)/C makes C = ρ_m/(ρ_m+ρ_DE) = Ω_m(a) by construction, so "C₀ = Ω_m" is an identity, not a calibration, and the page says so. I verified the asymptotics: w → −2γ in the far past and −1 in the future, ρ_DE → ρ_crit/γ at low density, and at γ = ½ the thing is exactly Λ (ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit, constant). I re-derived the sound speed from the single-fluid Cardassian definition c_s² = x h″/h′ with h = x/C and got 0.223(2γ−1) against the page's 0.22(2γ−1). The page's caveat that this depends on the sound-speed convention is also right: the adiabatic dP/dρ of the DE component alone gives −0.35 at γ → ½, a different number with a different sign, which is why the (k/aH)² pin "binds the local reading as stated, not every completion." Ġ/G = 3H₀ dlnC/dlnρ = 3(1−Ω_m)H₀ = 1.4×10⁻¹⁰/yr checks, and LLR at (7.1 ± 7.6)×10⁻¹⁴/yr is 2000× below it. So the modified-G reading is dead and the fluid reading is ΛCDM. The page's own verdict, "no novel dark-energy prediction," is the correct one.

Then I went looking for the dynamics and did not find one. The site offers three "wirings": g_obs = g_bar/C (algebraic; a prescription, not an equation of motion), ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ (which the site correctly identifies as Refracted Gravity's field equation, Matsakos & Diaferio 2016), and an "L3 action-derived wiring" with effective μ = C + ½xC′. The Galaxy Rotation page calls L3 "action-derived" and, two sections later under GW170817, says the relation between C(ρ) and the gravitational action "has never been written down." Both cannot be true. For a condensed-matter reader this is the whole problem in one sentence: an order parameter needs an equation of motion, and Ising's m = tanh(βJzm) has one (the self-consistency loop) while C(ρ) does not. The site makes exactly this comparison on the Coherence Function page and concludes "this tanh is chosen." I agree.

The density-keyed law's published calibration is worse than the site's scattered sentences convey. ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat² puts the knee at 1.5×10³ M☉/pc³ for the Milky Way and 64 M☉/pc³ for DDO 154, against disc midplane densities of 0.03–0.1 M☉/pc³. Every galaxy is then on the linear branch with C ≈ γx ~ 10⁻⁵, and the "boost" 1/C at the Sun is 3.5×10⁴ (I get 34,600 at γ = 0.489; 8,400 even at γ = 2). The Oort limit kills that at the first digit, and the wide-binary page says so. But then the question is how the published law was ever presented as fitting SPARC — and the Coherence Function page answers it: the plotter's displayed model is v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)². V_flat is an *input*. A law whose knee is ρ_crit ∝ V_flat² and whose amplitude is V_flat does not predict flat rotation curves; it is handed them. This is said on the site, but across three pages (Coherence Function, Parameter Derivations, Galaxy Plotter) and never as one sentence.

The Solar-System floor γ ≳ 0.87 from planetary precessions is correct physics — the compander has a power-law tail 1−C ≈ 2x^{−2γ}, which at Saturn (x ≈ 5×10⁵, γ = 0.489) gives a 5.3×10⁻⁶ fractional anomaly, I get the same — but it is the compander's encoding of a known result: MOND's simple μ, with its 1/x tail, has been excluded by planetary ephemerides for 15 years absent the EFE (Blanchet & Novak 2011; Hees et al. 2016; Famaey & McGaugh 2012 §6.4). The MOND page cites EPM2011 and reports the kill as the site's own finding at "γ ≈ 0.87, not 1.5"; it should say this is the simple-μ ephemeris constraint in new variables.

On the refutation count: the Tier 1 page says plainly that TEST-09's kill "does not survive its own sweep" — it fires at B_max = 1/Ω_m (|Δn| = 0.41) and not at 5.39 (0.297, missing by 0.003) or 6.39 (0.26), and "no archive document derives" 1/Ω_m. As a referee I would not call that a refutation; I would call it the class exclusion B_max ≲ 5.3, which the page also states and which is the useful result. The γ = 2 pin at ΔBIC ≈ +11 by galaxy (1.6–2.3σ) is likewise "disfavoured, not decisive" in the site's own words. The honest count is the floor of four, and the headline "6" leans on the two rows the counting-rules section itself labels "the ones that move."

Wide binaries: the DR4 reading key is a committed mapping from reported boost to compander γ, and the page is upfront that no DR4 outcome can raise the novelty count because every γ it maps to is already Cassini-excluded unless disc M/L exceeds twice stellar. That is the correct framing. Two cautions: the key is given as a repository path, not a link, and the page leans on a one-author, abstract-only preprint (Boufourou 2026, γ = 1.045, "16σ") for a strong exclusion — I would not cite a 16σ claim I could not read, and I would check for an erratum before December.

### Journey

I came for the novel claims and skipped everything I already know. The landing page says in its second sentence that no prediction differing from MOND or ΛCDM has survived, and that no scheduled test — Gaia DR4, DESI DR2/DR3 — can change that because every branch is shared or already excluded. That is a correct description of the modified-gravity landscape for an acceleration-keyed interpolating function with one scale, and it saved me the usual hour of hunting. So I went looking for the places where the framework *could* have differed, to check whether the site has shut each door honestly.

**Door 1: the external field effect.** The one structural statement a local-density law makes that MOND does not is EFE = 0. The Relation to MOND page now says what I would have said: EFE = 0 is the Strong Equivalence Principle, which ΛCDM also obeys for internal galaxy dynamics, so the channel is refutation-only and "no EFE outcome can ever select this framework over ΛCDM." Correct. The page records that until 2026-10-21 it called this "the one genuinely distinct external-field prediction," which is the kind of overclaim I would have flagged; it is gone. The surviving EFE-free corner (γ ≈ 0.87–0.95, "alive, disfavoured, not novel") is tested on globular clusters at Δχ² = +54 ± 19 vs Newton and pointed at Gaia DR4. Nobody mentions Crater II or Dragonfly 44. Crater II is the textbook EFE test — McGaugh 2016 predicted σ ≈ 2 km/s with EFE vs ≈ 4 without, and the measured 2.7 ± 0.3 is why MOND practitioners believe the EFE is real. An EFE-free corner has a free, existing-data kill sitting in the literature and the site is waiting for December instead.

**Door 2: density-keyed G.** Refracted Gravity (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016) is correctly identified as the nearest neighbour, same L2 field equation. The For Researchers page then does the one thing on this site I would cite: it writes Ġ/G for a G_eff keyed on smoothed stellar density as the Sun moves through it, gets |d ln G/d ln ρ| ≲ 4×10⁻⁹–10⁻⁸ from lunar laser ranging (Hofmann & Müller 2018), and kills 68 of 74 grid laws including both RG calibrations on the pointwise reading. I checked the scale: v/ℓ ≈ 20 km/s per pc ≈ 2×10⁻⁵ yr⁻¹, against LLR's 7.6×10⁻¹⁴, gives 4×10⁻⁹. The page's caveat that a 100-pc gradient scale weakens it by 10² is also right, and at 0.1 M☉/pc³ against RG's 0.0083 knee the sech² derivative is still ~10⁻², six orders above the bound. Two things it does not say: at 1 pc smoothing the Sun's own mass (≈0.24 M☉/pc³ smoothed) dominates the local density and travels with the Earth–Moon system, so the fractional variation is diluted by an O(1) factor; and this is an argument against *Refracted Gravity*, a published theory, which is why it is interesting. Nearby, the Honest Assessment's landscape section says covariant RG (Sanna, Matsakos & Diaferio 2023) has PPN γ = 0, failing Cassini at ~4×10⁴σ, and Nordtvedt η = 1. A 4×10⁴σ exclusion of a peer-reviewed completion, attributed to an in-house check dated 2026-10-22, needs either a citation to a published PPN analysis or an explicit "our computation; the authors claim otherwise." Right now it reads as settled.

**Door 3: dark energy.** I know the DESI DR2 w₀wₐ table well and the page's Table 5 transcription is exact (DESI+CMB −0.42 ± 0.21 / −1.75 ± 0.58; the 2.8/3.8/4.2σ with Pantheon+/Union3/DESY5; 3.1σ BAO+CMB).
