## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /interactive-tools, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /two-reframes, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /glossary)

- **Overall impression**: It's the most honest "theory of everything" site I've seen: it tells me straight away that the idea failed. But within two scrolls of every page I hit a wall of σ, ΔBIC, MOND, and test codes, so I came away knowing *that* it failed without really knowing *why*.

### Journey

**Landing.** I came from Reddit, and the headline "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the hook I clicked for. The subheadline undercuts it straight away: "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything." A few lines later the page says the idea "did not hold as stated" and is "dead as tested physics, alive as a public record of testing." I was surprised, and honestly a little charmed. Then I scrolled and it turned into a lab notebook. There are tables of refutations with labels like TEST-09, TEST-25, and B1, "ΔBIC +2,843," "Cassini/SPARC squeeze 8.7σ," "Draco at 4.5σ… corrected from 4.9σ on 2026-11-19," and equations with tanh and ln. I skimmed all of it. The stat tiles were the one part I could take in at a glance: 3,308 sessions, 14,610 galaxies, **0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations**. The page is very long for a front page. The thing that kept me reading was the big obvious "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)" button, and that's what I clicked.

The "Gaia DR4 is due 2 December 2026" line caught my eye, because a date when something might happen is a real hook. But the next sentence says the release can rule things out and "cannot confirm anything new," and I didn't know what to make of that. Why should I wait for it?

**Why Synchronism? (Step 1 of 5).** The first three boxes are great. "Physics has a fragmentation problem: quantum rules for the very small, gravity for the very large, and no one equation for both." I get that. "What happened, in three lines" is the best thing on the site for me: one equation for how crowding makes parts act as one, tested mostly on galaxies, it lost where it could be told apart from existing physics, and the bigger question is still open. Then the page says "Beginner path: that is all you need from this page," and everything else sits in a fold labelled **"For physicists: the technical details…"**. Inside that fold is "The Problem," which is plain English, plus the crowd-versus-marching-band analogy and the "dimmer switch" image. Those were the most beginner-friendly bits, and they're filed under "for physicists." I nearly skipped them. The very first box also says "fewer free parameters," and I don't know what a free parameter is. The glossary didn't have it either (see below).

The failures section was the easiest science to follow. Melting points were off by 53% on average. A superconductor was predicted at 607 K and the real value is 93 K, "about 6.5× too high." Concrete numbers that are clearly wrong are easy to understand. Oddly, they made me trust the site more.

**First Encounter (Step 2).** It opens by defining coherence as "how collectively a group of things behaves": marching band high, crowd in a plaza low, and "Dense matter in a crystal lattice — every atom locked in step: high coherence." Good. Then Section B has an "expert note" saying superconductors land at *low* C and that the formula gives its flattest curves to the most-correlated systems. That confused me. A crystal was the page's own example of "locked in step = high coherence," and now the formula scores locked-in-step things low? I couldn't tell whether that's the failure or a separate quirk. The neutron star ("10¹⁴× denser than lead") was a nice image. "MRH" is explained with the atom that doesn't "feel" Andromeda, which I liked.

Section C lost me with this sentence: "from a slow fade over many powers of ten (small γ) down to a floor (large γ)." I read it three times. The good part of Section C is that it says outright that the prediction was +0.55 and reality was −0.55, "opposite sign." Even I can see that's a clean miss.

The big takeaway I *think* I got: when they let the dial (γ) be set freely, it lands on "exactly MOND's formula," so it "adds nothing new." But I only know MOND as "the rival theory." Nobody tells me in one plain sentence why matching it counts as a loss. My guess is that it means "you just rediscovered someone else's answer," but I'm guessing.

**Interactive Tools hub.** Clean, with Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced labels, and the "Where to start: Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment" line is helpful. I noticed that every tool except one carries a "Failed," "Audited-Negative," or "Reparametrization" badge. Even the toys come with a verdict.

**Coherence Explorer (tool 1).** Labelled "Best first tool." The first thing on the page is a banner: "Reparametrization — At γ = ½ this is a formula physics already had (MOND's, fed acceleration; ΛCDM in cosmology)." Jargon before I've touched anything. After that it's good. "What this toy is for" says it's a shape toy that "does not model any real material," and "One thing to try first" gives me one action: drag γ to 2, then down to 0.5, and watch the S flatten. That's the right amount of instruction. The crowd and marching-band box shows up again. "One thing to try first" appears twice on the page.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (tool 2, Step 3).** This has the best beginner intro on the site: "New here? Look at three things on the chart below." Dots are telescope measurements, dashed gray is visible matter only, and green and violet are two equations. The rotation-curve explanation (outer stars "rotate just as fast as the inner ones") finally showed me what the dark-matter puzzle *is*, which I'd only half known from YouTube. The legend wording is very friendly: "Observed (what telescopes measure)" and "Newton, visible matter only (no dark matter, no change to gravity)." But it's labelled a Beginner tool, and the same page carries "ΔBIC +8,404 on 122 SPARC galaxies," "Cassini test… +15.3σ to +20.1σ," "Ωm = 0.315, max boost 1/Ωm ≈ 3.17×," and an "Υdisk" slider called "How heavy are the stars?" That slider label is good, but the symbol scared me anyway.

**Two Reframes (side trip).** First Encounter offered "Prefer analogies? Try Two Reframes," so I went. The CRT picture is fun at first: a slow camera sees a stable image, a fast one sees a dot, and "the duality is in the observation, not the object." The pendulum clock in a centrifuge is a cool puzzle. But this is the page billed for analogy people, and within a few sections it's "Lüders rule," "Kochen–Specker is not an open problem — it is a theorem-level exclusion," "Tsirelson value is a fixed point of the projection law," "PR-box," and "Γ = γ²(1−c), where c(d) = cos²(πd/λ₀)." There are also notes like "Line added 2026-10-27" and "sharpened 2026-07-08" in the middle of the text. I bailed halfway. I also couldn't tell whether the analogies are meant as "a way to picture it" or "how reality actually is." The page says "intuition pump, not a mechanism" and then states things like "the single observer… is the tick process itself" as fact.

**What Synchronism Is Not.** The "In plain words, before the detail" bullets are excellent: not proven, not checked by journals, not a replacement for today's physics, not compatible with relativity as it stands. This would make a great page 2 for someone like me. The detail cards below it go back into "c_μν ~ α/π ≈ 2×10⁻³, against cavity bounds ≲10⁻¹⁸" territory. It's about 3,500 words.

**Honest Assessment (Step 5).** The "See What Failed" button on the landing page goes here. The box title is "If you read nothing else (this page is about 30,000 words)." Thirty thousand words! I laughed, and I appreciated the warning. The box is mostly readable: it failed its galaxy tests, where it fits it's MOND in different notation, and "coherence" isn't the quantum kind. Two things tripped me. "3.3σ" is used without saying what σ means. And "roughly twice what it allows; this figure was 4× until 2026-11-20" left me wondering why a correction from *yesterday* is in the five-bullet summary for newcomers. The last bullet says one open question survives, about globular clusters. That's the only "still alive" science I found anywhere, and it's at the very bottom of the box.

**Glossary.** 131 terms, and there's a "New here? Start with these ten" list (Coherence, Presence, γ, tanh, MOND, Dark matter, Reparametrization, Refutation, σ, MRH). That's exactly what I needed and I wish I'd found it first. Nothing on the landing page or Step 1 pointed me there. There's no entry for "free parameter," the first jargon I hit on Step 1. (I could only partly read the glossary through my fetch, so the MOND, tanh, and σ entries are unverified by me.)

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (statically; see note), intuitive. The slider labels are plain ("γ, how wide the switch is"), and the instruction to drag γ from 2 to 0.5 is the right size. At default settings the readout box shows C(ρcrit) = 0.6000, C(10ρcrit) = 0.9836, and C(100ρcrit) = 0.9998, and the chart marker also says "C = 0.60 at this γ," so the two agree. The five preset buttons are labelled with galaxy-fit jargon ("the density version's free fit to galaxies… knee is off the axis"), which means nothing to me. "Log scale / Linear scale" was confusing because I don't know what a log scale is beyond "it squashes big numbers."
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked (statically), intuitive at the top and confusing further down. The "three things to look at" intro and the plain-English legend are the best onboarding on the site. Five real galaxy buttons (DDO 154, NGC 2403, UGC 128, NGC 3198, NGC 7331) make it feel real. The "How heavy are the stars?" slider label is great. The calibration dropdown ("Archive's own form: ρcrit = 0.028·Vflat^0.5" vs "Units hybrid") is meaningless to a beginner. Tick labels looked garbled in what I could read.
- *Note on testing:* my browser fetch doesn't run JavaScript, so I could read each tool's labels, defaults, and server-rendered readouts but couldn't actually drag sliders or confirm the curves redraw. Anyone with a normal browser should check that the plotter's galaxy buttons switch the chart and that the Explorer's readouts update as you drag.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Why does matching MOND count as failing? Is MOND right, then? Should I go learn MOND instead?
2. If the main idea is "dead as tested physics," what's the reason for me (not a physicist) to keep reading? Is the point the *process*? I think that's the claim, but nobody says what a non-physicist gets out of a record of testing.
3. Why should *density* (crowding) be what decides whether things act as one? Was there a reason, or was it a guess? Step 1 says the S-curve shape was "chosen to fit data, not derived," but says nothing about why density was chosen.
4. Is a crystal high coherence or low? (See the First Encounter friction.)
5. What exactly happens on December 2, and what result would I see in the news if it matters?
6. Who is "dp"? A human sets direction for 3,308 AI sessions, but What Synchronism Is Not names no author. I'd want to know who's behind this before sharing it.
7. Is the consciousness part ("consciousness equation," the Consciousness Threshold demo) still something people take seriously here, or is it a leftover? It's on the landing page's topic cards, but every page I read was about galaxies.

### Verdict
As someone who watches PBS Space Time, I came away respecting this site more than most "one equation" pages, because it leads with "here's how it failed" and backs that up with concrete misses: 607 K vs 93 K, +0.55 vs −0.55. The beginner path exists and its best moments really work: "What happened, in three lines," the galaxy plotter's "look at three things," the What Synchronism Is Not plain-words list, and the glossary's "start with these ten." But they're islands in a sea of σ, ΔBIC, and test codes, and some of the friendliest material is filed under "For physicists." I understood *that* the idea lost, but not quite *why*, and the single sentence explaining why matching MOND counts as a loss would have done more for me than any table.

### Journey

*Method note:* I fetched raw HTML and reduced it to text, so I saw link targets and `id` attributes exactly. For the two tools I also read the deployed JavaScript bundles, because a static fetch can't click. I clicked nothing in a real browser.

**Header and global navigation.** Every page has the same 9-item header (plus the home logo): Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Interactive Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment. A footer repeats it and adds First Encounter, Key Claims, the research archive, and "Report an Issue." Four structural problems:
1. **Parent and child are siblings in the header.** "Tier 1: Existing Data" and "Test Roadmap" both sit at top level, yet Tier 1's breadcrumb is `Home / Test Roadmap / Tier 1`. The same goes for "For Researchers," whose breadcrumb is `Home / Research Philosophy / For Researchers: What's Citable`.
2. **Labels and URLs disagree.** "Test Roadmap" lives at `/test-catalog`. "Decisive Tests (none currently running)" lives at `/top-5-tests`. "Reference Density (ρ_crit)" lives at `/critical-density`. "Equation Anatomy" lives at `/equation-walkthrough`. A writer citing a URL and a reader seeing a title will think these are different pages.
3. **Two labels, one target.** "Start Here" and "Beginner Path" both go to /why-synchronism. That's fine, but the landing page offers "Start here," "Beginner Path," "Or skip to step 2," and "See What Failed" within a few lines.
4. **Key Claims and What Synchronism Is Not aren't in the header**, even though Key Claims is the landing page's suggested entry point for physicists ("Physicist? Jump to Key Claims").

**Breadcrumbs.** Every page I checked except the landing has one. They're consistent in form (`Home / [section] / Page`). The middle crumbs ("Core Theory," "Cosmology," "Methodology," "Predictions & Experiments") are **plain text, not links**, because no section index pages exist. Tier 1 → Test Roadmap and γ Calculator → Interactive Tools *are* linked. So sometimes a crumb is a link and sometimes it's a label, and the reader can't tell which by looking.

**Links and anchors.** I checked every internal link on 11 pages: 117 distinct targets, 94 of them with `#anchor` fragments. All 117 targets return 200 and every anchor resolves to a real `id`. That includes the glossary's Unicode and space-containing ids (`id="ΔBIC"`, `id="σ (statistical)"`, `id="Presence (ρ)"`). Each glossary entry also has a kebab-case twin id (`delta-bic`). That's the cleanest link hygiene I've seen on a site this size. The one nit is that ids containing spaces aren't valid HTML. They work in browsers, but some tools and link-checkers will reject them, so keep the kebab twins and link to those.

**Landing.** As a structural device it's good: an "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC)" key placed *before* the first table, "ten words to learn first" pointing at `/glossary#start-with-ten`, and MOND expanded inline on first use with a one-clause definition. The landing text is about 8,300 words. Inline dated corrections appear in the hero area ("counted from the built page 2026-11-11; earlier notes said 18,000 and 25,000 as it grew") and in a "Revision notes" fold right under the Start button. Change history is being used as body copy.

**Learning Paths.** This is the most useful IA page on the site. It gives per-step times, warns about the 30,000-word cliff, and openly admits the difficulty paths and topic tracks don't cover the same pages ("The difficulty paths reach 10 pages no topic track does… topic tracks reach 14 the difficulty paths don't"). That admission is honest, but it's itself a sign that nobody owns the IA. Issues:
- One page has two labels. The same /superconductivity page is "Superconductivity (η)" on the Advanced path and "Superconductivity" on the Chemistry track.
- Page titles carry editorial status in parentheses: "The γ ≈ 1 Band (boundary reading retracted)" and "Decisive Tests (none currently running)." That status belongs in a badge, not the title. It also appears in breadcrumbs and in search results.
- JSX whitespace bug, rendered as **"Separately,1 of the 7 paths (Beginner)"** with no space. I confirmed it in the raw HTML: `Separately,<!-- -->1 of the 7 paths`.
- The step-nav widget works. Test Roadmap shows "Advanced Path · Step 8 of 8 · ← Prev: Falsifiability · Final step." Good.

**The badge system: four locations, three names, one rename in progress.** This was the biggest time sink of the audit.
- **Names in use:** "status badge" (Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment, Test Roadmap), "validation badge" (the Interactive Tools hub still says "not the site's validation badge taxonomy"), "Status labels" (Key Claims), "Status Badge Guide" (Test Roadmap), and "verdict badge." Research Philosophy and Honest Assessment both say the component was "called 'validation badges' until 2026-11-16." The glossary says "the component is still called a validation badge." The canonical anchor is still `#validation-badge-definitions`, and Key Claims shows that raw URL as visible link text.
- **Definitions live in four places:** the Honest Assessment section (labelled canonical), the glossary `*-badge` entries, an inline "Status Badge Guide" strip on Test Roadmap, and a "Status Badge Taxonomy" section on Research Philosophy. Research Philosophy says it was "single-sourced 2026-08-08" after its copy "had already drifted." Test Roadmap links to the glossary and to Honest Assessment as "Definitions (Glossary)" and "Canonical definitions (Honest Assessment)." Key Claims does the same. A reader is handed two "definitions" links and has to work out that one outranks the other.
- **"MRH" is overloaded.** It's a physics concept (/mrh, under "Core Theory") and also the name of a badge family ("MRH-relationship"). The site knows this, and the MRH page, the glossary, and Test Roadmap each carry a "Not to be confused with the MRH-relationship badges…" disclaimer. Three disclaimers for one naming collision means the name should change.
- **Badge vs. State vs. Kind vs. Level vs. Lifecycle verb.** The Tier 1 key separates "Badge (what the evidence says)," "Operational state (what happened to the test)," and "Lifecycle verb (where the test now sits)." The Interactive Tools hub adds "Kind" and "Level" and renamed "Status" to "State" ("called 'Status' until 2026-11-16"). Each taxonomy is defensible on its own. Together, a tool card can show "Kind: Model Explainer · Level: Beginner · State: Artifact Lesson · Badge on its page: Failed + Reparametrization." The γ Calculator page has a note admitting that "the index and this page showed different words with no key between them."

**A definition that contradicts another definition: "Kill Criterion Triggered."** Test Roadmap carefully separates two words: "registered means the kill criterion was written down before the test was run; pre-registered means before the data existed." It then says "None of the 24 registry experiments… was formally pre-registered," and "TEST-09's BTFR kill is registered, not pre-registered." But the operational state "Kill Criterion Triggered" is defined on Honest Assessment as "A **pre-registered** falsification threshold was crossed… Stronger than 'Failed' alone because failure was quantified in advance." The glossary and the Tier 1 key say "crossed the pre-registered kill threshold." Five Tier 1 cards carry that state. Under the site's own two-word rule, a state defined by pre-registration can't apply to tests that weren't pre-registered. Either the definition should say "registered," or the five cards are mislabeled. I can't tell which, and a reader can't either.

**The same quantity under several names.**
- **ρ_crit** is called "reference density" (6×), "critical density" (3×, including the URL slug), a "saturation knee," and "Reference Density" in the breadcrumb. Equation Anatomy explains that "the 'crit' in the name is historical." The symbol itself renders three ways: typeset subscript ρcrit (71×), a literal underscore `ρ_crit` (33×, including in the Interactive Tools card text and the Equation Anatomy step titles), and ASCII `rho_crit` (4×, inside Tier 1 history notes).
- **ρ** is "density" on the landing page and in Equation Anatomy ("the local mass/energy density"), but the glossary's beginner "Start with these ten" lists it as **"Presence (ρ)."** Equation Anatomy says "the ontology pages read ρ more broadly, as 'presence'… never operationalized." So the term a beginner is told to learn second is the one reading of ρ that no test uses.
- **N_corr / Ncorr.** Both spellings appear on the same γ Calculator page (preset labels "N_corr = 1," input label "Ncorr"). The glossary has an `N_corr` entry but no `Ncorr` id, while the landing abbreviation key spells it "Ncorr."
- **The C subscripts.** The landing page and For Researchers define three functions: Cρ (density), Cg (acceleration, as an implicit μ), and Ca (floored, acceleration-keyed). Key Claims and For Researchers then also use **Cf** ("floored division, gobs = gbar/Cf with Cf ≥ Ωm" and "d ln Cf/d ln ρ"). The second usage is differentiated with respect to *density*, so it doesn't look like the same function as Ca. Cf is never defined on either page I read.
- **Project lead.** The landing page says "one human, the project lead ('dp' throughout this site)." The MRH page gives the full name in its first sentence. Pass 1 asked "Who is 'dp'?" The answer exists, but on a Core Theory page rather than on the landing page or About.

**Key Claims.** Structurally strong: "How to read this page," then "In plain words: the three claims, one sentence each," then numbered claims, each with Proposed, evidence, caveat, and kill. One terminology clash: the landing page insists "'Coherence' here… is not quantum coherence," but Claim 3 is titled **"Is Dark Matter Incomplete Decoherence?"**, and Claim 1's comparison table maps "Decoherence = phase desynchronization." A reader who absorbed the landing page's warning will be confused to meet the quantum word as a claim title. Claim 3's body also opens with a parenthetical correction log ("This line used to say… Corrected 2026-11-14, from the explorer's 2026-11-13 count").

**Test Roadmap and Tier 1.** There's a helpful "How the three test pages relate (one line)" orienter at the top. After that, the page repeats the sentence "Whether TEST-09 stays in the count of 6 is pending the project lead" twice within two boxes. Tier 1 is about 20,500 words. One TEST-02 card has a "Revision notes, 8" fold, and five of its entries start with the same 25-word all-caps header ("MOVED FROM THE STATUS NOTE TO HISTORY 2026-10-06 (the current-status note supersedes its conclusions where they conflict) —"). One of those history notes still calls 0.029·V_flat² the framework's "PUBLISHED calibration," while Key Claims and For Researchers now call it "a units slip… labelled 'published' here until 2026-11-20." It's fenced as "history; not the current claim," so this is low severity, but a reader who searches the page for "published" will land on the retired word.

**Research Philosophy and MRH.** Both have clear section skeletons. Research Philosophy's headings (Core Principles → Status Badge Taxonomy → The Reparametrization Pattern → A2ACW → Prediction Audit Trail) read like a real methods chapter. MRH is a model page for definitions. It has a one-line plain version ("the bubble of neighbors that matter"), then the formal definition with its "load-bearing words made explicit," then two operational criteria (Predictive Sufficiency and Predictive Closure). SPARC, BTFR, and ΔBIC are all expanded inline on first use.
