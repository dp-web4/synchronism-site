## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 successful, 6 attempted that 404'd or errored
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Surprisingly honest about its own failures, but the front door is leaky — several links from the landing page go to dead URLs and the "casual reader" path keeps tripping over math jargon it promised to avoid.

### Journey

I clicked over from a Reddit link. The headline "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is a great hook — that's exactly the kind of thing PBS Space Time would tease. But then the equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` is right there on the landing page. I have no idea what tanh is. I think ln is "log"? It made me feel like I was already behind on day one.

I tried clicking the "What this is not" link in the nav because it was the most intriguing label — **404**. Then I tried "Why Synchronism" — **404**. That's the second thing I clicked on the home page and it didn't exist. Not great.

I went back and tried "Start Exploring," which got me to the **First Encounter**. That opens with a really nice sentence: *"A single electron floating in a vacuum — almost nothing around it to interact with. Now: the core of a neutron star, where matter is packed 10¹⁴× denser than lead..."* That I could picture. I liked that. Then it threw "Markov Relevancy Horizon" at me on step 1 and I bounced.

The **Tools** page was where I had the most fun. Picked the **Coherence Explorer** first (and had to hunt for the right URL — `/tools/coherence-explorer` 404'd, but `/coherence-explorer` worked). The marching band vs. crowd-of-strangers analogy finally made coherence click for me. Pulling the γ slider and watching the curve get sharper or gentler was actually intuitive.

Then **Galaxy Plotter** — I picked NGC 3198, saw the Synchronism curve match the observations, and started to feel impressed. Then I read the small print: "Vflat is taken from existing SPARC/MOND fits, not independently predicted by Synchronism" and "the scatter test... was triggered as Failed." Wait, so this isn't the prediction working? I'm confused but also a little impressed they admitted it.

I clicked the **Honest Assessment** page out of curiosity. It says: *"0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted by external data."* That is a wild thing to read after a landing page that asks "What if one equation described reality?" I respect the honesty enormously but I don't know what to do with this contradiction. Either this is a serious theory or it isn't, and the front and the back of the site are telling me different things.

I tried the **Equation Walkthrough** to see if it would finally explain tanh. Step 1 of 6 talked about density, but the full intimidating equation was sitting there the whole time. The promise of "step-by-step" felt undermined by the equation already being on display before I got to step 2.

The **Glossary** is decent and has good plain-language definitions for some terms ("Protons, neutrons, and everything made of them — the ordinary matter you can touch") but for others it just defines jargon with more jargon. The coherence function definition uses "Markov Relevancy Horizon" without unpacking it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. What does **tanh** actually do? Nobody told me. (I know it's a math function, but why this one?)
2. If there are 0 confirmed predictions, why is the landing page so confident? Is this a theory or a hypothesis or a "framework" (and what's the difference)?

The Coherence Explorer was the one place I actually felt like I learned something. If a friend asked me what this site is about, I'd say "it's a really honest research blog about an idea that hasn't worked yet" — which is interesting, but not what the landing page is selling.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structurally; not evaluating physics)
- **Would return?**: yes — the IA is salvageable and the content is good enough to be worth fixing
- **Overall impression**: The content is here and the breadcrumbs work, but the validation-badge taxonomy is fragmented across pages, several nav labels don't match their destination titles, and the URL hierarchy doesn't reflect the navigation hierarchy. These are exactly the things a doc audit catches before launch.

### Journey

I started at the landing page and walked the top nav left-to-right, then drilled into in-body CTAs, the Tools index, two tools I picked because Pass 1 didn't cover them (γ Calculator and Phase Boundary Visualizer), and finally the cross-cutting "structural" pages: Glossary, Honest Assessment, Test Roadmap, Key Claims, Parameter Derivations, MRH, and What Synchronism Is Not.

**Reproducing Pass 1's 404 reports.** Pass 1 reported `/why-synchronism` and `/what-synchronism-is-not` returning 404. I could not reproduce: both pages render fine and have proper H1s, breadcrumbs, and content. That's worth flagging — either Pass 1 hit a transient deploy/edge-cache miss, or there's an intermittent routing issue I should not paper over by silently dropping the report. I'll note it in the friction log as "investigate" rather than "fix" or "dismiss."

**Nav-label vs. destination-title mismatch.** This jumped out immediately. The top nav has `Get Started`, `Tools`, `Methodology`. Click those and the H1s are `Why Synchronism?`, `Interactive Tools`, `Research Philosophy`. The URL slugs are `/why-synchronism`, `/interactive-tools`, `/research-philosophy`. Three independent identifiers per page — nav label, URL slug, page H1 — and none of them agree. For SEO, deep-linking, breadcrumbs, and "where am I?" orientation, you want at most two of those drifting, and ideally all three aligned.

**URL hierarchy ≠ nav hierarchy.** The Tools index lives at `/interactive-tools`, but every tool linked from it lives at root: `/coherence-explorer`, `/galaxy-plotter`, `/gamma-calculator`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer`, `/equation-walkthrough`, `/consciousness-demo`, `/gamma-boundary`. Visitors will guess `/interactive-tools/coherence-explorer` or `/tools/coherence-explorer` (Pass 1 did exactly this) and get a 404. Either flatten the index URL to `/tools` to match the existing flat slugs, or move all tools under `/tools/...` to match the index. The current arrangement is the worst of both worlds.

**Validation-badge taxonomy is fragmented across at least four vocabularies.** I expected one canonical badge legend. I found five taxonomies that don't fully agree:

| Source | Badges |
|---|---|
| Glossary | Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed, **Post-diction** |
| Honest Assessment | Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed *(no Post-diction)* |
| Key Claims | Compound labels — `Speculative — geometric artifact`, `Failed — Mechanism Under Revision` *(suffixes not defined anywhere)*; Claim #1 has **no badge at all** |
| Parameter Derivations | A different vocabulary entirely — `Motivated Ansatz`, `Motivated Choice`, `Freeman's Law Re-expressed`, `Jeans Criterion`, `Dimensional Analysis` *(none of these are in the glossary)* |
| Test Roadmap | No badges shown next to tests; pass/fail reported in prose |

A reader who sees `Failed — Mechanism Under Revision` on Key Claims has no glossary entry to look up the suffix. A reader who lands on Parameter Derivations sees `Motivated Ansatz` and reasonably assumes that's a defined badge tier — it isn't. This is the single biggest IA fix on the site: pick one taxonomy, define every label in the glossary (or a dedicated `/validation-badges` legend page), and use only those labels everywhere.

**Glossary gaps for terms used on heavily-trafficked pages.** σ8 and DESI are both used on the Honest Assessment page (the headline number quotes "DESI DR1 contradicts the cosmological prediction at 2.4σ") but neither is in the glossary. σ (sigma, as statistical significance) isn't defined either. These are exactly the terms a non-physicist hits and bounces on. Other terms — MRH, MOND, SPARC, ΛCDM, BAO, A2ACW, tanh, ln — are well-defined.

**MRH page has no worked example and doesn't backlink to First Encounter.** First Encounter introduces MRH on Step 1 (and as Pass 1 noted, that's where casual readers bounce). The dedicated `/mrh` page opens with "The minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution…" — which is a glossary-grade definition, not a teaching one. There's no marching-band-style analogy, no concrete example (e.g., "for a hydrogen atom, the MRH is the proton + electron + nearby photons; for a galaxy, it's…"), and no link back to First Encounter where the reader first met the term. Either MRH deserves its own intuitive walkthrough or the dedicated page should redirect new readers to First Encounter / Coherence Explorer first.

**Test Roadmap has 24 tests but no test-ID convention or badge column.** The page introduces "24 specific, falsifiable experiments" across four tiers, but I don't see TEST-01 / TEST-04a identifiers, and there's no validation-badge column. Pass/fail is reported in inline prose ("TFR scatter, R² = 0.14, FAILED"). For a roadmap that's also referenced from Honest Assessment ("0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted"), a stable test ID + badge per row would let readers cross-reference between pages. Right now they can't.

**First Encounter step heading rendered as "Step 1 of 7ρ (presence)".** That looks like a missing space or `<br>` between "Step 1 of 7" and "ρ (presence)". Could be a fetch-rendering artifact, but worth a glance at the actual component — visitors using screen readers or copy-pasting will see it the same way I did.

**Tools tested.** I deliberately picked two that Pass 1 didn't:

The **γ Calculator** is the cleanest tool on the site from a doc perspective: defines γ and Ncorr inline, shows the formula `γ = 2/√Ncorr` prominently, gives six named presets (Ideal gas, Liquid water, Enzyme site, Ferromagnet, BCS superconductor, BEC), and labels output regimes with prose ("Weakly Correlated," "Boundary," "Strongly Correlated," "Collective"). This is the model the other tools should follow.

The **Phase Boundary Visualizer** is weaker. The γ-axis goes 0 to 3.5+ with three labeled regions (Collective, Boundary, Single-particle), and several systems are placed along it. But "phase boundary," "coherence," "ρ_crit," and "presence" — all of which appear in the visualizer's surrounding context — are not defined inline. The page also flags an honest caveat that "γ comparisons are method-dependent until standardized measurement protocols exist," which is great as a methodology disclosure but undermines the visual claim that, say, "galaxies and ideal gas both sit at γ=2" — a coincidence the page doesn't explain.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, parameter-derivations, equation-walkthrough, coherence-function, coherence-explorer, gamma-calculator, phase-boundary-visualizer, galaxy-plotter, measurement-without-observers, key-claims, honest-assessment, test-catalog — `/tier-1-existing-data` 404'd)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of what the framework is and isn't)
- **Would return?**: yes — as a case study in how to do honest theory-development in public, even if the physics doesn't (yet) clear the bar
- **Overall impression**: I came hunting for a derivation and found, repeatedly and to the site's enormous credit, the authors quietly conceding there isn't one. The headline equation is a phenomenological ansatz with a misleadingly named scale parameter; the "MOND unification" is a ~40-year-old dimensional coincidence; the only Tier-1 prediction that engaged real data was refuted at 2.4σ. The site's honesty is more impressive than its physics.

### Journey

I went straight to **Parameter Derivations** because that's where a grad student decides whether to keep reading. The page is an order of magnitude more honest than I expected from a "framework that explains 80 orders of magnitude." γ = 2/√Ncorr is labeled **Motivated Ansatz** with the explicit admission that "integrating out momenta introduces temperature- and mass-dependent factors, not a clean factor of 2." That is exactly right — and it's a concession most TOE-style frameworks would never make. The factor of 2 is post-hoc.

The **tanh form** page is the moment of truth. In any honest mean-field treatment, tanh appears as the saddle-point of a Z₂-symmetric Landau free energy or — more familiarly — from the Curie-Weiss self-consistency loop *m = tanh(βJm)*. The site itself acknowledges this on the parameter-derivations page: "tanh arises in mean-field Ising models … but that tanh comes from the *self-consistency equation*. C(ρ) has no such self-consistency loop: it is evaluated directly." The /coherence-function page goes further: tanh is "motivated, not derived," and "any sigmoid satisfying the four constraints (logistic, erf, arctan, Hill) would have been an equally valid choice." That is a phenomenological ansatz, full stop. Calling the surrounding apparatus "mean-field" is a category error; there is no field, no order parameter equation of motion, no broken symmetry, no Landau expansion. What's left is a sigmoid fit to a postulated relationship between density and an undefined quantity called "coherence."

The argument *for* the specific choice — "presence spans 80 orders of magnitude, so we need a log inside" — is a rationalization, not a derivation. The `+1` inside `ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)` is a regulator that prevents the log from diverging at ρ→0; it has no physical motivation that I can see. Worse: it asymmetrizes the sigmoid so that ρ_crit is *not* the half-maximum. Plugging in γ=2, ρ=ρ_crit gives `C = tanh(2·ln 2) = tanh(1.386) ≈ 0.88`. The Coherence Explorer confirms this — it displays C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88. So the parameter named "ρ_crit" is the density at which the system is already 88% classicalized. In Landau-theory or phase-transition language, that's not a critical density. It's a saturation knee. The naming is misleading.

I tested the **γ Calculator** next because the page-2 critic reports said it was the cleanest tool. The math is fine — γ = 2/√Ncorr is just algebra — but the Ncorr presets are where condensed-matter physics stops cooperating. BCS superconductors are listed at Ncorr = 10⁴, and the page itself admits "physical Cooper-pair coherence volumes in real materials contain ~10⁶–10⁹ pairs." That's a 2-to-5 order of magnitude undercount. Ferromagnets at Ncorr = 100 — what about correlation lengths near Tc, where Ncorr should diverge? The page acknowledges "a scale-invariant counting recipe for operational Ncorr is an open research question." Translation: the input parameter to the framework's central equation is currently uncalibrated for actual condensed-matter systems. That makes any γ value reported on this site a free fit, not a prediction.

The **Phase Boundary Visualizer** confirmed my suspicion. Galaxies and ideal gas are both placed at γ=2.0 (Ncorr=1). The page is admirably explicit: "When two physically unrelated systems produce the same γ, γ is classifying the *counting method*, not the system." Right — but then what is the visualizer *showing*? If the placements are method-dependent, the geometry of the figure is a Rorschach test for whoever counted, not a physical claim. The visualizer should either be retired or relabeled "γ values under one specific counting convention; not a cross-system prediction."

The **Galaxy Plotter** is where the wheels come off the "explains dark matter" claim. Vflat is *imported from SPARC/MOND fits*, not predicted. The framework matches rotation-curve *shape*, but shape-matching is what any function with a tunable transition at the right radius will do. The **TFR scatter test** — the genuine quantitative test of the framework on this dataset — failed at R² = 0.14, *below* the framework's own kill threshold of 0.20. And yet on the **Honest Assessment** page, "Galaxy Rotation (ALFALFA-SDSS)" is listed as **Validated** on the basis of "environment-dependent RAR scatter at p = 5×10⁻⁶." But the same paragraph notes that "kill criterion triggered at R² = 0.14 vs required 0.20." That is a self-contradicting badge. If the kill triggered, the claim is Failed, not Validated, regardless of a separate environment-correlation finding.

I went looking for **a₀ = cH₀/(2π)**, the headline "MOND unification" claim labeled **Strongly Supported**. The Parameter Derivations page itself notes: "This dimensional relation has been noted since Milgrom (1983) and independently derived by multiple frameworks." So this is a 43-year-old dimensional coincidence, not a Synchronism prediction. Likewise, **Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G)** is admitted to be "dimensional bookkeeping — expressing Freeman's empirical value via cosmological constants, not deriving it from physics." Both belong under **Reparametrization**. Calling them "Strongly Supported" inflates the framework's predictive scoreboard.

**Measurement-without-observers** is the most important page for any quantum foundations claim, so I read it carefully. The framework reframes wave-function collapse as decoherence at the MRH boundary. Fine — that is standard decoherence theory in different vocabulary. But the page concedes the critical gap: "Synchronism's MRH framework does not currently derive the Born rule, assume it explicitly, or flag it as an open question." That last clause is doing a lot of work. Without a probability rule, this is not a measurement theory; it's a relabeling of decoherence (which itself doesn't yield Born — that's the textbook lesson of Zurek '03 onwards). The page is honest about being "an interpretive overlay," but Claim #1 on the Key Claims page — "Quantum Mechanics Is Synchronization Physics" — has no badge at all. Given the open Born-rule gap, the honest badge is **Untested + foundationally incomplete**.

The **DESI refutation** (TEST-04a) is the only place the framework engaged a genuine independent dataset. Predicted fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418 (12% suppression below ΛCDM); observed 0.55 ± 0.06 — *above* ΛCDM, with the predicted inversion pattern reversed. 2.4σ disagreement, kill criterion triggered. The site reports this honestly. What it does *not* address on the test-catalog page I could find is whether the 0.418 prediction was derived prospectively or whether σ₈ was calibrated against earlier lensing tensions before being propagated to DESI. The latter would make this a doubly post-hoc test that the framework still failed — which is a worse failure mode than a clean refutation. I couldn't resolve this from the pages I read; flagging as a question.

**Failed claims I noticed**: melting points 53% error, critical exponents 2× off, YBCO Tc predicted 607K vs observed 93K (6.5× off). These are quantitative failures of the kind that one cannot "fix at the next iteration" — they suggest the framework's notion of γ-coherence simply isn't tracking the physics that sets these scales. The η-as-Abrikosov-Gor'kov-pair-breaking-efficiency identification ("identical to 1960 result") and "all 23 superconductor predictions are standard condensed matter in different notation" are exactly the right conclusion to draw, and the site draws it.
