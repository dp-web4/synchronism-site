# Topic: Entity Criterion (Γ < m) — Find a Genuinely Discriminating Example

## Question
The entity criterion Γ < m is currently cited as the "strongest candidate novel prediction" using f₀(500) (the σ meson) as the example. But the Pass 4 researcher found that f₀(500) is the textbook contested PDG state where one camp already says it's not a particle. Synchronism's criterion aligns with an existing position, not a novel one.

Can we find a resonance where Γ < m makes a call that NO existing analysis has made?

## Why It Matters
The current honest-assessment page calls Γ < m the "strongest candidate novel prediction surviving stress tests." If this example is just choosing sides in an existing controversy, the prediction is not novel. Finding a genuinely discriminating example would make the claim substantially stronger. If no example exists, the page should say "consistent with the non-particle camp on f₀(500)" rather than "novel prediction."

## Suggested Investigation
1. Search PDG for states with Γ/m > 1 and Γ/m < 1 where interpretation is genuinely contested (not just one camp vs. another)
2. Check a₀(980): Γ ≈ 50-100 MeV, m ≈ 980 MeV → Γ/m ≈ 0.05-0.10 (well below 1, would be "entity" under both camps)
3. Check f₀(980): Γ ≈ 40-100 MeV, m ≈ 980 MeV → Γ/m ≈ 0.04-0.10 (same)
4. Look for states where Γ/m ≈ 0.8-1.2 (near the threshold) where the PDG has unresolved debates
5. Check broad charmonium/bottomonium states above threshold, e.g., ψ(3770), X(3872) analogs
6. Look for light scalar nonet members with Γ > m (would all be "non-entities" under the criterion)

## Target outcome
Either: "State X at Γ/m = 1.05 is claimed as a particle by analysis Y but Synchronism predicts non-entity — testable by re-analysis of Z data"
Or: "No such state found — reclassify criterion as 'consistent with non-particle interpretation of f₀(500)' not 'novel prediction'"
