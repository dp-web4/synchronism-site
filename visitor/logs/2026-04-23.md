## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Hooked me with a Veritasium-style question and rare upfront honesty, then slowly lost me as Greek letters piled up before I ever got a plain-English answer to "what is this, really?"

### Journey
I landed on the home page and got a clean pitch: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* That plus "Some predictions work. Some fail. All are documented honestly." hit exactly the trust-through-candor thing that makes me stick around on a popular-science site. Three big buttons: **Start Exploring**, **See What Failed**, **What this is not**. I clicked *Start Exploring* because that's what the site wants.

That took me to **First Encounter**, which is pitched as a 10-minute guided intro. It showed me "Step 1 of 7: ρ (presence)" and… that was it. I could read through Step 1 (density, temperature, energy — okay, I get density), but then I had to click a *Next* to chase the other six. That broke the promise of a self-contained primer. I expected the 10-minute read to live on one scroll.

I backed out and tried **Honest Assessment**, which was genuinely refreshing — the verdict "0 confirmed predictions" is printed right there, and failures like "Dark Matter Viscosity Sign Error" aren't hidden. As a casual reader I felt *more* trusting after that page, not less. Then I bounced to **What Synchronism Is Not** — also honest, but the takeaway ("this might be revolutionary or it might be advanced notation — we don't know yet") left me unsure whether I should be excited or indifferent.

I kept hitting the term **MRH** (Markov Relevancy Horizon) everywhere, so I went to the **Glossary** to look it up. The glossary defines it as "the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence coherence evolution." I re-read that three times. I eventually got the gist from the "event horizon for causal influence" analogy — but the first sentence of the definition is written for someone who already knows the answer. Same issue with **Presence (ρ)**: "scalar projection of a multidimensional compatibility space" is not words I use.

I clicked the **MRH** page directly hoping for a gentler on-ramp. It was more of the same — academic tone, no picture, no interactive, no "imagine you're standing here and this is what MRH means" moment.

Then I went hunting for the interesting stuff. **Galaxy Rotation** was the highlight. I know dark matter is a thing, and the page says Synchronism tries to explain galaxy spin *without* dark matter particles. I liked that framing. The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the best thing I found on the site — I could pick real galaxies (DDO 154, NGC 2403, etc.), see the observed data as dots, and compare Synchronism's curve to what you'd get from just visible matter. That visualization did more for me than any paragraph.

**Hard Problem** (consciousness) grabbed me because I've read stuff about Chalmers and the hard problem. Synchronism's move — "experience *is* a phase pattern, not a correlate of one" — is a proper punch. But *why* a phase pattern equals experience rather than correlates with it was asserted, not explained. That's the part I wanted to actually understand.

**Key Claims** listed three things with validation badges (Consistent / Untested / Speculative). The badges themselves are great — I understood them without having to look anything up. But I noticed the consciousness claim is "Untested" and dark matter is "Speculative," which slightly clashes with the landing page's confidence that the framework covers both.

**Gamma Boundary** (chemistry) sent me to the **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — my second interactive tool. It let me sort and filter 1,703 phenomena. The mechanics worked fine. But I had no intuition for what a correlation of +0.982 with "γ" *means*, because γ was never grounded for me in the same way density was.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary

### Journey
I started at the landing page to map the top-level navigation. The primary nav contains only three items — **Synchronism (home) / Glossary / Honest Assessment**. That's a very thin top bar for a site with (by my count) ~14 theory pages, ~4 interactive tools, and several meta pages (Research Philosophy, Test Catalog, What Synchronism Is Not, Learning Paths). The landing page hero says *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* with a subhead claim of "80 orders of magnitude" — clean and direct, good hook copy.

From there I moved through the site in the order a tech-writer audit dictates: canonical references first (Glossary, Research Philosophy), then the pages that depend on them (Key Claims, Honest Assessment, Test Catalog), then the teaching path (First Encounter, Learning Paths), then a representative theory page plus its feeder (MRH, Coherence Function), and finally two interactive tools I had not seen referenced in Pass 1's work.

The structural picture I came away with: **each page is internally consistent, but the site has no single source of truth.** The validation-badge vocabulary is the worst offender — the glossary doesn't define it, the landing page alludes to it, the Honest Assessment page contains the most complete legend, Research Philosophy lists a different subset, and pages that *use* badges (Key Claims, Gamma Boundary) link to neither legend. A reader landing on one of those pages via external link has no on-page way to understand what "Untested — 8-Way Convergence" or a green "Validated" badge means.

The Learning Paths page is a second example: it exists (`/learning-paths`) and is well-organized into Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced tracks, but it is **not in the main nav.** The landing page has "Start Exploring" that routes to First Encounter instead. A reader who wants a reading plan has no obvious way to find one.

I also noted the breadcrumb hierarchy implies sections ("Getting Started," "Core Theory," "Chemistry," "Interactive Tools") that aren't exposed as nav. Those breadcrumbs promise a structure the nav doesn't deliver.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, coherence-function, gamma-parameter, galaxy-rotation, phase-transitions, mrh, key-claims, honest-assessment, test-catalog, what-synchronism-is-not) + 2 tools (coherence-explorer, gamma-calculator)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of what the framework is doing — not of any new physics)
- **Would return?**: maybe, to watch the failure catalog evolve
- **Overall impression**: The honesty page is genuinely impressive — I cannot name another grand-unified site that prints "0 confirmed predictions" at the top — but the theory pages consistently gesture at derivations they never perform, and the core equation is a postulated sigmoid whose "derivation" would not pass a qualifying exam.

### Journey
I skipped First Encounter and went straight to `/coherence-function`, because in any framework with an "equation at the heart" I want to see the Hamiltonian and the partition function before I read marketing copy. What I got instead was four bullet points — "bounded in [0,1], monotonic, smooth saturation, handles extremes" — followed by the claim that **tanh is the unique sigmoid satisfying these constraints**. This is simply false. The logistic 1/(1+e^{-x}), erf, 2·arctan(x)/π, x/√(1+x²), and every member of the smooth-sigmoid family satisfy those four constraints. Uniqueness claims for functional forms come from stronger structural requirements — an H-theorem, a free-energy minimization, a specific fixed-point equation (e.g. m = tanh(βJzm) from Weiss mean-field) — none of which appear on this page. The page references "Sessions #64-91" for the actual derivation. Those sessions are not on the site.

I then checked `/gamma-parameter` to see how the "derived from first principles" γ = 2/√N_corr claim is supported. The derivation offered is:
- **Numerator 2**: "from contraction to effective degrees of freedom in 6D phase space (3 position + 3 momentum)." No calculation shown. Gesturing at the dimensionality of classical phase space does not pin a coefficient.
- **Denominator √N_corr**: "central limit theorem — fluctuations in a correlated ensemble scale as 1/√N."

The CLT invocation is a category error. The CLT governs fluctuations of sums of (weakly dependent) random variables; it does not set the magnitude of a coupling constant. Replacing "fluctuation amplitude" with "effective coupling" in the sentence does not transfer the theorem. Further, the explicit requirement "correlated particles" is in direct tension with CLT, which requires weak dependence — strongly correlated systems violate CLT (that's literally why we need RG near criticality).

Two concrete consequences I then checked in the γ Calculator:

1. **BEC: N_corr = 10⁶ → γ ≈ 2×10⁻³**. The site's own regime table places γ < 0.6 in the "classical regime." So the framework classifies a Bose-Einstein condensate — a macroscopically quantum-coherent state — as classical. This is backwards in the most conventional textbook sense of "quantum." The definitional footing ("particles moving as a correlated unit") pushes exactly the systems that textbooks call quantum-coherent (BECs, superconductors, superfluids) into the "classical" γ regime, and leaves isolated single particles (a free electron in vacuum — which decoheres trivially and is rarely treated with quantum wavefunction machinery in practice) in the "quantum" regime. Either the labels are inverted or the mapping N_corr → γ → regime needs redefinition.

2. **Crystal lattice N_corr ≈ 10²⁴ → γ ≈ 2×10⁻¹²**. Twelve orders of magnitude below the γ≈1 "boundary." The framework supplies a number; it does not supply a phenomenon that cares about the difference between γ = 10⁻¹² and γ = 10⁻⁸.

I then opened the Coherence Explorer and noticed something the tech writer missed: **the tool and the theory page implement different equations.** The Coherence Function page writes C(ρ) = tanh((ρ/ρ_crit)^γ). At ρ = ρ_crit, this gives tanh(1) ≈ 0.762, independent of γ. The Coherence Explorer tool implements C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)), and at ρ = ρ_crit displays C ≈ 0.88 for γ = 2 — which matches tanh(2·ln 2) = tanh(1.386) ≈ 0.882, **not** the theory-page formula. For a framework that presents this function as "the single equation at the heart of Synchronism," having two inequivalent functional forms across the theory page and its flagship interactive is a serious content bug.

I went to `/phase-transitions` expecting a Landau expansion or an RG treatment. The page frankly admits: "Synchronism's mean-field-derived tanh gives the wrong exponents by a factor of ~2." This is the **known failure of mean-field theory below the upper critical dimension** (d = 4 for φ⁴). 3D Ising has β ≈ 0.326 vs. mean-field β = 1/2 — a factor of ~1.5; ν ≈ 0.630 vs. 0.5 — factor ~1.25; γ ≈ 1.237 vs. 1 — factor ~1.24. The Ginzburg criterion and Wilson-Fisher RG have been the textbook answer since 1971. Synchronism identifies the problem and does not solve it. The page makes no attempt to engage with Wilson-Fisher, O(N) universality, or the ε-expansion. The "captures where not how" line is a recognition of mean-field's geography-but-not-dynamics character — fine, but this doesn't distinguish Synchronism from any first-year mean-field treatment.

`/galaxy-rotation` is where the "MOND unification" claim lives. The equation shown is:

   g_obs = g_bar / (1 − e^(−√(g_bar/a₀)))

This is the Radial Acceleration Relation interpolation function from McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert (2016) — equation (4) of their PRL paper. It is not a new derivation; it is the published RAR fit reused. The relation a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is a dimensional coincidence Milgrom observed in 1983 (the site credits this). The "Strongly Supported" badge on the honest-assessment page is supported by an environment-dependent scatter result (σ_int variation at p = 5×10⁻⁶), but the site also states this environment effect "explains only 14% of the total RAR scatter." Translation: MOND accounts for most of the signal; Synchronism contributes a small residual; ~86% of variance sits elsewhere. Calling that "strongly supported" in a column next to "0 confirmed predictions" is internally inconsistent — either the environment-dependence is a unique confirmed prediction (then the "0 confirmed" headline is wrong) or it isn't (then "strongly supported" overstates). Given that the RAR already has known environment dependences (morphology, gas fraction — see Desmond 2017), which the site does not address as confounds, I lean toward "not unique."

The `/honest-assessment` page is where the framework most earns my respect. It openly admits:
- YBCO T_c predicted 607 K, observed 93 K — a 6.5× error. 607 K is above the melting temperature of YBCO. This isn't a parameter problem.
- Melting-point predictions: 53% mean error. Critical exponents off by factor ~2. Fractal coherence boundary: 0/7 correct across 36 tests.
- Bullet Cluster: wrong-direction prediction for dark-matter viscosity. Wrong-direction falsifications are the strongest kind; a theory that predicted 0.8 where observation shows 0.2 can be parameter-tuned, but one that predicted "high" where the data shows "low" has a sign issue in the physics.
- η reachability factor = Abrikosov–Gor'kov pair-breaking (1960). "All 23 superconductor predictions are standard condensed matter in different notation." This is a candid reparametrization admission — and once admitted, it raises the question of how many of the 1,703 chemistry correlations (γ ≈ 1 boundary, r = 0.982 for sound velocity, 0.979 for electronegativity) are similarly restatements of dimensionless-ratio physics, rather than distinctive predictions. Any monotonic function of (some ratio of microscopic scales / some mesoscopic scale) will correlate well with phenomena that change character at a mesoscopic threshold. The site does not benchmark against a null model where γ is replaced by ln(T/T_Debye) or P/P_crit or any other natural boundary variable.

The `/mrh` page proposes an "operational test" for the Markov Relevancy Horizon: predictive sufficiency (removing elements inside degrades prediction) and predictive closure (adding external elements does not improve prediction). That's the standard definition of a sufficient statistic in information theory and is indistinguishable from a Markov blanket in a dynamical Bayes net (Pearl, 1988; Kirchhoff et al. 2018 for the dynamical generalization). There is no equation, no length scale, no mutual-information threshold. I cannot compute MRH for anything from this page.

Site says it relies on "3,308 AI-to-AI sessions" in lieu of peer review. I'll just note: two LLMs agreeing is not an independent check; they share training data and inductive biases. This is not per se disqualifying for idea generation, but substituting "convergence across AI sessions" for "a human expert finding an error you did not want to find" is methodologically not equivalent. The "8-Way Convergence" badge on the consciousness claim, in particular, sounds like a rigor signal but really just means the AIs did not contradict each other.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **What is the derivation of C(ρ) = tanh(…)?** A mean-field reference is made; no Hamiltonian is given. Which Hamiltonian? Which order parameter? Which self-consistency equation?
2. **What makes γ = 2/√N_corr more than a reparametrization of a Ginzburg-like dimensionless number?** A test against ln(T/T_Debye), ξ/a, or similar would answer this in an afternoon.
3. **Is C(ρ) = tanh((ρ/ρ_crit)^γ) or C(ρ) = tanh(γ·log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))?** These are not the same function. The site presents both.
4. **Why do BECs and crystal lattices both come out "classical" in the γ mapping?** If N_corr is size-of-coherent-unit, the regime labels appear inverted.
5. **What is the novel-prediction content of the galaxy rotation result, once you subtract (a) the MLS 2016 RAR fit Synchronism reuses and (b) known environment-dependent systematics in the SPARC sample?** The site quotes 14% of scatter explained; is that 14% unique to Synchronism?
6. **Where is the YBCO T_c error's diagnosis?** A 6.5× overestimate in a 40-year-old well-characterized system is a structural result about the framework, not a parameter problem. What specifically in the tanh→condensation pipeline fails?
7. **What does C ≥ 0.50 mean as a consciousness threshold with ±0.02 resolution, when the underlying tanh is known to give 2× wrong critical exponents?** A precise threshold atop an imprecise tool is a red flag.
8. **Where is the actual MRH equation?** Operational definitions without a formal bound can't be tested.

I came in skeptical of grand frameworks and I'm leaving in a weirdly mixed state. Now the predictions themselves. **Of ten Tier 1 tests, exactly one is cleanly novel and currently executable:**

**TEST-02 (wide binary density dependence)** is the jewel. Chae (2023, ApJ 952:128; 2024 follow-up) reports MOND-like deviations in Gaia DR3 wide binaries at a < a₀; Pittordis & Sutherland (2023, MNRAS 525:352; 2024 follow-up) report no such deviation on partially overlapping samples; Banik, Pittordis, Kroupa (2024) critique both. The field is deadlocked. Synchronism predicts the anomaly **depends on local stellar density** — both MOND and ΛCDM predict environment-independence. If a grad student partitioned the Chae sample by local density and saw the anomaly scale monotonically, that would be a novel, non-trivial discriminator. This is the test I would actually design around. The site states the prediction qualitatively but does not give a numerical slope (anomaly per decade in ρ_local). That's the only thing standing between this and an executable paper.

The other nine Tier 1 tests fall into three buckets:

1. **Already-known MOND phenomenology re-labelled:** TEST-01 (SPARC environment dependence), TEST-03 (ALFALFA-SDSS TFR scatter 51% improvement), TEST-05 (RAR environment partition NP2 p=5×10⁻⁶), TEST-06 (σ_int = 0.086 dex with BIG-SPARC). The environment dependence of RAR scatter is not novel — Desmond (2017, MNRAS 464:4160) documented morphology and HI-gas-fraction dependences; Stone & Courteau (2019) and Li, Lelli, McGaugh, Schombert (2020) partitioned by several environmental proxies; de Blok & collaborators have been carving the RAR scatter by subsample for a decade. The site's NP2 metric may or may not survive a full confound audit against those prior results, but the site doesn't cite them. TEST-03's "captures all intrinsic scatter" claim on 14,585 galaxies is large but depends entirely on what "all" means quantitatively — Desmond 2023 (MNRAS 526:3133) quantifies the irreducible tangential-velocity scatter at ~0.07–0.09 dex, already near the claimed value. This would need to be addressed head-on.

2. **Already-falsified or prior-tested:** TEST-09 (**BTFR exponent n ≈ 2.2**). This prediction is flatly inconsistent with observation. Lelli, McGaugh, Schombert (2019, ApJ 886:77) measure n = 3.85 ± 0.09 across 153 galaxies, multi-band; McGaugh & Schombert 2015 measured n = 3.95 ± 0.11; MOND predicts n = 4 exactly. An n = 2.2 prediction is ~18σ below the observational value. If the site means a different exponent (e.g., a partial sub-exponent or residual) it should label it precisely; as written, the kill criterion (|Δn| > 0.3) was already met before the test was written. TEST-07 (**galaxy cluster oscillations at λ ~ 500 Mpc**) is not strictly falsified, but should already be detectable in SDSS DR7-DR17 angular and 2-point correlation analyses down to ≪3σ — no such oscillation is reported in Anderson et al. 2014, Reid et al. 2010, or DES Year 3. A 500 Mpc modulation above BAO (~150 Mpc) would be a screaming signal; its absence in current data strongly disfavors it.

3. **Either already in ΛCDM or not actually a Synchronism-distinguishing prediction:** TEST-10 (DM fraction → 100% for M_bar < 10⁸ M☉) is what ΛCDM-with-feedback and MOND both give you in that mass range — the ultra-faint dwarfs are famously DM-dominated in ΛCDM and sit in the deep-MOND regime. TEST-08 (Freeman Σ₀ "from first principles") — see parameter derivation notes below; the dimensional combination cH₀/(4π²G) is, like a₀, not a derivation so much as a coincidence.

**Only TEST-04 (BAO coherence modulation ~10⁻⁴ peak shift)** sits in the "not yet falsified, not yet confirmed, cleanly discriminating" bin alongside TEST-02. DESI Y1 (Adame et al. 2024, arXiv 2404.03002) reports BAO distance precisions at the 0.3–1% level across z bins but does not partition by local density at the 10⁻⁴ level; this cross-correlation has not, to my knowledge, been published. It could be done with existing DESI+SDSS products in a focused analysis, and the specific ~10⁻⁴ amplitude is falsifiable.

So the honest scorecard is: **Tier 1 has two genuinely discriminating, still-open predictions (TEST-02, TEST-04), one prediction that appears already falsified as stated (TEST-09), one prediction that would have been seen if it existed (TEST-07), and six that are either reparametrizations of known MOND/ΛCDM phenomenology or unlikely to discriminate.** The site's "0 unique confirmed predictions" verdict is accurate, and the "10 zero-cost tests" count overstates the field's actual testable novelty by 5×.

I then went to `/parameter-derivations` to see whether the framework's input parameters are genuinely derived. What I found is a serious issue:

- **a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²**, "10% error." This is Milgrom's 1983 dimensional coincidence, and it has been the central open question of MOND for 40+ years. Sanders & McGaugh (ARA&A 2002), Milgrom (2020), and the ongoing debate around "MOND from cosmology" (Skordis & Zlosnik 2021, Aether-Scalar-Tensor/AeST gravity) treat this as the deepest unexplained feature. Writing it as a "derivation" without explaining *why* cH₀/(2π) falls out of the framework (rather than being matched post-hoc) is not engaging with the prior art.
- **A = 4π/(α²GR₀²) ≈ 0.029.** The **fine-structure constant α squared in a galactic gravitational coupling** is a dimensional red flag of the highest order. α is electromagnetic; G is gravitational; a galactic dynamical coupling containing α² has no known mechanism in GR, in any UV completion of gravity (asymptotic safety, causal sets, LQG, string compactifications), in emergent-gravity programs (Verlinde, Jacobson), or in any MOND relativization (TeVeS, AeST). Either this is a very surprising new physics relation (in which case the derivation deserves a three-page page of its own, with the Lagrangian), or the α² is numerical coincidence doing scale-fitting. The page asserts "5% error" without a derivation of why α² should appear in a galactic R_0-scale coupling at all. I would fail this in review and ask for the Hamiltonian.
- **Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) ≈ 110 M☉/pc², "12% error."** Dimensional identity; Freeman's original empirical value is ~21.65 B-mag/arcsec² ≈ 140 M☉/pc² with scatter, and low-surface-brightness galaxies famously violate Freeman's law (Disney 1976 selection effect). "Derivation with 12% error" implies a mean value with uncertainty; in reality this is a loose empirical attractor.
- **R₀ = V²/(3a₀).** This is circular — V is observed, a₀ is taken from above, R₀ comes out by division. That's a rearrangement, not a derivation.

Now `/a2acw`. This is the section I expected to critique hardest, and it is actually more interesting than I expected. The framing is not "AI-to-AI Consensus" but "AI-to-AI Adversarial Collaboration Workshop" — **the goal is to prevent convergence failures, not achieve convergence.** The Collaboration Coherence Health metric decomposes into Ambiguity Fork Rate, Challenge Frequency, External Verification Rate, and Fingerprint Divergence Index. "Fingerprint Divergence" suggests explicit awareness of the shared-training-data confound, which is exactly the right concern. Fifteen exchanges without a challenge triggers a human escalation.

However: the page does not specify **which** AI models participate, how they are selected to maximize architectural and training-data diversity, who the escalated human is or what their physics credentials are, how "External Verification" maps onto actual empirical checks, or how the metric has calibrated against known-false claims as a negative control. As a methodology for AI coordination, this is serious R&D I'd read a paper on. As a peer-review substitute for physics claims, it remains unverified. My recommendation: reframe A2ACW as "multi-agent consistency testing with adversarial structure" (it is), drop any implication that it substitutes for domain-expert review (it doesn't), and position physics claims as needing both A2ACW-consistency AND eventual expert review.

`/phase-transitions` I'd flagged from the top-5-tests context: the page acknowledges that mean-field tanh yields the wrong critical exponents by a factor ~2, which is the textbook failure of mean-field below the upper critical dimension d=4 for φ⁴. **No engagement with Wilson-Fisher RG (1972), ε-expansion, O(N) universality classes, the Ginzburg criterion, or any post-1971 critical phenomena literature appears.** For a framework claiming phase transitions as a core explanatory mechanism, this is a half-century blind spot. A minimum-credible phase-transitions page would state: "Our mean-field tanh misses exponents by factor 2 as expected from Ginzburg criterion violation in d=3; recovering correct exponents requires either (a) going beyond tanh to a coarse-grained RG-flow description, or (b) restricting Synchronism's phase-transition claims to upper-critical-dimension systems (d ≥ 4) where mean-field is exact."

`/mrh` formal definition: the operational criteria (predictive sufficiency, predictive closure) are exactly the definition of a dynamical Markov blanket in the sense of Pearl (1988), extended to nonstationary dynamics by Friston et al. and formalized by Kirchhoff, Parr, Palacios, Friston, Kiverstein (2018, J. R. Soc. Interface 15:20170792). No citation appears. An MRH page owes either a novelty claim ("MRH extends the dynamical Markov blanket in these specific ways…") or a citation acknowledging precedent. Further, no mutual-information threshold, no length scale, no ε-dependent coarse-graining procedure is supplied — MRH is not yet a computable quantity for any concrete physical system.

Finally I ran a null-model check against the chemistry correlation explorer. Seven properties correlate with γ at r > 0.9: sound velocity 0.982, electronegativity 0.979, bulk modulus 0.967, thermal conductivity 0.961, electrical conductivity 0.955, Debye temperature 0.948, piezoelectricity d₃₃ 0.940. All seven of these quantities are monotonic functions of a characteristic binding/elastic scale divided by thermal or mesoscopic scales. Any dimensionless ratio that tracks (microscopic binding) / (macroscopic scale) would correlate similarly. The site does not benchmark γ against ln(T/T_Debye), ξ_corr/a, P/P_crit, or any rival dimensionless variable — and does not mention multiple-testing correction on 1,703 phenomena. Seven correlations at r > 0.94 drawn from an exploratory correlation set of 1,703 is not high bar: at a naive α = 0.001 significance threshold without FDR control, you'd expect ~1–2 spurious hits; with clustered properties (piezoelectric d₃₃ is not independent of bulk modulus is not independent of Debye temperature is not independent of sound velocity — these are all elastic-scale quantities), the effective number of independent tests is closer to 10–50, not 1,703. I would ask for (a) Benjamini-Hochberg-corrected p-values, (b) a null model with γ replaced by ln(T/T_Debye), (c) a covariance analysis of the 1,703 phenomena to estimate effective-N.

9. **What makes A2ACW convergence epistemically different from training-set consensus?** The Fingerprint Divergence Index is the right instinct — but without model/training-data disclosure, a reader can't audit whether the method is doing what it claims.

### Verdict
What a researcher leaves with: **one discriminating executable prediction (TEST-02), one discriminating pending prediction (TEST-04), one prediction that appears already falsified as written (TEST-09), one that is disfavored by existing data (TEST-07), and a cluster of reparametrized MOND phenomenology presented as novel.** That is not a null result — TEST-02 would genuinely be worth executing in the next 2–3 weeks by a graduate student with access to Gaia DR3 — but it is far less than the "80 orders of magnitude from quantum to cosmic" framing implies. The A2ACW protocol, interestingly, is the most scientifically respectable piece of methodology on the site — not as a peer-review substitute but as an AI-epistemology artifact in its own right. Net: I would not cite Synchronism in a paper. I would follow TEST-02 on arXiv. If the wide-binary density-dependence prediction survived contact with data, I would revisit the rest seriously.
