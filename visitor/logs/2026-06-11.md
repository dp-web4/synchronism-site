## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, first-encounter, core-idea, tools, coherence-explorer, galaxy-plotter, honest-assessment, why-synchronism, consciousness-threshold)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Way more honest than I expected from a "one equation explains everything" site, but the honesty itself left me confused about why I should keep reading.

### Journey

Clicked through from Reddit expecting either crackpot stuff or a cool Veritasium-style explainer. The landing page headline — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — grabbed me. The marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza analogy actually worked for me; I got "coherence" immediately from that. But right under the exciting headline it says "0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted by external data" and honestly my first reaction was: wait, so... it doesn't work? Why am I here? I kept reading mostly because the honesty was so unusual it felt like a hook in itself.

"Start Exploring" took me to First Encounter, which was the friendliest page — Step 1 of 7, good analogies, the Andromeda joke landed. But "Markov Relevancy Horizon" stopped me cold. The *idea* (only your nearest neighbors matter) is simple and I got it, but the name sounds like it was designed to scare me off. Also symbols like ρ and γ show up before I felt properly introduced to them — I just started mentally calling them "the density thing" and "the sharpness thing."

The Core Idea page repeated a lot of First Encounter but with more math. "tanh" still means nothing to me, but "S-shaped curve / dimmer switch" was enough to follow along. The stuff about "motivated, not derived" and "Ising model" and "demoted to Reparametrization" — I skimmed all that. "Reparametrization" appears everywhere on this site and I never found a plain-English sentence telling me what it means. I *think* it means "the equation can be massaged to match older theories so it's not really new"? Not sure. That word is doing a LOT of work on this site and I'm guessing.

Tools page was nicely laid out with Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced labels — I appreciated being told where to start. Tried the two Beginner tools (below). Then clicked "See What Failed" because that framing is irresistible. The Honest Assessment page is clearly the soul of the site but it's written for scientists — ΔBIC, σ₈, DESI DR1, SPARC — I understood maybe 40% of it. The plain-language paragraph ("we predicted the universe's large-scale structure should be growing slower... but real data shows it growing at or above standard-model rates") was great. I wanted the WHOLE page at that level.

Ended on the consciousness threshold page because, come on, consciousness. The claim that experience switches on at C ≈ 0.50 is the single most exciting thing on the site for someone like me — and also the page where the caveats ("entirely theoretical," "Synchronism-internal cross-checks, not eight independent measurements") most felt like they took the toy away right after showing it to me.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What does "reparametrization" actually mean in plain English? (Biggest one — it's the verdict word for the whole site and I'm only guessing.)
2. If the theory has 0 confirmed predictions and its big test failed, what is the site hoping I'll *do* — learn from it, contribute, just watch?

I'd come back if a friend sent me a specific page, but I probably wouldn't return on my own.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, learning-paths, glossary, interactive-tools, gamma-calculator, equation-walkthrough, honest-assessment, mrh, key-claims, test-catalog)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Strong documentation bones — consistent breadcrumbs, a genuinely comprehensive glossary, canonical badge definitions — but the badge taxonomy leaks badly in practice, and the site's most load-bearing term ("reparametrization") is defined only behind a link.

### Journey

I went through this the way I'd audit any doc set: nav structure first, then the reference pages (glossary, badge definitions), then spot-checks for consistency on content pages.

**Navigation and breadcrumbs**: genuinely good. Every deep page I visited has a consistent three-level breadcrumb ("Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator", "Home / Core Theory / MRH: Markov Relevancy Horizon", "Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap"). The header nav is stable across pages and the footer always offers exit routes. The landing page's audience-routing block ("No physics background? → ... Physicist? → ...") is a pattern I'd recommend to clients. One gap: the learning paths page has no "you are here" indicator once you're inside a path, so a reader three steps into Intermediate has to remember where they are.

**Glossary**: 59 terms, and every critical site-wide term I checked is there — reparametrization, MRH, MOND, all the badge labels, DESI, SPARC, N_corr, A2ACW. About 85% of entries cross-link onward. That's better coverage than most commercial doc sets I've worked on. But it is **not alphabetized** — "Phase Transition" is followed by "A2ACW"; "Mean-Field Theory" sits between "ALFALFA-SDSS" and "BCS". The grouping seems thematic, but there are no section headers saying so. A reader who arrives looking up one word can't scan for it.

**Badge taxonomy**: the Honest Assessment page has a "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" section that is exactly what a doc system needs — every badge with a one-line definition, deprecated ones flagged. The problem is everything downstream of it. On the very same page, content sections apply labels that aren't in the taxonomy at all: "Kill Criterion Triggered," "Disfavored ~2σ," "MOND-shared," "Dimensional Identity — Same Class," "Wrong Category," "Not Discriminating." Some of these may be deliberate prose, but they're styled and positioned like badges, and a reader can't tell which labels are canonical and which are improvised. "Withdrawn" appears in the deprecated/operational list with a do-not-use note yet is actively applied to the BAO test. The Test Roadmap page presents the badge guide and then applies it to *zero* of the 24 proposed experiments on that page.

**First-use discipline is inconsistent rather than absent**: Key Claims expands MOND, ΛCDM, SPARC, and RAR inline at first use — textbook practice — but the landing page uses MOND twice with no gloss, the tools index uses "SPARC galaxy" unexpanded, and Key Claims itself drops BTFR, CPMG, PRL, and T₂ with no expansion. So the site clearly *knows* the convention; it's applied page-by-page rather than site-wide.

**"Reparametrization"**: used 6 times on the landing page alone, as the badge on 4 of the 4 domain cards. Its definition exists (glossary + honest-assessment anchor) but never appears inline where the badge is used. The honest-assessment definition — "Equivalent to existing physics in different notation" — is one line, ten words. It would fit as a tooltip or a parenthetical under the first badge on every page. This is the highest-leverage ten words on the site.

**Terminology drift on "coherence"**: the site has clearly worked on this (the Claim 3 dark-matter section carries an explicit note: "'coherence' here means density-driven collective ordering... not quantum phase coherence"), but the disambiguation appears on *some* uses and not others on the same page. Claim 2 (consciousness) uses coherence/γ language with no equivalent note. When a term is overloaded, the disambiguation has to travel with every load-bearing use, not just one.

**MRH page**: the formal definition ("The minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system") is in a blockquote partway down, not at the top. It also frames MRH as correlation-decay-across-distance, while the First Encounter framing (per the landing page's framing and Pass 1's account) is "only your nearest neighbors matter." Those are reconcilable, but the page never reconciles them. And it uses "coherence evolution" before this page defines coherence.

**Test numbering**: the roadmap says "24 specific, falsifiable experiments" but only TEST-01 through TEST-10 (Tier 1) carry identifiers; the 14 tests in Tiers 2–4 are unnumbered on the roadmap. The summary table ("24 Proposed / 0 Pre-registered & Completed") is admirably blunt, but a reader can't map the count to a list. Key Claims also shows a badge/footer mismatch: Claim 3's badge reads "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision" while its own footer reads "Status: **Failed** — R² = 0.14 < 0.20 kill criterion." One of those is the status; pick it.

### Tools Tested

A two-day editorial sweep (inline badge tooltips, acronym first-use pass, badge-vs-prose styling rule, glossary headers) would fix most of what I found without writing a single new page.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, parameter-derivations, coherence-function, interactive-tools, honest-assessment, key-claims, dark-matter, gamma-calculator, phase-boundary-visualizer, chemistry-correlation-explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The self-audit is more rigorous than most published papers' limitations sections — the framework is dead by its own scoreboard and says so — but several front-facing tools still display parameter values the site's own audit has refuted.

### Journey

A colleague sent this with "interesting AI project," so I went in expecting either crackpottery or hedge-everything mush. It's neither. The landing page puts "0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted" directly under the grand headline, which bought enough goodwill that I went straight for the jugular: the parameter derivations page.

**Parameter derivations**: This page is the most honest thing on the site, and I checked it line by line. It states flat out: "Zero parameters have first-principles derivations with independent support. A more accurate title: Parameter Calibration & Honest Ansätze." The γ = 2/√N_corr entry openly admits the contradiction I was about to write down myself: CLT 1/√N scaling governs *iid* variables, but N_corr is by construction the count of *correlated* DOF — the exact regime where iid fails. The amplitude A ≈ 0.029 carries an "Audited-Negative — Chain-of-Custody Failure" badge documenting that the number came from a script using ρ_crit ∝ V^0.5 while the framework uses ρ_crit ∝ V², that the stated formula actually yields 4.6×10⁻⁵ (600× off), and that nobody re-ran it for ~600 sessions. I have never seen a project publish its own chain-of-custody failure at this level of detail. The a₀ = cH₀/(2π) and Freeman's Law Σ₀ entries are correctly classified as dimensional analysis / reparametrization (the cH₀ coincidence has been known since Milgrom 1983, and the page says so).

**Coherence function**: I verified the basic math. C(ρ_crit) at γ=2 is tanh(2·ln 2) = 15/17 ≈ 0.882 — and the site correctly calls ρ_crit a "saturation knee," not a transition midpoint (a trap a lot of sigmoid phenomenology falls into). C(10ρ_crit) ≈ 0.9999 as claimed. ✓ The classification of C(ρ) as a logarithmic compander (μ-law / Hill / Naka-Rushton lineage), explicitly "not an order parameter," is correct category work. Crucially, the page states the right disanalogy with Ising mean-field theory: m = tanh(βJzm) is *self-consistent* (m on both sides); C(ρ) is an explicit forward map with no feedback loop, hence no critical point, hence no universality classes. That's exactly the objection I came to make, pre-made.

**Honest assessment**: The quantitative results check out internally — DESI fσ₈ sign-wrong (predicted suppression to 0.418, LRG1 measured 1.16±0.13 *above* fiducial, 2.4σ), RAR transition shape refuted at ΔBIC = +184 with the free-γ fit converging to γ ≈ 0.49 ≡ MOND, cluster no-go (required ρ_crit for Coma is 10⁴–10⁶× off the galaxy value; one ansatz structurally bounded at velocity ratio ≤2 vs observed 4.6). The no-go is carefully scoped to ansätze "keyed on local volumetric density ρ(r)" — correctly *not* claiming to kill acceleration-keyed or enclosed-mass-keyed theories like MOND or Verlinde. That scoping discipline is rare.

**But here's where it unravels at the edges.** The free-γ fit of 0.49 implies, via the framework's own γ = 2/√N_corr, that N_corr ≈ (2/0.49)² ≈ 17 for galaxies. Yet the Phase Boundary Visualizer places "galaxies/ideal gas" at γ = 2.0, N_corr = 1 — the exact value the site's own ΔBIC = +184 audit refuted three weeks ago, with no mention of the refutation on the tool. A visitor who only opens the tools gets the refuted parameter presented as the placement. Same tool: BCS placement text says N_corr = 10,000 while quoting γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ — but 2/√10⁴ = 0.02, not 6×10⁻⁴; the quoted γ corresponds to the γ-calculator's preset of N_corr = 10⁷. Two tools, same system, three orders of magnitude apart, and the visualizer's own pair of numbers don't satisfy its own formula.

**Key claims**: The consciousness claim now correctly identifies C ≈ 0.50 as the arithmetic midpoint of tanh's output range — "a normalization artifact, not independent empirical evidence," with the eight-model convergence called "geometrically forced." Good. But the kill criterion proposed for it is "EEG phase coherence during anesthesia" — and the site spends substantial effort elsewhere (correctly!) insisting that C is density-driven collective ordering, *not* phase coherence, with BCS (maximal phase coherence) at C ≈ 0. The proposed falsification test measures the quantity the framework explicitly says C isn't. As written, the kill criterion can neither kill nor confirm the claim. Also: key-claims states a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², but cH₀/(2π) with H₀ ≈ 70 gives 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ (the value parameter-derivations and dark-matter correctly use, noting the ~10% miss vs Milgrom's observed 1.2). Rounding the derived value to the observed value makes a 10% miss read as an exact hit.

**Dark matter page**: The Bullet Cluster sign error (mechanism predicts high-viscosity dark matter, observation shows low-viscosity pass-through) is admitted in plain text, and the unaddressed-scope list (CMB peaks, lensing, LSS) is complete and damning — by the page's own admission, particle dark matter "remains far more comprehensive." One quibble: the honest-assessment RAR section says "2,807 SPARC galaxies" — SPARC contains 175 galaxies; 2,807 is almost certainly the data-point count. Sloppy units-of-analysis inflation, the only place I caught the site accidentally overstating its own evidence base.

### Tools Tested
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — worked — intuitive UI, problematic content — The slider and regime axis render fine, and to its credit the page does carry the correlation-sharpness inversion caveat ("small γ gives a nearly flat tanh curve... despite large N_corr") with a pointer to the γ Calculator. But the placements themselves are stale: galaxies at the refuted γ=2, BCS at an N_corr that contradicts both the companion tool and the tool's own displayed γ. As a physicist I'd trust the caveats and distrust the markers, which is backwards.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — worked — intuitive — Sort/filter over 23 hand-curated rows from 1,703 phenomena, with genuinely good warnings ("selecting the highest-correlation rows from a large pool inflates apparent effect sizes by construction"). What's missing is the one number that would settle it: what r does a generic monotone-in-density null achieve on the same targets? Without that baseline, r = 0.982 on sound velocity is uninterpretable — sound velocity is monotone in density for almost any sensible map.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Given that the free-γ RAR fit collapses to MOND (γ ≈ 0.49) and the cluster no-go rules out local-ρ keying, is there *any* observable left where C(ρ) and MOND + ΛCDM diverge? If not, what is the research program now — finding a non-local state variable?
2. Why is γ = 2/√N_corr retained at all? The site itself documents that it inverts the correlation→sharpness relationship (BCS, with a real sharp Tc, gets the flattest γ). What would a correctly-signed ansatz even look like, and has one been tried?
3. What observable *does* correspond to C, operationally? Every proposed test I found either measures phase coherence (which C isn't) or fits C's parameters to the data being predicted.
4. The honest assessment says the framework "documents what a density-based coherence function structurally cannot do." Is the no-go theorem itself being written up as the deliverable? It's the most defensible result on the site.
5. Where does the N_corr for any system actually come from? The γ-calculator admits every preset is a back-fit; the visualizer presents the same numbers as placements. Is there even a candidate protocol for computing N_corr from a Hamiltonian?

### Verdict
The badges are honest — strikingly so. Every quantitative claim I checked by hand (tanh values, the Ising disanalogy, the ΔBIC verdicts, the dimensional-analysis classifications, the C=0.50 midpoint artifact) is correctly stated, and the reparametrizations are not just identified but taxonomized. As physics, the framework is a logarithmic compander with back-fitted parameters whose every novel prediction has failed or collapsed onto MOND — and the site is the first place I've seen that says this about itself, in detail, with the receipts. The remaining sins are propagation sins: the interactive tools still display refuted and mutually inconsistent parameter values that the audit layer retired weeks ago, and the one consciousness kill criterion tests the wrong observable. I'd cite the honest-assessment methodology to colleagues as a model; I would not yet trust any number displayed in the tools without checking it against the audit pages.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, interactive-tools, test-catalog, honest-assessment, research-philosophy, tier-1-existing, what-synchronism-is-not, consciousness-demo, galaxy-plotter, open-questions→research-philosophy)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: no — but I would cite two specific things from it
- **Overall impression**: There is no new physics here and the site knows it; what's potentially citable is a controlled null result on AI-adversarial physics generation and a quantified locality no-go — but both are weakened by missing engagement with the literature that already exists around them.

### Journey

I came to answer three questions: is anything here actually new, does any prediction diverge from MOND + ΛCDM, and is A2ACW sound as a generation methodology. I skipped the pedagogy and went straight at the claims.

**Is anything new?** By the site's own front-page scoreboard — "0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted by external data," 0 of 6 "Validated" badges surviving expert audit — no. I verified this isn't false modesty: the Tier 1 page shows every galaxy-scale test either failed (TFR scatter R²=0.14 vs 20% kill threshold), collapsed onto MOND (free-γ RAR fit → γ≈0.49, RMS matching McGaugh to four digits, ΔBIC=+184 against the framework's γ=2), carries a MOND-shared flag (TEST-09, TEST-10 — correctly flagged as non-discriminating even if positive), or was withdrawn (BAO). The page concludes "Currently discriminating tests from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM: 0" and that is, as far as I can check, exactly right. The only formally novel prediction path admitted anywhere is an R(I) viscosity correction at ~10⁻⁸⁰ — Planck-scale, permanently untestable. So the answer to my second question is also no: there is no observable on this site where Synchronism and MOND+ΛCDM diverge measurably, and the site says so itself in those words.

**The wide-binary test (TEST-02)** is where I expected to catch an overclaim and instead found honest self-elimination — with a gap. The framework predicts a 0.05–0.4% velocity deviation in low-density environments vs MOND's ~18%; i.e., it predicts the *Newtonian null*, so Gaia can refute it but never confirm it against Newton. The page's EFE analysis (bounded-C(a) caps the boost at ~3.17× while 42% of SPARC RAR points need more — no parameter value keeps EFE distinctness AND RAR consistency) is genuinely sharp; "SELF-ELIMINATING-OR-TIE" is the right verdict. **But the page never mentions the live Chae vs. Banik dispute** — the actual ongoing Gaia DR3 wide-binary fight. Chae's claimed ~20% kinematic boost and Banik et al.'s Newtonian null are *the* dataset context for this test: if Chae's analysis stands, both Newton and Synchronism are refuted at once; if Banik's null stands, MOND is in trouble and Synchronism merely ties Newton. A page about a Gaia wide-binary test that doesn't cite the one active observational program on exactly that question reads as written from inside a bubble — which, given the generation methodology, it literally was.

**DESI currency.** The fσ₈ refutation rests entirely on DESI DR1 full-shape (arXiv:2411.12021, LRG1 fσ₈ ratio 1.16±0.13; σ₈=0.841±0.034). The internal numbers are consistent (0.474×1.16 ≈ 0.55, matching the tier-1 page's "observed ≈ 0.55"). But it's mid-2026: DESI DR2 is out, and DR2 full-shape growth constraints would tighten this verdict — likely in the site's disfavor-of-Synchronism direction, so engaging it costs nothing and buys currency. Bigger missed framing: predicting σ₈ ≈ 0.76 in late 2025 was not a random error — it was a bet on the S8 lensing tension (KiDS/DES) being physical. KiDS-Legacy and DESI full-shape have since moved against that camp. The site presents its DESI failure as a bare wrong-sign result; presented as "we sided with the S8-tension camp and the tension dissolved under us," it becomes a scientifically interesting documented bet, and the mechanism-class claim ("any coherence-damped growth-suppression framework sits in the same disfavored box") becomes a statement about a real corner of the literature rather than about one AI artifact.

**Is A2ACW sound?** As a generation methodology for physics: no, and the site's own controls prove it with unusual rigor — 3,308 sessions, 1.4% internal-survival yield, 0 novel survivors after expert audit, plus the two controls that impressed me most: a temporal-asymmetry test (0/6 later-demoted claims caught; median prior-art year ~1996) and a specificity framing ("A2ACW cannot distinguish 'novel' from 'rederived from the same training corpus'"). A controlled negative result on adversarial AI-pair science, with both sensitivity and specificity measured, is publishable in the AI-for-science methods literature — frankly it's the most novel artifact on the site. **But the methodology pages cite no prior art for the protocol itself.** Defender/Challenger adversarial AI pairs are a direct descendant of AI Safety via Debate (Irving, Christiano & Amodei 2018), and structured multi-agent role protocols (CAMEL, MetaGPT) and the multi-agent failure-mode literature are unmentioned. For a site this scrupulous about flagging its *physics* as reparametrization, presenting its *methodology* without a prior-art section is an unforced inconsistency — and a referee would catch it in the first pass.

**What's actually defensible here?** Two items. First, the locality no-go: the honest assessment correctly cites Milgrom's non-locality obstruction (astro-ph/0510117) up top and scopes its cluster result as "the quantified local-density instance" — clusters need ρ_crit 10⁴–10⁶× off the galaxy value; theories keyed on non-local variables (Verlinde's enclosed mass, acceleration-keyed MOND) explicitly escape. That scoping is correct and the quantification is a modest, real contribution — a useful triage lemma for anyone proposing density-keyed MOND mimics. Second, the A2ACW program-level null with controls. Neither requires the framework to be right; both survive it being wrong.

**Loose numbers an expert trips on:** the honest assessment's "47 research contributions" sidebar quotes a 6-variable MOND offset model (LOO R²=0.938) and a TFR-residual M/L predictor ("51.4% improvement on 14,437 galaxies") with no prior-art comparison — residual modeling on SPARC/ALFALFA is a worked field, and these read as exactly the kind of uncontextualized "contribution" the rest of the site has learned not to claim. Also the ALFALFA-SDSS sample is 14,585 galaxies on tier-1-existing and 14,437 on honest-assessment — pick one. (I independently confirm Pass 3's catches: SPARC is 175 galaxies / ~2,800 points, and cH₀/2π ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰, not 1.2.)

**Tools.** The Consciousness Threshold Demo is, to my surprise, the best-disclaimed speculative tool I've seen on any framework site: it states on the tool itself that the eight-approach convergence on C≈0.50 is "geometric, not empirical," that the approaches "share the same framework assumptions and are not independent," and that "no calibration procedure exists to map actual EEG, fMRI, or IIT-Φ measurements to the C-axis." That last admission concedes my main attack — the anesthesia literature *does* have an empirically calibrated threshold candidate (PCI* = 0.31, Casali et al.), and the framework has no map from C to it or any other measurable. Which sharpens Pass 3's point: with no calibration procedure, the "EEG phase coherence" kill criterion is doubly void — wrong observable *and* no map. As stated, Claim 2 is unfalsifiable, and one page (consciousness-demo) effectively admits this while another (key-claims) still presents a kill criterion. The Galaxy Plotter, viewed as an expert, is the *counterexample to Pass 3's propagation complaint*: it discloses on the tool itself that its violet curve "uses the same parametrization (γ=2) that the RAR ensemble test rejected at ΔBIC=+184," that ρ_crit is per-galaxy refit to a SPARC/MOND-derived V_flat, and that overlap with MOND "is what a reparametrization looks like." So audit-to-tool propagation is solved on one tool and absent on another (Phase Boundary Visualizer) — it's a discipline gap, not a capability gap.

### Tools Tested
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — intuitive — Best-disclaimed tool on the site: the C≈0.50 midpoint artifact, the non-independence of the eight approaches, and the absence of any EEG/fMRI/IIT-Φ→C calibration are all stated *on the tool*, not behind a link. What's missing is the consequence: those admissions make the framework's own kill criterion unrunnable, and the tool stops one sentence short of saying so.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — intuitive — Re-tested with expert eyes after Pass 1: this is the propagation success story. The tool itself discloses the ΔBIC=+184 rejection of the very γ=2 curve it plots, the per-galaxy ρ_crit refit, and the V_flat provenance from SPARC/MOND fits. Pity the Phase Boundary Visualizer (per Pass 3) doesn't meet the standard this tool sets two clicks away.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Has anyone run DESI DR2 full-shape against the fσ₈ kill criterion, or is the verdict frozen at DR1?
2. Where does the framework stand if Chae's wide-binary boost is confirmed — is "both Newton and Synchronism refuted together" stated anywhere?
3. Is the A2ACW null result being written up for the AI-for-science literature, with the Irving et al. debate lineage acknowledged? It's the one artifact here I'd referee favorably.
4. Who audits the "47 contributions" sidebar items (MOND offset model, TFR M/L predictor) — and against what null models?
5. Is there a protocol — even a sketch — for computing C from any measurable, in any domain? Every falsifiability claim on the site ultimately bottlenecks on this absence.

### Verdict
As physics, this is a closed case and the site closed it itself: zero discriminating observables against MOND+ΛCDM, the one structural novelty (local-density keying) ruled out by its own cluster analysis, correctly scoped under Milgrom's non-locality obstruction. As a methodological artifact it's more interesting than its physics: a 3,308-session controlled demonstration that adversarial same-corpus AI pairs filter consistency but cannot generate or detect novelty — measured, not asserted. I won't return as a physics reader; I would cite the locality no-go lemma and the A2ACW null if they were extracted into citable form, and the site's failure to engage the literature adjacent to its two best artifacts (Irving et al. for the methodology, Chae/Banik and DESI DR2 for the tests) is now its most fixable weakness.
