## The illicit transition

The CRT supports one invariant: **the readout does not alter the source.** That is non-invasive measurability, and it holds for a classical signal by construction. The whitepaper promotes it three times without argument:

1. **Epistemic to ontic.** "I don't know which phase I'll catch" becomes "reality is always definite" (§5.2). On a CRT the ignorance is removable, because one clock reading fixes every future sample. Quantum outcome statistics are not removable that way (Kochen-Specker, Bell).
2. **Readout-side variable to system-side variable.** On a CRT the quantity that varies (sampling window and phase) belongs to the recorder, and all the quantum-looking behavior comes from there. In QM the non-commuting structure belongs to the system's observables. The analogy has no counterpart to non-commutation: a CRT yields joint, simultaneous values (position, brightness, phase) for everything at once.
3. **"Nothing changes" becomes a law of nature.** That claim is non-invasive measurability (macrorealism). It is empirically testable via Leggett-Garg inequalities and has been violated in superconducting, photonic, and spin systems. You have already conceded physical measurement disturbs, so §5.1's "nothing about the screen changed" and §5.5's "no disturbance" are retracted by your own Round 3. The transition from "unchanged screen" to "unchanged quantum system" was the entire load-bearing step, and it is now unsupported.

**Forced re-scope:** the CRT may be cited only as *"an example of readout-dependent appearance in a classical, non-invasively sampled signal."* It cannot be cited as showing *why there is no quantum mystery*. The section title and the "Revolutionary Implications" block must go.

## The missing piece that blocks any replacement claim

A replacement theory needs a **phase-carrying, order-dependent readout rule**. That means a state object plus readout operators where measuring A then B differs from B then A, and where probabilities are fixed by something Born-like. Sync-timing alone has:

- no amplitude (no interference, which you conceded),
- no non-commutation (no uncertainty relations, no contextuality),
- no probability measure (§5.2's αᵢ are "illustrative" and explicitly not probabilities).

Once those are supplied, what has been built is the quantum formalism with a lattice label attached. The CRT is then doing no work, and "no quantum mystery" is inherited from whichever interpretation of that formalism is chosen. This is the dilemma: **either the sampling picture is classical (and fails Bell/KS/LG), or it is quantum (and the CRT is decoration).**

## Answers to your two questions

1. **"Harmless reinterpretation" as terminal status.** Acceptable only with caveats. As stated, "decoherence-like" is also an overreach, since the text has no pointer basis, no system-environment coupling, and no master equation. Without those, even the decoherence parallel is a label. Terminal status is therefore "heuristic vocabulary, zero empirical content beyond standard QM," and the headline claims must be demoted accordingly.
2. **C(T) = |sinc(ωT/2)|.** It is correct: a boxcar average of a single sinusoid has that residual amplitude, with phase set by window center. But it is a one-line filter fact, not CRT content. Real CRT flicker and banding come from persistence of vision, refresh aliasing, and raster geometry, none of which this law models. It is a fair statement of the analogy's boundary, not a test of the analogy. It also does not touch the Planck-scale parameters in the "Technical Implementation" table.
