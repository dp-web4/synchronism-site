**Short answer:** I don't know of a published model with a₀ = c·H₀·Ω_m^φ, and I didn't search. The golden-ratio exponent looks like numerology. The underlying idea, that a₀ is tied to cosmology, is well known.

**Arithmetic check.** With H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, cH₀ ≈ 6.8×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². Then 0.3^1.618 ≈ 0.143, so a₀ ≈ 0.97×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². That is about 20% below the stated 1.2×10⁻¹⁰, so the stated number doesn't follow from the formula. The two differ by more than the usual uncertainty in H₀ and Ω_m. The observed value is about 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻², e.g. from the radial acceleration relation (McGaugh, Lelli & Schombert 2016, PRL 117, 201101), so the formula undershoots it.

**Known results the construction resembles or overlaps with** (from memory, so citations are uncertain):
- **a₀ ~ cH₀ coincidence:** Milgrom 1983 (ApJ 270, 365, and the companion papers) noted it. He also gave a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π ≈ 1.1×10⁻¹⁰.
- **Λ-based version:** a₀ ~ c²√(Λ/3) ~ c·H₀·√Ω_Λ. Milgrom has discussed this, and Famaey & McGaugh's Living Reviews (2012, LRR 15, 10) cover it. It gives a similar magnitude.
- **Other cosmological links for a₀:**
  - Verlinde 2017 (SciPost Phys. 2, 016), emergent gravity and de Sitter entropy, which gives a₀ ~ cH₀/6.
  - Modified-inertia/Unruh-type horizon arguments (Milgrom 1999, Phys. Lett. A 253, 273).
  - Smolin's 2017 paper on MOND and the cosmological constant (Phys. Rev. D 96, 083523).
  - Various papers with a₀ = cH₀/(2π) or cH₀/(4π) and similar factors (uncertain).
- **Ω_m-dependent forms:** a few papers fit a₀ ∝ cH₀ Ω_m^n or Ω_Λ^n (uncertain), but I can't cite one with a golden-ratio exponent.

**Assessment.**
- Any a₀ = cH₀·f(Ω_m) formula is a one-parameter fit. With Ω_m uncertain at about ±0.01 and H₀ at the 5–8% level (the Hubble tension), many exponents and prefactors can match. φ has no known derivation from MOND or cosmology, so I'd treat the match as numerology unless the program derives the exponent from a mechanism.
- The program should fix the numerical inconsistency. cH₀Ω_m^φ gives ≈1.0×10⁻¹⁰, not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. It should also compare against cH₀/2π ≈ 1.1×10⁻¹⁰, which has no free exponent and fits comparably well.
- The formula has a time-dependence problem. If Ω_m and H both evolve, a₀ would run with redshift. That is a testable prediction, and the program should state it. Observations of high-z rotation curves and the high-z Tully–Fisher relation constrain it.

I'd call the result unpublished as far as I know, but only moderately confident of that, since I didn't search.
