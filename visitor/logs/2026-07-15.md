## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary

- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The friendliest "we might be wrong" science site I've ever seen — but after an hour I couldn't tell you what I'm supposed to *hope* is true.

### Journey

Clicked in from Reddit. The landing headline — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — is a great hook, very Veritasium-thumbnail. The marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza analogy landed instantly; I actually get what "coherence" is supposed to mean from that alone. Then the equation appeared (C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))) and I bounced off it — "tanh" means nothing to me — but the "Beginner Path (6 steps, ~30 min, no equations required)" button was right there, which felt like the site anticipating me.

Step 1 (/why-synchronism) reads fine without math. The S-curve idea and crowd→band metaphor carry it. But it hit me with "zero confirmed predictions and the decisive galaxy test collapses to MOND" before I even knew what a prediction here would look like. I don't know what MOND is at this point (the glossary later told me — the definition there is actually good). It's like a movie trailer that opens with the review scores.

Step 2 (/first-encounter) was the best page of my visit. Plain language, one new term (MRH, "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" — genuinely nice phrase), clear next button, no equations. If the whole beginner path is like this, it works.

/core-idea is where the beginner path stopped feeling beginner. The "dimmer switch" explanation of tanh helped, but then "Ncorr," the Ising model, and "phenomenological, not derived" showed up and I was skimming. I noticed there's nothing to *play* with on that page — this is exactly where I wanted a slider.

So I went looking for toys. The Interactive Tools nav item took me to the galaxy plotter and the γ calculator (I first guessed /calculator by typing a URL and got a 404 — my own fault, the nav is fine).

The galaxy plotter is the highlight of the site for someone like me. Pick a real galaxy, see the gray line ("what visible matter predicts") sag below the blue dots that stay flat — "the gray line sagging below the dots is the dark matter puzzle." I have watched maybe five YouTube videos about dark matter and this one chart taught me more than most of them. It even explains rotation curves from scratch ("like planets in the solar system — the further out, the slower"). But: five lines is a lot, and two of them are violet (the real equation "labeled as failing" and a "hand-tuned stand-in"). As a casual visitor I genuinely couldn't work out why the framework's own curve is shown failing while a fake tuned version is shown succeeding — I get that it's honest, but the chart doesn't tell me what I should conclude from that.

The γ calculator was a stranger experience. Before I could touch it, three numbered warnings told me the formula isn't derived, the direction is "backwards," and the presets are "back-fits, not measurements." The page literally says to treat outputs as "what the inverted formula predicts rather than as physical estimates." So... I typed some numbers, watched γ change, and learned nothing, because the tool told me not to believe it. Honest, yes. But why is a knowingly-broken calculator a featured interactive tool for newcomers? It felt like a museum exhibit with a sign saying "this exhibit is wrong."

/hard-problem pulled me in because consciousness content is exactly my YouTube diet. The one-sentence version ("phase patterns... ARE experience, not correlates of experience") is a cool idea I could repeat at a party. But the page immediately lists three reasons it can't be tested, and I left thinking "so this is philosophy with Greek letters." Respectably hedged, but as a casual reader I wanted at least one "here's what would prove it" I could understand.

Ended at /honest-assessment because the landing page kept advertising it. It's enormously long and the middle is way over my head (fσ₈? BIC?), but the tone is unforgettable — "reads like an autopsy written by the patient." The phrase "the honest map of what failed — and why — is the actual product" is the closest thing I found to the site telling me what it's actually for.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /learning-paths, /interactive-tools, /glossary, /research-philosophy, /key-claims, /honest-assessment, /coherence-explorer, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /mrh)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I didn't evaluate the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Well-built information architecture with one systemic defect — the status-label system has quietly become three overlapping vocabularies, and the site's own summary numbers contradict its own corrections.

### Journey

I did this methodically: landing page first, mapping every nav item, then the index pages (learning paths, tools, glossary), then the pages the badge links point to, then two tools, then one deep concept page (/mrh) to spot-check terminology.

**Navigation and breadcrumbs are genuinely good.** Every page I visited had a working breadcrumb trail (Home / Interactive Tools / Coherence Explorer, etc.). The three learning paths each state audience and prerequisites explicitly ("No physics background needed" / "Undergrad science background" / "Grad-level physics") — this is textbook progressive disclosure and most science sites don't bother. The Beginner path steps match what the landing page advertises. The footer nav mirrors the header. No broken links found in ~40 checked.

**The glossary is the best-documented part of the site.** ~130 entries, alphabetized with a stated sorting convention ("Greek-symbol terms sort after the Latin alphabet"), predominantly plain-language-first ("Baryon — protons, neutrons, and everything made of them — the ordinary matter you can touch"), and nearly every entry cross-links to a deeper page. Every acronym I went hunting for (MOND, SPARC, DESI, RAR, ΛCDM, fσ₈, BAO, ALFALFA, CFD, A2ACW, N_corr) is present. The problem is upstream of the glossary: the landing page *uses* SPARC, DESI, ΛCDM, fσ₈, BAO, CFD, and ALFALFA-SDSS in body text without expansion or inline links. The reference exists; the pointer to it doesn't. A2ACW, notably, IS expanded on first use — so someone knows the rule; it's just applied inconsistently.

**The badge system is where the architecture is breaking down.** I traced status labels across four pages and found what is effectively three vocabularies:

1. The **formal taxonomy** on /research-philosophy and /honest-assessment: five MRH-relationship tags (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative) + four descriptive tags (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed) + two deprecated (Validated, Strongly Supported). This part is clean and the two pages agree with each other.
2. The **"operational states"** on /honest-assessment: Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, and more — documented as "not badges," but they render in the ledger where badges render, and there's no legend distinguishing the two classes at the point of use. Plus free-text descriptors ("Naturalness Gap," "Wrong Category," "Dimensional Identity," "Ontological Reframe") that appear in ledger entries and are defined nowhere central.
3. The **tools-page grouping label** "Model Explainer" on /interactive-tools, which appears in exactly the visual position where Reparametrization/Speculative badges appear on sibling cards, and which the page itself has to disclaim: "this is a *content grouping*, not the site's validation badge taxonomy." When your copy needs a disclaimer explaining that a label that looks like a badge isn't a badge, the fix is visual differentiation, not more copy.

A first-time reader cannot reliably tell which of these three classes any given label belongs to. The taxonomy documentation is good; the *application* has drifted.

**The stale-summary problem.** /key-claims states "0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted by external data" in its header — and then a dated correction (2026-07-09) further down the same page retracts the refutation ("The registered TEST-03 environment-density test... has never actually been run"). I can't judge the physics, but as a document this is a header contradicting its own body. Worse, the "1 refuted by external data" phrase also appears verbatim at the bottom of /coherence-explorer — so the stale number is syndicated to at least one other page. Summary lines that get repeated across pages need a single source, or corrections will always lag somewhere.

**Terminology spot-check on MRH.** The site defines MRH four different ways depending on entry point: the landing page expands the acronym but doesn't explain it; the beginner path calls it "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"; the glossary says "minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom" (and its Presence entry defines ρ *in terms of* MRH, which is circular); /mrh itself opens with a formal definition ("minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system") with no plain-language paraphrase — and the nice "bubble of neighbors" phrasing from the beginner path never appears on the concept's own page. These aren't contradictory, but they're not unified either. Pick the plain phrase, lead with it everywhere, and let the formal definition follow.

**Tools.** Both tools I tested worked. The Coherence Explorer is the better documented of the two: clear "What to notice" guidance, consistent terminology (γ, ρ_crit, "saturation knee" all match the rest of the site), honest terminology caveat for physicists. Its gaps: ρ has no units anywhere (dimensionless? normalized? say so), and the "0 confirmed predictions, 1 refuted" footer inherits the stale number noted above. The Phase Boundary Visualizer has a self-contradicting title: the page copy explicitly says "'boundary' on this page means a regime boundary along the γ axis, not a phase transition... despite the historical name." If the name is historical and wrong, rename the page — a title that needs an in-page rebuttal is naming debt. Also its most extreme example (BCS, γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴) sits "three orders of magnitude off the left edge" of the visible axis, i.e., the tool can't display its own most dramatic data point.

One smaller thing: /research-philosophy deprecates "Validated" and "Strongly Supported" but never says where they still appear live or that migration is in progress — a deprecation notice without a migration status invites the reader to treat any live instance as an error.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /equation-walkthrough, /for-researchers, /honest-assessment, /interactive-tools, /two-reframes, /galaxy-plotter, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /key-claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — as a case study in negative-results reporting, not as a framework to work on
- **Overall impression**: The math I could check is correct and the badges mostly *under*-claim — but the one place overclaiming survives is the tie badge, and I found a tie ("MOND-shared, cannot discriminate") that the site's own ledger logically contradicts two rows down.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project," so I skipped the beginner material and went straight for the derivations. The landing page already told me most of what I needed: the equation is admitted phenomenological, γ = 2/√N_corr is flagged "sign-inverted vs. mean-field physics and audited-negative" *on the landing page itself*, and ρ_crit is explicitly called a "saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)." That last one preempted my first planned attack — I came in ready to ask where the critical exponents were, and the site had already filed that comparison under "category error: C(ρ) is analytic everywhere with no critical point." Fair enough. That's the correct self-diagnosis and most amateur frameworks never make it.

**/parameter-derivations** is the most honest page with that title I've ever seen: it opens by admitting "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations" and suggests its own title should be "Parameter Calibration & Honest Ansätze." I checked what's checkable:

- The **sign-inversion critique of γ = 2/√N_corr is physically correct**, and it's devastating. 1/√N is a fluctuation *width* — it belongs in the argument of an error function describing how sharp a transition is, where more correlated units → narrower relative fluctuations → sharper transition. Sitting in the *rate* slot of tanh, it does the opposite: BCS (N_corr ~ 10⁷, one of the sharpest transitions in nature) gets γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, the flattest curve on the chart, while an ideal gas (no transition at all) gets the sharpest. The CLT objection (N_corr counts *correlated* DOF, exactly where iid fails) is also right. The site states all of this itself. As a condensed-matter person: once you've conceded this, γ has no physical content left — it's a per-fit shape knob.
- The **Ising disclaimer is exactly right**: "The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen." The mean-field self-consistency loop m = tanh(βJzm) is what makes the Ising tanh mean something — a fixed-point equation with a bifurcation. Here there's no loop, no free energy, no order parameter symmetry. Correctly not claimed.
- The **A ≈ 0.029 amplitude entry** is a chain-of-custody autopsy: stated formula gives 4.6×10⁻⁵ (600× off), the only computation producing 0.0294 uses ρ_crit ∝ V^0.5 while the framework asserts V², and MOND-matching would require V⁻². Three incompatible provenances, none derived. Badged Audited-Negative. Honest.
- **a₀ = cH₀/(2π)** correctly badged Reparametrization with prior art (Milgrom 1983, McCulloch, Verlinde, Smolin). **Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG)** correctly flagged as "the a₀ row propagated through a linear relation, not a second derivation" — and the warning that a 0.5% match against Freeman's law (known to a factor ~2) is "precision theater" is the kind of sentence referees write, not proponents.

**/equation-walkthrough**: I verified the Hill identity by hand. tanh(γ ln(1+x)) with e^(γ ln(1+x)) = (1+x)^γ gives exactly [(1+x)^(2γ)−1]/[(1+x)^(2γ)+1] — correct, and it matters: the mystique of "tanh of a log" evaporates into a garden-variety Hill/Naka–Rushton rational function. Also verified C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = tanh(2 ln 2) = 15/17 ≈ 0.882 — matches the site's claim that ρ_crit is near saturation, not a midpoint. And γ = 2/√N_corr with N_corr ≥ 1 does bound γ ≤ 2, consistent with /key-claims' usage of that bound to catch a mislabeled B = 10.82. The arithmetic on this site is sound everywhere I probed.

**/two-reframes** (CHSH): the quantum bookkeeping is right. Classical bound 2, Tsirelson 2√2, PR box 4 — correct. Their local construction landing at S = 1.98 ≤ 2 is exactly what a local-realist model must do; the "nonlocal-grid" construction pinned at S ≡ 2.00 with no signaling is correctly read as a local model in disguise; and reaching 2.67 only with signaling is the expected failure mode. The triptych framing (real-local = 2 < cos² projection = 2√2 < PR = 4, with "no derivation of 2√2 from substrate dynamics exists; it enters only by importing the answer") is an honest statement of where the framework actually stands: it pays Bell's price rather than evading it. The page even concedes the Kochen–Specker contextuality problem for its deterministic-scanning picture. I have nothing to add — which is remarkable, because Bell-adjacent pages on alternative-physics sites are where I usually find the bodies.

**The one real catch — TEST-09's tie badge contradicts the ledger it sits in.** The /honest-assessment ledger badges the BTFR test "Reparametrization / MOND-shared — cannot discriminate Synchronism from MOND; both predict identical behavior." Two rows down, the same ledger's strongest entry says the framework's gravitational boost is capped at B_max ≈ 3.17, that 579 SPARC galaxies exceed this, and that the deepest bin needs ⟨B⟩ = 10.82 — calling it "the strongest direct refutation in internal audit." These two entries cannot both be right. MOND's BTFR slope of 4 is not an accident: in the deep limit ν(g_N/a₀) diverges as √(a₀/g_N), which is precisely what turns M ∝ V² into M = V⁴/(Ga₀). A framework whose boost saturates at ~3.17 cannot follow that divergence — its deep limit is Newtonian-times-a-constant, which gives slope → 2, not 4. So the BTFR is exactly a regime where a bounded-boost model *must* diverge from MOND, and the ceiling entry already establishes the bound binds on real SPARC data. "Cannot discriminate" isn't a modest claim here — it's an unfalsifiable one, and it's the only badge type on the site that protects the framework from a test instead of exposing it to one. The tie badge should be re-derived or the test actually run; my strong suspicion is it fails with the same data that killed the ceiling.

**Tools** (physics-representation check, below) were mostly honest, with one propagation gap: the galaxy plotter's ρ_crit = 0.029·V²_flat is the very V⁺² scaling that /parameter-derivations flags as sign-inverted (MOND-matching requires V⁻²), and the plotter's disclaimers (A's chain-of-custody, ΔBIC = +184) don't include that one. A reader of the tool alone would miss that the scaling law painted on the screen is the one the audit says has the wrong sign.

One numeric loose thread: /honest-assessment quotes Σ₀ ≈ 119 M☉/pc² while /parameter-derivations computes 123 (I get 123.3 from a₀/(2πG) with their a₀, so the derivations page is right). Looks like a partially-propagated arithmetic correction.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Has anyone actually run the BTFR through the bounded-boost model on SPARC? The tie badge asserts the answer without the computation, and the ceiling entry already contains everything needed to predict the outcome (slope → 2, kill).
2. Is there *any* system for which N_corr is measured independently rather than asserted or back-solved from a fitted γ? Every placement I checked (BCS 10⁷, water ~4, galaxies 1 or 17) was an input, not a measurement. Without one anchored rung, γ = 2/√N_corr is unfalsifiable even before the sign problem.
3. What exactly does "1 refuted by external data" refer to, canonically? I found at least two candidate identities across pages.
4. The site rejects the mean-field label (correctly — no self-consistency loop), but then what *selects* tanh∘ln over the rest of the compander family it names (μ-law, Hill, logistic)? ### Verdict
The mathematics is sound everywhere I could check it — the Hill identity, the 0.88 saturation value, the γ bound, the CHSH arithmetic, the γ placements — and the self-criticism is not performative: the sign-inversion and chain-of-custody audits are the kind of critique I'd write as a hostile referee, published by the framework about itself. What's left after the audits is essentially nothing physical: zero derived parameters, a shape knob with inverted physics, and galaxy fits that converge to MOND when freed. The residual danger isn't overclaimed successes — it's overclaimed *ties*: "MOND-shared / cannot discriminate" badges that quietly exempt a test from execution, when the site's own boost-ceiling result implies at least one of those ties (BTFR) is actually a loss.
