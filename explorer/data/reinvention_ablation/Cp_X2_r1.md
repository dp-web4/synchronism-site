**Short answer:** I haven't found this exact construction in the literature, and I didn't run a search. I believe it is unpublished, but I'm not certain. The relation a₀ = c·H₀·Ω_m^φ looks like numerology unless the program derives the exponent φ from a mechanism.

**Arithmetic check.** The stated value doesn't follow from the formula.
- c·H₀ = 3×10⁸ × (70 / 3.086×10¹⁹ s⁻¹) ≈ 6.8×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻².
- 0.3^1.618 ≈ 0.143.
- The product is ≈ 9.7×10⁻¹¹ m s⁻², about 20% below the quoted 1.2×10⁻¹⁰.

The empirical a₀ is about 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻². For comparison, the simpler cH₀/2π ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ is closer to it than this formula is. Any fit of an arbitrary exponent to Ω_m = 0.3 is also very sensitive to the uncertainties in Ω_m and H₀. With H₀ = 73 and Ω_m = 0.3 the formula gives about 1.0×10⁻¹⁰, and with Ω_m = 0.315 it gives about 1.05×10⁻¹⁰. The result is therefore not robust.

**Known related results.** These are the published coincidences and models that this construction would reduce to or be compared with:
- Milgrom 1983 (ApJ 270, 365, and the companion papers) noted a₀ ~ cH₀. He also noted a₀ ~ c²√(Λ/3), the cosmological-constant coincidence. Both are standard.
- cH₀/2π: this appears in Milgrom's later reviews. See Famaey & McGaugh 2012, Living Rev. Relativ. 15, 10 (arXiv:1112.3960). I'm fairly sure of this citation.
- a₀ ≈ c√Λ/(2π)-type or cH₀√Ω_Λ-type relations appear in several papers. Examples are Milgrom's "MOND as vacuum effect" idea, and Verlinde 2016/2017, "Emergent Gravity and the Dark Universe" (SciPost Phys. 2, 016; arXiv:1611.02269), where a₀ = cH₀/6 comes from de Sitter entropy. I'm fairly sure of Verlinde's citation. I'm less sure of the other papers (uncertain).
- Models that tie a₀ to Ω_m or Ω_Λ through a power law are unfamiliar to me. Several papers on "MOND from the cosmological horizon" and on a Λ-dependent a₀ exist, but I can't cite specifics (uncertain).

**Golden ratio.** I know of no accepted physical derivation of MOND or a₀ using φ as an exponent. Papers on golden-ratio cosmology, such as El Naschie's E-infinity work, are fringe. I can't name one that treats a₀ (uncertain).

**Recommendation.** Before treating the result as novel, fix the arithmetic. Then test the relation against the Planck and SH0ES parameter uncertainties. Compare it with the simpler cH₀/2π and with Verlinde's cH₀/6. Finally, show a dynamical origin for the exponent φ. Without that origin, it is a one-parameter fit.
