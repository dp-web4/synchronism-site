### Journey
I came from a Reddit link expecting another person who solved physics. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", read like exactly that. The next bold line, "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated.", stopped me, and that's the reason I kept reading. I've never seen a theory site open by saying it lost. Learning that AI agents did the work, with one human directing and no journal or outside physicist checking it, was also surprising and made me curious.

The crowd versus marching band picture landed immediately. I got it: sparse things act alone, packed things act together. The route guidance pointing non-physicists somewhere was welcome.

Then the landing page kept going, and going. Below the fold I hit an abbreviation key, a table of TEST-numbers, "A2ACW", a "1.4% internal-consistency survival rate", and a line about 4–8 refutations depending on counting rules. I skimmed all of it and took in only one thing: 0 confirmed, 6 refuted. "So is the idea alive or dead?" was a great heading, and I'd have liked to reach it much sooner.

I took "Start here" to /why-synchronism. The top half was the best writing on the site for me. "What happened, in three lines" is the thing I'd show a friend, and "you can stop here" felt like permission rather than a dead end. I only learned what MOND was here: a 1983 rival idea that tweaks gravity rather than adding dark matter. That made the landing page's "collapsed onto MOND" make sense in hindsight.

/first-encounter promised 10 minutes and started well. A lone electron versus a neutron star is a fun contrast, the dimmer switch finally made "tanh" mean something to me, and "predicted means fixed before looking, fitted means a knob was turned" is a distinction I'll actually keep. The predicted +0.55 versus measured −0.55 was memorable too. But Section C is where I started to drift. I was being taught a γ formula while being told it failed, then BEC and BCS showed up unexplained, and I used ρ_crit in Section B without learning what it was until Section E. "A about 0.029 in these units, not derived from anything" is refreshingly blunt, but I didn't know what to do with it.

The Galaxy Plotter was the page where the point of it all became concrete. "Dots, gray line, green line" is a reading instruction I can follow, and the sentence "the only version of this framework's equation that fits these dots is MOND's formula written differently" is the clearest statement of the verdict I saw anywhere. But the page runs on after that with a parsimony note, a γ note, a mass-model note and formulas. It also told me that the default galaxy has a ρ_crit of 64 M☉/pc³, which meant nothing to me.

The γ Calculator was where I got properly confused. It's labelled Beginner, and it opens by telling me it's a failed formula kept to show how it fails. Fine, but I don't yet know what γ is for, so watching it fail doesn't mean much. Worse, it says giving a superconductor the flattest curve is backwards, because that's the most collective system. Yet the glossary's Coherence entry says superconductors "sit at LOW C", which sounds like exactly what the flat curve shows. I couldn't reconcile those on my own.

/what-synchronism-is-not has a good five-bullet "In plain words" opener, then quickly moves into "c_μν ~ α/π" territory. The glossary's "Start with these ten" is a very good idea. Past it, the entries I read are written for specialists (Källén-Lehmann, Naka-Rushton, Friedmann equation).

/where-its-useful looked like the answer to "so why should I care?" and was where I'd have bounced. The words "load-bearing", "Active-MRH", "AGPL", "SAGE", "IRP" and "hestia" meant nothing to me, I couldn't tell what any of these things actually do, and the evidence was links to what seem to be the same people's repositories. I respected the note that this is not evidence for the physics, but I left without one concrete example of something being better because of this.

### Journey
I started with the header: ten items, each with a useful hover description, including counts ("123 terms defined", "26 tests by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)"). /learning-paths is well made, with seven paths, stated step counts and a clear "each path builds concepts sequentially". The Beginner path's per-step times add up to its stated total (8+10+6+9+4 = ~37 min), and step bars on the pages I checked match the path list.

Then I went looking for the badge definitions. The system is real: nine badges in two families, with one canonical table at /honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions, linked from the glossary and from pages that use them. Badges on the tool pages I read carry an ⓘ. That's more discipline than I usually see. The cost is that the label system has grown past the badges: the glossary itself refers to "All eight label systems". Operational states, lifecycle verbs, tool kinds, levels, finding descriptors and ledger buckets are all separate vocabularies, and test cards combine several at once. The site has to spend a lot of text explaining what its labels are not ("not a badge", "not a level", "not a lifecycle verb").

The real trouble showed up when I compared the pages that describe the tests. /test-catalog contradicts itself on how many cards /tier-1-existing holds. It also still says something about TEST-10 that /tier-1-existing records having corrected on 2026-11-06. That correction note says the old sentence contradicted the card, but the same sentence survives on the sibling page. TEST-26 is "catalog-registered" on one page and "PROPOSED, NOT REGISTERED" on the other, even though /test-catalog has a box defining "registered" carefully. For a site whose main asset is exact bookkeeping, this is the failure that costs the most trust.

/test-catalog, /tier-1-existing and the tool pages are otherwise navigable. Breadcrumbs appear on every page I checked except the landing page, where none is needed. Forward links are good: /tier-1-existing ends with "Tier 2: Pilot Experiments →" and "Back to Test Roadmap". But the same page has three names: nav and H1 say "Test Roadmap", the URL says /test-catalog, and child breadcrumbs say "Predictions & Experiments".

The acronym picture splits in a pattern. The site has good keys (landing, /tier-1-existing, a list in /for-researchers), but they often come after the terms' first use, so the reader meets the term first and the key later. Pages entered directly suffer most, and a citation-minded reader will enter /for-researchers directly. Its opening line uses MOND and ΛCDM unexpanded, and the part I read never expands A2ACW or defines "Bucket 0".

### Verdict

I'd fix the three test-status conflicts today, then invest in single-sourcing status data and shrinking the reader-facing label vocabulary, since more explanatory prose would only add more copies to drift.

A framing point sits underneath this. Pinned γ = 2 is presented as a non-MOND law: "Pin γ=2 → refuted. Fit γ → MOND." So refutation #3 selects among μ-functions and does not test a rival to MOND. The reparametrization verdict is therefore stronger than the badge says ("exactly MOND's simple μ"), and the refutation counts for less than its ledger row suggests.

That also makes this sentence on /equation-walkthrough false as a class statement: "Without it, tanh(γx) has an exponential tail and matches no MOND function." μ(x) = tanh(x) has the correct limits (μ → x, μ → 1) and is a legitimate μ. McGaugh's own ν approaches Newton as a stretched exponential, so an exponential-type tail isn't foreign to MOND phenomenology. What the ln does is put the function in the simple-μ family. Then I turned to my own field, and this part was the least satisfying. The landing page says "a superconductor scores low" on C, and /gamma-parameter says a BCS superconductor "ends up at C ≈ 0". A function of density alone can't see that transition. The only way it enters is by assigning a different N_corr by hand, and the site says N_corr has no measurement protocol. To get its 607 K for YBCO from any plausible gap needs η < 1, and the measured 93 K needs η > 1. I don't recognize this η as AG, and I couldn't check what it is from that page.

**A prior-art identity I didn't find on /dark-energy.** The γ > ½ branch is emergent dark energy: density falls into the past, w approaches −2γ, phantom throughout. At γ = 1 the identity is exact. I checked this numerically to 2×10⁻¹⁵ for z < 5 and it also follows algebraically. **The identity is in the functional form, not the model**: GEDE fixes z_t by ρ_DE = ρ_m, which in the family occurs at z ≈ 0.24, while the form-matching transition is at z ≈ 0.73. Other γ match less well (best-fit GEDE differs by 1–2% at γ = 0.55–0.7 and ~21% at γ = 2, z < 3). "Emergent", "PEDE", "GEDE", "Shafieloo" and "H₀ tension" don't appear on /dark-energy. This is a small correction with a closing use: that literature's motivation is H₀, and the site's own fit (γ = 0.487 −0.021/+0.024) excludes γ = 1 at about 20σ if the errors are taken at face value. So a phantom H₀ rescue is closed for this family, and the page could say so in a sentence. This belongs on the /a2acw prior-art list, which has four entries.

**Quantum foundations.** This is honest and mostly empty, which is a respectable place to be. "There is no decoherence rate, no Lindblad form, and no apparatus calibrated to Ncorr" tells me the measurement page doesn't compete with CSL or Diósi–Penrose. The diffusion-channel bounds are better than I expected. The weak spot is how the Bell work is counted. The landing row reads "S ≤ 2 on every arm, no signaling", while /two-reframes reports a third arm reaching "S up to 2.67" with signaling. The nonlocal-grid arm stays at S ≡ 2.00 because "a uniform shared phase only adds a constant offset", which the page calls gauge-equivalent to relabeling angles. So that arm never carried setting information across the wing. It's a demonstration that this coupling can't test the nonlocal horn, more than a test of that horn. The landing narrative rightly calls these "construction nulls, not a theorem about the substrate class". Yet the row is counted as a refutation, and it is one of the four that "survive every rule" (#2, #4, #5, #6). Of those four, #6 has no data, #5 is inherited from MOND, #4 is a refuted registration, and only #2 attacks framework-specific content on data, conditional on the gas census. Reporting failure this firmly is admirable, but a null from a weak construction shouldn't sit in the robust floor. /two-reframes also cites the scan picture's conflict with Zeno and Kochen–Specker, but Leggett–Garg appears there only in a source filename, though it is the natural frame for "non-invasive sampling". /key-claims does mention it.

**Is A2ACW sound for generating physics?** On the site's own evidence, no, and it says so: about 3,300 sessions produced zero refutations on their own, and code run against outside data produced five of the six. No session cites its four prior-art matches, including the galaxy formula itself (Famaey & Binney 2005). So the generator's most basic failure is a missing literature search, which adversarial roles between LLMs can't fix. As an audit tool it is unproven. Under the literal rule, sensitivity 3/3 and specificity 0/6 give a likelihood ratio of 1 for a "flagged" verdict. In that case, "0 of 9 survived" carries no information about the claims, whatever its Clopper–Pearson bound. The steelmanned rule gives J = 1.0, so the headline's evidential weight rests on the choice of rule until the post-cutoff arm runs. The 9 are also selected claims, not a sample. Run-to-run agreement of 0.86 against cross-vendor 0.41–0.52 suggests many verdicts depend on the rater. The CCH health metric can't reach its "Healthy" threshold (maximum 0.49) and no script computes it, which the page discloses. I'd delete it. **The method that actually produced knowledge here is running registered code against external data, and the debate protocol isn't what made that work.**
