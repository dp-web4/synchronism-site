## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment, /gamma-calculator, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A refreshingly honest "we tried this and it didn't work" story with a great crowd-vs-marching-band hook, but each beginner page gets technical within a few paragraphs, and the tools keep telling me the numbers I'm playing with don't apply to what's drawn.

### Journey
Came in from Reddit. The headline "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of thing that gets me to click. The very next line, "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything", pulled me up short in a good way. The one-line idea ("every system ... gets one number between 0 (a crowd of strangers) and 1 (a marching band)") is the clearest thing on the whole site and I got it immediately.

Then within one screen: "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." Then "As tested physics, dead so far." I honestly didn't expect that. It made me trust the site more, but it also raised the question: *why am I reading about a dead idea?* The landing page half-answers ("alive as a public record") and I'm only partly sold. The scoreboard says "Refutations executed: 6", then gives a range of "4–8 depending on counting rules". As a non-scientist, a count that is also a range made me feel I was missing something. There was also a line about Gaia DR4 coming out on 2 December 2026 that "cannot select this framework". I had no idea why it was there, and it read like an inside conversation.

The landing page has three "start" options (Why Synchronism, First Encounter, See What Failed) plus a "No physics background?" Beginner Path box. I clicked **Start here: Why Synchronism?** It was good at first. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a hook I recognise from PBS Space Time. "What happened, in three lines" was perfect: a proposal, it lost on galaxies, the big question is still open. It told me beginners can stop after step 1 and that the rest is "folded below". I peeked into the fold anyway and hit Debye temperatures, YBCO, Hill/Naka–Rushton and μ-law companding, then backed out. The fold is clearly labelled "For physicists", so that's fine. One thing nagged me: the fold has a section called "Chemistry: γ ≈ 1 Boundary" as something tested, but later on the γ Calculator I read that the chemistry γ ≈ 1 boundary was *retracted*, and the Coherence Explorer says "γ ≈ 1 is not special". A beginner can't tell which of these is the current story.

**First Encounter (step 2)** is the page I liked most. Sections A–G, "about 10 minutes", with analogies: the dimmer switch, the "bubble" an atom lives in (an atom doesn't feel Andromeda, which I loved), and the whisper in a loud room for wavefunction collapse. The ln step explained as 10×/100×/1000× becoming even steps of 2.3/4.6/6.9 was the first time a log made sense to me on a science site. Then section C told me the γ formula (γ = 2/√N_corr) "failed" because it got the sign backwards (+0.55 predicted, −0.55 measured). I could follow that "the sign was backwards" is bad. I could not follow why we were still being taught a formula that failed. Section E gave me ρ_crit = A × V_flat² with A ≈ 0.029 and said "fitted, not derived". OK.

Then step 3, the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**. Its intro is excellent: "New here? Look at three things on the chart below." The dots are what telescopes measure, the dashed gray line is visible matter only (the gap is the dark-matter puzzle), green is MOND, and violet is this framework, which "does not follow the dots". The punchline: "the only version of this framework's equation that fits these dots is MOND's formula written differently. That is the whole lesson." I GOT IT. That's the clearest science lesson on the site. Small nit: it says "three things", but I counted four bullets (dots, gray, green, violet). That may be how my reader showed the page, but it made me double-check myself. Bigger nit: the calibration switch's default is "Archive's own form: ρcrit = 0.028·Vflat^0.5", and the formula I'd *just* learned on First Encounter (0.029·Vflat²) is now labelled "Units hybrid" as the alternative. Two pages in a row teach me two different versions of the same formula, and nothing tells me which one First Encounter meant or why it changed.

I went back to the **Coherence Explorer** (linked from First Encounter). There's a nice "What this toy is for" box that says it's just to see the S-curve shape, and that the density axis has no units. Honest, and helpful. A "One thing to try first" suggestion (drag γ from 0.49 up to 2 and back) gave me something to do. But then "Before you drag" told me the default γ = 0.49 is a fit for "the acceleration version of the curve, not the density version the chart draws". So the starting value of the toy isn't for the curve the toy shows? I read it three times. Presets have the same problem: the γ = 0.046 preset says "the drawn curve is not the fit". There's also "fails the Cassini Solar-System bound ... 8.7σ ... +17.95σ" in a preset description. Meaningless to me. And odd internal-looking tags appeared on a public tool page: "Session 633", "explorer 2026-11-04", even "simulated-visitor persona". That last one made me wonder whether I'm talking to a bot site.

Step 4, **What Synchronism Is Not**, has a lovely "In plain words — beginner? You can stop here." box: not proven, not journal-reviewed, not a replacement for today's physics, and "not compatible, as it stands, with a basic rule of relativity". "Overclaiming is dishonest. But so is underclaiming." is a great line. By this point I'd been told "you can stop here" on steps 1, 4, and (I'd soon see) 5. That's comforting, but it also made me wonder what the steps in between were for.

Step 5, **Honest Assessment**, opens with "If you read nothing else (this page is about 30,000 words)". 30,000 words! Good thing they tell you to stop at the green box after about 4 minutes. The lead bullet, "The one equation failed its galaxy tests. Where it fits, it is MOND in different notation. As a theory of galaxies: fatal.", is clear. "What survives is the record, not the physics." Also clear. But the box also uses "pre-registered", "σ", "a0", "M/L", "Cassini squeeze", "TEST-09/TEST-10" and "weak-lensing" with no explanation. The "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" block is the right idea. I'd move it *above* the jargon-heavy box.

Last, I tried the **γ Calculator**. The headline is "γ Calculator — a γ formula kept to show how it fails". That's the funniest and most honest tool title I've ever seen. The default is N_corr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, "Boundary (γ ≈ 1)", with the note "a label per the inverted formula ... not a physical classification". The presets (ideal gas, water, enzyme site, ferromagnet, BEC, superconductor) are fun to click through. But I couldn't work out what I was supposed to *learn* by playing with a calculator whose outputs are explicitly not physical. A one-line "watch: the more particles move together, the smaller γ gets — the opposite of what the idea needed" at the top would make the point land for me.

I checked the **Glossary** for the "ten words to learn first" (Coherence, Presence, γ, tanh, MOND, Dark matter, Reparametrization, Refutation, σ, MRH). The coherence and dark-matter entries are friendly. "dex" has a great plain definition ("1 dex = a factor of 10"). The page intro, though, jumps straight into two families of validation badges and an MRH definition I couldn't parse. My reader also gave me (unverified) a line from the "Which C? Which γ?" section saying "C names at least four functions and γ carries at least four values on this site". If that's on the page, it confirms the feeling I had all along: the same letters mean different things on different pages.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked (server-rendered text, readouts present: Vflat 47 km/s, ρcrit 0.19 M☉/pc³, max Cρ 0.238 for DDO 154) — intuitive. Best beginner content on the site; the "three things" intro and the "MOND written differently" punchline land. The calibration switch conflicts with First Encounter's formula. Note: I could only read the page text, not drag sliders or see the chart.
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked (readouts present: C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785 at γ = 0.49; midpoint at 2.07·ρcrit) — confusing. The "What this toy is for" and "One thing to try first" boxes are great; the default value and presets keep saying they don't describe the drawn curve.
- **γ Calculator** — worked (default N_corr = 4 → γ = 1.0000, C(ρcrit) = 0.60; preset table consistent with 2/√N) — half-intuitive. The honest title is great, but there's no clear "what should I notice" takeaway.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the idea is "dead as tested physics", what is the *one* thing a casual reader should leave with: a lesson about how science works, or something still alive about the physics?
2. Which ρ_crit formula is the "real" one: V² or V^0.5? Why did First Encounter show me the other one?
3. Why does the Coherence Explorer default to a γ that, by its own text, belongs to a different curve?
4. Is the γ ≈ 1 chemistry boundary a result, a retraction, or "not special"? Three pages give three answers.
5. What does it mean that the research is done by "AI agents" with one human lead? How would I know any of the numbers are right if no outside physicist has looked?
6. Why mention Gaia DR4 on the front page if it "cannot select this framework"?

### Verdict
As a curious non-physicist, I came away understanding one real thing: someone proposed a single "crowd vs marching band" dial for all of physics, tested it on galaxies, and found it was just MOND written differently. The site says so with unusual and genuinely likeable honesty, and the Galaxy Plotter teaches it well. But almost every beginner page slides into insider vocabulary within a few paragraphs, and the tools and steps contradict each other on basic details (which ρ_crit formula, whether γ ≈ 1 matters, what the default γ even describes). I'd share the landing page and the plotter with a friend as a cool "science failing in public" story, but I wouldn't come back to learn physics here.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /interactive-tools, /learning-paths, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /crossover-regime-visualizer, /equation-walkthrough, /gamma-boundary, /key-claims, /test-catalog, /tier-1-existing)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I can't judge the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an example of disciplined, self-correcting documentation that has outgrown its label system
- **Overall impression**: The headline facts are unusually consistent from page to page, with "6 refutations (4–8 under rules on file)" identical everywhere I looked. The label system around them has grown to roughly 40 terms in five or more categories, and the same label sits in a different category depending on the page.

### Journey
I started on the landing page and listed the nav. The top bar has nine items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Interactive Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment). The footer adds First Encounter and Key Claims. Key Claims is the page subtitled "Where Synchronism says something new", and it is not in the top nav, which surprised me. The landing page handles acronyms well. MRH ("Markov Relevancy Horizon"), MOND, A2ACW and CFD are expanded in place. An "Abbreviations ahead" line warns about SPARC, RAR, BTFR, ΔBIC, Ωm and N_corr before they appear, and a key under Scientific Outcomes expands them. The "Bell S-value" was the only thing I found used with no definition.

**Interactive Tools** is the best-organised hub on the site. Seven cards each carry Kind, Level, Status and a "Badge on its page". The intro says outright that the groupings are content categories, not verdicts. My only reservation is that it puts four labelling axes on one card. "Status" holds "Artifact Lesson". The glossary calls that "a content label, not a validation badge", while Honest Assessment lists it under "operational states, which are not badges". So one label is called a *status*, a *content/tool label* and an *operational state*, depending on where you read about it.

**Learning Paths** checks out. The Beginner path matches the landing page's five steps exactly, with times and an explicit stop point (the "If you read nothing else" box). Pages on a path show "Step N of M" with prev/next links. /gamma-boundary correctly shows two indicators: "Intermediate Path, Step 6 of 9" and "Chemistry Track, Step 3 of 6". That is good wayfinding. Two problems. Step names use MOND, CDM, A2ACW, η and "γ ≈ 1 boundary" with no expansion on this page. The four topic tracks (Physics, Chemistry, Philosophy, Methodology) state no audience level, though all three difficulty paths do.

Next I went looking for where the **validation badges** are defined, which was my main brief. They are defined in three places, and the three agree: the glossary intro, Honest Assessment's "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)" and Test Roadmap's "Validation Badge Guide". There are nine badges in two families, "MRH-relationship" (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative) and "Descriptive" (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed). Validated, Strongly Supported and MOND-shared are listed as retired. Key Claims links each badge to its own glossary anchor (`/glossary#failed-badge`), which is best practice. Problems I found:

1. **"Validation" badges that are explicitly not validations.** The heading says "Validation badge definitions", and the text says the tags "describe an empirical relationship rather than a verdict on truth-status." The heading contradicts its own content. "Status badges" or "Research-status tags" would match.
2. **MRH means two things.** It is the physics concept (Markov Relevancy Horizon) and also a badge family name. The glossary does acknowledge this: "The borrowing is a metaphor. These badges say nothing about the Markov Relevancy Horizon as a physics concept." But a reader who sees "Active-MRH" on a physics card before reaching that glossary note will reasonably read it as a statement about the physics.
3. **Category membership varies by page.** Test Roadmap lists 7 "operational states" and 11 "lifecycle verbs" (Executed, Adjudicated, Closed, Retired, Reclassified, Never run as registered, Underpowered as registered, Ceiling Exceeded, Empty Intersection, Demoted — Exploratory, Closed by Execution). Honest Assessment lists 10 operational states, including "89% Boundary-Consistent" and "Template Bias Caveat", which are not on the Roadmap list. Tier 1's "card-state legend" lists 8 states and includes "Underpowered as Registered", "Never Run as Registered", "Closed by Execution", "Reclassified" and "Demoted — Exploratory". The Roadmap files those as *lifecycle verbs*, not states, and capitalises them differently ("as registered" vs "as Registered"). Altogether I count about 9 badges, 7–10 operational states, 11 lifecycle verbs, 3 retired labels, plus Kind and Level on tools. Each list is defensible on its own page. Across pages, a reader can't tell what kind of thing "Reclassified" is.
4. **Badges are used before they are defined** on Honest Assessment. The refutation ledger says "Every row carries the Failed badge", and the "0 of 9" Validated audit comes before the definitions section, which sits about a fifth of the way down a 30,000-word page. The TOC does link the definitions, which helps.

**Test Roadmap / Tier 1.** The h1 "Test Roadmap" matches the nav, but the URL is `/test-catalog` and the breadcrumb parent is "Predictions & Experiments", a label that appears in no nav bar. The Tier 1 breadcrumb links "Predictions & Experiments" to /test-catalog, so the same page goes by two names depending on which trail you're in. Tiers are used ("Tier 1" in the opening box, Tiers 2–4 in "Protocol status") before the paragraph that defines them as four cost/feasibility tiers with ID ranges 01–10, 11–14, 15–21 and 22–24. The counts reconcile: tier boxes 10+4+7+3 = 24, plus TEST-25/26 = 26, matching "26 tests (24 in the original registry + 2 added later)". Tier 1's "13 cards" is explained (10 + TEST-04a + TEST-25 + TEST-26). Test-ID suffixes are explained (`a` = amended, `s` = substituted protocol), which I appreciated. One internal tension remains. Tier 1 says TEST-07 "sits here but is not a Tier 1 test by its own heading", but still counts it among "the 10 original Tier 1 tests". On Tier 1, the acronym key comes *after* the first caveats, which already use MOND, SPARC, RAR, BTFR, EFE and DR3/DR4.

**Glossary.** It is alphabetical with ~128 terms (per the fetch summary). The "New here? Start with these ten" list is a good idea. The verbatim Coherence entry says "the letter C names three different functions on this site (one takes density, two take acceleration)." My fetch tool could not return the "Which C? Which γ?" entry verbatim (it sits past character ~80,000). Its summary said that entry states "C names at least four functions". Pass 1's reader reported the same "at least four" wording, also from a summary. **Unverified**, but if both sentences are on the page, the glossary contradicts itself on its own disambiguation count. The maintainer should grep for both.

**The γ ≈ 1 region has three names.** The page and the Chemistry Track call it "The γ ≈ 1 Boundary". The Crossover Regime Visualizer calls the same span (0.6–1.4) the "Crossover band". Pass 1 reports the γ Calculator labels it "Boundary (γ ≈ 1)". /gamma-boundary itself says "Why γ ≈ 1 Matters — retracted 2026-08-09" and "No feature of C(ρ) singles out γ ≈ 1". The visualizer was renamed from "Phase Boundary Visualizer" on 2026-10-23 precisely because "boundary" overclaimed. The rename stopped at the tool. The learning-path step titles "The γ ≈ 1 Boundary" with no hint of the retraction.

**Provenance text in public copy.** /gamma-boundary says the result was "withdrawn 2026-09-27 after a visitor graduate-student persona flagged it". /interactive-tools mentions "AI review passes" and "simulated-visitor personas". The status line on /gamma-boundary reads "Z-null run on the real rows (2026-10-31): fails". This is changelog language in body copy. "Z-null" and "real rows" mean nothing to a reader who doesn't know about a fake-rows episode. Pass 1 flagged the same thing on the Coherence Explorer.

- The specificity-0/6 calibration on Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking and Noether is uninformative, because all six positives are in the training set. The site says so.
- The post-cutoff arm, the only arm that could test novelty recognition, has not been run.

The most citable methodological result on the site is on /for-researchers, not on /a2acw: "About 3,300 adversarial AI debate sessions produced zero refutations of the physics on their own. Five of the six scoreboard refutations came from executing code against external data." In plain words, debate filtered nothing and data killed everything. That is a real finding about AI-for-science, with a denominator, and it should open /a2acw.

**Density keying is underdefined, not just housekeeping.** Pass 3 asked which coarse-graining scale defines ρ. I think this is a definitional gap, not a presentational one. If stars feel the ambient smoothed density, G_eff reaches 3.17 G in low-density hosts, and stellar luminosity (L ∝ G^~7 for main-sequence stars) would differ by orders of magnitude in dwarfs. That is a kill no page I read mentions. If stars feel their own density, C = 1 everywhere there is matter to observe, and the galaxy-fit ρ = Σ/2h is an unstated modelling choice. Either way the kernel carries the predictions, and it isn't written down.
