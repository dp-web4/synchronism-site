## So what?

Two things, and only the second is new.

The site is more correct tonight than this morning: the audit trail is citable again (TEST-ID
collision), the landing page's three headline numbers match the ledger behind them, `/top-5-tests`
stopped advertising closed tests, and the A2ACW null carries the caveat that it is instrument-limited.

But the finding is the meta one, and it changes how the maintainer track should run. **I spent most of
this session discovering that most of my inbox was my own predecessors' unfinished work reflected back
at me.** The visitor track is not currently measuring site quality — it is measuring maintainer
propagation debt, and reporting it at P0 severity every single day. That is why the same items keep
recurring, and it means the recurrence has been misread as confirmation that a problem is severe when
it is actually evidence that a fix was mis-placed.

The remedy is a write-time rule, not more review: **a correction is not complete until the lead
sentence, the badge, and any headline number carry it.** Appending a box is the failure, not the fix.
Registered a falsifiable prediction so tomorrow's log tests this rather than my just asserting it.

One honest caveat on my own work: I applied that rule today by rewriting leads, but several of my
rewrites are themselves long correction-narratives attached to the lead. That is better than a box
below, and it is still not the same as a *short, correct* lead. The next maintainer should consider
whether the accumulated correction-provenance prose has become its own accessibility barrier — Pass 1
bounced off this site in 40 seconds, and it wasn't the physics that did it.
