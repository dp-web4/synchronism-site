## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, beginner-path, first-encounter, core-idea, tools, galaxy-plotter, coherence-explorer, honest-assessment, glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A genuinely intriguing "what if" hook and a great 10-minute intro, but the site keeps interrupting itself with corrections until I couldn't tell what I was supposed to believe anymore.

### Journey

I clicked in from Reddit expecting either crackpot stuff or something cool. The landing page surprised me: "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of question PBS Space Time would make a video about. There's a scary-looking equation right there (C(ρ) = tanh of... something), but under it it says it's just "a smooth S-curve from sparse/independent to dense/collective," which I could picture. What really got my attention was that the site says, right on the front page, that it has *zero confirmed predictions* and "Some predictions work. Some fail." I've never seen a science site do that. It made me trust it more, honestly.

I followed "Beginner Path." Good structure (The Problem → The Approach → What We Tested → What Failed), and the marching-band-vs-crowd idea clicked immediately. But even on the *beginner* page I hit words like "ansatz," "phenomenological," "MOND," "compander," and "Hamiltonian" with no explanation. I skimmed past them, but each one made me feel a little more like this page wasn't actually written for me.

"First Encounter" was the best page I read. Ten minutes, no equations, all analogies — marching band, crystal lattice, "a single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy." I actually understood the core idea from this page alone: density is a dial, and stuff acts more "together" when it's packed tighter. The MRH thing ("event horizon for influence") mostly made sense, though the sentence about "degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence" lost me completely.

"The Core Idea" kept the streak going with the dimmer-switch metaphor and a labeled graph before showing any math. But this is also where the site's weirdest habit showed up: boxes saying earlier claims were "refuted" or "uncaveated." As a casual reader I can't tell which parts of the page I just read are the good parts and which are the walked-back parts. It's like reading a textbook where every third paragraph has a sticky note saying "actually, maybe not."

Then I went to the Tools gallery and played with two tools (below).

Out of curiosity I clicked "Honest Assessment" because the front page kept pointing at it. I bounced after maybe two minutes. It's enormous — it reads like a legal document about being wrong. I got the message from the top ("0 confirmed predictions... 6 refutations") and the one line that stuck with me: "the honest map of what failed — and why — is the actual product." That's a cool sentence. But the badge system (Active-MRH? Parallel-Paths? Reparametrization?) meant nothing to me, and the corrections-on-corrections ("there is no field equation" → "actually there is one" → later something else) read like watching someone lose an argument with their own footnotes.

Last stop: the glossary, to look up "coherence." The definition ("how collectively a group of elements behaves, from 0 to 1") plus the stadium analogy was clear. Some other entries I peeked at were circular — "Compander" defined using math words I'd then have to look up somewhere else.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — mostly intuitive — Picking different galaxies from the dropdown and seeing the dots stay flat while the gray "physics prediction" line droops is the single best explanation of the dark matter problem I've ever seen, better than most YouTube videos. But there are FIVE lines, and one of them is admitted to be "hand-tuned to fit, not computed." I get that they're being honest, but as a newbie I stared at it thinking "so is your theory the purple line or the orange line?" The honesty needs one more sentence of hand-holding.
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — confusing in places — Dragging the sharpness slider and watching the S-curve snap steeper is satisfying and taught me what γ *does* even though I couldn't define it. Two things threw me: the second slider "just shifts the curve without changing its shape" (so... why do I care?), and a warning that "coherence" here means roughly the *opposite* of quantum coherence — which is the one place I'd heard the word before. ### Unanswered Questions

1. If the theory has zero confirmed predictions and the site itself says the key test "kills this framework" — why does the site exist? Is this a graveyard, a work in progress, or a lesson? (I genuinely can't tell, and one sentence on the landing page would fix it.)
2. What's the relationship between this and dark matter? The plotter shows the mystery beautifully but I left not knowing whether Synchronism says dark matter exists or doesn't.
3. Is "MOND" a competitor, an ancestor, or a part of this theory? The green line is just there with no introduction.
4. Who is behind this? I never saw a person's name, a lab, or an "about" — for a site this unusual, I wanted to know who's talking.

### Verdict

This is the most honest science site I've ever stumbled into, and that honesty is both its superpower and its problem: the intro path (First Encounter especially) is genuinely excellent pop-science writing, but the constant self-corrections and the wall-of-text audit made me feel like I'd walked into the middle of an argument the site is having with itself. I'd send a science-curious friend the First Encounter page and the galaxy plotter; I would not send them the front door.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, tools, learning-paths, γ-calculator, phase-boundary, glossary, honest-assessment, key-claims, beginner-path → why-synchronism, gamma-parameter)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes

### Journey

I audited methodically: landing page first, then the nav skeleton (Tools, Learning Paths), two interactive tools, and the reference layer (Glossary, Honest Assessment badge legend), finishing with Key Claims and two cross-reference targets.

**What works.** Breadcrumbs exist on every page I checked, are accurate, and even carry path-position context ("Beginner Path — Step 5 of 6"). I specifically cross-checked the step indicators across three pages — Why Synchronism? (1 of 6), Honest Assessment (5 of 6), Glossary (6 of 6) — and they're mutually consistent, which is rare. The glossary is the strongest reference page I've seen on a site this size: all 13 acronyms I went hunting for (MOND, ΛCDM, SPARC, ALFALFA, DESI, BAO, CHSH, BTFR, fσ₈, A2ACW, MRH, N_corr, plus "compander" and "reparametrization") are present, entries cross-link to deeper pages, and the front matter even documents its own sort-order conventions. The Honest Assessment page has a real canonical badge legend at a stable anchor (`#validation-badge-definitions`), and Key Claims correctly links to it.

**What doesn't.** The problem is everything *between* the reference layer and the reader. The landing page uses MOND, ΛCDM, SPARC, CFD, DESI, and BAO without expansion or glossary links — the definitions exist one click away, but the click isn't offered. The γ Calculator puts "Audited-Negative" in its page *title* and never defines it or links to the legend. Tool cards carry labels ("Model Explainer," "Artifact Lesson") that look exactly like validation badges but turn out to be a separate tool-card vocabulary, defined only in a parenthetical deep inside Honest Assessment. Counting everything — MRH-relationship tags, descriptive tags, deprecated tags, operational states, tool-card labels — I found roughly twenty distinct status labels. No documentation system survives twenty statuses; readers compress them into "good/bad/unclear" and the taxonomy's precision is wasted.

**Numeric self-consistency** is the other systemic issue. One landing-page tile shows three different galaxy totals (header "14,610", footnote "175 + 14,435", and a correction note saying it used to say "14,760"). The refutation scoreboard says "6 refutations executed" while footers qualify "3–4 independent roots" and another passage says only 2 are framework-specific. Each individual number may be defensible, but as a documentation product the site never picks one canonical figure per fact and references it; every page re-derives its own copy, and they drift.

**The correction-stack pattern.** Dated inline corrections ("Corrected 2026-08-10", "added 2026-07-27") appear 40+ times on Honest Assessment alone and on nearly every page I read. As an audit trail this is admirable; as information architecture it's upside down. The current-best statement should read clean, with provenance displaced into a per-claim changelog or errata section. Right now the reader parses the wrong version, then the correction, then sometimes a correction to the correction — three reads to extract one fact.

**Symbol hygiene.** Key Claims uses C for two different functions (C(ρ) the coherence function, and an admittedly "distinct, undefined construct" C = f(γ, D, S) for consciousness) — the disambiguation warning arrives *after* the second formula. The site itself admits γ "runs as three incompatible quantities." A notation table exists nowhere I could find.

One navigation note: `/beginner-path` landed me on "Why Synchronism?" (Step 1 of 6) directly — I never saw an overview page listing all six steps in one place. Learning Paths lists a "Beginner" difficulty row (the landing page calls it "Beginner Path"), but if there's a page enumerating the six steps, I didn't find it from the path I took.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** — worked — confusing purpose — The controls are fine (N_corr input, linear/log scale toggle, presets, live curve), but the page can't decide what it is. The title announces the formula is "Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted," four caveat blocks precede the first interaction, and then the copy cheerfully invites "Try it: click 'Ideal gas'…" A calculator whose outputs you're told not to trust needs to say, in sentence one, "this is a cautionary exhibit — watch the formula fail." ### Unanswered Questions

1. Is there a single source of truth for the badge taxonomy that pages consume, or does each page hand-copy its labels? (The drift pattern suggests hand-copying.)
2. Why does the tool-card vocabulary ("Model Explainer," "Artifact Lesson") live in the validation-badge legend if it's explicitly not a validation badge?

The single highest-leverage fix is mechanical, not editorial: link first uses to the glossary, link every badge to the legend, and establish one canonical figure per fact. The second is editorial: stop appending corrections and start rewriting leads, with history displaced to changelogs — the audit-trail instinct is right, but an audit trail belongs behind the current-best text, not in front of it.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, parameter-derivations, interactive-tools, equation-walkthrough, chemistry-correlation-explorer, galaxy-rotation, honest-assessment, key-claims, cdm-discrimination, mond-unification)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The framework is a MOND reparametrization and the site knows it and says so — the remaining scientific exposure is that some of the *refutation statistics* are less audited than the claims they kill, and two of six refutations rest on an asserted, underived constant.

### Journey

A colleague sent me this as "an interesting AI project," so I came in expecting either crankery or vaporware. Neither. The landing page states its own scoreboard — 0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations, 0 of 6 "validated" claims surviving audit — before you've scrolled once. I've refereed papers that fought harder to hide less.

I went straight to `/parameter-derivations`, because that page is where frameworks like this usually die quietly. Here it dies loudly, in a table: every parameter row carries a negative verdict written by the site itself. The claimed Jeans derivation of ρ_crit is off by a stated factor of ~600 from its own propagated constant; the golden-ratio exponent is documented as fitted-then-named, with the archive's own earlier ruling against it quoted; a₀ = cH₀/2π is correctly labeled dimensional analysis available to anyone (Milgrom himself, McCulloch, Verlinde). I spot-checked the algebra I could do on paper: Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) with a₀ = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² gives 0.248 kg/m² ≈ 119 M☉/pc² — the site's number is right, and its harsher point is also right: Σ₀ and a₀ are one number in two rows, so the "Freeman's Law match" is not independent evidence. The rejection of the Ising analogy for tanh is textbook-correct: mean-field tanh arises from the self-consistency loop m = tanh(βJzm); this C(ρ) has no fixed point, so it's a chosen sigmoid, not a derived order parameter. And the γ = 2/√N_corr sign inversion is real — BCS with N_corr ~ 10⁶ has the *sharpest* transitions in nature and this formula files it at the flattest end. The site badges all of this against itself. Genuinely unusual.

`/galaxy-rotation` confirmed what I suspected from the equations: the production fits use McGaugh's RAR interpolating function — i.e., MOND's machinery — and the compander only appears as an alternative μ-function. The decisive comparison: γ=2 pinned loses to MOND's ν at ΔBIC = +184; γ freed fits to 0.49 and matches MOND to Δχ² ≈ −0.84. I checked the γ=1/2 case by hand and it's stronger than "matches": tanh(½ ln u) = (u−1)/(u+1) identically, so with u = 1 + ρ/ρ_crit, C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) — at the fitted γ the compander *is* MOND's simple μ-function, algebraically. The "collapse to MOND" is a theorem, not a fit outcome, and the site could state it as one.

Two things bothered me on that page, though. First, it admits three mutually exclusive conventions for how C enters the force law (amplitude, divisor, multiplier) are live on the site simultaneously, each missing observations by 10²–10³× in different directions. If the model was never uniquely specified, "the model was refuted" needs a subscript saying which one. Second, the ΔBIC = +184 uses 2807 RAR points from ~175 galaxies. Points within one galaxy's rotation curve are not independent — radially correlated residuals are the first thing a referee flags on SPARC fits. With N_eff closer to the galaxy count than the point count, that BIC penalty shrinks a lot. The kill probably survives (γ=2 is badly wrong in shape), but the headline number is inflated by the same class of statistical carelessness the site spends thousands of words confessing to elsewhere. Over-refutation is still mis-estimation.

`/honest-assessment` is the most self-incriminating document I've read outside a retraction notice — it catalogs its own fabricated p-value splices, a verdict inversion, a "no field equation exists" claim that was false for seven months. The badge system held up under my checking: nothing physics-load-bearing still wears "Validated"; the entity criterion (Γ < m) is correctly demoted to standard narrow-resonance; chemistry's r = 0.982 is correctly demoted via a 2-parameter polynomial null in Z that ties or beats it. But the refutation ledger has a soft spot: refutations #1 and #2 (BTFR slope, dwarf DM fractions) both rest on B_max = 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17, which the parameter page says in plain text is "asserted, not derived" — and under the alternative reading Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.40 at least one verdict flips. Two-sixths of the headline scoreboard hangs on an undefended constant, and the footer's honest "3–4 independent roots" concedes this without quite saying it. Also worth noting: killing γ≈0.49 with Cassini (+17.95σ) kills MOND's simple μ too — that constraint is known in the MOND literature and doesn't discriminate this framework from its parent.

`/cdm-discrimination` and `/mond-unification` were consistency checks. The former is now aligned with the ledger (both original CDM-beating claims retracted on-page). The latter still displays a "novel prediction" card (TDG velocity dispersions) whose own text says the intervals are nested inside MOND's and "the specific numbers in this card do not and should not be cited" — a card that disclaims its own citability should be an archived changelog entry, not a live card.

### Tools Tested

- **Equation Anatomy (/equation-walkthrough)** — worked — intuitive — Step-by-step decomposition with honest limits: it states C(ρ_crit) = 0.88, *not* a midpoint, which I verified (tanh(2 ln 2) = 0.882) — most sites would quietly imply the knee is the half-way point. It flags the γ sign inversion inside its own walkthrough, which takes nerve. My one physics complaint is the +1 regulator framing (see friction log): calling it numerical bookkeeping hides that it dictates the low-density asymptotics, which is exactly the regime where the galaxy claims lived.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer (/chemistry-correlation-explorer)** — worked — intuitive, honestly self-undermining — Filters and sorting behave; the best feature is the polynomial-null comparison stated right on the tool: a 2-parameter polynomial in Z matches or beats the framework's correlations (|Δr| ≤ 0.07), so high r is density-monotonicity, not C(ρ) physics. That's the correct null and most papers in this genre never run it. The friction is presentational: the r = 0.982 rows still carry the visual weight, and only 23 of 1,703 phenomena are shown with no way to see the rest.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Can B_max = 1/Ω_m be derived from anything in the framework, and what happens to refutations #1–2 under Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.40? This is the single highest-leverage open item on the site.
2. What is the effective N for the ΔBIC = +184, accounting for intra-galaxy correlation of RAR residuals?
3. Which of the three C-entry conventions was the *intended* model when the refutations were registered?
4. Given that γ=½ is algebraically simple-μ and γ, ρ_crit are then degenerate with MOND's a₀ — is there any parameter regime left where this framework is a distinct theory rather than a coordinate change on MOND?

### Verdict

The physics verdict is one the site has already written about itself, and it's correct: this is a reparametrization of MOND with zero independently derived parameters, and everywhere it differed from MOND the data killed the difference. What impressed me is that the self-audit is technically competent — the Ising rejection, the polynomial null, the Σ₀ ≡ a₀ identity are all correct physics arguments deployed against the site's own claims. What worries me is asymmetric rigor in the other direction: the refutation ledger's numbers (ΔBIC = +184 on correlated points, two kills resting on an asserted ceiling) get less scrutiny than the claims they demolish, and over-refutation is still error. As a physics theory this is closed; as a public autopsy of a theory, it's the best-documented one I've seen, and I'd show it to my department's methods seminar for exactly that reason.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, interactive-tools, tier-1-existing, research-philosophy, for-researchers, dark-matter, measurement-without-observers, test-catalog, consciousness-demo, galaxy-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — as a case study, not as physics
- **Overall impression**: The physics contains nothing new that survives — and the site says so more precisely than I could — but the site is sitting on one genuinely citable novel result (its own audit-instrument calibration) that it under-promotes, and it is silent on the one live channel where a density-coupled framework could actually be discriminated in 2026: DESI's evolving dark energy.

### Journey

I came to answer three questions: what's actually new, does anything discriminate from MOND+ΛCDM, and is the A2ACW methodology sound. I skipped the pedagogy and went straight for the ledgers.

**Novelty hunt.** The landing page pre-empts me: "What would count as a confirmation is a prediction that *differs* from MOND and ΛCDM and wins; no test currently on the books can deliver that." The Tier-1 page backs it by execution: "Net discriminating galaxy tests vs MOND: 0." TEST-09/10 discriminate — and the framework loses both (BTFR deviation 0.41 > 0.3; 69% of dwarfs exceed the B ≤ 1/Ω_m ceiling). TEST-02 (wide binaries) is stated as "self-eliminating": the framework predicts 0.05–0.4% velocity deviation — the Newtonian null — so a Chae-style ~18% detection refutes it and a Banik-style null confirms Newton, not Synchronism. That branch analysis is correct, and the page's handling of the Chae (2023–2025) vs Banik et al. (2024) dispute is current and fair. TEST-25 correctly credits Desmond et al. 2024 (8.7σ) and correctly labels the Cassini squeeze non-discriminating (it kills simple-μ MOND too). The test-catalog page closes the loop: "Of these 24 proposed tests, 0 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." I tried to find a claim the site had missed in its own favor. I found none. The only surviving candidates for "new" are two *negative* results on /for-researchers: the quantified local-algebraic-density no-go — properly scoped after the Burrage–Copeland–Millington 2017 counterexample forced "algebraic" into the claim, with the Milgrom non-locality lineage credited — and the A2ACW null itself.

**The gap that surprised me.** The dark-matter page admits BAO/LSS "has not been confronted," and nowhere on the site — /for-researchers, /tier-1-existing, /test-catalog — is there a dark-energy sector. The only DESI engagement is DR1 fσ₈ (TEST-04a, disfavored 2.4σ on σ₈, correctly downgraded as underpowered vs GR, with Ishak et al. arXiv:2411.12026 cited). But the biggest result in my field right now is DESI's *background* evolution: DR2 BAO combined with CMB and supernovae prefers w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0 over ΛCDM at up to ~3–4σ depending on the SN compilation. A framework whose central object is a density-dependent coupling C(ρ) that runs with cosmic epoch should *generically* induce an effective w(z) — that's not optional, it's what the mechanism does to the expansion history. ### Verdict

I'd referee a paper built on the calibration result; I'd reject one built on anything else here; and I'll check back once to see if they ever confront the w₀–wₐ plane.
