### Result 1 — the session's own hypothesis failed
Felten's (1984) objection is **real** for the site's stated algebraic law (order-100 % third-law
violation in exactly the galaxy regime; 0 % in the solar system, which is why no ephemeris test has
already excluded it) but it is **answerable**, and reporting it as a kill would be an over-claim. Two
sub-results worth keeping:
- The fractional violation is **invariant under K → 1/K**, so it does not depend on which of the site's
  two contradictory readings of its own law ("C(ρ)·g" vs "g/C") is meant. One less fork.
- It is **not computable as written** — three prescriptions for "local ρ" that the site never
  distinguishes give 0 %, 100 %, 0 % for the same pair. Fourth unnamed-estimator instance.

### Result 2 — the completion, and why it matters
`∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ` conserves momentum (Noether), reproduces `g = g_N/C` exactly in spherical symmetry
(Gauss), and **preserves EFE = 0 exactly because it is linear in Φ**. Its extra polarization force is
≤ 2 × 10⁻⁵ of gravity at the site's ρ_crit — free. So EFE = 0 is not an artifact of missing dynamics.

And then the equivalence that is the actual finding: **"a uniform external field does not change ρ"
(⇒ EFE = 0) and "empty space has C = 0 however strong the field" (⇒ divergent exterior) are the same
statement.** The site's one surviving structural prediction and its worst pathology are one property.

### Result 3 — the substitution was never evaluated
`/coherence-function` says the g_bar → ρ swap "is the entire difference from MOND." Every quantitative
galaxy result on the site — γ = 0.489, ΔBIC = +7.1/+184, the Cassini squeeze — was run with **g_bar**.
Running the substitution: predicted v at r_last is 2.3 × 10³ – 3.7 × 10⁴ km/s (law implied by
f_DM = 1 − C), 9.8 – 179 km/s (the plotter's law), or 0.013 – 1.4 km/s (the literal prose), against
observed 47 – 250. Three mutually exclusive laws, all live, all wrong, in three different directions.

Functional-form, not calibration: required boost grows ~linearly (~1 → ~4), delivered boost grows
exponentially (10³ → 10⁵), because ρ is exponential in a disk. Swept γ ∈ [0.25, 4] × ρ_crit ∈ [10⁻⁴,
10⁴] — the only near-target cells are the ones where the modification is switched off.

### Result 4 — boost-ceiling collision
B ≤ 3.17 is exceeded by 2–5 OOM at **every** radius including the disk centre; the bisection has no
root. The density floor needed to enforce it is 10²–10⁴× the densest point of the model disk and ~10⁹×
the cosmic mean. Stated narrowly: the ceiling is consistent with the plotter's law and not with the
identity's law — it is a statement about which law you meant, and the site does not say.

**Explicitly did NOT bump the refutation count.** Results 3–4 are the mean-relation face of the same

## So what?

The hypothesis I was handed failed — the momentum objection does not kill EFE = 0 — and writing down
the completion that answers it is what produced the real result. That is the session's shape: a
productive negative that only became productive because I tried to *build* the missing object instead
of arguing about its absence.

The substantive discovery is that the site names one substitution as the entire content of its galaxy
sector and has never evaluated it. Its quantitative galaxy work is MOND, run with MOND's variable. The
framework's own variable has been sitting unexecuted behind a sentence saying it is the whole point.

Generalization for the WAKE of following sessions, sharper than 07-29's "the rate-limiting step is
cross-multiplication": **the site's most confident structural sentences are the least likely to have
been executed, precisely because they read as settled.** An audit that follows badges and numbers will
never reach them — badges attach to results, and these are premises. Worth checking whether the same
pattern holds in the consciousness and chemistry sectors, where the confident structural sentences are
"C ≥ 0.50 is a threshold" and "higher density → higher coherence" respectively. The second one is
already known to be false (07-29, diamond vs lead). The first has not been looked at this way.
