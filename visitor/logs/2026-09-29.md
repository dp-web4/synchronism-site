## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, The Core Idea, What Synchronism Is Not, Honest Assessment, Dark Matter Reframed, Learning Paths, Interactive Tools, Coherence Explorer, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Consciousness Threshold Demo, Glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A surprisingly honest site about an idea that didn't work, but it never tells me in one sentence why I, a non-physicist, should spend an hour reading about a failed equation.

### Journey

I clicked the Reddit link expecting a "one equation explains everything" pitch. The headline *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* is exactly that and it hooked me. Then the very next line: *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* Wait, what? I scrolled back up to check I hadn't misread. So the whole site is about an idea that failed? That is genuinely unusual and I kept reading mostly out of curiosity about *why* a site would exist for that.

The equation on the landing page (C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))) looked like a wall of Greek. I don't know what tanh is, I don't know what ln is, and there are two Greek letters. But the line right under it, "Coherence is a smooth S-curve from sparse/independent to dense/collective," was enough. An S-curve I get. I moved on.

The scorecard confused me. "Prospective predictions confirmed: 0." "Refutations executed: 6." "'Validated' claims surviving audit: 0 of 9." I understood that means it's 0 for 9, but the words "prospective," "retrodictions," and "refutations executed" sound like legal language. Below that, "3,308 A2ACW research sessions" — A2ACW is never explained on the landing page. I assumed it was some kind of software.

The coverage table had a "Reparametrization" badge on two of four rows. I had no idea what that meant. I found out three pages later (in the glossary: "known physics in new notation"), and once I knew, the whole site made more sense. That word should be explained the first time it appears, because it's basically the punchline of the entire site.

I clicked **Start Here (Why Synchronism?)**. The pitch is that one principle could connect separate areas of physics. Then it says the galaxy test "collapses to MOND." I know MOND from PBS Space Time as "the alternative to dark matter," so that landed, but only because I'd seen that video. It also lists chemistry results with "r = 0.982" and then says a polynomial in atomic number does just as well. I got the gist: it looked impressive but it was already-known stuff.

**First Encounter** was the best page for me. The marching band vs. gas cloud picture of "coherence" clicked immediately. The "dimmer switch" for the S-curve was great. "Presence (ρ)" as "density of neighbors inside your bubble" was fine. But then the bubble gets called a "Markov Relevancy Horizon" and I bounced off that name hard. Then it introduces γ = 2/√N_corr and immediately says this formula "did not survive testing." So why am I being taught it? The failure list (melting points 53% wrong, superconductors 6.5× wrong) was blunt and I respected it.

**The Core Idea** (Beginner step 3) got too technical fast. "Hill coefficient," "Naka–Rushton," "SPARC," "the logarithm is cosmetic." I skimmed. The one thing I took away: when they let the data pick, the curve "barely responds to density at all, the opposite of the idea." That's a clear sentence buried in jargon.

**What Synchronism Is Not** was reassuring at first (not proven, not peer-reviewed, not replacing Einstein), then dropped a bomb: the framework's universal clock would break relativity "by 16–28 orders of magnitude." I don't know what an order of magnitude is exactly but I know 28 of them is a lot. If it's that broken, why is the rest of the site still walking me through it like a student? I would have liked one sentence answering that.

**Honest Assessment** and **Dark Matter Reframed** were mostly over my head (ΔBIC=+184, +17.95σ, "Bmax=1/Ωm") but the plain-English verdicts were readable: the galaxy boost caps at about 3× and galaxy edges need up to 14×, so it can't be dark matter. Got it.

**Learning Paths**: the Beginner path is a nice idea, but step 5 says "Honest Assessment (~4 min summary; full page optional ~80 min)." An 80-minute page on a beginner path? And the total says "~45 min to 2h 40m," which is a big range. I'd want the 45-minute version clearly marked.

The **Glossary** (98 terms) saved me several times. tanh is defined ("smoothly maps any input to between −1 and +1"), so is MRH and reparametrization. But I only went there because I was writing this log; a normal me would have closed the tab at "Markov Relevancy Horizon."

One thing that nagged me: the landing page says "14,610 galaxies analyzed" and the glossary says SPARC is "175 galaxies." Which is it? Presumably two different datasets, but a casual reader sees two galaxy counts that differ by 100× and doesn't know which one the "failed" verdict is based on.

Also: "0 confirmed predictions; 6 refutations executed" appears on basically every page, including the footer. By page 6 it felt like a chant. Once or twice is honest; ten times is a bit like the site apologizing for existing.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — half intuitive — Dragging γ makes the S-curve steeper or gentler; dragging ρcrit slides it left/right. Fun for 30 seconds, but I never learned *density of what*. The three live values (C at ρcrit, 10×, 100×) meant nothing to me. The preset names assume I know SPARC and MOND. Best part: the C = 0.50 line, because the consciousness page made that number matter.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — intuitive — This was my favorite. Eight "independent" methods all land on C ≈ 0.50, and then the page says "they all share one assumption, so of course they agree." That's a lesson about science I actually took away. The jargon in the fine print didn't matter.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — mostly intuitive — Picking DDO 154 and seeing the gray "visible matter only" line sag while the blue dots stay flat is the clearest "dark matter problem" picture I've seen. Five curves is a lot, but the "max C on this disk: 0.001 — the equation never switches on here" note made the failure concrete. The dotted amber "hand-tuned stand-in formerly shown" line confused me: was it a mistake they're admitting? (Seems so.)

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation failed, what does the site want me to *do* with that? Learn how science fails? Try to fix it? Just watch?
2. What is "density" for a galaxy vs. for a brain? The same slider seems to apply to both and I can't picture it.
3. Is 0 out of 9 unusual, or do most new physics ideas score like this? I have no baseline.
4. What actually happens after a "refutation"? Does someone change the equation, or is it over?
5. Who's writing this? "3,308 AI sessions" makes it sound like AIs wrote the site. Is that true, and does that matter?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've stumbled on, and that honesty is the reason I stayed past the first screen. But the site is written as if I already know what MOND, SPARC, and "reparametrization" are, and the beginner path gets technical by step 3. The real hook for someone like me — "watch a big idea get tested and lose, in public" — is true and interesting, but the site never says it out loud.

### Journey

I started at the landing page and worked the top nav left to right, then followed the Beginner path and the Methodology track by their prev/next links, and finished on the tools index and three tools.

**Landing.** The top nav has nine items. The footer has thirteen, including "Key Claims" and "First Encounter," which are not in the top nav at all. That is a common pattern but worth noting: two of the six "Where to Start" cards point to pages you cannot reach from the header.

Pass 1 reported that A2ACW is never explained on the landing page and that "Reparametrization" is undefined until the glossary. I checked the page text directly and both are defined on the landing page. The problem is placement, not absence. A2ACW is expanded in a dense "Methodology note" paragraph, and the badge legend ("Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed · Audited-Negative") plus the one-line reparametrization gloss sit directly under a heading that reads "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)." A reader who skips history, which is what that heading tells them to do, skips the legend. Worse, the badge names are used earlier on the same page, in the methodology note ("demoted to Reparametrization, Failed, Audited-Negative or Speculative"), roughly 280 text lines before the legend. So Pass 1's experience is the correct experience for a skimmer, and the fix is to move the legend, not to write one.

**Why Synchronism? (Beginner step 1).** MOND is expanded on first use. SPARC, BTFR, RAR, and CHSH are not defined on this page; they appear in the shared ledger footer that is stamped onto every page. Badges appear here ("Failed | TEST-03/05 Environment Run — Refuted by Execution", "89% Boundary-Consistent | Template Bias Caveat") with no legend and no link to one. This is step 1 of the beginner path and it is the first place a beginner meets a badge.

**First Encounter (Beginner step 2).** Good page. MRH is expanded on first use with a plain gloss. Two terminology problems though. ρ is called "presence" in the section title and "density" in the body, and the page says "presence is density," which is fine as a bridge but then the Equation Anatomy tool carries a revision note saying the "Presence" definition was removed in favour of physical mass/energy density. So one page teaches a term that another page has retired, and the glossary entry for C(ρ) still reads "Maps presence to coherence." Second, ρcrit is written in prose as "critical density" while the glossary and the landing page both insist it is a "saturation knee, not a critical point." The subscript says "crit." The prose says "knee." Pick one, or say once that the subscript is historical.

**Learning Paths.** Breadcrumb works ("Home / Learning Paths"). Seven paths (three by difficulty, four by topic). The page admits "The two groupings are not equivalent" with a dated correction, which is honest. Beginner step 5 links to an anchor on Honest Assessment ("~4 min") rather than a page, and the landing page labels that page "Shortcut · also Beginner Path step 5 of 6," so the two are consistent. The Methodology track lists six steps ending in Honest Assessment, and Honest Assessment's own step label says "step 6 of 6 on Methodology Track." Consistent. Good.

**Research Philosophy (Methodology step 1).** This page has two different "Next" links. The step bar at the top says "Next: A2ACW Protocol →" (to /a2acw, which is step 2 in the Learning Paths listing). The navigation at the bottom of the page says "Next: How We Handle Failure →" (to /handling-failure, which is step 4). Both target pages exist. A reader following the bottom link skips two steps of the track without knowing it. This page also states, in its own words, that "the site's status vocabulary has grown from the documented 10 badges to 40+ ad-hoc strings across five pages." I can confirm that from the outside; see the tools index below.

**Honest Assessment.** The canonical badge legend is here, at an anchor that resolves. It defines two badge families (five MRH-relationship tags, four descriptive tags), then a further list of "operational states" (Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Artifact Lesson, Template Bias Caveat, 89% Boundary-Consistent, and more) and a set of lifecycle verbs (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED). That is three vocabularies in one legend, and the legend section alone is long enough that the page warns readers about its total length up front (~18,000 words). Having a single canonical source is right. Having it be this long, on the page beginners are told to read for four minutes, is the trade-off.

**Key Claims.** Uses Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed, Untested. No legend on the page; one sentence points at the Honest Assessment anchor. Acronyms used before definition: IIT, BEC/BCS, QBism, ΛCDM. "MOND" is used at length before its expansion.

**For Researchers.** Breadcrumb reads "Home / Methodology / For Researchers: What's Citable." The "Methodology" segment is plain text, not a link, even though a /methodology page exists and returns 200. Same pattern on Tier 1: "Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1" where "Predictions & Experiments" is unlinked and /predictions exists. Neither "Methodology" nor "Predictions & Experiments" is a top-nav item, so the breadcrumb names a level of hierarchy the navigation never exposes. The acronym block on this page arrives mid-page, after the opening sections have already used BTFR, EFE, CMB, LSS, GRB, DESI, LIV, SME, BIC, and OOM.

**Tier 1: Existing Data.** Has an "Acronym key (used throughout the test cards below)" placed before the test cards. Good. But the two sections above it ("Three caveats," "Inherited MOND exposures") already use RAR, BTFR, and EFE. The test naming scheme (TEST-01 through TEST-10, then TEST-25, plus TEST-03s and TEST-04a) is never explained as a scheme; the page explains the TEST-11 to TEST-25 renumbering but not what the "s" or "a" suffixes mean. The page's own revision note says "every hand-typed count on this site has drifted at least once." QUMOND, AQUAL, EFTofLSS, and LRG appear inside test cards without a key entry.

**Glossary.** 98 terms, alphabetical with Greek letters last, letter anchors, no search. It does define A2ACW, MRH, reparametrization, all nine badges (with "Validated" marked retired), Tier 1, prospective, retrodiction, refutation, N_corr, Artifact Lesson, Model Explainer, Kill Criterion, Withdrawn, and Underpowered. ## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 (landing, Honest Assessment, Key Claims, Test Roadmap, Interactive Tools index, Parameter Derivations, Galaxy Rotation, Dark Matter Reframed, Dark Energy & DESI, γ ≈ 1 Boundary, Measurement Without Observers, MRH, CDM Discrimination, Tier 1: Existing Data, What Synchronism Is Not, Falsifiability) plus 3 tools
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe, as a worked example of how a phenomenological ansatz gets audited to death; not for the physics
- **Overall impression**: The site has already done the referee report on itself. Every check I ran by hand reproduced the site's own number, and every reparametrization I spotted the site had already labelled. What is left to criticize is a few places where the audit language outruns what was actually swept, and one notational sloppiness that matters.

### Journey

My colleague said "interesting AI project," so I went in expecting a grand unified framework with a tanh in it and a "validated" badge on every page. The tanh is there. The badges say Failed, Reparametrization, Audited-Negative, and the scorecard on the landing page reads 0 confirmed predictions, 0 of 9 formerly-validated claims surviving. That set my expectations, and I spent the rest of the visit checking whether the self-audit is right rather than whether the theory is.

**Parameter Derivations** was my first stop, since that is where a framework like this lives or dies. There are no derivations. The page says so in its own voice: γ = 2/√N_corr "borrows from central-limit-theorem statistics," the factor 2 "is not rigorously derived," and the CLT step is applied to *correlated* degrees of freedom, which is exactly where 1/√N does not hold. The page also states the sign problem I would have raised: in any mean-field or finite-size-scaling picture, more correlated units give a *sharper* transition, and this ansatz gives a flatter one (BCS superconductors get γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, a "long gentle ramp"). The tanh itself: "The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen." Correct. The Ising tanh comes from a self-consistency condition m = tanh(βJzm); this one has no fixed-point equation, no free energy, no exponents. Calling it "mean-field" anywhere would be wrong, and the site does not.

The ρ_crit = A·V_flat² row is the ugliest thing on the site and the site knows it. The stated formula A = 4π/(βJ²GR₀²) evaluates to ≈4.6×10⁻⁵ in the stated units, the calibrated value is 0.029, and the archive bridges them with an unexplained 644× "unit conversion." That is not a derivation with an error; it is a fitted number with a formula pasted next to it. The page labels it "Fitted factor." Fine.

**Galaxy Rotation** is where I did the most algebra. The claimed identity: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1], and at γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). I checked it; it is exact for all x, not asymptotic, as the page says. So the "coherence function" in the galaxy sector is MOND's simple μ with a₀ rescaled by 2, and the free fit landing on γ = 0.489 (N_corr ≈ 17, not the "independent stars" N_corr = 1 the framework wanted) is the data telling you it wants MOND. The ΔBIC arithmetic is right: +7.1 for free γ against ln(2807) = 7.94 penalty means Δχ² ≈ −0.84, i.e. the extra parameter bought nothing.

Here is the one place I would push back. The identity only means "MOND for all x" if x = g_obs/a₀ (the μ-form, where the code solves g_bar = g_obs·tanh(γ ln(1 + g_obs/a₀'))). One sentence on the page says exactly that. But the page also writes the compander as a function of x with x = g_bar/a₀ in at least one place, and it lists g_obs = g_bar/C as a live convention. If you take those two together, at γ = ½ you get g_obs = g_bar + 2a₀: a constant acceleration offset, v² rising linearly in r, not a flat curve and not MOND. I computed both wirings side by side: at g_bar/a₀ = 0.01, the μ-form gives g_obs/a₀ = 0.147 (deep-MOND √(2x) = 0.141), while the g_bar-keyed division form gives 2.01. The page's conclusion is correct for the wiring it actually used, but the notation lets a reader think the identity holds for the other wiring too. Define x once, in the μ-form, and say the identity does not survive keying on g_bar.

**Tier 1 / Honest Assessment** on the boost ceiling. The framework's own discriminating prediction is B_max = 1/Ω_m = 3.17, and the SPARC dwarfs need f_DM = 0.927, which is B ≥ 13.7. The Honest Assessment says "no cosmic ratio supplies this." The 2026-09-18 sweep, quoted on the same page, tried (Ω_m−Ω_b)/Ω_b = 5.4 and Ω_m/Ω_b = 6.4, and the BTFR kill does not fire at either. Neither of those reaches 13.7 either. But 1/Ω_b ≈ 20.4 does, and it is the same construction as 1/Ω_m with baryons in the denominator. I could not find it in the sweep. Either it was tried and excluded (then say by which test) or it was not (then the "no cosmic ratio" sentence is stronger than the sweep). I suspect the honest answer is that B_max ≈ 20 never binds on any SPARC disc and the model is then plain MOND, which lands in the Reparametrization column rather than the Failed column. That distinction matters for the scorecard's "2 framework-specific mechanism roots."

**Globular clusters**, on Honest Assessment. The table quotes outer-slope residuals ⟨obs − pred⟩ of −0.057 (Newtonian), −0.093 (MOND+EFE), −0.211 (density-keyed γ = 0.489), and a "× Newtonian" column of 1.0, 1.7, 3.7. The ratios reproduce (I get 1.63, 3.70, 1.95). But the Newtonian residual is described as the "systematics budget," i.e. consistent with zero within its error, and a ratio to a number consistent with zero is not a statistic. The −0.211 should be quoted with its own uncertainty against the Newtonian one, not as "3.7×."

**Dark Energy & DESI**. The Friedmann substitution at γ = ½ gives ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C = 2ρ_crit, a constant, so the family contains Λ exactly. I checked that line and it holds. The sign lock sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a) is a real structural statement, and the page is right that DESI's preferred quadrant (w₀ > −1, w_a < 0) is forbidden by it, and also right that this is not a kill because γ = ½ is Λ. The claim I found most interesting on the whole site is buried here: reading the compander at *local* density with pressure (fluid Cardassian) and applying matter-power-spectrum limits pins |2γ − 1| ≲ 10⁻⁵. Galaxies retain γ ∈ 0.425–0.600. So the cosmological γ and the galaxy γ can only be the same parameter if γ is ½ to five decimals, i.e. if the framework is MOND + Λ exactly. That is the cleanest single sentence against a "one equation" framing on the site, and it is on a sub-page most readers will not reach.

**γ ≈ 1 Boundary (chemistry)**. Sound velocity r = 0.982 against γ, and a 2-parameter polynomial in Z matches within |Δr| ≤ 0.07 and sometimes wins (0.99 vs 0.87). Any monotone function of Z gets r ≥ 0.9 on density-monotone targets. The page says this. The original rationale (γ ≈ 1 maximizes curvature of C) was withdrawn on 2026-08-09 because dC/dx is monotone in γ and C is globally concave, which I confirmed in my head in about ten seconds. The remaining hole: how γ (or N_corr) was assigned to each material before fitting is "undocumented." Without that, the r values are not evaluable at all, null or no null.

**Measurement Without Observers** and **MRH**. The decoherence rate Γ = κ²(1−c) is labelled as a special case of correlated-dephasing variance (Palma–Suominen–Ekert 1996), and the Born rule is labelled Gleason/Zurek. Both correct identifications. "MRH crossing" is a renaming of environment-induced decoherence with the same predictions. The MRH definition (predictive sufficiency plus predictive closure) is a Markov blanket with a dynamics word attached. Speculative badge, appropriate. Nothing to test.

**What Synchronism Is Not**. The relativity statement: a discrete absolute-time substrate gives c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻²–10⁻³ against cavity bounds ≲ 10⁻¹⁸ and comagnetometer bounds ≲ 10⁻³⁰, a 16–28 order gap. The α/π is the standard one-loop lattice-regulator argument (Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia, Vucetich 2004) and it is not cited. It should be, because that citation is the whole argument; without it the reader is asked to accept a loop factor from nowhere.

**Falsifiability**. 2 of 26 kill criteria well-formed, 11 with no number, 0 of 24 registered before the data were looked at. As a grad student who has sat through enough journal clubs, that is roughly the batting average of the field's "we make testable predictions" sections, and the site is the only one I have seen count it.

**What is worth a researcher's time** is the For Researchers page's five "framework-independent negatives." Two are genuinely useful and, as far as I know, not written down elsewhere in this form:

- **Unidentifiability of a knee above the sampled range** (Fisher ρ(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000; only γ/A identifiable; χ²/N moves 1.07–2.7× while A moves by 10⁹). Generic, but nobody publishes it because nobody publishes the negative. I could cite it against the next density-keyed proposal I referee.

SPARC wants γ ≈ ½, which is the slow simple-μ return that Cassini kills (Hees et al. 2016; Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024 at 8.7σ). Any one-parameter family with that tie is squeezed. The site presents this as an inherited MOND failure, which is correct, but the family-level reason for the inheritance is the transferable content and it is not stated anywhere.

The A2ACW page is where I expected to find methodological overreach. Instead I found the calibration data: canonical arm false-flagged 6/6 genuine discoveries (Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking, Noether) as reparametrizations, Youden's J = 0 on n = 6 with a 95% CI spanning [−0.71, +0.46]. That is an instrument with no measured discrimination. The site then correctly says the "0 confirmed" headline cannot rest on the audit and rests on external data instead. What it does not do is run the positive-control arm (a verified post-cutoff discovery, citations stripped, scored blind). That has been "never executed" for months. It is cheap and it is the only thing that would tell you whether A2ACW can detect novelty at all.
