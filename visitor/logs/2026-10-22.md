## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (/, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /hard-problem, /key-claims)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: It's refreshingly honest that the idea mostly lost, but almost every page buries its simple story under walls of caveats and jargon I can't read.

### Journey
I came in from Reddit expecting a "one equation explains everything" crank site. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", plus "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything", and then the bold line "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated", stopped me from leaving. Nobody does that. I was hooked for about a minute.

Then I scrolled. The homepage is huge (the fetch put it at roughly 7–8k words). It covers the marching-band-vs-crowd idea for "C", which I liked, and then I hit tanh, ln, ρcrit, MRH, MOND, ΛCDM, SPARC, BTFR, ΔBIC, dex, CHSH, fσ₈, Youden's J and DESI, all on the landing page. The scoreboard ("we ran six tests that could have shown it wrong, and it lost all six") was the clearest sentence I read all day. "So is the idea alive or dead?" says "dead as far as it has been tested" but "alive and still being worked." I sort of get that, but as a layperson I wondered why I should spend time on something that lost six out of six.

**Why Synchronism** (Start Here, ~8 min): I liked the pitch about physics having a "fragmentation problem". But the page quickly fills up with YBCO, Abrikosov–Gor'kov, Hamiltonian, p=5×10⁻⁶, Tully–Fisher and "ansatz". The first visible line is a navigation note ("Beginner path, first step. This is the page the nav's 'Start Here' points to"), which is an odd thing to read first, like seeing a stage direction. The "Step 2: First Encounter →" link was easy to find.

**First Encounter**: This is the best page for me. It has sections A–G, a progress bar and a Continue button, and analogies I could use: a marching band, a dimmer switch, a whisper in a loud room, and an atom that doesn't "feel" Andromeda. The sentence "the sign came out backwards (predicted correlation +0.55, measured −0.55)" is something I could repeat to a friend. I still lost the thread at BEC/BCS/Tc, "Zeno effect", "critical exponents" and "reparametrization".

**Coherence Explorer**: Two sliders and an S-curve, so at least I could play with something. The "sharp cliff vs gentle ramp" description of γ made sense. But between the controls and the plot there are long retraction notes. The page tells me the default setting "fails two tests" and that the tool is "a one-parameter family plus a pan control, presented as two parameters." I didn't understand that, and it made me feel the toy I was playing with was broken on purpose.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter**: The "New here?" box was great: look at the dots, the gray Newton line sagging below them, and the green and violet curves. "The gap is the 'dark matter problem'" is the clearest explanation of dark matter I've seen on any site. The violet curve visibly misses and MOND fits. But the legend label for violet is very long: "This framework's equation, the tested reading (failed): Newton's shape ×1.78 in speed (= √3.17, the boost in gravity)". There's also a dashed "untested display reading: no visible effect here", and I don't know why a curve with no effect is shown. Disclaimers and a "For physicists" block sit above the three-step "how to read this chart."

**γ Calculator**: Presets for ideal gas, water, galaxy, BEC, and so on. It's fun to click, but the page tells me the formula is "Audited-Negative", "gets the direction backwards", and that the preset values are "back-fits, not measurements." So why is it a calculator at all? The page answers "kept to show how the formula fails," which is fair, but the tool's name sets up the wrong expectation.

**Honest Assessment**: The "If you read nothing else" section is exactly what I needed, and the plain-language list of what failed was good. Then I learned the page is about 18,000 words. Even the "6 refutations" headline wobbles here: it's "4 to 8 depending on the rule", "at most 5 independent", and one is "pending the project lead." I appreciated the honesty, but the one number I thought I understood turned soft.

**Glossary**: Every term I looked up was there (tanh, MOND, MRH, reparametrization, ΔBIC, dex). MOND and "reparametrization" were readable. The tanh entry jumps straight into "Ising mean-field theory". MRH ("the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter") is nice, but the full name "Markov Relevancy Horizon" never really clicked. I saw no search box, just an A–Z index.

**Hard Problem / consciousness**: I clicked this because consciousness is what Reddit loves. It's short (~900 words body) and readable, and honestly labeled "Speculative — Proposed Reframing — not a finding." It says outright that the C ≈ 0.50 threshold is "non-falsifiable as currently stated." That's fair, though it leaves me with nothing to hold onto.

**Key Claims**: There are three claims (quantum = synchronization, consciousness equation, dark matter = incomplete decoherence) with badges Speculative / Speculative / Failed. The question-style titles are inviting. The consciousness section presents "8 approaches agree on 0.50" as evidence, and then the caveat says the agreement is "expected, not discovered." Even I noticed the whiplash.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked; the sliders are intuitive, but the surrounding page is confusing. The S-curve and the cliff-vs-ramp idea land. Caveats between the controls and the plot, and a default that is "failing two tests", make it feel like a broken toy.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked; intuitive for the core lesson. The "New here?" box and "the gap is the dark matter problem" are excellent. The legend labels are too long and there's one curve too many.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the idea lost all its tests, what exactly is "still alive"? Is it a different equation, or the same one with new tests? The alive/dead section didn't give me one concrete thing being worked on now.
2. What does "coherence" have to do with "synchronization" in the name? Is something literally ticking in sync?
3. The plotter shows MOND fitting. Is MOND then the right answer, and why don't scientists just accept it?
4. Who is "dp"? "AI agents with one human overseeing it" is fascinating. Is the AI-research angle the real story here?
5. If the consciousness number can't be tested, why is it one of the three headline claims?

### Verdict
The honesty is the hook. "We proposed this, we tested it, it did not hold" made me trust the site more than any physics-y site I've clicked from Reddit. The galaxy plotter's beginner box actually taught me what the dark matter problem is. But nearly every page puts its simple story behind a thick layer of caveats, acronyms and revision notes written for physicists, so I got the headline ("nice idea, it failed, here's why") but not much more. I'd come back for First Encounter and the plotter, not for the rest.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (/, /parameter-derivation(s), /galaxy-rotation, /honest-assessment, /key-claims, /gamma-boundary, /dark-energy, /measurement-without-observers, plus tools /gamma-calculator, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe. I'd come back as a case study in self-auditing, not as physics.
- **Overall impression**: Every number I re-derived by hand checks out, and the site has already done to itself most of the demolition I came prepared to do. What's left are a few labels that outlived their premises and one structural point the site states piecemeal but never assembles.

### Journey
My colleague said "interesting AI project", so I expected a grand-unified sigmoid. The landing page disarmed that in one line ("We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."), followed by a scoreboard: 0 confirmed novel predictions, 6 refutations, 0/9 audited claims surviving. I went straight to the derivations.

**/parameter-derivation** is the most useful page on the site for my persona. I checked the algebra myself:
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1] is correct. At γ = ½ it is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), so MOND's simple μ with a₀ → 2a₀′, as the page says.
- On the "mean-field" question, the page beats me to it: Ising's tanh comes from a self-consistency loop, and C(ρ) has none. "The Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen." It also says there is no critical point, so this is not Landau. That is exactly the objection I had queued.
- For γ = 2/√N_corr, the page flags both the CLT circularity (correlated degrees of freedom are not independent variables) and the sign inversion (mean-field transitions sharpen with more correlated units, while this formula flattens them). Both criticisms are correct.
- a₀ = cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ✓. Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG): I get 0.248 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc² ✓, so it really is "one number, not two".
- A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²) with β_J = 1 and R₀ = 8 kpc: I get 3.09×10⁻³⁰ kg s² m⁻⁵ = **4.56×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻²**, which matches the page's "≈4.6×10⁻⁵, ~600× below 0.029". The summary never states the units of A. I had to reconstruct them to check the number, and the galaxy plotter's "ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat²" is likewise unitless.

**/galaxy-rotation**. In the deep regime of the acceleration-keyed compander, C ≈ γx, so g_bar = g_obs·C gives g_obs = √(g_bar·a₀′/γ). That is deep MOND with a₀ = a₀′/γ. This is why the page says a₀′/γ is the identified combination. The tail 1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ} is correct. The ΔBIC treatment is better than most papers I referee: +184 by point vs ≈ +11 by galaxy, a galaxy-block bootstrap giving N_eff ≈ 150, and an explicit retraction of an earlier guessed discount factor. One sentence threw me: "Free γ costs ln N ≈ 7.94 in BIC, so the observed +7.1 means Δχ² ≈ −0.84". +7.1 doesn't match the +11 quoted two lines earlier, and ln N = 7.94 implies N ≈ 2,807 (points) in a paragraph about the galaxy-level count (N = 166, ln N = 5.1). I can't tell which estimator +7.1 belongs to.

**/honest-assessment** has the nine-badge legend and a refutation table. TEST-09: 0.40/√(0.07² + 0.10²) = 3.28σ ✓ (4.0σ without the prediction's error ✓). ### Journey
I came in to answer one question: is there a single prediction here that differs from MOND + EFE + ΛCDM and hasn't already been killed? In 25 years of refereeing I have rarely seen a framework write its own obituary this precisely. So I went looking for what's left.

**Dark energy (/dark-energy).** The mechanism is G_eff = G/C(ρ̄) in Friedmann, giving ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C, with w running monotonically from −2γ in the far past to −1. That implies sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a). I checked the DESI DR2 w₀w_a table against memory, all four rows: DESI+CMB −0.42±0.21 / −1.75±0.58 / 3.1σ; +Pantheon+ −0.838±0.055 / 2.8σ; +Union3 −0.667±0.088 / 3.8σ; +DESY5 −0.752±0.057 / −0.86 / 4.2σ. All correct. The site states plainly that every DESI row sits in the forbidden (thawing) quadrant. It places the lock correctly in Caldwell & Linder's (2005) freezing class ("A freezing detection would select 'freezing dark energy', not this family"). It credits Cardassian (Freese & Lewis 2002) and cites the Sandvik–Tegmark–Zaldarriaga–Waga (2004) perturbation problem for unified-DM models. That is the right literature, and it is cited. The fit lands at γ = 0.487 with Δχ² = −0.3 vs ΛCDM for one extra parameter, which is nothing. The page is honest that TEST-26 "cannot single out the framework", because any DR3 kill also kills Λ. Agreed. On the dark-energy side this is a freezing-quintessence parametrization with ΛCDM at its centre, and the page says so.

**Galaxies / MOND (/mond-unification, /tier-1-existing).** In the acceleration-keyed branch γ ≈ 0.49 is the MOND simple ν, and TEST-25 books the Cassini/RAR quadrupole tension (Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024, 8.7σ marginalized) as "Failed (inherited from MOND)". The card separates the published 8.7σ from the in-house unmarginalized 17.95σ, and admits that at galaxy level the γ interval opens to ~0.3–1.7. Good bookkeeping. One thing I couldn't resolve: the Cassini quadrupole *is* an external-field effect (the Galactic field acting on the Solar System). Yet /for-researchers says "EFE = 0 exactly holds for the algebraic reading". So which reading carries TEST-25 and TEST-02? If the wide-binary prediction uses the algebraic acceleration reading (EFE = 0), binaries at g_int ~ 0.1 a₀ would get the full deep-MOND boost, roughly 3× in velocity. DR3 excludes that on *both* sides of the Chae–Banik dispute, because even Chae's ~1.4× in g is an EFE-suppressed number. The "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie" label is only right if the reading includes an EFE. The card should say which reading it uses.

**The real finding of this pass: the density-keyed field equation is Refracted Gravity.** /mond-unification#field-equation gives the working equation ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ and credits it, correctly, as Matsakos & Diaferio (2016), with C playing the role of RG's permittivity ε(ρ). The ledger then says the density-keyed branch is excluded: "ΔBIC +2843 with γ free" on SPARC, plus lunar laser ranging (Ġ/G, Hofmann & Müller 2018: "excludes 68 of 74 laws at 1 pc"). But /honest-assessment and /for-researchers both state "Refracted Gravity is **not** excluded, even with this framework's Ωm floor substituted in". As far as I could tell from the text, the +2843 fit used "L2 or its algebraic form" (the spherical solution g = g_bar/C) on *disc* galaxies. In a disc the field equation refracts field lines vertically, and that is exactly the mechanism by which RG fits rotation curves (Cesare et al.'s disc work, and the E0–E7 flattening dependence in Cesare+2022 for ellipticals). So what is actually excluded is a specific tanh permittivity with this framework's knee, evaluated algebraically. The class is not excluded. Two consequences:
1. The two "predictions that differ from MOND" listed on /test-catalog (external-field *refraction*, and ambient density at fixed g_ext) are described as living on an excluded branch. The refraction effect is generic to the RG class, which survives, so it is RG's prediction (prior art), not a dead one. The ambient-density one is estimated as systematics-limited at signal/systematics ≈ 4×10⁻³, so it is dead in practice regardless.
2. Nowhere does the site name RG's own distinctive observable: the boost depends on system *flattening* at fixed baryonic mass (RG's E0 vs E7 ellipticals). That is the one place where this field-equation class differs from MOND in a direction current data can reach. If the framework adopts the RG equation, it inherits that test.
