## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, What Synchronism Is Not, Honest Assessment, Coherence Explorer, γ Calculator, Two Reframes)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest "big idea" site I've seen off Reddit: it tells me in the first paragraph that the idea lost, and the beginner path keeps telling me I'm allowed to stop. But every tool page starts with a wall of failed-badge fine print before showing me the toy.

### Journey
Clicked through from Reddit expecting a "one equation explains everything" crank site. The landing headline asks exactly that question, and then the second paragraph says "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." That stopped me from bouncing. The marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza picture made "coherence" click right away, and I liked the warning that this isn't quantum coherence (I'd have assumed it was).

The landing paragraph is very long, though. It packs in AI-run research, "dp", no journal review, MOND with a full parenthetical definition, a cluster-scale problem, and "retrodiction". I skimmed most of it. The numbers row ("Prospective predictions confirmed: 0", "Refutations executed: 6 … (3–8 under rules on file)") confused me. Why would the number of failures depend on rules? Later I learned it's about close calls and shared causes, but on the landing page "3–8" reads like they can't count.

**Why Synchronism? (step 1).** This was great for me. "What happened, in three lines" gave the whole story, then "Beginner path: you can stop here", so I did. The fold below is labelled "For physicists", so I peeked once, saw p=5×10⁻⁶ and R², and closed it.

**First Encounter (step 2).** This is the best page for someone like me. The "What tanh does, in plain words" line (S-curve, dimmer switch) and the "ln squashes huge ranges, so 10×, 100×, 1000× become 2.3, 4.6, 6.9" line actually taught me something. MRH as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter", with the Andromeda example, was clear. Section C ("the γ parameter — the original idea, and what happened to it") lost me: γ = 2/√N_corr, then "predicted +0.55, measured −0.55", then BCS (never explained). Section D's heading uses "reparametrization" with no explanation, and I only got that word later from a badge legend. The page says sections appear one at a time with a Continue button. In what I saw, the bottom of section G had a "Continue ↓ Section B" link, which felt backwards. Maybe the progress bar loops? Small, but it made me wonder if I'd missed something.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (step 3).** The "New here? Look at three things on the chart" box is excellent: dots = telescopes, gray dashed = visible matter only, the gap = dark matter puzzle. That's the clearest explanation of the dark matter problem I've had, and I've watched PBS Space Time on it. But above that box sit two badges written for physicists: "Failed — published density law C_ρ (γ = 2, ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat²) in the division wiring g = g_bar/C, floored at Ω_m…" and "the quadrature wiring (dashed) is inert". To me that's hieroglyphics, and it's the first thing on the page. The galaxy list (DDO 154, NGC 2403, UGC 128, NGC 3198, NGC 7331) is just catalogue codes with no hint of which is "a small one" or "a big spiral". The info panel helped a bit ("Dwarf irregular (gas-dominated)"). The "twin pair" suggestion (NGC 2403 vs UGC 128, same flat speed) is a nice thing to try. I didn't understand why green MOND "partly" follows the dots "because its flat level is set from each galaxy's own speed". Isn't that cheating? The page seems to admit it, but I couldn't tell if it matters.

**What Synchronism Is Not (step 4).** Short and plain: "Not proven", "Not checked by science journals", "Not a replacement for today's physics". The relativity bullet ("its built-in universal clock would naturally break that rule") was surprising and interesting. The page never told me before this that there's a universal clock. Where did that come from?

**Honest Assessment (step 5).** It sends me to the "If you read nothing else" box, which is good, because the page is enormous (the site says ~18,000 words). The opening explains clearly that the "visitor/maintainer/explorer" are AI personas and that no outside human expert has audited anything. I appreciated being told. The "What failed" table is all TEST-09, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC +184, Cassini 8.7σ. I couldn't follow any row except "Dwarf dark-matter fractions: 69% of galaxies exceed the 68.5% cap", which I sort of got: the formula can only add so much, and real dwarfs need more.

**Two Reframes (side-trip).** The CRT analogy is a great hook: a beam scanning so fast you see a still picture, so is superposition just fast scanning? Then the page immediately says the literal reading "was tested … and failed." The rest of the page (Bell, CHSH, Tsirelson, Kochen–Specker, PR-box) went way over my head.

Across the tools, the "what went wrong" fine print comes before the fun. I'd come back for the honesty, but I'd want each tool page to put the toy first and the autopsy second.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Learning Paths, Glossary, Interactive Tools hub, Honest Assessment, Key Claims, Crossover Regime Visualizer, Consciousness Threshold Demo, Consciousness Threshold)

- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The site has a strong information architecture: a canonical badge table, a label-system map, per-step reading times that add up, and status-first glossary entries. The remaining defects are consistency defects: one number that differs between the glossary and every footer, terms used before they are defined, and duplicate names for the same tool or concept.

### Journey

The footer repeats the main routes in two rows and ends with a status line: "0 confirmed predictions · 6 refutations executed under the current counting rule (3–8 under rules on file)". It links to the ledger and to the counting rules. A page that puts its scoreboard in the footer is unusual, and I liked it.

**Acronyms on the landing page.** MOND, MRH and A2ACW are expanded at first use. The page also has an "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC)" line linking to a key, which is a good pattern. The key itself sits under "Scientific Outcomes", well below the first uses. Several terms never get expanded on the page: BCS ("Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs…"), MNRAS (in a table row), LLM ("The auditors were LLM agents"), and dex (in the Key Claims teaser). The Glossary does define BCS, dex and CFD. It has no LLM entry and no AI entry, although the site's whole methodology rests on AI agents.

**Learning Paths.** This page is the cleanest on the site. The Beginner path's per-step times (8 + 10 + 6 + 9 + 4) sum to exactly the stated ~37 min. Step 5 points to the `#if-you-read-nothing-else` anchor, so "stop at the box" is built into the link, not just stated. The step positions match what the landing cards claim: Test Roadmap is Advanced 8/8 and Research Philosophy is Methodology 1/6. The landing "Where to Start" grid has no card for Beginner step 4 (What Synchronism Is Not), while Honest Assessment appears twice, as "Shortcut" and as step 5.

**Glossary.** The glossary is well built. It opens with "How to read labels on this site", a table mapping each label system (badge families, operational states, tool kinds, levels) to the question it answers. It has an A–Z index, 123 terms, and a "Learn more →" link on each entry. Entries put status first ("Status first: the original formula γ = 2/√N_corr is audited-negative…"), so a reader can't take the formula as current. All nine badges have entries, and the retired ones say plainly: "⚠ Retired badge — no longer used; kept so old links resolve".

I found these defects:

I checked the raw HTML of the live page, and both strings are really there; this is not a fetch artifact. A reader who sees two ranges for the site's headline number will distrust both.

Ceiling Exceeded and Empty Intersection call themselves operational labels but are not in the table's operational row. Sign Correction and "Unrunnable as stated" are in the row but have no entries.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, Interactive Tools hub, Parameter Derivations, Coherence Function, Galaxy Rotation, Tier 1: Existing Data, Honest Assessment, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Chemistry Correlation Explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every derivation I redid by hand is correct, and the site makes the reparametrization case against itself more sharply than I would have. The defects I found are second-order: one muddled identifiability sentence, a σ that combines two errors drawn from the same 123 galaxies as if they were independent, and a beginner tool whose "visible matter" baseline is built from the BTFR.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as "an interesting AI project". I expected a tanh with a dozen free parameters and a "validated" sticker on every slide. What I found is closer to a referee report the authors wrote against themselves. I skipped the beginner material and went straight to Parameter Derivations, Coherence Function and Galaxy Rotation, then to the test ledger. I checked the algebra with pencil and calculator as I went.

**The algebra checks out.** I verified all of these:
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1]. This is just tanh u = (e^2u − 1)/(e^2u + 1).
- At γ = ½ this reduces to x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), identically.
- Small x: C ≈ γx. Large x: 1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^−2γ.
- dC/dx = γ(1 − C²)/(1+x), and C is concave on x ≥ 0.
- C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = tanh(2 ln 2) = 0.882, and C(10ρ_crit) = 0.99986.
- Refracted Gravity's floored-Hill permittivity at γ = 2 and x = 1: 0.059 with the knee doubled, 0.5 without.

The physics claims also hold. "Not Landau" is right: a saturating compander with a non-negative argument is analytic and has no critical point. The Ising comparison is right too: m = tanh(βJzm) is a self-consistency loop, and C(ρ) has no loop. The γ = ½ cosmology identity, (1−C)/C = 2/x ⇒ ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit = const, means that branch *is* Λ, and the page says so. That is the cleanest piece of self-criticism on the site.

The page does not say how the ansatz went wrong, but I think the diagnosis is simple. 1/√N is the CLT's relative fluctuation *width*, and the ansatz uses it as a *slope*. Sharpness ∝ 1/width ∝ √N. The original error looks like a width/sharpness conflation, not an arbitrary guess, and saying so would make the "sign-inverted" verdict feel diagnosed rather than merely observed.

The second-order expansion is C = γx − (γ/2)x² + O(x³). In ρ that is C ≈ (γ/ρ_c)ρ − (γ/2ρ_c²)ρ². The γ–ρ_crit degeneracy is broken at second order by the −γx²/2 term for *every* γ. γ(2γ−1)x²/2 is the deviation from the *Hill/μ_simple* shape γx/(1+γx), which is a different question. My arithmetic on the MOND line gives (3.81 − 3.753)/√(0.10² + 0.04²) = 0.53σ; the page says 0.6σ, which needs either the observed error alone (0.57) or rounding up. **Galaxies.** The framework's two departures from MOND were (i) a bounded boost, a ceiling B_max on g_obs/g_bar, and (ii) a density-keyed switch, C(ρ) instead of C(g). Both are dead, and the site says so plainly.
- *The ceiling.* TEST-10 has 106/153 discs above 1/Ω_m. More decisively, the KiDS galaxy–galaxy lensing RAR (Brouwer+2021) excludes the ceiling under all three candidate conventions (1/Ω_m, (Ω_m−Ω_b)/Ω_b, Ω_m/Ω_b). That is correct physics: the lensing RAR shows no saturation down to g_bar ~ 10⁻¹⁵ m/s², where the boost is well above 6.4.
- *The density switch.* The environment test came back at r² = 0.0001. The Solar-neighbourhood Ġ/G no-go (For Researchers item 6) kills 68/74 algebraically density-keyed laws against LLR's (7.1 ± 7.6)×10⁻¹⁴ yr⁻¹, and the density-keyed RAR fit loses by ΔBIC +2843.
- *What remains.* With γ free, the acceleration-keyed C(g) lands on γ ≈ 0.49, and at γ = ½, tanh(½ ln(1+x)) = x/(x+2) is exactly MOND's simple μ. The site calls this a reparametrization. It is right to.
- *Upcoming tests.* Gaia DR4 wide binaries (TEST-02, December 2026) and BIG-SPARC can only re-test the γ = 2 pin or tie with MOND. The site says this itself ("cannot move Bucket 0", "cannot confirm novelty").

**Cosmology.** I checked the Dark Energy page's algebra and it is right:
- For any slaved ρ_DE = ρ_m F(x), continuity gives w = dlnF/dlnx.
- For this F, w runs monotonically from −2γ in the past to −1 in the future. That forces sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a): freezing only, never thawing, never crossing.
- At high density this is Cardassian with n = 1−2γ (Freese & Lewis 2002, credited).
- At γ = ½ it is exactly Λ (ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit).

DESI DR2 prefers the opposite quadrant at 2.8–4.2σ, depending on the supernova sample. The quoted DR2 w₀w_a numbers match the published table as I know it. So the family nests ΛCDM, can't reach DESI's preference, and TEST-26 at DR3 is a kill-or-tie with no win branch for the framework as postulated. The page says this.

Two things the page does *not* say:
1. **The modified-G reading has no Ġ/G check.** It is offered as an equivalent background reading, G_eff = G/C(ρ̄). At γ = ½, C = x/(x+2) gives dlnC/dt = −3H(1−C). Today C = Ω_m, so Ġ/G = +3(1−Ω_m)H₀ ≈ 2.06 H₀ ≈ 1.4×10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹. That is ~1,900× above the LLR bound the site itself quotes on For Researchers. On the modified-G reading, then, the background is excluded locally at the ~10³σ level before any perturbation is computed. The only reading that survives is a dark-energy *fluid*, which is ordinary dark energy. The Dark Energy page has no mention of LLR, Ġ/G or time-varying G. The same LLR argument appears in the galaxy-sector no-go but is never applied here.
2. **The status line contradicts itself.** The page calls the sector "Reparametrization", "a live position" and "a currently-disfavoured falsifiable position" on the same page. A reader can't tell which one the site stands behind.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked (static check). ### Unanswered Questions
1. Under the mean-density modified-G reading, has anyone written down Ġ/G? My estimate, 3(1−Ω_m)H₀ ≈ 1.4×10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹, excludes it by ~10³ against LLR. If it's excluded, why is it presented as an equivalent reading of the DESI fit?
2. Why is the KiDS lensing exclusion of every boost ceiling not a scoreboard row, when a MOND-inherited row (TEST-25) and a Bell-theorem construction check are?
3. Is there *any* registered test whose win branch selects this framework rather than MOND, ΛCDM, Refracted Gravity or Cardassian? From the Test Roadmap I count zero. If so, should the headline be "0 live distinguishing predictions" rather than "0 confirmed"?
4. A2ACW's calibration arm used one model to write and score restatements. Has the cross-vendor arm or the post-cutoff arm (both "registered as proposals") been scheduled? Without them, J = 0 can't distinguish "no novelty present" from "the instrument is blind to novelty".
5. If the solar-neighbourhood Ġ/G no-go is framework-independent, has it been checked against the chameleon/symmetron literature for prior art (e.g. Brax et al. on density-dependent couplings with LLR)? The scope note excludes gradient couplings, but algebraic density-dependent G has a literature too.
6. Wide binaries: the density branch is "identically null in this ratio". Is there any Gaia DR4 observable (e.g. a residual against ambient stellar density at fixed g_ext) that the density branch predicts and MOND doesn't, or is the density branch already dead by LLR in that regime anyway?

### Verdict
As a referee, I'd say this site makes no claim I'd reject. It makes almost no claim at all that survives, and it says so first. The physics content reduces to MOND (galaxies, at γ ≈ ½) and ΛCDM or Cardassian (cosmology, at γ = ½). Every branch that departed from them has been executed and killed, including one cosmology reading (modified-G) the site hasn't yet noticed is dead by LLR. The defects are in accounting rather than physics: a refutation count that includes shared and theorem rows while omitting the cleanest independent kill, and one status line that can't decide between "reparametrization" and "live". What is genuinely new and worth citing is the methodology finding: an AI adversarial loop with measured specificity 0/6, whose only discriminating power came from running code on public data. That deserves to be the headline result.
