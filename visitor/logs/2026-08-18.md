# Synchronism Visitor Browse Log — 2026-08-18

Four-persona pass over https://synchronism-site.vercel.app/

---

## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The opening question is genuinely great and one page (First Encounter) is written for me, but by step 3 of the *beginner* path I'm reading about Hill functions and universality classes, and the site never tells me what I'm supposed to do with the fact that its own scoreboard says the idea failed.

### Journey

I clicked through from Reddit. The headline — **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** — is exactly the kind of thing that makes me watch a 25-minute YouTube video. Good hook. Two buttons: "Start Exploring" and "See What Failed." I've never seen a site offer me the failures on the front page. That made me trust it immediately.

Then, still on the landing page, I hit the equation:

`C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`

There's a little key underneath telling me tanh is an "S-curve saturation function" and ln is "logarithmic compression." Neither of those phrases means anything to me. I know what an S-curve *looks* like, but "saturation function" and "logarithmic compression" are two more things to look up. I scrolled past it, which I suspect is what most people do.

Below that was the scoreboard, and this is where I got confused in a way that never really resolved:

- Confirmed predictions: **0**
- Refutations on external data: **6**
- "Validated" claims surviving expert audit: **0 of 6**

So... the answer to "what if one equation described reality" is "it doesn't"? I admire that this is on the front page. But nothing on that page told me why the site still exists, or what I'm meant to feel. Am I reading about a discovery or an autopsy? I clicked "Start Exploring" mostly to find out.

**Start Here** was fine. It repeated the equation, gave me the crowd-vs-marching-band idea, and had a results table where every row said something like "Failed," "Reparametrization," or "Template bias." There were greyed-out asides marked "(optional — skip freely)" full of words like *ansatz* and *Hamiltonian*. I appreciated being told I could skip them. I skipped them.

**First Encounter** (step 2) was the best page on the site for someone like me. "A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence." Then it explained density using a lone electron versus neutron star material at 10¹⁴ times Earth's density — that's a Veritasium-grade comparison, I got it instantly. And the Markov Relevancy Horizon, which sounds terrifying, was explained as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" with the line *"a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy."* I understood that completely. If the whole site were written like this page I'd have read all of it.

**The Core Idea** (step 3) is where I fell off. Within one screen I was reading about a "Hill saturation function," an identity showing the logarithm is "cosmetic," and then a list of things the framework lacks: "singularities or critical points (thus no critical exponents)," "universality classes." I don't know what any of those are. I don't know whether not having them is bad. This is step **3 of 6 in the path labelled "No physics background needed."** It is not step 3 of a no-physics-background path.

The same page told me something that broke my mental model: coherence here is **not** quantum coherence, and superconductors — the one "coherent" thing I've actually heard of, the thing YouTube always calls "quantum weirdness at human scale" — score *low* on this scale. I re-read that three times. So the site's word "coherence" means roughly the opposite of the word "coherence" as I've encountered it everywhere else. That's not necessarily wrong, but it's a big ask and it's mentioned in passing.

**What Synchronism Is Not** (step 4, still "beginner") name-drops the Standard Model, QFT, GR, ΛCDM, MOND, "Session #616," and "3,308 AI-to-AI sessions (A2ACW protocol)." At this point I stopped pretending I was on a beginner path.

I went to **Tools**, because clicking things is more fun than reading things.

**Coherence Explorer** — this was good. Two sliders, a curve, three numbers underneath. The instruction "move γ from 2.0 downward and watch the curve flatten" is exactly the kind of hand-holding I want; more tools should tell me what to try. But then I noticed a warning saying the γ-to-particle-count relationship "runs backwards against real condensed-matter physics." So the tool is showing me something the site says is wrong? I don't know whether I just learned something or wasted two minutes.

**Equation Anatomy** — genuinely excellent, and it's *hidden*. This is where tanh finally got explained in words I understand: *"like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0, input very large → output near 1."* And ln: *"a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger."* Those two sentences are worth more to me than everything on the landing page. They should be **on** the landing page, right under the equation. Instead they're buried behind Tools, which I only found because I was clicking around.

But this page also gave me my sharpest confusion. It states, as a plain fact, that **C(ρcrit) ≈ 0.88**. I had just come from the Coherence Explorer, where the readout at *its own default settings* said **C(ρcrit) = 0.3272**. Same quantity, two pages, two very different numbers, no explanation. (I eventually guessed it's because the two pages use different γ — the plotter uses γ=2, the explorer defaults to 0.49 — but nothing told me that, and I shouldn't have to reverse-engineer it.) Which brings me to the broader problem: I have now seen γ = 0.49, γ ≈ 1, and γ = 2 presented on three different pages, each as if it were *the* value. Nobody tells me which one is real, or that the question is open.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** — I was excited for this one; dark matter is the reason I clicked the Reddit link. It has five curves. Reading the legend as a normal person:

- blue dots — what we actually see. Fine.
- grey dashed — what we'd expect from visible stuff. Fine.
- **violet — "Synchronism — REAL C(ρ), γ=2 (max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on)"**
- **amber — "Illustration only — hand-tuned to fit, NOT computed from the theory"**
- green — MOND, someone else's 1983 theory, which fits.

So the theory's actual line does nothing (it sits on top of the "visible matter only" line), there's a fake line in the chart that someone drew by hand to look right, and the competitor's line fits. I think I understood the point — the honest version of the theory fails here, and the amber curve is there to show what a dishonest version would look like. But I had to work that out myself from parenthetical asides. A single sentence above the chart saying *"watch what happens: the theory's real prediction is the violet line, and it never leaves the grey one — that's the failure"* would have made this the most powerful page on the site instead of the most confusing.

**Honest Assessment** was long but I read a lot of it, because at this point I wanted to know whether the whole thing was dead. It's blunt in a way I found weirdly refreshing: "the framework does not survive as physics." I couldn't follow the specifics — "3.3σ," "ΔBIC=+184," "17.95σ" — sigma is a word I know from headlines about the Higgs boson and that's it. But I got the gist.

Except: this page says **"the honest count: 4 genuine framework-specific failures."** The landing page scoreboard says **6**. Both numbers are presented confidently. I don't know which one to repeat if someone asks me about this site.

**Consciousness Threshold Demo** — I clicked this because "consciousness" is catnip. It turned out to be the most understandable thing on the site: eight different approaches all land near C = 0.50, and the page immediately tells me this is a *mathematical artifact* — 0.50 is just the middle of a 0-to-1 range, so of course things land there — and not evidence of anything. It even says a previously claimed statistic "lacks source documentation." I understood that argument completely, without any physics. It's a small masterpiece of explaining a negative result to a layperson. Whoever wrote this page should rewrite The Core Idea.

**Glossary** — good definitions, plain English, would have helped enormously if I'd found it earlier. It's step 6 of 6. By then I'd already met "reparametrization" about five times.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — Two sliders and a live curve; the explicit suggestion to "move γ from 2.0 downward and watch it flatten" is the best piece of guidance on the site. Undercut by a warning that the relationship it demonstrates is backwards from real physics, which I couldn't interpret. Its C(ρcrit) readout (0.3272) also contradicts the number stated on the Equation Anatomy page (0.88).
- **Equation Anatomy** — worked — very intuitive — The only place the math got explained in language I understood ("like a dimmer switch"). Best explanatory writing on the site and the hardest to find. Should not live behind a "Tools" menu.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — confusing — Five curves, one of them explicitly hand-faked, with the actual verdict ("boost never turns on") hidden in a legend parenthesis. I think I understood that the theory failed here, but I'm not confident, and I shouldn't have to be a detective about a chart that's doing the site's most important work.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — intuitive — Clearest reasoning on the site. Eight methods agreeing near 0.50, and the page explains why that's inevitable rather than impressive. I followed the entire argument with zero physics.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is this theory dead or alive? Zero confirmed predictions and six (or four?) refutations sounds dead, but the site is still enthusiastically building things. What's the actual status?
2. Is it 6 refutations or 4? Both numbers are stated as the honest one.
3. Which γ is the real one — 0.49, 1, or 2? Or is that the open question, in which case why does no page say so?
4. Why does C(ρcrit) equal 0.88 on one page and 0.33 on another?
5. If "coherence" here isn't quantum coherence, and superconductors score *low*, why is it called coherence?
6. What is MOND, in one sentence, for me? It's mentioned on nearly every page as the thing this keeps collapsing into, but I never got a plain-language explanation of what it is or why collapsing into it is bad.
7. If this were right, what would be different? No page told me what's at stake.
8. Why is a hand-drawn fake curve in the galaxy chart?
9. What does σ mean in "3.3σ"? I know it's from physics headlines; I don't know if 3.3 is a lot.
10. What am I supposed to *do* with this? Is it asking me to believe something, check something, or just watch?

### Verdict

The honesty is the product and I could feel that, even without the physics — a site that puts "0 confirmed predictions" on its front page and a "See What Failed" button next to "Start Exploring" earns a level of trust that almost nothing else on the internet does. But the beginner path is beginner for exactly two steps, the best plain-language explanations on the site (the dimmer switch, the Andromeda line, the consciousness-artifact argument) are scattered in places a casual reader reaches last or never, and the site contradicts itself on its own headline number where I can plainly see it. I'd come back if someone linked me a specific page, but I wouldn't recommend it to a friend yet, because I couldn't summarize what it says in one sentence — and I'm not sure the site could either.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The prose is unusually clear and the breadcrumbs are the most consistent thing on the site, but the status-badge system — the site's core epistemic device — has no single source of truth: I counted four separate legend blocks on four pages and at least twenty distinct status labels, of which the canonical legend defines eleven.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a re-platform: walk the nav, walk the paths, check whether the reference layer agrees with the content layer, check whether a term means the same thing on page 12 as on page 1.

**Structure and navigation — mostly good.** Eight top-nav items, a footer that's a superset of the nav, and breadcrumbs on every single page I opened. That last part is worth saying plainly because it's rare: `Home / Getting Started / Glossary`, `Home / Core Theory / The Coherence Function`, `Home / Cosmology / MOND Unification`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap`, `Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator`. Consistent depth, consistent phrasing, page name always matches the H1's tail. Whoever built this had a template and stuck to it.

The problem is that the middle crumb names a section that doesn't exist anywhere else. "Core Theory," "Cosmology," "Getting Started," "Predictions & Experiments" are not in the top nav, not in the footer, and not on the Learning Paths page. So the breadcrumb promises a taxonomy the site never exposes — I can see I'm in "Cosmology" but I can't get a list of what else is in Cosmology. Either surface those five sections as a nav tier, or drop the crumb to `Home / Page`. (I didn't verify whether the middle crumb is clickable; if it is, and it goes somewhere, the fix is just adding those hubs to the nav.)

**One page, four names.** This is the clearest IA defect I found. The nav item is **"Start Here."** It points to **`/why-synchronism`**. The breadcrumb on arrival reads **"Why Synchronism?"**. The H1 reads **"Start Here: Why Synchronism?"**. And `/start-here` also resolves to the same content — I fetched both URLs and got byte-comparable pages. Meanwhile Learning Paths lists beginner step 1 as "Why Synchronism?" while the nav calls the identical destination "Start Here."

Same pattern on Tools: nav says **"Tools"**, href is **`/interactive-tools`**, breadcrumb and H1 say **"Interactive Tools"**, and **`/tools`** also serves the page. And on the roadmap: nav says **"Test Roadmap"**, H1 says "Test Roadmap", href is **`/test-catalog`**.

None of these is fatal on its own. Together they mean a reader can't build a stable mental index of the site, external links will fragment across two URLs per page, and anyone writing about the site has to guess which name is the real one. Pick one label per destination, use it in nav + breadcrumb + H1 + path list, and make the alias a 301 rather than a second live copy.

**The badge system is the real finding.** The site's whole value proposition is calibrated honesty, and badges are how that calibration is transmitted. So I traced the vocabulary.

`/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` is presented everywhere as canonical. It defines eleven labels in three groups: five MRH-relationship (`Active-MRH`, `Parallel-Paths`, `Sidelined`, `Superseded`, `Audited-Negative`), four descriptive (`Untested`, `Speculative`, `Reparametrization`, `Failed`), and two deprecated (`Validated`, `Strongly Supported`). That's a good taxonomy — the MRH/descriptive split is a genuinely thoughtful piece of information design, and the note that the deprecated pair "conflicts with stewardship discipline" tells me *why*, which most docs never do.

Then I found the same legend re-authored three more times:
- `/glossary` — "Validation badges" section, adds `Kill Criterion Triggered`, `Post-diction`, `Withdrawn`
- `/test-catalog` — "Validation Badge Guide — MRH-relationship"
- `/tier-1-existing` — "Status badge definitions," adds `MOND-shared (retired)` and `Kill Criterion Triggered`

Four legends, already drifted. And then labels appear in the wild that are in no legend at all:
- `/key-claims`: "untestable as stated," "unfalsifiable as stated," "New Ontology," "Post-hoc Fit," "Citation discipline"
- `/interactive-tools`: "Model Explainer," "Artifact Lesson"
- `/tier-1-existing` status column: "Self-eliminating or tie; adjudicable now," "Never run as registered; substituted protocol executed," "Underpowered as registered," "Demoted to Tier 2"

That last set is prose in a column that looks like a controlled vocabulary. It can't be scanned, sorted, or counted, and it's where a reader most wants to scan — thirteen tests in one table.

Union across the site: **≥20 distinct status labels; the canonical legend covers 11.** For a site whose product is its epistemic bookkeeping, that's the highest-leverage fix available. One legend file, imported everywhere, rendered by a shared component; every badge string validated against it at build time; any status that needs prose gets a badge *plus* a note, not prose instead of a badge.

**The deprecated badge is still on the front page.** The landing page's Key Claims summary describes the consciousness claim as "**1 validated badge**." `/key-claims` badges that same claim `Speculative`. `/honest-assessment` says `Validated` is deprecated. So the retired vocabulary survives on the highest-traffic page in the site, and it disagrees with the page it's summarizing. Highest-traffic page should be first in the migration queue, not last.

**The glossary publishes a formula the site has retracted.** This one I'd escalate. The glossary entry reads:

> **γ (Gamma Parameter):** Transition-sharpness coefficient (dial): γ = 2/√N_corr.

No caveat, no badge. But `/gamma-calculator`'s subtitle is "**Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems**," and `/phase-boundary-visualizer` opens with "This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose," explaining that the formula "predicts the *opposite* of real condensed-matter behavior."

So the reference layer — the thing readers consult precisely when they're unsure — carries the un-caveated version of a relation two content pages label audited-negative. Glossary entries need to inherit the badge of whatever they define, or at minimum carry a "see [page] — this relation is audited-negative" line. Generalizing: the glossary is a *derived* artifact and should be generated from, or validated against, the same source as the page badges.

**Numbers without their parameters.** `/coherence-function` writes `C(ρcrit,γ=2)≈0.88` — parameter set inline, exactly right. Other pages quote `≈0.88` bare while a tool computes `0.3272` at a different default γ. Same defect class as the glossary one: a number is being treated as a fact when it's a function evaluation. House rule, cheap to enforce in review: **no derived number ships without its parameter set adjacent.** `/coherence-function` is already the model; propagate its formatting.

**Counts that don't reconcile.** `/test-catalog`'s subtitle is "Untested — **24 Proposed Experiments**," and Learning Paths links it as "Test Roadmap (24 experiments)." The same page then describes TEST-25 and TEST-26. `/tier-1-existing` lists TEST-25 as executed and failed. So "24" is stale, and "Proposed" is wrong for the tests that have run. Separately, `/test-catalog`'s status box says "**0 Pre-registered & Completed**" while `/tier-1-existing`'s subtitle says "**4 executed**." Those are almost certainly different metrics — pre-registration is a stricter bar than execution — but the site never says so, and a reader lands on a flat contradiction. Define both terms once, in the glossary, and use them consistently.

Pass 1 flagged 6-vs-4 refutations. From my seat the interesting part is that `/honest-assessment` and `/tier-1-existing` both render it correctly as "**6 refutations executed / 3–4 independent roots**" — the two-part form is right there, already written. It's the landing page that ships the bare "6." The content exists; it just isn't propagating to the summary layer. That's the same shape as the `Validated` badge and the bare `0.88`: **detail pages are more accurate than summary pages, consistently, in the same direction.**

**Acronyms.** The glossary is genuinely strong — 72 terms, alphabetized, and the definitions are written for a non-specialist. `MRH`, `A2ACW`, `SPARC`, `RAR`, `BTFR`, `ΛCDM`, `CHSH`, `DESI`, `ΔBIC`, and `σ` all have clear entries. So the asset exists; the linking discipline doesn't. The landing page uses `SPARC`, `ALFALFA–SDSS`, `BTFR`, `RAR`, `DESI`, `CHSH`, `ΛCDM` unexpanded. `/coherence-function` uses `MRH`, `BCS`, `BEC`, `RAR`, `BTFR`, `CHSH` unexpanded. `/tier-1-existing` *has* an acronym key and still leaves `ALFALFA`, `SDSS`, `Gaia`, `BIC`, `RSD`, `ΛCDM`, `BAO` outside it.

The site already demonstrates the fix twice: `/why-synchronism` writes "MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics, a 1983 rival gravity idea — explained below)," and the landing page links "MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)" straight to `/mrh`. Make that the rule — first use per page gets expansion + glossary anchor link — and it's mechanically checkable in CI against the glossary's own term list.

Structurally, the glossary being **step 6 of 6** on the beginner path is backwards. It's a reference work; readers need it from step 1. Move it out of the numbered sequence and into persistent navigation, or make it step 0.

**Path architecture.** Two orthogonal path systems — three "By Difficulty" (Beginner 6 / Intermediate 8 / Advanced 8) and four "By Topic" (Physics 7 / Chemistry 6 / Philosophy 7 / Methodology 6). Difficulty paths state prerequisites ("No physics background needed," "Undergrad science background," "Grad-level physics"); topic paths state none, which is a gap since the Physics track includes `/mond-unification`, an Advanced-path page.

The two systems overlap heavily and the site handles it well *on the page* — `/coherence-function` shows "Intermediate Path, Step 1 of 8 | Physics Track, Step 1 of 7," which is exactly the right dual-context breadcrumb. But `/learning-paths` never explains that the systems overlap, so a reader who finishes Intermediate and starts Physics re-reads pages 1, 3, 4, and 6 without warning. One sentence at the top of the Topic section fixes it.

**Corrections are appended, not integrated.** `/mond-unification`'s subtitle still reads "Reparametrization — Dimensional Analysis — 13% Error." Below it sit an "Update 2026-08-01" (the emergent-a₀ reading is "disfavored, not refuted," 2σ–6σ) and a further correction dated 2026-08-12. So the page's *identity* — H1 plus subtitle, the part every skimmer reads and every link preview shows — asserts the pre-correction framing, while the correction lives in a dated block below the fold. Documentation convention here is unambiguous: when a correction changes the claim, it goes in the lede and the old text is struck or removed; the dated block records *that* it changed, not *what is true*. This page is on the Advanced path at step 3 and the Physics track at step 6, so it's not a backwater.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project," which I read as a warning. I went straight past the landing page to `/coherence-function`, `/parameter-derivations`, and `/honest-assessment` — the three places where a framework like this either survives or doesn't.

It survives the first pass with unusual dignity. `/coherence-function` opens by calling C(ρ) "a phenomenological ansatz, not a derived first-principle result," and states outright that "the tanh shape is *motivated* by the Ising analogy, not derived from it." `/parameter-derivations` is not a derivations page at all — it's an autopsy of failed derivations, ending in "Zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." I have reviewed papers that would not concede a tenth of this.

`/core-idea` contains the single best paragraph on the site, and it is the paragraph I was preparing to write myself:

> "C(ρ) is real-analytic everywhere on its domain — no singularity, no free-energy functional, and no length scale anywhere in the equation — so it has no critical point and therefore no critical exponents (β, ν) to compare. Asking for them is a category error, not a near-miss with Landau theory."

Correct, and correctly stated. The same page gives the exact algebraic identity

    tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^2γ − 1] / [(1+x)^2γ + 1]

which I verified (set u = γ ln(1+x), then e^{2u} = (1+x)^{2γ}; substitute into tanh u = (e^{2u}−1)/(e^{2u}+1)). So C is an ordinary Hill function of index 2γ in the variable (1+x). There is no order parameter, no symmetry breaking, no feedback. The site says this. Good.

I'll add one thing the site does *not* say and should, because it's the sharpest version of its own point: **C is concave in ρ everywhere on ρ > 0.** Differentiating twice,

    C'(x) = γ sech²(u)/(1+x)
    C''(x) = −γ sech²(u)/(1+x)² · [2γ tanh(u) + 1] < 0  for all x > 0, γ > 0

There is no inflection point in density. The S-curve exists *only* against log ρ — it is a property of the axis, not of the system. Every "transition," "boundary," and "regime" on this site is a feature of a plotting choice. That's a stronger statement than "no critical point" and it follows in two lines.

Then I went to the galaxy sector, and this is where it comes apart — not because the physics fails (the site is candid that it fails) but because **three different force laws are being used on three different pages, and they have qualitatively different asymptotics.**

**(a) `/mond-unification`:** `g = g_bar/C(ρ)`, with C evaluated on *local density only, not external fields* — which is precisely what buys the structural claim "EFE = 0 exactly."

**(b) `/galaxy-rotation`:** the compander is fitted in the μ role, `μ = tanh(γ ln(1+y))`, with the stated return-to-Newtonian exponent 2γ; free-γ SPARC fit gives γ ≈ 0.489.

**(c) `/galaxy-plotter`:** `v(r) = √(v_b² + [V_flat·C(ρ(r))]²)`, labeled "Synchronism real equation."

These are not three presentations of one theory. Take the deep-field limit. For small x, C ≈ γx. Under (a) with an *explicit* argument — ρ or g_bar, i.e. C evaluated on something you already know without solving for g:

    g_obs = g_bar / C ≈ g_bar / (γ g_bar/a₀) = a₀/γ = constant
    ⇒ v² = g r ∝ r ⇒ v ∝ √r

**Rising rotation curves, not flat.** Form (a) with an explicit argument cannot produce a flat curve at any γ, and no fit to SPARC could ever have returned γ ≈ 0.489 from it. The γ ≈ 0.489 result is obtainable *only* if the argument is the total acceleration g_obs/a₀ — i.e. the implicit equation μ(g/a₀)·g = g_bar, which in the deep limit gives g = √(g_bar a₀/γ), correct MOND scaling with a₀_eff = a₀/γ.

But an implicit equation in the total acceleration **is a self-consistency equation** — exactly what `/core-idea` denies ("C(ρ) has no feedback loop — ρ goes in, C comes out") and exactly what `/mond-unification` denies in order to derive EFE = 0. A μ-function whose argument is the total field responds to the external field by construction. **The site's flagship galaxy result and its flagship structural prediction cannot both be true.** Neither page states which argument the fitted objective function actually used. This is one grep away from resolution and it decides whether refutation #1 was run on the model the site describes.

Form (c) is worse still and it's the *default interactive tool*. As ρ falls outward C → 0, so (c) gives v → v_b: a Keplerian *decline*. Form (a) gives v → ∞ at the same limit. The two curves the site draws in the same figure family diverge to opposite infinities.

I then checked what the plotter's "real equation" curve is actually made of: `C(ρ) = tanh(2·ln(ρ/ρcrit+1))` with `ρcrit = 0.029·V_flat²`. Both ingredients are separately retracted elsewhere on the same site. γ = 2 is the pin refuted at ΔBIC = +184 on the same SPARC data (`/tier-1-existing`). A = 0.029 is badged **Audited-Negative — Chain-of-Custody Failure** on `/parameter-derivations`, where the stated formula A = 4π/(βJ²GR₀²) is noted to give 4.6×10⁻⁵ — 600× off the number in use. The site's most-visited tool is running on two withdrawn inputs under the word "real."

Finally, `/gamma-calculator`. The tool honestly flags that γ = 2/√N_corr runs backwards (more correlation → flatter transition). I'd add the diagnosis of *why*, which the page doesn't give: N^{−1/2} is the scaling of the *fluctuation* of a mean of iid variables. Sharpness of a mean-field response scales with coordination number (βJz) and *increases* with correlation. The relation imports the width of a sampling distribution and uses it as the steepness of a response function. Those are different objects; the sign error is a symptom, not the disease. The page's own note that invoking CLT for N_corr *correlated* units violates the iid premise is the same observation from the other side.

Two arithmetic consequences the site misses. If N_corr is a particle count it's an integer, so γ = 2/√N_corr is **quantized**: γ ∈ {2, 1.414, 1.155, 1.000, 0.894, ...}. The SPARC preference γ = 0.489 needs N_corr = 16.7 — **not attainable by the relation at all**. And the nearest attainable value is N_corr = 16 → γ = 0.500 exactly, which is the exact MOND point of this family: at γ = 1/2 the Hill identity collapses to

    C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2)

identically, for all x — not asymptotically. So the framework's own parameter relation, evaluated at its nearest integer to the data's preference, lands *exactly* on MOND with a₀ rescaled by 2. `/tier-1-existing` says "no single γ selects Synchronism over MOND." It's stronger than that: there is a γ at which the two are the *same function*, and the data sit 2% from it.

**Then I went hunting for the thing that isn't MOND or ΛCDM.** By the site's own accounting there are four candidate artifacts: the local-density no-go, the A2ACW program null, the dim-4 LIV naturalness gap, and the CHSH substrate null. Three are correctly labelled as inherited or instrument-limited. That's an honest null.

So the entire live surface is `/dark-energy` and TEST-26. I spent most of the session there.

**The sign lock.** The claim is clean and I like it: w runs monotonically from w = −2γ in the far past to exactly −1 in the far future, so sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) and the class cannot cross w = −1. DESI DR2 (arXiv:2503.14738 — correctly cited) prefers exactly the forbidden quadrant, w₀ > −1 with wₐ < 0. Structurally this is the best-posed thing on the site.

Three problems, in increasing order of how much they matter.

*First, the σ is not a σ.* The page reports "offsets 3.4–5.4σ across the combinations" and then, to its credit, adds "sign-and-scale statement only — no covariance is claimed." But the DESI CPL posterior in (w₀, wₐ) is the textbook case where the covariance *is* the constraint: the two parameters are strongly anti-correlated (ρ ≈ −0.9), the degeneracy direction is well outside the marginal 1D errors, and a procedure that fixes w₀ at the model value and then measures wₐ is profiling along the single worst direction in that plane. A number printed with a "σ" glyph will be read as a significance no matter what the sentence next to it says. The fix is not hard and does not require a fit: DESI publishes the chains. **The covariance-correct statistic is the posterior mass in the allowed quadrant** — one number, no fitting, no covariance assumption, directly interpretable ("DESI DR2 puts p% of its (w₀,wₐ) posterior in the region this model class can occupy"). That single number should replace all four σ values.

*Second, TEST-26's kill is shared with ΛCDM, and the site only says half of this.* `/top-5-tests` correctly marks it "not discriminating in the confirming direction — outcome is kill-or-tie only." But the other direction is worse: any DR3 result that *robustly requires* a w = −1 crossing also kills ΛCDM, at higher significance. A test that fires on you and on the standard model simultaneously is a test of the standard model with you as a passenger. So TEST-26 cannot separate this framework from ΛCDM in *either* branch — which makes calling it "the framework's one live falsifiable position" an overclaim of specificity, even though every individual sentence about it is true.

*Third, and this is the one I'd raise in review: the tie is guaranteed by construction, and nobody has printed the amplitude.* At the framework's own preferred γ = 0.489, w runs from −2γ = **−0.978** to −1. That is the entire dynamic range of the dark-energy sector: |1 + w| ≤ 0.022 at all epochs, giving ≤ ~6% total drift in ρ_DE over Δln a ~ 1 and an effective constant-w of roughly −0.99. Current constant-w constraints are at the few-percent level; DR3 improves them by tens of percent, not by the factor needed to resolve 0.022. **So the "tie" branch of TEST-26 is not a measurement outcome — it is arithmetic.** The page already says "at γ = 1/2 the model is algebraically identical to ΛCDM" and "the galaxy free fit returns 0.489, within 2.2% of it," but it never carries that 2.2% through to the observable, where it becomes a sub-σ deviation in w. The site has the two halves on the same page and doesn't multiply them.

**The fork I'd actually put to the authors.** The page states the deep constraint: for any DE sector whose density is slaved to matter density, continuity collapses to w_DE(z) = dlnF/dlnx, and DESI's crossing needs an interior maximum in ρ_DE(x) that this family can't produce. Fine — but that's a *background* statement, and slaving is not a background-only relation if the theory is local. If ρ_DE = F(ρ_m) holds locally, then it holds in perturbations: δρ_DE = (dF/dρ_m) δρ_m, and since w_DE = dlnF/dlnx ≈ −1 over the whole observed range, dlnF/dlnρ_m ≈ −1, i.e. **δρ_DE/ρ_DE ≈ −δρ_m/ρ_m — the dark-energy component clusters with order-unity amplitude, anti-correlated with matter.** With ρ_DE ≈ 0.7ρ_crit against ρ_m ≈ 0.3ρ_crit, the perturbed source of the Poisson equation flips sign in overdensities. That is not a 2028 test; it is a linear-growth and lensing statement testable against DESI DR2 + DES Y3 + Planck *today*, and I would expect it to close at very high significance.

So the fork: either the slaving is local (⇒ the class dies now on linear growth, no DR3 needed, and TEST-26 is superseded) or it is background-only (⇒ there is no field theory, which the site already concedes via the Bianchi-identity violation — and then the sign lock is a property of an ansatz, not a prediction of a theory, and shouldn't be registered as a test either). Both horns close TEST-26 in 2026 rather than 2028. I can't run either calculation from a browser, and the sign of the effect deserves a careful check against the covariant completions the page mentions — but this is the question I'd send back with the review.

**A2ACW.** `/research-philosophy` is straight about the structure: 3,308 sessions → ~47 internal-consistency survivors (1.4%) → 6 external audits → 0 novel. The retrospective control fed six subsequently-demoted claims to models with different training cutoffs and caught 0 of 6. And the informativeness statement is exactly right: 0/47 "cannot distinguish 'no novelty exists' from 'the method is systematically blind to novelty when present.'"

Two things follow that the site doesn't draw.

*The registered control is no longer blocked.* The page defers the cross-vendor corpus control "pending access to a genuinely different-corpus model pair." In 2026 that blocker has dissolved — open-weight families (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek) are one API call away and cost approximately nothing at 47 claims. A training-cutoff variation is not a corpus control; same-family models share nearly the entire prior, which is the specific confound at issue for an AI-generated framework audited by AI. **This is the highest-value unrun experiment on the site**, and it isn't astrophysical: it is the only registered test that could separate "the framework has no novelty" from "the instrument cannot see novelty." Everything else on the roadmap is kill-or-tie.

*The landing page headlines a null instrument's output.* The scoreboard reads "'Validated' claims surviving audit: **0 of 6** — all demoted." `/for-researchers` reports that the same audit protocol has a measured false-positive rate of 1.0 and Youden's J = 0 with CI [−0.46, +0.46] — every genuine discovery in the control was flagged as reparametrization. If the instrument's specificity is zero, then "0 of 6" is what it would output for *any* corpus, including a corpus of Nobel results, and it is not evidence about this framework in either direction. The site says this explicitly on the detail page ("instrument-limited and carries no information about actual framework novelty") and then promotes the instrument's reading to the front page anyway. Note the direction: this is self-deprecation, not overclaiming — but an uninformative statistic quoted as a headline is the same methodological error either way, and it's the one I'd be least likely to forgive in a paper *because* it flatters the author's humility.

Separately: `/research-philosophy` doesn't carry the J = 0 statistic or its CI at all. The methodology page is missing the statistic that its own citable-artifacts page headlines. That's the reverse of the usual propagation direction on this site.

**The counting.** The landing page says "Refutations executed on external data: 6 — but only **2** are framework-specific independent roots." `/honest-assessment` says "6 executed refutations across **3–4** independent empirical roots" and enumerates Roots 1–4. `/test-catalog` says "6 Refutations Executed with **3–4** independent roots." That's three surfaces, two values, and the qualifier that would reconcile them ("framework-specific") appears on exactly one of the three with no definition anywhere. I can guess the intended reduction — Roots 1 and 2 are framework-specific, Roots 3 and 4 arguably aren't — but a reader can't reconstruct it, and this is the site's single most-quoted number.

**Wide binaries.** `/tier-1-existing` lists TEST-02 as "Pending adjudication"; `/top-5-tests` lists it "Closed as self-eliminating." Same test, two states. On the physics I have no complaint: the site says the predicted signal sits ~80× below Gaia DR3 systematics, and that is the right call. The live literature disagreement (Chae's ~10σ MOND-like detection vs. Banik et al.'s Newtonian-consistent result) is entirely about the treatment of undetected triples and sample cuts at the ~20% signal level — a signal 80× smaller than *that* is not reachable, and the site is one of very few places I've seen decline to claim a wide-binary result in either direction.

### Tools Tested

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is ρ_DE = F(ρ_m) a local relation or a background one? Everything about TEST-26 turns on it: local ⇒ order-unity anti-correlated DE clustering, closable on DR2-era data now; background-only ⇒ no field theory, and the sign lock isn't a prediction to register.
2. What fraction of the DESI DR2 (w₀,wₐ) posterior lies in the sign-locked quadrant? This is a one-number, covariance-correct replacement for "3.4–5.4σ" and it requires only the public chains.
3. Given that a robust DR3 crossing kills ΛCDM too, in what sense is TEST-26 a test *of this framework*?
4. Why is the cross-vendor A2ACW control still unexecuted when the stated blocker — access to a different-corpus model pair — dissolved years ago? Is there a constraint I'm not seeing, or is it just unqueued?
5. If the audit protocol has measured specificity 0, on what basis does "0 of 6 surviving audit" appear on the landing page as a finding?
6. Are the MRH-width γ and the C(ρ)-sharpness γ the same parameter, and if so what equation relates them?

### Verdict

This is the most epistemically disciplined physics site I have read, and it has one live scientific claim: the dark-energy sign lock. That claim is registered against 2028 data with a σ computed without the covariance that dominates the posterior, at a γ where the sector deviates from ΛCDM by 2.2% in w — meaning the "tie" branch cannot fail and the "kill" branch fires on ΛCDM at the same time. All three of those are fixable from public DESI DR2 products this month, and one of them (the locality fork on ρ_DE = F(ρ_m)) would close the test outright rather than waiting for DR3.

The methodology story is the same shape one level up. The A2ACW null is the site's most-promoted result and its own control shows the instrument has zero specificity — which the detail pages say and the landing page ignores. The single registered experiment that could break that circle, the cross-vendor corpus control, is described as blocked by a constraint that no longer exists. If I were advising this program on where to spend the next month, I would not spend it on astrophysics: every remaining astrophysical test on the roadmap is kill-or-tie by construction, and the one experiment that could return information about the actual question — is this method blind, or is there nothing there — costs a few API calls and has been sitting registered and unrun.
