## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The honesty is genuinely disarming and the marching-band analogy clicked instantly, but the site keeps handing me the plain-English version and then immediately showing me an equation with symbols I can't read, and by page three I'm reading about something that already failed without ever being told why I should care.

### Journey

I got here from a Reddit comment that said something like "someone published all the ways their own theory failed." That's a good hook, so I clicked.

The landing page opens with **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** — great. That's the kind of question PBS Space Time opens with. Then immediately: **"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."** I actually stopped and re-read that. I've never seen a science site lead with its own failure. Respect.

Then the equation shows up: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is. I know ρ is a Greek letter. So my eyes slid right off it. The good news is the site immediately says what it *means* — "how collectively does this system behave?", a number between 0 and 1 — and gives me **"A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence."** That analogy is the single best thing on the site. I understood coherence in about four seconds.

The scoreboard is where I got wobbly. **"Prospective predictions confirmed: 0"** and **"Refutations executed: 6"**. Okay... so the theory is wrong? Then what am I reading? I get that failing honestly is admirable, but nobody told me what's still *alive* here. Am I reading a science site or a post-mortem? I kept scrolling looking for a "but here's why it's still interesting" and didn't find one that landed for me.

I took the **Beginner Path** because it promised 6 steps, ~30 minutes, no equations required. Step 1 is "Why Synchronism?" and it does keep its promise — it puts the plain-English version first and hides the math behind **"Show the equation (optional — the plain-English version above is the whole idea)"**. That's exactly right. More of that, please.

Then Step 2, **First Encounter**, billed as a 10-minute guided intro. This is where I started sliding. Coherence, fine. Presence (ρ), okay, it's basically density, fine. But then: **Markov Relevancy Horizon**. It explains it as "the bubble of neighbors close enough to influence the system," which is actually a nice idea — *"A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy — it only responds to what's immediately around it."* That I get! But the name is terrifying and then there's a "formal version" in parentheses that made things worse, not better. Also "degrees of freedom" shows up with no explanation. I don't know what that means. And suddenly there are three Greek letters in play.

I went to **Tools** next because charts are easier than paragraphs. It told me to start with the **Coherence Explorer**, which I appreciated — being told which door to open first is underrated.

**Dark Matter** was the page I most wanted, because dark matter is the thing I actually know from YouTube. It was a wall. MOND, BAR, CMB, SPARC, CDM, BAO, Bullet Cluster, Tully-Fisher, BTFR — I counted nine acronyms I couldn't define, none of them spelled out on the page. There's a glossary link, but I'm not opening a glossary nine times in one page; I just left. The idea underneath sounded genuinely cool — *stop asking what dark matter is, ask why some stuff only interacts through gravity* — and then it told me it predicted a 3× gravity boost and galaxies need 14×. So it's off by more than four times over. Even I can do that division. Fair enough, they said so themselves.

**Key Claims** had colored badges next to each claim — "Speculative," "Failed." I liked seeing them but the page didn't tell me what they mean; it pointed me somewhere else to find out. I want the definition where the badge is, or at least on hover.

Last stop was the **Consciousness Threshold Demo**, because of course it was. That's the most clickable thing on the whole site. And this is the one that worries me: it shows eight different methods all landing on the same number (~0.50) in a bar chart, which *looks* like eight independent confirmations. My gut reaction was "whoa." Then the fine print says the agreement is **"geometrically forced — any approach keyed to the midpoint of [0,1) will land near 0.50 by construction."** So the chart is showing me nothing. I only caught that because I was reading carefully. Someone skimming screencaps that chart and posts it to Reddit as proof, and the site becomes the thing it's trying not to be.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — Dragging "transition sharpness" and watching the S-curve go from a gentle ramp to a hard cliff is genuinely satisfying, and it taught me what the equation does better than the equation did. But the axis says "log₁₀(ρ)" and the second slider is "reference density (ρcrit)", and I never fully understood what I was changing or what a "density of 1.00" is a density *of*. I was playing, not learning. Labeling the sliders in plain words ("how sudden is the switch" / "where does the switch happen") with the symbols in parentheses would fix most of it.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — confusing — This one has real explanatory text and it's good: *"The dashed line is what rotation curves should look like with only visible matter. The blue dots are what we actually observe. The gap is the 'dark matter problem.'"* That sentence taught me the entire dark matter problem in one go — it should be on the dark matter page. But then there are **five** curves on one chart in five colors and styles, one of which is explicitly a *discarded wrong version they used to display* ("it fit because it was drawn to fit"). I admire the honesty, but as a beginner I couldn't hold five lines in my head and I couldn't tell which one I was supposed to be looking at. Default to three (observed, visible-matter-only, Synchronism) with a toggle for the rest.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked — misleading at a glance — See friction log. The interaction (hover a bar to see the methodology) is nice; the framing is the problem.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the theory failed six tests and confirmed zero predictions, what is still standing? Is there anything here that's *alive*, or is this entirely an archive of a wrong idea?
2. Who made this, and why? I never found a person, a lab, or a motive. That would help me decide how to feel about it.
3. What is "presence" actually — is it just density with a fancier name, or is it something different? The site uses both words and I couldn't tell if they're the same thing.
4. If Synchronism's galaxy fit is "as good as MOND but never better," why not just use MOND? What did I gain?
5. Why does the equation have that particular shape? The site says none of the numbers come from first principles and everything is fitted — so is the equation *guessed*?
6. Is the consciousness part serious, or is it a thought experiment? The badge says speculative but it's featured as prominently as the physics.

### Verdict
The intellectual honesty here is real and rare — leading with "we were wrong" bought this site more goodwill from me than any amount of hype would have, and the marching-band-versus-plaza analogy is the best explanation of an abstract physics idea I've read this year. But the site never answers the question a casual visitor arrives with: *why should I spend thirty minutes on an idea you've already told me is refuted?* It keeps promising no-math and then showing me tanh, and the pages I most wanted (dark matter, consciousness) are the two that most punished me for showing up unprepared. I'd come back if someone linked me a specific page, but I wouldn't explore on my own.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 14
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Structurally this is one of the better-organized research sites I've audited — real breadcrumbs, real prev/next chains, a real glossary, and a canonical definitions page — but the badge vocabulary has outgrown its own documentation, and at least one headline number is stale on the page most likely to be cited.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a rewrite: landing page, then the nav bar left to right, then the two beginner-path entry points, then a spot-check of whether the reference material actually resolves the terms the prose uses.

**Navigation.** Eight top-level items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment). Every label I checked resolves to a page whose H1 matches or contains it — "Learning Paths" → "Learning Paths", "Tier 1: Existing Data" → "Tier 1: Existing Data", "Test Roadmap" → "Test Roadmap", "Start Here" → "Start Here: Why Synchronism?". That sounds trivial. It is not; most sites fail it. Only "Tools" → "Interactive Tools" drifts, and that's defensible.

**Breadcrumbs work, but describe a site that doesn't exist.** Every content page carries a three-segment trail: `Home / Getting Started / First Encounter`, `Home / Cosmology / Dark Matter Reframed`, `Home / Core Theory / MRH`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / Tier 1`, `Home / Methodology / For Researchers`. I checked on `/why-synchronism`: **"Home" is a link; the middle segment is not.** So there are five section names — Getting Started, Cosmology, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments, Methodology — that appear on nearly every page, are not clickable, have no index page, and do not correspond to anything in the top navigation. Meanwhile the *actual* organizing structure is a third thing: seven learning paths (3 by difficulty, 4 by topic). That's three taxonomies over one content set. The breadcrumb one is the only one a reader sees constantly and the only one that goes nowhere.

**The learning-path spine is genuinely good.** `/why-synchronism` says "Beginner Path — Step 1 of 6" and offers "Next: First Encounter →". `/first-encounter` says "Step 2 of 6", offers prev and next. `/dark-matter` carries *two* prev/next pairs because it sits on both the Intermediate and Physics tracks, and labels them. `/learning-paths` even warns that the difficulty and topic groupings "are not equivalent." Someone thought about this. It's the strongest information-architecture feature on the site and it's underadvertised — it's buried behind a nav item called "Learning Paths" rather than being the default reading affordance.

**Then I went looking for the definitions of record, and that's where it comes apart.**

The glossary defines **11 validation badges** in a tidy block: five MRH-relationship tags, four descriptive tags, and two deprecated ones. Clean. Documentable. I could put that on a reference card.

The canonical reference — `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, the page every other page points to — defines those 11 **plus** a family called "Operational states" (Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (RETIRED), Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat) **plus** a family called "Status lifecycle verbs" (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, Ceiling Exceeded). That's roughly thirty labels across five families. Some of them are not badges in any normal sense — "89% Boundary-Consistent" and "Template Bias Caveat" are findings wearing a badge's clothes, and "Withdrawn" appears in two families with different casing.

A reader cannot hold thirty status labels. More to the point, the glossary and the canonical page now disagree about *what the vocabulary is*, and the glossary is the one people will reach for.

**And in the wild, badges are compound strings.** What's actually rendered on pages:

- `/dark-matter`: "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision"
- `/galaxy-rotation`: "Untested — TEST-03 Never Run As Registered — see correction below"
- `/gamma-calculator`: "Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems"
- `/mrh`: "Speculative — Theoretical Framework"

Only the first token is in the taxonomy. The rest is editorial annotation fused into the badge. A reader who has just learned that "Failed" means *"prediction was tested and contradicted by data"* now has to decide whether "— Mechanism Under Revision" narrows it, softens it, or is a separate status. Badges should be atomic; annotations belong next to them, not inside them.

**Nobody defines a badge where a badge is used.** `/key-claims` displays three badges and then says, explicitly, that definitions live on the Honest Assessment page. So the page that exists to state the claims requires a round trip to be read at all.

**Number check.** I chased the galaxy count across four pages because that's the number a journalist copies:

- Landing: "14,610 Galaxies analyzed"
- `/why-synchronism`: "Tested against 14,610 galaxies (175 SPARC + 14,435 ALFALFA–SDSS (quality cut))"
- `/key-claims`: 175 + 14,435 → "14,610 galaxies"
- `/galaxy-rotation`: gives both — "**14,585 galaxies** from the ALFALFA HI survey" as the raw sample, and "**14,610** galaxies run (175 SPARC + 14,435 ALFALFA–SDSS (quality cut))" as the analyzed count

Three of four agree, and `/galaxy-rotation` correctly distinguishes the raw sample from the quality-cut sample. Good.

Then `/dark-matter` says: *"The '14,760 galaxies' figure pools two different measurements: 175 resolved SPARC rotation curves … and 14,585 ALFALFA–SDSS Tully–Fisher objects."* That's 175 + 14,585 — the *pre-cut* number — and 14,760 appears nowhere else on the site. So the dark matter page is running a caveat against a headline figure the site no longer publishes. A reader who takes the warning seriously will go hunting for 14,760, find 14,610, and conclude one of the two pages is lying. The caveat itself is exactly right in spirit; its arithmetic is one revision behind.

**Glossary as a reference tool.** 72 terms, alphabetical, with a stated sort convention ("Greek-symbol terms sort after the Latin alphabet, and validation-badge terms cluster as a group") — which is thoughtful and which I'd normally praise, except it means the badges are *not* where alphabetical order would put them. There is no filter box. Coverage is uneven in a way that maps badly onto reading order: **ΛCDM is defined but CDM is not**, and `/dark-matter` uses bare "CDM" repeatedly ("Particle dark matter (CDM) explains a vast range of cosmological observations"). **CMB is not in the glossary** and is not expanded on the page that uses it. **"Degrees of freedom" is not defined anywhere** — and it is the load-bearing term in the formal MRH definition. Also missing: OOM, AQUAL, PRL, Null-Class.

**MRH has two definitions of record, and the canonical page has the worse one.** The glossary and `/first-encounter` both lead with *"the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter"* — plain, memorable, correct enough. `/first-encounter` then supplies the formal version *"the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence."* Good escalation. But `/mrh` — the dedicated page, the one linked from the landing page — opens with only the formal version. The page a confused reader clicks *through* to is the least accessible statement of the concept. To its credit, `/mrh` has an "Open Gap" section admitting the name promises a mathematical structure not yet delivered. That's the right instinct applied in the wrong order: caveat present, plain-language anchor absent.

**Acronyms on the beginner on-ramp.** `/why-synchronism` is Step 1 of the Beginner Path. It expands MOND and BEC. It does not expand SPARC, ALFALFA, SDSS, BTFR, or CHSH. Five unexpanded acronyms on the first page of the path explicitly marketed as requiring no background.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — mostly clear — The single input is labeled **"Ncorr"** with the parenthetical "number of correlated particles," which is the right call; the glossary carries `N_corr` with an underscore, so the two spellings differ but the meaning survives. Seven presets (Ideal gas → BCS superconductor) are excellent documentation practice — they turn an abstract slider into a set of worked examples — and the **"Try it: click 'Ideal gas' (γ=2, the steep violet S) then 'BCS superconductor'"** instruction is the best piece of task-oriented writing on the site. The structural oddity: the page opens with **"Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems"** and four numbered warnings, then invites you to compute with the formula anyway. As pedagogy I think that's defensible — you're being shown the failure — but the output label **"Boundary (γ ≈ 1) — a label per the inverted formula"** is doing far too much work in nine words. A reader who hasn't read all four warnings will read "Boundary" as a result rather than as a scare quote.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — best-documented tool on the site — It states its learning objective outright: *"drag the slider to see which physical systems the γ formula groups together — and why that grouping is now known to be misleading."* That is exactly what a tool caption should do — tell me what I'm about to learn before I touch anything — and no other tool I saw does it. The payoff line is genuinely clarifying: *"Ideal gases and galaxies both map to γ = 2.0 (Ncorr = 1) … γ is classifying the counting method, not the system."* Two defects. The tool's most persuasive case is the one it cannot show. A log axis would put it back on screen.

2. Are "Operational states" and "Status lifecycle verbs" meant to be badges at all, or did a status-tracking vocabulary leak into a reader-facing legend?
3. Do the five breadcrumb section names (Getting Started, Cosmology, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments, Methodology) correspond to anything in the source, or are they per-page strings? If the latter, they will drift.

5. Where should a reader who lands mid-site (from a search engine, on `/dark-matter`) be sent? The prev/next chain assumes they entered at Step 1.
6. Is there a style rule for acronym expansion, and if so why does it hold on `/dark-matter` (MOND, CDM, CFD expanded inline) but not on `/why-synchronism` (SPARC, ALFALFA, SDSS, BTFR, CHSH bare)?

### Verdict
The bones are unusually good: labels match headings, breadcrumbs and prev/next chains exist and mostly behave, there's a canonical definitions page, a 72-term glossary, and a genuinely well-constructed multi-track learning spine that even warns you its two groupings aren't equivalent. Fix the badge taxonomy and reconcile the galaxy count and this jumps a tier; both are afternoon-sized jobs. The one structural change I'd argue for beyond that is making the learning paths the site's visible spine rather than a nav item — it's the best thing here and most visitors will never find it.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The negative results here are real, correct, and better than the site knows — I re-derived three of them by hand and they hold — but the headline "equivalent to MOND" verdict is argued asymptotically when it is available *exactly*, and the exact version is both cleaner and more damning.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project," which is usually a warning. I went straight past the onboarding to `/parameter-derivations`, `/for-researchers`, `/galaxy-rotation` and `/tier-1-existing`, and I checked the algebra myself rather than trusting the prose.

**First impression: the badges are honest.** I came looking for the usual tell — a "Validated" chip on something that's a fit. There are none; the scoreboard says *0 of 6 "Validated" claims survived audit*, and I couldn't find a survivor. The sharpest test of badge honesty is `/key-claims` on a₀: the page reports a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² against g† = 1.20 ± 0.02 (rand) ± 0.24 (syst) ×10⁻¹⁰, calls it **0.66σ — consistent**, and then badges the whole claim **Reparametrization** with prior art (Milgrom 1983, McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017). That is a project labelling its single best-looking numerical agreement as *not a result*, against its own interest. I checked the arithmetic: H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc → 2.184×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹, cH₀/2π = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ ✓, and inverting a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ gives H₀ = 2πa₀/c = 77.6 km/s/Mpc, exactly the figure `/honest-assessment` quotes. Freeman: a₀/(2πG) with a₀ = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ gives 0.2485 kg/m² = **119 M☉/pc²**, −4% from 124 ✓. Every number I spot-checked reproduced. That is not common.

**`/parameter-derivations` is a derivations page that opens by saying it isn't one.** γ = 2/√N_corr is "motivated ansatz," ρ_crit is "calibrated," the tanh is a "phenomenological choice… not uniquely derived," B_max = 1/Ω_m is "asserted, not derived," and the page closes with *"zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations."* Fine — I can work with that. The interesting part is where the audit is *sharper* than the framework and where it's *duller*.

**Where I think the site under-claims its own result — the exact MOND identity.**

The compander has a closed form. Writing u = ρ/ρ_crit:

C(u) = tanh(γ ln(1+u)) = [(1+u)^{2γ} − 1] / [(1+u)^{2γ} + 1]

because e^{γ ln(1+u)} = (1+u)^γ. No approximation. At **γ = 1/2** this collapses to

C(u) = u / (u + 2) = μ_simple(u/2),  where μ_simple(x) = x/(1+x)

which is **exactly** MOND's "simple" interpolating function — for all u, not asymptotically. `/galaxy-rotation` doesn't say this. What it says is that "tanh(γ·ln(1+y)) approaches 1 like (1+y)^{−2γ}, so the return exponent is q = 2γ," and that the fit pins q ≈ 0.98 "the value hard-coded in MOND's 'simple' μ-function." That asymptotic statement is correct (1 − tanh z ≈ 2e^{−2z}, and e^{−2γln(1+y)} = (1+y)^{−2γ} ✓), but it is the weak form of a fact that holds identically. The free-γ SPARC fit landing at **0.489 is 2% from the unique point where the model *is* the simple μ-function**, and the site is reporting that as a matched tail exponent.

**And the same γ = 1/2 is doing the work in the dark-energy sector, for the same algebraic reason.** `/honest-assessment` states that the substituted model ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C "is exactly ΛCDM when γ = 1/2" and leaves it there. One line explains it: at γ = 1/2, (1−C)/C = [1 − u/(u+2)]·[(u+2)/u] = **2/u**, so ρ_DE = ρ_m · 2ρ_crit/ρ_m = 2ρ_crit — a constant. A cosmological constant falls out because the Möbius form makes ρ_m cancel identically.

So the two sectors are one fact. **At γ = 1/2 the compander is a Möbius transform of density; that single degeneracy makes it exactly MOND-simple in the galaxy sector and exactly Λ in the cosmological sector; and given a free γ, SPARC selects that point to 2%.** That is the strongest available statement of the reparametrization verdict and it is more elegant than the six-way ledger the site currently uses to reach the same place. I'd put it on `/galaxy-rotation` and `/key-claims` in two lines.

**Where the audit is duller than it could be — γ = 2/√N_corr.** The site badges this *audited-negative, sign inverted*: BCS superconductors get the flattest curves despite being nature's sharpest transitions. Correct, but "sign inverted" is the wrong diagnosis. The 1/√N is CLT scaling — that's the *width of the sampling distribution of a mean*, i.e. a fluctuation amplitude. Transition sharpness is a *response* quantity and its finite-size scaling is a different law. The page half-sees this ("N_corr is by construction the count of correlated degrees of freedom — exactly the regime where the iid hypothesis fails") and then still reports the defect as a sign. What's notable is that the correct law lands nearby: mean-field finite-size rounding goes as N^{−1/2}, so sharpness ∝ √N_corr, and **γ = 2√N_corr is defensible from finite-size scaling** — the "sign flip" happens to hit the right exponent for the wrong reason. Worth saying, because it's the difference between "we wrote a minus sign" and "we used a fluctuation law where a response law belongs."

**A structural defect I didn't see flagged: γ is not a sharpness parameter.** `/equation-walkthrough` correctly notes ρ_crit is not the midpoint ("at γ=2, C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88" — I get 15/17 = 0.882 ✓). But the consequence isn't drawn. Solving C = 1/2 gives the knee in closed form:

ρ_½ = ρ_crit · (3^{1/(2γ)} − 1)

γ = 0.49 → 2.07 ρ_crit (the Coherence Explorer reports 2.07 ✓); γ = 2 → 0.316 ρ_crit; γ = 10 → 0.056 ρ_crit; γ = 0.1 → 242 ρ_crit. So γ moves the knee by **three and a half orders of magnitude** across the tool's own slider range. γ and ρ_crit are not orthogonal — they both set location — which is the *structural* reason behind the Fisher correlation ρ(ln γ, ln A) = **+1.000000** reported on `/for-researchers`. That page derives the degeneracy from the small-x linearization; it's also visible in the exact form with no limit taken. The Coherence Explorer would teach this in one caption: print ρ_½ next to the slider and watch it run away.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary

- **Understanding achieved**: solid

### Journey

I came in via a preprint-adjacent mailing list where someone described this as "the AI framework that published its own autopsy." My question was narrow: **is there a single number in here that MOND + EFE + ΛCDM does not already predict?** Everything else is literature review.

The landing page answers before I ask: *"0 prospective predictions confirmed, 6 refutations executed, 0 of 6 'Validated' claims surviving audit."* That is an unusual thing to lead with and it saved me an hour. So I went hunting instead for the *structure* of the failure, which is where the transferable content lives.

**`/honest-assessment`** is the real paper. The six refutations sort into two framework-specific mechanism failures (TEST-09 BTFR 3.35 ± 0.07 predicted vs 3.75 ± 0.10 observed, 3.3σ; TEST-10 boost ceiling), one registration failure (TEST-08/03s, r² = 0.0001 against a registered r² = 0.14), one MOND-inherited failure (TEST-25 Cassini, +17.95σ), one theorem (CHSH, S ≤ 2), and one shape mismatch (ΔBIC = +184 at γ = 2). Reading the classification column, five of those six are not about *this* framework: TEST-25 is MOND's problem, CHSH is Bell's theorem, and the ΔBIC = +184 row resolves by the free fit converging on γ ≈ 0.49 — MOND again. The framework has essentially two of its own failures, and both trace to the same B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17 ceiling. The site says as much one layer down. It should say it on the scoreboard.

**`/galaxy-rotation`** does the equivalence properly enough: return exponent q = 2γ, fit q ≈ 0.98, independent free-Hill fit n = 0.975, ΔBIC +7.1 against ln N = 7.94, so Δχ² ≈ −0.84. A one-parameter extension that buys you Δχ² = −0.84 over 2807 points is not an extension. Fine. What stopped me is the residual: *"a coherent S-shaped ≈0.05–0.10 dex signature at the transition, significant at ~8σ per bin,"* while *"the fit improvement is zero."* Those two sentences sit next to each other and are never reconciled. An 8σ-per-bin **coherent** residual with zero global fit improvement is not a null — it is a statement that both curves are wrong in the same place in the same direction. That residual is the most interesting object on the site and it is being used as evidence of equivalence when it is evidence that the RAR transition region has structure neither function captures. That is publishable *against MOND*, not for this framework, and nobody here seems to want it.

**`/dark-energy`** is where the ledger breaks. The page states a hard structural constraint — w runs monotonically from −2γ to exactly −1, therefore **w₀ and w_a must carry the same sign** — and then states that DESI DR2 *"prefers precisely the forbidden combination — w₀ > −1 with w_a < 0."* That is a genuine, framework-specific, forbidden-region prediction, and the data lands in the forbidden region. Then, two paragraphs later, the direct fit (2026-08-12): best γ = 0.487, Δχ² = −0.3 vs ΛCDM, projected (w₀, w_a) = (−0.993, +0.023), and the verdict *"it is ΛCDM."* Both readings are on one page and the ledger row says "not counted." You cannot hold both. Either the same-sign constraint is a real prediction, in which case the framework is excluded at whatever significance DESI DR2 actually prefers evolving dark energy (~3σ DR2 alone, higher with CMB+SNe), or the family collapses to ΛCDM at γ = 1/2 and the same-sign constraint is unfalsifiable because the model sits at the boundary. My read is the second — at γ = 0.487 you get w_a = +0.023, which is inside the forbidden-for-DESI region but statistically indistinguishable from zero — and if so, the honest line is *"this sector inherits ΛCDM's tension with DESI DR2 exactly, no more and no less."* That sentence is shorter than what's currently there and it closes the row.

**`/for-researchers`** is, as far as I can tell, the actual output of this program, and it is three clicks deep. The local-density no-go is correct and its scope statement is unusually disciplined: it applies to *algebraic* coupling C(ρ)·g, explicitly **not** to gradient schemes like symmetron or to non-local variables like enclosed mass. The density-keyed unidentifiability result with the Refracted Gravity contrast case — same equation, knee inside the sampled range, therefore identifiable — is a clean framework-independent statement about when ρ-keyed modified gravity has one measurable number instead of two. Fisher ρ(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000 is the whole result in one line. Both of these are real. Neither needs Synchronism to be true.

The third "citable" artifact is where I stopped being a reader and started being a referee.

**The A2ACW J = 0 result does not mean what the site says it means.** The stated numbers: 3,308 sessions → ~47 internally-consistent candidates (1.4%) → 6 externally audited → 0 survived, reported as *"J = 0, sensitivity/specificity both 100% under opposite conventions."* Sensitivity is TP/(TP+FN) and specificity is TN/(TN+FP). If all six audited claims were genuinely non-novel, the audit set contains **zero members of the positive class**. Sensitivity is then 0/0 — undefined, not 1.0 — and Youden's J = sens + spec − 1 is undefined with it. The site is reporting a point estimate for a quantity that has no estimator in this sample. The correct statement is not "the detector performs at chance"; it is **"the detector's discrimination is unmeasured, because the evaluation set contains no positive controls."** Those are very different claims: the first says A2ACW was tested and failed, the second says it was never tested. The page's own caveat gestures at this (*"cannot discriminate between 'no novelty detected' and 'detector cannot discriminate'"*) — and then the headline number contradicts the caveat. This matters disproportionately because `/for-researchers` lists J = 0 as one of four citable results, and because `/open-questions` already registers the fix: the vocabulary-asymmetry test (feed pre-Planck-era results in post-2015 vocabulary) **is** the positive-control experiment. Run that and you have a measured J. Until then, drop the number and keep the caveat.

**`/mrh`** gave me the last piece. The definition is *"the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system,"* with everything beyond the horizon *"effectively independent."* Now look at what the Galaxy Curve Plotter actually computes: **ρ_crit = 0.029 · V_flat²**. V_flat is the asymptotic flat rotation speed — a global attribute of the whole galaxy, defined in the r → ∞ limit, measured outside any local neighborhood. The framework sets a *local* coherence threshold using a *non-local* parameter. That is an MRH violation by the framework's own definition, and it is independent of every empirical failure on the ledger: even if SPARC had fit perfectly, C(ρ(r)) would not be a function of local state. It is also, I think, the mechanical origin of the unidentifiability result — if ρ_crit is per-galaxy and proportional to V², then in the small-x regime the model has exactly one number per galaxy, which is what Fisher ρ = +1.000000 is telling you. The site treats the sign error (V^{+2} asserted, V^{−2} required, 240×–300,000× magnitude) as the finding. The sign error is downstream. The category error is that a local field was keyed to a global label at all. I did not see this stated anywhere.

One more thing nobody on the site asks: **which physical systems actually cross the knee?** Take the plotter's own normalization — for V_flat = 200 km/s, ρ_crit ≈ 1.2 × 10³ M☉/pc³. Disk midplane densities are ~0.1, which is why the site correctly reports SPARC sampling x at a median ~7 × 10⁻⁵ and concludes *"no galaxy crosses the coherence knee."* True. But 10³ M☉/pc³ is not an empty part of the universe — globular-cluster cores, nuclear star clusters and dense molecular cores live at 10²–10⁵. And globular clusters are precisely where a ρ-keyed theory and a g-keyed theory come apart structurally, because MOND has an external field effect and this framework has none. The site declares "0 currently discriminating tests" without having looked in the one density decade where its own knee sits. I am not claiming that test would pass — I would bet against it — but "we looked and it doesn't discriminate" and "we never looked" are different entries in a ledger that otherwise polices that distinction well.

### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics: the compander is a one-parameter family of MOND interpolating functions that lands on μ_simple when fitted, becomes ΛCDM in the cosmology sector at the same parameter value, and violates its own locality axiom in the sector where it makes predictions. The site says most of this about itself, which is remarkable, and the two genuinely transferable results — the local-density no-go with its careful scope statement, and the density-keyed unidentifiability theorem with the Refracted Gravity control — are real, framework-independent, and currently buried three clicks deep under a nav item. This project's characteristic failure mode is no longer overclaiming — it is **overclaiming its own refutations**, with the same lack of error bars, and the cure is identical.
