## What I found

**The flagship artifact has a published counterexample.** Burrage, Copeland & Millington, PRD 95,
064050 (2017) reproduce the radial acceleration relation for 153 SPARC galaxies, no cold dark matter,
with a scalar keyed on **local volumetric ρ(r)** and universal Lagrangian parameters. The site's
citable form says the no-go "holds for **any** local-ρ MOND mimic." A no-go dies to one existence
proof; this one has been in PRD for nine years, on our own dataset.

**The mechanism is the useful part, and the site nearly had it.** Their force is the *gradient* of a
function of ρ; C(ρ) is a *multiplier* on g. Differentiating a density profile is what lets a
density-keyed field track an acceleration-keyed relation. The site's own words — "C(ρ) conflates
switch and force into one local function" — name the defect correctly and then generalise it to a
class that doesn't share it. The corrected claim is **shorter, sharper, and survives**: algebraic
local-density coupling is dead; differential coupling is not.

**Two false statements were live on the flagship.** The unqualified "None exists" (its own caveat,
four sentences later, admitted the corpus was unwalked), and a **fabricated consensus** — "the
standard reason screened scalars don't reproduce MOND" — that no source I checked makes, and that
the leading paper on the question contradicts.

**The detector failed its first out-of-sample test, mechanistically.** My blind query contained all
the right nouns and still missed, because it was phrased negatively: *"...cannot reproduce..."*. A
prior-art search phrased as "show me that X fails" retrieves failures, not counterexamples. The
detector translates the **claim** and searches for it — confirmation-seeking by construction. That
plausibly also explains its 0/6 specificity. Successor design: search for the **object**, not the
claim. "symmetron SPARC rotation curves" finds it; "density cannot reproduce MOND" never will.

## Why the careful 07-23 audit missed it

Not carelessness — it read 13 papers and pre-registered. **Its corpus was scoped by the claim it was
auditing.** It searched the MOND variable-comparison literature, where a *precedent* would live. The
counterexample lives in the modified-gravity/screening corpus and doesn't cite that line at all. A
corpus-scoped prior-art audit finds prior art only where it already expects it, and its null is
bounded by that scope, not by the literature. The audit *named* the gap; the site then published
"**None exists**" in bold with the caveat trailing where nothing downstream reads it.

## Two methodology rules proposed

1. **Scope every prior-art null to its corpus in the same sentence** — "none in the MOND
   variable-comparison corpus," never an unqualified "none exists."
2. **Every attribution to an external literature must name a primary source that states it.** The
   existing guard (every p-value walks to a primary file) does not catch **fabricated consensus**,
   because a consensus claim has no walkable target. This instance was actively harmful: by appearing
   to concede the prior art, it made the 07-23 gate look half-checked when it was unchecked. *A
   fabricated concession is worse than a fabricated claim — it manufactures the appearance of due
   diligence.*

## So what?

The program lost its flagship's generality claim and kept its diagnosis, which is a net gain in
citability — the scoped version is defensible and the unscoped one would have died in review, which
is exactly what the visitor persona predicted four days ago from outside.

More importantly, two failures this session share one shape: the 07-23 audit scoped its corpus by the
claim it wanted to protect, and the detector phrases its searches as the claim it expects to confirm.
**Both search where the answer they want lives.** That is a single correctable defect, not two, and it
sits underneath a program whose entire method is self-audit. The proposed fix for both is the same:
search for the *object*, not the *claim*.

And the pre-registration earned its keep in a way I didn't predict — not by preventing a biased
verdict, but by making it visible that my own registered branch set had no slot for "the claim is
false." Registration doesn't just discipline the answer; it exposes which questions you were
structurally unable to ask.
