## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (1 was a 404)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The site asks a question I find compelling and is unusually honest about its own failures, but the moment I scratch beneath the headline I'm staring at `tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` and feeling like I wandered into the wrong classroom.

### Journey
Landed via the Reddit link expecting a "weird physics theory of everything" vibe. The opening question — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is exactly the hook that makes me click. Veritasium energy. I like that.

But then immediately under the question is the equation. Big. Centered. With Greek letters. My eye bounced off it like a screen door. I clicked **First Encounter** because it sounded gentle. The neutron-star-vs-electron "presence" example was promising — I could picture that. Then it slipped into "ρ" and "MRH" before I had a foothold. I don't know what those are. I scrolled, didn't bounce, but felt like I was being introduced to people whose names I'd already forgotten.

Tried **Why Synchronism?** next. The "different equations for different scales" framing landed cleanly — I do remember being told quantum and gravity don't fit together. The page is short enough to read. But "MOND" and "pair-breaking" appeared without explanation and I don't know what either is.

I clicked **Honest Assessment** out of curiosity (the top-nav put it front-and-center, which is brave). This was actually the best page I read. *"All models are wrong; some are useful."* The "0 confirmed predictions across 3,302 sessions" line is shocking in a good way — I've literally never seen a physics-adjacent site lead with that. It made me trust them more, not less. This is the page I'd quote to a friend.

Tried **What this is not** from the top nav — **404**. That's a broken link on the landing page nav menu, which is a bad first-five-minutes experience.

**Test Catalog** felt like an outline of an outline. I saw "24 experiments" and "Tier 1, 2, 3, 4" but no flavor of what any of them actually look like.

**Core Idea** had the best analogy I encountered: the *dimmer switch* between quantum and classical. That clicked. But the very next paragraph hit me with "1/√Ncorr dependence resembles central-limit-theorem scaling" and I lost the thread.

**Glossary** was a relief — and also a trap. "Tanh" actually got a friendly definition. "Coherence" got a clean one too. But "Presence (ρ)" is defined using "Markov Relevancy Horizon," which is defined using "degrees of freedom," and I'm now five clicks deep in a chain of physics vocabulary trying to learn one concept.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — *intuitive mechanically, opaque conceptually* — I can drag γ and watch a curve get steeper. Cool. I have no idea what γ being steeper means about the world. Felt like a physics-themed Etch A Sketch.
- **γ Calculator** — worked — *better than the Explorer because of presets* — Clicking "ideal gas" vs "Bose-Einstein condensate" and seeing γ jump several orders of magnitude actually taught me something — namely that "more correlated particles = smaller gamma." But I still couldn't tell you why I should care.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is "presence" actually a measure of, in a way I could describe to my mom? "Compatible degrees of freedom inside an MRH" is not it.
2. Why this particular curve? The dimmer-switch analogy explains *what* tanh does but not *why this is the one true equation*.
3. The site says galaxy rotation curves "succeeded" on 14,760 galaxies and other things "failed." If 0 predictions are confirmed, what does "succeeded" mean here?
4. If the framework can be applied to consciousness, dark matter, *and* superconductors, am I supposed to take that as ambition or as a warning sign? The site's own honesty makes me uncertain which framing it wants me to hold.

### Verdict
The site has done something rare: it tells me up front that most of its predictions haven't been confirmed and it shows me the failures without flinching. I genuinely respect that — it's the most trustworthy physics-fringe site I've ever clicked on. But everything between the inviting question on the landing page and the brave honesty of the assessment page is a wall of equations and Greek letters I can't climb. As a casual reader, I leave knowing the site is honest and ambitious, but not knowing what synchronism actually *says*. Fix the broken top-nav link, push the equation below the fold, and add one good diagram — and I'd return.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of structure; physics not evaluated)
- **Would return?**: yes — to audit again after fixes
- **Overall impression**: A site with disciplined breadcrumbs and a real glossary, undermined by a thin global navigation, validation badges that are used in three places but defined in two, and core terms that are introduced before they're explained.

### Journey
I went in with one job: see whether a literate non-physicist could *navigate* this site without stumbling — separate from whether they'd understand the physics. I started at the landing page, mapped the chrome, walked the "Where to Start" path, then audited the spots Pass 1 flagged.

**Top-of-page chrome.** The persistent top nav has exactly three items: the logo, **Glossary**, and **Honest Assessment**. That's it. No way to reach Key Claims, Test Catalog, Predictions, Interactive Tools, Research Philosophy, or any of the 74 pages I later confirmed exist — except by hunting through inline links or the footer (which has only three more items: Research Archive, Research Philosophy, Predictions). For a site with this much content, the global IA is doing almost no work. Everything depends on the user landing on a page with a good "Related Concepts" block.

**Pass 1's "404."** I want to flag a discrepancy: Pass 1 reported a 404 on a top-nav "What this is not" link. The current top nav doesn't contain that label — it's a *call-to-action button on the landing page*, and the page (`/what-synchronism-is-not`) loads cleanly with proper breadcrumbs. Either Pass 1 misread the chrome or the link was fixed since. Worth the maintainer verifying — but reporting it as a top-nav 404 was inaccurate.

**Breadcrumbs.** Genuinely good. Every subpage I checked uses the same `Home / Section / Page` pattern (`Getting Started`, `Predictions & Experiments`, `Methodology`, `Interactive Tools`). This is the most rigorous IA element on the site. Sad that there's no equivalent global nav reflecting these same sections — the breadcrumbs imply a site map that the top nav never reveals.

**Validation badges — the central IA failure.** The badges (Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed) are the site's core honesty mechanism. They appear on Key Claims, get listed on Honest Assessment, and get the most thorough treatment on Research Philosophy. The problem: when I visited Key Claims and saw "Untested — 8-Way Convergence" and "Speculative — Structural Problems" stamped on big bold claims, **nothing on the page told me what the badge meant or linked me to a definition.** A reader hitting Key Claims first (a plausible entry point — it's a CTA on the landing page) gets the labels with none of the framework. The taxonomy exists in two places and is anchored from neither place where it's actually used.

**Glossary — defines its own terms, but not for outsiders.** The first 8 entries are functional but completely circular for a newcomer. "Presence (ρ): Compatible Structural Elements within MRH" — the *definition of the framework's central variable* is six words long and references two other jargon items. "MRH" is defined further down using "degrees of freedom." There's no alphabetical index, no anchor links, no search. For a glossary called out in the top nav with "30+ terms defined," this needs an A–Z scaffold and a plain-language hook on every entry.

**First Encounter.** Pass 1 was right that MRH appears in the introductory tutorial. To be fair, the page *does* define MRH inline ("Markov Relevancy Horizon, or MRH") in the opening sentence — the issue isn't undefined acronyms, it's that the opening sentence introduces *three* new concepts at once: "presence," "compatible structural elements," and "Markov Relevancy Horizon." That's not a navigation problem; it's a writing problem. The 7-step format is a strong scaffold; step 1's first sentence wastes it.

**Test Catalog.** Clean tier structure (Tier 1: 10 / Tier 2: 4 / Tier 3: 7 / Tier 4: 3 = 24 tests, matches the headline). I dove into Tier 1, which uses a different status vocabulary entirely — "TEST-01," "Kill criteria," and `⚠ Status note:` labels. None of the validation badges from Honest Assessment / Key Claims appear here. The site has *two* parallel status vocabularies (badges vs. test status notes) that aren't reconciled. This is exactly the kind of thing a reader will read as "different teams, no convention."

**Tools.** I tested two Pass 1 hadn't tried.

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — works, with three-curve charts (observed / Newtonian / Synchronism) for five preset SPARC galaxies. Good axis labels (kpc, km/s) and a clear "What You're Seeing" interpretive panel ("the gap between Newtonian and observed is what ΛCDM calls 'dark matter'"). Nicely scoped tool. **But** it never defines what SPARC is. The dropdown labels galaxies as "SPARC galaxies"; a tech-writer reader who doesn't know the acronym is left guessing whether SPARC is a survey, an instrument, or a physicist's last name.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — name says "phase boundary," tool offers a single 1D γ slider. There's no ρ axis at all despite the name implying a γ–ρ phase diagram. The visualization is more a "γ regime explorer" than a phase-boundary plot. Either rename the tool or upgrade it to deliver what the name promises. Also, neither γ nor ρ is defined on the page — the user is expected to bring those definitions in from elsewhere.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (1 was a 404: `/coherence-equation`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of what the framework claims and what it doesn't establish)
- **Would return?**: yes — this is more interesting than I expected, but not for the reasons the front page advertises
- **Overall impression**: An unusually self-aware framework whose `Honest Assessment` is doing harder physics than its derivation pages. The central equation is asserted, not derived; the headline "derived from first principles" claim for γ = 2/√N_corr does not survive contact with mean-field theory; but the site itself has already noticed most of this, which is rare and worth taking seriously.

### Journey

I came in skeptical. "One equation across 80 orders of magnitude" with a `tanh` in it tells me, before I read a word of derivation, that I'm looking at a mean-field order-parameter ansatz. So my first stop was `/key-claims`, then I went looking for the actual derivations.

**Central equation, `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))`.** The functional form is the giveaway. `tanh` of a coupling-vs-coupling-critical argument is precisely the Ising-mean-field self-consistency solution `m = tanh(βJ⟨m⟩)`. That's not a problem — it's a venerable starting point — but it has consequences: mean-field theory has known critical exponents (β=½, ν=½, η=0) that are *wrong* below the upper critical dimension, and you can only fix that with a Ginzburg-Landau expansion, fluctuation corrections, or RG. If a framework is implicitly mean-field, it will inherit mean-field's failures. So I went looking for the derivation page.

**`/coherence-equation`: 404.** This is *the* page a grad student wants. Dead. The walkthrough page (`/equation-walkthrough`) was supposed to substitute, but it shows "Step 1 of 6" — and step 1 is purely definitional ("ρ is the local mass/energy density"). I couldn't progress through the steps via WebFetch, so I cannot evaluate whether steps 2-6 contain a genuine derivation or a sequence of motivations. **This is the single most important page on the site for a technical audience, and it's either broken or unfetchable.**

**`/gamma-parameter`: the claimed first-principles derivation of γ = 2/√N_corr.** Two assertions:

1. *"Phase space has 6 dimensions (3 position + 3 momentum). Through contraction to effective degrees of freedom, this yields a factor of 2."* This is not a derivation — it is verbal arithmetic. Phase-space dimension does not generically halve to "effective DOF"; that depends on the Hamiltonian, the constraints, and the symplectic structure. A grad student reading this is thinking: "where is the action, where is the constraint surface, what's the projection?" None of that appears. The factor 2 is asserted and then defended by "if you change it, chemistry correlations degrade" — but that is precisely *fitting*, not derivation.

2. *"In a system of N_corr correlated particles, the effective coupling decays as 1/√N_corr. This is the standard central limit theorem result."* The CLT applies to **independent** random variables. For correlated variables the scaling depends on the correlation structure: it can be 1/N (long-range order), 1/√N (short-range, finite correlation length, post-coarse-graining), or anomalous (critical regime). Invoking the CLT for "correlated particles" without specifying the correlation kernel is exactly the hand-wave the term "central-limit-like" was invented to disguise. *If the answer is "we treat coarse-grained patches as approximately independent past the correlation length," then say that and quote the correlation length.*

So γ = 2/√N_corr is, charitably, a **scaling argument with a fitted prefactor**, not a derivation from first principles. The "Validated" framing on the front of the site overstates this.

**`/galaxy-rotation` and the MOND scale.** The site claims `a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²` "emerges from the framework." This is **Milgrom's coincidence**, known since 1983. It appears in Verlinde 2017 entropic gravity, McCulloch's quantized inertia, Smolin 2017, and is essentially a dimensional-analysis identity once you decide a₀ should be cosmological. The honest-assessment page itself admits this is dimensional analysis, not unique. The galaxy-rotation page does not present a derivation chain from `(γ, ρ, MRH)` to a₀ — it just states the result. What I needed to see: where does the factor of 2π come from? In Milgrom it's set by external-field-effect fits. In Verlinde it's the boundary entropy of de Sitter. In Synchronism, it's not shown. *This claim should be a "Reparametrization" badge, not "Strongly Supported," because every framework that gets a₀ in the cH₀ ballpark gets it for the same dimensional reason.*

The Bullet Cluster sign error, in contrast, is **handled correctly**. The viscosity interpretation predicted dark matter sticks more than baryons; observation shows the opposite. The site labels this a *structural* failure (a sign error cannot be retuned away) and keeps it visible. That is the right call, and it's the most credible single thing on the site.

**`/measurement-without-observers`.** As a CMT student I read this looking for new content. What I see: "MRH crossing IS collapse — derived from γ." No Lindblad equation, no master equation, no einselection-vs-MRH comparison. The mechanism described — system couples to ~10²³ environmental DOF, N_corr increases, γ decreases — is **standard environmental decoherence** (Zurek, Joos-Zeh, Schlosshauer) with the substitution `pointer basis` → `MRH`. The cited PRL 2024 "10× T₂ improvement" is post-diction; resynchronization is the only untested prediction and isn't formalized. This page is the cleanest case where the framework is doing **renaming, not new mechanism**. The badge for this entire page should be Reparametrization, with the resynchronization prediction split out and labeled Untested.

**`/hard-problem` (consciousness, `C = f(γ, D, S)`).** Here the site is more honest than its front page implies. D (dimensional embedding) and S (self-modeling depth) are **explicitly not operationally defined** — the page admits there is "no stated measurement procedure or unit." That means the equation is not yet a function — it's a schema. The "8-way theoretical convergence" at C ≈ 0.50 is also explicitly walked back: the page acknowledges the eight approaches are not independent. *This is a candor I respect.* But then the front page calls this "Untested with 8-way convergence" as if it were a strength. It isn't — dependent convergence is just one model viewed from eight angles. The badge should be Speculative until D and S have units.

**Critical exponents — the smoking gun for mean-field.** Honest Assessment quietly notes: *"Predicted critical exponents differ from observed by a factor of ~2."* This is *exactly* what naïve mean-field gives you on a 3D Ising-class system: β_MF = 0.5 vs β_3D-Ising = 0.326 (~1.5× off), ν_MF = 0.5 vs ν_3D-Ising = 0.630 (~1.25× off), and gross errors on η. A factor-of-2 mismatch on critical exponents is the **signature** of a framework that needs RG/fluctuation corrections it hasn't applied. The melting-point 53% error is also consistent with mean-field failure on a first-order transition driven by lattice geometry, where mean-field is famously bad. **The framework's failures are exactly the failures any condensed-matter theorist would predict for a `tanh` ansatz applied without fluctuation corrections.** The site treats these as separate failures; they are one failure, and naming it would tighten the diagnosis dramatically.

**`/tier-1-existing` — actually surprising.** I expected a wishlist of vague observations. What I found: 10 tests, of which **6 are genuinely discriminative against MOND/ΛCDM**. TEST-02 (wide-binary anomaly's dependence on local stellar density) is the cleanest discriminator — MOND predicts pure acceleration-dependence, Synchronism predicts density-dependence, Gaia DR3 can decide. TEST-04 (BAO peak shifts of ~10⁻⁴ between high and low-density regions) is concrete enough to attack with DESI. TEST-07 (galaxy-cluster oscillations at λ ~ 500 Mpc) is bold and falsifiable. TEST-09 and TEST-10 are correctly self-flagged as "MOND-shared." This catalog is the most respectable artifact on the site — and it's buried two clicks deep behind a top nav that doesn't link to it.

### Tools Tested
- **Equation Walkthrough** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — partial — *broken in WebFetch, can't evaluate beyond Step 1* — Step 1 is purely definitional. The other 5 steps are exactly where the derivation lives or doesn't. **This is the single most important interactive artifact for a technical audience and I cannot evaluate it.** If you want grad students to take this site seriously, this tool needs to be (a) statically scannable as a single page and (b) include the actual algebra: from what objective do we Legendre-transform, what's the partition function, what's the order-parameter equation that yields tanh?
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/gamma-boundary` Chemistry Correlation Explorer) — works — *suggestive but methodologically thin* — Shows 23 curated rows from 1,703 phenomena with correlation coefficients up to r=0.982. **What's missing**: residuals, R², scatter plots, hold-out test, Bonferroni correction. Cherry-picking 23 high-correlation rows from 1,703 candidates is N=1703 multiple-comparison territory; you would need to show that the *full* distribution of correlations is different from null, and that prediction accuracy survives a held-out partition. As displayed, this is exploratory data analysis presented as evidence. The honest-assessment page already concedes "the 1,703 phenomena are not all statistically independent" — that admission should be visible *inside* the tool.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **Where does `tanh` come from?** Is there an action / free energy whose stationary point gives `m = tanh(...)`, or is the form chosen because it interpolates 0→1? If the latter, the framework should say "phenomenological order parameter" rather than presenting a derivation.
2. **What is the upper critical dimension?** A framework that lives or dies on critical exponents needs to commit. Mean-field becomes correct above the upper critical dimension; below it, fluctuations dominate. Where does Synchronism sit?
3. **Is N_corr a number or a function of (T, ρ, scale)?** The chemistry correlations imply it is system-specific. If so, "γ = 2/√N_corr" is a *family* of equations indexed by N_corr, not one equation. The "one equation, 80 orders of magnitude" framing depends on this distinction and the site doesn't clarify it.
4. **What does the framework predict for the fluctuation spectrum?** Power-law tails, 1/f noise, anomalous diffusion — these are signatures any condensed-matter theorist would interrogate. None appear in what I read.
5. **Why does the Tier 1 catalog look genuinely good while the front-of-site claims look overstated?** I suspect the Tier 1 list was written by someone who actually does observational physics and the front page was written by someone who wants the framework to win. The site would benefit from letting the Tier 1 author write the front page.
6. **The 500 Mpc oscillation prediction (TEST-07) — where does 500 come from?** If it's a free parameter, the test is much weaker than presented. If it's derived, the derivation is the most important paragraph on the site.

### Verdict
This is a more interesting framework than I expected, judged by the standard a grad student actually applies: *can you state a falsifiable prediction that distinguishes you from existing alternatives?* Tier 1 has six. That's six more than most fringe frameworks. But the front-of-site framing oversells the derivations and undersells the tests. The single equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` is a phenomenological mean-field ansatz dressed as a first-principles result — and the framework's documented failures (critical exponents, melting points) are exactly the failures a CMT theorist would predict for an uncorrected mean-field theory applied across phase boundaries. The honest assessment is the best part of the site; the path forward is to let it propagate into the badging, retire the "derived from first principles" language for γ until it actually is, and surface the Tier 1 catalog into the top nav. If those changes happened, I'd send this site to my advisor. As is, I'd send him the Tier 1 page only.

### Novelty audit (the only thing that matters from this seat)
