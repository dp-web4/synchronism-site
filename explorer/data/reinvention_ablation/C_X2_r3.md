**Short answer:** I know of no published derivation of a₀ = c·H₀·Ω_m^φ, and I believe it is not in the literature. It looks like numerology unless a mechanism is supplied.

**Arithmetic check first.** With H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc, c·H₀ ≈ 6.8×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². Ω_m^φ = 0.3^1.618 ≈ 0.143. The product is ≈ 9.7×10⁻¹¹ m/s², not 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. That is about 20% low. It is closer to the observed value (≈1.1–1.2×10⁻¹⁰) than a naive guess, but the stated number does not follow from the stated inputs. A 20% gap is also larger than the scatter in the fitted a₀ from rotation curves (roughly 0.02 dex in the RAR fits).

**What is known (the closest published results):**
- Milgrom (1983), ApJ 270, 365, and later papers note the coincidence a₀ ~ cH₀ and a₀ ~ c²√(Λ/3). The usual form is a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π ≈ 1.1×10⁻¹⁰.
- Milgrom (1999), Phys. Lett. A 253, 273, "The modified dynamics as a vacuum effect," gives a₀ ~ cH₀/2π from the Unruh-like effect of the de Sitter or cosmological horizon.
- Verlinde (2017), SciPost Phys. 2, 016 (arXiv:1611.02269), emergent gravity, uses a₀ = cH₀/6.
- Famaey & McGaugh (2012), Living Reviews in Relativity 15, 10, review the cosmological coincidences. I recall that some papers propose a₀ ∝ c·H₀·Ω_Λ^(1/2) or similar Ω-dependent forms, but I can't give a reliable citation. Treat this as uncertain.

**Novelty and concerns:**
- I know of no paper using the golden ratio as an exponent on Ω_m in a₀.
- Several features make it look like a fit rather than a derivation:
  - The exponent φ is chosen from a continuum of possible values.
  - Ω_m changes with epoch, so the formula predicts a₀ evolving in time. That would conflict with, or have to be checked against, high-z RAR and Tully-Fisher constraints.
  - Ω_m^φ is not a quantity that arises in standard MOND or horizon-based derivations.
- Several nearby combinations also match: cH₀/2π ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰, and cH₀/6 ≈ 1.1×10⁻¹⁰. These need no free exponent.

**Recommendation:** Do a literature search (ADS/arXiv) on "a₀ Ω_m", "MOND cosmological coincidence", and "golden ratio cosmology" to confirm. Then show a dynamical origin for the exponent φ, and quote the corrected numerical value of ≈ 0.97×10⁻¹⁰ m/s².
