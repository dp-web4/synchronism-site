## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Journey

I clicked this from Reddit expecting either crackpottery or a fun rabbit hole. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a good hook. The marching band vs. wandering crowd analogy landed instantly. That's the best thing on the site: I could explain it to a friend at a bar after reading one paragraph.

Then, still on the landing page, three big numbers: **0** prospective predictions confirmed, **6** refutations executed, **0 of 6** "validated" claims surviving audit. My honest reaction was: *oh, so it's dead?* I respect it — no other physics site I've ever landed on tells you upfront that it failed — but nobody put a reason to keep scrolling next to those zeros. There's no "here's the one weird thing we still can't explain" line. The honesty is the first thing I meet, and it reads as a scoreboard where the home team is losing 0–6.

I clicked **Beginner Path**. It took me to "Start Here: Why Synchronism? — Step 1 of 6." Then I clicked **Start Here** in the top nav to see what *that* was, and got the same page. Two doors, one room. Minor, but I wasted a click.

Step 1 was good. Step 2, **First Encounter**, is where I first felt out of my depth. It introduces *presence (ρ)* — fine, that's density, and "a neutron star core 10¹⁴× denser than lead" is a fantastic image. But then it hands me the **Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)**, and while the plain-language version is genuinely lovely ("a single atom doesn't feel the Andromeda galaxy — Andromeda is outside its MRH"), it's immediately followed by *"the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence."* I don't know what a degree of freedom is. That sentence is on **page 2 of 7 of a beginner path**. I skimmed the rest of that block.

Step 3, **The Core Idea**, is titled *"One function. Three parameters."* But Step 1 told me there were **two** tunable parameters (sharpness and reference density), and the Coherence Explorer only has **two** sliders. I genuinely could not tell whether I'd missed a parameter or whether the heading was wrong. Small thing that made me trust the page a bit less.

I went to **Tools** because sliders are more fun than prose. The descriptions there are weirdly self-deprecating — "*catch a refuted formula being wrong at every stop*", "*then see why the formula is audited-negative*", "*because they share one assumption*". I get that it's honest, but as a sales pitch for a toy it's odd: why would I play with something advertised as broken? I picked the two that sounded like they'd actually teach me something.

**Coherence Explorer** was the highlight. Two sliders, a live S-curve, and — crucially — plain-English glosses for ln, tanh, γ and ρcrit right there under the chart. The dimmer-switch metaphor for tanh worked. I now understand what the equation *does* even though I couldn't do the math.

Then I hit the thing that actually broke my understanding. The γ slider **defaults to 0.49**. But the Equation Anatomy page and The Core Idea both teach me that γ = 2/√Ncorr — and the Equation Anatomy page then says that rule is *"sign-inverted against real finite-size scaling,"* and the Tools page calls it *"audited-negative."* So: the one formula the site gives me for the one number that controls the whole shape of the curve is flagged as wrong, and the actual number in the tool (0.49) arrives with no explanation of where it came from. I was left holding an equation with a knob and no story about the knob. That's the single biggest comprehension failure of my visit, and it's not a jargon problem — it's a missing sentence.

Same category, smaller: ρcrit is called "critical density," and then I'm told it's *"not the midpoint"* and *"not technically a critical point"* — it's a "saturation knee." The tool confirms it: C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, not 0.5. So the parameter's name means the opposite of what it does. I understand *why* after reading it three times; a casual reader who reads once will just be wrong.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** was where I bounced hardest. The setup is great — "the gap is the puzzle," visible matter can't explain how fast galaxies spin, everyone's seen that on YouTube. Then there are **five** curves. The dashed gray one is what physics predicts. The blue dots are reality. Good, I see the gap. But the violet curve — *Synchronism's actual theory* — is labeled *"max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on,"* meaning the theory does nothing here. The amber curve, which is the one that visually *fits* the dots, is labeled *"Illustration only — hand-tuned to fit, NOT computed from the theory."* And the green one is MOND, somebody else's theory, which apparently works.

So the flagship demo of the site shows: rival theory fits, this theory is flat, and the pretty curve is a fake. That is admirably honest and I want to say so. But as a first-time visitor I stared at it and thought — *why is this the demo?* Nothing on the page tells me what I'm supposed to feel about that picture. And the amber decoy is dangerous: it's the curve my eye goes to, and if I'd skimmed the legend I'd have walked away telling people the theory fits galaxy rotation.

I finished on **Honest Assessment**, which is the most impressive page on the site and the one I'd actually link on Reddit. But it's wall-to-wall σ values (3.3σ, +17.95σ, 2.4σ) with no inline explanation of what σ means; I had to guess "amount of wrongness" from context. The glossary does define it — but nothing on the assessment page told me the glossary existed or linked to it. Same for BTFR, RAR, SPARC, Cassini, CHSH. The glossary itself is good (MRH, tanh and MOND are genuinely plain-language) but I only found it because it's in the top nav, and by then I'd already skimmed past the terms.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary

- **Overall impression**: Every closed-form identity I checked by hand was correct — which makes it all the more striking that the flagship galaxy demo plots a force law that contradicts the one the theory page states, and that the page named after ρ_crit describes ρ_crit as something the function provably isn't.

### Journey

A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project," which usually means I get to spend an afternoon finding the place where a sigmoid got mistaken for a phase transition. That is roughly what happened, but not in the way I expected.

**I checked the algebra first, because that's where these things normally die.** It didn't die. The core object is C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)), and `/coherence-function` asserts a set of identities. I verified six independently:

- **Hill equivalence.** tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. Substituting e^u = (1+x)^γ into tanh(u) = (e^u − e^{−u})/(e^u + e^{−u}) gives it in one line. **Correct**, and correctly described as an exact identity rather than an asymptotic one.
- **Small-x limit** C ≈ γx. **Correct.**
- **Large-x limit** C → 1 − 2(1+x)^{−2γ}. **Correct** — (a−1)/(a+1) = 1 − 2/(a+1).
- **Derivative** dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x), maximal at x = 0 with value γ. **Correct**, and both factors are monotone decreasing, so the concave-everywhere / no-inflection claim follows. This is the single most important structural fact on the site and it is stated accurately.
- **MOND point.** At γ = 1/2 the identity gives C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). **Correct.**
- **Coherence Explorer readouts** at γ = 0.49: C(ρ_crit) = 0.3272, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.8259, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.9785, and C = 0.5 at x = 2.07. I get 0.32717, 0.82587, 0.97852, and atanh(0.5)/γ = 1.1210 ⇒ x = 2.0683. **All four correct to the digits shown.**

That's a better hit rate than most published papers I referee. So the interesting failures here are not algebra failures. They're *wiring* failures — places where two pages hold incompatible versions of the same physics and neither knows about the other.

**The one that matters most is the galaxy plotter.** `/for-researchers` states the dynamics plainly: the modified Poisson equation ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ, the algebraic force law **g = g_bar/C(ρ)**, and the identification **f_DM = 1 − C**, with C playing the role of MOND's μ. Under any of those three statements, C is in the *denominator*: as C → 0 the boost diverges, and f_DM → 1 means all of the observed rotation is "dark."

The plotter computes the violet Synchronism curve as

```
v(r) = √(v_b² + [V_flat · C(ρ(r))]²),   ρ(r) = Σ(r)/2h,   ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat²
```

Here the dark contribution is **proportional to C**, capped at V_flat, and switches *off* as C → 0. That is the inverse of the site's own force law and the inverse of f_DM = 1 − C. The legend then draws the headline conclusion from exactly this inversion: *"max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on."*

Run the site's own law on the site's own number instead. DDO 154: V_flat = 47 km/s ⇒ ρ_crit = 0.029 × 47² ≈ 64 M☉/pc³; taking Σ ~ 20 M☉/pc² and h ~ 300 pc gives ρ ≈ 0.033 M☉/pc³, so x ≈ 5×10⁻⁴ and C ≈ 2γx ≈ 1×10⁻³ — the quoted 0.001 is arithmetically right. But g = g_bar/C with C = 10⁻³ gives v = v_b/√C ≈ **31× the baryonic curve**, not zero boost. Under f_DM = 1 − C you get f_DM = 0.999, i.e. the theory says DDO 154 is 99.9% dark matter, which is also a 31× velocity overshoot, not a flat line.

So the framework's most-linked demo doesn't fail the way the demo says it fails. It fails ~31× *high*, and the plot shows it failing 100% *low*, because the plotter uses an additive-in-quadrature stand-in formula that appears nowhere else on the site and has the sign of the C-dependence backwards. Both outcomes are refutations, so the scoreboard doesn't move — but "the boost never activates" and "the boost overshoots by a factor of 31" are different physical diagnoses that would send you to different repairs, and the site is currently publishing the one its own equations don't support. Note also that the rest of the site *has* the sign right: TEST-10's "bounded boost caps apparent DM fraction at f_DM ≤ 68.5%" only makes sense with C in the denominator. The plotter is the outlier, not the tests.

**Second wiring failure: `/critical-density` contradicts the function.** The page states *"the density at which the coherence function transitions from quantum (C → 0) to classical (C → 1),"* and *"Below ρ_crit: quantum effects persist. Above ρ_crit: classical behavior dominates."* But C(ρ_crit) = 0.327 at the fitted γ = 0.49 and 0.882 at the asserted γ = 2 — two wildly different places on the curve, neither of them a transition — and, more fundamentally, dC/dx is maximal at x = 0, so **there is no transition at ρ_crit or anywhere else**. The function is concave on the whole domain. `/coherence-function` and the glossary both say this correctly ("saturation knee — NOT a phase-transition critical density"). The page named after the parameter is the one that gets it wrong, and it's the page a reader lands on from the equation.

**Third: the "+1" is load-bearing and never justified.** `/equation-walkthrough` walks ln, tanh, and γ, and gives the +1 no discussion at all. It deserves the most. Drop it: tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit)) is a genuine sigmoid, antisymmetric in ln ρ about ρ_crit, zero at ρ_crit, with maximum slope exactly there. That object *earns* the name "critical density." Map it to [0,1] the obvious way — ½[1 + tanh(γ ln x)] = x^{2γ}/(x^{2γ} + 1) — and it satisfies all four of the site's own stated requirements (bounded on [0,1], monotone, smooth saturation, correct ρ→0 and ρ→∞ limits) *while keeping the midpoint at ρ_crit with C = 1/2*. The regularized form buys nothing the alternative doesn't also give, and it costs the entire physical interpretation of the parameter.

Here's the part the site has all the pieces for but doesn't assemble: that alternative is precisely **Hill in x**, and Hill n = 1 is the *best-fitting row in the site's own form-selection table* (ΔBIC = −0.7, beating the framework's own tanh-log free-γ at +7.1 with one fewer free parameter). So the data prefer the un-regularized class, and the un-regularized class is the one where ρ_crit means what it's called, and nobody has drawn the line between those two facts.

**Fourth: `/phase-transitions` is a page out of time.** It presents γ = 2 (quantum) / γ ≈ 1 (boundary) / γ → 0 (classical) as "Core Framework," carries no audited-negative badge, and says of critical exponents that *"measured discrepancies are approximately 2×."* `/coherence-function` says the opposite in kind: the function has no inputs for spatial dimension, order-parameter dimension, or symmetry group, and *"therefore cannot predict critical exponents or distinguish universality classes."* That's the correct statement — C(ρ) is analytic on ρ > 0 with no singularity, so it has no critical exponents to be 2× off by. "Off by ~2×" reads as a quantitative near-miss (mean-field β = 1/2 vs 3D Ising 0.326 is a 1.5× gap, ν 0.5 vs 0.63 a 1.26× gap, so the number is even superficially plausible) when the honest statement is that the quantity doesn't exist. This is the one place on the site where a limitation is described as *softer* than the site's own analysis elsewhere. Everywhere else the bias runs the other way.

**Fifth, and this one is a two-line glossary entry:** N_corr says *"A crystal lattice might have N_corr = 10²⁴ (γ ≈ 10⁻¹², classical)."* But γ = 10⁻¹² gives C = tanh(10⁻¹²·ln(1+x)) ≈ 0 for every physically reachable x — you'd need x ~ e^(10¹²) to lift it. And C ≈ 0 is defined throughout the site as the **sparse/independent/quantum** regime. So the framework's own classical limit lands on its own quantum label. The γ entry carries the audited-negative caveat; the N_corr entry states the contradiction flatly with no caveat at all, and `/phase-transitions` builds a three-regime ladder on top of it.

**On ρ_crit ∝ V_flat².** `/parameter-derivations` correctly flags that MOND-matching requires ρ_crit ∝ V⁻², not V⁺². I'd push further: the MOND-equivalent local threshold has **no velocity dependence at all**. The threshold is a surface density, Σ_crit = a₀/2πG, so the corresponding volume density is ρ_crit = a₀/(4πGh) — set by scale height, not by V. Numerically, with h = 300 pc that's ≈ 0.23 M☉/pc³, against the framework's 64 M☉/pc³ for DDO 154: **~280× too high**, which is the entire reason max C = 0.001. (Assumptions: h = 300 pc, a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², thin-disk midplane ρ = Σ/2h — the ratio scales linearly in h⁻¹ so this is an order-of-magnitude statement, and it sits comfortably between the site's own "~40×" virial-ratio bound and its "240×–300,000×" exponent-inversion range.) `/critical-density` states ρ_crit = A·V_flat² flatly with only an "A-from-Jeans" badge; the exponent problem never reaches it.

**On the statistics, one commendation and one gap.** The commendation: the glossary's ΔBIC entry states *"ΔBIC = +184 (point estimate) or ≥+33 under conservative intra-galaxy correlation correction."* Somebody knows that 2,807 RAR points drawn from 175 galaxies are not 2,807 independent measurements. That is exactly right and it's the correction most papers in this area skip. (My own crude galaxy-level N_eff ≈ 175 gives something nearer +11; either way it clears the >10 bar, so the verdict is unchanged.) The gap: **that correction exists only in the glossary.** `/honest-assessment` lists the refutation as "ΔBIC = +184" bare, and `/coherence-function`'s form-selection table prints "+184.0" bare. The two pages a reader consults to learn what the refutation *is* both quote the number the site itself has already superseded.

Related: `/honest-assessment` states that all 192 tested parameter combinations miss DESI DR2's preferred quadrant *"by 3.4–6.3σ."* I can't evaluate that without knowing what was held fixed. A grid scan over 192 combinations is not a fit, and a "σ from a quadrant" is not a likelihood-based statistic. It matters a great deal whether w₀ was profiled or pinned, because DESI DR2's own preference for evolving dark energy is only ~2.8–4.2σ depending on the SN compilation — so ΛCDM itself sits ~3σ from DESI's preferred point. A model quoted at 3.4σ is therefore barely distinguishable from "behaves like ΛCDM," and one at 6.3σ is a very different claim. Stating which nuisances were marginalised and which were fixed would resolve it in one sentence.

Two smaller statistical notes. TEST-25's Cassini result is quoted as *"+17.71σ to +18.00σ"* — four significant figures on an 18σ number is not information; at that level you are quoting the tail of a Gaussian you no longer believe. And the landing page says the 6 refutations have *"2–3 independent framework-specific roots"* while `/honest-assessment` says *"3–4 independent empirical roots."* One of those is stale.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Which map from C to dynamics is canonical — ∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ, g = g_bar/C, or the plotter's v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)²? The first two differ from each other by an EFE-like term and the third is not derivable from either. The site calls this the "force-law fork" on `/for-researchers`, but the fork is unresolved *and* the plotter has silently picked a fourth branch.
2. If ρ_crit = A·V_flat² is inverted relative to MOND, and the MOND-equivalent threshold is V-independent (ρ_crit = a₀/4πGh), is there any reading under which ρ_crit is a property of the *system* rather than of the local *scale height*? That's what would decide whether "per-system ρ_crit" is physics or bookkeeping.
3. What is the +1 for? Nothing on the site defends it, and the bounded alternative that preserves the midpoint is the form the site's own fit prefers.
4. Was w₀ profiled or pinned in the 192-combination DESI scan? The whole weight of "the framework fails the dominant cosmological anomaly" rests on this.
5. C is described as doubly unanchored — no protocol maps any observable to C, and N_corr is asserted rather than counted. Given that, what would it even mean to *measure* γ outside a fit? Every γ on the site is a fitted or asserted number.
6. `/phase-transitions` reports 89% of 1,703 chemical phenomena falling in the "boundary region." Against what null? If γ is fitted per phenomenon and the acceptance band is wide, 89% is a statement about the band, not the physics. The chemistry explorer computes a null model; the phase-transitions page quoting the 89% doesn't reference it.

### Verdict

The mathematics that is written down is correct — I checked six identities and four numerical readouts by hand and all ten held, including the non-obvious ones (the exact Hill equivalence, the γ = 1/2 collapse to μ_simple, the concave-everywhere result that kills the "maximum curvature at γ ≈ 1" story). That is not what I expected from a grand unified framework, and it earns the site a serious reading. The framework itself is, by its own correct accounting, MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17} — a strict submodel that can tie or lose and never win — and the site says so on the page a researcher lands on.

What's broken is propagation, not algebra, and it's broken in the two places that do the most damage: the flagship galaxy demo plots a C-to-velocity map that inverts the theory's own force law, so the site is publishing the wrong *diagnosis* of a real failure (boost never activates, when the stated law gives a 31× overshoot); and the page named after ρ_crit describes it as the phase transition the rest of the site correctly insists it isn't. Add the ΔBIC correction that reached the glossary and neither page that presents the refutation, and the pattern is clear — this project audits itself well and *distributes* its audits badly. Fix the plotter's formula and `/critical-density`'s framing and the technical layer becomes genuinely trustworthy, because everything underneath them already is.

### Journey

I came for the novelty audit. A colleague described this as "the AI physics project that grades itself," and I wanted to know two things: whether anything here is new, and whether the machinery that concluded *nothing is new* can be trusted to have looked.

**The galaxy sector is closed and closed correctly, and I want to be explicit about that before I start finding fault.** `/for-researchers` states the result cleanly: any gravity modification keyed on local volumetric density ρ(r) through an algebraic coupling fails in three independent ways — SPARC rejects it at ΔBIC ≥ +33 (conservative) to +184, the density–acceleration offset spans ~1.7 dex, and cluster calibrations demand ρ_crit factors of 10⁴–10⁶ away from the galaxy value. The triage that separates *local* density schemes (dead) from *non-local* variable schemes (alive) is a genuinely useful lesson for the emergent-gravity literature, and it is stated at the right generality. That's a publishable negative result. Not a large one, but real.

**And the citation practice is better than the field's.** I went hunting for the failure mode I expected — an "executed refutation" that is a rediscovery of published work, presented as the project's own. TEST-25 is exactly that shape: a joint SPARC-RAR + Cassini-quadrupole squeeze, +17.95σ, headlined as one of six refutations. Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024, MNRAS 530, 1781) did this, at 8.7σ, for AQUAL and QUMOND. I expected to find it uncited. Instead `/tier-1-existing` says, in its own words, that the result "is, to within the choice of interpolating-function family, Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024)," and that it was "executed here 2026-07-23, two years later, without citing it" — the admission of the un-attributed execution is *on the page*. That raised the stakes on the rest, because a project this careful about attribution has no excuse for the two things I did find.

**Finding 1: the dark-energy sector is the galaxy sector again, and the site hasn't run its own diagnosis on it.**

`/dark-energy` is where the live claims are. The structure: w(z) runs monotonically from w = −2γ in the far past to w = −1 asymptotically, which forces sign(w₀ + 1) = sign(wₐ) in CPL. DESI DR2 prefers w₀ > −1 with wₐ < 0 — mixed signs — so the framework structurally cannot reach the preferred quadrant. Zero of 192 γ values do, at every Brans-Dicke ω tested. TEST-26 registers DR3 (~2027–28) as the adjudicator.

This is the best-formed prediction on the site. It's structural rather than fitted, it's prospective, and it's stated before the data. But two things are wrong with how it's presented, and they're the same two things the galaxy sector took three years to learn.

*It's a nested submodel again.* The sign lock is not framework-specific. Any single-field freezing/tracker dark energy relaxing to w = −1 from above obeys sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) = (+,+); any phantom field relaxing to −1 from below obeys (−,−). The framework spans both branches across γ = 1/2, which is slightly more than quintessence can do, but the *lock itself* is the generic signature of monotone relaxation to −1 — it is shared with an entire established model class. So if DR3 confirms the crossing, TEST-26 fires, and what dies is the class; Synchronism dies with it as a passenger, exactly as it died a passenger on TEST-25's exclusion of the RAR-preferred interpolating family. Meanwhile the model DESI actually motivated — thawing quintessence, where w departs *from* −1 in the past rather than approaching it in the future (Shlivko & Steinhardt 2024; Cortês & Liddle 2024) — sits comfortably in the preferred quadrant and is the live competitor this page never names. The site diagnosed "MOND ∩ {B ≤ 3.17} — can only tie or lose" for galaxies with real rigor. Nobody has applied that same test to the DE sector, and it returns the same answer.

*And its power is uncomputable from anything the site publishes.* The page says γ = 1/2 makes the background algebraically identical to ΛCDM and that the galaxy fit returns γ = 0.489, "within 2.2% of it," which it calls "a theory whose best fit is its own disappearance." Good line, and it undersells the problem. Because w₀ lies between the endpoints −2γ and −1, |w₀ + 1| ≤ |2γ − 1|, which at γ = 0.489 is **0.022** — an upper bound, and a loose one: at γ = 2 the page's own w₀ = −1.24 realizes only 8% of the corresponding bound of 3, which if it carries over puts the actual deviation nearer 0.002. Against DESI DR2's σ(w₀) ≈ 0.06–0.08, that is ≲ 0.3σ *at the ceiling*. At its own preferred γ this model is not merely ΛCDM-like; it is ΛCDM to well inside DR3's reach.

Which means TEST-26's discriminating power rests entirely on how far γ can move from 0.489 — and **the site publishes γ = 0.489 with no error bar, anywhere, on any page.** I looked on `/dark-energy`, `/for-researchers`, `/tier-1-existing`, and the tool pages. Four significant figures, no interval. If σ(γ) is ~0.02 the test is dead on arrival; if it's ~0.1 there's real power at 2σ. That single missing number decides whether the framework's only prospective test is a test at all, and it is the number the site never prints. This also disposes of the "0 of 192 miss by 3.4–6.3σ" headline that Pass 3 flagged from the statistics side: I can now say what the mechanism is. The scan's grid includes γ values the galaxy sector rejects; the only w₀ the page quotes (−1.24) is at γ = 2, which the site's own SPARC fit rejects at ΔBIC ≥ +33. So the DE sector's headline exclusion is evaluated at the γ the galaxy sector kills — the precise mirror image of the galaxy plotter publishing a verdict computed from a formula the theory page contradicts. Same failure, different sector.

And the lower end of that range is not a statement about this model. ΛCDM itself sits ~3σ from DESI DR2's preferred point. A model quoted at 3.4σ is reporting, to within noise, that it behaves like ΛCDM — which, at γ = 0.489, it provably does.

**Finding 2: `/mond-unification` contains a live, currently-runnable discriminator, and the site's own headline says none exists.**

The EFE section is the sharpest paragraph on the site. It states that C depends on local density alone, so EFE = 0 exactly; it cites Chae, Lelli, Desmond, McGaugh, Li & Schombert (2020, ApJ 904, 51) for the contested ~4σ detection *and* the Freundlich et al. and Paranjape & Sheth systematics rebuttals; and it draws the logic correctly — "a confirmed EFE detection wounds this framework and ΛCDM alike while selecting MOND, and no EFE outcome can ever select this framework over ΛCDM." That's right, and it's the kind of asymmetry most modified-gravity papers get backwards. It then explains why EFE can't test the framework at all right now: at Chae's measurement radii the density law misses the rotation curves by 3–4 dex, 38–92× the entire EFE signal.

And then it says the genuine discriminator is elsewhere: *two satellites at the same external acceleration but different host gas content behave identically under MOND and differently here.* That is a real ρ-keyed-vs-g-keyed test. It is not hypothetical and it is not expensive — SAGA DR3 (Mao et al. 2024) has ~100+ spectroscopically confirmed satellites around 101 Milky Way analogs with host properties including HI mass, and ELVES adds a comparable low-mass sample. The data to run this exist today. The site does not name either survey, does not assign it a TEST-ID, and does not put it in the Tier-1 table.

Two consequences. First, the landing page's "0 confirmed novel predictions" and `/test-catalog`'s "0 of these 24 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" are, on the site's own showing, an *under*-claim — there is a 25th, it isn't in the catalog, and it discriminates. Second, and this is the part that needs a physicist rather than an editor: the discriminator is proposed two paragraphs after the page explains that the framework's density baseline is off by 3–4 dex in exactly that regime. A 3-dex baseline error swamps a gas-content contrast the same way it swamps the EFE signal. Either the satellite test inherits the same disqualification — in which case say so, and the "genuine discriminator" line is wrong — or it doesn't, and someone needs to write down why. As published, the page asserts a discriminator it has already, unknowingly, argued against.

**On A2ACW as a methodology, since I was asked to judge it.** The design is a two-agent defender/challenger protocol assembled from Irving, Christiano & Amodei (2018) plus CAMEL/MetaGPT role structure. Nothing wrong with that as engineering. As an *instrument for detecting novelty* it has been calibrated, and the calibration is damning: on 2026-05-22 the team ran it against six held-out canonical discoveries — Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking, Noether — and it classified **all six as reparametrizations**. False-positive rate 1.0. Specificity 0. `/for-researchers` draws the correct conclusion (Youden's J = 0, the detector is uninformative). `/research-philosophy`, which is the canonical methodology page, reports the control and then concludes that the confound between "the framework contains no novelty" and "the detector cannot find novelty" is unresolved — "a single-arm, single-corpus retrospective with N = 6 audited demotions cannot separate those readings." But the control *does* separate them, in the direction unfavourable to the null: an instrument that cannot recognize Noether's theorem as new cannot support "0 novel results" as a finding about the framework. The conclusion is one sentence away on a page that already contains the premise.

Two statistical points on the null itself, both of which cut against the site's own headline. The "0 survivors" result has **N = 6**. The exact one-sided 95% upper bound on the underlying novelty rate for 0/6 is 1 − 0.05^(1/6) = **39%** — these data are consistent with nearly two in five claims being genuinely novel. And the 3,308-session stat card on the landing page is an effort metric standing where a sample size should be; the 1.4% "session yield" (47/3,308) is a compute statistic, not a discovery rate, and putting 3,308 in 48-point type next to a null lends the null power it does not have. A referee would ask for the denominator to be 6 everywhere it matters, with that 39% bound printed beside it.

I'll note the one thing the null-detector correction does *not* license: it doesn't resurrect the physics. The galaxy sector is dead on data, independently of whether any AI could have recognized novelty in it. What the correction kills is the *program-level* claim — "AI-to-AI adversarial collaboration yields no novel physics" — which is currently unsupported and, on the site's own control, unsupportable with this instrument.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — **diagnosis correct, priority inverted** — The sign-inversion claim is right, and right for the right reason: finite-size scaling rounds a transition over a width ~N^{−1/(dν)}, so sharpness *grows* with system size, while γ = 2/√N_corr makes it *fall* — the exponent has mean-field magnitude with the wrong sign. ### Verdict

The honest answer to "what's new here" is: one modest negative result (local-density algebraic couplings are dead, stated at the right generality and worth citing), one methodological null that its own calibration has invalidated, and one structural dark-energy prediction that is real, prospective, and — on the site's own logic, unapplied — nested inside an existing model class the same way the galaxy sector was nested inside MOND. Nothing here would change how I do my work, and the site says so more clearly than I would have.

What it gets wrong is asymmetric in an unusual direction: it over-refutes its physics and under-claims its own live content. The DESI exclusion is quoted over a γ grid that includes values the galaxy fit rejects, while the fitted γ makes the model ΛCDM to 2% — and the error bar that would settle whether the test has any power is not published anywhere. Simultaneously, `/mond-unification` carries an unregistered, runnable discriminator with public data behind it, and the landing page still says none exists. The self-audit has become the more reliable instrument than the physics, and it now needs to be pointed at the two sectors it hasn't reached: dark energy, and its own null.
