## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /why-synchronism, /learning-paths, /interactive-tools, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /equation-walkthrough, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /two-reframes)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science website I've ever clicked from Reddit. The galaxy tool finally showed me what "the dark matter problem" looks like. But a few pages still read like the theory worked, and one tool contradicts itself about which way its main dial goes.

### Journey
I came in expecting a crackpot "theory of everything" page. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", is exactly that pitch. The very next line undercut it: *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* I've never seen a site do that, and it's the reason I kept reading. The scoreboard was harder. "Prospective predictions confirmed: 0" I could sort of parse. "Refutations executed: 6" I honestly read wrong at first. It sounded like *they* had refuted six things, like a boxer's record, until later pages made clear the idea was the one getting knocked down. "Post-hoc retrodictions attempted: 1 — underpowered" meant nothing to me.

I clicked **Start Here**. The marching-band-vs-crowd analogy is great: one S-curve from "everyone doing their own thing" to "everyone locked in step," plus a dial for how sudden the switch is. I got that. They even explained tanh in plain words ("an S-shaped saturation function"), which I appreciated. Then the page got dense fast. One helpful aside about "compander" curves (like audio volume-levelling, fine) listed four more names in the same breath: μ-law, Hill/Naka–Rushton, Langevin/Curie–Weiss. Further down were "Abrikosov-Gor'kov pair-breaking," "YBCO," "BEC, BCS," "a₀ = cH₀/(2π)," and "Template Bias Caveat," none explained in words I could use. The learning path calls this an ~8-minute beginner step. I skimmed the second half.

**Learning Paths** was clear: a Beginner path, "no physics background needed," in 6 steps. **First Encounter** (step 2) started well. An electron alone in a vacuum vs. the inside of a neutron star, "presence" = how much stuff is packed nearby. The MRH explanation ("Markov" = "only the current neighbors matter, not the history," and the Andromeda-galaxy example) actually landed. The page says "Section 1 of 7" with a Next button. I only saw section 1 (see note below).

**The Core Idea** (step 3) opens with *"One function. Three parameters. Every scale from Planck to cosmic."* and the browser tab says "One Equation, Every Scale." Two clicks ago the homepage told me it didn't hold. The demotion notes come further down, but the first thing I read on this page sounded like a victory lap. I also couldn't work out what the three parameters were. The Coherence Explorer has two sliders, and the list I found named ρ, C and γ, but C is the answer, not a setting. The "Why log? (Honest answer: it's cosmetic)" heading made me laugh; I liked that.

I went to **Tools**. There's a clear "start here" suggestion (Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment), which I followed. **Coherence Explorer**: two sliders, readouts, and two preset buttons, one of them literally labeled "γ = 2 — framework value (refuted)." Then I tried to understand which way the γ dial goes, and the page lost me. It says small γ is "a long gentle ramp," and Start Here says superfluid-type systems "sit at *low* C due to their tiny γ." But the explorer also says *"At very low γ the curve is nearly flat — high coherence at almost all densities, which is the strongly collective regime,"* and labels low γ the "collective / correlated regime." On the homepage, "collective" meant C near 1. So does turning γ down give me high coherence or low coherence? I genuinely can't tell from the words.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the highlight. *"Pick a real galaxy and watch the gray line (what visible matter predicts) sag below the dots (what telescopes measure). That gap is the puzzle."* After years of YouTube videos, that one sentence plus the picture is the first time I've *seen* the dark matter problem. The line *"Plain verdict for casual readers: these curves look great but don't prove the idea — all three models fit galaxy rotation curves about equally well"* is exactly what I needed. The theory's real curve sits right on top of the gray line ("inert by construction"). The pretty amber curve is clearly marked "Illustration only — hand-tuned to fit, NOT computed from the theory." That's refreshingly upfront. The x-axis is in "kpc," which is never explained.

**Equation Anatomy** has a "Step 1 of 6" walkthrough. Its line about logarithms, *"A number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger,"* is the first time ln has meant anything to me.

**Honest Assessment** is 18,000 words, which I was never going to read. Its "If you read nothing else" box saved me: *"The one equation did not hold as stated… 6 refutations executed, 0 predictions confirmed,"* and *"What survives is the record, not the physics… and one live fork (globular clusters)."* I still don't know what a globular cluster fork is. The box also opens with a note that it was "added 2026-09-08 at a casual reader's request," which reads like an edit log rather than content.

I went to the **Glossary** to look up "dark matter," since a whole tool is built around the "dark matter puzzle." There is no Dark matter entry. There's DESI, Decoherence and Dynamical Decoupling, and dark matter only shows up inside the ΛCDM, MOND and NFW definitions. The site otherwise does a nice job defining its own words (72 terms).

Finally, **Two Reframes**, advertised as "Prefer pictures and stories?" with a warning that "some math does appear, including a square root and a cosine." The old-TV-screen (CRT) analogy for superposition was fun and I kind of got it. The page is labeled Speculative, which is fair for "a shift analogous to heliocentrism." It mentions the airplane atomic-clock experiment, which I was going to ask about. But the real hurdle wasn't the square root. It was "Kochen–Specker … theorem-level exclusion," "PBR further constraining the epistemic reading," and "the observer-relative CHSH experiment," none explained. The warning prepared me for the wrong thing.

*Note on method (out of character):* WebFetch can't move sliders or click "Next →". First Encounter and Equation Anatomy expose only step 1 to the fetch, so I have **not** flagged their later steps as missing. In a real browser they're presumably there. Tool tests below use the default state, readouts and page text.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (default readouts present: C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785; presets "γ = 0.49 — SPARC free fit (= MOND)" and "γ = 2 — framework value (refuted)"). Mostly intuitive: the crowd/marching-band framing and the "shifts curve left" help for ρcrit are good. But the low-γ explanation contradicts itself (see friction log), so I came away unsure which direction the main slider goes. A note says superconductors (γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴) are below the slider's 0.01 floor, which is honest and helpful.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked, intuitive. The best page on the site for my level: gray line sags below dots = the puzzle; the real equation's violet line sits on the gray line and doesn't fix it; the amber fit is clearly marked "Illustration only." The "Plain verdict for casual readers" sentence is exactly right. The only snag was the unexplained "kpc."
- **Equation Anatomy**: worked (step 1 of 6 visible to me), intuitive. The ln explanation ("1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger") was the clearest bit of math on the site. It carries an Audited-Negative note about the γ formula, which I noticed but didn't fully follow.

### Unanswered Questions
1. When I turn γ down, does coherence go up or down? The explorer page seems to say both.
2. Did the "6 refutations" refute the theory, or did the theory refute six things? (I think the former, but the homepage label didn't tell me.)
3. What actually *is* dark matter, and does this site think it exists? I couldn't find a plain answer, even in the glossary.
4. What is the "one live fork (globular clusters)" that still survives, in plain words?
5. If the equation "did not hold as stated," why does The Core Idea still open with "Every scale from Planck to cosmic"? Is that the original pitch or the current claim?
6. What's a kpc?
7. Is Two Reframes saying time dilation isn't real, even though the airplane clock experiment measured it? Or just that clocks are the thing being affected?

### Verdict
I came for a "one equation explains everything" pitch and stayed because the site told me in the second sentence that the equation failed. That honesty, plus the Galaxy Curve Plotter, taught me more about the dark matter puzzle than several YouTube videos. What pushed me out were three things. The Beginner path's first step is packed with names I couldn't look up. The Core Idea opener reads like the failure never happened. And the first recommended tool contradicts itself about which way its main dial goes. Fix those and a curious non-physicist could genuinely follow the whole beginner path.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /interactive-tools, /gamma-calculator, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /galaxy-plotter, /key-claims, /test-catalog, /tier-1-existing, /tier-2-pilots, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /for-researchers)

The defects come from the same fact being described in several places that have drifted apart: a tool card describing an old version of the tool, a glossary definition of "Tier" that contradicts the tier pages, and a count whose breakdown doesn't add up on the page that shows it.

### Journey
I started with the site skeleton. The top nav has eight items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment), and the footer adds Key Claims and For Researchers. The nav is odd as a hierarchy. "Tier 1: Existing Data" sits beside "Test Roadmap," but the breadcrumbs put Tier 1 *under* the roadmap (both are "Predictions & Experiments"). Tiers 2–4 aren't in the nav. For Researchers, the page an arriving expert most needs, is footer-only.

The landing scoreboard is well built: six rows, with a "Full ledger →" link and a pointer to badge definitions (`/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`). The page expands MRH, A2ACW and BAO in running text. It does not expand SPARC, ALFALFA, DESI, ΛCDM, CHSH, BIC or CFD, though CFD is explained by context ("computational-fluid substrate").

I checked the badge system next, since that's where science sites usually fall apart. This one has a real contract. Honest Assessment's "How to read a badge" says the color and leading word(s) are the formal status, "drawn only from the two families below". Anything after the dash is "a free-text finding descriptor… Descriptors are not additional badge types." The glossary intro repeats "Validation badges come in two families." That's a good rule. The content doesn't follow it:

- **Tier 1** statuses include "Self-Eliminating-or-Tie," "Never Run As Registered," "Protocol Deviation," "Underpowered on Registered Statistic," "DEMOTED TO TIER-2," "Failed, Ceiling Exceeded" and "Failed, Empty Intersection." TEST-01's badge slot shows **"$0 / 6 weeks"**, a cost rather than a status. The Tier 1 legend adds a third group, "Status lifecycle verbs."
- **Key Claims** renders "New Ontology" and "untestable as stated — ontological reframe" as their own pills, separate from "Speculative."
- **For Researchers** uses "Underpowered" and "Executed" as labels, with no legend link.
- **Interactive Tools** adds "Model Explainer" and "Artifact Lesson" as "content groupings, not validation verdicts." Yet Honest Assessment defines Artifact Lesson inside its validation-badge section, and the glossary has no entry for it.

In practice I count five or six label families, not two.

The **Tier** terminology is the biggest documentation defect I found. The glossary entry reads: *"Tier 1: falsification tests runnable against existing public data… Tier 2: exploratory hypotheses lacking a derived amplitude or mechanism — not yet falsifiable as stated. The tier system classifies proposed tests by readiness, not importance… There is no Tier 3."* The Test Roadmap has **four** tiers organized by cost and duration: "Tier 3: Major Experiments" and "Tier 4: Frontier." `/tier-2-pilots` links onward to "Tier 3: Major Experiments →". `/tier-2-pilots` defines Tier 2 as *"4 Tests, $50K–$200K. These experiments require modest funding… feasible with current technology"*, which is the opposite of "not yet falsifiable." TEST-07 on Tier 1 reads "DEMOTED TO TIER-2," which uses the glossary's readiness sense, but TEST-07 does not appear on the Tier 2 page. One word is carrying two unrelated classification schemes.

On **Tier 1** the headline line reads *"6 refutations executed (5 on external data + 1 theorem): 2 framework-specific mechanism roots (boost ceiling, γ=2 pin) + 1 refuted registration (environment amplitude) + 1 inherited from MOND (Cassini/SPARC) + 1 theorem (Bell/CHSH)."* That breakdown sums to **5**. I went looking for the missing one. Honest Assessment's classification table explains it: TEST-10 is "the same asserted Bmax — corollary, not an independent root," so there are 6 runs and 5 roots. Nothing near the Tier 1 sentence says so, and a reader who adds it up will assume an error.

The **Test Roadmap** leads with the banner *"Untested — 24 Proposed Experiments"* and a "0 Pre-registered & Completed" stat. The site's own Untested badge means "Prediction exists, no data yet." Yet the roadmap's Tier 1 child page lists TEST-09 as Failed and TEST-04 as Withdrawn. A paragraph lower down ("What's Already Been Analyzed") explains that "0 Completed" means formal pre-registered runs. The explanation is honest, but the banner borrows a defined badge word and contradicts its definition.

For the tools, I compared the index cards with the tools they describe. The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** card says *"Synchronism consistency check (violet, fitted to V_flat)… Synchronism and MOND nearly overlap — this is what a reparametrization looks like"* and carries a Reparametrization tag. The tool page says the violet curve *"hugs the baryon line and never fills the gap,"* with *"max C on this disk: 0.001 — inert by construction."* The card describes a curve that overlaps MOND; the tool shows one that sits on the Newtonian line. The card appears to predate a rework of the tool.

The tool pages themselves have good breadcrumbs ("Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator"). Section breadcrumbs are weaker. "Predictions & Experiments" (roadmap and tier pages) and "Methodology" (For Researchers) are **unlinked** labels that match no nav item. "Start Here" links to `/why-synchronism` on Key Claims, so Glossary, Honest Assessment and Key Claims all appear as children of one page.

**Acronyms.** Tool and theory pages use many acronyms without expanding them in the text (SPARC, MOND, ΔBIC, CHSH, BTFR, RAR, EFE, IIT, OOM). I checked each against the glossary before flagging. Nearly all have entries (72 terms), and the glossary reports inline hover definitions site-wide, so this is mostly a discoverability problem, not a missing definition. One case shows why body-text expansion still matters. The Test Roadmap's discrimination statement uses "EFE" unexpanded, and in a gravity context a non-physicist can plausibly read it as "Einstein field equations." The glossary says External Field Effect. (The automated reader I fetched through made exactly that wrong guess.) Terms with no glossary entry: **kpc**, **OOM**, **Artifact Lesson**, **Model Explainer**.

*Method note (out of character):* WebFetch can't hover, drag sliders or see rendered pills. I dropped five candidate findings after verifying them against the live page:
1. "Coherism Explorer" was a fetch misread; the card says "Coherence Explorer".
2. The "Home / / / Interactive Tools" breadcrumb is just how the fetch renders separators.
3. The "undefined acronyms" lists were mostly already covered by the glossary.
4. One fetch showed "Kill Criterion Triggered" under two headings on Tier 1. A verbatim re-fetch shows a single "Operational state" line, which matches the glossary.
5. The "EFE = Einstein field equations" reading was the fetch model's guess, not site text. It is kept above only as evidence of ambiguity.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator**: worked. Default N_corr = 4 gives γ = 1.0000, with the regime label "Boundary (γ ≈ 1) — a label per the inverted formula (Caveat 2), not a physical classification." It has a Linear (1–1000) / Log (1–10⁷) toggle and seven presets (Ideal gas, Liquid water, Galaxy — SPARC best fit (γ ≈ 0.49), Enzyme site, Ferromagnet, BEC, BCS superconductor (10⁷)). The input is intuitive, and BEC, BCS and N_corr are defined right on the page, which is good. The numbers agree across tools: the BCS preset at 10⁷ gives γ = 2/√10⁷ ≈ 6.3×10⁻⁴, matching the Phase Boundary Visualizer's "γ≈6×10⁻⁴" marker. The downside is four caveats (~800 words) that outweigh the tool, plus the badge-ordering issue above.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer**: worked, going by the page text (γ slider over 0–4, three regime cards: γ < 0.6 "High-N_corr (strongly correlated)", γ ≈ 1 "Boundary", γ > 1.4 "Single-particle"). The framing is clear and inviting: "your task is to catch the formula being wrong at every stop." The naming caveat is honest but at odds with the title. The BEC/SC marker sits "~3 OOM off-scale left," so it only indicates direction, and "OOM" is undefined. I couldn't see the promised "reality check line" on each card through WebFetch, so I haven't flagged it.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (description audit only; Pass 1 tested the tool): the tool page is internally consistent (legend: gray Newtonian, violet real C(ρ) "inert by construction," amber "Illustration only," green MOND). Its card on /interactive-tools contradicts it (see friction log).

### Unanswered Questions
1. Does "Tier 2" mean a readiness level (glossary) or a funding band (tier pages)? Where does TEST-07 actually live now?
2. Should a citation say 6 refutations (runs) or 5 (independent roots)? Which is the canonical headline number?
3. Is there one page that lists *every* label family in use (badges, operational states, lifecycle verbs, descriptors, retired markers, tool groupings, level tags)?
4. Is the Galaxy Curve Plotter card's Reparametrization tag left over from an earlier version of the tool?
5. Is "Predictions & Experiments" meant to be a hub page? And "Methodology"?
6. Do glossary hover definitions work on touch devices and with screen readers?
7. Why is Tier 1 top-nav while For Researchers ("What's Citable") is footer-only?

### Verdict
As documentation, this site is better than most: the glossary covers nearly every term I looked up, labels carry "not a badge; relabelled 2026-09-08" notes, and the badge contract on Honest Assessment is a rule most projects never write down. The failures are all drift between surfaces describing the same thing: an index card describing an older tool, a glossary tier scheme contradicting the tier pages, a count whose breakdown doesn't sum where it's shown, and a label vocabulary that has outgrown its two-family contract. That is what page-by-page editing by many hands produces. More caveats won't fix it; the fix is structural: generate counts, tool blurbs and the label legend from one source, and lint pages against it.

**Theory pages.** These are more candid than I expected. /equation-walkthrough says outright that "tanh and γ = 2/√Ncorr are motivated choices, not derived results (there is no derivation to walk through — only choices to examine)." It also points out that the log is cosmetic: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ}−1]/[(1+x)^{2γ}+1]. I checked that identity. It holds, since e^{2γ ln u} = u^{2γ}, and it means C = 2H(u) − 1 with H a Hill function of u = 1+x with exponent 2γ. /coherence-function calls C "a logarithmic companding function, not an order parameter" and admits "no protocol maps any laboratory or astronomical observable to the coherence value C, in any domain." As a condensed-matter person that is the right call: there's no self-consistency equation, no symmetry breaking, and C is analytic for all x > −1, so there's nothing critical about it. /parameter-derivations is the best page on the site. γ = 2/√Ncorr is "an ansatz with a fitted prefactor" whose N-dependence is "sign-inverted relative to the mean-field physics it borrows." That's right: in Curie–Weiss the effective sharpness βJz grows with coordination. ρcrit = A·V² is "Audited-Negative," with "the stated formula gives A ≈ 4.6×10⁻⁵ — not 0.029." ### Journey
I came because a colleague said an AI system had "generated a physics framework and then refuted it." I went straight for the novelty claims. The landing scoreboard (0 prospective confirmed, 6 refutations executed "(5 on external data + 1 theorem-level check)," 0 of 6 "Validated" surviving) is the right shape. One sentence stopped me: *"The physics ledger reads zero confirmed novel predictions, but the honest reason is **lack of instruments, not refutation**."* That is false on the site's own ledger. TEST-09 tested what Honest Assessment calls the framework's only structural difference from MOND (the bounded boost) and lost at 3.3σ, and the same page says "Every decisive test it ran on real data, it lost." The headline is softer than the ledger underneath it.

**What's citable (/for-researchers).** Four items: a local-density no-go, the A2ACW detector audit, a dim-4 LIV naturalness gap, and density-keyed unidentifiability. I checked each one.

- **LIV** is Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia–Vucetich (PRL 93, 191301, 2004), correctly cited and applied: one-loop c_μν ~ α/π against 10⁻¹⁸ (cavity) to 10⁻²⁹–10⁻³⁰ (comagnetometer) bounds gives 16–28 orders. That is a known obstruction for any preferred-frame substrate, inherited rather than contributed.
- **Unidentifiability**: the Fisher correlation ρ(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000 at median x ≈ 7×10⁻⁵ is the standard statement that only γ/A is measurable when C ≈ γx. It's correct and worth a sentence, but it's textbook.
- **The no-go** is the substantive one, and its scope is where the problem is. The verbatim scope sentence restricts it to *"C(ρ)·g as a multiplicative scalar on the force — the class this framework belongs to; gradient-based schemes such as symmetron screening and non-local state variables such as enclosed mass are not covered."* The same page then says *"a field-equation completion exists (∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ)"*. It also says Refracted Gravity's permittivity *"is the same object as this framework's CΩ (closed-form identity, 2026-08-26)."*

Those three sentences don't fit together. Outside spherical symmetry, ∇·[C∇Φ] = C∇²Φ + ∇C·∇Φ. The second term is a gradient coupling, and it is exactly the mechanism RG uses: field-line refraction in flattened systems (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016). The same mechanism gives RG its predicted DM-content–flattening correlation in ellipticals (Cesare et al. 2022). So the framework's own completion, which is RG's equation, is outside the no-go's stated scope. /mond-unification confirms the reduction g = g_N/C holds "exactly in spherical symmetry" and says nothing about discs.

The page then quotes a head-to-head: *"at Refracted Gravity's f = 0.089 the ceiling problem is solved… and χ²/N rises to 195–2700 — 3 to 17× worse, because the boost then arrives in the inner disc."* A verbatim re-query found no statement of whether that χ²/N came from the algebraic reduction or a disc-geometry solve. It also found no mention of refraction and no comparison with Cesare et al. 2020's published disc fits. A reduction that drops ∇C·∇Φ is not RG. If that's how the number was computed, the site is refuting a straw version of the one theory it names as the identifiable member of its class.

To be fair to the refutations: for the framework's *own* parameters, the knee sits far above disc densities, so ∇C ≈ 0 inside discs and the refraction term is negligible. TEST-09/10 stand for Synchronism as parameterized. What is over-scoped is the class claim, and that's the part a referee would cite.

**Prior art on the equation pages.** /mond-unification writes ∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ and credits Verlinde for a similar relation. A verbatim re-query found no mention of Refracted Gravity there or on /dark-matter. As I recall the published form, RG's permittivity is ε(ρ) = ε₀ + ½(1−ε₀)[1 + tanh(Q·ln(ρ/ρc))]: a floored tanh of log density with a steepness parameter and a critical density. The site's floored C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) with C ≥ Ωm is the same construction, and RG predates it (2016). /for-researchers knows this ("same object"), but the pages where the equation actually appears don't say so.

**Which branch was refuted.** TEST-09's n = 3.35 is *"computed from the framework's own formula (C(a) = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)x/(1+x), boost capped at 1/Ω_m = 3.17)"*, which is **acceleration-keyed**. /mond-unification says *"C is a function of local matter density ρ alone."* Both branches carry the ceiling, so the refutation transfers, but that's an inference the reader has to make. The deep limit is stated honestly ("a constant rescaling of G… the framework's deep limit is n → 2"). Any bounded boost cannot give asymptotically flat curves, so this refutation is robust and close to trivial. /parameter-derivations is excellent on ρcrit. It flags that ρcrit = A·V_flat² scales as V⁺² while matching MOND requires ρcrit ∝ a₀²/(GV²) ∝ V⁻², *"an inverted exponent"* that no recalibration of A can repair. It is also candid that V_flat is an *input*. I'd add one thing: a permittivity that depends on the host system's V_flat is not a local field theory. The field equation cannot know V_flat, and the page never says what V is for a wide binary or a globular cluster.

**What actually differs from MOND: EFE.** /mond-unification: *"EFE = 0 exactly"* under g = g_bar/C(ρ), because *"a uniform external gravitational field does not change ρ."* That is the framework's cleanest qualitative departure from MOND. It is also a property of any linear-in-Φ permittivity law, so it should hold for RG too, which is worth checking against the RG literature. It would survive as a *class* test even though the framework itself is dead in galaxies. /test-catalog's verdict *"0 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM"* is accurate for the registry, but the one discriminating axis isn't in the registry. A verbatim re-query of /test-catalog and /honest-assessment found no mention of Chae et al. 2020 (ApJ 904, 51, a claimed EFE detection in SPARC outer rotation curves), McGaugh & Milgrom 2013 (Andromeda dwarfs), Crater II, or NGC 1052-DF2/DF4. Those are $0 existing datasets that bear directly on EFE = 0. The only EFE evidence on the site is indirect, in the globular-cluster table (MOND with EFE off is excluded at 4.3×).

**Wide binaries (TEST-02).** The site is well informed here. It cites Chae, Banik, Pittordis & Sutherland, Hernandez and Saurabh, and includes the MW external field (~1.8 a₀; "AQUAL/QUMOND + EFE give a 1.0–1.4× boost in g"). It computes the density-keyed null as 0.05–0.4%, "~80× insufficient" for Gaia DR3, and labels the test "SELF-ELIMINATING-OR-TIE." That's correct against the MOND+ΛCDM union. **DESI / dark energy.** Honest Assessment gives the sector as ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C, fit to DR2 BAO + Planck priors + Dovekie SN: γ = 0.487, Δχ² = −0.3 against ΛCDM. TEST-26 registers *"cannot produce the w = −1 crossing DESI DR2 prefers, at model-class level"* as kill-or-tie. **I checked this myself.** With u = 1 + ρ_m/ρcrit and p = 2γ, the ratio (1−C)/C = 2/(u^p − 1). That gives 1 + w(z) = F(u)/(u^p − 1), with F(u) = u^p − 1 − p(u−1)u^{p−1}. F(1) = 0 and F′(u) = −p(p−1)(u−1)u^{p−2}, so **sign(1+w) = sign(1−2γ) at every redshift**. At γ = ½ the ratio is exactly 2/x, giving ρ_DE = 2ρcrit = const, which is ΛCDM. I'm assuming an unfloored past and a constant ρcrit. At γ = 0.487 I get w₀ ≈ −0.993, rising toward −0.974 at high z. The one-parameter family therefore runs along the (1+w₀) ∝ +w_a diagonal, perpendicular to the long axis of DESI's w₀–w_a degeneracy. That explains Δχ² ≈ 0 and makes TEST-26's no-go exact for this sector, not just a class argument. It still can't discriminate from ΛCDM, which cannot cross either. γ ≈ ½ recurring in two sectors isn't a physics coincidence: ½ is where tanh(γ ln u) collapses to the Möbius (u−1)/(u+1), the simplest rational interpolant.

**An observation I haven't seen on the site. It's my extrapolation, not a site claim.** In a flat universe, ρ_DE/ρ_m = Ω_Λ/Ω_m forces C(ρ̄_m,0) = Ω_m *identically*. So the galaxy floor C ≥ Ω_m equals the dark-energy sector's C at today's mean cosmic density. If the floor is really C at the ambient cosmic density, not a fixed number, the ceiling evolves. Using the DE-sector ρcrit, B_max(z=2) = 1/tanh(0.487·ln 26.7) ≈ 1.09 and B_max(z=4.2) ≈ 1.02: near-Newtonian discs at high z. KMOS3D's baryon-dominated declining curves (Genzel et al. 2017) point that way qualitatively, but are contested. ALMA [CII] discs at z ≈ 4 (Rizzo et al. 2020; Lelli et al. 2021) would be the sharp test. This is a structural prediction that MOND (constant a₀) doesn't share. It's weakly separable from ΛCDM inside R_e, and it only exists if the floor's meaning is pinned down.

**A2ACW.** The protocol is well specified: PRIMARY, CHALLENGER (≥1 challenge per 10 exchanges), OBSERVER and COORDINATOR.
