## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /two-reframes, /honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest science site I've seen, and the beginner boxes really work. But each of them sits on top of a huge amount of material I can't read, and I kept leaving the boxes by accident.

### Journey

**Landing page.** I came from a Reddit link. "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" got me in the door, the way a good PBS Space Time thumbnail does. A few lines down it says "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." That was a real surprise. My first thought was "then why am I here?", and the "So is it alive or dead?" section answered it ("dead so far" as physics, "alive" as a public record of testing). I liked that. The disclosure that it's an AI-run notebook with one human lead and no outside physicist was also clear, and I appreciated it.

Then the page kept going. There's a "For physicists" fold with three different C formulas, one involving φ = 1.618, plus a "presence" paragraph, a methodology note with "1.4% internal-consistency survival rate," a table saying "Is MOND" and "Is Λ," and a scoreboard: "Refutations executed: 6," then "4 to 8 depending on the counting rule." The page says "New here? This is a good place to stop scrolling," and I should have. I didn't, because the scoreboard looked important. I clicked the big "Start here: Why Synchronism?" button.

**Why Synchronism (Step 1 of 5).** "What happened, in three lines" is the best thing I read all session. One equation for how crowding makes things act as one, tested mostly on galaxies, 0 confirmed and 6 refutations, the bigger question still open. Then "Beginner path: that is all you need from this page." That was a huge relief. The first line, "Physics has a fragmentation problem," is stated as if I'd know what it means. I don't. Is it that quantum and gravity don't fit together? I skipped the folds and continued.

**First Encounter (Step 2).** This page is good. Marching band vs. a crowd in a plaza. tanh as "a dimmer switch — flat at first, then rising." "ln squashes huge ranges": 10×, 100× and 1000× become 2.3, 4.6 and 6.9. For the first time I understood what the equation *does*. Then Section C opens with "In short: the formula for γ failed." So I'm learning a dial whose formula is wrong. That's fine, but then the page describes γ two ways: "how early the dial starts to turn" and "meant to set how sharply the dial turns." I couldn't tell which one to hold in my head.

The superconductor part also threw me. Superconductors are THE famous weird quantum thing from every YouTube video. This site says they score *low* on its coherence, and that this coherence is "not quantum phase coherence." I believe them, but it means the word "coherence" means something different from what I came in knowing. Section D says γ ≈ 1 is "roughly the quantum-classical boundary," and Section C says the galaxy fit was γ ≈ 0.5. I don't know how those numbers relate.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (Step 3, via "Next").** "New here? Look at three things on the chart below." The dots are what telescopes see, the gray dashed line is what visible stuff predicts, and the gap between them is the dark-matter puzzle. I GOT IT. That's the clearest explanation of the rotation-curve problem I've seen, and it's better than some videos. Green (MOND) follows the dots and violet (this framework's equation) doesn't. The conclusion, that the only version that fits is MOND written differently, landed. The "twin pair" suggestion (NGC 2403 vs UGC 128, same speed, one dimmer) is a nice "try this." Below that, the page turns into ΔBIC, Cassini, Ωm floor, "quadrature wiring," "units hybrid," and lots of dated revision notes. There's also an amber hand-drawn line marked "not a prediction of any theory." It's hidden by default, which is good, but if I ticked it I wouldn't know why a made-up curve is on a science chart.

**Coherence Explorer.** First Encounter called this the "best hands-on intro on the site." "One thing to try first" is good: drag γ from 2 down to 0.5 and watch the S flatten. Then I read that the density axis "has no units," the tool "does not model any real material," and "No setting here is one the framework predicts." I understand why they're honest about that, but it left me asking what I'm learning from moving it. The presets are all bare γ numbers (0.046, 0.49, ½, 1, 2) with captions like "ΔBIC +2843." I was hoping for things I recognise, like water or a galaxy.

**γ Calculator.** The title is "a γ formula kept to show how it fails," and the opening line is "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails." I laughed. This is the one tool that *does* have recognisable presets (Ideal gas, Liquid water, BEC, BCS superconductor), and the default output showed numbers: N = 4, γ = 1.0000, C(ρcrit) = 0.60. But the result is backwards from my intuition. Ideal gas, where nothing is together, gets labelled "Weakly Correlated" with the *earliest* rise. BEC, where everything is together, gets "Collective" with the *flattest* curve. The page says that IS the failure (it's "sign inverted"). It took me a while to realise the tool is showing me the mistake on purpose, not teaching me physics. A one-line "notice: the most 'together' things get the flattest curve, and that's the bug" right at the top would have saved me.

**Two Reframes (side trip from First Encounter, "Prefer analogies?").** I clicked because I like analogies. The page opens by comparing the framework to heliocentrism vs epicycles. That's a big claim, and the same page says the CRT analogy "Failed" on its literal reading and is a "Reparametrization" on the other one. It then gets heavy fast: Tsirelson bound, PR-box, Kochen–Specker, PBR, Lüders rule. Nothing says I can skip those. The breadcrumb says "Quantum Physics," but half the page is about relativity (the pendulum clock). The pendulum-in-a-centrifuge idea was the one bit I could follow, and the page's own caveat says it "reframes existing predictions, it does not generate new ones." So I was offered analogies, given analogies, and told they don't work. I respect that. It wasn't the gentler side trip the link promised.

**Honest Assessment ("See What Failed").** The "Not a physicist?" box and "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" are excellent, and "On the Beginner Path? You're done." made me smile. "As a theory of galaxies: fatal, by its own scoreboard" is a sentence I will remember. But the non-physicist take-away bullets say "about 3.3σ off" and "kill line crossed by about 1σ." σ is never explained there, and I don't know what it means. The page is "about 30,000 words." I stopped at the box.

**All pages.** Almost every page has dated editor's notes in the main text, e.g. "(Added 2026-11-10 after a first-time reader asked…)" or "(This card said '14,760' until 2026-09-06…)." I like that the site corrects itself in public. Reading them is like seeing the tracked changes in a Word doc: I keep stopping to wonder whether the note matters to me. Every footer also repeats "0 confirmed predictions · 6 refutations executed … (4–8 under rules on file)." A count with a range still feels odd to me.

### Tools Tested
*(Note: I read these through a text fetch, so I could read controls, defaults, presets and default outputs, but couldn't actually drag sliders.)*
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: content present (5 galaxy buttons, Υdisk "How heavy are the stars?" slider default 0.50, legend explained). **Intuitive**: the "New here? Look at three things" intro is the single best beginner explanation on the site. Below the twin-pair box it becomes confusing.
- **Coherence Explorer**: content present (γ default 1.00, ρcrit default 1.00, Key Values C(ρcrit) = 0.6000, C(10ρcrit) = 0.9836, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9998, which are consistent with tanh(ln 2) = 0.6). **Somewhat confusing**: "one thing to try first" is good, but the unitless axis and numeric-only presets left me unsure what I was supposed to learn.
- **γ Calculator**: content present (default N = 4 → γ = 1.0000; presets from Ideal gas γ = 2 to BCS γ = 6.32e-4; all seven presets match γ = 2/√N). **Confusing until it clicked**: it's a "watch the mistake" tool, and that only becomes clear after the caveats.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation failed and the working version "is MOND," what is the thing that's still "open"? I read "the broader question of the frame is still open" several times and couldn't say in my own words what that question is.
2. What is σ, and why is being "1σ" past the kill line a failure but also "narrow"?
3. Why does a superconductor, the most quantum-y thing I know of, get a low score on a "coherence" scale? I get that it's a different coherence, but what does this coherence actually measure that's useful?
4. Which γ is "the galaxy one": 0.5, 0.49, or 0.046? All three appear across pages.
5. What will happen on 2 December 2026 (Gaia DR4), and should I come back for it?

### Verdict
I came for "one equation for everything" and stayed because the site told me straight away that it failed. That's rare and it made me trust it. The beginner path (Why Synchronism → First Encounter → Galaxy Plotter → the Honest Assessment box) works: I now understand the rotation-curve puzzle and why "it turned into MOND" counts as a loss. Every page, though, is a short clear beginner layer on top of a huge expert layer with no clear floor between them, and I kept falling through. I'd come back to see what happens with the December data, if someone told me in one line what result would matter.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /glossary, /learning-paths, /honest-assessment, /research-philosophy, /interactive-tools, /test-catalog, /equation-walkthrough, /crossover-regime-visualizer)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I'm not judging the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes, as an example of how to document a negative result. Not as a reader who wants a quick answer.
- **Overall impression**: The site has better information architecture than most docs I audit: a counted, anchored glossary, one canonical badge table, learning paths with honest timings, and a tool index that explains its own labels. But the *status vocabulary* has grown past what any legend covers, and the same test or object gets different labels on different pages.

### Journey

I audited with the browser's text view, and cross-checked anything I wanted to cite against the page HTML. Several things that first looked broken turned out to be artefacts of text extraction, not defects: the breadcrumb "/" separators rendered as list items, the anchor ids were dropped, and superscripts were flattened (1−C ≈ 2x^−2γ). I've left those out. Every anchor I tested resolves: #sector-status, #alive-or-dead, #why-keep-reading, #terms-on-this-page, #start-with-ten, #outcome-words, #validation-badge-definitions, #count-rule, #refutation-ledger, #gc-fork, #test04a, #research-outputs and #if-you-read-nothing-else.

**Landing.** The nav is clean and every item has a tooltip that tells you what's behind it ("131 terms defined", "26 test IDs by tier (24 registry + 2 added later)", "badge families (definitions on Honest Assessment)"). I checked both numbers against their target pages and both hold. MOND, MRH and A2ACW are expanded at first use. DR4 is expanded in the same paragraph, just after its first use. Above the formula there's an "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC): short key" line, which is a good pattern. **But the key it links to sits in the wrong place.** The "short key" anchor (#terms-on-this-page) is a block headed "Abbreviations used in the table below". In the page text it starts roughly 4,000 characters *after* the "Where each piece stands" table, and that table already uses ΛCDM, ΔBIC, Υ, M/L, σ, RAR and BTFR. So a reader who meets ΛCDM in the table finds the key later on the page, and the key says "below". Υ, M/L, LIGO, RC100 and H(z) appear on the landing page with no expansion I could find.

The landing table uses its own status words: "Excluded", "Is MOND", "Is Λ", "Excluded at its own cap", "No number to test", "Inside Bell's limit", "Survives; does not discriminate". The link says it explains "what Excluded, Is MOND, Is Λ and the other status words mean" and points to /glossary#outcome-words. That section covers Excluded and IS MOND / IS Λ. It does **not** cover "Excluded at its own cap", "No number to test", "Inside Bell's limit" or "Survives". The link promises more than the target delivers. The same object also gets two different labels on one page. The acceleration-keyed law is "Is MOND" in the table and "Reparametrization" in the Key Claims teaser. The density law is "Excluded" in the table and "Failed — density-keyed" on the Galaxies card. Each label probably fits its own context, but the page never says that they are two vocabularies (scoreboard outcome vs. status badge) describing one thing.

**Glossary.** This is the best page I saw for my purposes. It has 131 entries, which matches the tooltip (I counted the headings). It is alphabetical with Greek after Latin, has an A–Z jump bar (J/X/Z are greyed out, which is correct), and has a "New here? Start with these ten" list that includes σ, so it answers Pass 1's σ question. It also defines all nine badges, and each badge entry links back to the canonical table. Problems:
- **Two entries both headed "Outcome words"**, with overlapping content ("Failed vs refuted vs kill vs excluded…" and "failed / refuted / killed / withdrawn…"). "Kill criterion", "Kill Criterion Triggered" and "Falsification Threshold" also overlap.
- **ρ gets two definitions in one entry** ("In every density-keyed test: physical density" / "In the ontology: compatible structural elements within an MRH"). The page is honest that the second has never been operationalised. But the **C(ρ) entry contradicts itself**: the gloss says it "Maps presence to coherence" and the body says it "Takes a density (ρ)".
- Many definitions rely on terms the glossary doesn't define: Tsirelson, PR-box, chameleon/Vainshtein, Källén–Lehmann, N_eff, "galaxy-block bootstrap", and the Web4 equation's MCP/RDF/T3/V3/ATP. LIGO is used on the landing page and has no entry.

**Learning Paths.** This is well built. There are 7 paths, every step is linked, and the Beginner "~37 min" equals the sum of the step times (8+10+6+9+4). The step-5 link goes straight to #if-you-read-nothing-else, which is a nice touch. Equation Anatomy says "Step 1 of 8 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)", which is exactly the disambiguation a reader needs. My only gaps: Intermediate and Advanced give path totals but no per-step times, and some step titles carry their retraction in the name ("The γ ≈ 1 Band (boundary reading retracted)"). That is honest, but it reads oddly as a syllabus heading.

**Honest Assessment.** The page has a full on-page TOC with 22 entries and a canonical "nine badges in one table" split into MRH-relationship (5) and descriptive (4) families. It also has a separate "Operational states (not badges)" list, a "Status lifecycle verbs" list, and a Deprecated list. It is ~190,000 characters long, and I estimate about 150 dated notes ("Added 2026-…", "until 2026-…"). Concrete problems:
- **TEST-04a is labelled differently on two pages.** The Honest Assessment card reads "Failed — Disfavored 2.4σ on σ₈ …; ~0.9σ on the registered fσ₈(z=0.51); underpowered as registered; post-hoc, not counted as a refutation". The Test Roadmap reads "Underpowered as registered (not Failed, not counted as a refutation): TEST-04a." One page says Failed and the other says not Failed. The glossary gives the same test two more numbers: "met at ~1.5σ against a demand of more than 3σ" (Underpowered entry) and "~0.9σ past the threshold against a 3σ bar" (Kill Criterion Triggered entry). That makes three σ figures and two labels for one test, with no sentence that ties them together.
- **The ledger's single badge doesn't match its own row text.** The ledger states "Every row carries the Failed badge". Row 3 (RAR at γ = 2) then says "Read this row as disfavoured at about 2σ", and the glossary's ΔBIC entry calls it "not categorically rejected". As a non-physicist, I can't tell whether "Failed" means "contradicted" (the badge definition) or "disfavoured at 2σ" (the row).
- The badge section heading notes the label was "called 'validation badges' until 2026-11-16". The rename is good, but the anchor is still #validation-badge-definitions and the landing-page link text still points readers to "validation-badge-definitions". This is minor.

**Research Philosophy.** Its intro says the taxonomy has "two families, plus three retired labels (Validated, Strongly Supported, MOND-shared)". The Deprecated paragraph on the same page lists only "Validated" and "Strongly Supported". The Test Roadmap lists all three as retired. The page also uses "26" for two different denominators. "of 26 listed criteria" is the kill-field census, which the Test Roadmap says was counted over TEST-01–24 + 04a + 25. "26 test IDs" is TEST-01–26. These are the same number for different sets. The archive size is "3,308" sessions in some places and "~3,300" in others. The breadcrumb's middle level, "Methodology", is plain text with no link. Tool pages link their middle level ("Interactive Tools"). Glossary, Learning Paths and Honest Assessment have no middle level. Test Roadmap has "Predictions & Experiments". Pass 1 saw "Quantum Physics" on a page that is half relativity. The breadcrumb taxonomy isn't one taxonomy.

**Interactive Tools index.** This is clear. There are 7 cards, each with Kind, Level, State and "Badge on its page", plus a one-line "Where to start" route and definitions of the labels it uses ("Model Explainer … not a status badge"; "Artifact Lesson … a tool kept deliberately to demonstrate a documented failure"). It is honest to the point of comedy. The page calls one of its seven tools "A static chart with hover notes, not an interactive tool: no controls," and six of the seven are Artifact Lessons. So the "Interactive Tools" section is mostly a museum of failure demos, labelled "Model Explainer". I'd retitle the Kind or the section.

**Test Roadmap.** The URL is /test-catalog and the page calls itself "Test Roadmap" throughout. That's harmless, but other slugs drift the same way: /mond-unification renders as "Relation to MOND", /equation-walkthrough as "Equation Anatomy", and /dark-energy is linked as "the cosmology page". The 26-test count holds (TEST-01–26, tiers 10+4+7+3 = 24 plus 25/26 added later), and the tier definitions are crisp. "registered" vs "pre-registered" is defined precisely, which I appreciated. The page has its own legend, but it lists **~35 distinct labels**: 9 badges, 7 operational states, 11 lifecycle verbs and 3 retired labels. On top of those come the landing-page scoreboard words and the glossary's outcome words (kill, fork, refuted, excluded). This is the single biggest structural problem I found.

**Equation Anatomy (tool).** It has a "Variables in this equation — defined before we start" legend, which is the right idea. But the ρcrit entry uses γ before γ's own entry, and "+1" is never named as a term. In the text view I could only see Step 1 of 8, because the steps are client-side. It describes γ as "Sets the width of the switch". C is "a number from 0 … to 1" in one legend entry and "stays in [0, 1)" in the tanh entry.

**Crossover Regime Visualizer (tool).** It has one γ slider (0–4, default 1.0) and three labelled bands. It opens well: "This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose … your task is to catch the formula being wrong at every stop". Its band arithmetic is internally consistent (γ = 1.4 → Ncorr ≈ 2, γ = 0.6 → ≈ 11). The rename from "Phase Boundary Visualizer" was carried through the title, H1 and breadcrumb, and "boundary" is explicitly redefined. Two terminology slips remain. First, the intro says γ "sets how early the curve rises; it was first proposed as the sharpness of the S-curve, which it is not". Later the page says "this tool positions systems by transition sharpness." Second, the axis label says "Collective" while the band label for the same region says "High-N_corr (strongly correlated)".

**γ across the site.** The site is meant to be about one parameter, and I found three descriptions of it: "how early the switch starts to turn" (landing, crossover tool), "how wide the switch is and how early C leaves zero" (landing fold, glossary: "Sets how wide the S-curve's switch is and how early it rises"), and "the width of the switch, with a floor" (Equation Anatomy). Pass 1 hit this from the reader's side. From the writer's side, the fix is one canonical sentence in the glossary, reused verbatim on every page.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Under the 1–10 pc smoothing, what does the density-keyed law predict for K_z(z) over |z| ≲ 1 kpc once lab G is set equal to the local G_eff? This is the statistic that can actually exclude it at the Sun. My guess is that it still dies, through the vertical gradient, but nobody has computed it.

### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics, and the site says so first. Every surviving branch is MOND's simple μ, Λ, or prior art (Cardassian, cosmologically coupled MOND). The literature use is better than in most submissions I referee: DESI DR2, Mayer 2023, Toner–Bacon, Desmond–Hees–Famaey, Hofmann–Müller and Wolf 2010 are all cited correctly where I checked. What's worth my time is the by-products: a clean Ġ/G no-go for ambient-density-keyed G, a mechanism for why compander-type μ functions fail Cassini, and a sharpened wide-binary crux. Two of those currently overstate their scope in exactly the place an outsider would cite them (the "Refracted Gravity excluded" label and the Oort-limit amplitude argument). Fix those scopes, cite the 2015 Bell experiments against the local substrate, and this becomes a citable negative-results resource. The framework itself isn't one.
