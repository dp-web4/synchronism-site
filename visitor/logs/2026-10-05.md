### Journey
Came in from Reddit. The landing page hooks with "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" and then, almost straight away: "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated. What follows is what we learned." I've never seen a site do that. My first reaction was respect; my second was "so why am I reading this for 45 minutes?" The scoreboard (0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations, 0 of 9 audited claims surviving, 3,308 "AI-to-AI" research sessions) makes it clear this is a research notebook and not a pitch. But nothing on the page tells me what *I* get out of the Beginner Path. "Watch how an idea gets tested and broken" would be a good reason, but I had to work that out myself.

Clicked **Start Here** (/why-synchronism). The plaza crowd vs. marching band analogy is great. "How densely packed the parts are is what moves a system from crowd-like to band-like" is one sentence I can actually repeat to a friend. Then the same page tells me the self-audit found "zero confirmed predictions and the decisive galaxy test collapses to MOND". I don't know what MOND is yet, so "collapses to" means nothing to me.

**First Encounter** is billed as 10 minutes. It's well paced, with the progress bar and Continue buttons, but by section A it has already introduced ρ ("presence") and "Markov Relevancy Horizon", and by section C it's telling me the γ formula failed before I really understand what γ does. I keep getting told what's broken before I know what it is. The "whisper in a loud room" line for quantum correlations was nice. "No observer needed. No consciousness required. Just a boundary crossing" sounds cool, but I can't tell whether that's a claim, a finding or a hope.

Opened **Honest Assessment** and bounced. It's huge (the fetch estimated ~18,000 words, so I'm not sure of the exact size) and full of ΔBIC, BTFR, RAR, "disfavored 3.3σ" and "Bmax". To be fair, the preamble does say casual readers can stop after the opening summary, and I did. The opening list of six failures was readable-ish: the galaxy one, a 69% dark-matter-fraction miss, an environment effect that came out as nothing, and something about the Cassini spacecraft.

**Key Claims** was where I most wanted to dig in, because the consciousness claim jumps right out at you, and where I understood least. "Quantum 'mysteries'... are synchronization phenomena in a phase field" means nothing to me. The badges (Speculative / Speculative / Failed) are clear words, so that part worked.

Then the tools, which were the best part (details below). The **galaxy plotter** is the single most useful page on the site for someone like me. The **γ calculator** is fun to click but left me with some odd questions. I peeked at the **Glossary**. The first-sentence definitions are good ("tanh: a mathematical function that smoothly maps any input to a value between −1 and +1"), but they quickly run into terms like "companders" and "N_corr".

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — Dragging γ visibly changes how sharp the S-curve is, and ρ_crit slides it sideways; that I got. The default readouts (C(ρ_crit) = 0.33, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.83, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.98) show "it switches on over about two factors of ten", which is nice. The 0.49 vs 0.5 labels confused me (see friction log). The log/linear toggle means nothing to me.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — intuitive — Best thing on the site. Gray Newton line vs. blue dots made the dark-matter gap obvious in seconds. Violet = Synchronism (failed) hugging the gray line, green = MOND fitting the dots with one constant. Very honest. The amber "hand-tuned stand-in, drawn to fit" curve is a nice touch of candour. Only five galaxies, no other controls.
- **γ Calculator** — worked — confusing in places — Clicking presets is fun. The message "more correlated systems get *flatter* curves, which is backwards (superconductors have super-sharp transitions)" is understandable even to me. But Galaxy = 17 particles made me laugh.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — worked (hover) — confusing — Eight dots clustering at C ≈ 0.50, then a long explanation of why that's an artifact. Honest, but I'm not sure what I was supposed to learn beyond "this doesn't prove anything".

### Unanswered Questions
1. If dark matter is supposed to show up where C is low, and the galaxy plotter says C is only 0.001 on the disk, why does the equation "never switch on" and fail to explain the rotation?
2. What *is* MOND, in one sentence, and why is "collapses to MOND" bad news instead of good news (it fits the dots, right)?
3. Did the AI sessions do the physics? Has any human physicist looked at this?
4. After all the failures, what's still alive? Is there anything I should keep watching for?
5. What does "density" mean for a galaxy vs. a quantum particle vs. a brain? Is it the same kind of density?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've ever seen, and the galaxy plotter taught me more about dark matter in two minutes than several YouTube videos have. But the site keeps telling me what broke before I understand what it was, and pages like Key Claims and Honest Assessment lose me fast. I'd come back for the tools and the "how science self-corrects" story, not for the physics itself.
