**Short answer:** The construction is not new, and its "unobservable" conclusion is contradicted by published results. I answered from memory and did not search, so treat citations marked uncertain with caution.

**1. The substrate is a known idea.**
- Zuse, *Rechnender Raum* (1969), and Fredkin's digital physics describe a discrete cellular-automaton universe.
- 't Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation of quantum mechanics (book, 2016) is a close modern version.
- Wolfram's *A New Kind of Science* (2002) and the Wolfram Physics Project (2020) are others, though they are not cubic-grid models.
- Quantum walks and quantum cellular automata on lattices give the Dirac equation in the continuum limit:
  - Bialynicki-Birula, Phys. Rev. D 49, 6920 (1994).
  - Meyer, J. Stat. Phys. 85, 551 (1996) (citation uncertain).
  - Strauch, Phys. Rev. A 74, 030301 (2006).
  - D'Ariano and Perinotti, Phys. Rev. A 90, 062106 (2014).
  - Bisio, D'Ariano and Tosini, Ann. Phys. 354, 244 (2015) (citation uncertain).
- In these models Lorentz symmetry holds only approximately at low momentum, with deformations of order (ka)². D'Ariano and Perinotti also analyse the deformed dispersion and Lorentz transformations.

**2. The claim that violations are suppressed by powers of E/E_Planck is a known problem.**
- Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia and Vucetich, Phys. Rev. Lett. 93, 191301 (2004), showed that a Planck-scale cutoff with a preferred frame induces unsuppressed dimension-4 Lorentz-violating operators through radiative corrections. These operators are of order 1 times the coupling factors, not (E/E_P)^n. This is an extra fine-tuning problem.
- Without a symmetry or fine-tuning, a hard cubic grid with absolute time is therefore not expected to reproduce Lorentz invariance at low energy. Cubic symmetry removes direction-dependent terms only at the leading order. Species-dependent isotropic speed differences, of the Coleman-Glashow type (Phys. Rev. D 59, 116008, 1999), remain allowed.
- Myers and Pospelov, Phys. Rev. Lett. 91, 211601 (2003), treat the dimension-5 operators.

**3. The grid is already constrained observationally.**
- Fermi GRB 090510 rules out a linear-in-E/E_P photon dispersion (Abdo et al., Nature 462, 331, 2009).
- Gamma-ray polarization limits birefringence.
- The Crab Nebula's synchrotron emission and UHECR propagation constrain the dimension-4 and dimension-5 terms.
- Collins et al. argue that the dimension-4 coefficients are bounded to roughly 10⁻²² or better, far below what "Planck suppression" would predict.
- Reviews of these tests:
  - Liberati, Class. Quantum Grav. 30, 133001 (2013).
  - Kostelecký and Russell, "Data Tables for Lorentz and CPT Violation", Rev. Mod. Phys. 83, 11 (2011), updated on arXiv:0801.0287.

**4. Discreteness without a preferred frame.**
- Causal-set theory avoids the problem with a Poisson-random, Lorentz-invariant discreteness: Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin, Mod. Phys. Lett. A 24, 2579 (2009); Dowker, Henson and Sorkin, Mod. Phys. Lett. A 19, 1829 (2004) (volume and page uncertain).
- A regular grid with global ticks is the opposite case. It has a preferred frame and a preferred lattice.

**Assessment:**
- The discrete-grid model with absolute time is a special case of known lattice and cellular-automaton models.
- The statement that Lorentz-violating effects are Planck-suppressed, so the grid is unobservable, is not an established result. It is contradicted by Collins et al. (2004) and by existing bounds. The program needs one of two things:
  - a symmetry or mechanism that protects the dimension-4 operators, or
  - a Lorentz-invariant discreteness, as in causal sets.
- I know of no published work showing that a cubic grid with global ticks and interacting fields avoids this problem.
