## WAKE

**1. What assumptions am I inheriting from the primer and topic queue?**

The topic queue's P0 asks me to *find* the coarse-graining length ℓ per sector and compare.
It inherits three assumptions from this morning's maintainer session, all unstated:

- that R₀ in `A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²)` *is* a coarse-graining length;
- that the 635× gap between A = 0.029 and A = 4.6×10⁻⁵ needs a new explanation;
- that ρ stays fixed while ρ_crit moves with ℓ (this is what produced the headline
  "x = 0.91, knee crossed").

The third is the one that jumped out. A coarse-graining length that smooths one side of a
ratio and not the other is not a coarse-graining length. I checked that before running
anything else.

**2. What if the current research frame is wrong?**

It was, in two places. `/critical-density` defines β_J = λ_Jeans/**R_half** — "the
Jeans-length-to-**galaxy-size** ratio, empirically 1.1 ± 0.2 across SPARC galaxies" — and
then calls R₀ a smoothing length three paragraphs down, same page, same day. The archive's
Session 53 is unambiguous that R_half is a galaxy size. And the 644× already had an
explanation on file since 2026-06-07: Session 687 §1.2 decomposed it into (β_J = 4.5,
R₀ = 0.07). Since the formula depends only on the product β_J·R₀, forcing β_J = 1 relabels
that product as a length — 4.5 × 0.07 = 0.315 kpc vs the inverted 0.3174 kpc, **0.8%**.

**3. What experiment would most increase information right now?**

Do the coarse-graining self-consistently and see what survives. That turned out to be a
two-line derivation rather than an experiment, which is the strongest kind of answer:
x(ℓ) = (3/16π²) β_J² [V_c(ℓ)/V]² — ℓ cancels, and x is a virial ratio with a universal
ceiling ≈ 0.019 β_J². Verified numerically on all five plotter disks (max 1.1×10⁻²) and,
to four digits, at Cassini and wide-binary scales.

**4. What would falsify the current research posture?**

Listed in §6 of the finding. The live one: at β_J = 4.5 (Session 66's own value) the fully
self-consistent x reaches 0.385 and C(γ=2) = 0.57, escaping the ceiling — at the price of
being 17σ from the β_J = 1.1 ± 0.2 the same derivation cites as its justification. That is
the only escape I found and it is a real fork, not a rescue.

**Verdict on the queue**: the topic was the right target, and the highest-value move inside
it was to audit its premise rather than execute its plan. The plan (find ℓ per sector,

## What I found

1. **317 pc is not a physical scale.** It is Session 66's documented β_J·R₀ = 4.5 × 0.07,
   re-factorized with β_J forced to 1. 0.8% agreement. Matching it to the plotter's
   h = 300 pc is a 5% coincidence.
2. **Two further gaps surfaced en route**, neither remarked anywhere: the site's formula
   carries a **4π that Session 53's does not** (12.57 of the 635), and **β_J = 4.5
   contradicts the site's own β_J = 1.1 ± 0.2 by 17σ**.
3. **The identity.** x(ℓ) = 0.01900 β_J² [V_c(ℓ)/V_flat]². ℓ cancels. x ≤ ~0.02 for every
   bound system at every ℓ. Kernel-robust (Gaussian gives 0.00505, i.e. moves the ceiling
   *down* 3.8×). Estimator-independent, which is the property this ledger's galaxy kills
   have never had.
4. **Cassini is ℓ-independent.** x = 0.00091 unsmoothed, 0.01899 smoothed at r_Saturn — the
   identity to four digits. C ≈ 0 either way, because the ρ jump and the ρ_crit jump are the
   same jump. TEST-11 is strengthened, not reopened.
5. **Wide binaries: the ρ lever is flat for a deeper reason than "the local density is
   uniform."** x is a virial ratio; it is pinned at ~0.02 by construction for every bound
   system. No sample selection opens it.
6. **The maintainer's knee-flip is a law swap, not a parameter choice.** Under the archive's
   own per-galaxy R_half, A_eff spans 1.6×10⁻⁶–3.0×10⁻⁵ across five galaxies (factor 19),
   A ∝ R_half⁻² ∝ V⁻¹·⁵, so ρ_crit ∝ V^0.5 — exactly the two-law fork
   `/parameter-derivations` item 1 has documented since 06-07.
7. **Two numbers shipped today don't reproduce.** ρ(0) for NGC 3198 is **0.6164**, not
   0.934, from the plotter's own `midplaneDensity()` run verbatim; x(0) is 9.45×10⁻⁴, not
   1.43×10⁻³; and the headline **0.91 is 0.60**. One density error, factor 1.515, now on
   three pages.

## So what?

**It closes a P0 by dissolving it rather than answering it**, which is worth more than the
answer would have been: "the required ℓ differs by OOM across sectors" would have been a
sixth galaxy-sector no-go on a new axis, and it is not available, because there is no
ℓ-dependence at all. Buying that back cost one identity and no data.

The portable result is the ceiling: **x ≤ (3/16π²)β_J² [V_c/V]² ≈ 0.019 β_J²**, parameter-free,
estimator-robust, sector-independent. It says the coherence knee is unreachable by ~40× for
any bound system under any smoothing, and it is the first galaxy-sector statement on this
site that survives without an estimator choice, a velocity definition, or an external
measurement.

The uncomfortable part is the failure mode. This is the fifth same-day over-correction in a
month and the first where the over-correction *was itself a correction of an
over-refutation*. The topic's own guardrail — "do not treat 4.6×10⁻⁵ as the audited-correct
A; that would be the mirror-image over-claim" — was aimed one level too shallow. The
mirror-image over-claim wasn't a value of A. It was the existence of ℓ.

**Two rule candidates**, both in the finding:
- When a discrepancy is re-explained, check whether it already had an explanation on file.
  This one did, two months old, in the paragraph directly above where the new story landed.
- A quantity appearing only as a *product* cannot be inverted into one of its factors
  without an independent measurement of the other. The whole ℓ story rests on setting
  β_J = 1 against the same page's β_J = 1.1 ± 0.2.
