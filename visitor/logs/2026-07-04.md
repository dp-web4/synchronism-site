## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, start-here, learning-paths, key-claims, why-synchronism, tools, coherence-explorer, galaxy-plotter, honest-assessment, glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: minimal
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The big question hooks me and the crowd-vs-marching-band picture clicks — then every page immediately drops me into `tanh`, `γ`, and "the theory hasn't actually worked yet," and I bounce.

### Journey
I found this on Reddit and the landing headline grabbed me: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* That's exactly the kind of "one weird trick unifies physics" thing Veritasium would make a video about, so I clicked in. I liked that the header underneath said *"An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything"* — felt humble, not culty.

The **Start Here** page gave me the one good mental picture I actually kept: a **crowd** (people acting on their own) vs a **marching band** (people moving as one), and the idea that how tightly *packed* things are decides which one you get. Cool! I was with them. Then one line later came the actual equation, `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`, and a warning that *"'Coherence' here is not quantum coherence — superconductors and BECs score low."* I don't know what a BEC is, I've barely heard "superconductor," and I have no idea what tanh does. So the friendly analogy and the scary math are back-to-back with nothing in between to ease me across.

I clicked **Beginner Path** from Learning Paths — it goes to `/why-synchronism`. Good hook again ("Physics has a fragmentation problem") but within a screen I hit "phenomenological," "compander," "Hamiltonian," and a "Circularity caveat" that basically admits the theory was fit to the data after the fact. As a casual reader that last one is a mood-killer — it reads like "we made the curve match on purpose."

**Key Claims** was the most honest and the most deflating. Three claims: quantum stuff is really "synchronization" (untested), consciousness might have an equation (their *own* test rejected it, p<0.0001), and dark matter is "incomplete decoherence" (failed against ~14,760 galaxies). So of the three headline ideas, one's untested, one they disproved themselves, and one flopped. I respect the honesty but I keep waiting for the part where something *works*.

**Honest Assessment** confirmed it: "Zero predictions have been independently confirmed." The best-case win is that it matches galaxy rotation curves "no better than MOND," a 40-year-old theory. So the pitch quietly becomes "we might have re-derived something that already exists." That's a hard sell for a casual reader who showed up for a revolution.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If nothing's been confirmed and the best result just matches MOND, why should a casual reader care yet — what's the "someday this could be huge" payoff?
2. What does `tanh` actually *do*? Nobody ever tells me in words.
3. Is "coherence" here anything like the "quantum coherence" I've heard about, or a totally different thing? (The site says different, but never says what it *is* in one plain sentence.)
4. Consciousness having an equation was the most clickbait-exciting claim — why is it presented as basically already dead?

### Verdict
The site is admirably, almost painfully honest, and the crowd-vs-band analogy is genuinely good — but it's built for skeptics who already speak physics, not for the curious newcomer the "Beginner Path" promises. For someone like me it reads as "here's a brilliant idea that hasn't worked and might just be re-labeling MOND," which is intellectually respectable but emotionally a downer. Give me *one* clear, jargon-free win (or even a "here's the exciting bet we're making") before the equations and caveats, and I'd stick around.

### Journey
I walked every top-nav link and traced the three Learning Paths step-by-step, checking that labels, URLs, and destinations agree. First impression is that this site takes documentation seriously in ways most research sites don't: breadcrumbs are present and correct (`Home / Getting Started / …`), a "Beginner Path — Step N of 6" indicator rides along the beginner pages, prev/next links are wired, and `tanh` is consistently glossed as "an S-shaped saturation function" everywhere it appears. The validation-badge system — which a first-time reader might dismiss as decoration — turns out to have a **canonical reference** on `/honest-assessment` defining both families (MRH-relationship: active-mrh / parallel-paths / sidelined / superseded / audited-negative; Descriptive: untested / speculative / reparametrization / failed), and the glossary restates them. That's exactly what a documentation auditor hopes to find and rarely does.

The friction is in the **naming and wayfinding**, not the definitions:

1. **Two progress counters for one path.** On `/first-encounter` the breadcrumb says "Beginner Path — **Step 2 of 6**," but the page is *also* the second page of an internal **seven-page** First Encounter sequence with its own unlabeled "Next →". So a reader watching their progress sees "2 of 6" and a second, denominator-less counter advancing underneath it. Which one is "how far am I"? Pick one model (nest the 7 sub-pages *inside* step 2, or flatten them into the 6), and label the inner counter.

2. **"Start Here" isn't a page titled Start Here.** The nav item **Start Here** resolves to content headed **"Why Synchronism?" (Step 1 of 6)** — and the landing page's primary CTA **"Beginner Path →"** *also* lands on `/why-synchronism`. So one destination carries three names (nav "Start Here," CTA "Beginner Path," H1 "Why Synchronism?"). Functionally fine; for a newcomer building a mental map it's three pins on the same spot.

3. **Duplicate URLs for one page.** `/test-roadmap` and `/test-catalog` render the *same* page ("Test Roadmap — 24 Proposed Experiments"). Top nav points at `/test-roadmap`; the Advanced path's step 8 (labeled "Test Roadmap") points at `/test-catalog`. Two canonical routes for identical content is a maintenance and bookmarking hazard — one should 301-redirect to the other.

4. **"Where are the actual tests?" costs three page-names.** The individual TEST-01…TEST-10 cards (with badges) don't live on "Test Roadmap" *or* on the thing called "Test Catalog" — the roadmap page is a birds-eye overview and explicitly forwards you to **"Tier 1: Existing Data"** for the cards. So finding a specific test's status means knowing that Roadmap ≠ Catalog ≠ Tier 1, and that the third one holds the data. Three names, one job.

I tested the **γ Calculator** and the **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (both different from Pass 1's picks). Both work smoothly and both are unusually candid — each carries a prominent caveat that its own core relationship (correlation → transition sharpness) is *inverted* relative to real physics. From a docs standpoint that honesty is right, but the *placement* fights the tool: the reader manipulates a live widget and only afterward reads that its outputs point the wrong way. A status banner **above** the interactive element ("Known issue: this curve's direction is inverted — see why") would resolve the tension Pass-1 also flagged on the galaxy plotter.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, why-synchronism, parameter-derivations, key-claims, galaxy-rotation, honest-assessment, galaxy-plotter, coherence-explorer, measurement-without-observers, gamma-boundary)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I can reconstruct the framework's claims and verify its own arithmetic)

- **Overall impression**: The forward math is arithmetically correct and, remarkably, the site has already reached the verdict I would have written — it's a compander fit, not a theory — but the honesty lives in below-the-fold caveats while the mean-field vocabulary and the flagship demo tools still front-run a relationship the site itself calls backwards.

### Journey
I went straight to `/parameter-derivations` expecting the usual crank move — a "derivation" that's a fit in a lab coat. Instead the page opens by conceding it: **"Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations."** γ = 2/√N_corr is labeled a "Motivated Ansatz"; the 1/√N is hand-waved from the CLT and the factor of 2 from a "6D → 3 effective" phase-space argument that is asserted, never shown. The page even flags the fatal internal contradiction itself: the CLT governs *independent* variables, but N_corr counts *correlated* degrees of freedom, and the resulting scaling is **"sign-inverted relative to the mean-field physics it borrows"** — larger N_corr gives *flatter* transitions, the opposite of what happens when you add correlated spins to an Ising-type system. That is exactly the objection I came to raise, printed on their own page. Disorienting.

I verified the compander arithmetic by hand and it holds:
- C(ρcrit) = tanh(2·ln2) = tanh(1.386) = **0.882** ✓ (site: 0.8824)
- C = 0.5 ⇒ 2·ln(ρ/ρcrit+1) = atanh(0.5) = 0.549 ⇒ ρ ≈ **0.316·ρcrit** ✓ (site: "0.32·ρcrit, well below ρcrit")
- γ(N=2) = 2/√2 = **1.414**, γ(N=10⁷) = **6.3×10⁻⁴** ✓

That 0.88 is the whole story for a condensed-matter reader: an order parameter sitting at a *critical* point takes a value fixed by critical exponents (½ in mean field), not 0.88. C(ρcrit)=0.88 is the fingerprint of a **saturation function with no singularity** — no diverging correlation length, no universality class, no phase transition. To the site's credit, `/coherence-explorer` says this outright ("ρcrit is a *saturation knee*, not a critical density in the phase-transition sense") and `/why-synchronism` correctly places tanh in the **compander family** (μ-law, Hill/Naka–Rushton, Langevin) and admits any smooth S-curve fits equally well with **"no variational principle [that] justifies tanh specifically."** So the framework has already conceded that its central equation is an interpolating convenience, not dynamics.

Then the load-bearing check: does C(ρ) actually produce flat rotation curves? I audited `/galaxy-plotter`'s real formulas. The "Synchronism" violet curve is **v(r) = √(v_baryon² + [V_flat·tanh(0.4·r/r_s)]²)** and the "MOND" green curve is the same with **√tanh(0.55·r/r_s)**. *Neither is C(ρ).* Both are ad-hoc sigmoids **in radius**, and the √tanh green curve is just the MOND simple-μ interpolation shape. The page discloses this and — more damningly — states that when the genuine C(ρ) is evaluated on measured density profiles with framework parameters, **the entire disk sits at C ≲ 0.28**: the coherence never climbs past its knee, so the real equation produces *no* flat curve at all. The flagship demonstration works only by swapping in a substitute function. That is the single most important physics result on the whole site, and it's a caveat under a plot rather than a headline.

`/galaxy-rotation` and `/honest-assessment` close the loop with the numbers I'd want: pinned γ=2 is refuted at **ΔBIC = +184**; free γ relaxes to **≈0.49** and reproduces MOND at **ΔBIC = +7** (pure parameter penalty). So on real data the compander is either refuted or a MOND reparametrization — **"no γ for which the compander is both distinct from MOND and consistent with SPARC."** a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is correctly badged a *reparametrization*: any physicist sees the classic a₀ ≈ cH₀/6 numerology (2π ≈ 6.28), and the site refuses to call it a derivation. `/measurement-without-observers` is honest that the Born-rule "derivation" is a reparametrization resting on a coherence-conservation *axiom*, distinguishable from standard decoherence by nothing measurable. `/gamma-boundary` concedes a 2-parameter polynomial in Z matches or beats its chemistry correlations (Δr ≤ 0.07). And `/honest-assessment` states the preferred-frame problem cleanly: absolute time ⇒ one-loop c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻², a **16–28 OOM** naturalness gap against Lorentz bounds, with the two standard perturbative escapes named but **unexhibited**.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — the physics is *correctly* represented — I used it to stress the compander claim: sweeping γ from 2 → 0.01 broadens the knee exactly as tanh(γ·ln(·)) should, and the printed table (C(ρcrit)=0.8824, C(10ρcrit)=0.9999, C(100ρcrit)=1.0000) matches my hand calculation to the digit. Crucially it does *not* pretend ρcrit is a critical point — it says C(0.5) occurs at 0.32·ρcrit and calls ρcrit a saturation knee. This is the tool that most cleanly demonstrates the framework's own honest conclusion: it's a companding curve, full stop.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — but the tool undercuts the theory it advertises — Cycling DDO 154 → NGC 2403 → NGC 3198 → UGC 128 → NGC 7331, the violet and green curves track each other and the data equally well, which the page admits proves nothing. The decisive catch is in the disclosed formula: the violet curve is tanh(0.4·r/r_s), a radius sigmoid, **not** C(ρ) — and the page states the real C(ρ) never crosses its knee on these galaxies. So the single most persuasive widget on the site runs a stand-in function because the actual equation fails here. Honest to disclose; damning as physics.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If γ = 2/√N_corr is sign-inverted vs mean-field, why is it still the engine of two live calculators and the phase-boundary axis? Either the inversion is real (retire the tools) or it isn't (retract the caveat) — the site currently asserts both.
2. Is there *any* galaxy for which C(ρ) evaluated on the measured density profile crosses ρcrit and produces a flat curve without the radius-sigmoid substitution? If not, the galaxy program is falsified by the framework's own function, independent of the MOND-degeneracy result.
3. The naturalness gap cites two perturbative escapes (SUSY-type, scale-hierarchy) as existing in the literature but "unexhibited." Has anyone checked whether either survives an *absolute-time* substrate specifically, or is that an open derivation?

### Verdict
This is the most intellectually honest fringe-adjacent framework I've read — it has adversarially audited itself into the same corner I would have backed it into. The core equation is arithmetically sound and correctly identified as a compander with no critical behavior; every parameter is openly labeled a calibration or reparametrization; the galaxy fit is shown to be either refuted (ΔBIC=+184) or MOND in disguise (ΔBIC=+7); and the flagship plotter honestly admits it substitutes a radius-sigmoid because the real C(ρ) never leaves its low-coherence floor on actual data. As physics it is a well-documented null result; as methodology it is exemplary. My only substantive complaint is one of *placement*: the mean-field vocabulary and the two calculators built on an acknowledged sign-inverted law still lead the presentation, while the corrections and the load-bearing "C(ρ) doesn't actually produce flat curves" finding are distributed into caveats below the fold. The labeling lags the verdict — and this site's verdict is unusually worth surfacing.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, for-researchers, cdm-discrimination, tier-1-existing-data, honest-assessment, a2acw, tools, chemistry-correlation-explorer, equation-walkthrough)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I can locate every novelty claim and check it against the current literature)
- **Would return?**: yes — to see whether the one page that still overclaims gets reconciled with the rest
- **Overall impression**: The framework's *self-audited* verdict — "0 tests discriminate from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" — is correct and well-argued, but one legacy page (`/cdm-discrimination`) still advertises a CDM-beating result the rest of the site has already retracted, and it does so on the wrong dataset, a contested measurement, and a discriminant that separates the wrong pair of theories.

### Journey
I came to answer one question a referee asks: *is there a single prediction here that is novel, falsifiable, and different from MOND+EFE and ΛCDM?* I went straight to `/for-researchers`, which — to its credit — answers "no" before I could: **"0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, and 0 tests currently discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM."** The three "citable artifacts" it offers are all negatives: a **local-density no-go** (a quantified re-derivation of Milgrom's non-locality obstruction — correct, and correctly credited as non-original), the **A2ACW program-level null**, and the **DESI growth-suppression disfavor at 2.4σ**. That is an honest ledger, and it matches my own read of the state of the art: a density-keyed C(ρ) modification is exactly the class of local theory that Milgrom's non-locality argument already excludes, and the surviving escapes (MOND itself, Verlinde, AeST) are all non-local. Nothing new there, and the site says so.

Then I hit the page that breaks the pattern. **`/cdm-discrimination`** claims the RAR intrinsic scatter **σ_int = 0.086 ± 0.003 dex** lands *below* the CDM simulation range (0.11–0.16 dex from EAGLE/TNG/FIRE), badges it **"Strongly Supported,"** and says environment dependence is **"confirmed at p = 5×10⁻⁶,"** distinguishing Synchronism from *both* MOND (zero scatter) and CDM. This is the only place on the site claiming a live CDM-discriminating win — and it is wrong on four independent counts, each checkable against the site's *own* other pages:

1. **It contradicts the site's own verdict.** `/honest-assessment` adjudicates the very same σ_int/environment result as **"Kill Criterion Triggered"** (R²=0.14 < 20% threshold) and states **"Novel-survivor count: 0."** `/tier-1` lists the σ_int claim as **TEST-06 "Untested"** and the environment claim as **TEST-05 "Failed."** So the same measurement is "Strongly Supported" on one page and "Failed/Kill-triggered" on two others. `/cdm-discrimination` is a fossil that never got the retraction memo, and it still wears the **deprecated "Strongly Supported" badge** the site's own conventions say to migrate off.

2. **Wrong dataset.** σ_int of the RAR is a **SPARC** measurement (~150 rotation-curve galaxies; Lelli/Li et al. 2018). The page attributes 0.086 dex to **"14,760 galaxies"** — that's the ALFALFA+SDSS *TFR* sample used for the environment test, a different relation on a different data product. Conflating the RAR-scatter number with the TFR sample size inflates the apparent weight of the result.

3. **Contested, not settled.** "The RAR is too tight for CDM" is a live *argument*, not a fact: several CDM hydro works (Keller & Wadsley 2017; Dutton et al.; Navarro et al. 2017 on EAGLE) reproduce a tight RAR, and the intrinsic-scatter estimate is sensitive to error modeling and radial range (Desmond 2017; Stone & Courteau). Presenting 0.086 < 0.11 as "Strongly Supported" discrimination is exactly the overclaim a referee sends back.

4. **Wrong pair discriminated.** Even granting the tight-RAR reading, a "too tight for particle-halo diversity" result is the classic argument for **MOND-like modified gravity over CDM** — it does *nothing* to separate Synchronism from MOND, which is the framework's actual burden. And the environment-dependence "discriminator" fails the same way: **MOND's External Field Effect predicts environment dependence too** — `/tier-1` says so explicitly (TEST-05: "MOND+EFE shared prediction"). So the page's headline discriminant separates Synchronism from neither of the two theories it needs to beat.
