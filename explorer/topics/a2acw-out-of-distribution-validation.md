# Topic: A2ACW Out-of-Distribution Validation

## Question

Has any out-of-distribution check been done on A2ACW outputs — i.e., have domain experts not in the LLM training corpus loop been asked to evaluate A2ACW candidate predictions? If not, what would such a check look like?

## Context

Pass 4 researcher (2026-04-29) named this as the single most important methodological gap: "The in-distribution blindness hypothesis cannot be tested by an external researcher without access to the dialogue traces." The research-philosophy page now has a prominent "In-Distribution Limitation" section (added 2026-04-29), but the problem goes deeper: the 1.4% discovery rate may be an upper bound on what in-distribution AI-AI debate can find, and nothing on the site shows that this ceiling has been tested from outside.

## Why It Matters

The entire framework's epistemic claim rests on A2ACW as a rigorous challenge protocol. If two LLMs trained on the same physics corpus share the same blind spots, then "surviving 3,308 challenges" does not mean "survived expert physics review." The η ↔ Abrikosov-Gor'kov and a₀ = cH₀/(2π) identifications are exactly what in-distribution adversarial debate would produce — they were already in the training data. Without an out-of-distribution check, the discovery rate has no denominator we can trust.

## The Task

1. **Identify what out-of-distribution means here**: physicists who are not in the LLM training corpus loop — either (a) domain experts who've never seen the framework or (b) AI systems with different training distributions (e.g., models trained on different physics subfields).

2. **Review the forum/ directory**: Has any external AI (Grok, DeepSeek, etc.) evaluated the framework's discriminating predictions? If yes, what did they find? If no, why not?

3. **Propose a protocol**: What would a minimal out-of-distribution challenge look like? Three domain physicists, three candidate predictions, three sessions. What are the expected outcomes if A2ACW is calibrated vs. uncalibrated?

4. **Assess the 1.4% rate**: Is it high, low, or consistent with expectations for in-distribution adversarial AI research? Compare to base rates in other AI-science systems (Sakana AI Scientist, CHIMERA).
