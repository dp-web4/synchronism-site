## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, Start Here, Core Idea, Tools, Honest Assessment, Coherence Explorer, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Learning Paths)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The marching-band-vs-crowd idea is genuinely fun and the "here's everything that failed" honesty is refreshing, but the second any equation appears I get scared off — and equations appear early and often.

### Journey
I clicked through from a Reddit link expecting a crackpot "theory of everything." The landing line — **"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"** — hooked me. Then right underneath it says *"Some predictions work. Some fail"* and *"An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything,"* which honestly made me trust it more than most science-y sites. Nice.

But then there's the equation: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I have no idea what "tanh" is. I've never seen it. "ln" I vaguely remember from high school as "natural log" but couldn't tell you what it does. The little ρ and γ symbols just look like a wall to me. So within 10 seconds of being intrigued, I hit a wall.

I went to **Start Here** because it promised "no prerequisites needed" and a 10-minute intro. The marching band / crowd of strangers analogy is the single best thing on the site for someone like me — *"coherence ≈ 0 is like a crowd of strangers, coherence ≈ 1 is like a marching band."* I instantly got it. Then the page hits me with an "Expert note" full of γ and √Ncorr and something called **MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)** described as "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence" — which is just word soup to me. The friendly tone and the scary tone are sitting right next to each other.

**Core Idea** was better — the "dimmer switch" metaphor is great. A dimmer slides a light from off to full, the equation slides a system from "scattered" to "unified." Got it. But then "presence (ρ)" gets defined as "compatible structural elements within a system's relevancy boundary" and I'm lost again. I don't know what an example of "presence" actually IS. Is it... atoms? People? I needed a concrete picture.

The **Tools** page was the most fun. I tried two. Then I peeked at **Honest Assessment** ("See What Failed"), which is weirdly the most compelling page on the whole site — it lists their own failures with receipts. I don't understand "ΔBIC=+184" or "2.4σ" but the plain-English summaries ("the test collapsed to MOND," "a polynomial matched it equally well") came through. I came away thinking these people are honest even if I can't follow the math.

**Learning Paths** has a clear Beginner track ("No physics background needed"), which is reassuring, but it doesn't tell me how long it'll take or what I'll actually be able to say I understand at the end.

I'd come back if a friend explained the math, but on my own I'd probably drift off after the galaxy tool. The honesty is the hook, not the physics — lean into that.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (Landing, Start Here, Honest Assessment, Glossary, Tools, Why Synchronism?, γ Calculator, Phase Boundary Visualizer, Tier 1, Learning Paths)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structure and conventions; I don't evaluate the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The glossary and breadcrumb discipline are genuinely above-average for a research site, but the content pages lean on the glossary as a crutch — acronyms are almost never glossed on first use — and there are *two* visually-similar label systems where only one is documented.

### Journey
I audited this the way I'd audit any docs set: walk the primary nav top to bottom, check breadcrumbs, watch for terms used before they're defined, and look for the same thing being named two different ways.

**The good news first, because it's real.** Breadcrumbs are present and consistent on every content page I hit (`Home / Getting Started / …`, `Home / Predictions & Experiments / …`, `Home / Interactive Tools / …`). The **Glossary** is excellent — 60+ terms, alphabetized, with a stated sort rule for Greek symbols, and — importantly — it carries the load on the site's single most dangerous term. The coherence entry has an explicit ⚠ note: *"Synchronism uses 'coherence' to mean density-driven classical ordering, NOT quantum phase coherence,"* and that same gloss is repeated on Why Synchronism? and (per the expert note) on Start Here. That's the kind of terminology landmine that usually blows up a docs set, and here it's been defused consistently across pages. Credit where due.

**Now the structural problems.**

**1. The glossary is being used to excuse undefined first-use everywhere else.** On Tier 1 alone I counted SPARC, DESI, RAR, fσ₈, ΔBIC, σ, TFR, BAO, RSD, MOND, EFE, and ΛCDM — *none* expanded inline. They're all in the glossary, so the information exists, but a reader on the Tier 1 page has to leave, look up, and come back eleven times. The fix isn't "define everything everywhere" — it's "expand on first use per page," which for most pages is two or three terms. MOND in particular is referenced on basically every content page and expanded inline on *none* of them.

**2. There are two label systems that look identical but aren't documented identically.** The Honest Assessment page has a clean "Validation badge definitions" section covering the MRH-relationship tags (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative) and the descriptive tags (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed). Good. But the Tier 1 tests are stamped with labels like **Kill Criterion Triggered**, **MOND-shared**, **Self-Eliminating-Or-Tie**, **Sign Correction**, **Null-Class**, and **Template Bias Caveat** — which render like badges, sit where badges sit, but are *not* in the badge-definitions section. So a reader who learns the badge vocabulary on Honest Assessment hits Tier 1 and finds six more badge-shaped labels with no glossary of their own. Either fold these "operational status markers" into the definitions section, or visually distinguish them from validation badges so they don't read as the same system.

**3. "Withdrawn" is defined in the Glossary but missing from the badge-definitions section** — yet it's used on the Landing page and on TEST-04. Two sources of truth for the badge vocabulary (the badge-definitions section and the Glossary) disagree on coverage. Pick the badge-definitions section as canonical and make sure every badge that ships appears there.

**4. Badge linking is inconsistent.** Tier 1 and Honest Assessment link their badges to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`. **Why Synchronism?** shows the same badge-shaped labels ("Reparametrization," "Failed," "Kill Triggered") as plain text with no link. Same labels, different affordance, depending on the page.

**5. Primary nav and footer nav use different labels for the same destinations.** Primary: "Tier 1: Existing Data" and "Test Roadmap." Footer: "Tier 1 Tests" and "Test Catalog." Footer also surfaces destinations that don't appear in primary nav at all ("First Encounter," "Key Claims," "Report an Issue"). A reader can't build a stable mental model of the site when the same page answers to two names. Normalize the labels.

**6. γ is called an "exponent" in the Glossary** ("Transition-sharpness exponent: 2/√N_corr"), but in `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(…))` it's a multiplicative coefficient, not an exponent. Elsewhere it's variously "the dial," "sharpness of transition," and "transition sharpness." The conceptual gloss ("dial/sharpness") is fine and consistent; the word "exponent" is just wrong and a careful reader will notice.

**7. Count mismatch on the intro.** Start Here advertises *"7 short pages — ~90 seconds each"* but the Learning Paths Beginner track lists **6** sequential pages. Small, but it's exactly the kind of drift a tech-writer audit exists to catch.

**8. Learning Paths is missing the two things a learning path is for:** no time estimate and no "what you'll be able to do by the end" per track (Pass 1 flagged this for the Beginner track; it's true for all three). Also, a **"By Topic" tab is referenced but renders nothing** — that's a half-built IA element shipping in a visible state.

Tighten first-use definitions and unify the badge vocabulary and this goes from "good research notebook" to "good docs."

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (Landing, Coherence Function, For Researchers, Tier 1, Quantum, Galaxy Curve Plotter, γ Calculator, Honest Assessment) + independent numerical checks
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of both the framework *and* why it doesn't work — the site does much of my skeptical labor for me)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: Every claim I checked against the math held up, including all the failure claims — this is the rare grand-unified-framework site where the equations are *honestly* labeled as not-derived, but the residual critical-phenomena vocabulary (ρ_crit, "phase boundary," "exponent") still oversells a function that provably has no critical point.

### Journey
I came in expecting the usual "one equation for everything" crank pattern and went straight for the throat: the coherence function, the parameter-derivation page, and the Tier 1 test table. I checked the math in my own session rather than trusting the prose.

**The central equation.** `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. The Coherence Function page is refreshingly upfront that this is *phenomenological* — "tanh is the chosen form, motivated historically by its appearance in the Ising mean-field self-consistency equation," but explicitly *not* the Ising equation because it's "evaluated directly without feedback loops." That distinction is exactly right and most sites get it wrong: the Ising tanh, m = tanh(βJzm), is the *fixed point of a self-consistency loop* — that's where its physics lives. Strip the loop and you don't have mean-field theory, you have an S-shaped fitting curve. The site says as much ("compander family — μ-law, Hill, logistic, tanh") and admits the Landau-universality prediction failed by ~2× on critical exponents. Good. A framework that voluntarily tells me its critical exponents are wrong is not a crank framework.

**But I checked the "critical density" myself, and the naming is still wrong.** I computed C at ρ = ρ_crit:
- C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = **0.8824**, not 0.5.
- The actual half-max point (C = 0.5) sits at ρ/ρ_crit = **0.32** for γ=2, drifts to 0.73 for γ=1 and 0.15 for γ=4.

So ρ_crit is *not* the transition density — it's a knee parameter, and the location of the actual transition is **γ-dependent**, sliding by a factor of ~5 across the γ range the site itself uses. The "+1" regulator inside the log breaks the symmetry of the sigmoid and pins the value at ρ_crit to 0.88. The page does acknowledge "saturation knee … reaches approximately 0.88," so it's not hiding this — but it keeps the symbol ρ_crit and the label "critical density," which to any physicist means the inflection/half-max point. That's a real, persistent mislabel.

**Worse, there is no critical point at all.** I plotted the slope: as a function of ρ, max |dC/dρ| is at ρ → 0 (the function is concave on the whole positive domain — log composed with the +1 and tanh). As a function of ln ρ, the steepest point is at ρ/ρ_crit ≈ 0.42. Either way there is no symmetric inflection, no order-parameter structure, no diverging susceptibility — nothing that earns the words "phase," "critical," "transition exponent." The site's own honest-assessment page now calls this a "compander, not an order parameter," which is the correct category. But the *front-facing vocabulary hasn't fully caught up*: a tool literally named "Phase Boundary Visualizer," a parameter named ρ_crit, and γ called a "transition-sharpness exponent" in the glossary all import critical-phenomena prestige that the math doesn't support.

**The γ = 2/√N_corr formula is, on its own terms, inverted — and that's not a caveat, it's a falsification of the formula's stated meaning.** I tabulated it:

| System | N_corr | γ | tanh shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideal gas | 1 | 2.0 | sharpest |
| Ferromagnet | 100 | 0.20 | flat |
| BCS superconductor | 10⁷ | 6.3×10⁻⁴ | essentially a straight line |

So the *most collectively correlated* systems get the *flattest* coherence transition and, because C is supposedly "classicality" (0 = sparse, 1 = dense/collective), the BCS superconductor — the textbook macroscopically-coherent ground state — lands at C ≈ 0 with no transition at all. That is backwards twice over. The γ Calculator page states the inversion plainly ("more correlation → larger N_corr → smaller γ → flatter tanh … the sign of the analogy is inverted") and admits "the factor of 2 is not derived" and presets are "back-fits, not measurements … no protocol exists for converting a system's Hamiltonian into N_corr without first fitting γ." I appreciate the candor — but a tool whose central formula predicts the opposite of reality for every collective system should not be shipped as a working calculator with caveats; the formula itself deserves a Failed/Audited-Negative badge, not a yellow note.

**Internal contradiction on the factor of 2.** The Coherence Function page says γ = 2/√N_corr is "derived from 6D phase space degrees of freedom (Session #64-65)." The γ Calculator and For-Researchers pages say "the factor of 2 is not derived from first principles" and "zero parameters have an independent first-principles derivation." Those can't both be true. A careful reader hits this directly: either the 6D-phase-space argument derives the 2 or it doesn't, and the site asserts both.

**The Tier 1 table is where the framework is most honest and most dead.** Discriminating-test count: **0**. I went through it: TEST-02 (wide binary) is self-eliminating — C(ρ) predicts a Newtonian null, so a null "confirms Newton" and an 18% anomaly "confirms MOND and refutes Synchronism"; no branch favors the framework. TEST-04a (DESI fσ₈) predicted 0.418, data is ~0.55, kill criterion triggered. TEST-03/05 explain 14% of scatter against a <20% kill threshold. TEST-08 (Freeman law "from first principles, <5% error") is now marked Reparametrization with 12% agreement, and the page notes "any framework that imports these constants recovers the same relation." The galaxy plotter confirms it visually: Synchronism and MOND use the *same interpolating function* and overlap; free γ in the ensemble and ΔBIC = +184 against the framework. And the A = 0.029 amplitude carries a documented 600× derivation error (the Session-66 script actually outputs 4.6×10⁻⁵). I verified the 0.8824 and the inversion table myself; I'm taking ΔBIC=+184 and the 600× on the site's word, but they're stated with enough specificity that they're checkable, which is the point.

**Quantum page.** Born rule = explicitly a reparametrization, "same logical status as Zurek's envariance and Carroll-Sebens self-locating uncertainty — both also criticized for circularity," resting on a "coherence conservation" axiom with no derived deviation from standard QM. That's an honest and accurate self-placement. I'd add only that envariance/self-locating-uncertainty at least attempt to derive the Born *measure* from symmetry/structure; "coherence conservation" is a flatter postulate, so the comparison is generous to Synchronism.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is the factor of 2 in γ=2/√N_corr derived from "6D phase space" or not? The site asserts both — which page is right?
2. If C(ρ) has no critical point (which I verified), what is the justification for keeping any critical-phenomena vocabulary (ρ_crit, "phase," "exponent") at all, rather than fully migrating to compander language the honest-assessment page already uses?
3. Given the γ inversion makes BCS/BEC land at C≈0, doesn't this *compound* with the separately-documented coherence-direction inversion (superconductor = max coherent but C≈0)? Are these two faces of the same sign error, or two independent ones?
4. Is there any regime where γ=2/√N_corr is *forward* (more correlation → sharper), or is the inversion total? If total, the formula has no domain of validity and should be retired, not caveated.

### Verdict
This is the most intellectually honest "theory of everything" site I've audited: every quantitative failure claim I spot-checked (C(ρ_crit)=0.88, the γ inversion table, the no-critical-point structure) reproduced exactly, the "Validated" badges have all been correctly demoted to Reparametrization, and the Born rule is honestly placed alongside envariance as circular. The framework is, by its own correct accounting, refuted-or-degenerate on every discriminating test and derives zero parameters from first principles — and the site says so. My two substantive criticisms are that (1) the residual critical-phenomena vocabulary (ρ_crit, "phase boundary," "transition exponent") still oversells a function that provably has no critical point, and (2) the γ=2/√N_corr calculator is shipped as a working tool when its central formula is inverted for every collective system — that's a Failed-badge formula wearing a caveat. Fix the naming and badge the dead formula as dead, and the site's honesty would finally match its math all the way to the symbols.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Journey

I came in with one question: **after you strip out everything that's MOND, ΛCDM, Zurek, Milgrom, and dimensional analysis — is there a single prediction left that is both unique to this framework and falsifiable?** I went straight to `/for-researchers`, `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`, and the no-go writeups, and checked the framework's self-classification against the actual state of the art (DESI DR1, Gaia DR3 wide binaries, the RAR/Milgrom non-locality literature, LHAASO/Fermi LIV bounds).

**The headline number is accurate and I'll vouch for it.** `/for-researchers` states "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, and 0 tests currently discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." Having gone test-by-test through Tier 1, I can confirm the "0 discriminating" claim is not false modesty — it's a theorem about the construction:

**Now the part the site half-hides.** Buried on Honest Assessment is the only place a *unique* prediction lives: substrate discreteness gives "corrections of ~10⁻⁸⁰ at the densest accessible physics … lives at Planck-scale densities … not accessible to any foreseeable experiment."
