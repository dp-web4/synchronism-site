## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (`/`, `/why-synchronism`, `/first-encounter`, `/core-idea`, `/galaxy-plotter`, `/gamma-calculator`, `/hard-problem`, `/honest-assessment`, `/glossary`)

### Journey
I clicked in from Reddit expecting another "one equation explains everything" site. The landing page surprised me: right under the big question it says **0 predictions confirmed** and "0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit." I've never seen a theory site lead with its own failures. That's what kept me reading — it felt like a Veritasium video where the twist is "we were wrong."

I followed the Beginner Path. **Why Synchronism?** (Step 1) was the best page for me — the crowd-in-a-plaza vs. marching-band analogy immediately made sense, and admitting melting points were off by 53% made me trust the rest. But even here, words like "ansatz," "compander," and "BEC/BCS" floated by with no explanation.

**First Encounter** (Step 2) says "no prerequisites needed," then hit me with ρ in a section header and something called the "Markov Relevancy Horizon." I don't know what a Markov is. The marching-band opening was great; the MRH part I skimmed and honestly bounced off.

**The Core Idea** (Step 3) shows the big equation. The "dimmer switch" metaphor helps, but the page never actually tells me what tanh *is* — the "Why tanh?" section answers with "it's an S-shaped curve," which just moves the mystery one word over. And I never saw the S-curve actually *drawn* on this page. A picture of the curve would have done more than three paragraphs.

Then I played with the two tools (below), read the consciousness page because obviously I clicked the one titled **The Hard Problem**, skimmed the **Honest Assessment**, and ended in the **Glossary** to look up the words that had been stacking up.

The Honest Assessment was the emotional peak of the visit. "The honest map of what failed — and why — is the actual product" is a sentence I'll remember. I didn't follow the details (BTFR? 3.3σ?), but the plain-language boxes meant I always got the gist: the equation only ever ties an existing 40-year-old idea called MOND, never beats it.

### Journey
I audited methodically: landing page nav inventory first, then the pages the nav promises, then two interactive tools, then cross-reference checks between the three places badge definitions live.

The landing page's layered navigation (top menu → role-based entry points → content cards → footer) is genuinely good IA. "No physics background? … Physicist? … Researcher?" routing is exactly what a docs site should do. Then I clicked **Learning Paths**, whose nav tooltip reads *"Choose your journey: Physics, Chemistry, Philosophy, or All"* — and found a page organized purely by difficulty (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced). The page's own headline says "Choose by difficulty level or by topic," but no topic-based paths appear. That's a broken promise at the top level of the primary nav.

Next, terminology. I counted ~14 acronyms on the landing page alone that are never expanded (MOND, MRH, BTFR, RAR, DESI, SPARC, ALFALFA, SDSS, CFD…). The one that matters most is **MRH**, because the site's own *badge taxonomy* is named after it ("MRH-relationship tags"). I tracked its first use on each page I visited: the landing page links "See MRH →" without expanding it; `/research-philosophy` presents "MRH-relationship tags" as a heading with no expansion anywhere on the page; `/honest-assessment` uses it in a section heading and defines it "much later"; `/key-claims` drops "resonant selection at an MRH" in its first paragraph. Only `/mrh` itself and the glossary expand it. A reader who hasn't found those two pages will invent their own expansion — while auditing I caught myself provisionally reading it as "Model Reparametrization Hypothesis," which is wrong and actively misleading in a taxonomy context.

The badge system was my deepest dive, across three pages. Good news: the definitions on `/research-philosophy` and `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` agree, and the latter is correctly declared canonical with a working anchor link. Bad news: a reader actually encounters **four** label families with no single map — (1) MRH-relationship badges (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative), which the taxonomy calls "preferred" but which I saw on **zero** claims on `/key-claims`; (2) descriptive badges (Untested/Speculative/Reparametrization/Failed), which do all the visible work; (3) "operational states" (Withdrawn, Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared (RETIRED), Null-Class…) that look like badges but are declared not-badges; and (4) the Tools page's "Model Explainer" tag, whose disclaimer ("a content grouping, not a validation badge") lives on `/honest-assessment` — a different page from the one where the tag is used. On `/key-claims` the badges are static text, not links, and are freely composed with ad-hoc modifiers ("untestable as stated," "unrunnable") that appear in no taxonomy.

Breadcrumbs: present and functional on every page I checked — genuinely rare and appreciated. But section assignments wobble: Honest Assessment breadcrumbs into "Getting Started" while the top nav presents it as a top-level destination, the landing page calls it "Shortcut · not a path step," and the Beginner Path calls it Step 5. Four different classifications of one page.

Tools tested below. Copy hygiene note from `/key-claims`: the parenthetical "(0=sparse/independent, 1=dense/collective)" appears verbatim three times on one page — a define-once-and-link candidate.

The glossary is the best-executed page on the site: ~80 terms, stated sort order (Greek after Latin, badges clustered), every one of my seven spot-checks present (MRH, MOND, coherence, tanh, γ, SPARC, BTFR), entries cross-linked outward with "Learn more →". The problem is directional: the glossary links out to pages, but pages mostly don't link in to the glossary at first use.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (`/`, `/for-researchers`, `/galaxy-rotation`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/interactive-tools`, `/key-claims`, `/honest-assessment`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer`, `/chemistry-correlation-explorer`, `/measurement-without-observers`)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — as a case study in negative-result documentation, not for the physics
- **Overall impression**: The badges are honest and the site's self-criticism is technically correct — but I caught one algebraic inconsistency the audits missed, and the tools still lead with formulas the site itself has refuted.

### Journey
A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project," so I went in expecting the usual grand-unification overreach. The landing page disarmed that: "0 prospective predictions confirmed," "0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit." Fine — but self-flagellation can be its own marketing. So I went straight to the derivations to check whether the humility is load-bearing or decorative.

**Parameter derivations** is the page I'd grade first, and it mostly survives. The tanh discussion makes the exact distinction I'd have raised: mean-field Ising gives m = tanh(βJzm) as a *self-consistency* condition, while C(ρ) evaluates tanh directly with no fixed-point loop — "these are structurally different." That's correct, and it's the kind of thing frameworks in this genre never admit. Same for γ = 2/√N_corr: the site itself notes the sign inversion (in real finite-size scaling, transition width ∝ N^(−1/2), so *more* correlated DOF means *sharper* transitions — the formula predicts the opposite, putting BCS superconductors at the flattest end). I'd have written that referee comment; they wrote it for me.

Then I checked their arithmetic, and found a real error. The page quotes a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ("within 10%" of Milgrom's 1.2×10⁻¹⁰), and separately Σ₀ ≈ 119 M☉/pc² "at H₀ = 67.4." These cannot both be right, because the page itself proves Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) *exactly* — one number, not two. I ran it: H₀ = 67.4 gives a₀ = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ (13.2% below Milgrom) and Σ₀ = 119 ✓; H₀ = 70 gives a₀ = 1.082×10⁻¹⁰ (9.8% off) and Σ₀ = 123.6. The page is quoting a₀ from the H₀ = 70 world and Σ₀ from the H₀ = 67.4 world. Ironic on a page whose Σ₀ caveat correctly calls percentage agreement "precision theater."

**Galaxy rotation** is the strongest science page. The boost function is McGaugh 2016's RAR interpolating function, credited as such. The compander test is properly done: γ = 2 pinned loses to McGaugh at ΔBIC = +184; free γ converges to 0.49 with RMS identical to four digits and ΔBIC = +7.1, which they correctly identify as "entirely the BIC parameter penalty" — I checked, ln(2807) ≈ 7.9, so yes, that's the one-parameter cost with essentially zero likelihood gain. The conclusion "no γ for which the compander is both distinct from MOND and consistent with SPARC" is exactly what that analysis supports. I also recomputed the BTFR kill: (3.75 − 3.35)/√(0.10² + 0.07²) = 3.28σ, matching the quoted 3.3σ. The numbers on this site walk.

**Key claims / honest assessment**: I hunted for dishonest badges and didn't find one. Nothing wears "Validated" except deprecated-and-flagged relics; the a₀ and Σ₀ relations are correctly labeled Reparametrization with Milgrom (1983) and Freeman cited; the consciousness C ≈ 0.50 "convergence" is flagged as geometrically forced (midpoint of tanh's range — correct); the CHSH construction honestly tops out at S = 1.98 < 2, i.e., it's a local model that pays Bell's price rather than dodging it. The site even documents its *own* fabricated statistics (a golden-ratio p < 0.0001 "with no source in any repository") — I've never seen a project retract its own numbers at this granularity. One muddle: the MOND-shared dissolution text mixes TEST-05's r² = 0.0001 (an effect-size null) with "levers differ by 50–5,000×" (an amplitude-scaling argument) in one breath; these are different quantities and the passage reads as if they're one.

**Measurement without observers** is the weakest theory page. It concedes it's "an interpretive overlay on decoherence, not a complete measurement theory," and that the Born-rule argument has "the same logical status as Zurek's envariance" — fair. But its comparison table still says definite outcomes are "derived from γ parameter crossing MRH," which overstates what an interpretive overlay can deliver, two paragraphs after admitting as much.

### Tools Tested
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — worked — mostly intuitive — Physics evaluation: the tool honestly displays a formula the site has refuted, which is a defensible pedagogical choice, but the presentation order is wrong: the map (BCS flattest, ideal gas and galaxies sharing γ = 2) is exactly the picture real condensed matter contradicts, and the "backwards" admission comes after you've internalized it. ### Unanswered Questions

The physics verdict is that there's no living theory here: everything either reduces to MOND's curve, reparametrizes known relations, or died on data. The site knows this and says so. As a scientific artifact it's a failure; as documentation of *how* a framework fails under honest adversarial testing, it's the best specimen I've seen, and I'd show it to my group for that reason alone.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (`/`, `/for-researchers`, `/a2acw`, `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/interactive-tools`, `/honest-assessment`, `/two-reframes`, `/consciousness-demo`, `/galaxy-plotter`)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — to check whether the DESI DR2 pre-commitment and the A2ACW cross-vendor control ever materialize
- **Overall impression**: The zero-survivor ledger is accurate and the kills are real, but the site's frontier engagement is 12–18 months stale in places, and "For Researchers: What's Citable" offers nothing a researcher can actually cite.

### Journey
I came in with the question every referee asks of an "AI-generated physics framework": is there one prediction here that differs from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM and survives contact with data? The site answers this itself, on `/for-researchers`, in the first screen: "0 confirmed predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, and 0 tests currently discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." So I spent my time doing what I actually do for a living — auditing whether that self-verdict is correct, whether the negative results are sound, and whether anything transfers.

**Is the zero-survivor verdict accurate?** Yes, and — this is the unusual part — the framework *did* make predictions that differ from MOND. It has exactly two structural departures: a bounded boost (ceiling 1/Ω_m ≈ 3.17) and a coupling to *local* density rather than acceleration. Both were pushed to executed kills: BTFR slope n = 3.35 ± 0.07 predicted vs 3.75 ± 0.10 observed (3.3σ, kill bar 0.3 crossed at 0.41); dwarf DM fractions capped at 68.5% while 69% of SPARC galaxies exceed the cap; environment coupling predicted at >20% of scatter, measured at r² = 0.0001 against Cosmicflows-4 densities. The tier-1 page's closing line is the correct anatomy: the framework's only two differences from MOND "each force structural disagreement exactly where tests execute." Everywhere else it's the RAR interpolating function wearing a tanh costume (free-γ → 0.49, ΔBIC = +7.1 = pure parameter penalty — I agree with Pass 3's check). The dichotomy is complete: where it differs it's dead, where it lives it's MOND. That's a real result, properly executed, and I've reviewed submissions to serious journals with worse statistical hygiene than TEST-09's registered-criterion discipline.

**Is A2ACW sound as a physics-generating methodology?** No, and the site's own audit is the strongest evidence: 3,308 sessions → 47 internally-consistent candidates (1.4%) → 0 of 6 audited survive expert review. The retrospective detector analysis is honest about being uninformative — the three-axis protocol flags every demoted claim *and* every genuine discovery, so Youden's J = 0. The diagnosis quoted on `/a2acw` is exactly right: "same-corpus self-play without an external oracle converges on internal consistency, not discovery," and the vocabulary-translation axis catching 4/4 *prior-art rediscoveries* confirms the mechanism. What's missing to make even the null citable: a control arm (human pairs or cross-vendor models on the same corpus) and preregistration — both acknowledged as unrun. One apparent copy error on the flagship researcher page: the detector stats render as "Specificity (false-positive rate): 0/6 = 1.0." Specificity of 0/6 is 0.0; the 1.0 is the false-positive rate 6/6. On the page pitched at exactly the audience who will notice, that's an own-goal (flagged for verification against source below).

**Does anything transfer?** The page claims four citable artifacts. My referee assessment: (1) The **local-density no-go** is correct physics and correctly credited as a Milgrom-2005 instance — RAR phenomenology lives in acceleration space, and the V⁻² vs V⁺² sign statement is the crispest formulation of it I've seen.
