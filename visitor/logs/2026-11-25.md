## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

*Method note: pages were read through WebFetch (via fetch agents), which returns page text, not a live browser. I could see every control's label, default and readout text, but I could not drag a slider and watch the chart redraw. The landing page (~60k characters) and Honest Assessment (~199k characters) came back truncated: verbatim to ~40k, then a model summary. Any quoted wording below from those tails is second-hand.*

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Interactive Tools, Coherence Explorer, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Glossary, Honest Assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: An unusually honest "here's an idea, here's how it failed" notebook with a really good beginner path, but its first screen mentions MOND, Bell tests and Gaia DR4 before telling me what any of them are.

### Journey
I came from a Reddit link. The headline, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, is exactly the PBS Space Time hook that makes me click. The subtitle "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" reassured me it wasn't crank stuff. Then within a few lines the hero told me the idea "did not hold as stated", that AI agents did the research with one human lead, that no outside physicist has reviewed it, and that it is "dead as tested physics so far, alive as a public record." I've never seen a site lead with its own obituary, and it made me trust it more. It also made me ask, *so why am I reading this?* The answer ("the audit is the product") only clicked later, on the Honest Assessment page.

The same hero also mentions MOND, "the cluster scale needs a second density parameter," retrodictions, a Bell test ruling out "the simplest version of the grid," and Gaia DR4 on 2 December. Before the "for people checking the work" divider I had no idea what any of those were. The divider is a good idea, but some of the hard stuff sits above it. I skipped the wall below it: an abbreviation key with ~30 entries, four versions of C, ΔBIC +2,843. The crowd-vs-marching-band picture of "coherence" from 0 to 1 is the thing I took away from the landing page.

I clicked **"Start here: Why Synchronism?"**. This was the best page for me. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" made sense straight away. "What happened, in three lines" told me the whole story, and "Why does matching an existing theory count as losing?" answered the exact question I had. Then it said "Beginner path: that is all you need from this page." I love being told I'm allowed to stop. The plain part is ~600–700 words, about 3 minutes, under the "~8 min" the landing promised. The page itself doesn't show a reading time.

**First Encounter** (step 2) says "No prerequisites needed" and uses the marching band and crystals as "high coherence". Its intro then says the formula "scores superconductors low, the opposite of the picture it draws." That confused me. I had only just accepted the picture, and now I'm told it's wrong for the most famous "everything in lockstep" material, with the reason held back to Section B. Section B shows the full tanh/ln equation. The site explains tanh as an S-curve and ln as squashing big ranges, which I could follow, but then there's "γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴ for BCS," and BCS is never expanded. Section E has "ρ_crit = 0.028 × V_flat^0.5" and "a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π)". I skimmed. Section G ("Where It Fails") was satisfying: melting points 53% off, YBCO superconductor predicted 607 K vs 93 K real. Concrete numbers I can grasp even if I don't know what YBCO is. In the fetched text the Continue button after Section G still read "Continue ↓ Section B". That might just be how the fetch sees a step-by-step page, with all sections shown at once, so it needs checking in a real browser.

The **Interactive Tools** index was clear. Seven tools, two marked Beginner, and "Best first tool" points at the Coherence Explorer. One "tool", the Consciousness Threshold Demo, says on its own card it has "no controls," which is honest but a bit odd in a tools list.

The **Coherence Explorer** starts well: "What this toy is for: seeing the shape of the S-curve," and it "does not model any real material." It also suggests what to try (drag γ to 2, then to 0.5). The presets lost me. "γ = 0.05 · density fit (lost)": lost what? "γ = 0.49 · galaxy fit" and "γ = ½ · same as MOND" sit next to each other and are almost the same number, and I don't know why both exist. The γ readout says "10–90% width 1.50 dex." I had to go to the glossary for "dex". The ρcrit slider is called a "saturation knee", which means nothing to me.

The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** has the best onboarding on the site: "New here? Look at three things on the chart below." The dots are what telescopes see, the gray line is visible matter only, and the gap between them is "the dark-matter puzzle". That is the clearest explanation of dark matter I've had, and the legend is in plain English ("MOND, the rival theory (gravity changes in weak fields)"). Then the controls get hard. There's a "Calibration selector" offering "Archive's own form: ρcrit = 0.028·Vflat^0.5" or "Units hybrid: 0.029·Vflat²". As a beginner I have no basis for picking either. A checkbox offers to "Show the old hand-drawn stand-in … kept for the record". Why is a known-wrong curve in a beginner tool? The readouts below the chart (floor Ωm = 0.315, boost ceiling 3.17×, C max 0.244) were beyond me. The "How heavy are the stars?" slider label is a nice touch.

The **Glossary**'s "New here? Start with these ten" row was exactly right. Coherence, density, γ, tanh ("An S-shaped curve…"), MOND, dark matter, reparametrization, refutation, σ, MRH. With those ten I could re-read the First Encounter and follow more of it. It would help if First Encounter linked to this row before Section B.

Last I clicked **"See What Failed"** (Honest Assessment). It says it is about 30,000 words, but the top tells a non-physicist to read one box and one paragraph, about 4 minutes, and stop at the green box. "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" did what it says. "As a theory of galaxies: fatal, by its own scoreboard. Where it fits galaxies, it is MOND in different notation." Now I get it.

I'd come back to see what DR4 does, mostly because a site that admits it failed this plainly is rare.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

*Method note: no direct WebFetch tool was available in this session, so I read the live site by fetching each page's server-rendered HTML with curl and converting it to text. Every word came through, with no model summary and no truncation. For client-rendered state (tool steps, button labels) I read the page's own JS chunk from the live deployment. I also checked anchors against the raw HTML `id` attributes, because fetched text drops them. No local source files were read.*

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 read closely (landing, Learning Paths, Glossary, Interactive Tools, γ Calculator, Equation Anatomy, Test Roadmap, Honest Assessment (structure and badge sections), Why Synchronism?, First Encounter (structure), MRH, Decisive Tests), plus Research Philosophy's badge section. Another 9 pages were fetched only to check links and breadcrumbs.
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The plumbing is better than on most documentation sites I audit: every anchor resolves, the path arithmetic adds up, and the badge list is single-sourced. The problems are vocabulary sprawl and history mixed into current-state prose, which make the site hard to scan.

### Journey
I started at the landing page with a checklist: acronyms on first use, label consistency, navigation, breadcrumbs.

**Acronyms.** First-use expansion is mostly good. MOND is expanded in the hero, DR4 gets a gloss ("Gaia is the European Space Agency's star-mapping telescope; DR4 is its fourth catalogue"), and CFD even carries a disambiguation ("not the 'counterfactual definiteness' of quantum foundations"). MRH and A2ACW are expanded where they first appear. The "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC): short key" pointer is a good pattern, but the key it links to (`#terms-on-this-page`) sits about 110 rendered lines further down. That is *after* the "for physicists" fold and the "Where each piece stands" table, which already use Υ, a0′, LLR, L2/L3 and "S177" (Υ, a0′ and L2/L3 are in the glossary but not in the short key; "S177" is in neither).

**Navigation.** I wrote a small anchor checker and ran it over the raw HTML of 22 fetched pages. Every `/page#fragment` link resolved: 0 broken. That includes the URL-encoded glossary anchors like `#%CE%94BIC` and `#Density%20(%CF%81)`. I also hand-checked Learning Paths. It claims "the difficulty paths reach 10 pages no topic track does … and the topic tracks reach 14 the difficulty paths don't". I counted both sets myself and got exactly 10 and 14. The Beginner per-step times (8 + 10 + 6 + 9 + 4) sum to the "~37 min" the landing and paths page both quote. That level of internal bookkeeping is rare.

**Breadcrumbs** are present on every page, but the hierarchy they imply is inconsistent. Middle crumbs like "Core Theory", "Cosmology", "Methodology", "Quantum Physics" and "Predictions & Experiments" are plain text with no page behind them. "Interactive Tools", "Test Roadmap" and "Research Philosophy" are real links. Tier 1 sits under Test Roadmap (`Home / Test Roadmap / Tier 1`), Test Roadmap sits under "Predictions & Experiments", and For Researchers sits under Research Philosophy. Yet all of these are flat peers in the top nav bar. Relation to MOND, Dark Matter Reframed and Wide Binaries are filed under "Cosmology", though they are galaxy-scale topics.

**Labels.** This is where I spent most of the pass. The glossary's "How to read labels on this site" table says "The site uses **eight** kinds of label. Only the first two are verdicts on a claim." The landing page's "Which label answers which question" paragraph names categories that are *not* among the eight: scoreboard words (Excluded, Is MOND, Is Λ, Survives…) and ledger classes (framework-specific, inherited from MOND, construction check…). It also tells the reader that "to ask 'did this part of the theory survive contact with data?', read the scoreboard word", so a scoreboard word is plainly a verdict. A careful reader therefore counts ten label systems, two of them verdict-bearing but missing from the map. By contrast, the *badge* list itself is consistent everywhere I checked: two families, nine badges, three retired labels, the same on Glossary, Test Roadmap's legend, Research Philosophy and the Interactive Tools hub. Research Philosophy even says the badges were renamed from "validation" to "status" on 2026-11-16. Every link still points at `#validation-badge-definitions`, which is harmless. On Honest Assessment that anchor is a collapsed `<details>` element, so a reader arriving from a badge tooltip lands on a closed one-line summary.

**Terminology drift across the glossary itself.** The letter C is the site's own acknowledged trap, and the glossary gives three different answers to "which four?":
- **Coherence** entry: "at least four different functions … one takes density, two take acceleration, and the consciousness pages use a fourth, C = f(γ, D, S)."
- **Which C? Which γ?** entry: lists C_ρ, C_g, C_a and the consciousness C. No C_f.
- **C_f (floored density form)** entry: "The four: C_ρ, C_f, C_g, C_a." No consciousness C. Its "Learn more →" goes to `/`, the homepage, not to a section.

The landing page's four-row "Which C" table matches the C_f entry, and its revision note says C_f was added 2026-11-21. The other two glossary entries were not updated with it.

**A number that doesn't match across pages.** On the landing page's "What Synchronism Covers" grid, the Galaxies card says: "The environment-scatter add-on, **run on 14,610 galaxies**, failed." The tile above it reads "14,610 Galaxies analyzed — 175 SPARC + 14,435 ALFALFA–SDSS (the sample actually run)". Test Roadmap says of that same environment refutation (TEST-03s): "that run used **N = 141 SPARC galaxies** vs Cosmicflows-4 ambient density, not the registered N = 14,585 ALFALFA-SDSS … ~100× smaller sample." Test Roadmap also says the 14,610 run was a "qualitative curve match". So the card attaches the larger, different sample to one of the six counted refutations. I'm not a physicist and can't say which run is right. As an editor, I can say the two pages disagree by a factor of 100 on the sample size of the same counted test.

**History in the current-state text.** The site has a good convention: collapsed "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)" blocks. But much of the history is *not* in them. The landing page's raw text carries 59 ISO dates. Inside a single cell of the "Where each piece stands" table I found "(Until 2026-11-22 this row said the Bohm class was the only escape; explorer 2026-11-21.) (Row relabelled 2026-11-20 from 'Inside Bell's limit'; a researcher simulated-visitor persona. Abstracts read 2026-11-20.)". Glossary definitions carry the same kind of note ("Glossary entry rewritten 2026-07-17…", "Entry added 2026-10-13…"). Alongside that, the prose cites internal artifacts as if they were references: "explorer 2026-11-21", "FUNDAMENTALS", "the research repo's STATUS.md", "PREDICTIONS.md", "maintainer/scripts/lensing_ceiling_every_convention.py (+ _output.txt)", "S611 P611.2", "Session #1,840". Honest Assessment does explain the tracks ("the explorer track does research"), but that explanation is deep in a ~30,000-word page. The glossary cites "(explorer 2026-10-31)" in its own entries and has no "explorer" entry. A reader meets "explorer 2026-11-21" on the landing page with no key.

**Honest Assessment structure.** The heading "TEST-03: ALFALFA-SDSS TFR Scatter — REMOVED FROM 'WHAT FAILED' (2026-07-09)" sits *inside* the "What Failed" section, and a second card for the same test appears earlier under "What Was Tested". One heading also reads "RAR Transition Shape — … γ=2 Disfavoured, γfree=MOND" next to the badge "Failed — Kill Criterion Triggered — CLOSED". The glossary's own "Outcome words" entry defines DISFAVOURED as "the data lean against it at about 2σ, *not excluded*". So the reader gets two outcome words from the site's own taxonomy that point opposite ways, in the same line.

**Decisive Tests** is the final step of the Intermediate path ("Step 9 of 9 … Final step"), and the page opens: "This page is now empty at the top, and that is the result." Honest, but an Intermediate reader ends a 1-hour path on a page about a list that no longer exists. Its H1 ("Decisive Tests: Status"), the path label ("Decisive Tests (none currently running)") and the URL (`/top-5-tests`, explained inline) are three names for one page. The MRH page attributes the term to the project lead by full name, while the landing page establishes "dp" as the name "throughout this site". This is a small inconsistency in how a person is referred to.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator**: works; intuitive. The opening says plainly that this tool exists to show a failure, and it tells you what to click (Ideal gas → BCS superconductor). The Quick Reference table matches the formula (Ncorr 17 → γ 0.485, 10⁷ → 6.32×10⁻⁴). From the JS: clicking a preset with Ncorr > 1000 switches the slider to Log mode automatically, which is correct. One quirk: after that, choosing Linear pins the slider at 1000 while the readout keeps the preset value. "Two different 'galaxy' γ's, on purpose … so a third 'galaxy γ' exists" is honest, but it's the most confusing caption I read today.
- **Equation Anatomy**: works; mostly intuitive. Defining every variable *before* Step 1 is good practice, and "Step 1 of 8 (this tool's own tour — not a learning-path step)" heads off the step-counter collision I was about to log. Steps 2–8 are client-rendered, so I read them from the JS chunk: 8 titled steps, ending with "What the equation is at γ = ½". There are small terminology slips in Steps 4–6 (see friction log).
- **Resolved from Pass 1**: First Encounter's "Continue ↓ Section B" after Section G is a static-render artifact. The JS renders the last section's button as "Beginner path, step 3: see it fail on a real galaxy →" plus "Go deeper (Intermediate): The Core Idea →". No fix needed.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Which page is canonical for a *number* (sample sizes, counts), the way Honest Assessment is canonical for badge definitions? Without one, the 14,610 vs 141 kind of drift will recur.
2. Is there a written rule for when history goes in a collapsed Revision notes block and when it may stay inline? The site has both patterns.
3. How many label systems does the site have, eight or ten? And which of them are verdicts?
4. What do "Core Theory", "Cosmology" and "Methodology" in the breadcrumbs refer to? There is no page for them.
5. Every page carries the same ~90-word refutation summary in the footer. Who is it for? A first-time reader meets "1 registration that could not be met, 1 construction check" on the Glossary and on every tool page.

### Verdict
Structurally this is one of the more carefully cross-referenced sites I've audited. Zero broken anchors in 22 pages, path counts that check out, a badge taxonomy rendered from one source, and explicit disambiguation wherever two counters or two meanings collide. The weaknesses are editorial. The label vocabulary keeps growing faster than the map of it. Inline correction history makes current-state text hard to scan. And a fix that lands on one page (the fourth C, the environment sample size) doesn't always reach its siblings. A "no dates outside Revision notes" rule and a single glossary list for C would remove most of what slowed me down.

### Journey
I skipped the beginner material and went to **Coherence Function**, since that's where the equation is defined. I checked the core identity by hand: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1], because tanh u = (e^2u − 1)/(e^2u + 1). At γ = ½ it collapses to x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). That is correct, and it is the single most important fact on the site. I also checked the following, and all are right:
- the slope dC/dx = γ(1 − C²)/(1 + x), concavity on x ≥ 0, and the tail 1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^−2γ;
- the series C = γx − γx²/2 + …, and the departure from γx/(1+γx) of γ(2γ − 1)x²/2;
- the 10–90 % widths. I get 1.908 dex at γ = ½ (log₁₀ 81), 1.50 at γ = 1, and 1.166 as γ → ∞;

Three things on the same page are wrong, though, and all three are places where a later correction didn't reach an older sentence:
1. **The "Scope Limit" paragraph** still says the Honest Assessment documents "critical exponents 2× off". Higher up, the same page was corrected on 2026-11-24 to "factors of 1.3–1.6", and Honest Assessment itself calls "exponents 2× off" "a fossil from the phase-transition era". The same paragraph then argues that these failures came from "applying the function outside this scope, not evidence that the function itself is wrong within scope". 2. **The "C → 1: Dense/Collective" card** says "Galaxy dynamics (N_corr≈1, γ=2) lives in this regime." Nothing else on the page supports this. 3. **The low-density caveat** says C "equals γx/(1+γx) only through second order at γ = ½". A smaller rigor point. The "Why tanh" box says Spearman(C(ρ), sound velocity) = −0.32 "for every parameter choice tested". Spearman's ρ is invariant under any strictly increasing transform, and the page proves C is strictly increasing in ρ for every γ, ρ_crit > 0. So this is identically Spearman(ρ, v_s). No parameter choice *could* change it, and the phrasing turns a theorem into an apparent robustness test.

### Unanswered Questions
