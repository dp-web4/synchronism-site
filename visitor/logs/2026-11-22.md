## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

*(Fetched the live site; a WebFetch summary agent was used for the first look at the landing page, then the rendered HTML text of each page was read directly so quoted numbers are not fetch artefacts. Tool behaviour checked by reading the deployed page JS for the sliders and readouts, since I can't drag a slider through a fetch.)*

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (Landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Coherence Explorer, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Interactive Tools, What Synchronism Is Not, Glossary (top + 3 entries), Honest Assessment, top box only)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial. I can say what the idea was (one S-curve, "crowd vs marching band", density decides where you sit), that it was tested on galaxies, and that it lost to an older idea called MOND. I could not explain *why* the Bell-test or "preferred frame" stuff matters.
- **Would return?**: maybe. I'd come back for the Galaxy Plotter and to see what happens with Gaia DR4 on 2 Dec, not for the scoreboard.
- **Overall impression**: An unusually honest "we tried a big idea and it lost" story with a good beginner path buried under a landing page that reads like a lab notebook.

### Journey
**Landing page.** The headline grabbed me right away: "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" The first paragraph is great too: one dial from 0 (crowd of strangers) to 1 (marching band), then "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." It's refreshing, and I've never seen a Reddit-linked "theory of everything" site lead with that. I liked the plain statement that AI agents did the work and no physicist has reviewed it.

Then the second paragraph, "So is it alive or dead?", lost me fast: "Bell's limit (S = 1.85; the bare local construction gives 1.98 ≤ 2)… Hensen et al. 2015: S = 2.42 ± 0.20… Bancal et al. 2012… instantaneous update in a preferred frame." I know Bell's theorem only as "spooky action at a distance, Einstein was wrong". This paragraph is above the "Start here" button, so it's the second thing every visitor reads. The Gaia DR4 paragraph after it is understandable and kind of exciting, with a real date. I almost bounced at the Bell paragraph, though.

Next came the "Start here" button, the audience routing and "New here? That is everything you need from this page. Press Start here now." Good, and I would have. But scrolling to see what I was skipping, I counted about 8,700 words: a four-row "which C" table, a status table with words like "Is Λ" and "Excluded at its own cap", ΔBIC numbers, golden-ratio exponents, and several grey "Revision notes (history; not the current claim)" boxes *in the main flow*. One of them, right above the marching-band paragraph, is a note about how long the Beginner Path takes. It felt like reading someone's edit history. The parenthetical in the Beginner Path link alone ("Step 5 is a ~30,000-word page (counted from the built page 2026-11-11; earlier notes said 18,000 and 25,000 as it grew)…") is about 70 words of bookkeeping inside a link description.

**Why Synchronism? (step 1).** This is the best page I read. "What happened, in three lines" is exactly what I wanted. "Why does matching an existing theory count as losing?" answered the question I had just formed: it rediscovered a 1983 formula, and a new theory has to predict something new. The page says "that is all you need" and folds the rest away. 10/10 for the top third. When I opened the fold, "turn it up and it comes earlier, though never as a sudden snap" confused me. Turning a dial up makes something come *earlier*? I'd have expected "sharper".

**First Encounter (step 2).** The lettered A–G sections are nice. The "what tanh does / what ln does" boxes are the first time anyone told me what those squiggles do: ln turns 10×, 100×, 1000× into 2.3, 4.6, 6.9. That's actually clicking. Section C told me the γ formula "did not survive testing" (predicted +0.55, measured −0.55, the sign backwards), which is a cool concrete failure. Section E lost me on "ρ_crit = 0.028 × V_flat^0.5… Some older pages wrote 0.029 × V_flat² instead… a units mix-up that the plotter keeps as a switch". Why would a beginner page tell me about an old typo? The a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π box ("probably a coincidence, and not evidence for this framework") was honest and readable.

Section G jarred, though. It says: *"The function captures density→coherence well, but can't replace domain-specific physics…"* Everywhere else, including the landing page table, says the density law is **Excluded** and that the density version fails galaxies. So does it capture density→coherence well or not? This is the last paragraph of the beginner intro, so it's the takeaway a casual reader leaves with.

**Coherence Explorer (tool 1).** The intro is perfect for me: "What this toy is for: seeing the shape of the S-curve… It does not model any real material." It tells me exactly what to try first (drag γ to 2, then to 0.5). Below the chart the page lists key values: C(ρ_crit) = 0.6000, C(10ρ_crit) = 0.9836, C(100ρ_crit) = 0.9998. I checked them on a phone calculator using the formula on the page (tanh(ln 2) = 0.600, tanh(ln 11) = 0.984). They match, which made me trust it. The deployed JS has real sliders: γ from 0.01 to 4 and ρ_crit from 0.01 to 10, with readouts computed live. There's also a big "For physicists" fold. Unfolded, it says "the 10–90% width is 1.50 dex at γ = 1, 1.33 at 2 and 1.20 at 10, and never goes below 1.17 dex". I don't know what a "dex" is until the readout says "1 dex = a factor of 10". That definition should come before the first use, not three paragraphs later. Worked; mostly intuitive.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter (tool 2, step 3).** The "New here? Look at three things" opening is really good: dots = measured, gray dashed = visible matter only, sagging gap = dark-matter puzzle. Green (MOND) follows the dots and violet (this framework) doesn't. I *got* the dark-matter problem from this in 30 seconds, better than some YouTube videos. The galaxy buttons (DDO 154, NGC 2403, UGC 128, NGC 3198, NGC 7331) and the star-mass slider (0.2–1.0) are real controls in the JS.

Problems:
1. The page is about 8,800 words. "Beginner Path Step 3" then has a dozen "For physicists" folds, revision notes, and four formula boxes. The folds help, but the un-folded text between the chart and the buttons is still long.
2. On the default galaxy (DDO 154, the first button), the "where is the density law's switch?" box reads: *"Here ρ_crit = 0.19 M☉/pc³, and the highest C_ρ on this disc is 0.244. In plain words: … the violet line hugs the gray one near the centre (C_ρ near 1, little boost)…"* So the number says 0.244 and the plain-words sentence directly after it says "near 1". In the JS the plain-words sentence is fixed text that doesn't change with the selected galaxy, while the number does. It describes the bright galaxies, not the dwarf that is shown by default. (That box is a "for physicists" fold, but I opened it because it's next to the controls.)
3. Two ceilings: "3.17×" in some places and "1.78× ceiling" in the same box. I eventually worked out that one is gravity and one is speed (1.78² ≈ 3.17), but nothing on the beginner-facing text says so.

**Interactive Tools.** A clear card grid. Each card has a "Kind / Level / State" legend and a paragraph on why the Kind is not a badge. It's thorough, but I didn't need three label slots plus a revision note to choose between six tools. "Best first tool" on the Coherence Explorer card is the useful bit.

**What Synchronism Is Not (step 4).** The top "In plain words — beginner? You can stop here" box is excellent. "Not proven / Not checked by journals / Not a replacement / Not compatible with relativity / Not just new labels" is five bullets I could repeat to a friend. "That is either wrong or important" is a great line. The cards below drop into "Collins, Perez, Sudarsky… c_μν ~ α/π ≈ 2×10⁻³… 15 to 27 orders of magnitude", but the page warned me first, which I appreciated.

**Glossary.** The "New here? Start with these ten" line is helpful. Right after it comes a table, "The site uses eight kinds of label", with rows like "Lifecycle verb… Executed, Adjudicated, Closed, Retired, Reclassified, Never run as registered, Underpowered as registered, Ceiling Exceeded, Empty Intersection, Demoted — Exploratory, Closed by Execution". That's a wall. I just wanted to look up "tanh". The tanh entry itself is good ("Think of it as a dimmer switch").

I noticed two entries that read differently. **Reparametrization** says "not evidence for the framework: reproducing a known formula under a new name adds nothing new". **Reparametrization (badge)** opens "A reparametrization is not a failure — it shows the framework is consistent with known physics." As a casual reader I can't tell whether the orange badge is good news or bad news.

**Honest Assessment (step 5, top box only).** It says it's ~30,000 words, and the Beginner Path tells me to read just the "If you read nothing else" box, which I did. "As a theory of galaxies: fatal" is clear, and so is "On the Beginner Path? You're done", a nice ending. But above that box is a second summary, "What a non-physicist should take away", which says the galaxy prediction missed "by about 3.3 standard deviations… past 3 is conventionally a clear miss" and also "crossed the project's own pass/fail line… by only about one standard deviation: a narrow failure." Is it a clear miss or a narrow one? It also says "Its one galaxy prediction that differed from MOND", and then four lines later the box says "Its **two** genuinely different claims both failed." One or two?

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked; intuitive. The opening tells you what to try. Its key values (0.6000 / 0.9836 / 0.9998) match the formula when checked by hand. The γ slider (0.01–4) and ρ_crit slider (0.01–10) update the curve and readouts live (checked in the deployed JS). Only snag: "dex" is undefined until the readout.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked; intuitive at the top, overwhelming below. The three-things intro made the dark-matter problem click instantly, and the five galaxy buttons and Υ slider are live. The density-switch box's plain-words sentence contradicts its own live readout on the default galaxy (0.244 vs "near 1").

### Unanswered Questions
1. Why does a quantum test (Bell) belong on a page about galaxies and density? The landing page says the "local substrate" is excluded, but I never learned what the substrate is.
2. If every Gaia DR4 outcome "lands on Newton, on MOND, or on a version of this framework that is already excluded", why watch it here at all? Is it a check on MOND rather than on Synchronism?
3. Is the orange "Reparametrization" badge good news or bad news for the idea?
4. What exactly *is* left that could still be right? "What Synchronism Is Not" says it's "either wrong or important". Which part could still be important?
5. Who is "dp", and is there any human I could email with a question besides opening a GitHub issue?

### Verdict
The beginner route is well designed: Why Synchronism → First Encounter → Galaxy Plotter → What It Is Not → one box of Honest Assessment. The "lost honestly to MOND" story came through clearly, and the Galaxy Plotter taught me the dark-matter problem better than most videos. What nearly lost me is that the landing page's second paragraph is about Bell inequalities, and every beginner page still carries revision notes and physicist bookkeeping in the main text. The worst slip is First Encounter's closing line that the function "captures density→coherence well", which contradicts the site's own verdict right where a beginner leaves.

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13 read for content (Landing, Learning Paths, Why Synchronism? (nav only), First Encounter §A, Glossary (top, label tables), Honest Assessment (badge-definitions section), Test Roadmap (legend), Tier 1 (legend), Interactive Tools, γ Calculator, Equation Anatomy, Key Claims (top), MRH, Parameter Derivations (excerpts), Research Philosophy (top)). ### Journey
**Navigation first (mechanical).** I collected every `href="/…"` on the 15 fetched pages (69 unique paths) and requested each one: all return 200. I then checked every cross-page `#anchor` link on 13 of those pages (67 distinct, e.g. `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, `/glossary#%CE%94BIC`, `/wide-binaries#dr4`, `/learning-paths → /honest-assessment#if-you-read-nothing-else`) against the target page's `id=` attributes, and found zero misses. The landing page's 5 in-page anchors all resolve too. Top nav is identical on every page (9 items). Page `<title>` and H1 match the nav labels everywhere I looked ("Test Roadmap" = "Test Roadmap", "Tier 1: Existing Data" = same). This is genuinely good work.

**Breadcrumbs.** Present on every content page. Three different hub labels appear: "Core Theory" (MRH, Parameter Derivations), "Methodology" (Research Philosophy) and "Predictions & Experiments" (Test Roadmap). They render as plain unlinked text, and none is a page: `/core-theory` 307-redirects to `/learning-paths`. Readers will click a middle crumb expecting a section index. A second, better navigation bar sits under the crumbs on path pages ("Beginner Path · Step 2 of 5 · ← Prev / Next →", "Advanced Path · Step 8 of 8 · Final step"). Honest Assessment correctly shows two bars, since it is step 5/5 of Beginner and 6/6 of Methodology. First Encounter even explains that its A–G letters are separate from the path step counter. That is careful IA.

**Learning Paths.** Clear. Beginner times add up (8+10+6+9+4 = 37 min, matching "~37 min"). The page honestly says that difficulty paths and topic tracks don't cover the same pages ("difficulty paths reach 10 pages no topic track does … topic tracks reach 14 the difficulty paths don't"). One step title is itself a status line, "Decisive Tests (none currently running)", which is good. The Advanced step 1 link goes to "Parameter Derivations", a page whose own lead says "Zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations. A more accurate title: Parameter Calibration & Honest Ansätze." If the page knows its title is wrong, rename it; the Learning Paths blurb ("the only path that covers the derivation chain") inherits the mislabel.

**The label system (Glossary → Honest Assessment → Test Roadmap → Tier 1).** This is the area I was asked to check. The good news: the Glossary's "How to read labels on this site" table names **eight label systems** and says outright that only the first two are verdicts ("If a label is not in the first two rows, it is not a badge"). The canonical section on Honest Assessment opens with "The nine badges in one table". Test Roadmap and Tier 1 render the same lists (badge families; 7 operational states; 11 lifecycle verbs) and say they are drawn from one source. I diffed them by eye across all three, and they match. Badges are explained on each page before they're used (Key Claims has a three-badge mini-legend before the first card). I would hold this up as an example.

The costs:
- **Volume.** Counting members: 9 badges + 3 retired + 7 operational states + 11 lifecycle verbs + 2 descriptors + 3 tool kinds + 3 tool levels ≈ 38 controlled terms. Then the Glossary adds a third table, "States used on test cards", with 8 rows that mix operational states and lifecycle verbs. That's correct (the State slot draws from both lists) but it is a third grouping of overlapping members. A reader who just met "Seven states" on Honest Assessment then meets eight "States" on the Glossary.
- **Rename not fully propagated.** On 2026-11-16 "validation badges" became "status badges" (Honest Assessment: *"called 'validation badges' until 2026-11-16, though none of them is a validation"*). Three live uses of the old term remain: Glossary *"not one of the nine validation badges"* (Null-Class / qualifier entry), Honest Assessment *"Both are tool-card labels; neither is a validation badge"*, and Interactive Tools *"this is a content grouping, not the site's validation badge taxonomy"*. The Glossary intro also tells readers *"the component is still called a validation badge"*, which is an implementation detail that doesn't belong in reader copy. The anchor `#validation-badge-definitions` keeps the old name, which is fine since URLs should stay stable, but Key Claims shows it as raw link text: *"Definitions: /honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions"*, two sentences after linking the same target as "status badge taxonomy".
- **Duplicated phrase.** Glossary label table, MRH-relationship row: *"Where does this claim sit relative to current research focus? (where the claim sits relative to current research focus (MRH = Markov Relevancy Horizon, …))"*. The question and its gloss say the same thing, with nested parentheses. Test Roadmap's guide repeats the nested-paren form.
- **Slot names differ between index and tool page.** The Interactive Tools cards use *"Level: … State: … Badge on its page: …"*. The γ Calculator page calls the same slots *"the Interactive Tools index Kind and Status tags"*. Status ≠ State, and "Status" also collides with "status badge".

**Terminology drift on the core symbols.** This is the most important finding of the pass, because these are the words every page depends on.

1. **ρ: "density" or "presence"?** The landing page and Equation Anatomy say ρ is density. Equation Anatomy even carries a revision note: its legend *"used to define ρ as 'Presence …', which disagreed with Step 1 directly below it … Both rows now use the definitions from Step 1 and the Glossary."* But **First Encounter, Beginner step 2, Section A, is headed "Start with Presence"**. It says *"That difference is what Synchronism calls presence (ρ)"* and ends *"presence, measured within that bubble, is the universal input."* The Glossary says the presence reading *"has never been turned into a measurement"*. The MRH page also says *"Presence (ρ) … is defined relative to an MRH"*. So the beginner path teaches the definition that another page retired as inconsistent.

2. **ρ_crit: two formulas, two provenances.** First Encounter: *"ρ_crit = 0.028 × V_flat^0.5 (… 0.028 is a fitted constant, not derived from anything) … Some older pages wrote 0.029 × V_flat² instead: … a units mix-up."* Parameter Derivations (same claim, other page): *"A = 0.028 was derived (Session 53) for ρcrit = A·V0.5"*, so it was "derived", not "not derived from anything". It also says *"the framework's ρcrit ∝ V² used everywhere else (equations.ts)"*, so V² is the house form, not an "older page". Meanwhile the **MRH page's main current argument is built on the V² form** (*"ρcrit = A·Vflat² … C ≈ γρ/(A Vflat²)"*), and so is For Researchers (*"ρcrit = A·Vflat² keys the coherence threshold…"*). I can't judge the physics, but as an editor I can see that one named parameter has two defining equations across pages, and that the page calling one of them a legacy typo is contradicted by pages still reasoning from it. A reader needs one canonical definition with the variant explicitly labelled wherever it is used.

3. **γ: a sharpness dial or a position dial?** The landing page, Equation Anatomy's legend and the γ Calculator's one-sentence intro all say γ sets the *width* of the rise, with a floor, *"there is no sharp switch, and above γ ≈ 1 raising it mostly moves the knee."* The γ Calculator's JS even records that a regime label *"read 'γ-sharp' until 2026-11-10, under a sentence saying γ is not a sharpness; a technical-writer reader counted that as six names for one dial."* The fix didn't reach the preset captions in the same file:
   - Ideal gas: *"γ hits its maximum of 2. In this framework that means **the sharpest possible switch**"*. The regime row beside it says *"rise earliest … but no steeper … the family has no sharp member."*
   - Enzyme site: *"More teamwork → smaller γ → a gentler, **earlier-starting** S-curve."* The regime row for the same γ (0.365) says *"Strongly Correlated (0.2 ≤ γ ≤ 0.6: **later rise**)"* and *"large N_corr → small γ → **latest**, lowest-slope rise"*. So the caption and the label it sits next to say opposite things.
   - Liquid water: *"drops γ from 2 toward 1 — **the boundary zone where the framework says chemistry lives**."* The regime row it lands in says *"the 'boundary' reading is retracted … neither placement follows from the equation."*
   - Caveat 2 on the same page: *"This assigns the sharpest transition (γ=2) to the least-correlated system"*. Equation Anatomy's honesty note says the same (*"An ideal gas … gets γ = 2, the sharpest"*), and so does its legend (*"one particle would give γ=2 (sharp)"*), one sentence after *"there is no sharp switch."* The Interactive Tools card for Coherence Explorer says the transition *"sharpens or flattens"*.
   
   The underlying point (the formula runs backwards relative to real collective systems) survives either framing. But the page should pick one vocabulary: "earlier/later, gentler/steeper rise" if γ isn't a sharpness, and keep "sharp" only for the real materials being compared.

**MRH.** The term is expanded at first use on every page I checked (Key Claims: "MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)"; First Encounter; Glossary). The MRH page and the badge legend both warn that the MRH-relationship badges "borrow the word by analogy and say nothing about physics". That's good disambiguation. But the two pages explain "Markov" differently. First Encounter: *"'Markov' is the mathematician's word for 'only the current neighbors matter, not the history'"*. MRH page: *"inspired by Markov blankets — … the minimal set of other nodes that makes it conditionally independent of everything else."* "Not the history" describes a memoryless process, which is a different idea from a blanket of neighbours. A beginner who later reads the MRH page will notice that the two explanations don't agree. (Also: the landing page says the project lead is "'dp' throughout this site", but the MRH page names him in full. It's not wrong, but a reader can't connect the two.)

**Internal references with no target.** Reader-facing copy cites artefacts a visitor cannot open: *"(equations.ts)"* (a source file), *"Session 53"*, *"Session 65"*, *"Session 66 script"* (Parameter Derivations), *"research proposal gamma_ncorr_sign_inversion_sharpness.md"* (γ Calculator), *"Finding: explorer/findings/the-parameter-ledger-is-unfalsifiable-and-the-knee-is-misplaced-by-25000x.md"* (Parameter Derivations). None is linked. Either link them to the public archive or drop them from body text.

**Acronyms.** Mostly handled well. The landing page has a ~30-entry abbreviation key ("SPARC = …; BTFR = …; dex = a factor of 10 …; CFD = computational fluid dynamics") linked from an "Abbreviations ahead" line before the first technical section, and CFD is also expanded in-line where used, with a disambiguation from "counterfactual definiteness". A2ACW is expanded in its section heading on Research Philosophy (its first appearance is in the track "Next:" button, which is unavoidable). The MRH page expands SPARC, BTFR and ΔBIC in a parenthetical on first use. Gaps I hit: Key Claims uses *T2\**, *T2*, *GRW/CSL* undefined (it does warn that "the rest of this page is written for readers with some physics"). The γ Calculator uses "dex" ("1.2 dex, against 1.17 dex") without defining it on that page, while its intro says the same thing as "about a factor of 15 in density". Use one unit, or define dex at first use.

- RG knee 0.0083, γ = 0.489: 1.195 (v +9.3% ✓). Marginal, ~2–3σ. At γ = 2 it is Newtonian.

So the level *is* seen, by the integral over cosmic time rather than by LLR's present-day derivative. Unlike LLR, this needs no assumption about the density gradient along the Sun's path. It's a third root for the density-keyed kill, independent of SPARC and LLR, and it should be checked before DR4 readers meet the "no local measurement" sentence. Caveat: it applies when the smoothing scale is large enough that the laboratory and the solar neighbourhood share one smoothed density. Under pointwise density, lab G is measured at Earth's density (C = 1), and the ephemeris argument on the page is the right one.

**For Researchers: distinct-content register and referee box.** This is the most useful thing on the site for someone like me. The referee box (no covariant action, no c_T derivation, no CMB computation, no lensing rule; *"'does it pass GW170817 or the CMB?' is not yet a well-posed question"*) is exactly what I'd write in a report, and it's good that the site wrote it first. One row I dispute: **"Dim ≥ 5 dispersion (quadratic only) — Below reach … quadratic term at the Planck scale sits ~10⁷ below current sensitivity … None reachable."** That is true for *time-of-flight*, which is the weakest handle on n = 2. Threshold and kinematic bounds are far stronger for dimension-6 operators. The reason is that at UHECR energies the lattice term k⁴a² exceeds m_p² by ~10⁶ (k ~ 10²⁰ eV, a = ℓ_Pl), so O(1)-coefficient Planck-scale n = 2 terms distort GZK kinematics. Maccione, Taylor, Mattingly & Liberati (2009) constrain dimension-6 proton/pion coefficients well below O(1) for some signs. LHAASO's PeV photons constrain superluminal n = 2 photon decay at E_QG,2 ~ 10¹⁵ GeV, already ~10⁴ closer to Planck than time-of-flight (all recall). Whether the grid is excluded depends on its sign per species: a nearest-neighbour lattice is subluminal for every field, so universal subluminality partly cancels in threshold reactions. That calculation is owed before "none reachable" is printed. It is the one place on the site where a reachable discriminating bound may exist and hasn't been checked.

**Dark Energy.** I checked the DESI DR2 w₀w_a rows against my memory of Table 5 (−0.42 ± 0.21 / −1.75 ± 0.58 for DESI+CMB; −0.838, −0.667, −0.752 with SNe; 2.8/3.8/4.2σ). They match. **Research Philosophy: is A2ACW sound?** The site's own answer is careful, and I agree with almost all of it. The 2026-05-22 control *"could not have returned a specificity above 0, so J = 0 carries no information about the instrument"*. The cross-vendor arm was *more lenient* than the Claude–Claude re-run (all 17 disagreements harsher in-house). The 0/9 audit was done by the same uncalibrated instrument class. My own reading as a referee: A2ACW is a sound consistency filter and an unsound discovery method. Everything that carries weight here came from coding against external data with an outside answer key, as the landing page says.

**Two Reframes: the T_scan survivor.** The g²(0) argument is neat. Every clock the archive names is shorter than a 125 ps photon, so a mixed window predicts g²(0) ≈ 1, against Schweickert 2018's 7.5×10⁻⁵. The page then keeps *"a scan period T_scan ≥ 1.6–9.4 µs … which turns 'scanning' into a random variable drawn once per particle"* as the survivor, and lists *"g²(0) against photon duration"* as the cheap test. But Grangier, Roger & Aspect 1986 has two halves. Besides the anticorrelation at a beam splitter, the same paper sends the same heralded single photons through a Mach–Zehnder and sees single-photon interference with visibility ~98% (recall). A photon that occupies one mode for its whole lifetime is a which-path hidden variable, and it gives no fringes. So the survivor is excluded by the second half of the paper the page already cites. This is the data version of Pass 3's mixture objection: once T_scan ≫ photon lifetime, the state is the mixture Σ pᵢ|i⟩⟨i|, and the off-diagonal terms that make fringes are gone. "Interference" does not occur on Two Reframes, nor (per Pass 3) on Key Claims or Measurement Without Observers. The R0 "instantaneous sampling" escape should get the same check: if it reproduces fringes, it carries relative phases, i.e. the amplitude vector.

**Honest Assessment (top), Chemistry, dSphs.** The plain summary says the cap case *"rests on one small dwarf galaxy, Draco"*. Deeper in, the page states the stronger version (classical dSphs as a class, M/L_V ≈ 100–300; Mateo 1998; Walker et al. 2009). The plain summary undersells the kill, and a referee would add the ultra-faints (Segue 1, M/L ~ 10³) as the obvious next lever. Minor inconsistency: the landing page says *"1,703 logged chemistry sessions"*, while the Chemistry Correlation Explorer says *"the archive's '1,703' is a type counter read at session #1,840, not a corpus size or a count of distinct phenomena"* (corpus 2,671).

### Tools Tested
- **Crossover Regime Visualizer**: works. One slider (0–4, step 0.01, default 1). Regime lookup at 0.6 / 1.4 (N ≈ 11.1 / 2.04, both correct for γ = 2/√N), and the fallback card handles γ = 4.0 correctly. It's honest about being an "artifact lesson" for a refuted map. The Ginzburg physics on the page is right for BCS: Gi ∝ (k_B/ΔC ξ₀³)², Gi ~ (T_c/E_F)⁴ ∝ N_ξ^(−4/3), and the 2026-10-06 correction of the exponent is correct. The error is extending "sharpest in nature" to BECs and superfluids (see friction log). Scientifically that matters, because He-4 is the counterexample that shows *which* variable orders transition width.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer**: works. The category counts sum (4 + 12 + 4 + 3 = 23). Sorting is by |r|, with untraced rows keyed −2 and sign-failed rows −1, so the electrical-conductivity row (r = −0.414, "WRONG SIGN") sinks below weaker successes. That's a defensible choice, but say so in the sort label. The page's own identity argument is correct: at fixed T, r(θ_D, 1/γ_phonon) = 1 exactly, and v_s, the moduli and θ_D are one Debye relation seen five ways. As a tool it is now a good teaching example of how proxies containing the target inflate r. It carries no physics claim, and it says so.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Does the density-keyed law at ≥ pc smoothing survive BBN/CMB bounds on G_early/G_lab at any knee that the galaxy fits used? If not, that's a third independent root, and it doesn't need LLR's gradient assumption.
2. What is the per-species sign of the lattice's quadratic dispersion, and does universal subluminality survive UHECR threshold kinematics at a = ℓ_Pl?
3. Is there any known construction, other than equilibrium Bohm, with a dynamically real but radiatively invisible foliation? If not, is "discrete + Bell-violating + Lorentz-invisible" a no-go worth writing up as a standalone note?
4. Which of the 12 forks would the project lead pick if forced today? Would the site commit, before DR4, to evaluating only that variant for six months?
5. The site's most transferable outputs are about *other* theories: the slaved-dark-energy no-interior-maximum corollary, Cassini vs compander/MOND δ families, the S1 companion-rate crux. Has any been sent to the relevant authors or written as a short note? A two-page "no crossing for ρ_DE = ρ_m F(ρ_m)" note would be citable now.

### Verdict
Nothing here is a confirmed novel prediction, and the site says so accurately. My audit pushes the other way from the usual "overclaim" complaint: two of the site's listed survivors (T_scan ≥ µs; the density-keyed amplitude at knees LLR spares) are probably already dead on data the site doesn't cite (GRA 1986's interference half; BBN), and one "unreachable" verdict (n = 2 LIV) was judged against the weakest bound class. The real value is a well-annotated map of how a tanh-compander / Cardassian / lattice-substrate program fails, plus a few class-level results worth publishing as notes. The frame problem is now governance, not data: until a canonical variant and dynamics are chosen, "what does Synchronism predict?" has no single answer, and no dataset, DR4 included, can change that.
