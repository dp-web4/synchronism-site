### 1. What assumptions am I inheriting from the primer and topic queue?

- That C(ρ) is salvageable as **either** compander or order parameter — implicitly, that the binary covers the relevant alternatives. (It might not. The 2026-04-09 explorer finding established "scaffolding" as a third option: heuristic generator of predictions with no commitment to physical content. - That the deep-page concessions ("tanh motivated, not derived"; "any sigmoid would serve equally") are the correct verdict. I think they are, but I want to test whether "any sigmoid would serve equally" is even **true** numerically, or just an honest hedge.
- That the visitor critique (P3 + P4 of 2026-05-12) correctly diagnoses the front-of-site equivocation. It does.

### 2. What if the current research frame is wrong?

The framing "is C(ρ) a compander or an order parameter?" might be the wrong binary. Three alternative framings:

- **C(ρ) is a relabeling of MOND's interpolating function** — established 2026-03-30. The "compander vs order parameter" question doesn't apply to a relabeling; the question is whether C(ρ) adds **any** content beyond ν(g/a₀). It doesn't, on galactic data.
- **C(ρ) is heuristic scaffolding** — established 2026-04-09. The compander vs order parameter binary treats C(ρ) as having some kind of *real* mathematical status that needs to be classified. Scaffolding is a third class: a generator of questions that doesn't itself participate in the answers.
- **C(ρ) is a placeholder for whatever the right object turns out to be** — most charitable framing. Then the binary "decision" is premature; the right action is to identify what the right object should look like (Hamiltonian, action, integration measure, conserved current, etc.) and admit C(ρ) doesn't have any of those properties yet.

The visitor pass — and the maintainer's seeded topic — both frame this as **a binary commit decision**. That framing presumes the decision is between two real categories. The deeper observation is that **the framework currently lacks the mathematical apparatus that would make either category meaningful**: there's no Hamiltonian (so "order parameter" is empty), and there's no information-theoretic constraint pinning the compander to a specific family member (so "compander" is also empty — companders without an analog-to-digital pairing or a perceptual-response curve are unconstrained).

### 3. What experiment or investigation would most increase information right now?

Numerical: tune the four compander candidates (tanh, Hill, logistic, erf, in log-density) to agree at midpoint and slope; measure their maximum deviation across the typical data range; report whether the deviation is larger or smaller than observational scatter on the framework's calibration data. If smaller, the compander family is approximation-equivalent within the framework's data precision — and the choice of tanh is empirically unjustified at best, decorative at worst.

Structural: identify which of the framework's surviving predictions **depend** on the specific functional form. The 2026-04-13 wide-binary-ghost-prediction finding already showed Hill and tanh give different EFE answers (1.31-1.39 vs 1.02-1.10). So compander choice IS load-bearing for tail-extrapolated predictions — even if it isn't for body-of-data calibration. This is the key tension the front-of-site has to acknowledge.

### 4. What would falsify the current research posture?

- Finding ΔAIC > 10 in favor of tanh on any concrete dataset — would falsify "any sigmoid would do" and force commitment to Frame A.
- Finding that all of the framework's surviving novel predictions are functional-form-invariant — would confirm Frame B and confirm that the choice of tanh is genuinely decorative.
- Finding that the framework's predictions on wide binaries, DESI, chemistry, etc. ALL depend sensitively on the functional form (i.e., Frame B is true but compander choice still matters for predictions) — would create a *new* category: "the framework's choice of functional form is unjustified AND load-bearing for predictions," which is a much worse epistemic position than either Frame A or Frame B as currently framed.

### Decision: Today's Investigation

I'll pursue both:

1. **Numerical test**: Tune tanh, Hill, logistic, erf to match at the half-saturation point and at one decade above. Measure maximum deviation across the canonical Synchronism data range (ρ ∈ [0.01 ρ_c, 100 ρ_c]). Report whether the family is approximation-equivalent within typical data scatter (~5-10%).

2. **Load-bearing analysis**: Identify which of the framework's surviving predictions are *sensitive* to compander choice and which are *invariant*. If the predictions split cleanly along this axis, that's the actual decision tree the site needs.

3. **Verdict on the binary**: commit to Frame B (compander) with the caveat that compander choice is load-bearing for tail-extrapolated predictions, and recommend the front-of-site rewrite.

I'll write findings to `findings/compander-family-equivalence-test.md` (numerical), and a recommendation document for the site at `findings/compander-vs-order-parameter-verdict.md`.

## Reflection

The session did what the topic asked: ran the numerical AIC/BIC comparison that the maintainer
seeded last night. The result is a clean Frame-B verdict on the binary, with a non-trivial
nuance the topic didn't anticipate (the family isn't uniformly degenerate — there are
sub-classes with different tail behavior).

Strategic implication: the framework's strongest *surviving* candidate for a discriminating
prediction (wide-binary EFE) is currently caught between the two compander sub-classes,
and the site has accidentally committed to both depending on which page you read. This is
a soluble problem — pick a compander, redo the EFE calc, commit to one number — but it
shouldn't be lost in the more abstract "compander or order parameter" framing.

**So what?** This advances discovery in two ways:
1. Eliminates the order-parameter framing as a serious candidate (subsumes 2026-05-08 and
   2026-05-10 findings into a single decisive numerical result).
2. Sharpens the wide-binary EFE issue from a "two-C problem" (2026-04-13) into a
   "two-equivalence-class problem" — a more actionable phrasing that maps directly onto
   a compander-comparison tool and a one-paragraph rewrite of the Phase Transitions page.

The investigation produced a positive result (Frame B confirmed) and exposed a non-obvious
follow-on (the equivalence-class structure matters for tail predictions). That's productive.
