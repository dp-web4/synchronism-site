## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, why-synchronism, first-encounter, core-idea, interactive-tools, coherence-explorer, galaxy-plotter, equation-walkthrough, consciousness-demo, dark-matter, honest-assessment; plus glossary and what-synchronism-is-not skimmed)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The most honest "my theory failed" page I've ever seen on the internet — I get *that* it failed within 30 seconds, but I never really got to be excited about the idea before being told it's dead.

### Journey

**Landing page.** Came in from Reddit expecting a "one equation explains everything" crank site. The headline is exactly that — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — and then the very next line is "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." That genuinely stopped me. It's disarming. I also liked being told straight away that AI agents do the research with a human (dp) steering. OK, weird, but at least I know what I'm reading.

The "single dial" sentence is good: how together a system's parts act, computed from how crowded it is. I can hold that in my head. MOND gets a parenthetical ("the 40-year-old rival gravity theory it keeps losing to") — perfect, that's all I need.

Then the page gets long and the scorecard arrives: "BTFR slope test, Dwarf dark-matter fractions, RAR shape (γ=2), Environment amplitude, Cassini/SPARC joint squeeze, Bell/CHSH construction check." I understand none of these six names. I understand the *count* (6 failed, 0 confirmed) and that's all a person like me takes away. Also "reparametrization" shows up a lot before I've figured out what it means — eventually I worked out it means "same thing as an existing theory with the letters changed," which is actually a neat concept, but I had to go to the glossary for it.

The equation `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))` is right there near the top. I don't know what tanh is. I skimmed past it. It didn't scare me off because the sentence next to it ("crowding a system causes its parts to act as one") did the work.

**Why Synchronism? (Step 1 of 6).** The crowd-vs-marching-band analogy is the best thing on the site. "Dimmer-switch curve" — also great. I now have a picture. Then, a note: this "coherence" is *not* quantum coherence, and superconductors score *low*. Huh? I thought a superconductor was the ultimate everything-in-lockstep thing (that's how YouTube explains it). The page tells me my intuition is wrong but I didn't come away knowing *why*, and it made me trust the marching-band picture less. Then the results table: "R²=0.0001", "Template Bias", "polynomial null model matches equally", and further down "Hamiltonian", "ansatz", "Abrikosov–Gor'kov". For Step 1 of a *Beginner* path, that's a wall. I skimmed everything after the table.

**First Encounter (Step 2).** "Presence (ρ)" and "Markov Relevancy Horizon — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter." The bubble explanation is fine; the name is intimidating. "γ = 2/√N_corr" — I can read a square root, so I roughly get "more particles moving together → smaller γ." But Step 1 already told me this exact relationship is "inverted for all collective systems" and "audited-negative." So why am I being taught it in Step 2 as if it's how things work? I felt like I was learning a formula that the previous page had already thrown away.

**Core Idea (Step 3).** Same equation a third time, same three symbols explained a third time, more failure notes ("best-fit γ dropping to 0.046 — switching its own density dependence off", "Hill-type saturation functions", "critical exponents"). By here I had déjà vu: steps 1–3 feel like the same page at three levels of technicality, each ending in the same list of failures. I stopped the path here. I did not want steps 4–6.

**Tools.** The tools index is clear and tells me where to start (Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment). Appreciated. See below.

**Dark matter page.** I went here because dark matter is the thing I actually know from PBS Space Time. It opens by calling dark matter "one of the most loaded terms in physics" and describes it as "unknown particles with mass but no electromagnetic charge" — fine, but I wanted the 2-sentence version of the *puzzle* first (stars at the edge of galaxies go too fast). The glossary's one-liner for dark matter is actually better than the page's intro. Then "ΔBIC=+184", "power spectrum peaks", "BAO". Verdict badge "Failed — Mechanism Under Revision" is clear.

**Honest Assessment.** It tells me up front it is ~18,000 words. I laughed and read the first screen. The badge definitions are short and good. I did not read further, and I doubt any casual visitor does. It's a reference document, which is fine, but the landing page sends me there about ten times.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** — worked (as far as I can tell from the page; my browsing can't physically drag sliders) — intuitive — Two sliders, "transition sharpness" and "reference density," with the crowd/marching-band text right beside the graph. Default γ = 0.49, readouts C = 0.3272 / 0.8259 / 0.9785 at 1×, 10×, 100× the reference density. "Gentle slope vs sharp snap" is exactly the right language. The presets confused me: "γ = 0.49 (SPARC galaxy fit)", "γ = 0.5 (MOND's simple function)", "γ = 2 (original framework value)" — I don't know why the framework's own number is 2 but the default is 0.49, or what it means that the fit is almost the same as MOND's. I suspect that *is* the story (it collapsed onto MOND), but the tool doesn't say so in words. Also: log scale is the default and I don't really know what a log axis is.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** — worked — mostly intuitive — This is the best page on the site for me. Pick a real galaxy, see the gray "Newton says it should drop" line and the blue dots that stay flat. *That's* the dark matter puzzle, in one picture. Then the violet line (the framework's real equation) visibly fails, the green MOND line fits, and there's an amber line labeled "the hand-tuned tanh(radius) stand-in previously displayed. It fits because it was drawn to fit." I love that they left their old cheat on the chart and labeled it. The readout "max C on this disk: 0.001 — inert by construction" lost me: I get "0.001 is tiny" but "inert by construction" needs a plain sentence ("galaxies are so thin that the dial never turns on").
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — looked at, hover-only — confusing purpose — Eight bars converge on C ≈ 0.499 and the page immediately tells me this means nothing because nobody can place a brain on the C axis, and that it's "the single least supported thing here." Respect for saying so, but as a casual visitor I don't understand why the tool exists then. "Artifact Lesson" as a status label isn't explained where I saw it.
- **Equation Anatomy** — worked (6-step click-through) — intuitive — "Like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1" finally told me what tanh does. The ln explanation (1,000× bigger → only ~7 more) is good too. This should be linked right where the equation first appears on the landing page. Then it tells me the ln is "cosmetic" and equivalent to a "Hill-type form" — I don't know what that is and it undercut the thing I'd just learned.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If it failed, and it's the same as MOND where it works — what is left that someone is still working on? The landing mentions an "untested ontology" (reality as a "discrete computational-fluid substrate") — that sounds like the actually interesting sci-fi-ish idea and I never found the plain-language page for it.
2. Why do superconductors score *low* on "coherence"? That's the opposite of everything I've heard.
3. Is MOND itself right, then? The site keeps losing to it — should I go read about MOND instead?
4. What does "reparametrization" of MOND mean in practice — did they accidentally rediscover it, or build it in without noticing?
5. AI agents audited AI agents, and the landing page says the auditors "false-flagged all 6 canonical discoveries" in a calibration test. So… can I trust the *failures* either? (I think that's the site's point, but I couldn't tell what to conclude.)
6. Why is the original γ = 2 but the galaxy fit is 0.49? What did the theory say it should be?
7. Nine demoted claims or six?

### Verdict
As a casual reader I came away knowing the headline result — one equation for everything was proposed, tested, and it lost, mostly to a 40-year-old theory called MOND — and I believe the site, which is more than I can say for most "theory of everything" pages. The marching-band analogy, the Galaxy Plotter, and the dimmer-switch explanation of tanh are genuinely good and could carry a beginner. But the Beginner Path isn't really for beginners: it repeats the same equation and the same autopsy three times in increasingly technical language, and it teaches me a formula one step after telling me that formula is dead. I'd come back for the Galaxy Plotter, not for steps 4–6.

Method note: wherever I report a count, a label mismatch or a link problem below, I checked it against the raw page HTML, not only against my reader tool's summary. Two things my reader tool reported turned out to be artifacts and are *not* logged as friction: literal `&apos;` entities on /tier-1-existing (the HTML is correctly escaped and renders fine), and a "missing" middle breadcrumb on /interactive-tools (it is a two-level trail by design).

### Journey

**Landing.** Ten top-nav items, and the labels are mostly good plain nouns. First structural observation: nav label ≠ destination title ≠ URL in several places. "Start Here" goes to a page titled "Why Synchronism?" at `/why-synchronism`; "Test Roadmap" lives at `/test-catalog`; "Equation Anatomy" is `/equation-walkthrough`; "Crossover Regime Visualizer" is `/phase-boundary-visualizer`. Each rename is individually explained somewhere, but a reader who is sent a URL and a reader who follows the nav will use different names for the same page. The landing does the badge thing right: "Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed — definitions on the Honest Assessment page," plus an inline one-line gloss of Reparametrization. Acronym handling is good at the top (MOND, MRH, A2ACW, BAO all expanded inline) and falls off inside the scorecard table: fDM, Vflat, Vmax, "μ-form", DESI, σ₈ appear bare. The H1 is "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" while the `<title>` and visual headline is the "What if one equation…" question; fine, but the section between the H1 and "Scientific Outcomes" has no headings at all, which is where Pass 1's reader lost the thread.

**Glossary.** 90 terms, alphabetical, Greek after Latin, badge terms clustered. This is a strong page. The two badge families are stated in one sentence each: MRH-relationship badges "describe where a claim sits in the research inventory"; descriptive badges describe empirical status. Two problems. (1) The family is *named after* MRH — "Active-MRH", "MRH-relationship" — but nothing I found explains why a term defined as "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" (glossary) / "the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system" (/mrh) is also the name of a project-management state. I can guess (the claim is inside the *research program's* current relevancy horizon), but I had to guess, and the /mrh page never mentions the badge usage. A physics term doing double duty as a workflow term, unexplained, is the single most avoidable piece of jargon on the site. (2) "Parallel-Paths: In the framework's parallel hypothesis space" defines the term with itself. /test-catalog's version — "kept in the hypothesis space, not in active focus, not abandoned" — is better; use that everywhere.

**Badge definitions drift between pages.** I compared the same badge on three pages. Audited-Negative is "durable **negative result**; does not move" (glossary), "durable **record**; does not move" (honest-assessment, which calls itself the *canonical reference*), and "durable record." (test-catalog, no "does not move"). Active-MRH is "currently in active research focus; being extended or revised" (HA) vs "under active investigation" (test-catalog). These are small, but the HA heading literally says "(canonical reference)" — if it's canonical, the others should be transclusions of it, not paraphrases. "Does not move" is also opaque on its own: move *where*? (Between badge states, presumably.)

**Learning Paths.** Eight paths (3 by difficulty, 4 by topic, plus a "Related Concepts" pseudo-path with two pages that are both already in Beginner). Step indicators on destination pages work and handle multi-path membership well — Honest Assessment shows both "Beginner Path — Step 5 of 6" and "Methodology Track — Step 6 of 6". The page is honest about coverage ("topic tracks are entry points, not complete coverage"). Fourteen pages sit in two paths; that's fine, but a reader who does Intermediate then Physics re-reads 3 of 7 steps with no "you've seen this" cue. The Beginner time estimate "~40 min (short) / ~2h 40m (full)" is driven almost entirely by one step (HA: "~4 min summary; ~80 min full") — good that it says so.

**Honest Assessment.** Has an "On this page" TOC — with 12 entries, while the page's H2 outline as my reader saw it had 7. Either five TOC targets (Research Outputs, Modified-Gravity Landscape, Cosmological Tensions, A2ACW Self-Audit, Bottom Line) are H3s/non-headings, or my reader truncated an ~18,000-word page. I couldn't settle which; flagging it as a thing to check rather than a defect. Scoreboard line: "0 confirmed predictions, 0 prospective predictions tested, 6 refutations executed." As a non-physicist I stopped here for a full minute. If zero prospective predictions were *tested*, what were the six things that got refuted? The answer (I think) is that the six were tests against already-existing data, i.e. not "prospective". But "prospective", "retrodiction", and "refutation" are never defined side by side on any page I visited — the landing uses all three as scoreboard subheadings ("Prospective predictions confirmed", "Refutations executed", "Post-hoc retrodictions attempted") with no gloss, and /test-catalog uses "prospective" and "kill criterion" without defining either beyond one clause. This trio is the scoreboard's entire grammar; it deserves three glossary entries and a one-line legend under the scoreboard.

**Test Roadmap (/test-catalog) and Tier 1.** Headline: "26 tests (24 in the original registry + 2 added later)". Tier tiles: 10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24. The Tier 1 tile says "10 tests"; click it and the Tier 1 page opens with "These **11** numbered tests (TEST-01 through TEST-10, plus TEST-25)… plus TEST-04a, a sub-test". So the same tier is 10, 11, or 12 depending on where you stand. The roadmap does reconcile this in prose further down (TEST-25/26 "added after the registry closed and are listed but not in this count"), and I found the explanation — but only because I went looking. The tile is what people read. Also unclear: which tier TEST-26 belongs to; the namespace note assigns 01–24 to tiers and then lists 25 and 26 as "plus". Separately, /tier-1-existing has one link to `/test-roadmap`, which 307-redirects to `/test-catalog` — works, but it's the one stray among four correct `/test-catalog` links on that page.

/tier-1-existing itself: an "Acronym key" exists (good!) and the status vocabulary is explicitly split into three linked families — "validation badges · operational states · status lifecycle verbs". That is the right instinct and the best evidence that someone has noticed the vocabulary problem. But it means a single test card can carry three labels from three taxonomies (e.g. a Failed badge + "Kill Criterion Triggered" + "Killed by the 2026-07-14 Environment Run"), and the definitions for the second and third families live one click away. The page is ~73,000 characters of visible text; much of it is dated bracketed corrections ("[Sign corrected 2026-06-06; previous '…']") inline in the card bodies.

**Breadcrumbs.** Present on every page I visited, consistent format, `aria-current` set correctly. But the middle segment is a link only when it is "Interactive Tools". "Start Here", "Core Theory", "Predictions & Experiments" and "Methodology" are plain `<span>`s — section names with no section page. So a breadcrumb like Home / Core Theory / MRH tells me a category exists but gives me no way to see its siblings. Also, the section taxonomy in breadcrumbs (Start Here, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments, Methodology, Interactive Tools) does not match the top nav (ten flat items) or the Learning Paths (eight paths). Three different organizing schemes for ~74 pages.

**/mrh.** Clean H1 with expansion, "Physics Track Step 2 of 7", badge "Speculative — Theoretical Framework". Expansion is consistently "Relevancy", never "Relevance" — good. The formal definition leads and the friendly "bubble" one (which the landing and glossary use) doesn't appear first; "Markov blanket" is used before it's explained.

**Key Claims.** Intro: "Where Synchronism says something new — not restatements in different notation, but claims that would advance understanding if confirmed." The three claims that follow are badged Speculative ("untestable as stated"), Speculative/"Untestable as stated", and Failed. The intro is accurate if you read "would… if confirmed" carefully, but the page title plus that sentence sets up an expectation the badges immediately reverse. One framing sentence would fix it. Note "Untestable as stated" and "currently unrunnable" are used like statuses but are in none of the three taxonomies. Undefined on first use: PRL, CPMG, SNARC, PCI*, SH0ES, BEC/BCS.

**For Researchers.** Strong opening line ("An arriving expert should not have to excavate the failure documentation to find what survives it"). Seven numbered/unnumbered artifacts, ~8,500 words, **no table of contents** — odd, given HA has one and this is the page built for people in a hurry. It has an acronym section, but ΛCDM and EFE appear above it. By my reader's count it carries 18 embedded revision dates.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (/gamma-calculator) — worked (static inspection; my browsing can't drag the slider) — intuitive once past the header — The input is well specified: N_corr is defined on-page ("the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units"), default 4, linear (1–1000) / log (1–10⁷) toggle, formula shown, regime label on the readout, reference table of N_corr → γ → regime. The page tells you to click presets and watch the contradiction appear, which is good instructional design for a tool whose point is a failure. Friction: the caveat that "preset N_corr values are back-fits" comes *after* the presets; "back-fit" isn't defined; and the footer "next steps" go to concept pages (The γ Parameter, Phase Transitions) rather than to the page that explains the inversion the tool just demonstrated.
