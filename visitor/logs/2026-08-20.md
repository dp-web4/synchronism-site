## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The one-line pitch is genuinely gripping and the honesty is refreshing, but the "beginner" path drops me into a failure ledger written for people who already know what MOND, BCS, and ΔBIC are.

### Journey

I clicked in from Reddit expecting crackpot bingo and got something else. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is exactly the kind of question a Veritasium episode opens with, and the follow-up line ("a single dial — how coherently a system's parts act together, computed from its density") is the clearest sentence on the entire site. Sixty seconds in, I knew what the bet was. That's a win.

Then the landing page shows me a scoreboard: **0** confirmed predictions, **6** refutations. My honest gut reaction was *"...so why am I reading this?"* I understand *now*, after clicking around for twenty minutes, that the point is the notebook and not the answer. But at first glance it reads like landing on a memorial page. There's a heading — "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" — that's supposed to fix this, but it's below the scoreboard. Put the framing before the score and I'd have known how to read the score.

I clicked **Beginner Path → Step 1 of 6**, expecting to be eased in. The crowd-vs-marching-band metaphor is great. Then within the same page I hit: MOND (1983 gravity modification), YBCO predicted 607K vs actual 93K, Bose-Einstein condensates, "audited-negative," "reparametrization," Tc, and ΔBIC. That's step 1 *of 6*. Whatever step 6 looks like, I'm not going to make it. This page isn't a beginner path — it's a summary of the whole project's results, wearing a beginner path's name tag.

**First Encounter** was much better and I wish it had been step 1. It opens with "*Coherence is how collectively a group of things behaves. A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence.*" — no equation at all, just crowd/band, crystal/gas, electron/neutron-star. I followed all of it. Then it introduced the **Markov Relevancy Horizon** as "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence" and I fell off a cliff. The *idea* — only your nearby neighbors matter — I get instantly. That sentence, I don't. (The glossary version, "an event horizon for influence — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," is fantastic and is what should be on the page. The good explanation exists; it's just filed somewhere I only found because I went looking.)

The single most confusing thing on the site, for me: **superconductors score LOW on "coherence."** I've watched multiple videos telling me superconductors and BECs are the most *coherent* things in the universe — that's the whole point of them. This site uses the same word to mean roughly the opposite, flags it as a caveat, and moves on. For a casual reader that's not a footnote, it's a trapdoor. Every intuition I brought with me is now inverted and I can't tell which pages have compensated for that and which haven't.

I tested three tools (below). The **Equation Anatomy** walkthrough is the best writing on the site — "tanh is like a dimmer switch," "a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger" — I actually understood the equation after that, which I'd have bet against. It's buried under /tools. It should be the link directly under the equation on the landing page.

Ended on **Honest Assessment**. The opening line is plain English and lands hard: *"When its galaxy predictions were tested against real telescope measurements, it failed every decisive test."* I respect that enormously. Then it explains the 6 refutations rest on "3–4 independent roots," one of which "tests an asserted constant rather than a mechanism" and one of which "is a theorem rather than external data" — and I lost the thread completely. I can't tell if this paragraph is the authors being extra-rigorous against themselves, or quietly walking the number down. I genuinely can't tell which, and that ambiguity bothers me more than either answer would.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If it's called "coherence" but superconductors score low on it, what is C actually measuring, in words I'd use at a pub?
2. What would a *win* look like? After ten pages I know what would kill this. I still don't know what would make it exciting.
3. Is 6 refutations a lot? Is a framework with 6 refutations and 0 confirmations dead, or normal-for-its-age? I have no yardstick.

### Verdict

But the on-ramp is backwards: the gentlest, best-written pages (First Encounter, Equation Anatomy, the glossary's plain-English definitions) are buried, while the beginner path opens with a results audit written in vocabulary I don't have. I'd come back if someone linked me a specific page, but I wouldn't have found my way here on my own, and I'd struggle to explain to a friend what the idea *is* as opposed to what it *failed at*.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The vocabulary is unusually well-governed for a site this size — there is a real glossary, a real badge taxonomy, and real redirects — but almost none of that governance is wired to the point of use, and every page on the site shares one browser title.

### Journey

I came at this as a docs audit, so I walked the nav bar left to right and then walked the Beginner Path end to end, checking breadcrumbs, prev/next links, first-use definitions, and cross-references as I went.

The first thing I do on any site is open six pages in six tabs. That immediately surfaced the largest structural problem here: **every tab said the same thing.** I sampled fourteen pages — landing, glossary, key-claims, mrh, dark-matter, galaxy-rotation, coherence-function, gamma-parameter, first-encounter, core-idea, why-synchronism, what-synchronism-is-not, gamma-calculator, phase-boundary-visualizer — and all fourteen return `<title>Synchronism | One Equation, Every Scale`. The site has ~74 pages and one title. Tabs, bookmarks, browser history, and search results are all indistinguishable. I found exactly one page with per-page metadata, `/for-researchers`, and its title is malformed: *"For Researchers: What's Citable — Synchronism | Synchronism"* — the brand appears twice. So the mechanism exists and is applied once, incorrectly. This is the single highest-leverage fix on the site and it is mechanical.

Navigation itself is sound. The top nav is eight items, flat, no dropdowns, and every one resolved. Redirect hygiene is genuinely good — `/tools → /interactive-tools`, `/beginner-path → /why-synchronism`, `/phase-boundary → /phase-boundary-visualizer` all return clean 307s, which means old links and guessed URLs don't break. I appreciated that; most sites this age have rot.

Breadcrumbs are where the information architecture stops being honest about itself. On `/first-encounter` the breadcrumb reads `Home / Getting Started / First Encounter` — but "Getting Started" is a `<span>`, not a link. I checked whether it *could* be one: `/getting-started` returns HTTP 307 **with no `Location` header** — a redirect pointing nowhere. `/mrh`'s breadcrumb says `Home / Core Theory / MRH` and `/core-theory` is a flat 404. So the middle tier of every breadcrumb names a section the reader is shown but cannot enter. A breadcrumb whose parent isn't clickable is decoration; it tells you where you are but not how to go up. Depth is inconsistent too: `/interactive-tools` is `Home / Interactive Tools` (two tiers) while its own child `/gamma-calculator` is `Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator` (three) — so "Interactive Tools" is a section when you're below it and a leaf when you're on it.

The Beginner Path prev/next chain, by contrast, is clean: step 2 offers `← Prev: Why Synchronism?` / `Next: The Core Idea →`, step 4 offers `← Prev: The Core Idea` / `Next: Honest Assessment →`. No dead ends, no loops. Good.

Then I hit the terminology problem, which is subtler and more interesting than a broken link.

This site has a **validation badge taxonomy** — two families, nine live tags, four deprecated, all defined at `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`, and — nicely — all also carrying individual glossary entries clustered together. That is more rigor than most commercial docs sites manage. And yet: **Beginner Path Step 1 uses the badges before anything defines them.** I pulled `/why-synchronism` and counted `audited-negative` once, `Reparametrization` once, `failed`/`Failed` ten times — and **zero links** to the badge definitions anchor. The canonical definitions live on Step 5. The landing page *does* link `Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed` straight to the anchor, so the pattern is established and available; it just isn't applied on the page where a first-time reader meets these words. Same on `/key-claims`, which is one nav-click from home, carries badges on all three claims, and has no on-page legend — plus one label, **"Post-hoc Fit to PRL 2024,"** that belongs to neither documented family.

Related and worse: **the glossary documents a feature I could not find.** It states *"Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline."* I checked `/core-idea`, `/first-encounter`, and `/why-synchronism` for any tooltip affordance — `<abbr>`, dotted underlines, `title=`, `aria-describedby`, `cursor:help` — and found none, and the glossary's own distinctive phrasing ("event horizon for influence") appears zero times on all three. Either the feature was removed and the sentence outlived it, or it never shipped. Either way the glossary is currently promising a cross-reference mechanism that isn't there, which is exactly the mechanism that would fix the badge problem above.

That inline-definition gap has a second victim: **acronyms are expanded by page, not by first use.** The landing page expands MOND, BAO, A2ACW, and MRH inline — good — while using SPARC, DESI, BTFR, CHSH, fσ₈, and ALFALFA–SDSS bare in the same document. All six have glossary entries. Nothing links first use to the entry. The content is there; the wiring isn't.

The MRH definition is a clean case study in what happens without a single source of truth. I found three variants: `/mrh` says *"the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system"*; `/first-encounter` says *"the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence"*; the glossary says *"an 'event horizon for influence' — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter."* Three phrasings of one term, and the plain-English one — the only one a beginner can parse — is the one filed furthest from the beginner.

Finally, a naming collision the site has already noticed and papered over rather than fixed. `/interactive-tools` groups its seven tools under headings **"Model Explainer," "Reparametrization," "Speculative."** Two of those three are validation badge names. The page opens with: *"Before the legend: this is a content grouping, not the site's validation badge taxonomy."* When you have to warn readers that a word doesn't mean the thing it means everywhere else on the site, change the word. "Reparametrization tools" could be "Where the model reduces to known physics"; "Speculative" could be "Unconstrained." The disclaimer costs a sentence on every read; the rename costs one edit.

Same class of issue, smaller: one artifact carries three names. The nav says **"Tools,"** the H1 and breadcrumb say **"Interactive Tools,"** the URL is `/interactive-tools`. The walkthrough is card-titled **"Equation Anatomy"** at `/equation-walkthrough`. Redirects mean nothing breaks — but a reader searching the site for "Equation Anatomy" and a reader who bookmarked "equation walkthrough" don't know they have the same page.

Path aliasing is asymmetric: `/beginner-path` redirects helpfully, while `/advanced-path`, `/intermediate-path`, `/physics-track`, and `/methodology-track` all 404. If one guessable path URL is worth a redirect, all six are.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — intuitive, well-documented — Single input (N_corr, slider plus presets), clear output (γ, a classification label, a live C(ρ) curve). Documentation-wise this is the strongest tool page I saw: it defines its input in the reader's terms ("the count of particles that move as a correlated unit — dimensionless, no units"), states the formula's provenance honestly ("motivated, not rigorously derived"; the 1/√N scaling is "a dimensional ansatz"; the prefactor 2 has no first-principles justification), and leads with the status rather than burying it: **"Formula Audited-Negative — Sign Inverted for All Collective Systems."** My one note is a terminology one: this page is where a reader is most likely to first meet "audited-negative," and the phrase is not linked to its definition here either.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — intuitive, unusual framing — Opens with *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose."* The status is unmissable before you touch a control, which is the correct ordering and the opposite of what most sites do. The instruction *"your task is to catch the formula being wrong at every stop, not to learn its placements"* is genuinely good instructional design — it gives the reader a task, not just a warning. ### Unanswered Questions

1. What is the intended single source of truth for a term's definition — `/glossary`, `src/lib/terms.ts`, or whichever page introduces it? Three MRH variants suggest there isn't one enforced.
2. Was the hover-tooltip feature removed, or never built? The glossary sentence is the only trace I can see from outside.
3. Are the breadcrumb section names ("Getting Started," "Core Theory") supposed to become pages? `/getting-started` returning a 307 with no Location looks like an abandoned route, not a deliberate dead end.
4. When a page belongs to two learning paths, which path's "Next →" wins — and does the site track which path I arrived on?
5. Is "Post-hoc Fit" an informal descriptor or a badge candidate? If badges are the site's epistemic contract, ad-hoc labels beside them dilute it.

### Verdict

The underlying information architecture is better than the site's surface suggests: real redirects, a real glossary with real badge entries, clean prev/next chains, and status labels that lead rather than hide. The failure mode is consistently the *last mile* — definitions exist but aren't linked from first use, sections are named in breadcrumbs but aren't reachable, a cross-reference feature is documented but absent, and per-page metadata is supported but applied to one page out of seventy-four. None of these are content problems; they're wiring problems, and the highest-value ones (per-page titles, badge links at point of use) are mechanical enough to fix in an afternoon.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The self-audit is better than most published papers — the algebra I could check is correct and the reparametrizations are named — but the framework has no single force law, its "mean-field motivation" carries zero information, and its test suite is one-sided by construction, so the headline "0 confirmed / 6 refuted" is closer to a theorem about the test design than a measurement of nature.

### Journey

I went straight past the onboarding to `/coherence-function`, `/gamma-parameter`, `/key-claims`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/dark-matter`, and `/for-researchers`, and checked every piece of algebra I could do by hand.

**The identity is correct.** `tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]` — verified directly from `tanh u = (e^u − e^{−u})/(e^u + e^{−u})` with `e^u = (1+x)^γ`. And at γ = 1/2 it collapses to `x/(x+2)`, which is exactly `μ_simple(x/2)` — Milgrom's simple interpolating function with a rescaled argument. The site says this out loud. That is the single most important sentence on the site and it is stated plainly rather than buried. Credit where it's due: I came in expecting to have to *find* the reparametrization, and instead found it labelled.

Other arithmetic I checked and found correct: the C = 0.50 crossing at 2.068·ρ_crit for γ = 0.49 (atanh(0.5)/0.49 = 1.121, e^1.121 − 1 = 2.068 ✓); TEST-09's 3.3σ from combining 0.07 and 0.10 in quadrature (0.40/0.122 = 3.28 ✓); the consciousness t-statistic (0.14/(0.0196/√8) = 20.2 ✓); the a₀ prediction cH₀/2π = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ at H₀ = 67.4 ✓; the 630× chain-of-custody gap between A = 4.6×10⁻⁵ and A = 0.029 ✓; and TEST-08's Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) = 119 M☉/pc², ~4% from Freeman ✓. I found no arithmetic errors. The a₀ tension is stated as "the 0.16 offset is 0.7σ against the systematic — consistent," which is right (0.16/0.24), and the page explicitly refuses to score it because "it is Milgrom's own 1983 coincidence." That is unusually disciplined.

So the problems aren't arithmetic. They're structural, and there are five that I think matter.

**1. There is no single force law, and two of the six refutations live in a branch the site's own flagship plot doesn't use.** The Galaxy Curve Plotter computes `v(r) = √(v_b² + [V_flat·C(ρ)]²)`. The boost-ceiling refutations (TEST-09, TEST-10) compute `f_DM = 1 − C`. The plotter page names this — "a fork in the coupling law … opposite effects from the same symbol" — but does not follow the consequence through. These aren't two conventions. Under quadrature, C → 0 means *no* enhancement (the violet curve collapses onto the baryon line). Under f_DM = 1 − C, C → 0 means the boost *diverges*. The same symbol at the same value means "no dark matter" in one branch and "all dark matter" in the other. That is a sign inversion in the coupling, not a bookkeeping choice.

This propagates directly into the ledger. `/honest-assessment` says six refutations reduce to "3–4 independent roots" because TEST-09 and TEST-10 share the bounded-boost ceiling B ≤ 1/Ω_m. But B ≤ 1/Ω_m is a statement in the f_DM = 1 − C branch only — the ceiling isn't even *definable* in the quadrature branch, where the enhancement saturates at V_flat rather than at a multiple of the baryonic term. So one of the 3–4 roots is conditional on a force law the site has not selected. The root count should carry that condition on its face.

**2. The mean-field motivation for tanh transfers the shape and none of the physics.** `/coherence-function` motivates tanh from the Ising self-consistency relation `m = tanh(βJzm)`. In that equation the argument of tanh *is the order parameter*, and that self-reference is the entire content: it is what produces a bifurcation at βJz = 1 and therefore a phase transition. In `C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1))` the argument is exogenous — a log of density. There is no β, no J, no coordination number, no self-consistency, and consequently no bifurcation anywhere in the domain. The site calls this "motivated, not rigorously derived," which understates it: the motivation constrains *nothing*. It does not fix γ, it does not fix the log argument, it does not even single out tanh over any other bounded odd sigmoid. A weak derivation and a null derivation are different epistemic objects and the page files this one under the wrong heading.

**3. C(ρ) is strictly concave in ρ. There is no S-curve in the physical variable.** Differentiating twice: `C'' = −γ·sech²(u)·(2γ·tanh u + 1)/[ρ_crit²(1+x)²] < 0` for every γ > 0 and every x ≥ 0. The function has no inflection point in ρ at all — it rises from slope γ at the origin and bends over monotonically. The Coherence Explorer defaults to a log₁₀(ρ) x-axis, on which the curve does look sigmoid; the linear toggle is where the actual shape lives. So the tool's copy — "higher γ = sharper, more abrupt snap to coherent" — describes an artifact of the axis choice, not a property of the model. To the site's credit, `/coherence-explorer` does state that ρ_crit is "a *saturation knee*, not a critical density in the phase-transition sense." But that one sentence is fighting an entire surrounding vocabulary: "phase boundary," "transition sharpness," "snap," "Phase Boundary Visualizer." A condensed-matter reader arrives expecting an order parameter and finds a compander. The honest description is that this is a bounded logarithmic compression function, and every "transition" word on the site is borrowed against a bifurcation that doesn't exist.

**And the thing I'd actually want the program to do.** Pass 3 asked whether any regime exists where the model is not nested in MOND. There is one, it is a single parameter, and it is currently hidden inside a piece of notation the site describes as a convenience.

`/equation-walkthrough` explains the "+1" in ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1) as being there "to ensure the argument remains positive even when density approaches zero, preventing undefined logarithm values." That is true and it undersells the term completely. The "+1" sets the entire deep-field limit. Generalize the regulator to C_n = tanh(γ·ln(1 + x^n)), whose deep limit is C ≈ γ·x^n. Using the site's own identification of C with the MOND interpolating function (the γ=1/2 collapse C = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) makes this the framework's convention, not mine), the deep-field force law becomes γ·(g/a₀)^n·g = g_bar, so

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;g = (g_bar·a₀^n/γ)^{1/(n+1)}, and V² ∝ r^{(n−1)/(n+1)}.

Three consequences fall straight out:

1. **Asymptotically flat rotation curves force n = 1 exactly.** The "+1" is not a convenience — it is the unique regulator compatible with flat outer curves. That is *why* the framework is a strict submodel of MOND: the nesting is imposed by the regularization, not by the tanh and not by γ.
2. **At n = 1, V⁴ = G·M·a₀/γ — BTFR slope exactly 4**, MOND's value. So the coherence function *by itself* predicts slope 4, and every deviation toward the framework's claimed 3.35 comes from B_max, the asserted constant. TEST-09 and TEST-10 never tested the coherence function at all. They tested an assertion bolted onto it.
3. **n is the non-nested direction, and it is measurable.** The outer log-slope of a rotation curve is d ln V/d ln r = (n−1)/(2(n+1)). SPARC outer slopes are consistent with zero to roughly ±0.05, which already pins n to about [0.82, 1.22] — but that constraint has never been *applied* as a framework test, because n has never been treated as a parameter. MOND has no freedom here: n = 1 is built into its deep limit by construction. A framework that lets n float is genuinely not nested, and a stacked outer-slope measurement on SPARC or a larger sample would either detect n ≠ 1 (a positive result MOND cannot accommodate) or tighten the nesting to a stated precision (a real, quotable bound). That is a registrable Tier-1 test, on existing free data, that could *select* the framework. It does not appear anywhere in the 26-test catalog.

Also note that a₀ and γ enter the deep limit only through the ratio a₀/γ. So the free-γ SPARC fit "converging to 0.489" is not an independent measurement of γ; it is conditional on a₀ being fixed a priori to cH₀/2π — which the site correctly identifies as Milgrom's own number. Two of the framework's headline quantities are one quantity, and the concordance between them is not evidence.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — **scientifically the most accurate tool page on the site** — I went in expecting to have to point out the γ/ρ_crit degeneracy and found it already stated, and stated correctly: "C depends on ρ and ρ_crit only through the single ratio x = ρ/ρ_crit, *exactly*, not just asymptotically," with ρ_crit named a pan control carrying no shape information. The readouts are right (C(ρ_crit) = 0.3272 at γ = 0.49; atanh(0.5)/0.49 = 1.1210, e^1.1210 − 1 = 2.068, so the C = 0.50 crossing at 2.07·ρ_crit checks out), and the page explains the asymmetry by naming the "+1" regulator as its cause. That is the correct diagnosis and it is the only place on the site that treats the regulator as physics rather than notation. My objection is what the tool *doesn't* expose: the regulator is hard-coded. Two sliders vary the parameters that don't change the model class, and the one term that does — the exponent inside the log — isn't a control. Add an `n` slider and this becomes the site's discrimination tool instead of its explainer.
- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — worked — clear, but wrong about the most important term — The decomposition is pedagogically good and the ρ_crit caveat ("not the midpoint of C"; C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88 at γ = 2) is exactly the right thing to flag. But the "+1" is explained as preventing a log divergence at ρ → 0, which is the least of what it does. It is the term that makes C ≈ γ·(ρ/ρ_crit) linear in the deep limit, and linearity in the deep limit is precisely what reproduces MOND's √(g_bar·a₀). The site's central self-criticism — "we are a strict submodel of MOND" — is a direct consequence of this one character, and the page that teaches the equation attributes it to numerical hygiene. Two sentences would fix it and would tell a researcher where to push.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Has anyone fit CPL to mock observables generated from the framework's own H(z)? Until that exists, TEST-26 compares an analytic derivative to a fitted contour, and the no-go is unproven in the variable DESI measures.
2. If Completion A is exactly Einstein–de Sitter, why is it not counted as a refutation — and if it is discarded, is Completion B stated anywhere as the unique surviving branch?
3. Where does B_max = 1/Ω_m come from? Pass 3 asked this and I'll second it harder: it carries three of six refutations, it is a cosmological ratio applied as a local galactic ceiling, and `/parameter-derivations` says plainly that no document derives it. The three "refutations" are a falsification of this constant, which is a fine result — but it should be *named* as that.
4. Has anyone measured n? The outer log-slope test is free, runs on data already on the site, and is the only construction I found where the framework can differ from MOND rather than tie or lose.
5. Does the framework claim the objective-collapse class? "Irreversible MRH contraction" either is a real non-unitary process — in which case CSL/GRW bounds already apply and no C-calibration is needed — or it isn't, in which case "explains definite outcomes" should come down.
6. What is the false-positive rate of A2ACW? Every control reported is a miss-rate. Without a specificity arm the 1.4% is a number without a denominator.
7. Why does the site's only affirmative win condition rest on an effect (dynamical decoupling) that standard theory predicts and labs demonstrate routinely? Was the prior art checked, given how rigorously it is checked on the kill side?

### Verdict

This is the most epistemically honest physics site I have read, and its honesty is currently pointed in one direction. The kill criteria get attribution discipline, systematics discounts, retraction notices, and prior-art checks; the single confirm criterion got none of those and turns out to be a 1998 result. The same asymmetry runs through the scoreboard: "0 confirmed" is correctly diagnosed as structural, while "6 refuted" is quoted as a measurement even though the footnotes underneath it disqualify five of the six on the site's own criteria. Point the audit at the audit and the ledger reads "one asserted constant, falsified three ways, plus a program-level methodological null" — which is smaller, true, and publishable. The genuinely useful finding is that the framework's nesting inside MOND is imposed by a single character of notation: the "+1" is the unique regulator giving flat rotation curves, which means the exponent inside that logarithm is a one-parameter escape from the submodel, is already loosely bounded by SPARC outer slopes, and is a test MOND structurally cannot take. That is a research program. It is currently filed as a footnote about avoiding division by zero.
