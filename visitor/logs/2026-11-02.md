## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (home, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /consciousness-demo, /honest-assessment, /what-synchronism-is-not)

### Journey
I came over from Reddit. The headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", is exactly the kind of thing I click. The next line, "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated.", stopped me cold, in a good way. I've never seen a big-idea site admit it lost in the second sentence, and it made me trust it more. The plain line "we ran six tests that could have shown it wrong, and it lost all six" is the clearest sentence on the site.

Then the homepage turns into a wall of terms. A box says "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC)". Next comes a paragraph about "Which C the galaxy numbers used", with "γ = 0.49, ΔBIC +184 at γ = 2 by point" and an "Ωm-floored variant". All of this sits *above* the "Key Claims" teaser and *before* the abbreviation list that defines it. Warning me that abbreviations are coming doesn't help much if the definitions arrive later. I skimmed past and found the scoreboard: 0 confirmed, 6 refutations, 1 underpowered retrodiction, 1 withdrawn, 0 of 9 audited claims surviving. I liked that the "Research Activity" counts (3,308 sessions, 14,610 galaxies) are labelled "process volume, not evidence". That's honest. It also made me wonder why they're on the front page at all.

I clicked "Start here: Why Synchronism?". It opens with "Physics has a fragmentation problem." Good, I get that. The crowd vs. marching band idea and the "S-curve from independent to collective" landed. The page is about 2,500 words, though, and it's full of names I don't know: Debye model, YBCO, Tully–Fisher, R², Hill/Naka–Rushton/Langevin. I noticed the "What Worked" section was renamed because its results got downgraded to failed. Oddly, that kept me reading.

/first-encounter is the best page for someone like me. It has lettered sections A–G, a progress bar, the marching band, a dimmer-switch picture of tanh, and the bubble-of-neighbours idea for MRH ("an atom doesn't feel Andromeda"). The "Expert note" and "For the curious" sections are clearly marked as skippable, which I appreciated. I still bounced off "ln", "√N_corr", "ρ_crit = A × V_flat²", BCS and BEC. Even so, it's the one page where I felt the idea click.

The "Next" button took me to the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**, the most satisfying thing on the site. "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" told me exactly what to look at. The blue dots are real measurements, the gray line is what visible matter alone predicts, green is MOND (which fits), and the violet line is *their* equation, which doesn't fit. I finally understood what "the dark matter problem" looks like. I also understood that the site is showing me its own failure on purpose.

I checked the Interactive Tools page and tried the two other "Beginner" tools, the **Coherence Explorer** and the **γ Calculator**. I also looked at the **Consciousness Threshold Demo**, because consciousness is the topic that brought me here.

Last, I opened /honest-assessment. It describes itself as "~18,000 words", and I only read the "If you read nothing else" box, which is fine and readable. It also opens by saying every audit "was made by AI agents". That's a big deal to me and I wish the homepage said it as plainly. /what-synchronism-is-not has a great plain-words top section and eight "is not" cards, including "No academic journal has checked it" and "the checking so far was done by AI models reviewing each other". After that it's QFT, CHSH, Hořava–Lifshitz and comagnetometers.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked. Intuitive. The "look at three things" intro is the best onboarding on the site, and I got the dark-matter gap in one glance. There are no sliders, only five galaxy choices and two optional overlays. It clearly says the dots are real SPARC data.
- **Coherence Explorer**: worked (from the page's description; I can't move sliders through my browser). Mostly intuitive. The presets (γ = 0.046 "nearly flat", 0.49 "what galaxies prefer", ½ "same curve as MOND's formula", 2 "original guess, refuted") tell a little story. The default key values (C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, etc.) are just numbers to me. The tip "the dotted marker is not where C = 0.5" confused me more than it helped.
- **γ Calculator**: worked. Somewhat intuitive. "A γ formula kept to show how it fails" is a fun framing. The punchline is that the formula makes an ideal gas the "sharpest" and a superconductor the "flattest", which is backwards. I could follow that by comparing two presets. The default output is Ncorr = 4 → γ = 1, C(ρcrit) = 0.60. I understood the deeper caveats (back-fitted presets, inconsistent galaxy Ncorr) only vaguely.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (bonus): a static chart with hover only. It was surprisingly educational. Eight "approaches" all land near 0.50, and the page explains this is because they were all built the same way and basically pick the middle of a 0–1 scale, not because consciousness has a threshold. That's a nice lesson about fooling yourself.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the main equation lost every test, what is the project still trying to find out? I couldn't find a one-sentence "what we're doing next" for non-physicists.
2. "Coherence is not quantum coherence," so what is it, physically, for a real thing like a glass of water? Density of *what*?
3. If the galaxy part "just matches MOND", is MOND right? Does the site think dark matter exists?
4. Who are the humans here? The AI-agent auditing is mentioned, but who decides what counts as a refutation (the "pending the project lead" note)?
5. Why keep the consciousness pages if there's no way to test them yet?

### Verdict
The honesty is the hook. "We tested it and it didn't hold" plus the galaxy plotter made me trust this more than any "theory of everything" link I've clicked on Reddit, and /first-encounter is a genuinely good beginner page. The homepage loses people like me in the first scroll, though: it front-loads ΔBIC, Ωm-floored variants and an acronym list before it has earned my attention. I'd send a friend straight to /first-encounter and the plotter, not to the homepage or "Start here".

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (home, /why-synchronism, /learning-paths, /glossary, /interactive-tools, /equation-walkthrough, /crossover-regime-visualizer, /test-catalog, /tier-1-existing, /honest-assessment, /key-claims, /mrh). Its main defect is *ordering*: definitions, legends and disclosures exist but sit below or away from the first use.

### Journey
I started at the homepage and inventoried the header nav. It has ten items: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1, Test Roadmap, Interactive Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment, plus the logo. "First Encounter" and "Key Claims" appear only in the footer, although Pass 1 found /first-encounter the best entry page and the homepage teases Key Claims at about 7% of the page.

**Acronyms on the homepage.** The homepage has a real abbreviation key with about 30 entries, and the definitions are good, plain-language and one line each ("ΔBIC — a model-comparison score (positive = the framework fits worse; >10 is decisive)"). The problem is position. Measured by text offset, the "Abbreviations ahead" warning sits at about 11% of the page, the "Which C the galaxy numbers used" paragraph (ΔBIC, γ, Ωm, Cρ/Cg/Ca) at about 12%, and the key itself at about 40%, under "Scientific Outcomes". So the reader meets a third of the page's terms before the definitions. Terms used on the homepage but absent from the key include **DM** (in "Dwarf DM fractions"; only fDM is keyed), **CDM** (link text "CDM Discrimination"; only ΛCDM is keyed) and **BIC** (only inside ΔBIC). All three are in the glossary, but the homepage doesn't link them.

**Disclosure placement.** Pass 1 wrote that the AI-agent authorship wasn't visible on the homepage. It *is* there: "Who did this work. The research was carried out by AI agents arguing with each other — ~3,300 sessions — with one human (dp) overseeing the badge taxonomy. No outside physicist has reviewed these claims." But it is a bullet at about 30% of the page. So this is a findability problem, not a missing disclosure. A first-time reader decides what kind of document this is long before reaching it.

**Breadcrumbs.** They are present on every inner page I checked (16 of 16) and work. The hierarchy is inconsistent, though. "Methodology" is a link (to /research-philosophy), while "Core Theory" (on /mrh, /parameter-derivations) and "Predictions & Experiments" (on /test-catalog, /tier-1-existing) are plain text with no landing page. None of these three categories appears in the header nav, so the breadcrumb describes a hierarchy the menu doesn't show.

**Learning Paths** is well built. It has three difficulty paths and four topic tracks, with time estimates and an honest note that the two groupings don't cover the same pages ("difficulty paths reach 10 pages no topic track covers; topic tracks reach 14 the difficulty paths don't"). It also sends the beginner to only the "If you read nothing else" box of the 18,000-word Honest Assessment. That's a good fix. Inner pages carry "Physics Track · Step 2 of 7 · ← Prev / Next →" (on /mrh), which is exactly the wayfinding I'd want. Two flags: the topic tracks don't state an audience level, and the Beginner path's step 1 is still /why-synchronism, which Pass 1 found too dense.

**Badge system.** This is the site's most designed piece of IA, and the most complicated. The glossary has a "label map" with five separate vocabularies:
- **Validation badges**: nine, in two families (five MRH-relationship, four descriptive), plus deprecated Validated and Strongly Supported
- **Operational states**: Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Unrunnable as stated, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson
- **Lifecycle verbs**
- **Tool Kind**
- **Tool Level**

/interactive-tools explicitly separates Level, Kind, Status and "Badge on its page", and styles Status square-cornered and amber so it can't be mistaken for a level. That explains Pass 1's "Failed vs Artifact Lesson" confusion: they are different axes, both shown. The canonical legend is on /honest-assessment, with a jump link ("Badge definitions (canonical)") in the top table of contents. Good.

The defect is at the point of use. On tool pages the badge chip is a `<span>` with a `title=` tooltip ("Audited-Negative: Closed audit finding on a historical track…"), not a link. Hover-only text is invisible on touch screens and to keyboard users, and nothing tells you to hover. Only /key-claims links its badges to the glossary entries (#failed-badge etc.). The homepage and /why-synchronism link to the legend in body text, but the chips themselves don't. "Audited-Negative" in particular is a coined term that a reader can't decode from the words.

**Naming drift.** These are small but real:
- The header nav and page title say **"Test Roadmap"**, but the URL is /test-catalog, and the glossary label map still says "Test cards (Tier 1, **Test Catalog**)".
- /tier-1-existing says "**Top 5 Tests**", while that page is now titled "Decisive Tests: Status".
- The Ncorr symbol renders as **Ncorr**, **N_corr** and **N corr** (subscript lost in text extraction) on the same pages. The glossary headword is "N_corr".
- "36 tests" (fractal-bridge closure on /honest-assessment and /parameter-derivations) sits near "26 tests" (the Test Roadmap). They are unrelated counts, but neither sentence says which "tests" it means.

**Glossary.** It has about 130 entries with letter anchors, and every one of the 32 `/glossary#…` links I found across 18 pages resolves. One anchor defect: **a₀** and **a₀′** both emit a secondary ASCII anchor `id="a"`, a duplicate id in one document, so `#a` lands on a₀ even when a₀′ was meant. γ, η and ξ get no ASCII anchor at all. ΛCDM's ASCII anchor is `#cdm`, which is fine but means there's no separate "CDM" entry for the homepage's "CDM Discrimination".

### Tools Tested
- **Equation Anatomy** (/equation-walkthrough): worked (Prev/Next navigation, "Step 1 of 8"). Intuitive as a tour. The variable legend before Step 1 is excellent documentation practice: each symbol gets one plain line, and ρcrit includes "the 'crit' in the name is historical", a rare honest naming note. The "Audited-Negative" warning for γ = 2/√Ncorr comes *before* Step 1, which is the right order. It isn't really interactive (no inputs, no computed output), so the "tool" label oversells it a little. Many downstream terms (SPARC, RAR, kill criterion, "density-keyed") appear without links.
- **Crossover Regime Visualizer** (/crossover-regime-visualizer): worked. One γ slider (0–4, default 1.0) moves a marker across three bands (<0.6, 0.6–1.4, >1.4), and a card updates for each band. Clear "How to use it" instruction, and a nicely framed task: "catch the formula being wrong at every stop". Good that the page documents its own rename ("Formerly 'Phase Boundary Visualizer'; URL carried that name until 2026-10-23") and acknowledges that its enzyme placement (γ ≈ 0.85) disagrees with the γ Calculator's enzyme preset (Ncorr = 30, γ ≈ 0.37). As a writer, though, I'd say two tools disagreeing about the same example is confusing even when disclosed. The γ > 1.4 band lists "ideal gases" next to a "registered galaxy value — refuted" marker, which reads as if galaxies behave like ideal gases. Ncorr is undefined on the page (see friction log).

### Unanswered Questions
1. Is there an index page for "Core Theory" and "Predictions & Experiments"? The breadcrumb implies there should be.
2. Why five label vocabularies (badge, operational state, lifecycle verb, Kind, Level)? Could operational state and lifecycle verb merge for a lay reader? The label map is good, but its existence suggests the taxonomy has outgrown what the chips can carry.
3. Which page is canonical for badge definitions: /honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions or the /glossary badge entries? Both exist. The homepage points to the first, /key-claims to the second.
4. Should "Start Here" mean /why-synchronism (2,100 words, dense) or /first-encounter (1,900 words, scaffolded with a progress bar)?

### Verdict
Structurally this is one of the more carefully documented research sites I've audited. It has an explicit label taxonomy, a canonical legend, redirects for renamed URLs, step indicators on track pages, and a glossary whose cross-page anchors all resolve. The failures are about *sequence and point-of-use*, not absence. The homepage defines its abbreviations after using them and buries its most important disclosure, and badge chips don't link to the definitions that exist. Two component-level fixes would clear most of this pass's medium items: link the badge chips, and normalize the breadcrumb middle tier.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (home, /parameter-derivations, /interactive-tools, /galaxy-rotation, /honest-assessment (first ~100k characters of 175k), /dark-energy, /coherence-explorer, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /test-catalog, /gamma-boundary, /galaxy-plotter, /mrh)

- **Would return?**: maybe. I'd come back as a case study in how a framework audits itself, not for the physics.
- **Overall impression**: The site has already done most of the debunking I came to do. Every algebraic identity and number I checked by hand holds up. What's left is a few significance figures I can't reproduce, one tool that never exercises the equation it claims to show, and a cosmology sector that is ΛCDM by construction.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link as an "interesting AI project". I expected a grand-unification crank site and went straight to /parameter-derivations to find where the tanh came from. I didn't get the mean-field derivation I was ready to take apart. The page says first that there is none. It contrasts C(ρ) with the Ising self-consistency m = tanh(βJzm), says C(ρ) "has no such self-consistency loop", calls the form "a phenomenological choice" from the compander/sigmoid family, and says "No archive document motivates the log itself." It also states that this is not Landau theory: no critical point, no diverging ξ. That disarmed me. I'd have written exactly those sentences in a referee report.

**Algebra I checked by hand (all correct):**
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1]. At γ = ½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). So the γ = ½ compander is MOND's simple μ with a₀ → 2a₀′. Correct.
- Deep regime of the implicit fit g_bar = g_obs·tanh(γ ln(1 + g_obs/a₀′)): g_obs → √(g_bar·a₀′/γ). Only a₀′/γ is identifiable there, so it makes sense that the page reports a₀′/γ ≈ 1.07–1.09×10⁻¹⁰ and not a₀′. In the Newtonian regime, 1 − C ≈ 2(g/a₀′)^{−2γ}, so for γ ≤ ½ the anomalous acceleration doesn't decay. That's why TEST-25 (Cassini) is a failure shared with simple μ, and the page books it that way.
- A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²) with G = 4.30×10⁻³ pc (km/s)² M☉⁻¹ and R₀ = 8 kpc gives 4.6×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻². The page's own audit says the same: about 600× below the 0.029 actually used. Correct.
- a₀ = cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s², and Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) ≈ 119 M☉/pc². Both correct. The page labels both Reparametrization and cites Milgrom, McCulloch, Verlinde and Smolin as prior art. Right call.
- /gamma-boundary: dC/dx = 4γ t^{2γ−1}/(t^{2γ}+1)², C′(0) = γ, |C″(0)| = γ, κ(0) = γ/(1+γ²)^{3/2} with its maximum at γ = 1/√2. Concave on x ≥ 0 for every γ: the log-derivative numerator is (2γ−1) − (2γ+1)t^{2γ} ≤ −2. All correct. The page retracts the "γ ≈ 1 maximizes curvature" rationale, and that's right too. The maximum is at 0.71, and in any case κ depends on the units of x.
- Coherence Explorer key values at γ = 0.49: C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785, midpoint at 2.07ρcrit. Peak slopes are 0.58 per decade at γ = ½ and 0.86 at γ = 2. I reproduced all of them.
- Dark energy: with H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C), ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C. Using 1 − C ≈ 2x^{−2γ} in the far past, w → −2γ, and C ≈ γx in the far future gives w → −1. At γ = ½, ρ_DE = 2ρcrit, which is a constant, i.e. Λ. Correct.
- RAR statistics: ΔBIC = N ln(RSS ratio) = 2807 × 2 ln(0.1485/0.1437) ≈ 184. Scaling to galaxy level gives 166/2807 × 184 ≈ 10.9. The γ-free +7.1 is ln(2807) − 0.84. The sign test, 94 of 166 with one-sided p ≈ 0.05, is right under a normal approximation. All internally consistent.

So the badges hold up. I found no "Validated" or "Supported" badge on the 12 pages I read. The legend says those two are deprecated and should be read as "not yet re-badged". The "Reparametrization" labels on the RAR fit, the cosmology sector and the chemistry rows are what I'd have assigned myself. The site also catches a reparametrization I'd have been proud to find: on /dark-energy, C₀ = Ω_m "by definition" and C = Ω_m(a) is an identity. Cosmological "coherence" is just the matter fraction, which makes the background a Cardassian model. The site says so and cites Freese & Lewis.

**Where I couldn't reproduce or got suspicious:**

1. **The TEST-09 significance.** /honest-assessment gives predicted n = 3.35 and observed n = 3.75 ± 0.10 at "3.3σ". Naively, 0.40/0.10 = 4.0σ, or 4.1σ with the |Δn| = 0.41 from /test-catalog. Neither page says how 3.3σ is computed, for instance whether it includes uncertainty on the predicted slope from Ω_m or the BTFR fit. Either the error budget is missing from the page or the number is stale. Also, the registered kill threshold |Δn| > 0.3 misses at the (Ω_m−Ω_b)/Ω_b ceiling by 0.003 (0.297). That's a knife edge. The headline "it lost" for TEST-09 depends entirely on which of three boost-ceiling conventions you adopt, and the page does acknowledge the dependence.

2. **The Galaxy Curve Plotter never reaches the tanh.** The plotter's own caveats say C_ρ < 0.01 on every shipped disc, because ρcrit = 0.029·V_flat² is about 500 M☉/pc³ for V ≈ 130 km/s while disc midplane densities are about 0.1. So in the "tested form" C_f = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)C_ρ ≈ 0.32, and the violet curve is v ≈ 1.78·v_b at every radius: a constant rescaling of the Newtonian curve. γ and the shape of tanh have no effect on it. The plotter therefore shows the floor failing, not the coherence function failing. There's also a sharper point. If A had the value its own formula gives (4.6×10⁻⁵, 600× smaller), ρcrit would be about 0.8 M☉/pc³ and x ≈ 0.1. The knee would actually be in play. The 600× discrepancy is the difference between "the equation is exercised" and "the equation is a constant". I didn't see any page say that.

3. **The Coherence Explorer plots a γ from one function on the axis of another.** Its x-axis is density (C(ρ)), but the γ = 0.49 preset comes from the *acceleration-keyed* RAR fit. The density-keyed fit wanted γ → 0.046. The page does say "acceleration-keyed", but a reader who sets the slider to 0.49 is looking at a curve whose fitted context is g/a₀′, not ρ/ρcrit. A one-line axis caveat on that preset would fix it.

4. **The cosmology perturbation bound looks sign-blind.** /dark-energy gives c_s² ≈ 0.22(2γ−1) and a bound |2γ−1| ≲ 10⁻⁵ from the (k/aH)² ≈ 9×10⁴ enhancement. That's fine for γ > ½. For γ < ½, though, which includes the SPARC best fit 0.489 and the DESI fit 0.487, c_s² < 0 and the fluid is gradient-unstable, with exponential growth at large k. That should be a much harsher, one-sided exclusion than a symmetric |2γ−1| bound. The bound is either stated too symmetrically or the sign convention needs a sentence.

5. **γ_phonon = 2T/θ_D** (/gamma-boundary) makes γ a function of temperature, not a material constant. Then "γ ≈ 1 clustering" just means the data were taken near T ≈ θ_D/2, which is a choice of measurement conditions. The page already calls this a reparametrization of Debye, and this is the mechanism. Worth saying explicitly.

6. **Σ₀ attribution, minor.** /parameter-derivations calls a₀/(2πG) "Milgrom's critical surface density". In most of the MOND literature Σ_M ≡ a₀/G, and a₀/(2πG) ≈ 140 M☉/pc² is the Freeman-limit form McGaugh discusses. The 2π convention matters here because the page argues the 13% a₀ offset is "not meaningful" precisely because of factor-of-2π conventions.

The **MRH** page is honest in a way I didn't expect. It says the "Markov" label "is not yet earned" (no conditional-independence statement, measure, or boundary-finding procedure) and suggests renaming it "Relevance Horizon". I'd support the rename. As written, the MRH is a verbal coarse-graining scale with no kernel, so ρ in C(ρ) isn't operationally defined anywhere except the plotter's ad hoc Σ/2h.

A one-loop c_μν ~ α/π ≈ 2×10⁻³ against SME bounds at 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰ is the standard and correct statement of the problem. It's right to badge it audited-negative and not "untested".
