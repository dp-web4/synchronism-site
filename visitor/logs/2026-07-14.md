## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /consciousness-demo, /honest-assessment, /two-reframes, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The site sells me a grand unified equation on the way in and tells me it didn't work on the way out, and never quite explains what I'm supposed to take away from the gap between those two things.

### Journey

I clicked from Reddit expecting the usual crackpot theory-of-everything and got something much stranger: a theory-of-everything site that spends most of its energy explaining why the theory doesn't work. That's a hook. It genuinely made me keep reading.

**Landing page.** "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — great opener. It's a question, not a claim, and I felt invited rather than lectured. Then immediately: the equation itself, `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I don't know what tanh is. I don't know what ln is. I know ρ is "rho" because I've seen it in videos. So within ten seconds of the friendly question I'm looking at a wall of symbols. I almost bounced. What kept me was the "Beginners: 6-step pathway, ~30 minutes, equation-free" link — that was exactly the escape hatch I needed and I'm glad it's there.

But then further down the landing page: "0 of 6 'Validated' claims survived expert audit" and "zero confirmed novel predictions." I read that three times. Wait — is this a theory or an obituary? I couldn't tell if I was being invited to learn an idea or to watch one die. Nobody on the landing page tells me which of those two things I'm here for.

**The beginner path is the best thing on this site.** /first-encounter explains coherence with a marching band vs. a crowd wandering in a plaza. I got it instantly. The Markov Relevancy Horizon — "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter," an atom doesn't care about distant galaxies — that's a genuinely lovely idea and it's explained at exactly my level. Nine short pages, ninety seconds each. If the whole site were like this I'd have sent it to three friends.

**Then I clicked /core-idea and fell off a cliff.** Within a screen I hit "Hill-type saturation functions," "reparametrization-pending-audit," "ansatz," "compander." The dimmer-switch analogy is nice, but it's surrounded by words that are clearly aimed at someone else. This page seems to think I'm a grad student. The beginner path thinks I'm a curious adult. Both can't be right, and there's no signpost telling me which door I just walked through.

**Coherence Explorer** — I liked this. Two sliders, a curve that moves. "Coherence ≈ 0 is like a crowd of strangers; coherence ≈ 1 is like a marching band" right there next to the graph. It even defines tanh for me ("the S-curve shape") and logarithms ("compresses huge ranges into small ones"). This is how the whole site should talk. Playing with the γ slider, I could *feel* what "sharp transition vs. gentle ramp" meant, which no paragraph had managed to give me. Best five minutes I spent here.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** — this one lost me, and I think for a fixable reason. I pick a galaxy, I get five lines. Two of them I follow: the dots are what telescopes see, the gray dashed line is what gravity-with-visible-stuff predicts, and the gap between them is the dark matter mystery. Fine — that's the Veritasium version and I have it. But then there's a solid violet line (the real equation, which the page tells me *fails*), a **dotted violet line described as "the hand-tuned stand-in previously displayed"**, and a green MOND line. Why am I being shown an old wrong line that the site used to display? That's the authors' internal changelog leaking into a tool I'm supposed to learn from. I don't know what the site used to display. I wasn't here. Please just show me what's true now.

**Consciousness Threshold Demo** — I clicked this fastest of anything on the site, because of course I did. It's a beautiful chart: eight different approaches to measuring consciousness, all landing on C ≈ 0.50, standard deviation 0.012. For about fifteen seconds I thought I was looking at the coolest thing I'd read all month. Then the page explains that this convergence is "geometric, not empirical" — that *any* method picking the middle of a 0-to-1 range lands near 0.5 by construction, that no one has ever mapped a real brain measurement onto this axis, and that the eight approaches aren't independent because they all share the same assumptions. So the demo is a picture of nothing. I appreciate being told. I do not understand why the picture is still the thing I see first. It felt like being handed a magic trick and then being shown the wires, except the trick is still sitting there on the table, shiny.

**Honest Assessment** — I respect this page enormously and I did not finish it. It's roughly 25 minutes of reading and it's dense with things I can't parse: σ₈, fσ₈, ΔBIC = +184, "2.4σ tension," RAR, BTFR, SPARC, TEST-03. The plain-language opener is excellent — "The one equation fits galaxy rotation, but only as well as MOND, an existing 40-year-old idea" is a sentence I understood completely and will remember. Then the page becomes a different page. I skimmed to the bottom line and left.

**Glossary** — good, and it saved me twice. But I only found it because I went looking. Nothing on the pages where I met these words offered me a definition.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the equation doesn't work — galaxies are just MOND, chemistry is matched by a simpler polynomial, cosmology failed, the quantum test came out classical — then **what am I, a curious non-physicist, supposed to take home?** The site says the self-auditing methodology is the real product. Okay: then say so on the landing page, and pitch the site as "watch an AI-run research program kill its own theory in public," because *that* is a story I would read to the end. Right now the honesty is framed as a disclaimer attached to a theory, when it looks to me like it's the actual thing on offer.
2. What *is* ρ, physically? "Density within a meaningful radius" — density of what? Atoms? Energy? Information? The marching band analogy is about people, the equation is about... I never found out.
3. If C = 0 is "sparse/independent" and C = 1 is "dense/collective," and superconductors — the most famously quantum-coherent things I've heard of — land near *zero* on this scale, then the word "coherence" here means something close to the opposite of what it means in every YouTube video I've watched. The glossary flags this ("C is NOT quantum phase coherence") but only if you find the glossary. Why keep the word?
4. Who is writing this site? It's clearly partly AI-generated research ("3,308 adversarial AI sessions"), which is fascinating, but nothing tells me who or what I'm reading. Is a person standing behind this?
5. If every parameter is fitted rather than derived ("zero parameters have an independent first-principles derivation"), was there ever a version of this that could have worked, or was it always going to end here?

### Verdict

The idea is genuinely charming and the beginner path and Coherence Explorer sell it beautifully — for about ten minutes I thought I was learning something wonderful about marching bands and galaxies. Then every page I clicked told me, with obvious integrity, that it doesn't work, and I was left holding an equation I can't read and a verdict I didn't come for. The honesty is the most interesting thing here by a wide margin and it's positioned as the fine print. Lead with it: tell me on the landing page that I'm here to watch a theory get taken apart by its own authors, and I'll stay for the whole autopsy — but stop showing me shiny charts (consciousness convergence, a deprecated galaxy curve) that the surrounding text has already retracted.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a release: walk the nav, check that every controlled term is defined before first use, check that cross-references resolve, and check that two pages describing the same thing describe it the same way.

**The prose is good.** I want to say that first because most of what follows is criticism. Sentences like "ρcrit — saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)" and "Boundary means a regime boundary, not a mathematical phase transition" are the work of someone who has been burned by a reader's misreading and went back to fix it. The site pre-empts misunderstandings. That's rare and it's the hardest part to teach.

The failures are all in the layer above the prose — the parts a docs system is supposed to hold consistent for you.

**The badge system has five homes and they have already drifted.** This is the headline finding. Validation badges are the site's core controlled vocabulary; CLAUDE.md treats them as mandatory on every scientific claim. I found badge legends in five places:

| Page | What it hosts | What it says about authority |
|---|---|---|
| `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` | Full taxonomy, both families + deprecated | The canonical reference |
| `/research-philosophy` | Full taxonomy, both families | Explicitly defers: "canonical reference is on the Honest Assessment page" |
| `/test-catalog` | "Validation Badge Guide — MRH-relationship" | Defers: "Canonical definitions →" |
| `/tier-1-existing` | Its own 8-label legend under "Status badge definitions" | Claims no source; **does not match the cards on its own page** |
| `/glossary` | Badge terms clustered as glossary entries | Silent on canonicality |

And the landing page — which uses **Reparametrization, Speculative, Untested,** and **Failed** — hosts no legend and links to none of the five. So the site's most load-bearing vocabulary is used, on the first screen a visitor sees, with no definition and no path to one. Pass 1 independently hit this and had to hunt the glossary.

**`/tier-1-existing` is where the drift became a contradiction.** Its legend defines eight labels: Active-MRH, Reparametrization, Untested, Speculative, Failed, MOND-shared, Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn. Its test cards carry: Untested, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Withdrawn, Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared, Reparametrization, Demoted to Tier-2, Closed. Cross-reference those two lists:

- **Defined but never used on the page**: Active-MRH, Speculative, Failed.
- **Used on the page but defined nowhere on it**: Self-Eliminating-or-Tie (TEST-02), Demoted to Tier-2 (TEST-07), Closed (CLUSTER-SCALE).

A legend that defines three labels it doesn't use and omits three it does use is worse than no legend, because a reader now trusts it.

**The badge vocabulary has outgrown its own taxonomy.** The sanctioned set is five MRH-relationship tags + four descriptive tags (+ two deprecated). Actually deployed across `/honest-assessment` and `/tier-1-existing`, I counted these *additional* badge-styled labels: Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat, Demoted to Tier-2, Closed. The glossary covers Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared and Withdrawn. The rest — **Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat** — I could not find defined anywhere on the site. "89% Boundary-Consistent" and "Template Bias Caveat" aren't badges at all; they're findings wearing a badge's clothes. Once a badge can be an arbitrary phrase, it stops being a status and becomes a sentence with a border around it, and the reader loses the ability to scan.

**Breadcrumbs describe a site that doesn't exist.** Every interior page carries a three-part breadcrumb — Home / *Section* / Page. The sections are **Getting Started**, **Interactive Tools**, **Predictions & Experiments**, and **Methodology**. None of those four sections exists in the top navigation, which is a flat list of nine page links. And the middle segment is plain text, not a link — I couldn't click "Getting Started" from anywhere. So the breadcrumb is purely decorative: it names a hierarchy the navigation doesn't implement and that the reader can't traverse. Worse, the taxonomy is wrong where it does assert something — **Honest Assessment**, an ~18,000–20,000-word technical audit that also happens to be the canonical badge reference, is filed under *Getting Started*, alongside the beginner path.

**Two tools, one axis, two vocabularies.** The γ Calculator and the Phase Boundary Visualizer are adjacent cards on the same index page and both classify systems along the same γ axis. They do not agree on the regime names:

| | γ Calculator | Phase Boundary Visualizer |
|---|---|---|
| Regimes | 4 | 3 |
| High γ | "Weakly Correlated (γ-sharp)" | "Single-particle" |
| γ ≈ 1 | "Boundary (γ ≈ 1)" | "Boundary" |
| Low γ | "Strongly Correlated (γ-flat)" *and* "Collective Regime (γ-flattest)" | "Collective" |

A reader who moves from one tool to the other has to work out on their own that "Weakly Correlated" and "Single-particle" name the same end of the same axis. This should be one shared constant.

**Acronyms are essentially never expanded on first use.** The landing page uses A2ACW, MOND, BAO, RAR, DESI, SPARC, MRH and ΛCDM without expanding any of them. `/honest-assessment` runs ~28 unexpanded acronyms (SME, CPSU, LOO, SNARC, LQG, EFT, RSD, fσ₈, BTFR, TFR, KiDS, LHAASO…). The glossary is genuinely excellent — ~70 entries, well written, and it does expand all of these. But the glossary page states that readers can "hover over highlighted terms on any page," and on `/core-idea` I found *compander*, *ansatz*, *Hill-type*, *reparametrization* and *Presence* rendered as plain text with no links and no hover affordance. Either the tooltip system isn't applied to body copy on content pages, or the glossary is documenting a feature that doesn't reach them. Maintainer should check the source; from the outside, the promise reads as unfulfilled, and Pass 1's casual reader independently reported having to hunt for the glossary.

**Smaller structural knots.** `/key-claims` uses badges that are compound sentences — "Speculative (parent claim, unfalsifiable as stated) — both threshold values Failed" stacks two vocabulary terms and a clause into one chip. `/test-catalog` announces "24 proposed tests" but Tier 1 shows TEST-01 through TEST-10 plus TEST-04a and CLUSTER-SCALE; the other ~14 have no visible IDs and no page. TEST-07 is badged "Demoted to Tier-2" and there is no Tier-2 page anywhere in the navigation. The nav label "Tools" lands on a page titled "Interactive Tools," and both `/tools` and `/interactive-tools` resolve, so inbound links will diverge.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — **worked** — **intuitive** — Clean single-input tool: type or preset an N_corr, get γ = 2/√N_corr, a regime label, and a redrawn curve. Terms are defined *on the page* (N_corr, γ, the regime boundary at γ≈1) — this is the definition discipline the rest of the site should copy. It also does something I've rarely seen a product do: the caveat box states the tool's own formula is "inverted" — that BCS superconductors and BECs, the sharpest transitions in nature, get filed at the flattest end. The tool tells you it's wrong while you use it. As a writer I admire that; as a UX matter, the four regime labels ("Weakly Correlated (γ-sharp)") then read as actively misleading, since the parenthetical describes the curve and the noun describes the physics, and the page says the two don't correspond. Consider renaming the regimes after what they *are* (curve sharpness) rather than what they were meant to mean (correlation).
- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — **worked** — **intuitive** — Six-step decomposition of C(ρ). Genuinely the best-structured page on the site: one idea per step, a "Variables in this equation" block, tanh and ln both defined in plain language. Terminology matches the landing page exactly, which — after the badge audit — I checked specifically and was relieved to find. Two notes: it carries an "Audited-Negative" badge on γ with no on-page explanation of what that badge means (the recurring problem), and it mentions "Hill-type rational function" while `/core-idea` says "compander" and the glossary defines both — three names for one thing across three pages, with no page saying they're the same thing.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — **worked** — **confusing** — Single slider across γ ∈ [0, ~3.5] with systems placed along it. The conceptual payload is strong and honestly labeled ("γ is classifying the *counting method*, not the system"). But its three regime zones don't match the γ Calculator's four, its axis carries no γ ranges for the zones, and a reader arriving from the calculator has to silently re-map "Weakly Correlated" onto "Single-particle." Same axis, same framework, same index page, two vocabularies.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is the badge vocabulary **closed or open**? If closed (nine tags), then Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Null-Class, Sign Correction, 89% Boundary-Consistent and Template Bias Caveat are all violations. If open, then it isn't a controlled vocabulary and the badges can't be scanned or counted — which undercuts the site's own headline statistics ("0 of 6 Validated claims survived"). The site needs to decide, and the answer determines whether `/tier-1-existing`'s legend is a bug or a symptom.
2. **Who owns the canonical badge list?** Three pages defer to `/honest-assessment`; one (`/tier-1-existing`) doesn't and has drifted. Is the taxonomy stored once in `src/lib/` and rendered, or is it retyped per page? The drift pattern suggests retyping — which means it will drift again next week regardless of how carefully this one instance is fixed.
3. Does the **glossary tooltip system** actually run on content pages, or only on a subset? The glossary advertises it site-wide; `/core-idea` shows no evidence of it. This is the single highest-leverage fix on the list — the definitions already exist, they're just not reaching the pages where the words appear.
4. What are the **other ~14 tests**? "24 proposed tests" appears with no index. And what is Tier 2, given TEST-07 has been demoted to it and it has no page?
5. Are the breadcrumb sections (Getting Started, Predictions & Experiments, Methodology, Interactive Tools) an **aspiration or a leftover**? They look like an IA that was designed, encoded in the breadcrumb component, and never built into the nav.

### Verdict

The writing here is better than the documentation system that carries it, and the gap is widening. Individual pages are careful, self-correcting, and unusually good at pre-empting a reader's misunderstanding — but the badge vocabulary has escaped its own taxonomy (five legends, five locations, at least five labels defined nowhere, and one legend on `/tier-1-existing` that now contradicts the cards directly beneath it), the breadcrumbs promise a four-section hierarchy the nav never implements and won't let you click, and two adjacent tools disagree about what to call the same axis. Every one of these is a single-source-of-truth failure: the site has a `navigation.ts` for pages but apparently nothing equivalent for badges, regime names, or acronym expansions. Fix that layer once — one badge module, one regime constant, one tooltip pass over body copy — and the drift stops being an editorial chore and starts being impossible.

One thing, and it is real.

**6. The one runnable discriminator on the list is being retired without being run.**

### Pattern Note for the Maintainer

Three of the four P0 items are **over-refutations** — places where the site claims a stronger negative than its own evidence supports (the manufactured 3σ on DESI; the 0-of-47 denominator; the 3.17-vs-10.82 refutation stated under the wrong symbol). The grad student found the mirror image: three **descriptive over-promotions** that make the framework sound more like physics than its algebra allows.

Both biases have the same root, and it is not motivated reasoning — it is that **the self-critical statements on this site are the least-audited statements on this site.** Nobody checks a refutation. The physics claims get walked to primary sources; the claims about the *failure* of the physics get inscribed on trust, because they sound humble and humility reads as safe. It isn't. A false refutation is a false claim.

The rule that follows is one line and it is the highest-value process change available: **every self-refutation gets a citation walk, exactly like every prediction does.** Same standard, same primary-source requirement, no exemption for statements that flatter the auditor.
