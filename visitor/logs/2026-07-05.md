## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, first-encounter, beginner-path, galaxy-plotter, honest-assessment, coherence-calculator, what-synchronism-is-not, + landing revisit)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The honesty is genuinely refreshing and pulled me in, but the site keeps talking me *out* of being excited before I ever got to be excited.

### Journey

I landed here from a Reddit link. The headline — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is exactly the kind of thing that makes me click. That's a Veritasium thumbnail in one sentence. Good hook. And then right underneath it: "Some predictions work. Some fail." Okay, I respect that. Most crackpot physics sites I've stumbled onto scream that they've solved everything, so a site *leading* with "here's what broke" made me trust it more, not less. There were two big buttons — "Start Exploring" and "See What Failed" — and honestly "See What Failed" was more tempting, which is a weird thing for a website to pull off.

I took the polite route first and clicked into the **Beginner Path / First Encounter**. The crowd-vs-marching-band analogy is great — I actually got it. A crowd of people all doing their own thing vs. a marching band all moving together, and there's some number that tells you which one you're looking at. That clicked. The idea that "how dense your neighbors are" changes whether a group acts unified — fine, I'm with you.

Then it started slipping away from me. There's this thing called the **MRH** — "Markov Relevancy Horizon" — described as "the minimal set of degrees of freedom whose transitions materially influence coherence." I read that sentence three times. I know what a "bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" means, that part they explained nicely, but then they hit me with the fancy version and I just glazed over. "Degrees of freedom" isn't a phrase I use. Also the page kept telling me how fast it was going to be — "90 seconds each," "10-minute intro" — which weirdly made me *more* anxious, like a quiz was coming.

Then the equation showed up: **C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**. I have no idea what "tanh" is. No idea what that squiggle γ is. The site promised "no equations required" on the landing page and then there's an equation on step 1 of the beginner path. That felt like a small bait-and-switch.

I bailed to the fun stuff — the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**. This was the highlight. You pick a galaxy and see the real measured speeds (blue dots) vs. what gravity from the visible stuff predicts (gray dashed) — and there's a big gap. That gap IS the dark matter mystery, and seeing it drawn out made it real for me in a way a YouTube video never quite did. Cool. BUT — the plot has the Synchronism line (violet) and a MOND line (green) sitting basically on top of each other, and the page itself tells me all three models fit "about equally well" so looking at the picture tells me nothing. And then it confesses the violet line uses "hand-tuned constants chosen to look right." So the coolest thing on the site immediately explains that it's not actually evidence of anything. Deflating.

I checked the **γ Calculator** too. It's a slider for "Ncorr" (number of particles moving together) and it spits out a γ value and a label like "Strongly Correlated." I moved the slider, numbers changed, but I genuinely could not tell you what I learned. The page even warns the formula has a "structural inversion" that gives "opposite results from real physics." So... why is it here for me to play with?

Finally I read **Honest Assessment** and **What Synchronism Is Not**. This is where the site is most itself and also most likely to make a casual reader close the tab. "0 of 6 validated claims survived audit." "Zero confirmed novel predictions." "Works as well as MOND and never better." I actually admire it — it's the opposite of a scam. But as a casual reader who came in on a hopeful headline, by the end I felt like I'd been talked out of my own curiosity. I came for "one equation for the universe" and left with "a very honest team who found out their idea mostly doesn't work yet."

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, first-encounter, glossary, key-claims, honest-assessment, tools, test-roadmap, research-philosophy, coherence-explorer, equation-walkthrough)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (structurally — I can't judge the physics, but I can map the site)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The content discipline is exceptional and the glossary is a model of its kind, but the *scaffolding around it* — badge taxonomy, breadcrumb buckets, symbol reuse — has drifted into several small inconsistencies that a single style-pass would clean up.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit any docs set: land, map the nav, walk every top-level section, and check that terms mean the same thing everywhere they appear. My headline finding is that this is a genuinely well-maintained information product — most sites I review don't have a glossary at all, let alone an 80+ entry alphabetized one that actually defines its own status labels. The "Questions First" culture is real and consistent in *tone*. Where it falls down is in *systems*: the same information is defined in multiple canonical-looking places, and a few structural conventions aren't applied uniformly.

**Navigation.** Top nav is eight items: Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, Honest Assessment. Two problems jump out. First, "Tier 1: Existing Data" sits in the global nav as a peer of generic labels like "Tools" and "Glossary" — it's a jargon label ("Tier 1" means nothing to a newcomer) *and* it's redundant with "Test Roadmap," which contains all four tiers including Tier 1. A reader can't tell why both exist. Second, the audience-specific entry buttons on the landing page ("Key Claims — for physicists," "Tier 1 Tests — for researchers") are a nice touch but don't map cleanly to the nav labels, so the two wayfinding systems compete.

**Breadcrumbs.** Present on every interior page (good!), but the *category bucket* in the middle slot isn't consistent, and it's doing real classification work:
- First Encounter, Glossary, **Key Claims**, Honest Assessment → all under "Getting Started"
- Tools → "Interactive Tools"
- Test Roadmap → "Predictions & Experiments"
- Research Philosophy → "Methodology"

Filing **Key Claims** and **Honest Assessment** under "Getting Started" is a mismatch — Key Claims is explicitly the physicist entry point, and the breadcrumb tells me it's onboarding material. "Getting Started" has become a catch-all bucket for four pages that don't belong together, while three other pages each got their own tailored bucket. Pick a consistent taxonomy: either everything gets a real category, or the beginner sequence alone is "Getting Started" and Key Claims moves to something like "The Framework."

**Terminology — the big one.** The symbol **C** is defined three different ways depending on where you land:
- Landing page key: "C: Coherence coefficient (0–1 scale)"
- Coherence Explorer: "C (coherence)"
- **Key Claims: "C (consciousness function)" / "density-driven collective ordering"**

A reader who meets C as "coherence coefficient" on the landing page and then sees "C (consciousness function)" on Key Claims has no way to know whether that's the same C being reused for a consciousness claim or a different quantity colliding on the same letter. This is exactly the kind of thing a glossary is supposed to prevent — and the glossary defines C(ρ) as coherence, so the Key Claims usage is the outlier. Either disambiguate (C_consciousness, or a footnote "same coherence C applied to neural density") or don't reuse the bare letter.

**Badge taxonomy — defined in four places.** The validation-badge system is the site's signature feature, and it's documented redundantly:
1. Glossary (defines all 13 labels across both families + deprecated)
2. Honest Assessment `#validation-badge-definitions` — which Key Claims explicitly calls the *"canonical reference"*
3. Research Philosophy has its own "Validation Badge Taxonomy" section
4. Test Roadmap has a color-coded badge guide

Four independent definitions of the same taxonomy is four things to keep in sync, and they're already subtly diverging in emphasis (Honest Assessment leads with the MRH-relationship family; the landing page's badge explanation surfaces only the four descriptive labels; Test Roadmap's guide lists only the MRH-relationship five). If Key Claims says Honest Assessment is canonical, then the other three should be *short pointers to it*, not parallel full definitions. Right now a reader can't tell which is authoritative.

Also: **13 status labels across two families plus two deprecated** is a lot of taxonomy. The two-family split (MRH-relationship vs. Descriptive) is principled but never explained *to the reader* in one place — why does a claim get "Sidelined" vs. "Failed"? When would something be "Audited-Negative" instead of "Failed"? The glossary defines each label in isolation but there's no decision-guide for how the families relate. For a first-time reader the badges are the vocabulary of the whole site; they deserve one canonical page that says "here are the two families and here's when each applies."

**Acronyms.** On the beginner pages, definitions are handled well (MRH, coherence, ρ all glossed at first use). On the research-facing pages (Honest Assessment, Test Roadmap, Research Philosophy) the density is high — DESI, RAR, BTFR, TFR, fσ₈, ΔBIC, EFT, LIV appear mostly without first-use expansion. That's defensible given the audience, and the glossary backstops it, but a first-use link to the glossary anchor (as Key Claims does for a couple of terms) would make it airtight. "Degrees of freedom" in the MRH definition remains the one beginner-facing undefined term (Pass 1 flagged this too).

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, key-claims, parameter-derivations, equation-walkthrough, galaxy-rotation, honest-assessment, coherence-explorer, interactive-tools, galaxy-plotter, gamma-calculator)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (of what the framework *is* — a calibrated compander with an honest null result — not of any working physics, because there isn't a derived mechanism to understand)
- **Would return?**: yes — as a case study in how to report a null honestly, not as physics I'd build on
- **Overall impression**: The math is mostly *correctly labeled as not-yet-math*; the site's rigor is almost entirely in its self-audit, and the one place the rigor still leaks is that phase-transition vocabulary is draped over a function that has no phase transition.

### Journey

I went straight past the onboarding to the theory spine: Key Claims → Parameter Derivations → Equation Anatomy → Galaxy Rotation → Honest Assessment, checking numbers against what I know as I went. I came in expecting the usual grand-unified-framework pattern — a suggestive analogy, a reparametrized MOND, and a "validated" badge papering over a fit. That is *not* what this is, and I want to be precise about why.

**The core function.** C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1)). My first instinct as a condensed-matter person is to read tanh as the Curie–Weiss mean-field magnetization curve, m = tanh(βJz·m) — the classic self-consistent order parameter. It is **not** that. There is no self-consistency here: the argument is ln(density), an external field, not the order parameter fed back on itself. So this is not a mean-field result, it's a **sigmoidal compander** — a log-companding saturation map, the same family as μ-law audio compression or the Naka–Rushton/Hill response curve. Crucially, the Equation Anatomy page **does not claim otherwise** — it states outright "there is no derivation to walk through — only choices to examine." I went in ready to catch a mean-field-theory dress-up and the site had already disarmed the trap. That is genuinely unusual and I want to credit it.

**Verifying the numbers.** I checked the ones that are checkable:
- C(ρcrit, γ=2) = tanh(2·ln2) = tanh(1.386) ≈ **0.881**. The Coherence Explorer states ≈0.88. Correct. But note what this *means*: the coherence at the so-called "critical density" is 0.88, sitting deep in the saturated tail, not at any midpoint or inflection. ρcrit is a **saturation knee, not a critical point.** The site's own glossary/compander framing says as much — but the *word* "critical" and the whole "Phase Boundary" apparatus keep importing a connotation the function can't cash.
- a₀ = cH₀/(2π): with H₀≈70 km/s/Mpc, cH₀ ≈ 6.8×10⁻¹⁰, /2π ≈ **1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²** vs MOND's 1.2×10⁻¹⁰. This is the a₀ ≈ cH₀/6 coincidence known since Milgrom (1983); Key Claims labels it "dimensional bookkeeping … not a Synchronism-specific derivation." Correct and correctly disclaimed. (Minor: Key Claims quotes 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ while Parameter Derivations quotes 1.20×10⁻¹⁰ — different H₀, but the two pages should agree on a number they both call non-load-bearing.)
- γ = 2/√Ncorr: the site flags this as **Audited-Negative, sign inverted**, and it's right to. In any real critical system correlations *sharpen* the transition (ξ grows, the curve steepens); here γ sits in the rate slot of tanh and *shrinks* with Ncorr, so the model assigns the sharpest transition to an ideal gas and the flattest to a BCS superconductor — exactly backwards. And invoking the CLT (which requires independence) to scale the count of *correlated* d.o.f. is self-contradictory. Both failures are the site's own words. I could not find an error in their error-finding.

**The galaxy result is the honest heart of it.** Galaxy Rotation states the whole thing in two lines: "Pin γ=2 → refuted" (ΔBIC = +184 on SPARC), "Fit γ → MOND" (free-γ → 0.49, RMS matching McGaugh to four digits). That is the textbook signature of a **non-discriminating reparametrization**: there is no γ that is simultaneously distinct from MOND and consistent with the data. On ALFALFA-SDSS the environment-dependent-scatter prediction gives R²=0.14 against a *pre-registered* kill of R²<0.20 — tripped. A framework that pre-registers its own kill criterion and then reports that it fired is doing science correctly, even when the result is a refutation.

**Where I still push back (physics, not honesty).** My one substantive scientific objection survives the site's self-audit because the self-audit doesn't quite name it:

1. **tanh is analytic → there is no phase transition, anywhere.** C(ρ) is C^∞ in ρ; it has no non-analyticity, no symmetry breaking, no diverging length scale. It is a *crossover*, full stop. Yet the vocabulary — "Phase Boundary Visualizer," "phase transitions happen at γ≈1," "critical density" — is the vocabulary of genuine criticality. The compander framing is correct on the calculator pages but the phase-transition scaffolding elsewhere quietly re-imports the thing the math doesn't have. A condensed-matter referee would circle every instance of "phase transition" and write "crossover."

2. **The C→dynamics bridge is missing, and it's the whole ballgame.** Galaxy Rotation "does not explicitly quote the physical mechanism" — i.e., there is no equation taking a coherence C∈[0,1] to an *acceleration*. Without that map, C(ρ) is decorative: the rotation-curve fit is done by a *different* function entirely (see the plotter below). Everything upstream is a mood board until this bridge exists.

3. **Local ρ is the wrong variable for a MOND-like law.** MOND's a₀ regime is set by the *non-local* enclosed baryonic acceleration g_bar, not by local density. A modification keyed to local ρ will generically match rotation curves (where ρ and enclosed g track each other) and then fail in clusters (where they don't) — which is exactly the classic MOND cluster-mass problem. If the mechanism is "incomplete decoherence at low local density," I'd want the cluster prediction on the table, because that's where local-ρ models die.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — physics correctly represented — sliders are γ (2.0→0.01) and ρcrit; plots C(ρ)=tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit+1)); I verified C(ρcrit,γ=2)≈0.88 matches tanh(2ln2). Correctly flags that this "coherence" is *anti-correlated* with quantum phase coherence (BEC/BCS sit at low C). The one gap: it doesn't carry the γ sign-inversion caveat its sibling tools carry.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — physics *mislabeled by construction* — SPARC galaxies (DDO 154, NGC 2403/3198/7331, UGC 128), four curves. The honest confession is buried in body text: the "Synchronism" curve is tanh(radius) with hand-tuned 0.4 and 2.5 kpc, no C(ρ) anywhere. As a demo of the dark-matter gap (gray line sagging below the dots) it's excellent; as a demo of *Synchronism* it plots a function the framework didn't derive. "A good fit proves nothing" is stated — correctly, because a saturating function of radius fits any flat curve.
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — honest — input Ncorr → γ=2/√Ncorr, with regime labels. Caveat 2 flags the sign inversion cleanly (sharpest transition assigned to ideal gas, flattest to BCS superconductor) and Caveat 1 disclaims the CLT provenance. This is the sign-inversion honesty the Coherence Explorer is missing.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What is the derived map from C(ρ) to a modification of the gravitational acceleration? Until that exists, is C(ρ) doing any physical work at all, or is it a narrative attached to a MOND-shaped fit?
2. Given C(ρ)=tanh(...) is analytic, is the framework committed to there being *no* genuine phase transition — and if so, why keep the phase-transition vocabulary at all?
3. What does the local-ρ mechanism predict for cluster mass discrepancies (Coma), where MOND itself famously underpredicts? That's the discriminating test a local-density law can't dodge.
4. If γ=2/√Ncorr is Audited-Negative and sign-inverted, and there's no derived replacement, what — if anything — currently sets the transition sharpness from first principles?

### Verdict
This is the rare grand-unified-framework site where the physics I'd normally have to *extract* under protest is already sitting on the page, correctly labeled: tanh is admitted to be an underived compander, γ=2/√Ncorr is admitted to be sign-inverted, the galaxy program is admitted to be "refute γ=2 or become MOND," and the DESI result is a triggered kill criterion, not a spun success. The reparametrization attributions (Palma–Suominen–Ekert 1996, waveguide QED, Viola–Lloyd) are the ones I'd have cited myself. There are effectively **no live "validated" badges** to catch dishonest — they've been replaced by an honest taxonomy, and "0 confirmed predictions" is on the front page. My remaining objection is not to the honesty but to a residue of it: the framework still narrates a *crossover* in the language of a *phase transition*, and the flagship plotter labels a hand-tuned tanh-of-radius as "Synchronism" when the framework's own equation never enters the computation. Fix the phase-transition vocabulary and put the "this isn't C(ρ)" caveat *in the legend*, and the site's presentation would be as rigorous as its self-audit already is.

- **Overall impression**: As a referee I came hunting for the overclaim buried under the honesty, and the honesty is real — the one genuine research-grade artifact on the site (the dim-4 LIV naturalness gap) is a self-inflicted wound, correctly cited and correctly badged; what's missing is the one test that could actually discriminate, and it's missing because it appears to have been privately closed as a failure and never surfaced publicly.

### Journey

I skipped everything a grad student would read and went straight for the load-bearing claims: is there a single prediction here that a MOND practitioner or a ΛCDM cosmologist would have to take seriously? I walked Key Claims → For Researchers → Tier 1 → A2ACW → CDM Discrimination, checking each against the current literature as of mid-2026.

**Is anything empirically new? No — and the site says so first.** Every entry reduces cleanly: galaxy dynamics → MOND (free-γ→0.49, RMS matching McGaugh to four digits, ΔBIC=+184 against the pinned γ=2); DESI RSD → ΛCDM (predicted fσ₈(0.51)≈0.418, a 12% suppression that is simply *absent*; σ₈=0.841±0.034 vs predicted 0.76, a 2.4σ amplitude miss); BAO → ΛCDM at 0.0% by the framework's *own* forecast (TEST-04 withdrawn — the right call); chemistry → density-monotonicity (r up to 0.982 for sound velocity, but a 2-parameter polynomial in Z matches or beats it, which the tool states outright). The a₀=cH₀/(2π)≈1.08×10⁻¹⁰ is the Milgrom-1983 a₀≈cH₀/6 coincidence, and Key Claims correctly badges it "dimensional bookkeeping … not a Synchronism-specific derivation." The ledger reads **0 confirmed novel predictions, 0 independently-derived parameters, 0 tests discriminating from MOND+EFE+ΛCDM.** I could not find a number that contradicts that ledger. That is unusual: usually the honest summary and the actual content disagree, and here they don't.

**The one genuinely professional-grade artifact is a liability, not a prediction.** The For-Researchers page runs the Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Gambini–Pullin (PRL 93, 191301, 2004) argument *against itself*: any discrete absolute-time substrate without an added custodial boost symmetry radiatively generates a dim-4 SME coefficient c_μν ~ α/π ~ 10⁻² at one loop, against bounds of 10⁻¹⁸ (cavity MM) to 10⁻²⁹–10⁻³⁰ (Hughes–Drever) — a **16–28 order-of-magnitude naturalness gap.** This is exactly the critique I would level at *any* Lorentz-violating discrete-time proposal, and the site levels it at itself, badged "Naturalness Gap," with the correct escape-route literature named but explicitly *not* exhibited (Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov 2005 for SUSY forbidding dim-3/4 LV; Pospelov & Shang 2012 for the Hořava–Lifshitz scale-hierarchy). The time-of-flight (dim≥5) channel is honestly triaged as "structurally unreachable" — even-in-k lattice symmetry kills the linear term, leaving an n=2 signal ~10⁷ OOM below reach and non-unique anyway. This section is done at referee-grade. It is also the sharpest refutation exposure the framework has, and the site knows it. Credit where due.

**A2ACW is honestly characterized as a null.** 6/6 sensitivity on demoted claims, 0/6 specificity (COBE, Higgs, GW all flagged as reparametrizations) → "a retrieval aid, not a detector … adversarial AI self-play over a shared corpus is a reparametrization detector, not a discovery engine." I agree with the conclusion. I'd only add that 0/6 on n=6 is a *tiny* sample — the binomial CI on that false-positive rate is enormous — and the test is partly rigged, because every real discovery also sits atop decades of prior art, so "flagged as reparametrization" is over-determined. The null is probably right, but the specificity *number* is oversold by its own precision.

**Where I still push back — three things the other passes couldn't reach:**

1. **An internal contradiction in the a₀ pitch.** Key Claims says "MOND treats a₀ as an empirical constant. ΛCDM adds a new particle. **Neither explains why anomalies appear at a specific acceleration scale.**" The rhetorical implication is that Synchronism *does* — but the a₀ badge on the same page admits it's non-derived dimensional bookkeeping, i.e. Synchronism doesn't explain it either; it reproduces the same coincidence. And the premise is itself dated: ΛCDM+feedback simulations produce a characteristic acceleration scale / tight RAR without a fundamental a₀ (Navarro+ 2017; the Keller–Wadsley vs Ludlow debate). This is the one live overclaim I found — a sentence that claims explanatory advantage the framework's own badge retracts two lines later.

2. **The single discriminating test is absent from the public roadmap.** Tier 1 lists TEST-01…10 — SPARC, wide binaries, TFR scatter, DESI, BTFR slope, dwarfs — and *not one is a cluster test.* Clusters (Coma-type) are the one regime where a **local-ρ** coherence law must diverge from MOND, because in clusters local ρ and enclosed g_bar decouple, and MOND famously leaves a factor-~2 residual mass there. That's precisely where this framework could be non-degenerate. Its absence is conspicuous. (My strong suspicion, given how thorough the self-audit is elsewhere, is that the cluster case was worked and *failed* — a local-ρ bridge on Coma will miss by orders of magnitude — but if so, it belongs on the public roadmap as a badged failure, not omitted. Omitting your one potential discriminator reads, to a referee, as the opposite of the transparency the rest of the site earns.)

3. **The QM reframe is the only distinctive content, and "Untested" mis-badges it.** Claim 1 ("superposition = temporal scanning through phase modes," "collapse = resonant selection at an MRH crossing, no observer needed") is the only thing here that isn't a reparametrization of a fit — but as stated it makes no numerical prediction distinct from standard QM. "Untested — ontological reframe" is close, but "untested" invites the reader to think a test exists and nobody's run it. It should say **untestable-as-stated**: an interpretation, in the class of Bohm/many-worlds, not a deferred experiment.

### Tools Tested
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — **scientifically corrected since Pass 3 flagged it** — it now carries the right disclaimer verbatim: "C(ρ) is a smooth compander (μ-law/Hill/logistic family) with **no critical point** — 'boundary' … means a regime boundary along the γ axis, not a phase transition." That is the analytically correct statement (tanh is C^∞, no non-analyticity), and it even self-references the γ=2/√Ncorr sign-inversion. Propagation of the compander framing to this page is done. The residual leak is on the *sibling* calculators, not here.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (`/chemistry-correlation-explorer`) — worked — **honest about its own null** — reports Pearson r up to 0.982 (sound velocity), 0.979 (electronegativity) across 1,703 phenomena, then immediately discloses the kill: "a plain 2-parameter polynomial in atomic number Z … matches these correlations (|Δr|≤0.07) and sometimes beats them," and "High r here is evidence of density-monotonicity (known chemistry), not of C(ρ)-specific physics." That is exactly the null-model discipline most correlation dashboards omit. As a referee: correct — a monotone map of a monotone-in-Z quantity will correlate with anything else monotone in Z; the polynomial control is the right one and it wins. The tool is a teaching example of how *not* to be fooled by a high r.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Was the cluster (Coma) case for the local-ρ law ever worked, and if so what was the residual? Its absence from Tier 1 is the single biggest gap between the site's transparency and its roadmap.
2. If the QM reframe makes no numerical departure from standard QM, on what basis is it "Claim 1" (the lead claim) rather than an interpretation appended to the framework? What would falsify it *as an interpretation*?
3. Do the authors intend to exhibit one of the two custodial escape routes (SUSY or scale-hierarchy) for the dim-4 LIV gap, or is the honest end-state "unresolved naturalness problem, framework parked"? The For-Researchers page stops just short of saying which.
4. Given 0 discriminating tests by execution, is the program's forward value now entirely in the *transferable nulls* (locality no-go, mechanism-class DESI result, A2ACW null) rather than in Synchronism-qua-physics? If so, the preprint framing matters more than the site.

### Verdict
I came to catch the overclaim under the honesty and left having caught only one live one (the a₀ "neither explains why" sentence, which the same page's badge retracts) plus one conspicuous omission (no cluster test — the sole regime where a local-ρ law could differ from MOND). Everything else survives expert scrutiny *because the authors already ran that scrutiny*: the dim-4 LIV naturalness gap is cited and badged correctly, the A2ACW null is the right conclusion if oversold in precision, and every "prediction" is honestly reduced to MOND, ΛCDM, or known chemistry. This is not a physics program with results; it is a well-documented null with two or three genuinely transferable negative findings, and it says so. My trust is *high* — not because the framework works (it doesn't, by its own ledger) but because a site that refutes itself this competently, with these citations, is doing science. The one thing that would raise it further is putting the cluster test on the table: right now the roadmap omits its own sharpest possible discriminator, and for a site whose entire brand is "we show what failed," that omission is the one place the brand isn't fully honored.
