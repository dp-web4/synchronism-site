## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /galaxy-plotter, /interactive-tools, /honest-assessment, /coherence-explorer, /equation-walkthrough, /gamma-calculator)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A refreshingly honest "we tried a big idea and it mostly didn't work" story with good analogies, but every page buries the friendly part under layers of caveats, acronyms and editorial history I can't follow.

### Journey
I clicked through from Reddit expecting a "theory of everything" crank site. The headline ("What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?") read like one, but the next line ("We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated.") surprised me. That got my attention more than any bold claim would have. The marching band vs. crowd-in-a-plaza analogy for "C" clicked right away. Then the landing page threw a scoreboard at me (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, "0 of 9" audited claims) plus 3,308 sessions and 14,610 galaxies. I didn't know what a "refutation" vs. a "withdrawn" test was, but I got the gist: it lost.

**/why-synchronism**: The "What happened, in three lines" box is the best thing I read all session. In three sentences it covered the proposal, the test against galaxies, the loss (0 confirmed, 6 refutations), and what's still open. If I'd stopped there I'd have left happy. After that the page gets long and dense. I skimmed past "compander", "Naka–Rushton", "Abrikosov–Gor'kov", "BTFR" and "a₀ = cH₀/(2π)". The "For physicists" fold was clearly not for me, and I was fine skipping it.

**/first-encounter** (Beginner Path step 2): The one-section-at-a-time "Continue" format felt like a YouTube explainer, which I liked. "Presence is density — how much stuff is packed into a given space" is clear. The S-curve as "a dimmer switch — flat at first, then rising, then levelling off" is clear. Section C was a whiplash moment: the γ formula I'd just been introduced to "did not survive testing", and a fitted γ ≈ 0.5 "matches MOND's formula, so it adds nothing new." I don't know what MOND is beyond "a rival theory", so "it turns into MOND" didn't land as good or bad news. The "expert note" with BEC/BCS/N_corr in the middle of the beginner section broke the flow. Section G's punchline ("Melting points: 53% error. Critical exponents: 2× off. Superconductor Tc: 6.5× wrong.") was blunt and memorable. I didn't know what a critical exponent is, though.

**/galaxy-plotter**: This is where it clicked. "A rotation curve is just orbital speed plotted against distance from the centre", and the Neptune-is-slower-than-Earth comparison, made the dark-matter puzzle make sense for the first time in my life. I picked DDO 154 and saw the grey line sag below the blue dots. But the legend lost me: "division wiring" vs. "quadrature wiring", "Newton's shape scaled up 3.17×", a violet line that's "failed" and another that has "no visible effect here". So the site's own theory has two lines, both wrong in different ways, and the green rival line (MOND) is the one that works? I understood the problem the tool shows. I did not understand why there are two Synchronism lines.

**/interactive-tools**: Clean list with Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced tags. Odd: the γ Calculator is tagged "Beginner" here but its own description is "This formula failed; the calculator shows why."

**/honest-assessment**: The top screen hit me with MOND, Bell/CHSH, Cassini and refutation-ledger tables. The two sections I actually needed ("What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" and "What a non-physicist should take away") are about a quarter to a third of the way down a very long page. The "no jargon" paragraph isn't really jargon-free. It has a parenthetical about "caps below about 5.4" being ruled out. It also has an editorial aside about how the sentence used to say "four executed refutations" until 2026-08-09, and that "three simulated-visitor personas caught the drift". That's honest, but as a reader I don't care about the edit history of a sentence in the middle of the paragraph that's supposed to be the plain summary. The takeaway list was clearer. "The real product is this audit itself" is an unusual and interesting pitch.

**/coherence-explorer**: Sliders for γ and ρ_crit, a log/linear toggle, and presets. The "1,000× is about 7 units of natural log" explanation of ln actually helped. Two things confused me:
1. The page says tanh is "a dimmer switch that snaps rather than fading gradually". First Encounter told me the opposite: "the dial turns gradually, not a phase transition … (tanh is smooth, so nothing snaps here)". Same metaphor, opposite meaning on two Beginner pages.
2. The chart draws a "C=0.50" reference line and a "knee: ρ = ρcrit" marker. At the default γ 0.49 the readout says C(ρcrit) ≈ 0.33, so the knee is visibly *not* where the curve crosses halfway. A note does say ρcrit "is a saturation knee, not a critical density". But to me "knee" and a midline drawn together suggest they should meet, and I spent a while thinking the tool was broken.

I also noticed the default γ (0.49) is described as failing a "Cassini Solar-System bound (8.7σ)", so the tool opens on a setting the page itself says is ruled out. That's a bit disorienting.

**/equation-walkthrough** (Equation Anatomy): Through WebFetch I could only see Step 1 of 6 ("Start with density"). Previous/Next buttons, no sliders. Even the legend says ln is "cosmetic" because the whole thing equals "a Hill-type rational function". That sentence is for someone else. "Sigmoid", "Central Limit Theorem" and "audited-negative" appear without explanation in a tool tagged Beginner.

**/gamma-calculator**: "This tool lets you explore how a failed formula fails." The exercise of comparing the ideal-gas and BCS-superconductor presets and noticing the most collective system gets the flattest curve was a neat "spot the mistake" game. I'm not sure what a BCS superconductor is, so I took "backwards" on faith.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What exactly *is* MOND, and why is "it turns into MOND" bad news if MOND fits the galaxies? Is matching a working theory a failure?
2. Why does the Galaxy Plotter have two Synchronism lines, and what is "wiring"?
3. If C at ρ_crit isn't 0.5, what is ρ_crit *for*, in plain words?
4. The landing page shows 3,308 research sessions. Who or what ran them? (I got hints that AI was involved: "simulated-visitor personas", "A2ACW", "LLM".)
5. What are the "core ideas never testable with existing instruments" that keep the project alive? The Honest Assessment says they exist but I never found them listed plainly.
6. Is dark matter real, then? The site says its own idea failed but doesn't tell me what that means for the bigger mystery.

### Verdict
I came in expecting a crackpot and left respecting the honesty. "We tested it and it lost" is a rare thing to read, and the Galaxy Plotter taught me something real about dark matter. The friendly material is excellent but scattered: the three-line summary, the marching band, the rotation-curve explanation, the takeaway list. Each is followed by a wall of qualifiers, edit history and acronyms. Two of the Beginner tools also gave me contradictory or bug-looking signals (the snap vs. gradual wording, and the knee not sitting at the midline). I'd share the three-line box and the plotter. I wouldn't send a non-science friend to /honest-assessment.

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /key-claims, /galaxy-rotation, /dark-matter, /mond-unification ("Relation to MOND"), /interactive-tools, /galaxy-plotter, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /coherence-explorer (text only, for the Cassini statement))

### Journey
A colleague sent this as "an interesting AI project". I skipped the beginner material and went to **/parameter-derivations**. I was ready for a "derived from first principles" page, and instead its subtitle reads "Parameter Calibration & Honest Ansätze", and it says plainly that "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." It goes through each parameter:
- γ = 2/√N_corr is borrowed from the CLT, which the page admits is self-contradictory because N_corr counts *correlated* units. The page also flags that the sign is inverted relative to mean-field theory: in Landau/Ising, more correlated units sharpen the transition, and here they flatten it. That is exactly the objection I came in with, and they had already made it.
- A = 0.029 is shown to be about 600× off from its own stated formula.
- B_max = 1/Ω_m is called "asserted".
- Σ₀ is described as "one number, not two" with a₀.

This is the most self-critical parameter page I've seen on any framework site.

**Checking the math.** I did the algebra on paper:
- tanh(γ ln y) = (y^{2γ} − 1)/(y^{2γ} + 1). So with y = 1 + x the "ln" really is cosmetic: it is a Hill-type rational function, as the site says.
- At γ = ½ it gives x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), so a₀ = 2a₀′. /galaxy-rotation states this correctly, including the factor of 2.
- Small x: C ≈ γx, so solving g_bar = g_obs·C(g_obs/a₀′) gives g_obs → √(g_bar·a₀′/γ). That means deep-MOND scaling, flat curves and v⁴ ∝ M hold for *any* γ, with the identified scale a₀′/γ. The site says exactly this ("the identified scale is a₀′/γ"). Good.
- Newtonian return: 1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ}, a power-law tail. ✓
- The g_bar-keyed division gives g_obs → a₀′/γ (a constant), so v² ∝ r. The site says "not a flat curve and not MOND". ✓
- Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = cH₀/(4π²G). I get 119 M☉/pc² at H₀ = 67.4. ✓
- a₀: (1.20 − 1.043)/0.02 ≈ 7.9σ random and 0.66σ systematic. Scaled to H₀ = 73 it gives 1.13×10⁻¹⁰. Inverting gives H₀ ≈ 77.5 ± 15.5. ✓ All of these reproduce.
- Galaxy Plotter: with M_b = 47·V⁴ the MOND plateau is (47/62.8)^{1/4} = 0.930·V_flat (G·a₀ with a₀ = 1.2×10⁻¹⁰). The page says "about 0.93·V_flat". ✓ That's careful work.

- Hence sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a): the family is freezing or phantom-thawing-toward-Λ, never the w₀>−1, w_a<0 quadrant. ✓ Best member (−0.993, +0.023) at γ=0.487 is consistent with that.

- It correctly names the prior art (Cardassian, Freese & Lewis 2002). It says outright that the substitution is not a covariant theory: "conflict with the Bianchi identity", and both covariant completions lose by Δχ² ≥ 79.

  - The mass model is BTFR-derived (M_b = 47V⁴) and gas-free, so the MOND curve's plateau is partly built in, as the page admits.
  - The dots' provenance isn't cited.

  Swapping in the public SPARC V_gas/V_disk/V_bul columns would turn this into a genuine RAR demonstration at no extra cost.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **Is γ universal across sectors?** If yes, cosmology (γ=½ ± 5×10⁻⁶) plus the Solar System (γ ≳ 0.75 direct tail, ≳ 1.5 Q₂) kill it without SPARC. If no, how many free parameters does "one equation" actually have?
2. What is the N, density proxy and confidence interval behind the r² = 0.0001 RAR-vs-ambient-density null? Could it be published as a stand-alone constraint on density-screened RAR modifications?
3. Has the A2ACW out-of-distribution, post-cutoff, citation-stripped arm been designed with a sample size? The site gives ~41 per arm at 80/80 for J ≥ 0.3. Until it runs, "0 of 9" says nothing about the physics.
4. Does the dark-energy sign-lock no-go (sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a)) hold for *all* monotone G_eff(ρ̄) = G/C with C a Hill-type function, or only for tanh-log? If general, that's a short, citable class theorem.
5. Which ephemeris bound (INPOP/EPM) would the site adopt for the direct-tail check, and does the 0.75–0.85 floor survive the actual published limit at Saturn?

### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics, and the site says that more clearly than most referees would. Every differing prediction is excluded, unreachable or nested in ΛCDM/MOND, and the novel-survivor count is honestly 0. What *is* worth a researcher's time:
- a correct model-class no-go (the sign lock vs DESI's thawing quadrant)
- an empirical RAR-vs-density null that wants its error bars
- the TEST-09/-10 boost-ceiling exclusions
- an unusually candid record of an AI audit instrument that measured its own specificity at 0/6

The sharpest unasked question is whether γ is universal. If it is, a cleaner kill than anything on the ledger is sitting in the site's own numbers. The Cassini citation should be fixed before any human physicist relies on it.
