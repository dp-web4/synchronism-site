**Short answer:** I haven't searched, so this comes from memory. I don't know of a published paper with this exact construction. The idea is a standard one, though, and it falls under existing frameworks. Treat all citations below as uncertain.

**What it reduces to**
- **Environment-dependent coupling.** A G that depends on local density is the generic behaviour of screened scalar-tensor theories. These include chameleons (Khoury & Weltman, PRL 93, 171104, 2004), symmetrons (Hinterbichler & Khoury, PRL 104, 231301, 2010), and the Damour–Polyakov least-coupling mechanism (Nucl. Phys. B 423, 532, 1994). In those models the effective G or coupling depends on ρ through the scalar's local value, usually not through a density smoothed over a length ℓ. Your f(ρ̄_ℓ) with a smoothing length looks like a phenomenological or nonlocal version of this.
- **Varying constants from the Sun's motion through a gradient.** Uzan's review (Living Rev. Relativ. 14, 2, 2011) discusses spatial and temporal variation of constants and the resulting tests. Mota & Barrow (MNRAS 349, 281, 2004; PLB 581, 141, 2004) treat local-versus-cosmological variation of α and G with density. Hees, Minazzoli et al. (PRD 98, 064051, 2018) derive time variation of constants as the Earth moves through a scalar field or dark-matter background. I believe Stadnik & Flambaum have similar work on constants varying with a background field.
- **The LLR bound.** The standard values are Williams, Turyshev & Boggs (PRL 93, 261101, 2004), with Ġ/G = (4±9)×10⁻¹³ yr⁻¹. Hofmann & Müller (CQG 35, 035015, 2018) report a tighter value of order 10⁻¹⁴–10⁻¹³ yr⁻¹. Your |Ġ/G| ≲ 10⁻¹³ yr⁻¹ is consistent with these.

**Check of the estimate**
- v⊙ ≈ 20 km/s ≈ 2×10⁻⁵ pc/yr.
- Dividing 10⁻¹³ yr⁻¹ by v⊙ times a gradient scale of 1–10 pc gives d ln f/d ln ρ ≲ 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁷.
- The upper end of your range, 10⁻⁵, would need a gradient scale of about 100 pc or more. At that scale the bound weakens as the scale grows, so I would state the gradient scale explicitly and not tie it to ℓ.
- "Independent of the form of f" holds only to first order. It also assumes f is locally smooth and ρ̄_ℓ changes slowly.

**Caveats a referee would raise**
1. For ℓ of 1–10 pc, the Sun's own mass, about 1 M☉/pc³, is comparable to or larger than the ISM density of about 0.1 M☉/pc³. This part of ρ̄_ℓ is nearly constant along the Sun's path, so it doesn't drive Ġ. The gradient term must use only the external density.
2. The ISM is clumpy, so ∇ ln ρ̄ at a few pc is not well defined. The Sun's passage through the Local Bubble and the Local Interstellar Cloud gives sudden changes, not a steady drift.
3. LLR measures a combination of the Earth–Moon G M and the ephemeris, so it constrains Ġ only if the effect is the same for the Sun, Earth and Moon.
4. Screened models have a much stronger bound from the equivalence principle and from planetary tests. Those bounds may beat this one.

**Verdict:** I believe the specific f(ρ̄_ℓ) drift bound is probably not published as such. The result is a straightforward application of known varying-G and screening phenomenology. I'd cite the works above and describe it as a derived corollary, not a new model.
