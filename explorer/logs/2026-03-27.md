## Key Findings
- `hill-vs-tanh-baseline-artifact.md`: **The Hill function's victory over tanh was an artifact of a missing baseline parameter.** The coupling-coherence experiment's agents achieve C ≈ 0.34 with zero coupling (through individual Bayesian observation). Both 2-param models force C(0)=0, creating a +0.34 offset error. Hill's power-law onset (k=0.60<1) absorbs this error better than tanh's linear onset. With a 3-param fit including baseline: tanh wins by ΔAIC=17.6, R²=0.9984. This overturns a key experiment conclusion and actually validates tanh.

### Key quantitative results
| Comparison | Winner | ΔAIC |
|-----------|--------|------|
| 2-param (original) | Hill | 3.8 |
| 3-param (corrected) | **tanh** | **17.6** |

### Three insights beyond the reversal
1. **Hill and tanh are the same function family**: Both are "sigmoid of log." Hill = σ(k·ln(p/K)), tanh model ≈ tanh(γ·log(p/p_crit+1)). The +1 regularization is the structural difference. With baseline modeled, effective exponents converge (Hill k=0.80, tanh 2γ=0.70).
2. **Log-argument models crush linear-argument models**: tanh and Hill (log) beat logistic and erf (linear) by ΔAIC > 200. This is the most robust result — the logarithmic scaling of the effective coupling field is strongly confirmed.
3. **The baseline C₀ ≈ 0.34 is itself a finding**: Agents achieve substantial coherence alone. Coupling adds 0.60 units. The physics has no analogue to this (C(ρ=0) = 0 exactly).
