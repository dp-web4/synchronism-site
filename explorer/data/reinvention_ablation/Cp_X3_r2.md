**Short answer:** I found no published model with this exact form, and I didn't run a search. This answer comes from memory, so treat it as a judgement and not a verified literature check. The specific form m_eff = m[1+κ ln(1+P/τ)]⁻¹, with τ = 10⁻⁶·2π/H₀ and κ = 0.31, looks unpublished to me.

**Closest published relatives (none is the same construction):**
- **Milgrom's modified inertia (MOND).** Milgrom, "Dynamics with a non-standard inertia-acceleration relation," Ann. Phys. 229, 384 (1994). It also appears in Milgrom's 1983 ApJ papers and in later reviews such as Famaey & McGaugh, Living Rev. Rel. 15, 10 (2012). In these models the inertia depends on acceleration, not orbital period. The scale a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π ties MOND to H₀, which is the same cosmological link your τ makes. For circular orbits, acceleration and period are related through the orbit size, so a P-dependent inertia would act like a modified-inertia law. Your form does not reduce to a standard MOND interpolating function, though. It is logarithmic and has a hard onset scale, whereas MOND's scale is set by acceleration alone.
- **McCulloch's quantised inertia (MiHsC).** MNRAS 376, L6 (2007), and later papers. Inertia is reduced by a Hubble-horizon cutoff on Unruh waves. It is acceleration-based and has a different functional form. McCulloch has applied it to wide binaries. I am not sure of those citations.
- **Non-local or memory-type inertia, and time-dependent gravity.** Examples are Deffayet–Woodard-type non-local gravity and Milgrom's non-local MOND (Bimetric MOND is a different case). They involve timescales, but none uses this P-dependent mass rescaling. I am unsure of the specifics.

**Wide-binary data the model would need to match:**
- Chae 2023, ApJ 952, 128, reports a MOND-like boost.
- Hernandez 2023, MNRAS 525, 1401, also reports a boost.
- Banik et al. 2024, MNRAS 527, 4573, find no anomaly and exclude MOND.
- Pittordis & Sutherland have also published on this.

Treat these as uncertain citations and check the details.

**Physical concerns:**
- **Equivalence principle.** A period-dependent m_eff breaks the equivalence principle for bound systems unless gravitational mass is rescaled in step. Inertial mass that depends on a system property is not a local, covariant quantity.
- **Solar-system constraints.** At P ≪ τ the correction is of order κ·P/τ. For Earth, P/τ ≈ 10⁻⁵, so the shift is about 3×10⁻⁶ in m_eff, which is likely still constrained by planetary ephemerides. Check this.
- **Wide-binary scale.** P ≳ 9×10⁴ yr corresponds to separations of roughly 10⁴ AU for a total mass of about 1 M☉. This is the right regime for the wide-binary tests.
- **Rotation curves.** The logarithmic growth means the boost is never flat. It would not produce flat rotation curves without extra structure.
