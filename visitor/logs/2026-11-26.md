## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /interactive-tools, /coherence-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /glossary, /what-synchronism-is-not, /honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: An unusually honest "we tried an idea and it mostly didn't work" notebook. The crowd-vs-marching-band picture and the galaxy plot landed. Almost everything past the first screen of each page is written for someone who already knows what MOND, SPARC and ΔBIC are.

### Journey
I came from a Reddit link. The landing headline, "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?", is exactly the kind of hook that makes me click on a PBS Space Time video, so it worked. Then the subtitle undercut it right away: "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything." The first paragraphs say the idea was tested and "did not hold as stated," that AI agents did the research, and that no journal or outside physicist has reviewed it. Further down I found "As tested physics, dead so far." My honest reaction was a mix of "huh, refreshing" and "then why am I here?" The opening does point to an answer ("The full answer," next to "So is it alive or dead?"), and the line "Below this line: for people checking the work" was a helpful signal that I could stop scrolling. I didn't stop, being nosy. Just past the line I ran into "1.4% internal-consistency survival rate," "~3,300 A2ACW sessions," "ΔBIC +2,843," and "Refutations executed: 6," followed by a caveat that it's "4–8 under rules already on file." The site had warned me this part wasn't for me, so that's fair. Still, a refutation count that changes with a "counting rule" made me trust the number less, not more. (I first wrote this down as if those numbers were above the line. I rechecked, and they aren't. The divider is doing its job.)

I clicked the big "Start here: Why Synchronism? (~8 min)." The opening is good: "Physics has a fragmentation problem," with quantum rules for small things, gravity for big things and nothing joining them. I've heard that on YouTube, so I was nodding. The "what happened, in three lines" box was the clearest thing on the whole site: one equation for how crowding makes things act as one, tested mostly on galaxies, and where it could be told apart from existing physics it lost. Then came "Why does matching an existing theory count as losing?" I liked that one; it taught me something about how science works. The second paragraph lost me, though. It throws "density function," "self-audit," "the decisive galaxy test," MOND, and "the broader question of the frame" at me before anything is explained. The rest of the page is folded, which I appreciated. When I peeked into "The Approach," it went straight into tanh, ln, ρcrit, M☉/pc³, "compander," "μ-law," "Hill/Naka–Rushton" and "Hamiltonian." The page says the first two folded sections are plain English. They aren't, not for me.

Next I went to First Encounter (step 2). Section A was great: a lone electron versus a neutron star "10¹⁴× denser than lead." That's the stuff I come for. Section B gave me the marching band again, plus a "dimmer switch" for the S-curve, and I got it: more crowded means the parts act more together, and the change is gradual. But the equation in B already uses γ and ρ_crit, and γ isn't explained until section C. Section C ("the formula for γ failed...") turns on the difference between a "predicted" and a "fitted" number. I had to read it twice, and I'm still not sure I could explain it back. Sections E and F (galaxies, quantum measurement) brought in a₀ ≈ cH₀/2π, "wave function collapse," "interference fringes," "CRT" and "QM" without spelling them out. I skimmed them.

The Interactive Tools index was tidy, with six tools labelled by level. But several Beginner/Intermediate cards say things like "This formula is audited-negative; the calculator shows why" and "catch a refuted formula being wrong at every stop." Is a toy built to show me a wrong formula fun? Weirdly, a little. It also tells me most of these tools are exhibits of failure.

**Coherence Explorer.** I liked the "What is coherence?" box (crowd of strangers vs. marching band again). The controls confused me. The γ slider is labelled "how wide the switch is," which is nice, but the readout says "10–90% width 1.50 dex" and I don't know what a dex is. The preset buttons read "γ = 0.05 · density fit (lost)," "γ = 0.49 · galaxy fit," "γ = ½ · same as MOND," "γ = 2 · original guess." Lost to what? Same as what? The page says the density axis "has no units" and the tool "does not model any real material," which is honest but leaves me asking what I'm learning. My answer: the S-curve's steepness changes with γ and ρcrit slides it sideways. That part is clear.

**Galaxy Curve Plotter** (step 3) was my favourite page. "New here? Look at three things on the chart below" is exactly how to talk to me. Dots are telescope data, the grey dashed line is what visible matter alone predicts, and it "sags below the dots." That's the dark matter problem in one picture, and I finally understood why people say galaxies spin too fast. "The violet curve does not match. That is the whole lesson." Clear. Then the controls: "How heavy are the stars? Υdisk = 0.50 … mass per unit of 3.6 μm light" (what?), and a calibration dropdown choosing between "Archive's own form: ρcrit = 0.028·Vflat^0.5" and "Units hybrid: 0.029·Vflat²." I left that alone. The readout "the highest Cρ on this disc is 0.244" means nothing to me without a sentence saying whether 0.244 is a lot or a little.

The glossary has a "New here? Start with these ten" list, a great idea. But three of the ten (γ, tanh, σ) are maths symbols, and the entries I read were dense. With 136 terms it felt like an encyclopedia, not a cheat sheet. "What Synchronism Is Not" had a good "In plain words, before the detail" box, and its "Not proven / Not peer reviewed / Not a replacement" cards were the easiest thing to take in after the landing page. "Honest Assessment" has an "If you read nothing else" box ("zero confirmed predictions, six executed refutations") and a "What failed, in one paragraph — no jargon" passage. That paragraph still says "rotation curves," "dwarf spheroidal," "standard deviations," and a "cap" on missing gravity, so it isn't jargon-free for me. The page itself is enormous: dozens of sections and dated revision notes. I bounced after the first box.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer.** Worked: the page rendered with γ = 1.00, ρcrit = 1.00, readouts C(ρcrit) = 0.6000, C(10ρcrit) = 0.9836, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9998, a log/linear toggle and five γ presets. Partly confusing: the plain "What is coherence?" box and the "how wide the switch is" slider label are intuitive, but the preset labels ("density fit (lost)," "same as MOND") and "dex" are not. I could see the controls and default readouts but could not drag them through a fetch, so I didn't watch the curve move live.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter.** Worked: five galaxy buttons (DDO 154 default), Υdisk slider at 0.50, a calibration dropdown, and a chart with dots, a grey "visible matter only" line, green MOND and violet framework curves. Mostly intuitive: the "look at three things" intro is the best beginner writing on the site. The Υdisk and calibration controls are not intuitive. I didn't change controls, only read the default view.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the idea is "dead so far," what is the site for now? Is it a lesson in how to test ideas, or is someone still hoping part of it works? I couldn't tell which.
2. What exactly is MOND, in one sentence? It keeps coming up as the thing this framework "turns into," and I only got "a 1983 formula that tweaks gravity."
3. Why is matching MOND bad if MOND fits the galaxy dots? Is MOND itself right? (The plotter shows MOND's green line fitting, which made me wonder why everyone doesn't just use MOND.)
4. What is a "refutation," and why can the count be 4 or 8 depending on rules? To me a test is passed or failed.
5. What does "coherence" in this sense have to do with quantum stuff at all, if a superconductor scores *low* on it?
6. Who is "dp," and were the AI agents the ones deciding what counts as a failure?

### Verdict
This is the most self-critical science site I've seen, and the first screens of each page, plus the Galaxy Plotter's "look at three things" intro, are genuinely good at teaching me something (why galaxies need dark matter; why copying an old formula doesn't count as a discovery). But past the landing page, every page turns technical within one or two paragraphs. Even the boxes labelled "plain English" or "no jargon" slip in rotation curves, MOND and Greek letters. The landing page is the exception: its "below this line" divider fences off the expert counts well. I'd recommend the galaxy plotter to a friend; I wouldn't recommend the site as a whole to someone like me without saying "read the first box, then stop."

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Journey
I began on the landing page and mapped the information architecture before reading for content. The header has nine items (Start Here, Learning Paths, Tier 1: Existing Data, Test Roadmap, Interactive Tools, Glossary, Research Philosophy, For Researchers, Honest Assessment). The footer repeats them and adds Key Claims, First Encounter, What this is not, and three deep links into Honest Assessment (#what-was-tested, #count-rule, #pending-decisions). I checked all three anchors against the page HTML and they resolve. The landing page makes specific claims about the learning paths: "Beginner Path · Step 1 of 5", "Advanced Path step 8 of 8 / Test Roadmap", "Methodology Track step 1 of 6 / Research Philosophy". I checked each against /learning-paths. All of them match on step count, order and endpoint. The only slip is that the landing says "Advanced Path" while the paths page calls it "Advanced". It's rare to see cross-page promises kept this precisely.

The landing page's acronym handling is a half-solution I'd actually recommend to other teams, with one gap. MOND, A2ACW and MRH are expanded at first use. A line reading "Abbreviations ahead (RAR, ΔBIC, BTFR, Ωm, Ncorr, CFD, SPARC)" warns the reader before a dense section, and a key defines them further down. But three terms slip through on the landing page with no definition anywhere on it: **LIGO** (scoreboard table), **RC100** (a₀(z) row) and **Ġ/G**. I then searched the glossary's HTML for LIGO and RC100. Neither appears anywhere in the 136-term glossary. Ncorr is used in the opening section ("γ = 2/√Ncorr") before the "Abbreviations ahead" line names it.

Next I went to Interactive Tools. It lists **seven** cards (Pass 1 counted six; I verified seven links in the HTML). Each card has three labelled slots: Kind ("Model Explainer"), Level (Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced) and State ("Artifact Lesson"), plus the "Badge on its page". The intro explains those slots carefully, which helps, but a tool card now carries up to four kinds of label. One card, "Consciousness Threshold Demo", says on its own face that it is "a static chart, not an interactive tool, with no controls." A non-interactive item in the Interactive Tools hub is an IA smell. The same card says "no calibration to EEG/fMRI/IIT/PCI* exists". To a documentation reader, that asterisk looks like a footnote marker, and the page has no footnote. If it's notation (I gather it's the name of a threshold value), it needs a word saying so. None of EEG, fMRI, IIT or PCI is expanded on that page. Key Claims expands them.

On /honest-assessment I went for the badge reference that the landing page links to (#validation-badge-definitions). It's good: "The nine badges in one table", split into an MRH-relationship family (Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative) and a descriptive family (Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed). The count matches the landing's "full set of nine". (My fetch summary first said Failed belonged to no family. Reading the raw table, the family tag is printed only on the first row of each group, so Failed is descriptive. If a fetch tool misreads it, a scanning human might too. Putting a family column on every row would cost nothing.) Two things bothered me as an editor. First, the caption of this canonical reference reads: "added 2026-10-10; a technical-writer persona clicked a badge wanting one sentence and got a taxonomy", and a few lines later, "(Added 2026-11-24 after a technical-writer persona counted six label systems.)" Your reference docs are annotated with notes about your simulated reviewers. Readers don't know what a "technical-writer persona" is, and it isn't their concern. Second, that 11-24 fix responded to "six label systems" by adding a sentence explaining them, not by reducing them. The glossary's "How to read labels on this site" table now lists **ten** label systems: two badge families, scoreboard word, ledger class, retired label, operational state, lifecycle verb, descriptor/qualifier, tool Kind and tool Level. A separate list of "Values that appear in a card's State slot" draws from the operational states and lifecycle verbs. Every system is defined, but as a reader I now need a legend to read the legend.

The page itself is large (~200k characters of HTML) and has a 22-link "On this page" table of contents. That's the right call, and every anchor I tested resolved. The H1 sits under two step indicators ("Beginner Path, Step 5 of 5" and "Methodology Track, Step 6 of 6"). That's correct but crowded, and the page's own TOC is the stronger navigation aid.

The glossary (/glossary) opens with "136 terms", an A–Z index plus a Greek section, and marks the badges with "(badge)". The coherence entry clearly separates the site's meaning from quantum coherence ("this is NOT the 'quantum coherence' you may have heard of"). That is exactly the warning a first-time reader needs. The #outcome-words and #which-c-which-gamma anchors exist. (My fetch tool claimed #outcome-words was missing; the HTML says otherwise.) The glossary also has two anchors for the same term in different cases (id="MOND" and id="mond") and an id that contains spaces and parentheses (id="MOND-shared (retired marker)"). Neither is visible to readers, but the spaced id is not a valid fragment target.

Learning Paths is the cleanest page on the site. It has seven paths, each with a step count and time estimate, and each step links to the right page. Beginner even says "~37 min on the short route; ~2 h reading every page in full", the most honest reading-time estimate I've seen. On /coherence-function I followed the path furniture and it worked. The page shows "Intermediate Path, Step 2 of 9" and "Physics Track, Step 1 of 7" together, with correct Prev/Next for each and footer "Next on…" links. The breadcrumb is "Home / Core Theory / The Coherence Function", but "Core Theory" isn't a link. Its hover title says "no index page; see Learning Paths". A breadcrumb level that can't be clicked, explained only in a tooltip, will be clicked, fail, and confuse. The page's "Symbols in this equation" list defines C, ρcrit, γ, ln and tanh right under the formula, which is model practice. The "Which C, in one table" section is the right idea, but its cells contain whole research logs (one cell runs ~900 characters through ΔBIC values, smoothing scales and dated explorer results). A lookup table is for looking things up.

Key Claims is the best-documented page I read for badges. Next to the badges it gives one-line definitions linked into the glossary (#failed-badge, #speculative-badge, #reparametrization-badge), notes that "text after a dash is a finding descriptor, not a separate badge", and links the canonical reference. It also expands MOND, SPARC, ΛCDM, RAR and MRH at first use. Every badge-bearing page should copy this pattern. It does use a symbol I hadn't met elsewhere, "C_f ≥ Ω_m", so the "C" family on the site has at least Cρ, Cf, Cg, Ca, plus the consciousness "C = f(γ, D, S)". The glossary does carry a "C_f (floored density form)" entry, so this is defined, just widely spread.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (/gamma-calculator). Worked: the Ncorr input defaults to 4 and gives "γ = 1.0000" (2/√4 = 1, correct), a regime label "Crossover band (0.6 < γ ≤ 1.4…)", and a chart reading C(ρcrit) = 0.60 with a γ = 2 ghost curve. - **Crossover Regime Visualizer** (/crossover-regime-visualizer). Worked: one γ slider (0–4, step 0.01, default 1.0) with band edges labelled "N≈11.1" and "N≈2.04", and reference markers (BEC/SC at the left edge, water/enzymes, γ=0.49, γ=2). Partly confusing: the slider's axis has tick numbers but no axis title, and the zone vocabulary drifts (regime/band/range). The old name "Phase Boundary Visualizer" survives only in one explanatory sentence, which is right.
- **Equation Anatomy** (/equation-walkthrough), briefly. The server HTML shows the intro and a "Variables in this equation — defined before we start" block. The eight steps ("Step 1: Start with density" … "Step 8: What the equation is at γ = ½") are client-rendered behind a "Next →" button, so I read their titles from the page's JS bundle. A non-JS reader or search engine sees only the intro.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Which of the ten label systems does a reader actually need in order to answer "did this part survive?" The Honest Assessment caption says "read the scoreboard word". If so, why is the coloured pill (the badge) the most visually prominent label on every page?
2. Who is the audience for the dated revision notes in body text? If it's the research team, could they live in git history or a per-page changelog fold?
3. Is "Core Theory" meant to be a section? It appears in breadcrumbs but has no page and isn't in the header nav.
4. Where should an abbreviation be defined canonically: the landing key, the glossary, or each page's symbol strip? Today it's sometimes all three and sometimes none (LIGO, RC100).
5. Is the "γ Calculator" name kept for search/URL stability? If so, say so. If not, rename it the way the crossover tool was renamed.

### Verdict
The structure is better than most research sites I've audited. Learning paths match every claim the landing makes about them. Breadcrumbs and per-track Prev/Next work. The big reference page has a real TOC with working anchors. Key Claims, the γ Calculator's "watch / do / conclude" block and Equation Anatomy's "defined before we start" block are patterns worth copying everywhere. The debt is in labelling and editorial hygiene. Ten label systems, sentence-length badges, and revision notes (some about the site's own simulated reviewers) in reader-facing text mean each local fix has added words instead of removing concepts. The next pass should consolidate labels rather than explain more of them.

- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /interactive-tools, /parameter-derivations, /key-claims, /galaxy-rotation, /coherence-function, /honest-assessment, /for-researchers, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /consciousness-demo)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes, as a reference for how a MOND mimic fails and for its worked identities. Not as a theory.
- **Overall impression**: The algebra is almost all right, the reparametrizations are named before I could name them, and no live page still carries a "Validated" badge. I found one real algebra slip and two ambiguous sentences. The physics is easy to check. What's hard is pulling one number per claim out of the layers of corrections around it.

### Journey
I read the landing's "for people checking the work" fold first and checked the equation's stated properties numerically. C(ρcrit) at γ = 1 is tanh(ln 2) = 0.6 exactly. The 10–90% width tends to 1.17 dex as γ → ∞, and I got 1.50 at γ = 1 and 1.33 at γ = 2. C is concave in linear ρ, since both factors of dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x) decrease. The closed form tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^2γ − 1]/[(1+x)^2γ + 1] holds, so at γ = ½ it is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). That is exactly what /coherence-function and /parameter-derivations say. They also say the right thing about what it means: an identity of functional form, which is physically MOND only when x is g_obs/a₀′. The "four functions" table on the landing (Cρ, Cf, Cg, Ca) is the single most useful thing on the site for a physicist. Without it I would have assumed the refutations tested the headline equation. Most of them don't: TEST-09 and TEST-10 test a floored Hill function of g_bar with an exponent fitted and then named after φ.

On /parameter-derivations I recomputed the Jeans normalization. With G = 4.301×10⁻³ pc (km/s)²/M☉ and R₀ = 8 kpc, A = 4π/(G R₀²) = 4.565×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻². The ratio 0.0294/4.565×10⁻⁵ = 644 matches. 4.565×10⁻⁵ V² and 0.028 V^0.5 cross at V = 72 km/s, and the ×0.27 and ×8.5 ratios at 30 and 300 km/s check out. The MOND transition density ρ ∝ a₀²/(GV²) follows from M = V⁴/(Ga₀) and r_t = V²/a₀. a₀ = cH₀/2π gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² at H₀ = 67.4, and Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) gives 119.0 M☉/pc². Milgrom's a₀/G at 1.2×10⁻¹⁰ is 861, and dividing by 2π gives 137. The evolving ceiling gives B_max(z = 1) = 1.27 and B_max(z = 2) = 1.08, and Ω_b/Ω_m = 0.1565 gives a cap of 6.39. All correct. The CLT and Curie–Weiss critique of γ = 2/√N_corr is the critique I'd have written. Finite-size rounding in mean-field theory goes as N^(−1/2), so a sharpness parameter should grow as √N, and the Ginzburg number for clean BCS goes as (T_c/E_F)⁴. The page says the BCS figure is "recalled, not re-derived", which is honest.

On /coherence-function I checked the deep-limit conversion. Solving g_bar = g_obs·γ g_obs/a₀′ gives a₀ = a₀′/γ = 5.33×10⁻¹¹/0.489 = 1.09×10⁻¹⁰. The claim that a₀′/γ, not a₀′, is the identified combination is correct and is the right way to say it. The explicit-on-g_bar reading at γ = ½ gives g_obs = g_bar + 2a₀′, a constant additive floor, which is correct. The Refracted Gravity permittivity comparison also checks: at γ = 2, x = 1, p = 4 with the knee doubled it gives y = ½ and y⁴/(1+y⁴) = 0.059. The field equation ∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ reduces to g = g_N/C in spherical symmetry, as stated. It also states the vacuum problem itself: the operator vanishes where C → 0 and nothing constrains Φ. That gap is what I'd have used to dismiss the field equation, and the site says it first.

/key-claims checks out too. Γ = κ²(1−c) is the κ_A = κ_B case of (κ_A² + κ_B² − 2cκ_Aκ_B)/2. "10× T₂ at c = 0.9" is just c = 1 − 1/R, and the page says so. 512 = 2⁹ makes "0 of 512" the Peres–Mermin theorem itself, and the page says that too. The scanning-equals-mixture objection is correct: a time-average over modes kills the off-diagonals, so single-particle fringes kill the literal reading, and the phase-keeping version is QM relabelled. Quadrature v² = v_b² + (V_flat C)² gives an unbounded boost as v_b → 0, also correct. /galaxy-rotation's "γ ≈ ½ twice is not a cross-sector agreement" uses 1/C − 1 = 2/x at γ = ½. Keyed on g_obs that is the simple μ. Keyed on mean ρ_m it adds a constant to the Friedmann source term, which is Λ. That is the cleanest dissolution of an apparent coincidence I've seen on a site like this.

/honest-assessment's verdict box is honest. Of the six refutations, only TEST-10 survives every counting rule while also refuting the framework's own content on external data. The Draco estimators (Wolf's 4σ²R_e/G and Walker's 5/2) are the standard ones. /for-researchers withdraws its own no-go's generality on finding Burrage–Copeland–Millington 2017. That paper reproduces the RAR from local ρ via a gradient force, not a multiplier. The narrowed statement, which excludes algebraic local-density modulation C(ρ)·g and nothing more, is correct and is a genuinely reusable lemma.

The tools are where I found the slip. In the second explanatory paragraph on /consciousness-demo, the steepest point on a log-ρ axis is given as "C* = 1/(2γu*), u = ln(1 + ρ/ρcrit), which is C* = ½ at γ = ½". That is wrong. I derived it: d ln(dC/d ln ρ)/d ln ρ = 0 gives 2γCx = 1 with x = ρ/ρcrit, so the u inside the formula must be ρ/ρcrit. The calibration caveat on the same page says exactly that, "u = ρ/ρcrit". With u = ln(1+x), γ = ½ gives x* = 2, u* = ln 3, and 1/(2·½·ln 3) = 0.91, not 0.50. I checked numerically: x* = 2.000 with C* = 0.500 at γ = ½, x* = 0.416 with C* = 0.601 at γ = 2, and C* = 0.497 at γ = 0.487. So the caveat's numbers are right and the second definition is a typo-level error. It matters on the one page whose whole argument is about that point.

Worked: the filter chips "All (23) / Neutral (4) / Coherence helps (12) / Incoherence helps (4) / Barrier-dominated (3)" sum to 23, and my count of the rendered table matches each category. 13 rows print an r and 10 print "—" with an "Untraced" trace tag. ### Verdict
As physics, this is a reparametrization of MOND (acceleration-keyed) bolted to a density-keyed variant that loses, and the site says exactly that with correct algebra. I checked about thirty numbers and identities and found one genuine slip (a mis-defined variable on the consciousness demo) and two ambiguous sentences. No live "Validated" badges remain, and the Reparametrization badges sit on the right claims, including the strongest one, the exact γ = ½ ↔ μ_simple identity. ### Journey
I came in hunting for one thing: a prediction that differs from MOND + ΛCDM, has a number attached, and hasn't been tested yet. The landing answers early: "As tested physics, dead so far: no prediction that differs from MOND or the standard cosmology has survived." /for-researchers repeats it in its first line ("No dataset on the horizon can confirm a novel prediction of this framework") and then does what I'd ask of any paper under review. It gathers every item that differs from MOND, ΛCDM or standard QM into one "distinct-content register". I treated that table as the paper.

**Galaxies.** The acceleration-keyed law is MOND's simple μ at γ = ½. The density-keyed law is excluded by SPARC ΔBIC, by lunar laser ranging (Ġ/G = (7.1 ± 7.6)×10⁻¹⁴/yr, Hofmann & Müller 2018, which I recognised; 68 of 74 laws excluded at 1 pc smoothing), and by Big Bang nucleosynthesis. The BBN argument is the nicest physics I found today. Lab G is measured at the same smoothed density as the solar neighbourhood, so the local amplitude cancels, but BBN ran at C = 1, so G_BBN/G_lab = C(ρ_local). Against Alvey et al. 2020 (G_BBN/G₀ = 0.99 +0.06/−0.05) that excludes the archive knee at 17–36σ. The Oort check against Bovy & Rix's Σ₁.₁ = 68 ± 4 M☉/pc² is the right observable for a height gradient. I checked TEST-09's BTFR row expecting the usual trick of comparing against MOND's deep-limit n = 4 when the data say 3.75. It isn't there: MOND is run through the same pipeline (3.81 ± 0.04, 0.6σ), and the card separately notes that 3.75 sits ~2.5σ below canonical n = 4. That's like-for-like, done properly. The Cassini row correctly books the failure as inherited and cites Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024 for the marginalized number, not just its own unmarginalized +17.95σ.

**Dark energy.** H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C(ρ_m)) is a Cardassian model (Freese & Lewis 2002). The page says so, adds that it is Δχ² ≤ 0.42 from constant-w Cardassian at DR3 precision, and notes Koivisto, Kurki-Suonio & Ravndal 2005 on Cardassian ISW. I checked the claimed GEDE match myself: 1/(1+x/2) = ½[1 − tanh(½ ln(x/2))], and with x ∝ (1+z)³ this gives Δ = (3/2) ln 10, as stated. One thing bothers me: the page says Li & Shafieloo's equation "was read by a simulated-visitor persona, not re-read here." The algebra holds, but a literature claim should be checked against the paper, not against an AI reviewer's reading of it. The DESI verdict is the right one. The family nests ΛCDM, its non-Λ branch is freezing, and DR2 leans toward thawing/crossing, so it has "no win branch". The LLR bound kills the modified-G reading by ~2,000×, and I get dlnΩ_m/dt ≈ 2H₀Ω_Λ ≈ 1.4×10⁻¹⁰/yr, consistent.

**Wide binaries / DR4.** The MOND background is right: g_ext ≈ 1.8a₀ at the Sun, a 1.0–1.4× boost with EFE (≈ +18% in v), ~3.7× without. Pittordis & Sutherland, Banik et al. 2024 and the Chae–Saad & Ting semi-major-axis dispute are framed correctly as MOND's question, not this framework's. The DR4 reading key maps boost to compander γ, and every γ it can return is already excluded by Cassini. So DR4 cannot move the novelty count, and the site says that on the landing. The process problem: six days before the release, the key and analysis plan are "neither adopted nor rejected". The plan "returns 'no result' on both samples" when run on DR3. The open-cluster kill line (obs/pred ≤ 0.65) "was chosen after the pre-DR4 values were seen". All of this is disclosed, but a registration that isn't decided by data day isn't a registration.

**The distinct-content register — where the hunt ends.** Nine rows. Dim-4 LIV is Audited-Negative via Collins, Perez, Sudarsky, Urrutia & Vucetich 2004. The naturalness gap of c_μν ~ α/π against 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻³⁰ bounds is correctly computed, and the custodial escapes (Groot Nibbelink & Pospelov 2005; Pospelov & Shang 2012) are correctly cited. External-field refraction belongs to Refracted Gravity. The dark-energy sign lock belongs to Cardassian/wCDM. Intrinsic decoherence ∝ Dk² is killed by nuclear stability (Borexino ¹¹C, D/D_Pl < 4×10⁻³⁹). The ontology is 't Hooft's CAI. Only one row carries a framework-specific number that is not yet excluded: **dim ≥ 5 dispersion on the discrete grid, |η| ≈ 0.03–0.14** in E² = p² + m² + ηp⁴/M_Pl², set against composition-agnostic Auger bounds (photon η ≳ −0.015, hadron η < 0.15) that "sit right there". That is a real research handle: a Planck-suppressed n = 2 coefficient of order 10⁻¹, at current threshold reach. The page files it as "Not unique (loop quantum gravity, causal sets)". **Causal sets are the wrong citation.** Causal set theory is Lorentz-invariant by construction. Poisson sprinkling is the whole point (Bombelli, Henson & Sorkin 2009, "Discreteness without symmetry breaking"), and it predicts no modified dispersion of this kind. LQG (Gambini–Pullin 1999) is a fair, if contested, citation. More important, the register misses the thing that would make the row distinctive. A hypercubic grid with a fixed orientation does not produce an isotropic η. It produces a cubic-anisotropic dispersion: an ℓ = 0 part plus an ℓ = 4 cubic-harmonic part, the latter fixed in the substrate frame. The cell even says η varies "by stencil, direction and species". Isotropic η is generic to many quantum-gravity proposals. A direction-dependent η with cubic symmetry, locked to a preferred frame, is not. It maps onto the anisotropic nonminimal (d = 6) SME coefficients, which Kostelecký & Mewes have tabulated and bounded. The page cites the SME only for dim-4. So the lead sentence "No dataset on the horizon can confirm a novel prediction" is true only because the stencil isn't committed, and AugerPrime composition data are on the horizon. Commit a stencil, compute the ℓ = 4 coefficient per species, and this row becomes the only place where the framework could differ from everything else and win. The register also records that the archive's own "one cell per tick" is CFL-unstable on a face-neighbour 3D update (79% of modes grow). That's a genuine internal inconsistency in the foundations, and it's buried in a table cell.

**A2ACW.** The /a2acw lead is the most citable paragraph on the site: ~3,300 sessions, zero refutations from debate. The refutations came from executing code on external data, or from a theorem. Four prior-art convergences (Famaey & Binney 2005, Freese & Lewis 2002, Matsakos & Diaferio 2016, Collins et al. 2004) were each found by a lane with literature retrieval, never by the protocol. The contrast with FunSearch/AlphaEvolve/GNoME ("the boundary is the oracle") is the right frame. Two gaps make it unpublishable as it stands. First, there is no denominator. "Four instances, none cited in 3,308 sessions" needs "out of how many candidate claims checked for prior art". Second, the post-cutoff, citation-stripped arm, the only experiment that tests whether the auditor can recognise novelty at all, "has never been run". Methodologically the protocol is sound as a consistency filter and unsound as a discovery engine, and the page now says so. Smaller: the same page names the refutations' external data as "SPARC, Cosmicflows-4, Cassini" in the lead and as "SPARC, DESI, Cassini and the globular-cluster catalogue" further down. Neither list includes Draco (Geha et al.) or KiDS lensing.

**Research Philosophy** is Methodology Track step 1, and it still carries the old voice. Its "What this means in practice" list includes "The 89% chemistry validation rate sounds impressive…" and closes the reparametrization paragraph with "…and the testable predictions that remain open." That contradicts /for-researchers ("Is any prediction that differs from MOND or ΛCDM still alive? No"). The same page also introduces two label families I hadn't seen: an amber "Post-diction" sub-status, and five "Speculative sub-types" ("Motivated Ansatz", "Freeman's Law Re-expressed"…). That's on top of the ten systems Pass 2 counted.

**Quantum foundations.** /measurement-without-observers is honest in its table: "Decoherence explains loss of interference but not definite outcomes … Measurement problem reframed, not solved". Its Born-rule gap paragraph correctly describes the partial-trace circularity critique of einselection and the envariance, Deutsch–Wallace and Carroll–Sebens programmes. But the page's own headline sentence still says "that boundary crossing IS the measurement." In the quantum-foundations literature that is the overclaim decoherence was criticised for, and the page's table says so two screens later.

**Refutation count.** /honest-assessment "Which six?" is clear. Two rows test one inequality (the boost cap) on two observables. One is the γ = 2 pin, now "about 2σ once galaxies are the unit". One is a registration the equation "could never have met". One is inherited from MOND, one from Bell plus experiment. No referee would call a 2σ preference a refutation, and the framework-specific ≥3σ refutations on data come from one root: the boost cap, which "no archive document derives". The "by kind" paragraph says all this, but the headline number everywhere is still "6".

### Verdict
Nothing here is new physics that survives, and the site leads with that fact, not a disclaimer. I checked roughly twenty published values (LLR Ġ/G, Bovy & Rix Σ₁.₁, Alvey BBN G ratio, Collins et al. The one physics error I found is a prior-art misattribution (causal sets), and it sits on the one row that matters. That row is also where the program is mis-aimed. It has spent its effort confirming that galaxy and cosmology branches are MOND and ΛCDM. The only framework-specific, numerically live content is a Planck-scale lattice dispersion whose distinctive feature, preferred-frame cubic anisotropy, nobody has computed. The transferable results deserve a short paper: the C(ρ)·g locality lemma, the LLR and BBN bounds on any density-keyed G, and the A2ACW oracle null once it has a denominator. Most of the remaining effort would be better spent on the lattice row than on further MOND bookkeeping.
