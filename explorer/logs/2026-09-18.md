# Explorer Session — 2026-09-18

## WAKE

**1. What assumptions am I inheriting?**
From the primer and queue: that the ledger's *outcomes* are the thing under audit, and that the
instrument (registration → execution → record) is sound and only its inputs need checking. Ninety
topics in the queue, and nearly all of them audit a claim, a number or a page. Almost none audit the
apparatus that produces claims. The three seeded today are the exception, and that is why I took one.

**2. What if the frame is wrong?**
The program measures its pre-registration discipline on a *timing* axis — "0 of 24 completed
prospectively", "registered before the data were analysed", the commit-hash-before-execution habit. That
is half of what pre-registration buys. The other half is **specificity**: a criterion filed early but
stated as "no correlation" defers the decision rule to execution time exactly as completely as one filed
late. Nothing on the site measures that half. If the frame is wrong, it is wrong here — and the fix is
cheap, which is the best kind of wrong frame to find.

**3. What would most increase information right now?**
Counting it. The site's own registry is a `kill:` field in four page files, so the population is
enumerable and the statistic is mechanical. A denominator where there has never been one.

**4. What would falsify the current posture?**
If the registry turned out to be mostly well-formed (CLEAN ≥ 20 of 26), the topic's TEST-04a instance is
isolated and the apparatus is fine. I pre-registered that outcome as reportable. It is not what happened.

**Decision**: took `audit-registered-kill-criteria-for-internal-inconsistency.md` from the queue, and

### What was refuted along the way

- **Retro-fitting is not happening.** I expected the precise criteria got precise near execution.
  Reconstructing every `kill:` string over 65 commits back to 2026-02-21 shows the opposite: TEST-09's
  threshold was fixed eleven weeks *before* execution, TEST-04a's ten weeks before, and every
  post-execution edit to an executed row is a result annotation appended to the field, not a threshold
  change. A clean negative, and it makes the real finding (15 of 19 never-run criteria untouched since
  the first commit) harder to read uncharitably — not revising a criterion you are not executing is

## Rabbit Holes (Worth Revisiting)

- **The alias table.** I paired three site/archive rows by content in a few minutes and found a
  transcription loss in each. The archive's own namespace declaration says the reconciliation table "is
  the artifact worth producing" (REC-2026-036) and it has never been built. A content-similarity pass over
  the two registries produces the alias table *and* the full transcription-loss list in one run. Highest
  value thing I know of in this thread.
- **The equivalence-margin repair.** How many of the 15 exact-null criteria become CLEAN if each simply
  names the effect size it must exclude? My guess is most, cheaply — which would make this a repairable
  defect rather than a structural one. Untested.
- **TEST-01's power statement** generalises: a null-*acceptance* criterion stated at a σ with no
  equivalence bound asserts a precision no sample size on the card can deliver. N = 141 is ~284× short of
  what excluding the framework's own r² ~ 10⁻⁴ lever at 2σ requires. How many other criteria are
  null-acceptances?

## Self-Seeded Questions

- **Does specificity predict outcome?** Both CLEAN rows and all three significance-stating rows are
  executed or imminent. n = 26 with 7 executed cannot test it, but "did the criterion state a σ before it
  ran?" is a scorable prospective variable if the program keeps executing.
- **What is the registry *for*?** 15 rows untouched for seven months, several depending on quantities the
  site has globally adjudicated as unmeasurable, and the count ("24 registered experiments") is quoted as
  evidence of falsifiability. Sharpen them, or move them out of the count? dp-gated; not the explorer's
  call, and I have not made it.
- **Is the timing/specificity split the general shape?** The program has strong machinery for *when* a
  claim was made and weak machinery for *how much it says*. Correction-trail work (2026-09-17) measures
  when and in which direction; nobody measures whether the corrected sentence is more specific than the
  one it replaced. Same axis, different object.

## So What?

The site can now say how falsifiable its roadmap is with a number instead of an adjective, and the number
(2 of 26 well-formed; 42 % containing no numeral, replicated across two disjoint registries) contradicts a
sentence the site prints on four surfaces including its search-engine description — a sentence its own
TEST-07 row refutes in the field itself. That is discovery, not documentation: nothing here was known, the
denominator did not exist, and one concrete registered criterion (TEST-15) turns out to be materially
weaker on the cited registry than as registered.

The frame claim I would defend beyond this audit: **pre-registration has a cheap half and an expensive
half, and this program measures only the cheap one.** Timing is easy to check and the site checks it
relentlessly. Specificity is what makes a criterion adjudicate anything, and until today it had never been
counted — on either registry, in seven months, across ~3,300 sessions.
