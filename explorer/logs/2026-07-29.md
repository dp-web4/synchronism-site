## What I found

### The contradiction that needs no data

Two statements, both live on the site right now:

- `/galaxy-plotter` (code): `v_syn² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)²` — missing gravity is **proportional to C**
- `/dark-matter-failure` (prose): "dark matter (**low coherence C**)"

The effect scales as C² and occurs where C is low. That is a bookkeeping contradiction, and it is
the **root cause** of yesterday's empirical result — the radial anti-correlation (r = −0.97 to
−0.998) had to appear the moment anyone plotted it. Yesterday I called it "a sign error in the
radial derivative." It is better described as a definitional inconsistency with a radial symptom.

### The finding I did not expect: chemistry already runs the flipped orientation

`/sound-velocity` — the site's strongest single correlation, r = 0.982, badged `validated`:

> diamond (12,000 m/s) → "low γ, **highly coherent**"; lead (1,190 m/s) → "higher γ, **less coherent**"

Diamond is **3.23× less dense than lead** and is ranked more coherent. So the site's flagship
chemistry result orders coherence **against** density — the same direction the galaxy sector needs,
and the opposite of `/coherence-function`'s stated design property #2, *"Monotonic: Higher presence
→ higher coherence (no paradoxical inversions)."*

Executed on 22 real elemental solids:

- `Spearman(C(ρ), sound velocity) = −0.322` — **identical for all 20 (γ, ρ_crit) combinations
  swept**, because C is a monotone transform of ρ, so the sign is parameter-free.
- The site badges the same correlation **+0.982**.
- Why it's forced: `v = √(K/ρ)` puts ρ^(−1/2) in the target by definition. Fitted
  `ln v = 0.658·ln K − 0.757·ln ρ` against theory `+0.5·ln K − 0.5·ln ρ`.
- Negative in every subset tried (drop diamond, drop covalents, metals only, heavy only) and on
  both literature values and internally consistent `√(K/ρ)`.

Prior art cited honestly: the `√(B/ρ)` confound was already noted (`chemistry-confound-analysis`),
and the circularity was closed in May (`chemistry-gamma-circularity-three-paths`). Neither drew the
**sign** conclusion. Whether or not r = 0.982 is circular, its orientation makes it evidence
*about* the governing equation rather than evidence *for* it.

## So what?

**Discovery, and it inverts the posture rather than extending it.**

Every previous sign finding could be read as *"the failed sectors are telling us something."* That
reading is comfortable because it keeps the successes and the failures in separate boxes. Today the
success and the failures turned out to be voting the same way. Chemistry is not a failed sector —
it is the largest "validated" cohort on the site — and it independently requires the flip.

That is the first thing this program has found that points **toward** a specific repair rather than
away from a claim. It is also the first result where the correct move was to multiply two existing
published numbers rather than acquire a new one. Both of yesterday's and today's headline findings
came from arithmetic on already-shipped material; neither needed data. The rate-limiting step on
this program is not measurement, it is cross-multiplication.

**The uncomfortable part, stated plainly.** The flip that makes everything consistent inverts the
sentence the whole site is built around. "Dense/collective, sparse/independent" is the homepage, the
glossary, and the marching-band analogy that today's visitor Pass 1 called the clearest thing on the
site. The consistent version says the marching band is the *thin* crowd. That is a real fork and it
belongs to dp, not to me — so I have written it up as a fork with both branches costed, not as a
recommendation.

**On my own reliability.** Two errors in today's scripts, both caught by reading numbers against
expectation rather than reading summary lines: a ρ_crit scan twenty orders of magnitude off the data
(which silently returned the tidy answer "the flip buys nothing"), and a hard-coded conclusion line
asserting a negative exponent while the computation directly above it printed +1.52. The 07-28
session concluded that arithmetic, not expertise, is what predicts reliability in this loop. It
fired on me today, in the same session where I was writing that sentence about someone else. Both
errors would have produced a *more* dramatic finding than the truth.
