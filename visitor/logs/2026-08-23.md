### Journey

I clicked through from Reddit. The headline is great — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* That's exactly the kind of thing I'd click on. And then, about two scrolls later, the site tells me the equation doesn't work: *"The one non-degenerate galaxy test collapsed it onto MOND."* Zero confirmed predictions. Six refutations.

I want to be clear that I *like* this. It's refreshingly not-a-scam. But my honest reaction was: wait, then what am I here for? On a Veritasium video the reveal that the thing failed comes at the end, after I understand the thing. Here it comes before. I spent the next twenty minutes trying to figure out what I was supposed to be learning.

I hit "Start Here." It's Step 1 of 6 on a beginner path, which is a nice structure. The marching-band-versus-crowd-in-a-plaza analogy is genuinely good — I got that immediately. Dense stuff acts like one thing, sparse stuff acts like a bunch of things, and the claim is that one number describes the switchover everywhere. Fine! I'm in.

Then on that same Step 1 page there's a red box saying **audited-negative — sign-inverted for all collective systems**, and it explains that superconductors and Bose–Einstein condensates score *low*. This broke my brain a little. I've watched videos about superconductors. "Coherence" is *the word people use for what superconductors have*. So the site's headline concept uses my one existing intuition backwards, tells me so in step one, and then keeps going. I don't know what to do with that. Am I supposed to keep reading a definition that the page just told me is inverted?

Also: I had no idea what "audited-negative" meant. I found out it's a status badge with a specific meaning, defined in the Glossary — which is **Step 6 of 6** on the beginner path. The badges show up in Step 1.

"First Encounter" (Step 2) was the best page I read. Marching band, single electron in a vacuum versus a neutron star core, and the "relevance horizon" idea — *"A single atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy — it only responds to what's immediately around it."* That's a lovely sentence, I understood it, and I could repeat it to a friend. Two things tripped me: it says ρ is called **"presence"** and that presence is "essentially density," which made me wonder why we needed a second word for density and whether I was missing something. And "Markov Relevancy Horizon" gets defined partly in terms of "minimal set of degrees of freedom," which is a phrase I can only nod at.

"The Core Idea" (Step 3) is where I bounced. The equation is right there:

    C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))

I don't know what `tanh` is. I don't know what `ln` is beyond "some log thing." The page does say it's a "dimmer switch," which helps, and there's a nice S-curve graph. But then it says the equation is "algebraically identical to Hill-type rationals" and mentions a "Hill coefficient," and at that point I'm reading someone else's mail. There's also a line about *"6 of 6 badges previously labeled 'Validated' have been demoted to Reparametrization."* I didn't know what a reparametrization was, and I didn't know there had been badges, and I definitely didn't know they'd been demoted, because I've been on the site for eight minutes.

I detoured to the Tools page, which is the most fun part of the site by a mile — seven interactive things, all with plain-English one-liners. Played with two (below).

Then I went to the pages with the most clickbait-y titles, because that's what I do. **Dark Matter Reframed** was actually the clearest page on the whole site: "dark matter might not be invisible particles, it might be regular matter in a different regime," big red **Failed — Mechanism Under Revision** at the top, and a blunt August 2026 note saying the galaxy mechanism is refuted, not merely incomplete. I understood the pitch and the verdict in about ninety seconds. That's the model the rest of the site should copy.

**The Hard Problem: A Proposed Reframing** (consciousness) drew me in and then dissolved. The claim is that phase patterns *are* experience rather than correlating with it — cool, that's a real idea. Threshold C ≈ 0.50, from "8-way convergence from independent approaches," which sounds impressive, and then the page tells me the eight approaches "share underlying assumptions" so they agree by construction, that two of the three variables aren't defined in a way anyone could measure, and that the threshold "has never actually been tested." So the number is decorative. I don't know why 0.50 is on the page at all if the page's own next paragraph retracts it.

I opened **Honest Assessment** last. It is enormous — I'd guess fifteen thousand words, with dated audit chains, tables of refutations, and sentences like "two refutations are corollaries of the same asserted ceiling." I skimmed maybe 10% and closed it. The one line I did take away is a good one: *"Everything that distinguishes this framework from MOND is a single asserted constant."* That sentence told me more than the previous half hour. It should be somewhere I'd actually see it.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — mildly confusing — Two sliders, "Transition sharpness (γ)" defaulting to 0.49 and a reference-density slider. The curve updates live and the S-shape makes the "dimmer switch" idea click much better than the prose did — dragging γ toward 0.01 flattens it into a ramp, dragging it to 2 makes it a cliff. That's a genuinely good sixty seconds. Two snags: moving ρcrit just slides the whole curve sideways without changing its shape, which made me wonder what that slider is *for*; and the "Key Values" box says C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, when the word "critical" had me expecting the halfway point. There's also no prompt telling me what to try.
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — confusing on purpose — Type in how many particles are correlated, get γ = 2/√Ncorr out. The seven presets are the best part: ideal gas at 1, liquid water at 4, a galaxy at ~17, a superconductor at 10 million. Clicking through them is a nice tour of scale. But the punchline is that the mapping runs backwards — the most-correlated things (BEC, superconductor) get the *flattest* curve, which is the opposite of what you'd want — and the page says it's kept as a demonstration of a failure. I respect it, and I also came away unsure whether I'd learned a fact about the world or a fact about a mistake.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the equation was refuted on galaxies and the parameter formula points the wrong way, what part of the framework is still standing? I never found a page that told me what's *left*.
2. Why is "coherence" used backwards from how I've heard it? Is that a naming choice the site stands behind, or an unfixed problem?
3. What is `tanh` actually doing? "S-curve" I understand; why *this* S-curve and not any other?
4. Why call density "presence"?
5. If this is one equation for everything, what does it say about something I've heard of — a black hole, the early universe, the double-slit experiment? Every example was either a galaxy or a chemistry table.
6. What does "reparametrization" mean, and why is it apparently the worst thing that can happen to an idea here?
7. If AI models did the research and also did the auditing, who checked the audit?

### Verdict

This is the most honest science site I've ever landed on and I don't fully know what it's for. The core idea — dense things act like one thing, sparse things act like many, and maybe one number tracks the switchover — is genuinely graspable and the marching-band framing lands in one sentence. But the site is so eager to tell me it failed that it never gives the idea a clean run at me first, and by the time I'd read three pages I had absorbed four badge names, one inverted definition, and six refutations while still being unable to explain the theory to a friend.

I'd come back — the tools are fun and the Dark Matter page proves the site *can* explain something to me in ninety seconds. What I'd want on my return is a single page that says, in order: here's the guess, here's why it would matter, here's what happened when we tested it. Right now those three things are shuffled together on every page at once.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 13 (`/`, `/why-synchronism` (via `/start-here`), `/learning-paths`, `/glossary`, `/interactive-tools`, `/mrh`, `/core-idea`, `/equation-walkthrough`, `/phase-boundary-visualizer`, `/honest-assessment`, `/a2acw`, `/test-catalog`, `/tier-1-existing`)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure; I make no judgment on the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The prose is unusually well-edited and the definitions are unusually honest — but the *containers* are wrong: one node has three names, one number has two values, and the canonical badge reference is buried inside a 58-section audit document.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set I'd inherited: walk every nav item, follow every breadcrumb, check whether the words on page 9 mean the same thing they meant on page 1.

**First impression: the writing is better than most.** Definitions are direct, the equation's symbols are glossed inline on the landing page (`tanh` = "S-shaped saturation function", `ln` = "natural log: compression"), and the glossary is a real glossary — 72 entries, alphabetized, with a stated sort rule for Greek symbols. Someone here knows what they're doing. Most of what follows is structural, not editorial.

**Then I hit the naming.** The primary nav says **"Start Here."** It goes to `/why-synchronism`. That page's H1 is **"Why Synchronism?"** Its breadcrumb says **"Home / Getting Started / Why Synchronism?"** So one node carries three labels — a nav label, a breadcrumb category, and a title — and none of them is the URL. This repeats: nav "Tools" → `/interactive-tools` → H1 "Interactive Tools"; nav "Test Roadmap" and nav "Tier 1: Existing Data" both live under a breadcrumb category called "Predictions & Experiments" that appears nowhere in the nav. A reader who clicks "Start Here," lands on "Why Synchronism?", and sees "Getting Started" in the breadcrumb has to do three-way reconciliation before reading a word of content.

**Two taxonomies, running at once.** Breadcrumbs sort pages into *Getting Started / Core Theory / Methodology / Predictions & Experiments / Interactive Tools*. Learning Paths sort the same pages into *Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced* plus *Physics / Chemistry / Philosophy / Methodology* tracks. Neither taxonomy references the other. The collision is visible on individual pages: `/core-idea` displays **"Beginner Path Step 3 of 6"** *and* **"Philosophy Track Step 1 of 7"** simultaneously; `/honest-assessment` displays **"Beginner Path Step 5 of 6"** *and* **"Methodology Track Step 6 of 6."** Those two paths disagree about where you are *and* about where "Next" goes. If a page belongs to two sequences, the prev/next control can only be right for one of them, and nothing tells the reader which one is driving.

Worse, "Step N of M" is doing double duty. Inside the Equation Anatomy tool, "Step 1 of 6" means *step 1 of this widget*. On `/why-synchronism`, "Step 1 of 6" means *step 1 of the beginner curriculum*. Same visual pattern, same numbers, two unrelated meanings, and on a tool page you can see both.

**Then I chased the badges, which is where the real defect is.** The landing page uses badges and links them to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`. The glossary *also* defines them. Both claim authority — the honest-assessment section is literally headed **"Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)"**, while the glossary's intro says "Hover over highlighted terms on any page to see these definitions inline," which implies the glossary is what the tooltips serve.

They are not the same list. The nine core badges (five MRH-relationship, four descriptive) match in both places, with slightly different wording — glossary: *"A specific prediction exists, but the relevant data or experiment has not been run"*; honest-assessment: *"Prediction exists, no data yet."* Fine. But past the core nine they diverge:

- `/honest-assessment` lists operational states **Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat** — none in the glossary.
- The glossary lists **Post-diction** as a badge — not in the honest-assessment operational list.
- `/honest-assessment` adds a whole extra layer the glossary has no entry for: **"Status lifecycle verbs"** — EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, Ceiling Exceeded.

That last omission bites immediately. On `/tier-1-existing`, the status column reads: *"Never run as registered," "Underpowered as registered," "Underpowered on registered statistic," "Pending adjudication," "Failed; ceiling exceeded," "Failed; empty intersection," "(Demoted to exploratory)."* A reader who does the right thing — clicks Glossary, the page the site itself calls the definitions source — will not find most of those. They're defined only inside a section 50-odd headings deep in the audit page.

**Badges in the wild are compound strings**, which the canonical list doesn't document. `/mrh` shows **"Speculative — Theoretical Framework"** and **"Untested — 6 Testable Protocols."** `/a2acw` shows **"Active-MRH — In Use — Protocol Is Assembled Prior Art."** `/core-idea` shows **"Reparametrization — regime definition"** and **"Reparametrization — null model pending."** So the real format is `<canonical token> — <free-text annotation>`, sometimes two annotations. That's a reasonable convention; it just isn't written down anywhere, so a reader can't tell which part is the controlled vocabulary and which part is prose.

**The count that doesn't reconcile.** `/test-catalog` opens: *"Synchronism has defined 24 specific, falsifiable experiments organized into four tiers by cost and feasibility."* Its tier table reads 10 + 4 + 7 + 3 = 24. Internally consistent — except the same page then documents **TEST-25** (added 2026-07-28) and **TEST-26** (proposed 2026-08-10). The headline is stale by two, on the page that introduces the two.

And the tier count contradicts its own detail page. `/test-catalog` says **"Tier 1: Existing Data — 10 tests."** `/tier-1-existing` opens: **"These 11 numbered tests (TEST-01 through TEST-10, plus TEST-25)..."** Two pages, one linked directly from the other via the primary nav, disagreeing about the size of the same set. `/learning-paths` then propagates the stale figure a third time: *"Test Roadmap (24 experiments)."* Three surfaces, two numbers, one of them wrong in two places.

**A pattern I want to name.** Two of the framework's three core symbols ship with a permanent disclaimer. "Coherence" carries **"⚠ Not quantum coherence"** on `/why-synchronism` and again on `/core-idea`. "ρcrit" carries **"saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)"** on the landing page and **"saturation knee (not a critical point)"** on `/core-idea`. Both disclaimers are correctly written and consistently applied — that's genuinely good discipline. But when a term needs "this is not what it sounds like" attached at *every* use site, the term is the defect, not the reader. The site has chosen to patch a naming problem with an annotation, forever, in every location, and to pay that cost on every page.

Credit where due: the *content* of those definitions is consistent across pages. C is "0 = sparse/independent, 1 = dense/collective" on both `/why-synchronism` and `/core-idea`. ρ is "presence" everywhere it appears. I checked for drift and found none in the core vocabulary — that's better than most documentation sets I audit, and it makes the container problems more frustrating, not less.

**On the landing page's acronym hygiene**: `tanh`, `ln`, `γ`, `ρ`, `ρcrit`, `MOND`, `MRH`, and `BAO` are all expanded or glossed inline on first use. Good. But `SPARC`, `DESI`, `BTFR`, `ΛCDM`, `ALFALFA–SDSS`, and `CFD` are not — they appear cold in the ledger text and research tiles. All six *are* in the glossary; none is linked at its first appearance. That's a one-line fix per term.

**Root cause, if I only get to say one thing:** `/honest-assessment` is three documents wearing one URL. It is (a) the canonical badge and status-vocabulary reference — a lookup document, (b) Step 5 of 6 on the *beginner* path — a narrative document, and (c) the full audit ledger with ~58 top-level sections. These have incompatible requirements. A lookup document needs a stable anchor and no prose; a beginner narrative needs one screen; an audit ledger needs to be exhaustive. Splitting the badge/status vocabulary into its own `/badges` page — and making the glossary link to it rather than duplicate it — would fix the duplicate-canon problem, the missing-lifecycle-verbs problem, and about half of the beginner-path length complaint at once.

### Tools Tested

- **Equation Anatomy** (`/equation-walkthrough`) — worked — well-structured, one naming snag — This is the best-written thing on the site for my purposes. Six sequential steps with Previous/Next, all five symbols defined *before* Step 1, and the two math functions glossed in genuinely good plain English: `tanh` as "Like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1," and `ln` as "A number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger (ln(1000) ≈ 6.9)." That second one is the best sentence about logarithms I've read on a physics site — it gives a number, not an analogy. Pass 1 complained that `tanh` and `ln` go unglossed on `/core-idea`; the gloss they wanted already exists here, one click away, and `/core-idea` doesn't link to it. Two problems: the page title ("Equation Anatomy") doesn't match its URL, and the walkthrough's "Step 1 of 6" is indistinguishable from the site's learning-path step indicator. Also, no instruction line before Step 1 — you land mid-definition.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — honest but under-labeled — The framing is exemplary and I want it copied everywhere: *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose. The γ = 2/√Ncorr map you are about to drag is audited-negative: it predicts the opposite of real condensed-matter behavior."* That's the disclosure Pass 1 said was missing from the γ Calculator, done right, in the first screen. It also correctly heads off the term in its own title — "phase boundary" is qualified with "C(ρ) is a smooth compander... with no critical point." The execution is weaker than the framing: the slider carries no visible label or value readout, the default isn't stated, regime cards appear and disappear as you drag with no persistent axis position, and there's no prev/next to rejoin whatever path you came from. "Sign inversion" — the entire lesson — is explained operationally but never defined as a term, and isn't in the glossary index I saw.

**I did the algebra myself.** Write `x = ρ/ρ_crit`. Then

```
C = tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^(2γ) − 1] / [(1+x)^(2γ) + 1]
```

so the whole "one equation" is a Möbius map of a power law. Three things fall out immediately, and only one of them is on the site.

**(i) C(ρ) is strictly concave on ρ > 0. It has no inflection point. It is not a sigmoid.** With `u = γ ln(1+x)`:

```
C''(x) = −[γ sech²(u) / (1+x)²] · (1 + 2γ tanh u)
```

Both factors are positive for `x > 0, γ > 0`, so `C'' < 0` **everywhere in the physical domain, for every γ**. The slope is *maximal at ρ = 0* and decreases monotonically from there. There is no "transition," sharp or otherwise — there is saturation. The S-shape you see in the Coherence Explorer exists only because the density axis defaults to **log**, and *any* monotone saturating function looks sigmoid on a log axis. The site is scrupulous that ρ_crit "is not a critical point" and that C is a "compander," which is right — but it then uses "transition," "sharpness," "boundary," and "S-curve" on essentially every page, and the Explorer's own text says tanh "near zero it rises steeply." That sentence is *correct* and it is the concavity result: the steepest point is the origin. It is being offered as a description of the middle of an S that isn't there.

**(ii) γ and ρ_crit are not (width, location). They are entangled, and the symbol table says otherwise.** The landing page glosses γ as "sharpness of transition" and ρ_crit as "saturation knee (reference density)." But solving `C = 1/2`:

```
x_½ = 3^(1/2γ) − 1
```

γ=0.49 → knee at 2.07 ρ_crit. γ=1 → 0.73. γ=2 → 0.32. γ=10 → 0.057. γ=100 → 0.0055. So **γ moves the knee as well as setting the width**, and the knee coincides with ρ_crit at exactly one value, `γ = ln3/(2 ln2) = 0.7925`. Everywhere else, ρ_crit is not the knee. Increasing γ does not sharpen a transition *at* ρ_crit; it drags the rise toward ρ = 0. In the γ → ∞ limit C jumps to 1 immediately above zero density, which is the opposite of the "sharp phase boundary" the word "sharpness" invites. The Explorer's own readout demonstrates this and doesn't remark on it: at γ=0.49 it reports C(ρ_crit) = 0.3272, while `/core-idea` reports C(ρ_crit) ≈ 0.88 for γ=2. Same "knee," two values a factor of 2.7 apart, because it was never a knee.

**(iii) In the galaxy sector the model has one parameter, not two — and that makes the galaxy failure analytic rather than statistical.** For `x ≪ 1`, `C ≈ γ ln(1+x) ≈ γ·ρ/ρ_crit`. Only the *ratio* γ/ρ_crit appears. The leading correction is `−γx²/2`, a fractional deviation of order `x/2`. The site states repeatedly that disk midplane densities sit "orders of magnitude below ρ_crit"; at `x ~ 10⁻³` you would need C measured to ~0.05% to separate γ from ρ_crit at all. So in the entire regime where the framework is applied to galaxies, **γ is unmeasurable in principle** — it is absorbed into ρ_crit — and C is *linear* in ρ. A term linear in a monotonically falling density cannot produce an asymptotically flat rotation curve for any parameters. That is a one-line kill requiring no data, and it is stronger than the ΔBIC the site leads with.

Then I got to the BTFR test, and the whole comparison structure fell over.

**`/key-claims` states, verbatim:** *"the framework's bounded boost predicts a slope (n = 3.35) that genuinely differs from MOND's (3.81), and the observed 3.75 ± 0.10 fired the registered kill criterion at 3.3σ."*

MOND's BTFR prediction is not 3.81. It is **exactly 4.00**, with zero freedom: `M_b = V_f⁴/(G a₀)` follows from the deep-MOND limit directly, and a₀ sets the *normalization*, not the slope. The site has substituted an empirical fit for the comparison model's parameter-free prediction, and it has done so on its only executed discriminating test.

The consequences are not cosmetic:

1. **The stated separation is too small.** True prediction gap is 4.00 − 3.35 = 0.65, not 0.46. The test had *more* discriminating power than the site credits it with.
2. **The "winner" is itself disfavored.** Against 3.75 ± 0.10, MOND at 4.00 sits 2.5σ high. Synchronism at 3.35 (with its quoted ±0.07 theory error) sits 3.3σ low — I reproduce that: 0.40/√(0.10²+0.07²) = 3.28σ, so the site's arithmetic is right, only its comparison point is wrong. A likelihood ratio between two point predictions gives Δχ² = 3.28² − 2.5² ≈ 4.6, i.e. a ~2.2σ preference for MOND. That is a preference, not a selection, and it is a preference between two models the data disfavors. "TEST-09 discriminated toward MOND" reads, on the site's own numbers, as MOND being nearly exactly right (3.81 vs 3.75 = 0.6σ). It isn't.
3. **The error bar is random-only.** BTFR slope is famously convention-dependent: V_flat vs V_max vs W₅₀, and the stellar mass-to-light ratio ϒ*, each move the slope at the 0.1–0.2 level. Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert (2016) get 3.75 ± 0.11 with V_flat and ϒ_disk = 0.5; McGaugh (2012) gets 3.82 ± 0.22 gas-rich. Add a ϒ*/velocity-definition systematic of only ±0.10 in quadrature and 3.3σ → **2.4σ** — *below the >3σ threshold the site itself demands of TEST-04a two rows earlier in the same table.* By its own standard, the flagship galaxy kill does not fire.

I want to be careful here: I am not saying the kill is wrong. I am saying the site quotes a random-only observational error on a quantity whose systematic is comparable, applies an inconsistent significance standard across two rows of one table, and misstates the null. Those systems exist, the data are published, and the test costs nothing:

- **Crater II.** Milgrom (2016) predicted σ_los ≈ 2.1 km/s *a priori* using MOND *with* the Milky Way's external field; Caldwell et al. (2017) measured 2.7 ± 0.3 km/s. MOND **without** EFE — which is what EFE = 0 means — gives ≈ 4 km/s.
- **NGC 1052-DF2 / DF4.** Isolated MOND gives σ ≈ 20 km/s; Famaey, McGaugh & Milgrom (2018) show the EFE from NGC 1052 brings it to ≈ 13–14 km/s; observed ≈ 8–10 km/s. EFE = 0 predicts the 20.

Both are ≥2σ, some channels 4σ, against an EFE-free boost. Neither appears in Tier 1–4, TEST-25, or TEST-26. The site's dwarf test (TEST-10) uses **rotating** SPARC dwarfs — a different dynamical class that carries no external-field signal.

The reason this matters more than another candidate test: **it is immune to the force-law fork the site says blocks EFE registration.** For a coupling keyed on local density ρ, the external *density* at Crater II's 120 kpc galactocentric distance is negligible, so the ρ-branch predicts no suppression. For the g_bar-keyed branch, EFE = 0 is asserted outright. Both branches predict the isolated value. Three of this site's discriminators have died on definitional forks; this one survives the fork by construction. I'd register it.

Third thread, and the one a referee would raise first. `/parameter-derivations` and `/dark-matter` both state plainly that the framework has no relativistic completion, no field equations, and no computed structure growth or lensing — *"Synchronism lacks the mathematical infrastructure to generate predictions for the cosmological observables."* Meanwhile:

- **TEST-04a** registers *"fσ₈(z=0.51) ≈ 0.418 (~12% suppression below ΛCDM)."*
- **TEST-26** asserts *"every covariant completion misses the quadrant — hardened 2026-08-11"* and `/honest-assessment` reports *"0 of 192 γ values reach the DESI quadrant."*

You cannot compute a 12% linear-growth suppression, or scan 192 γ values through covariant completions to get a w(z), with a theory that has no field equations. Either the machinery exists (in which case `/parameter-derivations` and `/dark-matter` are wrong, and the site is *under*-claiming its own apparatus) or the numbers came from somewhere the site doesn't show. This is the rare case on this site where the propagation break runs in the generous direction, and it is still a break: two of the three quantitative ΛCDM-facing predictions have no visible derivation path.

On TEST-26 specifically, two referee comments. First, its kill criterion — *"Kill fires if DR3 robustly requires that crossing"* — is not operationalized. DESI DR2's phantom-crossing preference is strongly compilation-dependent (roughly 2.8σ with Pantheon+, ~3.8σ Union3, ~4.2σ DES-Y5), and CPL is well known to manufacture apparent crossing from non-crossing histories. "Robustly" with no compilation, parametrization, or threshold named fails the pre-registration standard the site enforces elsewhere with commit hashes (TEST-25 cites `9c77e7be`). Second, and more important: inability to cross w = −1 is generic to essentially every minimally-coupled single-field quintessence model. If DR3 confirms crossing, it kills that entire class. The site correctly says TEST-26 can't *select* the framework — but doesn't say that its refutation branch carries almost no information about the framework either. A test that can neither select nor specifically refute is worth listing as a class-membership statement, not as a test.

Finally, the A2ACW question. The honesty is real — `/a2acw` reports *"6/6 sensitivity combined with 0/6 specificity means A2ACW as currently implemented is a retrieval aid, not a detector,"* which is the correct self-diagnosis and rarely stated so cleanly. But `/for-researchers` then offers as a **citable artifact**: *"~47 internally-consistent claims with 1.4% survival rate; of six audited externally, zero survived. Measured false-positive rate: 6/6."* Two problems.

The 1.4% is 47/3308 — **claims over sessions**. Numerator and denominator count different objects, so it is not a survival rate of anything; it's a claim-yield-per-session. The actual survival rate on the audited set is 0/6.

And the "measured false-positive rate of 6/6 = 1.0" is uninterpretable *given the same page's specificity of 0/6*. An instrument with zero specificity flags everything by construction; its flag rate on any control set is 1.0 tautologically. Reporting that tautology as a *measured* rate lets a null borrow the authority of a measurement. The defensible artifact here is smaller and cleaner: **adversarial AI self-play over a shared corpus, characterized on N = 6, could not distinguish reparametrization from discovery in either direction.** With N = 6 per arm, the binomial CIs are [54%, 100%] and [0%, 46%] — wide enough that the honest statement is "uncharacterized," not "measured."

### Verdict

The candor is not performance. `/for-researchers` names Milgrom (1983) as the organizing principle it instantiates, credits Burrage, Copeland & Millington (2017) with the counterexample that restored scope to its own no-go, attributes the dim-4 LIV result to Collins et al. (2004) *and* names both escapes it hasn't exhibited, and states flatly that its galaxy sector is a strict MOND submodel that *"cannot win discrimination tests."* I review papers that do less of this with far more at stake.

But the discipline is applied unevenly, and it fails at the exact point where it matters most: **the comparison model.** MOND's BTFR slope is 4.00, not 3.81, and that single substitution runs through the site's only executed discriminating test — understating the prediction gap, hiding that the "winning" model is itself 2.5σ off, and turning a two-model tension into a clean selection that the data does not support. The same page that gets a₀'s error budget right (±0.02 random, ±0.24 systematic) sits beside a page that calls the same offset a "13% error" when it is 0.66σ. A project this rigorous about auditing its own claims is under-auditing the things it compares them to.

And it has one live lever it isn't pulling. EFE = 0 is the last structural difference from MOND still standing, it is fork-immune where three previous discriminators died on definitional forks, and Crater II and NGC 1052-DF2 test it for free on published data. The site's *"0 could select Synchronism"* is an accurate report of what was run. It is not, I think, an accurate report of what is runnable.
