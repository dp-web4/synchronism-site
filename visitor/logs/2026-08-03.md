## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /start-here, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /the-core-idea, /key-claims, /honest-assessment, /glossary, /tools, and 3 tool pages)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The big idea grabbed me in ten seconds and the honesty impressed me, but by page three I was reading a list of things that didn't work in a language I don't speak.

### Journey

I clicked from Reddit onto the landing page and the headline did its job: *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* That's a Veritasium thumbnail in one sentence. I like that it's a question. Under it, the equation itself:

**C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**

My honest reaction: I bounced off that instantly. Not because I hate math — because there are four symbols in it and I recognize exactly one of them (ρ, which I know is "density" from some documentary). "tanh" and "ln" are just letters to me. The page does explain each one right below, and "S-shaped curve" is a thing I can picture. But putting the raw equation *above* the plain-English version means the first thing I see is the thing I can't read. I'd have swapped them.

What kept me on the page was the Scientific Outcomes box:

- Prospective predictions confirmed: **0**
- Refutations executed on external data: **6**
- Withdrawn: **1**
- "Validated" claims surviving audit: **0 of 6**

That is *not* what I expected from a "one equation explains everything" site, and it's the single most interesting thing here. Every crackpot-adjacent thing I've ever been linked to on Reddit is 100% wins. This one leads with a scoreboard of zeros. I genuinely didn't know what to do with that emotionally — am I reading about a discovery or a failure? — but I trusted the site more, not less. "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" as a section heading is a good move.

I took the beginner path. **Why Synchronism?** gave me the analogy that made the whole thing click: *"A crowd milling around a plaza behaves like independent individuals; a marching band behaves like one organism."* That's it. That's the idea. Crowd = 0, marching band = 1, and the equation is the dial between them. If the landing page led with the marching band instead of the equation I'd have understood the framework thirty seconds sooner.

**First Encounter** was where I started to slip. It introduced "presence" (ρ) as the input, which is basically density but only counted inside a bubble — the **Markov Relevancy Horizon**. The definition it gives is actually great: *"an 'event horizon for influence' — the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter."* Andromeda doesn't matter to an atom in my hand. Fine, I got that. But then "MRH" starts appearing as three letters everywhere else on the site and I had to keep re-deriving what it meant. Also "degrees of freedom" showed up with no explanation and I nodded along without knowing what it meant.

**The Core Idea** told me γ = 2/√N_corr and called it "a Hill coefficient, not coupling strength." Both halves of that sentence are noise to me. I skimmed hard here. It also told me *"6 of 6 badges previously labeled 'Validated' on this site have been demoted to Reparametrization on closer review."* I don't know what "reparametrization" means, but from context I gathered it means "old physics wearing a new hat," which is a devastating thing to say about your own work and I respect that it's on the page.

Then I clicked **Honest Assessment**, expecting the usual mild hedging. It's not mild. It's a list of six things that got tested and lost. And the top of the page had one paragraph that was the best writing on the whole site — a plain-English "when tested against real telescope data, it failed every major test," including that where it *does* work on galaxies, it just reproduces MOND, a 40-year-old theory, without improving on it. I understood that paragraph completely.

Below it, I understood almost nothing. "ΔBIC=+184." "3.3 standard deviations." "RAR Transition Shape." "r²=0.0001." "+17.95 sigma." I know sigma is a bigness-of-surprise number from particle physics videos, and I know bigger is more surprising, so +17.95 sounds catastrophic — but I can't tell whether +17.95 sigma against the theory is 5× worse than 3.3 sigma or 500× worse. Nobody tells me. The one I *did* fully get was #6: the quantum test scores 1.85–2.00, real quantum mechanics scores 2.83, "it fails like classical physics, not like nature." That sentence is perfect. More of those, please.

**Key Claims** was the page I most wanted to like. Claim 2 is literally "Could Consciousness Have an Equation?" — that's the reason a person like me clicks a physics link at all. And the site immediately tells me the answer is basically no: consciousness supposedly starts at C ≥ 0.50, but 0.50 is *"the arithmetic midpoint of tanh's output range,"* so *"convergence on 0.50 is expected, not discovered."* I had to read that three times, but I think I got it: they found a magic number, then realized the magic number was baked into the math they chose. That's an honest and kind of fascinating admission. But the payoff for me as a reader is "the coolest claim on the site is admittedly circular," and there's nothing offered in its place.

The **Glossary** has 72 terms and does define RAR, BTFR, BIC, sigma, MOND, MRH, all of it. Good. But there's no search box, so I was scrolling an alphabetical list hunting for "BIC" while trying to remember which page I'd left off on. I gave up after two lookups and just kept reading past things I didn't understand.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** — worked — mostly intuitive — This was the best thing on the site for me. Two sliders (γ, ρcrit), a curve that responds, and an actual instruction: *"Move γ from 2.0 downward and watch the curve flatten."* Telling me what to *do* with a slider is worth more than three paragraphs of text. Two problems, though. First, the x-axis is labeled "log₁₀(ρ)" — I don't know what that means and it's the axis I'm supposed to be reading. Second, the "Key Values" readout gives me C(ρcrit) = 0.3272, C(10ρcrit) = 0.8259, C(100ρcrit) = 0.9785 to four decimal places, and I have zero idea whether 0.3272 is a good number, a bad number, or a number that means anything. Four decimals on a number I can't interpret just feels like being shown a receipt in a foreign currency.

- **Equation Anatomy** — worked — intuitive — Genuinely the tool that should be linked from the landing page. Six steps, Previous/Next arrows, one piece of the equation at a time. Its explanation of tanh — *"Like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1"* — did in one sentence what the rest of the site kept assuming I already knew. Same for ln: *"A number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger."* I finally understood why there's a logarithm in there at all. This tool is buried under /tools where I only found it because I went looking.

- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** — partly broken as advertised — confusing — I went to /tools/consciousness-threshold expecting the interactive demo the tools index promised ("Watch 8 'independent' approaches converge on C ≈ 0.50"). What I got was a text page with **no controls at all**, which then links to a *different* "Consciousness Threshold Demo" at the bottom. So the tool listed on the tools page isn't the tool at that address. It also mentions "Intent fluid" dynamics, which appears nowhere else I read and which I could not begin to picture.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If the equation just reproduces MOND for galaxies and MOND is 40 years old, what is Synchronism actually *for*? The site says this plainly but never tells me what's left.
2. Is "0 confirmed predictions after 6 refutations" a normal place for a young idea to be, or is that already the obituary? I can't calibrate.
3. Is +17.95 sigma worse than 3.3 sigma by a lot or a little?
4. What's a "reparametrization," in a sentence, without using the word "parameter"?
5. The chemistry section claims correlations of 0.98+ across 1,703 phenomena — that sounds like a *huge* win. Why isn't that on the landing page next to all the zeros? Is it not as good as it sounds?
6. What is "Intent fluid"?

### Verdict

The honesty is the product here, and it's remarkable — I have never been linked to a fringe-physics site that opens by telling me its own score is zero. But the site is written by someone who already understands it, for someone who already understands it, and then apologizes to me in the footnotes. The two things that actually taught me anything — the marching-band analogy and the Equation Anatomy walkthrough — are both buried below the equation that scares me off. I'd come back if someone told me the story had a next chapter; right now it reads like an extremely well-kept lab notebook for an experiment that already ended.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 distinct pages across 20 fetches (landing, /start-here, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /the-core-idea, /learning-paths, /key-claims, /honest-assessment, /tier-1-existing, /glossary, /interactive-tools, /tools, /galaxy-plotter, /tools/galaxy-plotter, /gamma-calculator, /consciousness-demo, /consciousness-threshold, /tools/consciousness-threshold, plus one deliberate 404 probe)

- **Would return?**: yes — the writing quality is high; the information architecture is what needs work
- **Overall impression**: The prose is unusually disciplined for a research site, but the URL space has an undocumented alias tree that silently serves the wrong page, and the status-label vocabulary has outgrown its own taxonomy — 27+ defined labels, four families, and the family the site calls preferred appears on zero content pages I visited.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set before a rewrite: walk every nav link, check breadcrumbs, check whether terms are defined before use, check whether the promised structure is actually navigable, and probe the URL space for aliases and dead ends.

**The URL space has an alias tree, and it collides.** This is the finding I'd fix first, because it's the root cause of a complaint Pass 1 filed and misdiagnosed.

Verified by fetch:
- `/interactive-tools` and `/tools` both serve the tools index.
- `/why-synchronism` and `/start-here` both serve the same page (H1 "Start Here: Why Synchronism?").
- `/core-idea` and `/the-core-idea` both serve "The Core Idea".
- `/galaxy-plotter` and `/tools/galaxy-plotter` both serve the Galaxy Curve Plotter, same breadcrumb.
- A nonsense URL (`/this-page-does-not-exist-qqzz`) correctly returns HTTP 404.

So this isn't a catch-all route swallowing everything — 404s work. There is a real, deliberate-looking `/tools/*` alias tree layered on top of canonical top-level slugs, plus a handful of one-off page aliases. Duplicate content at multiple URLs with no visible canonical is a routine SEO and maintenance problem, but here it does something worse:

**`/tools/consciousness-threshold` resolves to the Philosophy page, not the tool.** The tools index lists "Consciousness Threshold Demo" → `/consciousness-demo`. But `/consciousness-threshold` is a *different, legitimate page* — Philosophy Track step 3, breadcrumb "Home / Consciousness & Philosophy / Consciousness Threshold", H1 "Consciousness Threshold", no controls. Because of the `/tools/*` alias tree, guessing the tool's URL from its displayed title lands you on the philosophy essay under a tools-shaped path. Pass 1 hit exactly this, concluded "the promised tool is not at the promised address," and rated it high. Pass 1's symptom is real; the cause isn't a mislabeled index entry, it's that a wrong URL resolves to a wrong page instead of 404ing. Fixing the index label would not fix this.

Worth noting for the record: `/consciousness-demo` also has no working controls — it renders a static chart with a "Hover over each to see the methodology" instruction and an eight-item list. So the index's promise ("Watch 8 'independent' approaches converge") is thin at the correct URL too. Both complaints are live; they're separate bugs.

**The status-label vocabulary has outgrown its taxonomy.** `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions` is genuinely good documentation — four families, clean definitions:

- MRH-relationship (5): Active-MRH, Parallel-Paths, Sidelined, Superseded, Audited-Negative
- Descriptive (4): Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed
- Deprecated (2): Validated, Strongly Supported
- Operational states, explicitly "not formal badges" (10): Kill Criterion Triggered, MOND-shared [RETIRED], Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Sign Correction, Null-Class, 89% Boundary-Consistent, Template Bias Caveat, Ceiling Exceeded, [Brackets]
- Status lifecycle verbs (6): EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED

That's 27 labels a reader is expected to hold. Two structural problems with it:

*First, the preferred family is unused.* The taxonomy leads with MRH-relationship tags. I found zero instances applied to content on the landing page, /key-claims, /why-synchronism, /tier-1-existing, or the tools index. `/tier-1-existing` defines them in its own legend and then assigns none of them to any of its twelve rows. A vocabulary that's documented but never used is pure reader tax — I learned five terms I never needed.

*Second, the labels in the wild aren't in the legend.* `/tier-1-existing` has 12 tests carrying ~10 distinct status strings. Four of them appear nowhere in the canonical definitions: "Never Run As Registered" (TEST-03), "Underpowered As Registered" (TEST-06), "Demoted to Tier-2" (TEST-07), "Disfavored on σ₈; Underpowered on Registered fσ₈ Statistic" (TEST-04a). And TEST-05/09/10 say "Kill Criterion **Fired**" where the legend says "Kill Criterion **Triggered**" — same concept, two verbs, on pages that link to each other.

When a status system has nearly as many labels as items, it has stopped being a taxonomy and become prose in a colored box. That last one — TEST-04a's — is a sentence, not a label.

**The Beginner Path is advertised as 6 steps and is navigable for 1.** `/learning-paths` lists it cleanly: Why Synchronism → First Encounter → The Core Idea → What Synchronism Is Not → Honest Assessment → Glossary, "~30 min". Every page carries its step badge correctly ("Beginner Path — Step 2 of 6"). But the forward links break immediately:

- Step 1 `/why-synchronism` → "First Encounter →" ✓
- Step 2 `/first-encounter` → the bottom CTA is *"Prefer analogies to equations? Try Two Reframes (side trip — not part of this sequence) →"*. The only prominent forward link on step 2 leads explicitly off the path. There is no "Next: The Core Idea".
- Step 3 `/core-idea` → bottom is an undifferentiated seven-link list (Why Synchronism · First Encounter · Learning Paths · Key Claims · Honest Assessment · Tier 1 · Test Roadmap). No next, no previous, no path continuation.

The step badges promise a wizard; the footers deliver a link farm. A reader who takes the path at its word gets lost at step 2 — and the one link that *is* highlighted there is labeled "not part of this sequence," which reads as the sequence ending.

**"Not a path step" is factually wrong on the landing page.** The landing page labels three cards: "Deep dive · not a path step: Test Roadmap", "Methodology · not a path step: Research Philosophy", "Shortcut · not a path step: Honest Assessment". But per `/learning-paths`, Honest Assessment is **Beginner Path step 5**, Test Roadmap is **Advanced step 8**, and Research Philosophy is **Methodology track step 1**. All three are path steps. I think the intent was "not one of the three Beginner steps shown above" — but unqualified, the label contradicts the page it links to.

**Naming drift on the entry page.** One page is called four things: nav says "Start Here", H1 says "Start Here: Why Synchronism?", breadcrumb says "Why Synchronism?", `/learning-paths` says "Why Synchronism?", and it answers at two URLs. The page itself contains the line *"This is the page the nav's 'Start Here' points to"* — which is a docs writer's tell. When a page has to explain its own routing to the reader, the routing is the problem.

**Acronyms.** `/first-encounter` handles MRH exactly right: *"Synchronism calls that horizon the MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon)"* — expanded on first use, in a sentence, in context. That's the model. But the landing page — the page a stranger hits first — uses RAR, BTFR, SPARC, ALFALFA, SDSS, CHSH, DESI, and ΛCDM with no expansion. All eight are in the glossary; none are linked to it from the body text.

The glossary is 72 terms with no search or filter box (Pass 1 flagged this; confirmed) and, more importantly for me, it's not *reachable from the jargon*. Terms are defined in one place and used in another with no link between them. And it has gaps against actual body copy: `/core-idea` alone uses "Hill coefficient", "compander", "μ-law encoders", "Naka–Rushton curves", "saturation knee", and "log-log slope" — I found none of those in the glossary. `/galaxy-plotter` uses "V_flat" undefined on-page.

### Tools Tested

- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — **worked** — **structurally confusing, editorially excellent** — Dropdown of five SPARC galaxies, clean axes ("Radius (kpc)", "V (km/s)"), and a caption that actually orients you: *"The dashed line is what rotation curves should look like with only visible matter."* Two documentation problems. (1) The tools index promises *"four curves: Newtonian prediction, observed rotation, Synchronism consistency check, and MOND"* — the legend has **five**, and the fifth is the important one: *"Illustration only — hand-tuned to fit, NOT computed from the theory."* An index entry that undercounts the legend by exactly the curve carrying the disclaimer is the worst possible off-by-one. (2) The legend entries are sentences, not labels: *"Synchronism — REAL C(ρ), γ=2 (max C on this disk: 0.001 — boost never turns on)"*. I respect enormously that the caveat is *in the legend* rather than buried in a footnote — but a legend is a lookup table, and shouting REAL / NOT in caps inside it means the disclaimer has nowhere left to escalate to. Move the caveats to a caption block directly under the chart and let the legend be four or five short names.

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — **worked** — **intuitive** — Best-built tool I touched. Numeric input for N_corr with a linear/log scale toggle, seven preset buttons (Ideal gas, Liquid water, Galaxy — SPARC best fit, Enzyme site, Ferromagnet, BEC, BCS superconductor), live γ readout and live curve. Presets are the right call: they turn an abstract parameter into "here's what water looks like." And it does something I've rarely seen a demo do — it displays the result that *contradicts its own formula*, verbatim: *"γ = 2/√N_corr assigns the sharpest coherence transition to the least-correlated system (ideal gas) and the flattest to the most-correlated (BCS superconductor) — the opposite of real condensed-matter transitions."* A calculator that shows you its own formula is backwards is unusual and good. Two writing snags: the regime readout says *"a label per the inverted formula (Caveat 2), not a physical classification"* — a cross-reference by number, unlinked, forcing me to scroll and count caveats; and BEC/BCS are preset button labels with no expansion anywhere on the page.

- **Cross-cutting**: neither tool page carries a validation badge, but the tools index sorts all seven into badge-styled categories — "Model Explainer", "Reparametrization", "Speculative". So a reader arriving from search at `/gamma-calculator` never learns it's classified Speculative. Also, "Model Explainer" is rendered in badge position but is not in the 27-label taxonomy — it's a category, styled like a status.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is the `/tools/*` alias tree intentional (legacy URLs being preserved) or an accident of the router? The answer changes the fix from "add redirects" to "delete routes".
2. Are the MRH-relationship badges a planned migration that hasn't started, or a deprecated experiment still sitting at the top of the legend? A reader can't tell, and the legend presents them as primary.
3. Is "Withdrawn" a badge or an operational state? The legend lists it under operational states AND lists WITHDRAWN as a lifecycle verb, while the landing page's outcomes box uses "Withdrawn: 1" as a headline count.
4. `/consciousness-demo` promises "Watch 8 approaches converge" but has no controls. Was there an interactive version that regressed, or has the index entry always overpromised?
5. Who is the Beginner Path for? Steps 5 and 6 are Honest Assessment and Glossary — two reference documents. Ending a beginner walkthrough on a 72-term glossary is a strange destination.

### Verdict

The writing on this site is better than most funded documentation I audit — definitions are precise, caveats are load-bearing rather than decorative, and the γ Calculator displaying the result that contradicts its own formula is a genuinely rare piece of intellectual honesty in UI. The problems are structural, not editorial. Three things are broken in ways a reader will hit: an alias tree that serves the wrong page for a plausible URL, a 6-step path that stops signposting after step 1, and a 27-label status vocabulary whose most-documented family is unused while four labels in active use are undocumented. None of these require rethinking the content — they're routing, footers, and a vocabulary sweep. I'd fix the routing first, because it's the only one currently telling readers something false.

**The mathematics of the core equation is correctly characterized, and the site gets the hard part right.** `/core-idea` gives the exact identity

```
tanh(γ·ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^(2γ) − 1] / [(1+x)^(2γ) + 1]
```

which is correct, and which does something important: it kills the "logarithmic compression" narrative outright. The log is undone by the exponent; the function is a Hill/rational saturation with index n = 2γ, full stop. The page says this ("the logarithm is notational, not mechanistic"). Good.

It also correctly disqualifies itself from Landau theory: analytic on the whole domain, no singularity, no correlation length, so critical exponents are *undefined by construction*, not merely unmeasured. That is the right statement and I've seen far better-credentialed papers get it wrong by fitting β and ν to a smooth crossover. The tanh here is not the Ising mean-field tanh — Ising's is a *self-consistency* condition m = tanh(βJzm), where the tanh sits on both sides and the transition emerges from the fixed-point structure. C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(...)) has no feedback, so it's a fitting form that happens to share a function name. The site says exactly this. Credit where due.

**Then `/for-researchers` states the result that should be the headline of the entire site, in one line, buried under a subhead called "Mathematical Identity":**

> At γ=1/2, C(ρ) = x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) identically.

I checked it: at γ = 1/2 the compander identity collapses to [(1+x) − 1]/[(1+x) + 1] = x/(x+2), and since μ_simple(y) = y/(1+y), we have x/(x+2) = (x/2)/(1 + x/2) = μ_simple(x/2). Exact, no approximation.

The Hill coefficient is *by definition* a cooperativity count — it's how many units act together. **1. The only surviving non-MOND claim is EFE = 0, and it is uncatalogued.**

`/mond-unification` states it flatly: because C depends only on local ρ, and a uniform external gravitational field does not change ρ, the framework predicts **EFE = 0 structurally** and satisfies the Strong Equivalence Principle by construction. It then notes this contradicts Chae et al. (2020), ~4σ.

This is the sharpest thing on the site and it is being systematically mis-shelved. Three observations:

*(a) It is a strictly stronger claim than "no environment dependence."* SEP-exactness in the gravitational sector is not just a disagreement with MOND — it puts the framework in conflict with **every nonlinear modified-gravity theory**. In AQUAL/QUMOND the EFE is not a model choice; it falls out of the nonlinearity of the field equation. A theory that boosts gravity but has *no* EFE is asserting that its boost is a pointwise algebraic multiplier — which, as Milgrom noted in 1983 for the algebraic form, generically breaks momentum conservation and Newton's third law unless it descends from an action. I could not find that objection anywhere on the site, and it is arguably more damaging than any of the six listed refutations: it's a consistency problem, not a data problem, and it doesn't wait for a telescope.

*(b) The site has the discriminating number and reports it backwards.* `/tier-1-existing` TEST-05 and `/honest-assessment` both give it: MOND+EFE's external-acceleration lever moves outer g_obs by **~0.09 dex**; C(ρ)'s ambient-density lever by **≤2×10⁻³ dex**. That is a ~45× amplitude gap, and both pages read it as *"our effect is too small to detect, therefore underpowered."* That inference is inverted. The power in that comparison does not live in the framework's lever — it lives in MOND's. MOND predicts a 0.09 dex signal; the framework predicts approximately zero. If the 0.09 dex signal is detected, the framework is refuted, and Chae et al. (2020, 2021) claim exactly that detection at ~4σ. "My predicted effect is unmeasurably small" is not a defense when the competing theory's effect is measurable and has been measured.

*(c) Whichever way the Chae detection settles, the row needs an ID.* If Chae+2020/2021 stand, this is **refutation #7** at ~4σ, and it is the framework's *only* refutation on a claim MOND does not share — i.e. the only one that isn't inherited. If Freundlich+2022 / Paranjape & Sheth+2022 dissolve it (and the site cites both, correctly), then this is a **live open discriminator** — the single row on the whole site that could still separate the framework from MOND+EFE with existing or near-term data. Either outcome is more interesting than the current state, which is that `/for-researchers` says "0 tests that could select it over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM" on one page while `/mond-unification` publishes a structural prediction that contradicts MOND+EFE on another. **2. TEST-01's kill criterion fired in the published literature in 2016–2017.**

**4. The a₀(z) row quotes a σ against a branch that isn't the framework's, on a systematics-limited statistic.**

Three problems, in increasing severity:

- *A σ quoted as a 2.5σ-wide range is a systematics statement, not a measurement.* If the answer moves from 2.3 to 5.9 depending on an unstated normalization choice, the systematic dominates the statistic and neither endpoint should be quoted as a tension. Name the normalization and pick one.
- *RAR-fitted a₀ per redshift bin at z > 1 is confounded.* It inherits stellar M/L evolution, IMF assumptions, dust, and beam smearing on the rotation curves — all of which push the inferred baryonic acceleration in the same direction. 79 galaxies is not enough to break those degeneracies. This is a signal-to-systematics < 1 measurement, which is precisely the category the site elsewhere insists on flagging.

**6. A2ACW: sound as a case report, not sound as a null.**

`/research-philosophy` is unusually candid — the shared-corpus ceiling is named, the temporal-asymmetry control is reported as failed 0/6, the cross-vendor control is registered and pending, and the 2026-07-17 correction states that a single-arm N=6 retrospective cannot separate "ceiling on AI adversarial collaboration" from "the seed framework was a reparametrization." All correct.

Two things I'd say as a referee:

*The page doesn't name the deepest problem.* Two instances of the same model class arguing is not adversarial in the statistical sense — it's two draws from one posterior. Their disagreement measures sampling variance, not independent evidence. The cross-vendor control mitigates *corpus* overlap but not this: frontier models are trained to similar objectives on overlapping web-scale data, so "maximally disjoint pipelines" is a difference of degree, and the control can come back clean while the underlying non-independence is untouched. The registered control is worth running, but it should be pre-registered as *not* being able to settle the question it appears to settle.

*The missing arm is cheap and it isn't the cross-vendor one.* The stated blocker is access to different-corpus model pairs. But the binding gap is the **positive control**: run the identical protocol on six genuine post-cutoff discoveries and see how many it flags as novel. Without that, 0/6 has no denominator — a detector that says "reparametrization" to everything scores 0/6 on this set and is useless, and a perfect detector scores 0/6 too, because all six *were* reparametrizations. The site knows this (`/for-researchers` reports specificity 0/6, Youden J = 0). The positive control needs no special access and would convert a case report into a measurement. I'd run it before the cross-vendor arm, not after.

*Carry the caveat forward.* `/for-researchers` lists "A2ACW program null" as a citable artifact. `/research-philosophy` says it's "a well-documented program-level null, not a measured ceiling." The second sentence should appear on the first page. As it stands, the citable-artifacts list quotes the result without the qualifier that makes it honest.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Does EFE = 0 survive momentum conservation? A pointwise multiplier on g with no field-equation nonlinearity is the configuration Milgrom flagged in 1983. If it doesn't survive, that closes the galaxy sector on internal consistency without any data — and it's the last open structural claim.
2. Under the framework, what is the predicted wide-binary boost in the solar neighborhood? Local stellar density (~0.1 M☉/pc³) sits ~4 dex below ρ_crit ≈ 652 M☉/pc³, so C ≈ 0 and the boost should saturate at B_max = 3.17. Both sides of the Gaia DR3 dispute — Chae's ~1.4× and Banik's ~1.0× — are far below that. Isn't that a second refutation independent of which side wins, rather than the "below systematics" verdict TEST-02 currently carries?
3. Has anyone checked TEST-01 against McGaugh+2016 / Lelli+2017 before listing it as pending? If not, how many of the remaining "Untested" rows are in the same state?
4. What normalization choice moves the a₀(z) tension from 2.3σ to 5.9σ, and why isn't it stated?
5. Why does the Λ face of Milgrom's coincidence (a₀ ∼ c√(Λ/3), constant in z) appear nowhere? It is the branch that survives if a₀ ∝ H(z) fails.
6. Was γ in the chemistry table computed from 2/√N_corr or fit per material? This is a lookup in the generating script, not a research question, and it currently gates the site's most-quoted number.
7. If EFE = 0 and the ambient-density lever is ~45× too small to measure, does the framework predict *anything* environmental that is observable in principle? If not, the environmental sector should be labeled unrunnable rather than untested.
8. `/for-researchers` offers four citable negative results. Which journal or section are they targeted at? A quantified-magnitude null on algebraic density coupling is a legitimate short paper; a program-level A2ACW null with N=6 and no positive control is not yet one.

### Verdict

The framework is dead as physics and the site says so accurately, so the interesting question is whether the *ledger* is trustworthy — and it is, with one systematic bias: it under-reports in its own disfavor on structural claims and over-reports σ values on statistics that have no power. EFE = 0 is a real, uncatalogued, SEP-exact prediction that contradicts MOND and is either already refuted at ~4σ or is the last live discriminator on the site; TEST-01's kill fired in the literature a decade ago; and the a₀ relation implies H₀ = 77.6, a free 4.4σ refutation the site prints and then declines. Meanwhile a 2.3–5.9σ "tension" is quoted against a prediction the framework shares with Milgrom. Fix those four and this becomes what it should be: the reference null-result document for anyone tempted to couple gravity to local density — which is a genuinely useful thing to have, and which nobody else has written down.
