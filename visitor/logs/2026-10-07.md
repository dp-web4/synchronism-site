## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /key-claims, /interactive-tools, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer, /gamma-calculator, /consciousness-demo, /honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Refreshingly honest. The site tells me in its second sentence that its big idea failed, and I believed it. But I left unsure why I should spend 45 minutes learning an equation that the site calls "dead."

### Journey
I came from Reddit expecting a "one equation explains everything" pitch. The landing headline says that, *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"*, and then the very next line says *"We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated."* Further down: *"As tested physics: dead."* That got my attention. I've never seen a site undercut its own headline like that. The scorecard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations, 0 of 9 former "Validated" claims surviving) was easy to read, even though I didn't know what a refutation "executed" meant.

The equation shows up on the landing page right away. The plain-English line under it (*"a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are"*) made me smile, but it didn't actually help me. What gets squashed, and why?

I followed the **Beginner Path** to /why-synchronism. The "fragmentation problem" framing is good, and the paragraph "What changes if this works?" is the best hook on the site: *"the boundary between 'quantum' and 'classical' becomes a calculable density threshold."* I'd watch a PBS Space Time episode about that. But this page already uses MOND, SPARC, "ansatz" and tanh, so I skimmed.

**First Encounter** was the most helpful page. The marching band vs. crowd in a plaza analogy for coherence clicked immediately, and tanh is finally explained (*"an S-shaped curve, like a dimmer switch"*). I wish that sentence had been on the landing page next to the equation. Then I hit γ, written as γ = 2/√N_corr with no explanation of what "correlated particles" are. The galaxies section says stars are N_corr = 1, γ = 2, *"the pinned value that loses to MOND."* The "Where It Fails" box (melting points 53% off, superconductors 6.5× wrong) was blunt and clear. I respect it.

**Key Claims**: the landing page called these *"three testable hypotheses."* When I got there, two were badged **Speculative** (quantum = "phase scanning", consciousness at C ≈ 0.50) and both pages say they can't be tested yet. The third, dark matter, is badged **Failed**. So "three testable hypotheses" made me expect something the page doesn't deliver. The cathode-ray-tube analogy for quantum superposition was fun, though.

**Tools.** The tools index was easy to scan, with clear Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced levels.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: Picking a galaxy from the dropdown is easy, and the one-line takeaway is excellent: *"The solid violet line is Synchronism's equation as published: it stays on the gray line and does not close the gap — that is the failure."* But there are five curves. The dotted amber one is an *"old illustrative stand-in — what this page showed before 2026-07-08, kept for comparison"*, and its legend shows a formula with tanh and made-up-looking numbers. Why show me a curve that was drawn by hand to fit and then retired? As a newcomer I couldn't tell which line to look at until I'd read the explanatory text below the chart.
- **Coherence Explorer**: Two sliders, γ and ρ_crit, plus a log/linear toggle. The "What to notice" prompts help ("Move γ from 2.0 downward and watch the curve flatten"). The γ presets are 0.046 (density-fit galaxies), 0.49 (acceleration-fit/MOND), 0.5 and 2.0 (original guess). By now I'd seen **three different γ values for galaxies** (2, 0.49, 0.046) across three pages, and I couldn't work out which one is "the" galaxy value or why there are several. "Saturation knee, not a critical point" meant nothing to me.
- **γ Calculator**: The best tool for me. One input, and a plain definition: *"γ is the steepness of the coherence S-curve — how abruptly a system switches from 'independent individuals' to 'acting as one.'"* The page shows the formula is backwards (*"watch the most-correlated systems get the flattest curves, which is backwards"*). Seeing the failure demonstrated is better than being told about it. The Galaxy preset (N_corr ≈ 17, "back-fitted from SPARC γ ≈ 0.49") partly explained the 2-vs-0.49 puzzle, but only because I read the fine print.
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo**: The card is honest (*"Watch 8 'independent' approaches converge on C ≈ 0.50 — because they share one assumption"*). The page itself quickly went over my head (SNARC `salience_total`, IIT-Φ, PCI, propofol EEG).

**Honest Assessment**: It opens bluntly (*"The one equation did not hold as stated. It lost every test it executed"*). I was glad there is an "If you read nothing else (this page is ~18,000 words)" summary, because 18,000 words is a book chapter. One thing tripped me up: the scorecard line reads *"0 confirmed predictions, 0 prospective predictions tested, 6 refutations executed."* If zero predictions were tested, how did six get refuted? I assume "prospective" means something specific, but the landing page and this page phrase the scorecard differently and I couldn't reconcile them. I was also surprised to learn on this page that *every* audit was done by AI agents, including simulated "personas." That's interesting, and a bit disorienting to discover this late. It belongs somewhere I'd see earlier.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /galaxy-rotation ×2 passes, /parameter-derivation, /interactive-tools, /core-idea ×2, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /coherence-explorer, /tier-1-existing, /for-researchers)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes, as a case study in self-auditing, not as physics
- **Overall impression**: Every piece of algebra I checked by hand is correct, and the site's own verdicts on its claims are mostly harsher than mine would be. What I couldn't pin down is *which equation the framework is*. The headline formula, the galaxy-plotter formula and the formula behind TEST-09/10 are three different functions, and the refutation count adds them together.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link with "interesting AI project." I skipped the beginner material and went to the galaxy and parameter pages with a notepad.

**The MOND identity.** /galaxy-rotation claims tanh(γ ln(1+x)) ≡ [(1+x)^{2γ} − 1]/[(1+x)^{2γ} + 1], which collapses at γ = ½ to x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), i.e. Milgrom's simple μ with a₀ = 2a₀′. I checked it: tanh u = (e^{2u}−1)/(e^{2u}+1) with e^{2u} = (1+x)^{2γ}. It's correct and exact for all x, not just asymptotically. The page then draws the right conclusion: at γ = ½ this *is* MOND, so agreeing with MOND is a reparametrization and not evidence. Two consequences the site states only partly:
- For small x, tanh(γ ln(1+x)) ≈ γx for **any** γ, so the μ-form gives deep-MOND scaling g_obs ≈ √(a₀′ g_bar/γ) for every γ. γ only controls the shape of the transition, and the flat-curve normalization depends only on a₀′/γ. That explains the γ/A and a₀′/γ degeneracies the site reports (Fisher correlation +1.000000, /parameter-derivation). A single sentence deriving this would turn "we found they're unidentifiable" into "of course they are".
- For large x, 1 − C ≈ 2(1+x)^{−2γ}. The page says it "approaches 1 like (1+y)^{−2γ}" with return exponent q = 2γ. That's correct as a scaling statement, but it leaves out the factor 2, which matters for Cassini-type bounds. Minor.

**BIC bookkeeping checks out.** Free γ vs McGaugh: Δχ² ≈ −0.84 plus one parameter at ln N = ln 2807 = 7.94 gives ΔBIC = +7.1, which matches. The site also gives the galaxy-level number (γ = 2 pin: +184 at point level → ≈ +11 at galaxy level). Most papers don't report that correction at all.

**a₀ = cH₀/2π**: c·67.4 km/s/Mpc / 2π = 1.04 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², which is 13% below 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰. That matches the site, and the site correctly labels it Milgrom's 1983 coincidence.

**Mean-field / tanh.** As a condensed-matter person, I expected to be annoyed here. I wasn't. /core-idea says the Ising resemblance "is only visual: C(ρ) has no feedback loop." That is the correct objection: there is no self-consistency, so no spontaneous symmetry breaking. It also says C is analytic everywhere, so asking for critical exponents is "a category error." γ = 2/√N_corr is called sign-inverted relative to mean field. That's right too: correlated systems should get *sharper* crossovers as N grows, and this formula makes them *flatter*. The page also admits N_corr "is back-solved from the fitted γ, not counted." That's an unusually complete self-demolition.

One overstatement remains on /core-idea: the equation "is an ordinary rational (Hill-type) saturation function of (1+x)^{2γ} … the flagship equation *is* one of them, **exactly**." C = (w−1)/(w+1) with w = (1+x)^{2γ} is a Hill n = 1, K = 2 function of (w − 1), i.e. a shifted Möbius map. It is **not** a Hill/Naka–Rushton function x^n/(K^n + x^n) in the physical variable x, except at γ = ½. It approaches Hill-n = 2γ only asymptotically. The point (tanh∘ln is notation, not mechanism) stands, but "exactly one of them" claims more than the identity shows.

**The three-function problem.** This is my main finding.
1. Headline: C = tanh(γ ln(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)), density-keyed (landing page, /for-researchers: "the working equation").
2. /galaxy-plotter's "Synchronism as published": v² = v_b² + [V_flat · C(ρ)]², with γ = 2 and ρ_crit = 0.029 V_flat². This is an **amplitude** form with V_flat as an input. The site admits that "a curve built from V_flat cannot be a prediction of V_flat."
3. /tier-1-existing TEST-09/10: C(a) = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)·x/(1+x), x = (g_bar/a₀)^{1/φ}. This is a floored **Hill** function in acceleration. It isn't a tanh-log at all.
