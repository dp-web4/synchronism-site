## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (landing, /start, /beginner, /core-idea, /equation-walkthrough, /learning-paths, /tools, /coherence-explorer, /consciousness-demo, /galaxy-plotter, /glossary, /honest-assessment)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The opening question hooked me in about ten seconds, and then every single page spent more energy telling me it hadn't worked than telling me what it was.

### Journey

I clicked through from a Reddit comment. The landing page is genuinely good bait — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" is exactly the kind of thing that makes me watch a 25-minute YouTube video. The "one dial" framing (how coherently a system's parts act together, computed from its density) is something I could repeat to a friend at a bar. That's a real achievement and I want to say so before I complain.

Then, still on the landing page, I hit the scoreboard: **0 confirmed predictions, 6 refutations**. My honest reaction was "…oh. So it's wrong?" I respect that they put it up front — I've never seen a science site do that — but nobody ever told me why I should keep reading *anyway*. There's a "See What Failed" button competing with "Start Exploring." I've never seen a site try to get me to leave that fast.

I took the **Beginner Path**. Step 1 ("Why Synchronism?") is fine but already drops MOND, tanh, γ, ρcrit, Ncorr, BEC, BCS, and *ansatz* — an actual German word, untranslated, on step 1 of the path that says "no physics background needed." I skimmed it.

Step 2, **First Encounter**, is the best page on the site for me. "A marching band moving in lockstep: high coherence. A crowd wandering in a plaza: low coherence." That's it. That's the whole idea, and I got it instantly. Dense crystal = high, sparse gas = low. Great. It also says the section is "7 short pages — ~90 seconds each," which confused me, because the header right above it says "Step 2 of 6." Six or seven? Where are the other pages?

Step 3, **The Core Idea**, is where I fell off. It's something like 4,000 words. It has four equations, including one called a "Hill function equivalence" with exponents in it. It uses BTFR, RAR, Vflat, and Ncorr as if I know them. And it is stacked with caveat boxes — a "this scaling is refuted, and had been sitting here uncaveated" note, a "6 of 6 badges previously labeled Validated have been demoted" note, a criticality retraction dated 2026-07-23, and an admission dated 2026-08-08 that "its own best fit sets cooperativity to zero." I don't know what most of those mean, but I can read tone, and the tone is a page apologizing to itself. This is step 3 of the beginner path.

I bailed to the **Equation Anatomy** page and it saved the session. "tanh is like a dimmer switch: input near 0 → output near 0; input very large → output near 1." Perfect. "ln: a number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger." Also perfect — that's the first time natural log has ever made sense to me. **This page should be step 2 or 3 of the beginner path, not a side link from Tools.** It teaches the scary-looking symbol in two sentences, which means the equation on the front page stops being scary.

Then I played with the tools (below), looked at the **Glossary** (which is step 6 of the beginner path and starts with a₀, A2ACW, Active-MRH, AIC, ALFALFA-SDSS, Audited-Negative, BAO — an acronym wall, no search box), and finished on **Honest Assessment**, which I'll admit I liked. The "What a non-physicist should take away" section is the clearest writing on the site: the equation fits galaxy rotation but only as well as a 40-year-old idea and never better; its one distinguishing prediction was run and failed; no proposed experiment can currently tell it apart from normal physics. I understood every word of that. It's also, as far as I can tell, the site telling me the answer is no.

### Tools Tested

- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — mostly intuitive. Two things dented it: the "what to notice" instructions describe starting at γ=2.0 but the slider starts at 0.49, and the C(ρcrit)=0.3272 readout contradicted my assumption that the "reference density" was the middle of the curve. - **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (`/consciousness-demo`) — worked — conceptually clear, emotionally strange. Eight different methods all landing on C ≈ 0.499 ± 0.012 looks like a stunning result for about four seconds, until the caveat box explains it's "geometric, not empirical: every approach that picks the midpoint of a [0,1)-bounded output range will land near 0.50 by construction," that all 8 were developed in-house sharing the same assumptions, and that no procedure exists to connect it to an actual EEG or fMRI reading. That's admirably honest. But I genuinely don't understand why it's a featured tool rather than an example on the failures page — it's a demo whose only lesson is that the demo means nothing.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — confusing. I picked NGC 3198 because I recognized it from a video. Five curves is too many, and the amber "illustration only" line that was drawn by hand rather than computed made me distrust the chart itself. The one thing I did understand — the violet framework curve doesn't reach the blue observed dots — I understood only because the text told me so, not because I could see it.

### Unanswered Questions

1. If it's 0 for 6, why is this site still here? What would have to happen for the answer to become yes — and is anyone actually running that test?
2. What is coherence a coherence *of*? A marching band is people moving together. What is moving together inside a galaxy, or inside a proton?
3. Where does ρcrit's number come from? Both the walkthrough and the tool call it "a characteristic scale parameter" and then decline to say. Is it measured, guessed, or fitted to make the answer come out right?
4. If this equation only ever matches MOND and never beats it, what does the coherence story add — a better picture, or an actual difference?
5. Is dark matter real in this framework, or is it saying dark matter doesn't exist? I read four pages and still can't tell you which.
6. What was the 2026-08-08 note about "its own best fit sets cooperativity to zero"? It sounded like the most important sentence on the page and I couldn't decode it.

### Verdict

The landing page and the marching-band explanation are excellent — I understood the core idea in under two minutes, which is more than most physics sites manage. But the beginner path is not built for beginners: it routes a no-physics reader through a 4,000-word equation page, a full technical self-audit, and an alphabetical acronym dump, while the one page that actually makes the math friendly is filed away under Tools. The relentless honesty is the site's most distinctive quality and I'd defend it, but right now the failures are delivered before the stakes, so a casual reader learns what didn't work before ever learning what it was for. I'd come back if someone told me a test had actually fired — but nothing on the site made me feel that was coming.

### Journey

I audited this the way I'd audit a docs set: crawl the nav, check every link resolves, then follow the terminology and see whether it survives the trip.

**Link hygiene is genuinely good.** Every path in the primary nav and footer returns 200. The legacy URLs a reader might have bookmarked — `/tools`, `/start`, `/beginner`, `/predictions` — all 307 to their current homes (`/interactive-tools`, `/why-synchronism`, `/first-encounter`, `/test-catalog`). No duplicate content, no dead ends. I went looking for the usual rot (`/badges`, `/validation`) and got clean 404s on paths nothing links to. This is unglamorous work that someone actually did, and I want it on the record before the complaints.

**The badge system is where I spent most of my time**, because it's the site's central editorial device — every claim carries one — and because the site makes an explicit promise about it. `/research-philosophy` (Methodology Track, Step 1 of 6) lists the two families and then says something I almost never see in a docs set: *"The definitions now live in exactly one place; this page keeps only the rationale for having two families, which is what it is for."* Every badge on that page links to the canonical legend instead of restating it. That is exemplary. Whoever wrote that sentence understands single-sourcing.

Then I went to check whether the rest of the site honors it, and it doesn't.

The canonical legend on `/honest-assessment` defines **9** labels across two families, and states that *"the color and leading word(s) are the formal status, drawn only from the two families below."* But `/tier-1-existing` lists 14 test rows carrying status strings like **"Never Run As Registered"**, **"Disfavored on σ₈, Underpowered on Registered Statistic"**, **"Underpowered As Registered"**, **"Demoted to Tier-2 (Not a Tier 1 Test)"**, **"Failed, Ceiling Exceeded"**, **"Failed, Empty Intersection"**, **"Self-Eliminating-or-Tie"**, **"Kill Criterion Triggered"**, and **"Closed"**. `/interactive-tools` adds two more — **"Model Explainer"** and **"Artifact Lesson"**. The canonical page itself acknowledges a third family it calls "Operational states" with seven more labels.

So: ~9 documented labels, and by my count well over 20 in circulation. The "drawn only from the two families" sentence is describing a rule the site stopped following. I want to be precise about *why* this matters, because these strings are individually excellent — "Underpowered on Registered Statistic" tells me more in three words than "Failed" ever could. The problem is that a badge is a *scannable* device: its value comes from a reader learning ~9 tokens once and then reading a table at a glance. A free-text status column is prose, and prose has to be read. The site currently pays the visual cost of badges and gets the comprehension of a notes field.

The legend has also multiplied. `/honest-assessment` is canonical; `/tier-1-existing` ships its own "Status badge definitions" section; and the glossary clusters the badges as entries — where the wording has already drifted. Canonical **Reparametrization**: *"Equivalent to existing physics in different notation."* Glossary **Reparametrization**: *"The result is mathematically equivalent to existing physics expressed in different variables."* Same meaning today. Two strings to maintain, so not the same meaning forever. That's the exact failure mode `/research-philosophy` was written to prevent.

**Numbers that no longer match their content.** `/test-catalog` states *"Synchronism has defined 24 specific, falsifiable experiments organized into four tiers"* — and then, on the same page, documents TEST-25 (added 2026-07-28) and TEST-26 (proposed 2026-08-10). `/tier-1-existing` lists TEST-25 in its table. The nav tooltip repeats "24 proposed experiments." Two items were appended and the headline count wasn't touched. The tier breakdown has the same problem: Tier 3 is given as TEST-15–24 (7 tests) and Tier 4 as 3 tests "part of the 15–24 range" — ten IDs split 7/3 with no statement of which is which, so I can't reconstruct the tier boundary from the page.

**Progress indicators.** `/first-encounter` shows four different numbering systems in roughly one screen: "Beginner Path — Step 2 of 6", "7 short pages — ~90 seconds each", "Section 1 of 7", and "6–8 steps each". I eventually worked out that the page is *one step* of a *six-step path* and internally has *seven sections* — but I had to infer that. Pass 1 hit the same wall and read it as a contradiction, which tells you the inference isn't reliable.

Credit where it's due on the hard case: `/core-idea` belongs to *two* paths, and it handles that correctly — it shows both "Beginner Path — Step 3 of 6" and "Philosophy Track — Step 1 of 7", and offers both onward links ("What Synchronism Is Not" for the beginner path, "The Hard Problem" for philosophy). Multi-path membership is a genuinely hard IA problem and this is the right solution. It just needs the same care applied to intra-page section counts.

**Breadcrumbs** are present on every content page and read three levels: `Home / Core Theory / MRH`. But the middle segment is inert — "Core Theory" is not a link, and `/core-theory` returns 404. Same for "Predictions & Experiments". So the breadcrumb advertises a category layer that has no browsable page, and worse, its taxonomy (Getting Started, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments, Methodology, Interactive Tools) is a *second* IA that doesn't map onto the eight top-nav labels. Two org charts, one of them not clickable.

**Terminology.** First-use discipline is the weak spot on exactly the pages that can least afford it. `/first-encounter` is Step 2 of the path advertised as needing no physics background, and it uses RAR, BTFR, DM, and CHSH with no expansion. The glossary that defines them is Step 6 — definitions arrive four steps after the terms. And the glossary has no term filter: the only search box on the page is the global header input, whose placeholder is "Search..." and whose accessible name is **"Search pages"**. On a 72-entry alphabetical list, a reader will type "BTFR" into that box and get page results, not the definition sitting a few hundred pixels below.

Two smaller terminology notes. EFE's glossary entry opens with *"In MOND, a system embedded in an external gravitational field…"* — the expansion "External Field Effect" appears in the entry's *second* paragraph, so the lead line and the hover tooltip never expand it. Since "EFE" also standardly means "Einstein Field Equations," the one place guaranteed to disambiguate it doesn't. And *ansatz* appears inside the Gamma entry's own definition (*"A motivated ansatz, not a derivation"*) without being a glossary entry itself — a definition that leans on an undefined term.

**Tools.** I tested the two nobody covered in Pass 1. Both are unusually candid, and both drop the label their index card promised.

### Tools Tested

- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — clear mechanics, confusing framing. Breadcrumb and heading are correct; the N_corr input, linear/log toggle, and seven presets (ideal gas → BCS superconductor) are all well labeled. But no epistemic label appears on the page, and the readout does something I've never seen a calculator do: it returns "γ = 1.0000 · Boundary (γ ≈ 1) — *a label per the inverted formula (Caveat 2), not a physical classification*." The tool computes a number and then tells you the number's classification is not physical. That's admirable honesty and terrible interaction design — the caveat is attached to the output rather than to the control, so a reader learns the result is void only after committing to it. Also: ρ_crit appears in the plotted equation without being defined anywhere in the tool.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (`/phase-boundary-visualizer`) — worked — the most self-aware page on the site. It opens with *"This tool animates a refuted relation — on purpose"* and states the γ formula "predicts the *opposite* of real condensed-matter behavior." The caveat box (⚠ "N_corr-method caveat (important)") explains that ideal gases and galaxies both land at γ = 2.0, reflecting "the *counting method*, not the system." As documentation of a failure this is exactly right. Two writing problems: the γ axis runs 0–4 while the page notes BCS sits at γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, "three orders of magnitude off the left edge" — so the tool's headline counterexample is not on the tool; and the page uses **D**, **S** and **ΔBIC** without defining them (it even says "D and S remain undefined," which I think means undefined *in the theory*, but a reader will take it as undefined *on the page*).

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is the badge column meant to be a controlled vocabulary or a free-text status field? The canonical contract says the former; `/tier-1-existing` ships the latter. A reader can't tell which convention to trust, and neither can a future contributor.
2. What is the intended relationship between the breadcrumb taxonomy (Getting Started / Core Theory / Predictions & Experiments / Methodology) and the top-nav labels? They partition the site differently and only one is clickable.
3. Which tests are Tier 3 and which are Tier 4? The page gives overlapping ID ranges.
4. Are the "Operational states" (Kill Criterion Triggered, Null-Class, Sign Correction, …) badges or annotations? They're listed in the canonical legend, excluded by its own "only the two families" sentence, used on tables, and mostly absent from the glossary — three signals pointing three ways.
5. Do the tool pages intentionally omit their epistemic labels, or did the labels get added to the index cards and never propagate to the destinations?

### Verdict

The structural bones here are good — clean redirects, no broken links, working prev/next, correct handling of pages that belong to two learning paths, and an explicit single-sourcing policy on `/research-philosophy` that most documentation teams never write down at all. The failures are the classic maintenance-drift kind: a controlled vocabulary that grew ~20 labels past its 9-label contract, a legend that got copied to three places and has already diverged in one, and countable claims ("24 experiments", "Step 2 of 6") that stopped matching the content beneath them when items were appended. None of that is a writing-quality problem — the prose is unusually precise, and the status strings are more informative than the taxonomy they violate. It's a governance problem: the site's rules for itself are correct and no longer enforced, which for a project whose entire credibility rests on its own audit discipline is the one category of error it can least afford.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 (`/`, `/core-idea`, `/coherence-function`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/mond-unification`, `/galaxy-rotation`, `/dark-matter`, `/phase-transitions`, `/born-rule`, `/key-claims`, `/honest-assessment`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/learning-paths`, `/interactive-tools`, `/chemistry-correlation-explorer`, `/equation-walkthrough`)

### Journey

I went straight past the onboarding to `/coherence-function`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/galaxy-rotation` and `/tier-1-existing`, then checked the arithmetic myself. I want to lead with the credit, because it's unusual: **`/parameter-derivations` is one of the more honest theory pages I have read anywhere.** It states "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations," badges a₀ = cH₀/(2π) as *Reparametrization — Dimensional Analysis*, correctly identifies Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) as not an independent parameter, and correctly identifies the φ exponent as fitted-then-named. `/phase-transitions` contains the single best sentence on the site: *"the critical exponents are off by ~2× not because of a calibration miss, but because a compander has no critical point to generate exponents from."* That is exactly right. C(ρ) = tanh(γ ln(1+ρ/ρ_crit)) is real-analytic on the whole domain, monotone, with no free-energy functional, no conjugate field, no symmetry breaking and no correlation length. Asking it for critical exponents is a category error, and the site diagnosed its own category error correctly. Most frameworks at this stage would have quietly reported the 2× and called it "order of magnitude agreement."

So the badges are honest. I could not find a single overclaiming badge in 16 pages. What I found instead is that **the honesty is unevenly applied — and the numbers underneath the honest labels don't all hold.**

**Check 1 — the μ_simple identity is wrong as printed.** `/coherence-function` says: "At γ = 1/2, this becomes MOND's interpolating function μ_simple(x) = x/(1+x)." Substituting γ = 1/2 into the site's own (correct) Hill identity gives

  C = [(1+x)^1 − 1] / [(1+x)^1 + 1] = **x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2)**

not x/(1+x). I checked numerically: the residual against x/(1+x) is −0.167 at x = 1, so it is not a rounding artifact. It *is* absorbable — x = ρ/ρ_crit and ρ_crit is free, so the factor 2 rescales into ρ_crit and the one-parameter *family* claim survives intact. But the equation as displayed is false, on the page that carries the framework's most consequential structural result. A referee stops reading at that line. (Same page, smaller: "exactly a Hill function" — it is 2·H(u) − 1 with u = 1+x, n = 2γ, K ≡ 1, i.e. Hill up to an affine map with K pinned. Affine-equivalent, not equal. This one errs *against* the framework, which is its own kind of tell.)

**Check 2 — ΔBIC = +184 counts 2,807 correlated points as independent.** `/galaxy-rotation` declares a kill criterion: "ΔBIC = +184 ≫ 10 refutes the γ=2 compander." The arithmetic is right — 2807 × 2 ln(0.1485/0.1437) = 184.5, I reproduced it. The statistics are not. Those 2,807 radial points come from **175 galaxies**, and RAR residuals are dominated by *per-galaxy* nuisances: distance, inclination, and the stellar M/L normalisation shift every point in a galaxy coherently. The effective sample size is far nearer 175 than 2,807. At N_eff = 175 the same computation gives **ΔBIC ≈ +11.5** — over the threshold, but nowhere near "≫ 10," and it flips below 10 for any N_eff ≲ 152. A kill criterion that depends on an unstated independence assumption to a factor of 16 in N is not a kill criterion; it is a fit-quality statement wearing one.

The irony is that the site **already has the robust version and buries it**: γ free converges to 0.489 with RMS identical to McGaugh's ν (0.1437 vs 0.1437), i.e. ΔBIC ≈ +7.1 ≈ ln(2807), which is just the one-extra-parameter penalty. That is a *clean tie* and it is N_eff-independent, because a tie at equal RMS stays a tie however you count. The tie is the stronger and more damaging result. The site leads with the fragile kill and hides the robust one.

**Check 3 — the comparison models are never audited.** `/key-claims` reports "BTFR slope: framework predicts n = 3.35; MOND predicts 3.81; observed 3.75 ± 0.10." The framework's σ is computed correctly (0.40/√(0.07²+0.10²) = 3.28σ ✓). But **MOND does not predict 3.81.** MOND predicts n = 4.00 exactly — M_b = V⁴/(G a₀) is the deep-MOND limit, it is a theorem of the theory, not a fit. 3.81 is somebody's fitted slope. Against the same 3.75 ± 0.10, MOND's actual prediction is 2.5σ off. The framework is graded on its prediction, MOND on its best fit.

The same asymmetry runs through **TEST-25 (Cassini, +17.95σ)**, which is counted as one of the six refutations. The Cassini quadrupole bound excludes *simple-μ-family interpolating functions* in the solar system — this is Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024) and it kills MOND's simple μ just as hard. Since the site's own γ ≈ 0.489 is the simple-μ point, TEST-25 is not a framework-specific refutation at all; it is inherited prior art applying to a function form the framework shares with MOND. It should be badged *MOND-shared / inherited*, not counted as an independent root. And **EFE = 0** (on `/mond-unification`) is what GR and ΛCDM predict — the SEP. A test on it can only refute the framework; it can never distinguish it from the standard model.

Net: the site's own self-critical apparatus is aimed in one direction only. That biases the scoreboard **pessimistic**, which for a project whose whole product is its audit discipline is a defect of the same class as overclaiming, just pointed the other way.

**Check 4 — the load-bearing bridge doesn't exist, and the tested function isn't the framework's function.** `/galaxy-rotation` states plainly that "no algebraic chain from C(ρ) to g_obs exists anywhere in the framework," and `/dark-matter` carries f_DM = 1 − C only as a note in a related-concepts block. Meanwhile the *tested* object is μ_Syn = tanh(γ ln(1+x)) with x an **acceleration** ratio, while C(ρ) takes x = ρ/ρ_crit, a **density** ratio. Those are different functions of different variables, and the difference is not cosmetic. For a spherical enclosed mass,

  ρ̄/g = [3M/(4πr³)]·[r²/(GM)] = **3/(4πG r)** — mass cancels exactly.

So swapping ρ for g_bar is not a change of units: it converts an acceleration-keyed law into a **size-keyed** law, ρ̄ ∝ g/r. (For local rather than mean-enclosed ρ the divergence is larger still: local ρ in an exponential disc falls exponentially while g_bar falls as ~1/r.) This substitution is, by `/coherence-function`'s own words, "the framework reduces to MOND with density ρ substituted for enclosed-mass acceleration — though critically, this substitution has never been evaluated."

That sentence is the whole situation and it is buried in a caveat: **the single structural difference from MOND is untested, and all six executed refutations ran on the MOND-shared version.** The framework has not been refuted; a MOND variant has been refuted six times, and the framework's actual content has never been to bat.

**Check 5 — the boost ceiling contradicts the coherence function.** TEST-10 rests on B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17 ⇒ f_DM ≤ 68.5%. The arithmetic checks (1/0.315 = 3.175; 1 − 1/3.17 = 0.6845; f_DM = 0.927 ⇒ B = 13.7 ✓). But with f_DM = 1 − C we have B = 1/C, so B ≤ 3.17 requires **C ≥ 0.315 everywhere**, and C(ρ) → 0 continuously as ρ → 0. Nothing in the coherence function generates a floor at C = Ω_m. The ceiling is not merely "asserted rather than derived" (as `/parameter-derivations` correctly says) — it is *inconsistent with the framework's own equation*, and it is the sole support for two of the six refutations. Separately: Ω_m is a global, epoch-dependent cosmic mass fraction. There is no locality argument that lets it bound a *local* dynamical boost inside a galaxy, and the site notes without resolving it that a₀(z) and Ω_m(z) "evolve in opposite directions."

**Check 6 — the missing Lagrangian, and what EFE = 0 actually diagnoses.** I could not find a covariant formulation or an action anywhere on the site. That is the real gap, and "EFE = 0 structurally" is its symptom, not a prediction. In MOND the external field effect is a *consequence* of the nonlinear Poisson equation of the AQUAL Lagrangian — it is not an add-on. A theory that returns g_obs = f(g_bar) pointwise, with no action, generically violates momentum conservation for a two-body system; this is Felten (1984), and it is precisely why Bekenstein & Milgrom (1984) wrote AQUAL in the first place. The site presents EFE = 0 as a structural prediction. A referee reads it as evidence that the modification has no variational principle behind it — which also means conservation laws, the Newtonian limit, and lensing are all unchecked.

**Check 8 — γ = 2/√N_corr, and what the sign error does downstream.** The site correctly badges this *Audited-Negative — sign inverted*: the Hill index n = 2γ = 4/√N_corr *falls* with the number of correlated units, whereas cooperativity and finite-size scaling both make transitions *sharper* with N. The borrow is from CLT fluctuation scaling σ ∝ N^(−1/2), which implies γ ∝ √N_corr — the reciprocal of what's used. Good catch by the site. But the consequence is not propagated: **`/phase-transitions` still quotes "1,703 chemical phenomena, 89% within the predicted boundary region" with no estimator caveat**, and `/chemistry-correlation-explorer` still leads with r = +0.982 for sound velocity while flagging the inversion only as an unresolved "bookkeeping question." If the sign is inverted, a positive r is evidence *against*. That 89% cluster at γ ≈ 1 is therefore a property of the *counting method*, not of the systems — which is exactly what the Phase Boundary Visualizer says when it notes ideal gases and galaxies both land at γ = 2.0. The tool knows; the theory page doesn't.

**Check 9 — a₀ and which error bar.** cH₀/(2π) = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² at H₀ = 67.4 ✓; 1.129×10⁻¹⁰ at SH0ES ✓. Against g† = 1.20 ± 0.02 (random) ± 0.24 (systematic), the site quotes 0.66σ. I get 0.65σ on the systematic and **7.9σ on the random**. ### Journey

I came for one question: **is there anything here I haven't reviewed before?** I skipped the onboarding entirely and went to `/mond-unification`, `/tier-1-existing`, `/top-5-tests`, `/a2acw` and `/quantum-predictions`, then checked the framework's own equations against the literature.

Lead with the credit, because it is unusual and it is real. `/top-5-tests` opens by saying the page **is empty** — "there are zero active discriminating tests." `/test-catalog` states "of these 24 proposed tests, 0 could select Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM." `/a2acw` reports its own protocol as "a reparametrization detector, not a discovery engine" and prints a Clopper-Pearson interval on its own failure rate. I review for journals and I have never seen a research program publish a page whose content is the absence of its own content. That is worth more than most of what I referee.

Now the findings.

**Finding 1 — the field equation is Refracted Gravity, and the site does not know it.** `/mond-unification` gives the completion as **∇·[C(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ**, with C a monotone increasing function of *local mass density*, saturating at 1 in dense regions and falling toward a floor in sparse ones, and the site derives the resulting boost ceiling from 1/C.

That is **Refracted Gravity** (Matsakos & Diaferio 2016). RG's field equation is ∇·[ε(ρ)∇Φ] = 4πGρ, where ε — the "gravitational permittivity," by explicit analogy with a dielectric — is a monotonic increasing function of the local mass density, running from a floor ε₀ in sparse regions to 1 in dense ones. **C ≡ ε.** Not "similar to." The same object, defined the same way, in the same equation, with the same physical analogy. RG has an eleven-year literature: DiskMass Survey rotation curves (Cesare et al. 2020, A&A 637 A70), three E0 ellipticals (A&A 2022), galaxy clusters (A&A 2025), and — this is the important one — **a covariant formulation** (arXiv:2109.11217; A&A 2023), a scalar-tensor theory with self-interaction V(φ) = −Ξφ, which derives modified Friedmann equations and offers an explanation of a₀ ~ Λ^(1/2).

Four consequences, all of which change the site's own ledger:

1. **The "no Lagrangian" gap is closed by prior art, not open.** Pass 3's sharpest structural complaint (and the first thing any modified-gravity referee asks) has a published answer since 2021. The site should be checking whether CRG's scalar-tensor completion reproduces its C(ρ), not writing a "why there is no Lagrangian yet" page.
2. **The boost ceiling is a fitted parameter, not a cosmological identity.** In RG the maximum boost is exactly 1/ε₀, and ε₀ is *fitted* — the literature values sit around 0.56–0.67. The site instead asserts B ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17, i.e. ε₀ = 0.315, from a cosmological mass fraction with no locality argument. Pass 3 correctly noted this contradicts C(ρ)→0. The prior art dissolves the contradiction and the derivation at once: there *is* a floor, it is a free parameter of the theory, and it is not Ω_m. **Two of the six refutations rest entirely on this ceiling.** If ε₀ is free, TEST-09 and TEST-10 are not refutations of the framework — they are measurements of ε₀, and SPARC's f_DM = 0.927 measures ε₀ ≈ 0.073, which is the interesting number and is nowhere on the site.
3. **EFE = 0 is a known property of the class since 2016**, not a structural prediction of this framework. Any ε(ρ) theory satisfies the SEP for internal dynamics by construction, because ε keys on density rather than on the external field.
4. **The Coma cluster bridge was closed in-house in 2026 when the RG cluster paper existed.** `/tier-1-existing` reports four C(ρ) ansätze tested against Coma, all failed, M_app/M_B bounded at ≤2 vs. observed 4.6. RG's own cluster work is the correct comparison and is uncited.

I checked `/mond-unification`'s full citation list — Milgrom, McCulloch, Verlinde, McGaugh, Ciocan, Bournaud, Lelli, Chae, Freundlich, Paranjape & Sheth. No Matsakos, no Diaferio, no Cesare, no permittivity gravity of any kind. The site's prior-art screening covers MOND and emergent-gravity, and misses the one theory class its equation actually belongs to. That is the single highest-value fix on this site, and it is *good news* for the framework in three of the four consequences above.

**Finding 2 — the DESI exclusion is larger than the data can produce.** `/honest-assessment` reports that the framework's w(z) "runs monotonically from −2γ in the past to exactly −1 in the future," that 0 of 192 γ values reach the DESI quadrant at every Brans-Dicke ω tested, and that the offsets are **3.4–6.3σ** across DESI's four data combinations.

At the framework's own SPARC-fitted γ ≈ 0.489, −2γ = **−0.978**. So the predicted w(z) lives in the interval [−0.978, −1]: a total excursion of 0.022 in w, monotone, no crossing. DESI DR2's own uncertainties are σ(w₀) ~ 0.07–0.09 and σ(w_a) ~ 0.3. **The framework's dark-energy sector is ΛCDM to well inside a tenth of a sigma.**

Now the arithmetic ceiling. DESI DR2 excludes ΛCDM at **2.8σ (Pantheon+), 3.8σ (Union3), 4.2σ (DESY5)** — that is DESI's own headline. A model that sits 0.02 in w from ΛCDM cannot be excluded by that same dataset at 6.3σ. No dataset excludes a model more strongly than it excludes a model 0.02 away from it, plus the separation. The upper bound available here is ~4.2σ and the framework should be sitting *at* it, indistinguishably from ΛCDM.

`/top-5-tests` names the mechanism: "forced w_a comes out +0.23…+0.60, wrong sign every time." **Forced.** If w_a is pinned to the framework's value and w₀ profiled (or vice versa) rather than the pair marginalized, the resulting number is a distance-in-a-fixed-slice, not a model-comparison significance, and it inflates without bound as the slice moves off the degeneracy direction — and the w₀–w_a contour is famously a long thin banana. That is exactly the shape that turns a 0.1σ model into a "6.3σ exclusion" if you take a fixed-parameter cut across it.

The honest statement is the opposite of the one on the page: **the framework's DE sector is ΛCDM-degenerate, therefore DESI cannot refute it and cannot confirm it.** TEST-26 has no power in either direction and should not be Tier 1. As written, this is the same defect the site correctly diagnoses everywhere else — a test with no discriminating power — but recorded as a refutation instead of as a null. It biases the scoreboard pessimistic, which for this project is the same class of error as overclaiming.

**Finding 3 — two mutually exclusive force laws are live, with opposite limits.** `/mond-unification` states g_obs = g_bar/C(ρ), from ∇·[C∇Φ] = 4πGρ. `/galaxy-plotter` computes **v(r) = √(v_b² + [V_flat·C(ρ(r))]²)**.

These are not two parameterizations of one law. Take ρ → 0, so C → 0:

- Field equation: g → g_bar/C → **∞**. Unbounded boost in the outskirts.
- Plotter: the coherence term → 0, so v → v_b. **Zero** boost in the outskirts. Pure Newtonian.

Opposite limits, in the regime that carries the entire dark-matter phenomenology. And the plotter's is not the tested one: the RAR/BTFR/Cassini refutations ran on μ_Syn = tanh(γ ln(1+x)) with x an *acceleration* ratio — a third law. Three force laws, three pages, no cross-reference, and the site's headline count of six refutations does not say which law each one refuted.

**Finding 4 — the plotter already ran the experiment the site calls "never evaluated," and it failed catastrophically.** The site's most consequential caveat (Pass 3 identified it as the whole content of the framework) is that substituting density ρ for acceleration g_bar "has never been evaluated." But `/galaxy-plotter` evaluates C on each galaxy's actual density profile — ρ(r) = Σ(r)/2h from real surface-density data, five galaxies — and states the outcome plainly: disk densities sit orders of magnitude below ρ_crit, C stays near zero, and the framework curve fails to fill the gap.

I sanity-checked the magnitude. With ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat² and V_flat ~ 150 km/s, ρ_crit ~ 650 M⊙/pc³, against a disc mid-plane density of order 0.1–0.2 M⊙/pc³. So ρ/ρ_crit ~ 3×10⁻⁴, C = tanh(2 ln(1+3×10⁻⁴)) ≈ 6×10⁻⁴, and the coherence term contributes ~0.1 km/s to a 150 km/s curve. **The density-keyed law is not marginally wrong; it is wrong by ~3–4 orders of magnitude in C.** That is the evaluation. It is running in a browser, on the site, right now, badged *Reparametrization*.

Two things follow. The "never evaluated" caveat should be replaced by the executed result (with its honest caveat: 5 galaxies, one ρ_crit calibration). And the direction of failure matters — under the plotter's law the framework *under*-predicts to nothing, while under the field equation's g_bar/C the same C ~ 6×10⁻⁴ would *over*-predict by a factor of ~1700. The framework has a four-orders-of-magnitude sign-of-failure ambiguity that depends on which of its own two equations you use.

**Finding 5 — the A2ACW confusion matrix is transposed, and the null is instrument-limited.** `/a2acw` reports "Sensitivity (demoted claims caught): 6/6" and "Specificity (genuine discoveries passed): 0/6," with a Clopper-Pearson bound placing true specificity ≤ 0.46.

Both labels are wrong, in a way that flatters the protocol. If all six externally audited claims resolved as reparametrizations, then the *negative class is empty*: there were no genuine discoveries in the sample to correctly pass. Specificity is 0/0 — undefined — and a Clopper-Pearson interval on it has no denominator. Under the only reading that makes the number 0/6 meaningful, it is a **positive predictive value**: of six claims A2ACW passed, zero were genuine. And in that same reading, "sensitivity 6/6" is not A2ACW's — the *human external audit* caught 6/6. A2ACW passed all six reparametrizations to audit, so **A2ACW's own sensitivity to its target class is 0/6.** The page credits the protocol with the auditor's performance and then computes a confidence interval on a quantity with no sample.

The temporal-asymmetry control independently confirms this: 0 of 6 demotions caught by differently-cutoff models. So the measured sensitivity of the instrument is consistent with zero — and **an instrument with zero demonstrated sensitivity cannot support a null.** "0 novel survivors in 3,308 sessions" is a statement about the detector, not about the corpus. The page half-concedes this ("cannot separate whether the ceiling belongs to the method or to a corpus containing no novelty") but then continues to present the 1.4% yield as a measured property of AI self-play.

The fix costs nothing and is the most interesting experiment available to this project: **run a spike-in.** Take N genuinely novel results published after both models' training cutoffs, strip identifying vocabulary, feed them to A2ACW as candidate claims, and measure the recovery rate. That converts "0/47" from an uninterpretable null into a calibrated one. Right now the site's headline methodological result is an uncalibrated detector reporting zero.

**Finding 6 — the quantum predictions are the cited papers' own results.** `/quantum-predictions` lists two items, both correctly badged *Post-diction* — credit for that. But I pulled both citations:

- arXiv:2405.14685 is Hall, Sirkina, Morreau, Langbein & Muljarov, *Controlling dephasing of coupled qubits via shared-bath coherence* — which demonstrates reduced dephasing for two qubits in a common bath **at specific distances between the dots**. That is the site's prediction, including the distance dependence.
- arXiv:2508.07046 is Hatifi, *Geometry-Controlled Freezing and Revival of Bell Nonlocality through Environmental Memory* — in which qubit separation in a structured reservoir stores, revives or suppresses Bell nonlocality, with explicit **decoherence-free nodes**. That is the site's second prediction, node structure and all.

So both citations are real, correctly matched, and each *is* the claim being made. A post-diction that restates its own source contributes nothing beyond a vocabulary map. More usefully, the prior-art family cited for the first one is the wrong one: Viola, Knill & Lloyd 1999 is **dynamical decoupling** — active pulse control. Passive protection from a shared bath is **decoherence-free subspaces**: Zanardi & Rasetti (1997), Duan & Guo (1997), Lidar, Chuang & Whaley (1998), with the experimental demonstrations by Kwiat et al. (Science 2000) and Viola et al. (Science 2001). Γ ∝ (1−c) with T₂ ∝ 1/(1−c) is the standard collective-dephasing result of that literature, ~28 years old. The site's own A2ACW audit predicted this: median prior-art year ~1996.

Two technical notes. The stated correlation function **c(d) = cos²(πd/λ₀)** is platform-specific: in *free space* two-emitter collective decay goes as sinc(k₀d) (Dicke 1954; Lehmberg 1970), which decays with separation and has no exact nodes. Exact periodic nodes require a standing wave — which is precisely Hatifi's mirror-terminated guide. The framework inherits a boundary condition it doesn't derive, and the page should name the platform or the "prediction" is untestable in the geometry a reader will assume. And the kill criterion — "if T₂ does not scale as 1/(1−c), the model fails" — would also kill standard open-quantum-systems theory. It is a non-discriminating test, the same defect the site correctly diagnosed for EFE = 0.

**Finding 7 — the third "novel" quantum prediction is dynamical decoupling.** `/key-claims` lists "periodic resync protocols should outperform continuous isolation for certain noise profiles." That is dynamical decoupling versus passive isolation, correct for 1/f-dominated spectra, established by Viola & Lloyd (1998) and Uhrig (2007), and running in every superconducting-qubit lab on earth. `/key-claims` presents it under "novel predictions" with no badge and no citation, while `/quantum-predictions` cites Viola for a *different* claim. The one place the site cites the right paper, it attaches it to the wrong prediction.

### Unanswered Questions

1. Is C(ρ) the gravitational permittivity ε(ρ) of Refracted Gravity? If yes — and the field equation makes it hard to see how not — what does Synchronism add to Matsakos & Diaferio (2016) plus the covariant formulation (arXiv:2109.11217)? If no, what distinguishes them?
2. Which force law is canonical: v² = v_b² + (V_flat·C)², g = g_bar/C, or the acceleration-keyed μ_Syn the refutations ran on? They have different — in two cases opposite — limits, and the ledger of six does not say which law each refutation refuted.
3. In the DESI comparison, was w_a marginalized or fixed? What is the Δχ² between the framework's best fit and ΛCDM's on each of DESI's four combinations? At γ = 0.489 I expect ≈ 0 and I cannot reconcile that with 6.3σ.
4. In RG's language, where does the SPARC fit put ε₀? The literature fits sit near 0.56–0.67; the site's asserted 1/Ω_m gives 0.315; SPARC's maximum f_DM = 0.927 demands ≈ 0.073. Those three numbers are the actual open question and none of them is Ω_m for any stated reason.
5. Why has the A2ACW spike-in never been run? It requires no new data, no new access, and it is the difference between "we measured a null" and "we have an uncalibrated instrument."
6. Do the two quantum post-dictions survive removal of the waveguide boundary condition? In free space the correlation is sinc(k₀d), which has no exact nodes — does the framework predict nodes there, or does it inherit them from Hatifi's mirror?
7. If ε₀ is a free parameter rather than 1/Ω_m, do TEST-09 and TEST-10 come off the refutation ledger — and does the count of six become four?

### Verdict

There is nothing here that is new to modified gravity, but that is a less interesting finding than the one I actually came away with: **the site does not know which theory it has.** Its field equation is Refracted Gravity, a published 2016 theory with a covariant scalar-tensor completion since 2021 and an eleven-year observational literature — none of it cited — and that omission is currently costing the framework in both directions, inventing a "no Lagrangian" crisis that prior art resolved and manufacturing a boost-ceiling refutation out of a parameter the literature simply fits. Two of the six refutations depend on that ceiling and a third (the DESI dark-energy no-go) quotes an exclusion significance larger than the data can arithmetically produce for a model that is ΛCDM to 2% in w. The audit discipline here is genuinely better than most published work — `/top-5-tests` reporting itself empty and `/gamma-calculator` shipping its own refutation are things I have never seen a research program do — but the discipline is pointed inward at claims and never outward at the literature or at the site's own arithmetic. Fix the prior-art screening and the marginalization, and I would referee this program's next preprint without prejudice; leave them, and the honest scoreboard is honest about the wrong numbers.
