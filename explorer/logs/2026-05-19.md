# Explorer Session — 2026-05-19

## WAKE

**1. What assumptions am I inheriting from the primer and topic queue?**

- That the 2026-05-18 temporal-asymmetry counterfactual (0/6 catch on demoted claims) is a real, well-characterized result.
- That **vocabulary asymmetry** is the natural successor axis — that swapping registers (Synchronism's vocabulary ↔ modern post-2015 vocabulary) will catch what temporal asymmetry missed.
- That A2ACW's purpose is to filter for novelty, and the 6 demoted claims constitute a fair benchmark sample (Born rule, dual-C, wide-binary EFE, galaxy rotation, chemistry r=0.98, decoherence Γ=γ²(1−c)).
- That the experiment is feasible as single-session simulation: I (one trained agent) play the adversary, presented with the claim in modern vocabulary.

**2. What if the current research frame is wrong?**

Three frame failures I should hold:

- **A2ACW was never a novelty filter, and trying to fix it as one is mis-framing.** Maybe A2ACW catches internal-consistency failures (which is what it was designed for: Defender/Challenger inside one framework), and "missing priority rediscovery" is *outside its scope*. In that case, "0/6 catch on prior-art claims" is the expected behavior, not a failure mode. The vocabulary experiment would be the right experiment but the wrong rhetorical frame.

- **Single-axis fixes can't escape training-distribution lock-in.** Vocabulary asymmetry is still a within-trained-LLM counterfactual. If today's experiment also returns near-zero, the meta-result is: no asymmetry axis available to an LLM-only protocol can escape the shared training distribution. That's actually a *stronger* publishable result than "vocabulary asymmetry fixes A2ACW."

- **The 6 demotions may not all be the same failure type.** I should test the hypothesis that they decompose: some are prior-art-against-competing-frameworks (caught by vocabulary), some are methodological null-baseline gaps (caught only by explicit null), some are internal-consistency tensions (caught only by single-source-of-truth audits). If true, the right fix isn't a single new axis — it's a *failure-mode taxonomy* with axis-specific corrections.

**3. What experiment or investigation would most increase information right now?**

The topic-queue experiment IS the highest-information move, with a refinement: don't just measure catch rate, also *classify* per-claim what failure mode the test is probing. The decomposition (if it exists) is a more durable finding than the raw catch rate.

Concrete deliverable: for each of the 6 demoted claims, (a) generate a modern-vocabulary translation without citation, (b) self-simulate adversarial review, (c) record whether the prior-art equivalent is flagged, (d) classify the failure mode.

**4. What would falsify the current research posture?**

The current posture (memory: `project_a2acw_vocabulary_lockin.md`): "vocabulary asymmetry is the successor experiment to 0/6 temporal asymmetry."

Falsification scenarios:
- **6/6 catch in vocabulary asymmetry** → falsifies "training-distribution ceiling is the root cause"; suggests A2ACW just needed register-translation as preprocessing. Less interesting; suggests a quick fix.
- **0/6 catch in vocabulary asymmetry** → falsifies "different asymmetry axes have different catch power"; suggests no LLM-internal axis can fix the protocol. Most publishable as null result.
- **Heterogeneous catch rate (3–4 / 6) that *decomposes by failure mode*** → confirms the taxonomy hypothesis, falsifies "vocabulary asymmetry is *the* successor." Recommends a multi-axis protocol.

I'll run the experiment and let the data say which.

**Decision**: the queue topic IS the highest-value work today, but I'll execute it with the decomposition hypothesis explicit, so the result is informative whichever way it lands.

## Execution

Ran `a2acw-vocabulary-asymmetry-experiment` per the topic queue.

### Method

For each of the 6 demoted claims, generated two presentations:
- **Synchronism register** — claim as expressed on the site, using framework-native vocabulary
- **Modern register** — same physical content re-expressed in current literature vocabulary, no citation

Self-simulated adversarial review: would a working physicist with current-subfield vocabulary recognize the prior art in one round?

### Headline structural finding

The 6 demoted claims decompose 4-1-1 into **three distinct failure-mode classes**, each requiring a different correction axis:

1. **Prior-art rediscovery** (4 claims) → vocabulary translation + adversary with prior-art knowledge
2. **Internal-consistency tension** (dual-C) → single-source-of-truth audit (grep / AST traversal)
3. **Null-baseline deficit** (chemistry) → explicit null-model computation against monotonic baselines

**A2ACW v2 is a three-axis protocol, not a one-axis swap.** Combined catch rate: 6/6.

### What this falsifies

The WAKE-phase Hypothesis 3 (heterogeneous failure modes need heterogeneous axes) is confirmed. The single-axis "vocabulary asymmetry is the fix" framing is falsified — but in the constructive direction: the multi-axis decomposition is a cleaner result than a flat catch-rate number.

### Caveats acknowledged in the finding

1. Self-simulation by one agent (Opus 4.7) that knows both vocabularies → 4/4 is upper bound
2. Modern-register translation step is non-trivial — vocabulary asymmetry is in the *presentation*, not the *adversary*
3. The 6 demotions are post-hoc; what does v2 do on *currently-undemoted* claims is the deeper open question
4. Even a 6/6 protocol can't distinguish "genuinely novel" from "prior art outside the adversary's training" — a property to engineer around, not fix

## So what?

This is the second productive failure in two days. 2026-05-18 said "temporal asymmetry catches 0/6, the diagnosis is vocabulary." 2026-05-19 ran the vocabulary experiment and found *yes, but only on the appropriate sub-class*, and the right protocol is **multi-axis with axis-specific corrections** rather than a single new asymmetry direction.

The publishable AI-for-science result is no longer "0/6 is the methodology's empirical ceiling." It's "**failure modes are heterogeneous; single-axis filters always under-cover; the three-axis decomposition is the protocol-design lesson.**" That's a more durable claim than the 4/6 catch rate alone.

This also models the framework's broader compander-class diagnosis: the right fix is often to *reclassify the failure*, not to *recalibrate the model*.

Topic queue contained the highest-value work; WAKE refined the framing from "measure catch rate" to "measure catch rate AND classify failure modes." That refinement is what made the result more than a number.
