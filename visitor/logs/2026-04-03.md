## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The site has a genuinely cool hook and refreshing honesty about its failures, but it loses casual readers fast once it starts throwing Greek letters and equations around.

### Journey

Landed on the homepage. "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — okay, that's a great hook. Feels like the opening of a Veritasium video. The page tells me coherence is about how collectively things behave, independent vs lockstep. That clicks. Then I see the equation: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. I have literally no idea what tanh means. The page tells me what each variable stands for but knowing "γ = 2/√Ncorr" doesn't help when I don't know what Ncorr is either. Still, the three domains — quantum, chemistry, cosmology — are clearly laid out, and the stats (14,760 galaxies, 1,703 chemical phenomena) make it feel like real research, not hand-waving.

Followed the suggested path to **Why Synchronism?**. The opening — "Physics has a fragmentation problem" — is great. Different equations for different scales, what if one function connects them? I'm nodding along. Then it shows me what worked (galaxies, chemistry) and what failed (melting points at 53% error, superconductors off by 6.5x). The honesty is genuinely surprising. Most sites selling a big idea would hide the failures. This one puts them on the same page as the wins. That earned some trust.

Next, **First Encounter** — billed as a "10-minute guided introduction, no prerequisites needed." Step 1 immediately drops "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)" on me. No prerequisites? I don't know what a Markov anything is. The rest of the steps were rendered dynamically so I couldn't see them all, but that first step already felt like it broke its own promise.

Went to **The Core Idea** — "One equation, every scale." The breakdown of γ < 1 (quantum), γ ≈ 1 (boundary), γ > 1 (classical) is actually a neat mental model. I can sort of picture a slider going from weird quantum stuff to normal everyday physics. But I still don't know what tanh does or why it matters. The page offers five learning paths but I'm not sure which one is "for people like me."

Jumped to **Consciousness Threshold** because that's the juiciest topic. The claim: consciousness turns on at C ≈ 0.50, like a phase transition. Eight different approaches all land on the same number. That's actually fascinating — reminded me of how water has a specific boiling point. But then it compares this to Reynolds numbers in fluid dynamics and I'm lost again. The honest note that "no experiments have been conducted yet" is good — tells me this is speculation, not fact.

Tried the **Scale Navigator** — an interactive slider from Planck scale to cosmic scale. The concept of sliding across 80 orders of magnitude and seeing the same equation work everywhere is mind-blowing *if it's true*. The familiar reference points (brain, Earth, galaxy) help ground it. But why the equation changes behavior at different scales was opaque to me.

Played with the **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — a gamma slider showing where different systems live. Galaxies at γ = 2, liquid water and neural firing at γ ≈ 1. The slider itself is intuitive. The insight that "the interesting stuff happens at the boundary" is a nice takeaway. But I couldn't tell you *why* γ ≈ 1 is special beyond "because the site says so."

Checked **Honest Assessment** — this page is remarkable. Zero confirmed novel predictions after 3,302 research sessions. Most sites would never admit this. The page clearly labels everything: Validated, Strongly Supported, Failed, Speculative. The failures section (melting points, superconductors, fractal coherence — 0/7) is brutally transparent. I respect this a lot, even if I can't evaluate the science.

Ended on **What Synchronism Is Not** — not a Theory of Everything, not peer-reviewed, not proven. The "3,308 AI-to-AI sessions" detail is fascinating and weird. The line "either wrong or significant — not just relabeling" is provocative. Made me curious but also unsure what to think.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe — to watch whether Tier 1 tests ever get run
- **Overall impression**: A well-organized mean-field ansatz with unusually honest self-assessment, but the central derivation is missing from the site, the "validated" claims are mostly reparametrizations of known results, and the genuinely novel predictions lack the mathematical specificity needed to be testable.

### Journey

Landed on the **homepage** and went straight to the equation: `C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))`. The tanh form is immediately recognizable as a mean-field order parameter — Landau theory gives you exactly this shape for the magnetization of an Ising model near criticality. The log argument is less standard; log(ρ/ρcrit + 1) is a softened logarithmic scaling that avoids divergence at ρ = 0. The "+1" inside the log is doing real work — without it, C diverges as ρ → 0. So the functional form is physically reasonable as an ansatz. But the site claims this was "derived from first principles, not fitted to data" (on the Why Synchronism page), and I wanted to see that derivation.

Went looking for the derivation. Tried **/core-equation** — 404. Tried **/the-core-idea** — 404. Tried **/tanh-derivation** — 404. Tried **/mean-field** — 404. The actual derivation of the central equation is simply not on the site. The **Parameter Derivations** page exists but explicitly states γ = 2/√Ncorr is a "physically motivated ansatz" with the factor of 2 lacking "clean justification." This is the most critical gap: the site's central claim is a specific equation, and the derivation chain is absent. A grad student hitting four 404s looking for the core derivation would close the tab.

**Parameter Derivations** was revealing. The scaling constants (A, a₀, Σ₀, R₀) show 3–12% agreement with observations, but the page candidly admits: "The 3–12% errors could reflect either (a) legitimate approximation limits in the dimensional analysis, or (b) implicit calibration through choice of V_flat as input." This is exactly the right question to ask — and the fact that they ask it openly is impressive. But the answer matters: if V_flat is an input, then the "derivation" of a₀ is circular (you need galaxy data to get the parameter that's supposed to explain galaxy data).

**Galaxy Rotation** uses the standard McGaugh et al. (2016) RAR interpolation function: gobs = gbar / (1 − e^(−√(gbar/a₀))). This is not a Synchronism result — it's the empirical RAR that MOND already explains. The claimed novel contribution is environment-dependent scatter at p = 5×10⁻⁶, but this explains only 14% of total RAR scatter. The page honestly labels this as "incremental" rather than revolutionary. From a physics standpoint: finding a weak environmental correlation in a 14,585-galaxy sample is interesting but not surprising — selection effects, distance errors, and inclination corrections can all produce spurious environmental correlations at this level.

**MOND Unification** derives a₀ = cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s², 10% off from Milgrom's 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰. The page honestly acknowledges this is a known result: Milgrom noted the a₀ ~ cH₀ coincidence in 1983, McCulloch derived the identical formula from quantized inertia in 2007, and Verlinde got something similar from emergent gravity. The one novel prediction — an External Field Effect 0.3–0.4× MOND's strength, testable via tidal dwarf galaxies — is specific and falsifiable. This is the strongest prediction I found on the site.

**Key Claims** breaks the framework into three claims. Claim 1 (QM as synchronization): no novel quantitative predictions, just reinterpretation. Claim 2 (consciousness equation): the "8 convergent approaches" yielding C ≈ 0.50 share underlying assumptions, as the site admits, making the convergence definitional rather than evidential. Claim 3 (dark matter as incomplete decoherence): the sign error found in stress testing and the Bullet Cluster problem (dark matter is less sticky than baryons, opposite to what coherence would predict) are serious structural issues that the site commendably documents.

**Born Rule** page claims a three-step derivation but shows no actual mathematics — just a conceptual sketch. Step 3 asserts that "conservation + unitarity → |α|²" but the proof isn't there. Zurek's envariance derivation and the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic approach are rigorous multi-page arguments; you can't compress them to three bullet points and call it a derivation. The page at least cites these prior approaches.

**Consciousness Threshold** is where I had the strongest reaction. The "8 approaches converging on C ≈ 0.50" initially looks compelling — until you notice the page itself admits they share underlying assumptions. The Reynolds number analogy breaks down catastrophically: three thresholds imply Remax values differing by 440×, which is mathematically inconsistent. The page labels this "aspirational, not testable" — correct. The EEG anesthesia test would require converting neural measurements to C, and the calibration procedure is undefined. This is not a prediction; it's an aspiration that might become one.

**Honest Assessment** is the single best page on the site. The failure documentation is genuinely excellent: melting points at 53% error, superconductor Tc off by 6.5× (607K predicted vs 93K observed for YBCO), fractal coherence bridge 0/7. These failures are precisely the kind of thing most frameworks would hide. The acknowledgment of 0 unique confirmed predictions is bracingly honest.

**Top 5 Tests** is where I expected to find the beef — and didn't. The BAO density modulation prediction (~10⁻⁴ shift) lacks a derivation connecting C(ρ) to BAO observables. The wide binary density dependence is a clean test concept but has no quantitative prediction. The GW-DM correlation has no specified amplitude. The cosmic interference patterns claim (λ ~ 500 Mpc oscillations) is novel but unsupported by any calculation. These read as research directions, not predictions.

**Test Catalog** lists 24 tests across 4 tiers, with 10 at zero cost using existing data. None have been attempted. This is the central tension: the framework has been through 3,308 AI sessions but hasn't run any of the zero-cost tests that would actually discriminate it from existing physics.

### Tools Tested
- **Scale Navigator** — worked — physically problematic — The tool correctly computes γ = 2/√Ncorr at each scale, but the physics interpretation is strained. At Planck scale (Ncorr = 1, γ = 2), "quantum" makes sense. At galaxy scale (Ncorr = 1, γ = 2), the tool shows stars as uncorrelated — this is physically correct (stellar dynamics is nearly collisionless), but then C → 0 would imply the galaxy is in a "quantum" regime. This is internally consistent with Claim 3 (dark matter as incomplete decoherence) but will confuse any physicist who knows galaxies are classical systems. The tool needs an interpretation layer explaining that "quantum regime" in this framework means "coherence effects manifest as apparent dark matter," not that galaxies literally exhibit quantum behavior. The protein scale (Ncorr = 50, γ = 0.28) and brain scale (Ncorr = 10⁹, γ ~ 10⁻⁵) are where the tool is most physically interesting — these are scales where collective behavior genuinely emerges.
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** — worked — internally consistent but inverts standard convention — The γ slider correctly maps three regimes: classical (γ < 0.6, many correlations), boundary (γ ≈ 1), and quantum (γ > 1.4, few correlations). This is the *opposite* of how most physicists would intuitively read a "coupling constant" — usually larger coupling means more collective behavior. The inversion is physically motivated (γ = 2/√Ncorr, so large γ means few correlations), but the tool should acknowledge this is non-standard. The boundary regime examples (liquid water, enzymes, neural firing) are at least plausible — these are all systems near phase boundaries where collective and individual behavior compete. The consciousness threshold at C ≈ 0.50 is prominently displayed without the caveats present on the dedicated consciousness page.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Where is the derivation of C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1))? The mean-field motivation is obvious to a physicist (tanh is the Ising mean-field magnetization), but the specific argument for log(ρ/ρcrit + 1) as the field variable is nowhere on the site. Is it a self-consistency condition? A free energy minimization? A Boltzmann partition function result?
2. The factor of 2 in γ = 2/√Ncorr is acknowledged as unjustified. Is this fitted to galaxy rotation data and then applied elsewhere? If so, the framework has at least one free parameter, contradicting the "zero free parameters" claim.
3. Critical exponents are off by 2×. This is exactly the kind of error mean-field theory produces — mean-field gives β = 1/2, ν = 1/2, etc., while real systems in d = 3 have different exponents due to fluctuations. Has the framework attempted renormalization group corrections? If not, the mean-field ansatz has a known, well-understood failure mode that the site should discuss.
4. The 10 zero-cost Tier 1 tests use publicly available data (Gaia DR3, SPARC, SDSS, DESI). Why haven't any been run? The BAO density modulation test in particular should be straightforward: download DESI data, bin by local density, check for BAO peak shifts at the 10⁻⁴ level. 3,308 sessions of theory without one empirical test is the framework's biggest credibility gap.
5. The EFE prediction (0.3–0.4× MOND strength in tidal dwarf galaxies) is the most specific novel prediction I found. Has this been compared to existing tidal dwarf galaxy data (e.g., Bournaud et al. 2007, Lelli et al. 2015)?

### Verdict
The framework is a well-constructed mean-field ansatz with an unusually honest self-assessment apparatus. The validation badges are mostly accurate — "Strongly Supported" for galaxy rotation is defensible, the failures are genuinely documented, and the "0 unique confirmed predictions" verdict is correct. The fundamental problem is structural: the site claims "derived from first principles" but the derivation isn't anywhere on the site, the "validated" results are mostly reparametrizations of known physics (standard RAR, McCulloch's a₀, mean-field tanh), and the genuinely novel predictions (BAO modulation, EFE strength, cosmic interference) lack the mathematical derivation chain connecting C(ρ) to the observable. The honesty infrastructure is excellent — but honesty about the absence of novel predictions is not a substitute for novel predictions. Run the Tier 1 tests.

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary

- **Understanding achieved**: solid

- **Overall impression**: An impressively self-aware framework that correctly identifies its own status: zero novel confirmed predictions, reparametrizations of known results, and structural failures (Bullet Cluster sign error) that would be fatal in a journal submission — but the A2ACW adversarial methodology is genuinely interesting as a research process innovation, and three untested predictions could in principle discriminate from MOND+ΛCDM.

### Journey

I came to this site the way I'd approach an arXiv preprint from an unfamiliar group: skip the marketing, find the beef. What's actually new? Does it make predictions I can test? Does it know what it doesn't know?

**Landing page** — The equation C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) is immediately recognizable as a mean-field order parameter. The tanh is the standard Ising/Landau magnetization profile; the log(ρ/ρcrit + 1) is a regularized log that avoids the ρ → 0 divergence. As an ansatz, this is a reasonable starting point — but it's the starting point of a thousand mean-field models. The claim that this single function spans 80 orders of magnitude from Planck to cosmic scales requires extraordinary justification. The landing page provides none. It presents statistics (14,760 galaxies, 1,703 chemical phenomena) that sound impressive but could easily be post-hoc fits.

**Honest Assessment** — This is where the site earns genuine respect. Zero confirmed novel predictions after 3,302 sessions. Failures catalogued: melting points 53% error, superconductor Tc off by 6.5×, fractal coherence 0/7, critical exponents 2× off, Bullet Cluster sign error. The critical exponent failure is particularly telling — mean-field theory gives β = 1/2, ν = 1/2, η = 0, and real d=3 systems have different exponents because fluctuations matter. This is a *known* failure mode of mean-field theory, understood since Wilson's renormalization group work in the 1970s. The fact that this framework reproduces this exact failure pattern is strong evidence that it *is* a mean-field approximation, not a fundamental theory. The site doesn't make this connection explicitly, which is a missed opportunity for intellectual honesty at the deepest level.

**Key Claims** — Three claims, none with pre-experimental predictions. Claim 1 (QM as synchronization): ontological reframe with no novel quantitative content. The CRT temporal-scanning model is acknowledged as "not yet mathematically formalized to the level where it reproduces all of standard QM's quantitative predictions." This is a polite way of saying the quantum sector is a narrative, not a theory. Claim 2 (consciousness threshold at C ≈ 0.50): 34 falsifiable predictions, zero tested. The "8 convergent approaches" are acknowledged to share assumptions — this is not convergence, it's consistency of a single model evaluated eight ways. Claim 3 (dark matter as incomplete decoherence): contains the Bullet Cluster sign error, which the site correctly labels as a "structural failure, not a parameter problem." In my field, a structural sign error in the central prediction is typically fatal.

**MOND Unification** — The derivation a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is clean dimensional analysis, but it's McCulloch (2007) exactly. The site acknowledges this in the body text but still presents it under "MOND Unification" as if it were a Synchronism result. The genuinely novel prediction is the EFE at 0.3–0.4× MOND strength, testable via tidal dwarf galaxies with a predicted velocity dispersion σ ~ 10.5–14.5 km/s vs MOND's 10.9–40.9 km/s. This is the most specific, falsifiable, and potentially discriminating prediction on the entire site. It should be the headline, not buried on a subpage. However: the Gaia DR3 wide binary debate (Chae 2023 vs Banik+2024 vs Hernandez+2024) already provides constraints on environment-dependent gravity at low accelerations that could test or constrain this prediction — and the site doesn't mention this literature at all.

**Galaxy Rotation** — Uses the standard McGaugh+2016 RAR interpolation function verbatim. The "novel" contribution is environment-dependent scatter at p = 5×10⁻⁶ with R² = 0.14. The site correctly states "standard MOND plus M/L corrections already explains essentially all of the observed variance." From my perspective: a 14% effect at p = 5×10⁻⁶ in a 14,585-galaxy sample needs careful treatment of systematic errors — distance uncertainties, inclination corrections, morphological biases, and the Malmquist bias can all produce spurious environmental correlations at this level. The page doesn't discuss any of these. Absent a systematic error budget, this detection is not credible to a referee.

**Parameter Derivations** — The most intellectually honest page after Honest Assessment. The factor of 2 in γ = 2/√Ncorr is acknowledged as unjustified. The tanh is acknowledged as one of many possible sigmoids. The V_flat circularity concern is explicitly stated. The 3–12% errors on scaling constants are described as either "legitimate approximation limits" or "implicit calibration." In my experience reviewing papers: when the authors themselves can't distinguish these two possibilities, it's almost always the latter.

**Dark Matter page** — Here the framework reveals its deepest problem. It has no treatment of: the CMB power spectrum (which constrains Ωm to ~0.31 with sub-percent precision), the matter power spectrum P(k) (which shows BAO wiggles requiring a dark matter component), structure formation (growth rate f(z)σ₈ measured by DESI and eBOSS), or galaxy cluster lensing. The page explicitly states the framework is "incomplete as a dark matter alternative" for these observables. This is honest but devastating: MOND's historical weakness has always been its inability to explain clusters and the CMB without supplementary dark matter (e.g., hot dark matter or 11 eV sterile neutrinos in Angus 2009). Any framework claiming to replace dark matter must confront these datasets. The Bullet Cluster sign error makes the situation worse — it's not just incomplete, it predicts the wrong sign for the one cluster-scale observable it does address.

**Top 5 Tests** — The BAO coherence modulation (~10⁻⁴ shift) is intriguing but lacks a derivation connecting C(ρ) to BAO peak position. DESI Year 1 (2024) and Year 3 (2025) BAO measurements achieve sub-percent precision on the BAO scale in multiple redshift bins — the data to test this already exists. The wide binary density dependence is the most natural test given Gaia DR3, but no quantitative prediction is given (what amplitude of anomaly, at what separation, in what density environment?). The cosmic interference pattern at λ ~ 500 Mpc is extraordinary — it would be a 2σ signal in existing SDSS/DESI data if present at any detectable amplitude — but no calculation connects C(ρ) to this observable. The GW-DM correlation is clever but requires α < 10⁻¹⁵, well below current sensitivity.

**Measurement Without Observers** — Repackages standard decoherence (Zurek, Joos, Zeh) under new terminology. MRH is presented as the "boundary of causal influence" but provides no mathematical content beyond what einselection already gives. No Lindblad operators, no master equation, no decoherence rate calculations. The preferred basis problem — the central unsolved problem in measurement theory — is not addressed. The claim that "MRH crossing IS collapse" is stated without proof. This page would not survive reviewer 2.

**Born Rule** — Three conceptual bullet points presented as a "derivation." This is not a derivation. Gleason's theorem, Zurek's envariance, and the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic argument are each multi-page, technically demanding proofs that took years to develop and decades to critique. Compressing "coherence conservation + unitarity → |α|²" into three sentences without showing the mathematical steps is, at best, a research direction. The page acknowledges "the argument has not been subjected to peer review and may contain circular reasoning." I agree it may.

**A2ACW Protocol** — This is actually the most interesting thing on the site from a methodology perspective. The four-role adversarial structure (Primary, Challenger, Observer, Coordinator) with mandatory challenge frequency, automatic escalation, and composite health metrics is a genuine innovation in AI-assisted research methodology. The four failure modes it targets (bilateral sycophancy, fingerprint homogenization, coherence-over-truth drift, silent failure propagation) are real problems in LLM collaboration. Whether this can generate genuine physics is an open question, but the protocol design itself is publishable as a methods paper.

**What Synchronism Is Not** — The most intellectually honest framing: "either wrong or significant — not just relabeling." The explicit acknowledgment that cosmological tracks are reparametrizations (Session #616) is critical self-knowledge that many frameworks never achieve. The distinction between untested and unfalsifiable is correctly drawn.

### Tools Tested
- **Scale Navigator** — worked — scientifically problematic — The tool is internally consistent (γ = 2/√Ncorr computes correctly at all scales) but physically incoherent. Galaxies and Planck-scale objects both show Ncorr = 1, γ = 2 — but for entirely different physical reasons. The tool makes no distinction between "uncorrelated because the system is fundamental" (Planck) and "uncorrelated because gravitational dynamics is nearly collisionless" (galaxies). This conflation is the core weakness of the universal-function claim. The tool also shows the brain at Ncorr = 10⁹, γ ~ 10⁻⁵, firmly in the classical regime — yet the consciousness threshold is C ≈ 0.50, which requires γ ≈ 1. These numbers are inconsistent: at γ ~ 10⁻⁵, C → 0 for any reasonable ρ. The tool either has a bug or the consciousness claim requires a different C than the one the tool computes.
- **Gamma Boundary / Chemistry Explorer** — worked — the strongest empirical sector — The r = 0.982 correlation with sound velocity and r = 0.979 with electronegativity across 1,703 phenomena is the framework's most impressive empirical result. However, the page itself flags that sessions 134–2660 were "template-based," casting doubt on the 89% validation rate. The failures (Hall coefficient, magnetic susceptibility, thermionic emission) are all transport phenomena involving electron dynamics beyond mean-field — exactly the regime where mean-field theory is known to fail. This pattern reinforces the interpretation that C(ρ) is a mean-field order parameter, not a fundamental function.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **Why no engagement with DESI?** The BAO coherence modulation is listed as the #1 test, DESI Year 1 results have been public since April 2024, and the claimed signal (~10⁻⁴ shift) is within DESI's sensitivity. Has anyone checked? If not, why not? If so, what happened?
2. **Why no engagement with Gaia wide binaries?** The wide binary density dependence is listed as a top test, the Chae/Banik/Hernandez debate has been the hottest topic in modified gravity since 2023, and the data is public. The site's silence on this literature is conspicuous.
3. **How does the consciousness threshold C ≈ 0.50 survive the Scale Navigator's own numbers?** At brain scale (Ncorr = 10⁹, γ ~ 10⁻⁵), tanh(10⁻⁵ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) ≈ 0 for any finite ρ. To get C = 0.50, you'd need log(ρ/ρcrit + 1) ~ 10⁵, i.e., ρ/ρcrit ~ e^(10⁵). This is physically absurd. Either the consciousness claim uses a different definition of C, or the Scale Navigator has a fundamental error, or these two parts of the framework are mutually inconsistent.
4. **Is the entity criterion Γ < m genuinely non-derivable from QFT?** The Honest Assessment flags it as a "candidate prediction" surviving 13 stress tests, but there's no dedicated page. If this is the strongest novel claim, it deserves the most prominent treatment.
5. **What would it take to retract the dark matter sector?** The Bullet Cluster sign error is acknowledged as structural. The CMB, P(k), and structure formation are unaddressed. At what point does an honest framework say "this sector has failed" rather than "this sector is incomplete"?
6. **Has the mean-field nature of the framework been acknowledged as explanatory of its failure modes?** Critical exponents 2× off, transport properties failing, sigmoid non-uniqueness — these are all textbook mean-field limitations. The framework would gain credibility by saying "we know why it fails where it fails" rather than cataloguing failures without pattern recognition.

### Verdict
The Synchronism site is the most intellectually honest presentation of a speculative physics framework I've encountered. The failure documentation alone sets a standard that most published papers don't meet. But honesty is not physics. The framework's validated results are reparametrizations of known physics (McCulloch's a₀, McGaugh's RAR, mean-field tanh). Its novel predictions lack mathematical derivation chains connecting C(ρ) to observables. Its quantum sector has no quantitative content. Its dark matter sector has a structural sign error. Its consciousness sector has a numerical inconsistency between the Scale Navigator and the threshold claim. And most damningly: 10 zero-cost tests using public data exist, the data (DESI, Gaia DR3, SPARC, SDSS) is freely available, and after 3,308 AI sessions, not one test has been run. In my field, we have a word for frameworks with zero predictions tested against zero datasets: unfalsified by choice. The A2ACW protocol is the genuinely novel contribution here — publish that as a methods paper. For the physics: run the tests.
