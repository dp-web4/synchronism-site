## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Galaxy Curve Plotter, Learning Paths, Coherence Explorer, γ Calculator, What Synchronism Is Not, Two Reframes, Honest Assessment summary box)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A site that says "we tried one equation for everything and it didn't work" is weirdly refreshing, but the beginner path keeps dropping me into physicist territory within two paragraphs of any page.

### Journey

Clicked in from Reddit. The headline question — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — is exactly the kind of thing I'd click. The next few lines surprised me: it says the idea was tested and *didn't hold*, that failures are the main product, and that AI agents did most of the work with one human overseeing. I actually read that twice. I've never seen a theory site lead with "this failed." It made me trust it more, but it also made me wonder why I should keep reading — the page answers that with a "Why keep reading" link, which I appreciated.

The "no physics background" box pointed me at the Beginner Path or the Galaxy Curve Plotter, so I went to **Why Synchronism?** first. The opening ("Physics has a fragmentation problem") was great — I know from PBS Space Time that quantum mechanics and gravity don't talk to each other. The crowd-vs-marching-band picture of "coherence" landed for me. Then the page listed what was tested, and I lost the thread. "The predicted effect was over 20% of scatter; the measured one was about 0.01%" — I understand that 0.01% is way less than 20%, so it failed, fine. But "scatter"? "R² = 0.14"? "p = 5×10⁻⁶"? "Kill criteria"? "Null model"? None of those mean anything to me. I skimmed to "What Failed": melting points off by 53%, a superconductor predicted at 607 K when it's really 93 K. Those two I got instantly — big wrong numbers are universal.

**First Encounter** is the best page I saw. Seven short sections with a Continue button, ~10 minutes. Section A (presence = how packed stuff is; lone electron vs neutron star core) was clear. Section B showed the equation. I don't know what "tanh" or "ln" are, but the page called tanh a "dimmer switch" S-curve from 0 to 1, and that was enough for me to keep going. Section C told me the original formula for γ failed, and that when you let γ float freely it just turns into MOND — "a fitted match is not evidence." I understood the *sentence*, but I don't think I understood why it matters, because I only vaguely know MOND is "the other dark matter idea." Section F (wave-function collapse without an observer) is the part a YouTube viewer wants, and it was two paragraphs with "quantum Zeno effect" undefined. Section G listed the failures again. I got to the end but the collapsible "expert notes" about BEC and BCS kept tempting me to click and then losing me.

The **Galaxy Curve Plotter** was the page I was most excited for and the one that frustrated me most. The "New here? Look at three things on the chart" box is great. Blue dots = real galaxy, gray dashed = what visible matter alone would give, violet = the framework's equation, green = MOND. I could see the violet line "is the gray line scaled up" and gets the shape wrong. That's a genuinely clear lesson in one picture. But everything around the chart is a wall of letters: Vflat, Ωm, SPARC, BTFR, ΔBIC, QUMOND, EFE, "quadrature wiring," "kill criteria," TEST-xx. The physicist section is labelled "beginners can skip this," which helped, but the caveats *outside* that section are also written for physicists ("the 47 is also not exactly consistent with the a₀ used"). Two checkboxes offer to show lines the page itself says are "not the theory" and "do nothing" — why would I turn those on?

**Learning Paths** was tidy. Beginner = 5 steps, ~37 minutes. Fine. I noticed the Intermediate path's last step is called "Decisive Tests: Status (none active)," which reads like a joke I'm not in on.

**Coherence Explorer**: two sliders, a curve that moves. Dragging γ down made it flatter, dragging it up made it steeper. The chart literally labels "sparse" on the left and "dense, parts act together" on the right, which is the clearest thing on the whole site. But the subtitle — "Reparametrization: At γ = ½ this curve is MOND's simple μ (acceleration-keyed) and Λ (cosmology)" — is the first line on the page and I can't parse a single word of it. The default slider value is described as "what real galaxies prefer" but also "fails the Cassini bound at 8.7σ." I don't know what Cassini is (the Saturn probe?) or what σ is.

**γ Calculator**: title says "a γ formula kept to show how it fails." I pressed "Ideal gas" then "BCS superconductor" like it told me to. The curve went from steep to almost flat. The page says that's backwards from reality. I believed it but I couldn't tell you *why* a superconductor should be steep. Preset buttons were fun to poke. "Audited-Negative" badge: no idea what that means.

**What Synchronism Is Not** was the most readable long page. "Not a theory of everything. Not established. Not peer-reviewed. Not proven." I liked the honesty. The sections about Lorentz invariance and "C ≈ 0.50 consciousness threshold" went over my head but I could skim them.

**Two Reframes**: I came here because First Encounter said "prefer analogies?" The CRT analogy (old TV screen, electron beam scanning, you see a stable picture at slow sampling but a single dot at fast sampling) is the kind of thing Veritasium would do — I liked it. Then halfway down the page it says "Failed: non-invasive reading" and starts citing Itano 1990 with numbers like 0.194 ± 0.02, and then there's "Lüders rule," "Tsirelson bound," "Kochen–Specker." The pendulum-clock-in-a-centrifuge analogy for relativity was also nice. But the page is labelled "Speculative" and half of it is explaining why the analogy doesn't actually work, which left me unsure whether I was supposed to like the analogy or not.

**Honest Assessment** summary box: five lines. "The one equation failed its galaxy tests. Where it fits, it is MOND in different notation." Clear. "'Untested' means nobody has looked yet; 'Failed' means someone looked." Very clear — that's a distinction I'd never thought about. The rest of the page is enormous and I didn't try.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the equation failed, what's the actual *reason* someone would keep working on it? The site says "the record survives" — but a record of what, for whom?
2. What is MOND, in one paragraph, for someone who's never heard of it? The whole galaxy story hinges on it and I only got "rival gravity theory from 1983."
3. What does "Cassini" have to do with galaxies? I assume the Saturn probe, but how does a Saturn probe test a galaxy equation?
4. Is the consciousness stuff (C ≈ 0.50 threshold) serious, or is it the kind of thing the site is warning me about when it says "speculative"?
5. Did the AI agents *invent* this idea, or did a human invent it and AI agents test it? The landing page says AI did most of the research, but I never found who had the idea in the first place.

### Verdict
I came expecting a crank theory-of-everything and instead found a site that opens with "we tested it and it failed," which is genuinely unusual and kept me reading past the first screen. The First Encounter walkthrough and the galaxy chart are good enough that I understood the core claim and why it lost. But almost every page, including the beginner-labelled ones, slides into physicist vocabulary within a few paragraphs, and the interactive tools lead with their most technical sentence instead of their simplest one. I'd send this to a friend who's doing a physics degree; I wouldn't send it to the friend who got me into Veritasium.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 16 (landing, Why Synchronism?, First Encounter, Learning Paths, Glossary, Honest Assessment, Key Claims, Interactive Tools hub, Research Philosophy, Test Roadmap, Decisive Tests: Status, Tier 1: Existing Data, MRH, Equation Anatomy, Crossover Regime Visualizer, Chemistry Correlation Explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of the structure and the status vocabulary; I did not evaluate the physics)
- **Would return?**: yes, to finish the audit — the information architecture is unusually well thought out and the remaining problems are fixable
- **Overall impression**: A site with a rigorous, documented status vocabulary that is explained in three places, defined canonically in one, and used inconsistently in the places readers actually arrive from.

### Journey

I started at the landing page and walked the header nav in order. Ten items, consistent across every page I opened, same order in the footer plus three extras (Research Archive, Report an Issue, What this is not). That part is clean. The hero reads as a question followed by a verdict, and the verdict is repeated in the footer of every page as "0 confirmed predictions; 6 refutations executed." Good: a reader who lands anywhere sees the status.

**Terminology on the landing page.** The page carries an "Abbreviations ahead" line under the equation and an "Abbreviations used in the table below" paragraph under Scientific Outcomes. I count roughly 35 abbreviations in that second block alone. That is an honest solution, but it is a glossary pasted into a paragraph. MOND is used in the first paragraph and expanded only in the "Why Synchronism?" page. MRH is expanded in the ρ paragraph. A2ACW appears in the methodology note without expansion.

**Learning Paths** is the best-structured page on the site. Seven paths, every step a link with an estimated time, breadcrumb present. Two things stood out. First, the Intermediate path's final step is titled "Decisive Tests: Status (none active)" — Pass 1 flagged this as reading like a joke. I followed it. The target page, at `/top-5-tests`, explains itself well: ranked "Top 5" in May 2026, now zero active discriminating tests, and the URL is kept for history. So the parenthetical is accurate, but it is a status that has been promoted into a title. If the count changes, the Learning Paths label is a second place to update. Second, the Beginner path links Honest Assessment with `#if-you-read-nothing-else`. I checked: that anchor exists. Good.

**Three pages define the validation badges.** The Glossary has entries for each badge ("Failed (badge)", "Active-MRH (badge)" and so on). Research Philosophy has a "Validation Badge Taxonomy" section that lists the names and says "the canonical reference is on the Honest Assessment page." Honest Assessment has "Validation badge definitions (canonical reference)." Key Claims has its own one-line legend ("Reparametrization = known result, new notation" etc.) and links each badge to a glossary anchor. Four places, one canonical. That is a defensible pattern as long as the satellite definitions do not drift. They have, slightly: Key Claims defines Speculative as "not yet testable," Honest Assessment defines it as "a conceptual proposal with no quantitative test," and Key Claims' own descriptor on the consciousness claim says "unfalsifiable as stated." A writer cannot tell from the legend whether "unfalsifiable" is a kind of Speculative or something stronger. Research Philosophy itself notes that the "Failed" definition previously differed between pages and was consolidated — so the maintainers know this failure mode.

**Badge vocabulary vs operational-state vocabulary.** Tier 1: Existing Data has the clearest explanation on the site: four descriptive badges, five MRH-relationship badges, then a separate list of "operational states" (Kill Criterion Triggered, Withdrawn, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Underpowered as Registered, Never Run as Registered, Closed by Execution, Reclassified, Demoted — Exploratory), then "retired labels" (Validated, Strongly Supported, MOND-shared). Test Roadmap adds more states I did not see on Tier 1: Unrunnable as stated, Sign Correction, Ceiling Exceeded, Empty Intersection, Adjudicated, Closed, Retired, Inherited. Test Roadmap says "its badge and state lists are complete and rendered from one source." Two pages, both claiming completeness, with different lists. Either the source has grown since one page's list was rendered or the two pages draw from different sources. Note that I am not evaluating whether the states are correct, only that a reader cannot find one authoritative list.

**Three pages about tests with overlapping names.** The nav has "Tier 1: Existing Data" and "Test Roadmap." The Learning Paths and Test Roadmap link to "Decisive Tests: Status." Test Roadmap says the Tiers 1–4 hierarchy is defined there and Tier 1 says "The testing hierarchy (Tiers 1–4) is defined on the Test Roadmap." Tier 1 also explains why it counts 11 tests when Test Roadmap's "10 Zero Cost" count says 10 (TEST-25 was added later). None of the three pages says in one sentence how the three relate. I had to infer: Roadmap = the full 26-test registry; Tier 1 = the zero-cost slice with verdicts; Decisive Tests = a separate ranked shortlist, now empty. One sentence at the top of each would save every new reader the same inference.

**Breadcrumbs.** Present on every page, with Home linked. The middle segment is inconsistent in two ways. Some pages have no section segment at all (Why Synchronism?, Key Claims, Honest Assessment, Glossary, First Encounter, Interactive Tools). Others have one (Methodology, Core Theory, Predictions & Experiments). And the section segment is a dead label on most pages but a working link on Tier 1 (where "Predictions & Experiments" links to `/test-catalog`). I checked whether section pages exist: `/methodology` redirects to Research Philosophy, `/predictions` redirects to Test Roadmap, `/tools` redirects to Interactive Tools, but `/core-theory` is a 404. So the redirect targets exist for two of the three section labels and the breadcrumb does not use them.

**Broken anchor.** Why Synchronism? links the chemistry card's "89% Boundary-Consistent" badge to `/glossary#boundary-consistent`. The glossary's id is `boundary-consistent-89`. The link lands at the top of a 120-term page. I checked the other glossary anchors referenced from Key Claims, Honest Assessment, Tier 1, and Test Roadmap; they all resolve. This is the only one that does not.

**Acronyms on first use.** Honest Assessment uses A2ACW nine times and never expands it. Key Claims uses CPMG, UDD, DFS, GRW, CSL, SH0ES, BTFR, SNARC, BEC, BCS, DESI, DR2 without expansion; it spells out "External Field Effect" once but never ties it to "EFE". Equation Anatomy uses BCS, ΔBIC, SPARC, RAR, and Cassini in its legend rows with no expansion. Research Philosophy is the one page that expands A2ACW in a heading. The site's convention appears to be "expand in the glossary, not on the page," which works only if every page links to the glossary entry at first use, and most do not.

**Naming drift.** "Tier 1" in the nav and tooltip, "Tier-1 prediction" in Research Philosophy body text. "kill criterion" (singular, in definitions) vs "kill criteria" (plural, in the nav tooltip and Related Concepts lists). "Session #616" and "Session 616" on the same page. MRH is consistently "Markov Relevancy Horizon" everywhere I looked, which I had expected to drift and did not. The glossary has an id of `σ (statistical)` — a space and parentheses in an element id, which will need percent-encoding in any link to it.

**Stale-status risk in titles.** Several titles embed a status that can change: "Decisive Tests: Status (none active)", the Honest Assessment heading "The Verdict (Updated September 2026)", the Tier 1 top badge counting "4 numbered tests with the kill fired." These are accurate today. They are also three more places to edit when the count moves. The site already has a lint for retired phrases (Research Philosophy mentions consolidation work); counts in headings are the next thing it should catch.

**Pass 1's "Section Aρ (presence)" oddity.** First Encounter's Section A heading reads as "Section Aρ (presence)" in a text extraction. I checked the markup: "Section A: Start with Presence" is the title attribute and "ρ (presence)" is a separate styled span. Not a defect, a rendering artifact of text-only readers. Likewise the backslash-escaped underscores I saw on Tier 1 are not in the HTML.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 15 (landing, Key Claims, Honest Assessment, Parameter Derivations, Coherence Function, Galaxy Rotation, Relation to MOND, Dark Energy & DESI, For Researchers, Tier 1: Existing Data, MRH, γ ≈ 1 Boundary, plus Coherence Explorer, γ Calculator, Galaxy Curve Plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: maybe — for the negative-results register on /for-researchers, not for the framework
- **Overall impression**: Every equation I could check is algebraically right and every "this is MOND" admission is correct; the one place the site is softer than its own text is the headline "6 refutations," which includes a kill that its own Tier 1 page says only fires under the one boost-ceiling convention that contradicts the framework's premise.

### Journey

A colleague said "interesting AI project," so I expected the usual grand-unification pitch and went looking for the derivation of the function. The landing page says up front that the thing failed and is "MOND in different notation" when it works, which is not how these sites usually open. I took that as a prompt to check whether the admissions are precise or just rhetorical.

**Parameter Derivations** was the right first stop. It opens by saying it is "not a derivations page in the mathematical sense" and then lists eight parameters, none derived. I checked the algebra. The closed form tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^(2γ) − 1]/[(1+x)^(2γ) + 1] is just tanh(u) = (e^(2u) − 1)/(e^(2u) + 1), so this is a Hill function in (1+x) with exponent 2γ, and at γ = ½ it is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2). Correct, and the "scale doubled" remark is correct. a₀ = cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ (I get 0.868 of Milgrom's 1.2, i.e. 13% low, as stated; 6% low at 73, as stated). Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 119.0 M☉/pc² at 67.4 and 123.6 at 70 — both numbers reproduce. This is Milgrom's Σ_M, and the page now credits him. The Hubble-sphere "derivation" of a₀ gives cH₀/2 = 3.27×10⁻¹⁰, not cH₀/2π; the page says so itself. Nobody on this site is hiding that 2π is a fitted prefactor.

**Coherence Function** gives dC/dx = γ(1 − C²)/(1+x), maximal at x = 0 with value γ, and claims C is concave for all x ≥ 0 with no inflection. I checked: C″(0) = −γ, and C″ < 0 for x > 0. Correct. The Ising comparison is handled honestly: m = tanh(βJzm) has m on both sides and C(ρ) does not, so the "mean-field motivation" is a shape resemblance, not a self-consistency derivation. The page says exactly that. From a Landau-theory standpoint there is nothing here that could be an order parameter: C is single-valued in ρ, there is no broken symmetry, no susceptibility, no critical point. The page concedes "cannot encode universality classes." The ΔBIC table is internally consistent: free-γ tanh-log at +7.1 vs pinned Hill n = 1 at −0.7 differ by 7.8 ≈ ln 2807, the one-parameter penalty for identical RMS. Good.

**γ ≈ 1 Boundary** has a retracted section whose corrected derivatives I recomputed: max dC/d ln x = 0.250, 0.322, 0.375, 0.408 at γ = ½, 1, 2, 4 with a γ → ∞ limit of 0.448 from the maximum of u sech²u at u = 0.772; and κ(0) = γ/(1+γ²)^(3/2) peaking at γ = 1/√2 with κ ≈ 0.385. All reproduce to three figures. The conclusion that nothing singles out γ ≈ 1 is right. The page's 89% headline is then dismantled by the page itself (86% are Debye-temperature restatements, a degree-2 polynomial in Z matches within Δr ≤ 0.07). Fine.

**Dark Energy & DESI** is the most interesting physics on the site, and the one place I had to work. Substituting G_eff = G/C into Friedmann gives ρ_DE = ρ_m(1 − C)/C. At γ = ½, 1/C − 1 = 2/x, so ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit exactly: constant, i.e. Λ. **Negative result 1, the local-density no-go.** The sign argument is compact and right: at the a₀ transition, r_t = V²/a₀, so the mean density inside r_t scales as a₀²/V², i.e. ρ_t ∝ V⁻². Both cannot be true. Either the "knee exponent" is a fitted parameter of the density law in the regime where the site's own Fisher analysis says only γ/A is identifiable (ρ(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000, negative result 4 on the same page), in which case a velocity exponent of A alone is not a measurement; or it is a direct measurement of the midplane density at the rotation-curve transition, in which case it falsifies the a₀-keyed mapping too and the page should say what that means for MOND. The page does not say which. That is the single most important thing on the citable list and it needs one sentence of provenance.

Fine.

### Verdict
There is no living prediction here that differs from MOND + ΛCDM, and the site says so on the page built to answer that question. What survives is a small set of negative results, and the site's own ranking of them is off: its cleanest transferable no-go (any G that is O(1)-sensitive to smoothed ambient density in the Solar neighbourhood is dead by lunar laser ranging at eight orders) is buried in a test card, while a "negative" for a mechanism the site withdrew in August is on the citable list. The one number I would not let through review is the 10σ exclusion of V⁻² on the local-density page, because taken at face value it kills MOND on the data the site says MOND fits; it needs one sentence saying what was measured. The A2ACW question is well posed and unanswered, and the site is right that the answer is an experiment (post-cutoff positive control), not an argument.
