# Topic: Decompose the Chemistry 89% Validation Rate

## Question
Of the 1,703 chemical phenomena counted toward the "89% validated" chemistry rate: how many are statistically independent observables vs. derivatives of each other? How many were predicted prospectively vs. retrodicted post-hoc?

## Context
Three independent visitor personas (Pass 1, Pass 3, Pass 4) independently flagged the 89% rate as uninterpretable in its current form. Pass 4 (leading-edge researcher) was most specific:
- Sound velocity, electronegativity, and atomic volume are not independent observables — they co-vary across the periodic table for well-known bonding reasons
- Sessions 134-2660 are already flagged as "template-based" on `/honest-assessment`
- Without a decomposition, "89% validated" could be anywhere from "89% of independent prospective predictions" (impressive) to "89% of correlated post-hoc retrodictions" (uninteresting)

The data to answer this question likely exists in the session archive.

## Why It Matters
This is the site's flagship chemistry claim and it appears prominently on the landing page and honest-assessment. If the 89% is mostly post-hoc correlations of correlated observables, the headline should change. If it's mostly prospective predictions of independent observables, the headline is underselling the achievement. The answer matters either way.

## Suggested Starting Points
- Session archive: search for chemistry sessions that made explicit predictions before looking at data
- `/gamma-boundary` page: review which phenomena are listed and identify which are physically independent (e.g., sound velocity and bulk modulus are strongly correlated; treat as one)
- Research statistics literature on counting "independent" chemical observables across the periodic table
- Proposed table: list all 1,703 phenomena, tag each as: (a) independent vs. derivative, (b) prospective vs. retrospective, (c) archive session number where predicted

## Expected Output
A decomposition table (or at minimum a summary) that the maintainer track can use to update the chemistry headline to a more interpretable form: e.g., "89% of 1,703 correlated phenomena / 72% of N independent prospective predictions."
