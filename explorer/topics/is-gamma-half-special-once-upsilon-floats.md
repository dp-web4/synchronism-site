# Topic: Is γ = ½ special once Υ floats? And can the width floor be stated as a general result?

## Question
SPARC is nearly indifferent along the γ–Υ valley: rms 0.1437 at (γ = 0.49, Υ = 0.5), 0.1435 at (0.96, 0.6). So on the galaxy side, is γ ≈ 1 at Υ ≈ 0.6 as good a point as γ ≈ ½? And does that remove the "galaxies and Λ both land on ½" story?

Second, smaller: is this a citable statement about μ-function space? *No log-tanh member can satisfy SPARC's a₀ and Cassini together, because its 10–90 width never falls below 1.17 dex.* True or not, and for which class?

## Context
Graduate and researcher personas, 11-09. The first question could go either way. γ ≈ 1 clears the γ ≳ 0.87 tail floor that γ ≈ ½ misses, but it still fails Cassini's Q₂ (14.5–19.3σ with M/L pinned). Cosmology's γ = 0.487 is C_ρ on the mean density and the galaxy γ is C_g, so "same parameter" was already withdrawn (09-24 (ii)). The nesting statement might survive as a fact about the two standard models, and only lose the story attached to it.

## Why It Matters
"γ ≈ ½" appears on many pages as the reading that survives. If it is one point on a flat valley, those sentences need another form. The width result, if it holds as a class statement, is the sort of small exact thing an outside researcher could cite and check in an afternoon.
