**Verdict:** The central claim, that Lorentz violation is Planck-suppressed so the grid is unobservable, is not correct as stated. The construction is not yet a theory with testable content.

**The main flaw: the suppression is assumed, not derived.**
- A grid with a global absolute-time tick defines a preferred frame by construction. Tree-level dispersion corrections of order (E/E_Pl)² are the easy part. Once the substrate couples to interacting fields, loop corrections feed the Planck-scale breaking into the dimension-4 operators. This is the Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia–Vucetich argument (2004). These operators are unsuppressed unless fine-tuned or protected by a symmetry.
- Cubic symmetry helps only partly. It forbids anisotropic dimension-4 rank-2 terms. It does not forbid isotropic boost-violating terms, such as species-dependent limiting speeds, and nothing in the program sets these to zero. Experiments bound such terms far below 10⁻¹⁵ (and much tighter in some sectors, via vacuum Cherenkov, synchrotron and atomic clock-comparison tests).
- Time discretization permits dimension-5 CPT-odd terms unless a symmetry forbids them. Gamma-ray-burst and TeV observations already constrain linear Planck-scale effects to around or beyond E_Pl. Dimension-6 terms are probed by UHECR, photon-decay and Cherenkov thresholds, so "inaccessible energies" is also overstated. The program needs an explicit symmetry or mechanism that protects the dimension-4 terms, as supersymmetry or emergent-symmetry arguments aim to do.

**Other gaps**
- No dynamics are specified for "intent" patterns. Without a Hamiltonian or update rule, no dispersion relation, limiting speed or low-energy field content can be derived.
- Missing standard lattice obstacles: fermion doubling and chirality (Nielsen–Ninomiya), gauge invariance, a recovery of spin-2 gravitons and diffeomorphism invariance, and Bell-inequality-compatible entanglement under a preferred-time update.
- An absolute foliation sits uneasily with general relativity. The program should say what the grid is in curved or dynamical spacetime.

**Strengths**
- The substrate is concrete and discrete, so in principle it is computable.
- The program makes an explicit statement about Lorentz-violation scaling, so it can be falsified.
- Cubic symmetry limits the allowed anisotropy at low order.

2. Compute the radiative generation of dimension-4 Lorentz-violating operators in an interacting toy model (for example, a scalar plus a Yukawa-coupled fermion on the lattice). Show a symmetry that keeps them small, or accept the fine-tuning.
3. Confront the model with the current bounds in the Standard-Model Extension data tables, GRB time-of-flight limits, and UHECR and photon-decay thresholds. Give the bounds on each coefficient.
4. Address fermion doubling, gauge invariance and gravity.
5. Replace "unobservable" with specific predictions, such as the cubic p⁴ anisotropy coefficient and the energy at which it would be seen.
