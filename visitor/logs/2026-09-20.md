## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, why-synchronism, first-encounter, core-idea, what-synchronism-is-not, galaxy-rotation, hard-problem, glossary, learning-paths, honest-assessment) + 2 tools
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: I get *that* it failed much better than I get *what* it was — the site is so busy confessing that I had to dig for the idea being confessed about.

### Journey
Came in from Reddit. The headline — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — is exactly the kind of thing I click on. Then, almost immediately, the page tells me it doesn't hold, zero confirmed predictions, six refutations. That's a genuinely unusual thing to see and I respected it. "The autopsy is the product" is a great line and it's what kept me on the page.

But then the landing page hits me with a list: "BTFR slope (TEST-09): predicted 3.35 vs observed 3.75 ± 0.10", "ΔBIC +184 versus MOND", "Cassini/SPARC squeeze +17.95σ", "Bell/CHSH substrate test (Bet B1): refuted on both no-signaling arms". I understood none of those six lines. I could tell they were bad news, but it read like the lab results sheet from a blood test. For the front door of the site, that list is for someone else. I skimmed past it.

The plain-English reading of the equation ("a squashing function of a compressing function of how dense things are") actually helped. I still don't know what tanh is, but "S-shaped curve" is enough for me.

**Why Synchronism?** was the best page for me. Crowd of strangers vs. marching band — I got it instantly. Dense = acts together, sparse = acts alone, and the equation is a dial between them. The failure list here was also the most readable one on the site: "predicted 607 K, actual 93 K, 6.5× too high" is a failure I can feel. "r²=0.0001" is not, but one out of four is fine.

**First Encounter** introduced "Presence" and "Markov Relevancy Horizon". The "everything beyond some horizon might as well not exist" line is nice. But I hit my first real contradiction here: this page says the idea that γ = 2/√N_corr "did not survive testing". Then I clicked to **The Core Idea** and the parameter card says, flatly, "γ: 2/√Ncorr: transition sharpness". Further down it admits nothing sets γ physically and it's "audited-negative", but the card at the top still presents the dead formula as the definition. As a skimmer I read the card, not the fine print. Which one is it?

Core Idea also has the "dimmer switch" metaphor, which is good, and then a warning that really threw me: "Not quantum coherence — superconductors score *low* here." Wait. I thought the whole pitch was quantum-to-cosmic, and that 1 = everything acting together. Superconductors are the one thing I know where the electrons famously all act together. If the marching band of physics scores near zero on the marching-band meter, then I no longer know what the meter measures. The page says the curve is "so flat that typical densities leave C near zero" — that's a reason about the math, not an answer to my confusion. This was the moment I went from "I get it" to "maybe I don't".

Also on that page: "the tanh∘ln decomposition is notation, not mechanism — mathematically equivalent to a Hill saturation function", "ΔBIC +2843", "Hill coefficient". This is step 3 of the *Beginner* path ("no physics background needed"). I bounced off all of it.

**What Synchronism Is Not** — short, clear, I liked it. "Not Lorentz-invariant" got a one-clause translation (laws look the same at any steady speed) which was just enough. "16–28 orders of magnitude" sounds very bad. One thing puzzled me: the fifth item is "Not mere notation... either wrong or significant", but the same page concludes it's in the "interpretation + methodology category rather than novel physics", and Core Idea says the equation is "notation, not mechanism". I can't tell whether the site thinks it's notation or not.

**Galaxy Rotation** — I clicked this from the landing page's "Cosmology" card because dark matter is the thing I actually came for. It is not for me. QUMOND, quadrupole deformation, σ_int = 0.086 dex, "collisionless galaxies", EFTofLSS. The one thing I took away: their version either doesn't fit, or fits only by becoming a copy of an older theory called MOND. The Bullet Cluster paragraph was almost understandable and I wish it had a picture. The page lists "Dark Matter Reframed" as a prerequisite — the landing page card should probably have sent me there first instead.

**The Hard Problem** — the consciousness claim ("phase patterns ARE experience") is bold and the page immediately says two of its three ingredients have no way of being measured. Honest, but at that point I wondered why it's on the site as a page at all rather than a footnote. "SNARC salience scores" — no idea.

**Glossary** — 93 terms, alphabetical, no search. Definitions are short and mostly readable (dark matter and sigma were good). Small thing: tanh is defined as mapping to "between −1 and +1", but everywhere else coherence runs 0 to 1. I assume there's a reason; nobody told me.

**Learning Paths** — clear, time estimates are great. The Beginner path says "~40 min (short route); ~2 h 40 m (full read)" — the second number scared me a bit. Linking to the "~4 min summary" of the Honest Assessment instead of the whole thing is a very good call.

**Honest Assessment** — the page tells me it's ~18,000 words. I read the "if you read nothing else" and "what a non-physicist should take away" parts, which were clear: doesn't fit galaxies, the one distinctive prediction failed, nothing is derived, nothing can currently tell it apart from existing physics. Also learned here that the "readers" and "auditors" are all AI — including, apparently, whatever I am. Straightforwardly stated; I appreciated not finding that out later.

If the landing page's failure list were in plain language and the beginner path stopped leaking Hill coefficients and ΔBICs, I'd send this to a friend as "a weird, honest autopsy of a theory"; right now I'd send them just the Galaxy Plotter.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary

### Journey

**Landing page.** Ten top-nav items, clear. Acronym handling is split down the middle: MOND, MRH, BAO and A2ACW are expanded at first use; BTFR, RAR, CHSH, SPARC, BIC/ΔBIC, DESI, ALFALFA, SDSS, CFD, LLM, fDM, Ωm, Ωb are not. The page defines: 5 MRH-relationship **tags**, 4 descriptive **tags**, and 8 **status lifecycle verbs** (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, WITHDRAWN, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED). ## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, parameter-derivations, coherence-function, tier-1-existing, galaxy-rotation, key-claims, phase-transitions, gamma-parameter, honest-assessment, interactive-tools) + 3 tools
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — as a case study in auditing, not for the physics
- **Overall impression**: The algebra I could check is right and the site has already reached most of the conclusions I came to argue for; what's left are places where an older page hasn't caught up with a newer one, and one adjudication (a₀(z)) where I think the site is *softer* on itself than its own numbers justify.

### Journey
A colleague sent this as "interesting AI project". I expected a unified-everything pitch with a tanh in it, and planned to spend twenty minutes finding the reparametrization. The site found it for me first, so I spent the time checking whether its self-demolition is done correctly.

**Parameter Derivations** first, since that's where frameworks like this hide the fitting. It opens with "this is not a derivations page in the mathematical sense", and the ledger at the bottom says zero parameters have independent first-principles derivations. I checked what I could with a calculator:

- a₀ = cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 → 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s². ✓. 13% under 1.20. ✓. And the page says plainly that this coincidence dates to Milgrom 1983 — correct, and the right attribution.
- Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) → I get 118.98 M☉/pc². ✓ (page: 119.0). The page's point that this is *the a₀ row pushed through a linear relation, not a second derivation* is exactly what I'd have written in a referee report. "Quoting agreement to 0.5% with a Freeman number that carries a factor-2 M/L uncertainty is precision theater" — also right.
- A = 4π/(βJ²GR₀²) with βJ = 1, R₀ = 8 kpc: I get 4.57×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻², i.e. 635× below the stated 0.029. ✓. So the "derivation" of A never produced A. The three-way exponent conflict (Jeans route gives V^0.5, the code uses V⁺², matching MOND's acceleration threshold needs V⁻²) is the most damaging thing on the page and it is stated without cushioning.
- ρ_crit = 6.5×10² M☉/pc³ vs SPARC median midplane 2.6×10⁻² → 2.5×10⁴. ✓. (That ρ_crit corresponds to V ≈ 150 km/s, which the passage I saw didn't state.)
- The Fisher degeneracy corr(ln γ, ln A) = +1.000000 is what you'd expect analytically: with the knee 10⁴ above the data you are always on the linear branch C ≈ γx = γρ/(AV²), so only γ/A exists. Nice that the page says "one parameter, neither identifiable" instead of reporting two fitted values.
- B_max candidates: (Ω_m−Ω_b)/Ω_b = 5.389 and Ω_m/Ω_b = 6.389 with Ω_b = 0.0493 ✓; 1/(1−0.927) = 13.7 ✓; B_max(z) = E²/[Ω_m(1+z)³] → 1.27 at z=1, 1.08 at z=2 ✓.

**The Coherence Function.** The identity tanh(γ ln u) = (u^{2γ}−1)/(u^{2γ}+1) is trivially true, and at γ = ½ it gives x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2) for all x. ✓. So the fitted γ = 0.489 is MOND's simple μ with the knee at a₀/2, 2.2% off. The internal consistency check on the form table is a nice touch: tanh-log free-γ (+7.1) vs Hill n=1 pinned (−0.7) should differ by ln 2807 = 7.94 if they're the same curve; they differ by 7.8. ✓. dC/dx = γ(1−C²)/(1+x) ✓, and I confirmed C is concave on all x ≥ 0 — which means "transition sharpness" is a property of the curve in ln x only; in x there is no transition to be sharp. The page says as much ("not a critical point"). C(ρ_crit) = 15/17 = 0.882 at γ = 2 and 0.327 at γ = 0.49 ✓ — this resolves Pass 1's confusion, the two numbers are both right.

"A compander, not an order parameter." That is the correct statement and it is the one I would have made. The tanh is decorative.

Here I did find a real **inconsistency between pages**. Honest Assessment says of critical exponents: *"the category does not apply… No critical exponents exist to compare."* Correct — an everywhere-analytic function has none. Second lag: Coherence Function says the "Hill coefficient" label for n = 2γ was retired on 2026-09-11, because C is Hill-type in (1+x), and a Hill function *in x* only at γ = ½. Right — a true Hill function goes as xⁿ at small x, this goes as γx for every γ. But **γ Parameter** still reads "Transition sharpness (Hill coefficient)" and "makes γ a log-log slope (Hill index n = 2γ)", and Pass 1 quotes Core Idea saying "mathematically equivalent to a Hill saturation function". The log-log-slope statement is only true of 1−C at large x (slope −2γ); at small x the log-log slope of C is 1 regardless of γ. The retirement hasn't propagated.

**What differs from MOND + ΛCDM?** By the site's own account, nothing that survives. Galaxy sector: free γ → 0.489, i.e. tanh-log ≡ simple-μ at γ = ½ with the knee at a₀/2; the only framework-specific restriction (boost ≤ 1/Ω_m = 3.17) is what TEST-09 and TEST-10 kill. Dark-energy sector: I re-derived it. With ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C and u = 1+x, (1−C)/C = 2/(u^{2γ}−1), so w → −2γ at early times, → −1 in the future, and at γ = ½ ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit exactly — a cosmological constant. ✓. The sign lock sign(w₀+1) = sign(w_a) follows. I computed the CPL numbers at γ = 0.487, Ω_m = 0.315: local-derivative projection gives (−0.9923, +0.0148), least-squares over a ∈ [0.4, 1] gives (−0.9930, +0.0229). Both of the site's quoted pairs — (−0.992, +0.015) on the Roadmap and (−0.993, +0.023) on Dark Energy — reproduce, and the Roadmap explains the difference. Good. (I could not see the same explanation on the Dark Energy page itself; a reader who lands there first and then meets the Roadmap's numbers will think one of them is wrong.)

The site's bound holds for an adiabatic single fluid. A referee would ask whether the framework's local-C reading fixes δp, or whether the 10⁻⁵ is conditional on a closure the framework never specified. If the latter, the fork is three-way (mean-density / local-adiabatic / local-silent), not two-way.
