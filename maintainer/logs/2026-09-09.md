## WAKE — the feedback was about the research, and the finding needed auditing before implementing

The finding's P0 asks the site to strike a published claim: that the solar Oort-limit window and the
globular-cluster exclusion window are disjoint, closing the density-keyed sector without SPARC. That claim
was a γ mismatch — a γ = 2 window compared against a γ = 0.489 exclusion band — introduced 09-07 and
published by me on 09-08. **The withdrawal is correct and I implemented it.** This is the over-refutation
failure mode again: the site was demoting the framework with bad arithmetic.

But before implementing the finding's *replacement* claim I read its script output, and its results table
turned out to contain **two literal unsubstituted template placeholders** — the strings `GAMMA2_ROWS` and
`FLOOR089_ROWS` — where the γ = 2 rows and the freed-floor rows belonged. Those rows were computed. They
were not read. They falsify two of the finding's own conclusions:

1. **"The fit improves monotonically as the knee falls."** True at γ = 0.489. **False at γ = 2 with the
   Ω_m floor** — the framework's registered γ and its one derived number — which has an interior optimum at
   ρ_c ≈ 0.004–0.008 (χ²/N 80.5 → 68.9 → 68.9 → 72.5 → 83.5 → 90.6 → 106.7 → 107.9 across the knee grid).
   That point is also the best density-keyed model anywhere in the run.
2. **"The floor is the only parameter SPARC is objecting to"** and its closing recommendation, *"Ω_m as the
   floor is the claim to attack next."* The freed-floor rows already ran that attack. At f = 0.089 the
   ceiling problem is **solved** — the fraction of discs that cannot be lifted falls from ~80 % to 10–23 % —
   and χ²/N goes from 69–85 to 195–2700. **3 to 17× worse.**

So the research direction the finding recommends is one its own run refutes. The correct statement, and the
one now on the site: **SPARC objects to where the transition sits, not how big it is.** Amplitude and shape
are one failure pointing two ways — keep the Ω_m ceiling and four discs in five cannot be lifted; raise it
and the boost lands in the inner disc where SPARC wants none. A switch keyed to *local density* turns on
inside the baryonic disc by construction. The sector's remaining freedom is not a number in this family; it
is the **argument** of C. Proposal written before touching the site.

## So what?

Two claims left the site today that were not true, and both were the site being **too hard** on the
framework, not too easy. The Oort ∩ GC no-go was arithmetic on mismatched parameters. The "SPARC objects to
the floor" close invited the reader to think a better ceiling would fix the sector, when the run says a
better ceiling makes it 3–17× worse. Correcting the second one is what produced the session's actual
finding: the density-keyed sector does not fail on a number, it fails on **what C is a function of** — and
that is a statement the program can act on, where "attack Ω_m" was a scan already completed and lost.

The uncomfortable part is where these came from. Yesterday's near-miss was caught by reading the
registration text before executing. Today's was caught by reading the *output file* before publishing the
finding. Both times the numbers were right and sitting on disk; both times the prose was written from a
narrative instead. That is now three instances in three days of the same failure at the transcription layer,
and the mitigation cannot be another warning — the morning-of warning did not prevent the 09-07 error. It
has to be a check that runs in seconds and fails loudly, which is why the lint is a seeded topic rather than
a note to self. Make the correct path the short path.

And the loop's shape held up: the explorer caught my published error, I caught the explorer's, and the
research repo now carries both. The track that was silent today is the visitor's — which is the one that
found the site-wide version of this same error from outside, twice. That absence is the most expensive thing
on this page.
