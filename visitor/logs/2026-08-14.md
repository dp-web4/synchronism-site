## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /why-synchronism, /first-encounter, /core-idea, /tools, /coherence-explorer, /consciousness-demo, /galaxy-plotter, /honest-assessment, /glossary)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The "one equation for everything... that failed" hook is genuinely gripping, but somewhere around the middle of every page the site forgets I don't know what tanh, MOND, or a compander is.

### Journey

Clicked in from Reddit expecting either crackpot physics or a cool Veritasium-style idea. The landing page surprised me — it asks "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" and then immediately says the answer was basically *no*: "0 confirmed predictions," "6 refutations executed." I've never seen a science site advertise its own failures above the fold. That alone kept me reading.

I took the Beginner Path (/why-synchronism). The marching-band-vs-crowd analogy is great — I got the core idea in one paragraph: pack things densely enough and they start acting like one thing. But the same page later drops "tanh," "ansatz," and "compander" on me with no help, which felt like the page changed audiences halfway through.

First Encounter (step 2) was the most comfortable page for me — all analogies, no equations, and "the bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" made MRH almost make sense (though the full name "Markov Relevancy Horizon" is scary for no reason I can see). The Core Idea page then showed the actual equation, and honestly the "dimmer switch from sparse to dense" framing meant I didn't bounce. I can't read the equation, but I believed I knew what it *does*, which is all I wanted.

Then I went to the Tools page and played with three things (below). The Coherence Explorer sliders were fun for about a minute — I could see the S-curve get steeper — but the "Key Values" readout meant nothing to me. The Consciousness Demo was the page I clicked fastest (consciousness! a number!) and it's also where the site most aggressively talked me out of believing it: eight methods "converge" on 0.50 and then bold text explains the convergence is basically an artifact of picking the middle of a 0-to-1 scale. Weirdly deflating but also… respectable?

The Galaxy Plotter confused me most. Five curves, and two of them are *both* labeled as Synchronism — one that fails (the real one) and one amber "hand-tuned stand-in formerly shown" that fits. I get that they're being honest about a past mistake, but as a newcomer I couldn't tell which curve I was supposed to be judging. Also, the page never really explains what a rotation curve *is* — I know from YouTube that outer stars orbit too fast, but only because of YouTube.

Ended at Honest Assessment and the Glossary. The one-paragraph no-jargon summary of what failed is exactly what I needed, and I wish it appeared on more pages. The glossary is alphabetical only, no search — 72 terms is a lot of scrolling on a phone.

### Unanswered Questions
1. If the theory failed all its decisive tests, what am I being invited to *do* here — learn from the failure, or is there still a live idea somewhere?
2. What actually is "coherence" measuring in real units? Could someone measure the coherence of, say, water?
3. Why does the site keep saying its "coherence" is the opposite of quantum coherence — why not just rename it?
4. Is MOND a real mainstream thing or another alternative theory? The site assumes I know its status.
5. What's the amber curve on the galaxy plotter doing there if it's admitted to be hand-tuned and wrong?

### Verdict
This is the most honest science site I've ever stumbled onto, and that honesty is a genuinely compelling hook — I stayed far longer than I expected for a theory that admits it failed. But the beginner funnel leaks: nearly every beginner-labeled page eventually shifts into physicist vocabulary, and the interactive tools assume I know what I'm looking at. I'd come back if a friend asked "what's that failed theory-of-everything site?" — I'm not sure I'd come back on my own.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /tools, /glossary, /honest-assessment, /gamma-calculator, /equation-walkthrough, /learning-paths, /test-catalog, /key-claims, /coherence-function)
- **Understanding achieved**: good
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The information architecture is genuinely strong — breadcrumbs, learning paths, and a cross-linked glossary all work — but the terminology layer is buckling under its own audit history: one symbol has three names, one label vocabulary has ~28 entries in two "canonical" homes, and the site's headline metric ("refutation") isn't even a badge.

### Journey

I audited methodically: landing page first for the nav skeleton, then the reference pages (glossary, honest assessment, test catalog), then two tools, then spot-checks for terminology drift across content pages.

**Navigation: mostly excellent.** Every page I visited has a working breadcrumb trail with a sensible section hierarchy (Home / Interactive Tools / γ Calculator; Home / Core Theory / The Coherence Function; Home / Predictions & Experiments / Test Roadmap). Content pages show path context ("Intermediate Path — Step 1 of 8" and "Physics Track — Step 1 of 7" on /coherence-function — nice touch that one page can show membership in two paths). The Learning Paths hub offers 7 paths (3 by difficulty, 4 by topic) with step counts, time estimates, and an honest note that "topic tracks are entry points, not complete coverage." Two gaps: no progress tracking, and no completion guidance — when you finish a path, nothing tells you where to go next. One oddity: the Honest Assessment page's next-step footer includes a link to *Honest Assessment* — the page links to itself.

**Terminology: this is where the site struggles, and in an unusual way.** Most sites have too few definitions; this one has too many *competing* ones, because every audit correction has left a stratum of vocabulary behind.

The clearest case is ρ_crit. I found three different glosses on four pages: "saturation knee (reference density; not a critical point)" (landing, /coherence-function), "A characteristic scale parameter for the system" (/equation-walkthrough), and no definition at all (/key-claims, where it first appears inside a formula). Meanwhile the symbol itself is still spelled ρ_**crit** — the subscript says "critical" while three pages insist it is *not* a critical point. As a writer I can't rename physics notation, but when your documentation has to repeatedly disclaim its own variable name, the standard fix is one canonical definition sentence, reused verbatim everywhere the symbol first appears.

The badge system has the same stratification problem at larger scale. The Honest Assessment page (the stated canonical reference) defines 5 MRH-relationship tags, 4 descriptive tags, and 2 deprecated tags — reasonable — but then lists roughly **17 more "operational states"** (EXECUTED, ADJUDICATED, CLOSED, RETIRED, RECLASSIFIED, NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED, UNDERPOWERED AS REGISTERED, Ceiling Exceeded, Self-Eliminating-or-Tie, Null-Class, Artifact Lesson…). That's ~28 status terms a reader must hold. Worse, the vocabulary in actual use doesn't match the taxonomy: the test catalog says "environment prediction REFUTED" in body text while the badge list only contains "Failed," and the landing page's headline metric is "6 refutations executed" — "refutation" itself is not a defined badge anywhere. Failed vs. refuted vs. kill-criterion-triggered read as three spellings of one concept, and the reader has no way to know if they're distinct.

Badge definitions also live in **two** places billed as authoritative: the glossary (all badge families have entries) and /honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions ("canonical reference"). Duplicated reference content drifts; one should be canonical and the other should transclude or link.

**Acronyms: bimodal.** Core framework terms are handled well — C, γ, tanh, MRH, MOND, IIT, ΛCDM all get first-use expansions on the pages I checked. But astronomy/data vocabulary consistently isn't: SPARC (used 6+ times on the landing page), RAR, BTFR, fσ₈, σ₈, EFE, CMB, ALFALFA-SDSS, AQUAL, DFS, CFD all appear undefined at first use somewhere. The frustrating part: **the glossary already has good entries for SPARC, RAR, and BTFR** — the definitions exist, the first-use links to them don't. EFE is the worst offender: used repeatedly on /honest-assessment and /key-claims, never expanded on either.

**Test catalog:** TEST-01 through TEST-26 in a flat namespace across tiers, but the numbering has a documented scar: "The Cassini squeeze was renumbered 11 → 25 to clear the collision" — meaning TEST-11 has referred to two unrelated experiments. Renumbering an identifier that external readers may have cited is an anti-pattern; the note explaining it is good, but a permanent alias table ("TEST-11 (pre-2026-XX) = TEST-25") would be better. Individual test anchors resolve to a *different* page (/tier-1-existing#TEST-04a) than the catalog that lists them, which surprised me twice.

**The heading "24 Proposed Experiments"** sits atop a catalog of 26 tests, many with executed verdicts. Both the count and the word "Proposed" look stale.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (/gamma-calculator) — worked — confusing structure, clear intent — The four numbered caveats *before* the tool are admirably honest, but the tool's own output labels then depend on them ("a label per the inverted formula (Caveat 2)"), which breaks for anyone who scrolled straight to the widget. ### Unanswered Questions
1. Which is canonical for badge definitions — the glossary or /honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions — and which one gets updated first when the vocabulary changes?
2. Is "refutation" a formal status distinct from "Failed," or an informal synonym? The site counts one and badges the other.
3. Why is the symbol still ρ_crit if three pages insist it's not a critical point — is renaming (e.g., ρ₀) off the table for compatibility reasons?
4. What does a reader cite if they referenced TEST-11 before the renumbering?
5. Is there a style guide behind this site? Many pages read as if different authors (or different months of one author) wrote them, and the audit-trail annotations ("this read '4' until 2026-08-09") suggest content is versioned — but the terminology isn't.

### Verdict
Structurally, this is one of the better-organized science sites I've audited: real breadcrumbs, real learning paths, a genuinely cross-linked glossary, and tools with guided tasks. But the site's greatest editorial virtue — every correction leaves a visible trace — has become its greatest terminology liability: statuses, symbols, and counts accumulate strata instead of being consolidated, so the reader is asked to learn not just the framework but the history of its vocabulary. One editorial pass that picks a single canonical term for each concept (and demotes the rest to footnotes) would raise comprehension more than any new content.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /coherence-function, /mond-unification, /key-claims, /tools, /phase-boundary-visualizer, /chemistry-correlation-explorer, /honest-assessment, /dark-energy, /galaxy-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — though as a case study in adversarial self-audit, not as live physics
- **Overall impression**: The framework is a reparametrization of MOND + ΛCDM, and the site knows it — it prosecutes its own theory more thoroughly than most referees would — but a few statistics on the headline ledger outrun the analyses behind them, and the single sharpest reparametrization identity is stated on the *wrong* page.

### Journey

A colleague sent me this as "an interesting AI project." My prior for one-equation-across-all-scales frameworks is roughly zero, so I skipped the beginner material and went straight for /parameter-derivations, expecting the usual: a mean-field hand-wave for tanh, a fitted constant dressed as a derivation. What I found instead was a page that opens by announcing it is *not* a derivations page, retitles itself "Parameter Calibration & Honest Ansätze," and then dismantles its own parameter chain: the tanh is "chosen," not derived (explicitly contrasted with the Ising m = tanh(βJzm) self-consistency loop — a distinction most pop-physics sites never make); γ = 2/√N_corr is flagged as *sign-inverted* against real condensed matter (more correlation should sharpen transitions, their formula flattens them — correct, and they even note invoking the CLT on *correlated* DOF is self-contradictory); the constant A fails its own stated formula by 600×; and φ is labeled "fitted-then-named." Bottom line quoted on the page: "zero parameters in this chain have independent first-principles derivations." I have never seen a framework site write that sentence about itself.

I spot-checked the math I could re-derive:

- **Knee value**: C(ρ_crit, γ=2) = tanh(2 ln 2) = (4 − ¼)/(4 + ¼) ≈ 0.882. Site says 0.88 and correctly insists ρ_crit is a saturation knee, not a critical point. ✓
- **Compander-not-order-parameter claim**: tanh(γ ln(1+x)) on x ≥ 0 is analytic with no non-analyticity, no diverging correlation length, no universality class. Their refusal of Landau language is correct. ✓
- **Dark-energy asymptotics** (/dark-energy): from ρ_DE ≡ ρ_m(1−C)/C, deep past gives 1−C ≈ 2(ρ/ρ_crit)^(−2γ) so ρ_DE ∝ a^(−3(1−2γ)) ⇒ w = −2γ; far future C ≈ γρ/ρ_crit gives ρ_DE → ρ_crit/γ = const ⇒ w = −1. Both stated limits check out. ✓
- **The γ = ½ identity**: tanh(½ ln(1+x)) = x/(x+2) exactly. With ρ_crit = ρ_Λ/2 this is *identically* Ω_m(z), so H² = 8πGρ_m/3C reproduces flat ΛCDM with no approximation — and the /dark-energy page states this, alongside the galaxy-sector fit γ = 0.489, i.e. 2% from the identity point. Their phrase "a theory whose best fit is its own disappearance" is the most honest one-line summary of a null result I've read. ✓

But that same identity is where the site's page-level organization fails a physicist. x/(x+2) is MOND's *simple interpolation function* with a rescaled argument — so at the fitted γ the galaxy sector isn't "MOND-like," it is algebraically μ-simple. The /mond-unification page — whose entire job is the MOND relationship — never states this. Worse, it claims Synchronism is "distinct from, not nested in MOND" (via emergent a₀ and EFE = 0), while /honest-assessment says "everything that distinguishes this framework from MOND is a single asserted constant" and the RAR free-γ fit "converges to γ ≈ 0.489, identical to MOND." Those pages describe different relationships to MOND, and the sharpest statement lives on the dark-energy page where a MOND-curious reader will never look.

The refutation ledger (/honest-assessment) is the best failure accounting I've seen on a public site — it distinguishes 6 executed refutations from 3–4 independent roots, and it *removes* two tests from the count (TEST-04a underpowered as registered; TEST-03 never run as registered), which is over-refutation hygiene most published critiques don't practice. But one entry bothered me: the DESI dark-energy exclusion is quoted as "3.4–6.3σ across four data combinations." I chased it to /dark-energy, which discloses the number is "sign-and-scale statement only — no covariance is claimed... quadrant-level in CPL space, not a fit." A σ without a likelihood is not a σ. And the family *includes ΛCDM as an interior point* (γ = ½), so a proper direct fit cannot disfavor it by more than ΛCDM itself is disfavored — DESI DR2's own preference for w₀wₐ over ΛCDM is ~2.8–4.2σ depending on combination. The sector page half-knows this ("parameterization-projection biases remain unresolved"; verdict softened to "disfavoured at quadrant level"); the ledger still carries the raw 3.4–6.3σ. The number needs the caveat attached wherever it appears, or better, a direct-fit Δχ² replacing it.

One more thing the ledger states but the claims table doesn't absorb: the BTFR kill (TEST-09, the framework's sharpest framework-specific refutation at 3.3σ) rests on the boost ceiling B_max = 1/Ω_m, which /parameter-derivations classifies as "asserted, not derived." So the cleanest kill shot ran on an underived constant. The site says this in one place ("two of the six refutations test it") but /key-claims still presents "framework predicts 3.35 ± 0.07" as if it were a derived number with an honest error bar.

The most consequential caveat on the whole site is stated almost in passing on /coherence-function: "every quantitative galaxy result on this site… was computed with the argument g_bar, not ρ," and substituting ρ as the equation is actually written "moves the predicted rotation velocity by 2–5 orders of magnitude." As a physicist: that means the galaxy-sector results are results about a *different model* than the equation on the masthead. This should be a banner, not a footnote.

### Tools Tested
- **Phase Boundary Visualizer** (/phase-boundary-visualizer) — worked — intuitive, and physically *correct about being incorrect* — the tool animates systems along the γ axis and explicitly labels the γ = 2/√N_corr map "audited-negative: it predicts the opposite of real condensed-matter behavior." As a condensed-matter person I confirm the inversion is real: BCS and BEC transitions are among the sharpest in nature, and the formula hands them the flattest curves (N_corr = 10⁷ ⇒ γ ≈ 6×10⁻⁴). Displaying refuted physics *as* refuted physics, with "treat the positions as the formula's claims, not as data," is good pedagogy. My one quibble: N_corr values are admitted to be "asserted, not counted," which makes even the failure quantitatively soft.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (/chemistry-correlation-explorer) — worked — methodologically the strongest tool on the site — it shows r = 0.98 (sound velocity), 0.967 (bulk modulus), etc., and then *builds the null model in*: a plain 2-parameter polynomial in Z "matches these correlations (|Δr| ≤ 0.07) and sometimes beats them," because the targets are near-monotonic in Z. That is exactly the control a referee would demand, and it's presented before the reader can be impressed. It also discloses the sound-velocity *sign* problem (positive r where the framework's own γ bookkeeping wants negative). Correct physics, correct statistics, correct conclusion (density-monotonicity, not new physics).
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — worked — evaluated for physics fidelity — the violet "real equation" curve honestly fails (disk densities sit far below ρ_crit), and the caveat list is remarkable (spherical-vs-Bessel disk approximation inflating the visual gap ~15–20%, V_flat inherited from MOND fits meaning flatness "by construction," per-galaxy ρ_crit refit vs MOND's global a₀). The one physics-representation flaw is the MOND reference curve itself — see friction log.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Has anyone run the *direct fit* of the w(z) family against DESI DR2 (the page's 2026-08-12 "independent re-verification" hints at one)? If the answer is "ΛCDM-degenerate at fitted γ," the honest ledger number is ~0σ against the family and the 3.4–6.3σ applies only to forced-γ slices — which one does the count of 6 rely on?
2. What reconciles ρ_crit = 0.029·V_flat² (galaxies) with ρ_crit = ρ_Λ/2 (cosmology)? If nothing does, the "single equation" claim should be softened to "single functional form."
3. Is there a Lagrangian? The 2026-08-11 note that the Friedmann substitution violates the Bianchi identity implies the background model isn't a covariant theory at all — what, then, is the object being tested?
4. Where does 3.35 ± 0.07 (BTFR) actually come from, step by step, given B_max is asserted?
5. Why does the site not state anywhere prominent that the fitted galaxy sector is *algebraically* μ-simple MOND? It states the ΛCDM twin on the cosmology side; the MOND twin deserves equal billing.

### Verdict
As physics, this framework is what I suspected from the URL: a chosen sigmoid with per-sector recalibrated parameters that, when fit freely, collapses onto MOND on galaxy scales and ΛCDM on cosmological scales — a reparametrization with zero derived parameters, and the site *says so itself*, in more damning detail than I could have assembled in an afternoon. That inversion — the site as prosecution rather than defense — is genuinely novel and worth a colleague's hour. The residual failures are failures of placement, not honesty: the strongest identities and caveats (μ-simple equivalence, the g_bar-vs-ρ substitution, the no-covariance disclaimer on the DESI σ) live on the wrong pages or below the fold, so the headline ledger occasionally claims more precision than its own source pages defend.

### Summary

### Journey

I came for the question every "AI-generated physics" story raises: is there a single prediction here that a referee would call new? I skipped the pedagogy entirely and read the site the way I'd review a paper — claims ledger first, methodology second, tools last, checking everything against the current state of the art I work in.

**The novelty hunt (key question 1).** The landing page answers it in its own hero copy: 0 confirmed predictions, and the one "untested" item — a₀ ~ cH₀ — is correctly *credited away*: "The relation is not this framework's to score in either direction: it is Milgrom's own 1983 coincidence," with McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017, and Smolin 2017 listed as parallel appearances. That's the attribution I'd have demanded in review, already done. /for-researchers is even blunter: "the framework's galaxy sector is a strict submodel of MOND… so a discriminating test can only tie or lose." The four artifacts it offers as citable — the local-density no-go instance, the A2ACW program null, the DESI growth negative, the dim-4 LIV naturalness gap — are all negative results, and the page says so. I verified the no-go's shape against what I know of Milgrom's 2005 obstruction: the site's contribution is a quantified volumetric-density instance on SPARC, correctly scoped ("first for the algebraic-coupling class," not first conceptually). Modest, honest, citable.

**Does anything differ from MOND + ΛCDM (key question 2)?** Structurally, exactly one thing: EFE = 0. MOND's external field effect is its signature violation of the strong equivalence principle; this framework's local-ρ coupling predicts no EFE. But /tier-1-existing discloses that registration is blocked — "the two C conventions… give EFE of *opposite sign*, so there is no single prediction to register until the force law is fixed" — and there's a second problem the page does *not* state where it presents EFE as "the only theoretically discriminating prediction": EFE = 0 is just the strong equivalence principle, which ΛCDM + standard gravity already satisfies. Detecting no EFE confirms Newton, not Synchronism; detecting an EFE (as Chae claims) kills both. So even the framework's one structural difference from MOND is refutation-only — it can never *win*. Elsewhere: TEST-26 (DESI DR3) is explicitly "kill-or-tie"; the consciousness and quantum sectors are "unrunnable as stated" (no protocol maps any measurable onto C — the site's own words); and the wide-binary test is dead on arrival ("no outcome that favors Synchronism"). The answer to question 2 is no, and the site knew before I did.

**Where the site is state-of-the-art current.** I was braced for a stale or one-sided Gaia DR3 treatment and found neither: TEST-02 cites both sides of the live dispute correctly ("Chae (2023–2025) claims a MOND-like ~1.4× boost… Banik et al. (2024) claims Newtonian behavior excludes that boost at high significance, from the same data with different cuts") and then computes that its own predicted signal (0.05–0.4% velocity deviation) sits ~80× below the systematics floor — which is the right reason to call the test non-discriminating regardless of who wins the dispute. Likewise TEST-25 credits its prior art by name and number: "this result is, to within the choice of interpolating-function family, Desmond, Hees & Famaey (2024), MNRAS 530, 1781" at 8.7σ, with the site's +17.95σ explained as un-marginalized. That explanation cuts the wrong way, though: the marginalized 8.7σ is the *defensible* number, and the ledger headline should carry it, not the inflated one.

**The DESI sector (my closest expertise).** The sign-lock argument is clean: w runs from −2γ (past) to −1 (future) monotonically, forcing sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ) — and DESI DR2 (arXiv:2503.14738, correctly cited) prefers exactly the forbidden quadrant (w₀ > −1, wₐ < 0). The covariant-completion analysis is genuinely sophisticated for a public site: completion A collapses to Einstein–de Sitter ("no FRW solutions exist beyond a ≈ 1.04"), completion B breaks the lock but "0 of 192 γ values reach the DESI quadrant." But I caught two defects. First, **the headline number disagrees with itself across pages**: /honest-assessment quotes "3.4–6.3σ on DESI's own σwₐ" while /dark-energy quotes "3.4–5.4σ across the combinations." One of these is stale. Second, /dark-energy says the sector was "independently re-verified in the research repo" on 2026-08-12 but reports *no quantitative outcome* — no fitted γ, no Δχ² against ΛCDM. Since the family contains ΛCDM as an interior point (γ = ½ exactly), a direct fit is bounded by DESI's own ΛCDM tension (~2.8–4.2σ depending on combination) and most plausibly lands ΛCDM-degenerate — which would make the honest family-level number ~0σ and confine the 3.4–x σ range to forced-γ slices. Whatever the re-verification found, it is the single most decision-relevant number for this sector and it is not on the page.

**Is A2ACW sound (key question 3)?** As a discovery engine: no, and the page proves it against itself — specificity 0/6 (all six genuine discoveries in the control set false-flagged as reparametrizations), "Youden's J = 0 under either labeling, which is the formal statement that this detector carries no information." As a *null result about AI self-play*: nearly, but not yet. The controls are retrospective ("'Controlled' in the experimental-design sense would overstate it" — their words), n=6 gives a binomial CI wide enough to drive a truck through, and the one experiment that would break the central confound — cross-vendor corpus control, separating "AI can't detect novelty" from "this corpus contains none" — is registered but unrun. The page's own bottom line, "a retrieval aid, not a detector," is the correct one. What *is* sound is the practice: role rotation with a mandated challenger quota and sycophancy monitoring is a reasonable assembly of Irving et al. 2018 debate ideas, and the site claims only the assembly, not the components. My referee report on the methodology would be: publishable as a cautionary null once the cross-vendor arm runs; not before.

### Tools Tested
- **Consciousness Threshold Demo** (/consciousness-demo) — worked — evaluated for scientific accuracy — The self-debunk is *correct*: "The convergence of 8 approaches on 0.499 ± 0.012 is geometric, not empirical," and the follow-through — "the scatter is not just uninformative — it is too small to be honest," a "Millikan-style clustering signature" — is exactly the right diagnosis (estimators inheriting each other's assumptions produce under-dispersed agreement; any of the eight methods honestly estimated would scatter more). The PCI citation checks out against the literature (Casarotto et al. 2016, PCI* = 0.31, ~95% accuracy across wake/sleep/anesthesia/DOC), and using PCI as the calibration bar the framework *hasn't met* is the honest move. Two gaps: no decoherence-program connection anywhere on the page, and no acknowledgment that this C is anti-correlated with quantum coherence — see friction log.
- **Coherence Explorer** (/coherence-explorer) — worked — mathematically verified — I spot-checked the Key Values readout at γ = 0.49: tanh(0.49·ln 2) = 0.327, tanh(0.49·ln 11) = 0.826, tanh(0.49·ln 101) = 0.978 — all three displayed values correct. The tool also correctly discloses the "+1" regulator's asymmetry ("ρcrit is not the curve's midpoint… C = 0.50 sits at ρ ≈ 2.07·ρcrit") and labels the BCS/BEC placements as assertions under a formula it calls "structurally inverted relative to real condensed-matter physics." What it omits is the measurability gap — the sliders manipulate a quantity nothing can currently measure, and the tool doesn't say so.

### Unanswered Questions
1. What did the 2026-08-12 re-verification actually find? If the family direct-fits ΛCDM-degenerate, the honest DESI verdict is "no discriminating power," not "disfavoured at quadrant level" — those are different claims and the site currently straddles them.
2. Which DESI number is current — 5.4σ or 6.3σ — and what changed between the two pages?
3. Is the cross-vendor A2ACW control (the one registered experiment that would make the program null citable) scheduled, and on what corpus? The methodology's publishability gates entirely on it.
4. Has anyone attempted the one theoretical repair that would make the framework non-nested — a differential (gradient) coupling in place of the algebraic C(ρ), which the site's own Burrage–Copeland–Millington counterexample note points at? That's the only direction I can see where this stops being a submodel.
5. Why does the ledger headline keep the un-marginalized +17.95σ when the site itself identifies the marginalized 8.7σ as the published, defensible figure?

### Verdict
There is no new physics here, and the site is the rare venue that states this more precisely than its reviewer could: a strict MOND submodel on galaxy scales, a ΛCDM-containing family on cosmological scales, and unrunnable sectors everywhere else. What *is* new — and worth citing — is the artifact: ~3,300 adversarial AI sessions that converged on internal consistency rather than discovery, documented with retrospective controls honest enough to conclude the detector "carries no information." I'd send colleagues here as the best existing case study of what AI self-play can and cannot do in physics, with two reservations: the headline DESI number needs to stop disagreeing with itself across pages, and the missing direct-fit result is the difference between "disfavoured" and "degenerate" — a distinction this site, of all sites, should be the one to make cleanly.
