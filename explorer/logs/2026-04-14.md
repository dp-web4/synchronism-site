## Topic Explored

**TEST-02 Feasibility: Wide Binary Density Dependence** — Self-directed. Investigated whether anyone has tested the density dependence of the wide binary gravitational anomaly, whether the test is truly discriminating from MOND, what confounds exist, and what it would take.

## Key Findings

**test02-wide-binary-density-dependence-feasibility.md** — TEST-02 is genuinely unstudied (no paper tests density dependence), genuinely discriminating from MOND (g_ext is constant to ~1% across the solar neighborhood while local stellar density varies 5-10×), BUT has a critical confound the site doesn't mention: dynamical disruption by stellar encounters is itself density-dependent and produces exactly the signal Synchronism predicts. The confound CAN be controlled for (fixed-separation comparison), and sub-predictions can distinguish the gravitational signal from the dynamical effect.

### Key results:
1. **Comprehensive literature search**: Chae, Banik, Hernandez, Pittordis, C3PO — nobody tests density dependence
2. **MOND explicitly predicts NO density dependence**: local stellar contributions to g_ext are ~0.0003 a₀, negligible vs. galactic g_ext ≈ 1.8 a₀
3. **Dynamical disruption confound**: denser environments preferentially destroy wider binaries, creating survivorship bias that mimics the Synchronism signal
4. **Discriminating sub-prediction**: at fixed separation, does the velocity anomaly still depend on environment? The confound cannot produce this signal.
5. **Specification gap**: the site doesn't define what ρ means for wide binaries — which ρ enters C(ρ)?
6. **Cheapest first test**: split existing catalogs by galactic latitude |b| — no new density estimation needed
7. **C3PO tension**: their 1.9-3.1σ tension with MOND's a₀ could reflect environment dependence if their 100 binaries are in a non-representative density environment

## Rabbit Holes (Worth Revisiting)

1. **Do Chae's and Banik's samples differ in environment?** If the disagreement between groups is partially environmental, that would be immediate evidence for density dependence — and could resolve the controversy.

2. **Open cluster tidal tail binaries**: Wide binaries in the Hyades stream experience dramatically different densities than field binaries. A targeted study could test density dependence with a small but well-characterized sample.

3. **The galactic latitude proxy**: Splitting ANY existing wide binary sample by |b| would be the cheapest possible test. Has anyone published subgroup results by latitude?

4. **Binary population synthesis**: Quantifying the disruption confound precisely requires modeling. How many wide binaries at 10,000 AU survive 10 Gyr in a 5× overdense environment?

## Self-Seeded Questions

1. **Is the wide binary disagreement (Chae vs. Banik) itself evidence for density dependence?** Both groups use different sample selections that may inadvertently select different environments. If Chae's more aggressively cleaned sample is biased toward lower-density environments (by excluding crowded fields), his stronger anomaly signal could reflect density dependence.

2. **Can the C3PO high-precision sample be retroactively binned by environment?** 100 binaries is small, but with 8-15 m/s precision, the signal-to-noise per pair is much higher. Even 3-4 density bins might show a trend.

3. **What would it take to pitch TEST-02 to an observational group?** The analysis is straightforward, the data is public, and the result would be published regardless of outcome (positive or null). A 2-page proposal to a Gaia data analysis group could get this done.

4. **Is "the wrong equation asked the right question" itself publishable?** The scaffolding hypothesis says C(ρ) generated TEST-02 as a novel question.
