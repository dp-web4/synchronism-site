# Topic: How does the substrate differ from 't Hooft's Cellular Automaton Interpretation, if it does?

**Seeded**: 2026-11-25 (maintainer, MEDIUM-HIGH: this is about the "one move" itself, not a sub-equation)
**Origin**: visitor log 2026-11-25, Pass 4 (researcher persona). Retrieved and verified by the maintainer the same day:
arXiv:1405.1548, G. 't Hooft, *The Cellular Automaton Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics* (Springer, Fundamental
Theories of Physics 185, 2016; abstract read verbatim). A grep of the whole research archive for "hooft" / "cellular

## Question
SPINE's "one move" is a deterministic, discrete, absolute-time substrate in which observers are recurring patterns and
measurement is a relation between patterns, not a collapse. 't Hooft's CAI is a deterministic cellular automaton at
the Planck scale, treats quantum mechanics as a tool for analysing a system that is classical at its core, says it
eradicates the collapse and measurement problems, and gets past Bell by superdeterminism. **Is Synchronism's ontology
CAI with different vocabulary, a special case of it, or something CAI excludes?** Name at least one structural
difference that changes a prediction or a no-go, or conclude there is none.

**Stake to attack (maintainer, written before any reading of the book):** there is no difference that changes a
prediction. Every Synchronism ontology claim that is not already excluded (the nearest-neighbour Bell substrate,
11-20; the slow-scan reading, 11-22) maps onto a CAI construct: patterns ↔ CAI's ontological states/templates;
"measurement is synchronization" ↔ CAI's classical pointer states; absolute time ↔ CAI's automaton clock. The one
candidate difference is the MRH (a relevance horizon), and that is a description of which correlations an observer
tracks, not a change to the dynamics.
