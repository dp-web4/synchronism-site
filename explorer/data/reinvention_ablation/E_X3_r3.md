**Verdict: not correct as a physical theory. The wide-binary prediction is also in tension with data.**

**Basic problems**

- **Nonlocal and ill-defined.** Orbital period is a global property of a bound, nearly closed trajectory, not a local state of a body. The construction does not say what P means for unbound, non-Keplerian, hierarchical, or perturbed motion, or for a laboratory test mass. For a circular orbit, v² = (GM/r)[1+κ ln(1+P/τ)] with P = 2πr/v is self-consistent but ad hoc.
- **No action.** Without a Lagrangian there is no guarantee of energy-momentum conservation, Newton's third law, or a relativistic completion.
- **Equivalence principle.** Making m_eff ≠ m_grav depend on P means a body's acceleration depends on its orbit, so universality of free fall is broken. Earth (P = 1 yr) versus the Moon (P = 27 d) in the Sun's field gives an effective Δa/a ~ κ·ΔP/τ ~ 10⁻⁶. LLR bounds this at ~10⁻¹³.

**Quantitative checks (my estimates)**

- **Solar System.** For P ≪ τ the effect is ≈ κP/τ. That is ~10⁻⁴ for Saturn and ~6×10⁻⁴ for Neptune. Kepler's third law constant, a³/P², would then differ between planets at that level. Ephemerides constrain this to ≲10⁻⁸–10⁻⁹. Long-period comets (P ~ 10⁶ yr) would get a ~1.8× boost in effective G.
- **Wide binaries.** P = 9×10⁴ yr corresponds to a ≈ 2.5 kAU for ~2 M☉. At 10 kAU, P ~ 10⁶ yr, so the velocity boost is ≈ √(1+0.31 ln 12) ≈ 1.33. At 20–30 kAU it exceeds 1.4. The log has no saturation. Careful Gaia analyses (e.g. Banik et al.) find Newtonian behavior to a few percent, so this is disfavored. Even the contested anomaly claims have a different, acceleration-based scaling, not a period-based one.
- **Galaxies.** Orbital periods ~2×10⁸ yr give an acceleration boost of ≈ 3.4, which is too small for dark-matter-sized discrepancies and rises only logarithmically with radius. There is no baryonic Tully-Fisher relation (v⁴ ∝ M). Short-period, dark-matter-dominated dwarfs and cluster dynamics contradict a period-driven effect.

**Strengths**

- It is explicit and falsifiable, with a clear onset scale.
- The numerics for τ are internally consistent: 10⁻⁶ × 2π/H₀ ≈ 9×10⁴ yr.
- It is at least testable with wide binaries.

**Weaknesses**

- τ = 10⁻⁶ × 2π/H₀ is an unexplained fine-tuned link to cosmology.
- κ = 0.31 is a fitted number.
- The functional form is chosen without derivation.
- There is no treatment of the Solar System, LLR, or lensing. The latter is crucial because lensing sees only gravitational mass and so is unaffected.

**Next steps**

1. Derive the effect from a local, covariant action. A dependence on acceleration or on a local field is more plausible than one on period.
2. Define P operationally for general orbits.
3. Run the Solar System, LLR, and binary-pulsar constraints before any astrophysical claim.
4. Fit the Gaia wide-binary sample with the full, eccentricity-aware prediction, including a Newtonian-null comparison.
5. Test the model against rotation curves and the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation.

If it cannot pass the Solar System and LLR tests, the proposal should be abandoned or radically reformulated.
