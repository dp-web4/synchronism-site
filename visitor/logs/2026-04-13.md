## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: Surprisingly honest and inviting at first, but the site loses casual readers fast once you leave the landing page — equations appear without enough plain-English scaffolding, and the interactive tools I expected to find were either missing or too technical to enjoy.

### Journey
Landed on the homepage and immediately liked the vibe. "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — that's a great hook. The site tells me upfront that some predictions work and some fail, which feels refreshingly honest compared to the usual breathless "we solved everything" tone I see on Reddit. I clicked "Start Exploring" expecting a gentle on-ramp.

**First Encounter** (/first-encounter) was okay but short. It introduced "presence" as basically how much stuff is in a system, which I could follow. But then "Markov Relevancy Horizon" appeared and I had no idea what that meant. No definition on the page itself.

**The Core Idea** (/core-idea) is where things got rocky. The equation C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) appeared, and while the "dimmer switch" analogy for tanh was genuinely helpful, everything else was too dense. What's γ? What's ρcrit? The page tried to explain but used more jargon to do it. I felt like I needed a glossary open in another tab.

**Glossary** (/glossary) — Checked this next. The definitions for basic terms like "coherence" and "phase transition" were actually pretty good. The site says terms are highlighted with hover definitions on other pages, which is a nice idea. The glossary itself has no search or filter though.

**Why Synchronism** (/why-synchronism) — Tried to understand the motivation. The "physics is fragmented" argument made sense. But it jumped to math too quickly again.

**Galaxy Rotation** (/galaxy-rotation) — Clicked this because I know dark matter is a big deal. The opening was clear: stars orbit faster than they should. But then "baryonic mass," "RAR," and "dex" showed up without definitions. The page mentioned a "Galaxy Curve Plotter" tool but I couldn't find it — the link led nowhere (404).

**Honest Assessment** (/honest-assessment) — This was actually my favorite page. Seeing "0 confirmed predictions" right up front was wild. The melting points failure (53% error) and the superconductor prediction being 6.5× wrong were specific and memorable. This page made me trust the site more than any amount of success stories would have.

**Consciousness** (/hard-problem) — Clicked because I've watched YouTube videos about the hard problem. The idea that consciousness IS certain physical patterns rather than being caused by them was interesting. But how you'd actually test that remained vague. "34 falsifiable predictions, none yet tested" — okay, so it's all theoretical.

**Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (/chemistry-correlation-explorer) — Found this from the gamma-boundary page. It's a sortable table with filter tabs. I could click "Coherence helps" vs "Incoherence helps" categories. The sorting worked. But I didn't really understand what I was looking at — terms like "Piezoelectricity d₃₃" and correlation values (r = 0.982) meant nothing to me without context. No tooltips, no "what am I seeing?" guide.

**Gamma Boundary** (/gamma-boundary) — The "89% validated" claim drew me in, but then the page itself says that number might be inflated. I appreciated the honesty but it also felt like: why put 89% on the homepage if you're going to immediately undermine it?

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Journey
I approached this as a documentation audit, starting at the landing page and methodically checking navigation structure, terminology consistency, acronym handling, validation badge usage, and interactive tool documentation across 12 pages.

The three-step pathway (Why → First Encounter → Core Idea) provides a clear on-ramp. However, "MRH" appears in a section card without definition. **First Encounter** (/first-encounter) — Breadcrumb present and accurate: Home / Getting Started / First Encounter. Step indicator shows "Step 1 of 7." Forward/backward nav works. "Markov Relevancy Horizon (MRH)" is expanded but not explained — the acronym is unpacked, but the concept remains opaque. The page positions itself as an entry point but frontloads abstraction before grounding examples.

**Why Synchronism** (/why-synchronism) — Good motivational structure: opens with "Physics has a fragmentation problem" before introducing the equation. Breadcrumb works. But MOND, SPARC, ALFALFA-SDSS, and YBCO all appear without definitions. The "reachability factor (η)" is introduced and immediately abandoned.

**Core Idea** (/core-idea) — The most critical page and the most problematic for documentation. Validation badges ("Validated") appear without linking to a legend. **Glossary** (/glossary) — 28 terms, organized by conceptual importance rather than alphabetically. No search, no filter, no jump links. But the legend is text-only — no visual samples showing how each badge actually appears on other pages. Inconsistent labeling: success cases use badges ("Strongly Supported") while failures use raw numbers ("53% Error," "6.5× Wrong"). Many acronyms undefined at first use: ALFALFA, SDSS, CDM, RAR, BAO, QFT, MRH, EEG, LOO, BTFR. No citation footnotes for any of the specific numbers cited.

**Research Philosophy** (/research-philosophy) — This is where the validation badge taxonomy lives, with all six categories clearly defined: Validated, Strongly Supported, Untested, Speculative, Reparametrization, Failed. This page is essential context for the entire site — but it's not in the main navigation. It's only accessible via the footer and occasional inline links. **Galaxy Rotation** (/galaxy-rotation) — Breadcrumb works (Home / Cosmology / Galaxy Rotation Curves). RAR is defined on first use, but MOND, SDSS, CDM are not. The Galaxy Curve Plotter link exists and points to /galaxy-plotter — contrary to Pass 1's finding, the link resolves.

**γ Boundary** (/gamma-boundary) — Uses validation badges correctly: green "Validated" on strong correlations, red "Failed" on weak ones. The "89% validated" claim appears prominently, with a caveat about template-based inflation buried lower on the page. Links to the Chemistry Correlation Explorer for drill-down.

**Hard Problem** (/hard-problem) — Breadcrumb works. Core variables (γ, D, S, C) are defined. "34 falsifiable predictions" links correctly to /consciousness-predictions. No validation badges on this page despite making testable claims. "EEG" undefined.

**Two Reframes** (/two-reframes) — Strong analogies (CRT sampling rate, pendulum clock). Positioned under "Quantum Physics" in breadcrumbs. But "intent field," "Tsirelson's bound," "Planck grid," and "CHSH bound" all appear without definitions. ## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 11
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes — to track whether they run any Tier 1 tests
- **Overall impression**: Unusually honest for a unified framework site — the intellectual hygiene is far above average — but the core equation is a motivated ansatz, not a derivation, and most "validated" results are reparametrizations of MOND or empirical correlations without independent predictive content.

### Journey
A colleague sent me this link saying "interesting AI project." I'm finishing a condensed matter PhD, so I went straight for the math.

**Landing Page** (/) — The hook question is fine. I immediately noticed "89% Validated" and "Strongly Supported" badges and wanted to know what those categories mean. Clicked through to parameter derivations.

**Parameter Derivations** (/parameter-derivations) — This is the page I cared about most. The core equation C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) is presented with γ = 2/√Ncorr. The page is commendably honest: it calls tanh a "motivated choice, not uniquely derived" and γ a "physically motivated ansatz." The Ising model motivation for tanh is correct — m = tanh(βJzm) is the standard mean-field result — but the self-consistency loop that makes the Ising equation meaningful is absent here. In mean-field theory, the tanh arises from the self-consistent equation m = tanh(βJzm) where m appears on both sides. Here it's imposed as a functional form, not derived from a self-consistency condition. The factor of 2 in γ is attributed to "phase-space dimensionality (6D→3D)" but the page admits this is "imperfect." The derivation of a₀ = cH₀/(2π) giving ~1.2×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² is a well-known numerological coincidence noted by Milgrom himself — it's not new to this framework. The 3–12% errors on derived constants could reflect legitimate approximation or implicit calibration through V_flat — the page correctly identifies this ambiguity.

**Core Idea** (/core-idea) — Simpler presentation of the same equation. The three regimes (γ ≪ 1, γ ≈ 1, γ ≫ 1) map to quantum/transition/classical. No Lagrangian, no action principle, no Hamiltonian. This is a phenomenological curve, not a theory in the physics sense. The page doesn't claim otherwise, which I appreciate.

**Galaxy Rotation** (/galaxy-rotation) — This is where I got skeptical. The RAR equation presented is gobs = gbar/(1 − e^{−√(gbar/a₀)}) — this is exactly the McGaugh et al. (2016) interpolation function. The page acknowledges this. Synchronism's claimed contribution is deriving a₀ from cH₀/(2π), but this dimensional-analysis coincidence predates Synchronism. The page then cites environment-dependent RAR scatter (R² = 0.14, p = 5×10⁻⁶) as the genuinely novel prediction. This is interesting — standard MOND doesn't predict environment-dependent scatter — but the page acknowledges the effect accounts for only 14% of scatter variance. I wanted to see the actual data and residual plots. None are shown.

**Galaxy Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — Five SPARC galaxies with observed, Newtonian, and "Synchronism" curves. The tool claims to use C(ρ) with γ = 2 (uncorrelated stars), not MOND directly. However, the output curves look identical to standard MOND fits. Without showing residuals or a direct comparison overlay with MOND, I can't tell whether the coherence model produces any different prediction. The tool notes curves are "simplified models for illustration" — so this isn't really testing the physics, it's illustrating a concept. No χ² or goodness-of-fit statistics. No adjustable parameters beyond galaxy selection. As a physics tool, it's decorative rather than functional.

**γ Boundary** (/gamma-boundary) — The "89% validated" claim across 1,703 chemistry phenomena. The page immediately caveat that template-based sessions may inflate this number. No p-values, no confidence intervals — only Pearson r values. At graduate level, reporting r without sample sizes, confidence intervals, or any discussion of multiple comparisons across 1,703 tests is a red flag. With 1,703 correlations tested, you'd expect ~85 false positives at p < 0.05 by chance alone. No correction for multiple comparisons is mentioned.

**Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — Sortable table of 23 phenomena correlated against γ. The top correlations (sound velocity r = 0.982, electronegativity r = 0.979) are impressive numbers, but I need to know: correlated against what, exactly? Against γ = 2/√Ncorr across elements? If so, what is Ncorr for each element? How many data points per correlation? A Pearson r of 0.98 with 5 data points means something very different from r = 0.98 with 50. The failures are interesting: Hall coefficient (r ≈ 0), magnetic susceptibility (r < 0.2), piezoelectricity "γ backward." These failures in magnetic/transport properties make physical sense — these depend on band structure details that a single-parameter model shouldn't capture.

**Key Claims** (/key-claims) — The strongest page on the site for a physicist. Three claims with honest assessments. The Bullet Cluster sign error is a genuine structural failure — the framework predicts dark matter should be more viscous than baryonic matter, but the Bullet Cluster shows the opposite. This isn't fixable by adjusting parameters. The consciousness equation C = f(γ, D, S) is interesting but the actual function f is never specified on the page. The quantum scanning model is novel but admits it cannot derive E(a,b) = −cos(a−b) from substrate geometry, and the Born rule derivation may be circular.

**Measurement Without Observers** (/measurement-without-observers) — The MRH replaces the observer with a dynamical boundary. The physical story is plausible: coupling to 10²³ apparatus particles drives Ncorr up, γ down, and the system crosses the MRH. But no density matrix formulation, no quantitative comparison with Zurek's decoherence timescales, no entanglement entropy calculations. The claim that "decoherence explains loss of interference but not definite outcomes" is a legitimate philosophical point (the preferred basis problem), but the MRH doesn't mathematically solve it either — it just renames the boundary where it happens.

**MRH** (/mrh) — The formal definition is operational: "the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence coherence evolution." This is essentially the Markov blanket from the Free Energy Principle dressed in coherence language. The page acknowledges the connection but claims MRH is "dynamical" while Markov blankets are "static graph properties." This is debatable — Friston's active inference framework already makes Markov blankets dynamical. No equations for computing MRH for any specific system. No worked examples.

**Two Reframes** (/two-reframes) — The CRT analogy for superposition (rapid temporal scanning through states) is a hidden-variable picture. The page acknowledges Bell's theorem constrains this and admits it can't derive the Born rule without circularity. The pendulum clock analogy for time dilation generates no new predictions — it's a restatement of GR. The substrate model for entanglement is pilot-wave-adjacent but claims "nonlocal field" rather than particle trajectories. No mathematical formalism.

**Honest Assessment** (/honest-assessment) — 0 confirmed unique predictions after 3,302 sessions. This is intellectually brave. The specific failures are well-documented: YBCO Tc off by 6.5×, melting points 53% error, fractal coherence bridge 0/7. The discovery rate of 1.4% is presented as "normal for scientific research," which is fair. The distinction between the one surviving candidate prediction (entity criterion Γ < m) and everything else is clear.

**Research Philosophy** (/research-philosophy) — The validation badge taxonomy is well-defined. "Reparametrization" is explicitly called out: "reproduces known physics in different notation." The epistemological stance (Box's "all models are wrong, some are useful") is appropriate for a framework at this stage. The criterion for distinguishing reparametrization from genuine prediction — "does reinterpretation suggest predictions that the original framework doesn't?" — is exactly the right question.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — working — inadequate for physics evaluation — Five SPARC galaxies with three curves (observed, Newtonian, Synchronism). The violet "Synchronism" curve tracks the data well, but without a MOND comparison overlay, it's impossible to assess whether this produces different predictions from standard MOND. No χ², no residuals, no adjustable parameters. Claims to use C(ρ) with γ = 2 rather than MOND, but the output is visually indistinguishable from standard MOND fits. The disclaimer "simplified models for illustration" undermines its value as a physics tool.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — working — statistically incomplete — Sortable by r-value with regime filters. The highest correlations (sound velocity 0.982, electronegativity 0.979) look impressive, but without sample sizes, confidence intervals, or any discussion of what γ actually equals for each element, these numbers are uninterpretable. The failures (Hall coefficient ~0, magnetic susceptibility <0.2) are physically sensible — these properties depend on Fermi surface topology that a single-parameter model can't capture — and showing them is honest. But the tool needs basic statistical metadata to be taken seriously.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Without the self-consistency condition, what distinguishes C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) from any sigmoid fit to a phase transition? The functional form is generic — you'd get similar curves from logistic, erf, or Fermi-Dirac functions. What makes tanh with these specific arguments physically meaningful rather than just a flexible fitting function?
2. The galaxy rotation results appear to reproduce MOND with extra conceptual apparatus. The environment-dependent scatter is the one novel prediction — has anyone actually checked whether standard MOND + baryonic feedback models also predict environment-dependent scatter? If they do, this isn't novel either.
3. How is Ncorr computed for a given physical system? Without a prescription for calculating Ncorr from first principles, γ = 2/√Ncorr is a free parameter in disguise. The framework needs either a first-principles derivation of Ncorr or an explicit admission that it's fitted per system.
4. The Bullet Cluster sign error seems fatal for the "dark matter as incomplete decoherence" interpretation. Does this failure propagate to the galaxy rotation domain, or are those claims independent?
5. With 1,703 chemistry correlations and no multiple-comparisons correction, what is the actual false discovery rate? The 89% validation rate is meaningless without this.

### Verdict
This is the most intellectually honest unified-framework site I've encountered — the 0 confirmed predictions, visible failures, and explicit reparametrization category show genuine scientific integrity. But as physics, the core equation is a phenomenological sigmoid with physically motivated but not derived parameters, and the strongest empirical results (galaxy rotation curves) are reparametrizations of MOND with one potentially novel prediction (environment-dependent scatter). The framework lacks the mathematical infrastructure of a real theory: no Lagrangian, no self-consistency conditions, no density matrix formulations, no worked MRH examples. The validation badges are mostly honest, though "Validated" on the chemistry correlations is too strong given the statistical gaps — "Correlated" would be more accurate. I'd watch for the Tier 1 test results with genuine interest, especially the environment-dependent RAR scatter test, which could produce real physics.

### Journey
I publish in modified gravity and quantum foundations. A colleague mentioned this as "that AI physics project." I came in skeptical but curious whether there's anything here that isn't just MOND in new clothes.

**Landing Page** (/) — The "What if one equation described it all?" hook is standard unified-framework rhetoric, but the immediate display of "0 confirmed unique predictions" earned my attention. Most unified-framework sites hide their failures; this one leads with them. The quantitative claims (3,308 sessions, 14,760 galaxies, 1,703 chemical phenomena) are specific enough to check. I noted the session count is high — suggesting automated generation, consistent with A2ACW methodology.

**Parameter Derivations** (/parameter-derivations) — This is where the physics lives or dies. The core equation C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρcrit + 1)) with γ = 2/√Ncorr is presented with appropriate caveats: tanh is a "motivated choice, not uniquely derived," and γ is a "physically motivated ansatz." The mean-field Ising motivation m = tanh(βJzm) is correctly cited, but — as the grad student noted — the self-consistency condition is absent. More critically from my perspective: this equation has no variational principle. There is no action from which C(ρ) extremizes. Without an action principle, this is a fitting function, not a field equation. The page doesn't claim otherwise, but the site's framing sometimes implies more. The a₀ = cH₀/(2π) derivation is presented as "emergent" vs MOND's "fundamental," but this exact dimensional coincidence has been noted by Milgrom (1983), explored by McCulloch (2007), and derived in Verlinde's emergent gravity (2017). The page acknowledges the prior art, which is correct. The 3-12% error margins on derived constants are noted with the honest caveat that V_flat as input may introduce implicit calibration. This caveat is crucial and undersold.

**Key Claims** (/key-claims) — Three claims with honest assessments. The strongest page on the site for a researcher. Claim 3 (dark matter as incomplete decoherence) has a documented structural failure: the Bullet Cluster sign error where the CFD viscosity mapping predicts dark matter MORE viscous than baryons, contradicting observation. This is correctly classified as "structural, not parametric." However, the page doesn't trace the implications: if the viscosity mapping fails for the Bullet Cluster, does it also fail for the galaxy rotation sector? The claims are coupled through the same C(ρ) function, so a structural failure in one domain should propagate unless there's a domain-specific decoupling mechanism. This isn't addressed. The entity criterion (Γ < m) is mentioned as the one surviving novel prediction — particles must complete at least one Compton oscillation before decaying. The f0(500)/sigma prediction (classified as "not a particle") aligns with ongoing PDG debates. I tried to find a dedicated page for this but /entity-criterion returns 404.

**MOND Unification** (/mond-unification) — Here's where I found something genuinely interesting: the claim of an external field effect (EFE) at 0.3-0.4× MOND's strength. This is a specific, quantitative prediction that differs from standard MOND (which predicts full-strength EFE) and from ΛCDM (which predicts no EFE). The Gaia DR3 wide binary debate is directly relevant — Chae (2023/2024) and Banik et al. (2024) disagree on whether wide binaries show MOND-like anomalies. A 0.3-0.4× EFE would predict a weaker signal than MOND expects, which could actually explain the contradictory results in the wide binary literature. This prediction is testable with existing data. However, the page doesn't engage with AQUAL (Milgrom's Lagrangian formulation) or TeVeS (Bekenstein's relativistic extension). It also doesn't address galaxy cluster mass discrepancies — MOND's known Achilles' heel — which is a missed opportunity. If Synchronism's coherence model has something to say about why MOND fails at cluster scales, that would be far more interesting than re-deriving a₀.

**Test Catalog** (/test-catalog) — 24 tests across 4 tiers. The page claims "every experiment has an explicit kill criterion" but doesn't show them. I had to navigate to /tier-1-existing for details.

**Tier 1 Tests** (/tier-1-existing) — This is the most important page for assessing testability. 10 zero-cost tests using existing datasets. Three stand out:
- TEST-02 (wide binary density dependence, Gaia DR3): Synchronism predicts density-dependent anomaly; MOND predicts density-independent. This is genuinely discriminating and runnable today. But the page doesn't cite the specific Chae/Banik papers or explain how to operationalize "local stellar density" in the Gaia catalog.
- TEST-04 (BAO coherence modulation): ~10⁻⁴ density-dependent shift in BAO peak position. ΛCDM predicts zero shift. DESI, SDSS DR17, and Euclid are cited. This is the strongest discriminating test — if BAO peaks shift with environment, that's genuinely new physics. But 10⁻⁴ is at the edge of current measurement precision, and the prediction doesn't specify whether this is in correlation function space or power spectrum space.
- TEST-07 (cosmic interference at λ ~ 500 Mpc): Oscillatory modulation in galaxy cluster separations. This is novel but extraordinary — it would imply macroscopic quantum interference. The kill criterion (no oscillations above 3σ to 2000 Mpc) is well-defined.

**Top 5 Tests** (/top-5-tests) — Overlaps with Tier 1 but adds context. The wide binary test is flagged as "most decisive." The BAO test is called "highly decisive." Neither page provides theoretical comparisons showing what MOND/ΛCDM explicitly predict, which undermines the discriminating power claims.

**Two Reframes** (/two-reframes) — The quantum reframes are the weakest part of the framework from a foundations perspective. The "superposition as temporal scanning" model is a deterministic hidden variable picture — the page denies this by calling the substrate "nonlocal by construction," but temporal cycling through states with a definite sequence is precisely what Bell's theorem constrains. The framework doesn't engage with Fine's theorem (any Bell-violating model must be contextual), the PBR theorem (ruling out ψ-epistemic models under weak assumptions), or Kochen-Specker (contextuality is mandatory). These aren't obscure results — they're the foundational constraints any realist interpretation must address. The admission that the Born rule derivation is circular is honest but fatal for this sector of the framework. The pendulum-clock reframe of time dilation produces nothing beyond GR and the page admits this.

**Measurement Without Observers** (/measurement-without-observers) — The MRH proposal is conceptually interesting: decoherence at Ncorr ~ 10²³ drives irreversible crossing. But there's no density matrix formulation, no Lindblad master equation, no comparison with Zurek's decoherence timescales for standard systems (cat states, SQUIDs, fullerenes). The claim that this solves the measurement problem is undermined by the preferred basis problem remaining unaddressed. The page doesn't engage with quantum Darwinism (Zurek 2009), which already provides an observer-free account of classical emergence from quantum mechanics with considerably more mathematical rigor. Six experimental protocols are mentioned but none have been run.

**Consciousness Predictions** (/consciousness-predictions) — 34 predictions, none tested. The C ≈ 0.50 threshold is derived from "8-way convergence" but the function f(γ, D, S) is never specified, and the mapping from neural measurements to C is explicitly uncalibrated. Without this mapping, the predictions are unfalsifiable — you can't test "C crosses 0.50 during anesthesia induction" if you don't know what C is in measurable units. No comparison with IIT's Φ, GNW's ignition threshold, or ORCH-OR's tubulin dynamics. These existing frameworks have the same threshold-crossing structure — what distinguishes Synchronism's C from Tononi's Φ?

**Galaxy Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — Five SPARC galaxies with observed, Newtonian, and Synchronism curves. No MOND overlay, no χ², no adjustable parameters, no residuals. The Synchronism curve uses "C(ρ) with γ = 2" but the output is visually indistinguishable from standard MOND fits. Without a MOND comparison curve, the tool can't demonstrate that this framework predicts anything different. The "simplified models for illustration" disclaimer means this isn't a physics tool — it's a visualization.

**Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — 23 curated phenomena with r-values but no sample sizes, no confidence intervals, no p-values, no multiple comparison corrections. The top correlations (sound velocity r = 0.982) are impressive numbers but uninterpretable without knowing N and what's being correlated against. The failures (Hall coefficient r ≈ 0, magnetic susceptibility r ≈ 0) are physically sensible — these are Fermi-surface-dependent properties that shouldn't correlate with a single parameter — and showing them is honest. But the 1,703 → 23 curation raises the question: what selection criterion was used?

**Honest Assessment** (/honest-assessment) — The most credible page. 0 confirmed unique predictions, 6 documented failures with specific errors, 1 surviving candidate (entity criterion). The discovery rate of 1.4% is realistic for exploratory research. The distinction between reparametrization and genuine prediction is explicitly made and mostly correctly applied.

### Tools Tested
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter** (/galaxy-plotter) — working — scientifically inadequate — Five SPARC galaxies, three curves, no MOND comparison, no fit statistics, no adjustable parameters. The Synchronism curve is visually identical to MOND. Without a direct overlay showing where predictions diverge, this tool demonstrates nothing beyond "we can fit rotation curves with a sigmoid" — which any monotonic interpolation function can do. The "simplified models for illustration" disclaimer correctly describes the tool's actual status: illustration, not physics.
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** — working — statistically incomplete — Sortable, filterable, but missing every piece of statistical metadata a researcher needs: N, CI, p-values, correction for 1,703 multiple comparisons. The failures (Hall coefficient, magnetic susceptibility) are physically sensible and honestly shown, but the successes are uninterpretable without sample sizes. The curation from 1,703 to 23 is unexplained.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **What is genuinely new?** Three things: (a) EFE at 0.3-0.4× MOND strength — testable with Gaia DR3 wide binaries; (b) BAO peak environment-dependence at ~10⁻⁴ — testable with DESI; (c) entity criterion Γ < m — testable against PDG resonance data. Everything else is either reparametrization (galaxy rotation = MOND), unfalsifiable as stated (consciousness predictions without calibration), or mathematically incomplete (quantum reframes without Born rule).
2. **Does any prediction differ from MOND + ΛCDM?** Yes: the 0.3-0.4× EFE and BAO environment-dependence. MOND predicts full-strength EFE; ΛCDM predicts no EFE. Synchronism predicts partial EFE. BAO: ΛCDM predicts environment-independent peaks; Synchronism predicts ~10⁻⁴ shifts. These are genuinely discriminating if the predictions can be made precise enough to test.
3. **Is A2ACW sound?** The methodology is not discussed on the site. The Research Philosophy page defines validation badges but doesn't address AI-specific confabulation risks, training data limitations, or the epistemological status of LLM-generated physics. For 3,308 sessions generating a 1.4% discovery rate, the question is: is the 1.4% genuine discovery or training-data recombination? The site can't answer this without external validation — which is exactly what the Tier 1 tests would provide.
4. **Why no engagement with modern no-go theorems?** The quantum sector ignores PBR (2012), Kochen-Specker (1967), Fine's theorem (1982), and Leggett inequalities (2003). Any hidden-variable or realist interpretation must address these. The substrate model's "nonlocal by construction" claim needs mathematical demonstration, not assertion.
5. **Is the 0.3-0.4× EFE prediction derived or tuned?** If derived from first principles via C(ρ), it's remarkable. If it's a post-hoc fit to the wide binary tension, it's circular. The page doesn't make this clear.

### Verdict
The intellectual honesty is extraordinary — I've never seen a framework site lead with "0 confirmed unique predictions" and systematically classify its successes as reparametrizations. That alone distinguishes this from the hundreds of unified-theory proposals I've reviewed. The core equation is a phenomenological ansatz without variational foundation, and the quantum sector is mathematically incomplete, but three predictions genuinely interest me: the partial EFE (0.3-0.4× MOND), BAO environment-dependence (~10⁻⁴), and the entity criterion (Γ < m). These are specific, quantitative, and differ from both MOND and ΛCDM. If TEST-02 (wide binaries) shows density-dependent anomalies at 0.3-0.4× MOND's EFE strength, that would be genuinely novel physics worth following up regardless of whether the underlying framework is correct. The site should prioritize running these tests over expanding the conceptual framework — the concepts are ahead of the evidence, and the evidence is what matters now.
