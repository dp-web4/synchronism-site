## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary

### Journey
I came in from a Reddit link expecting another "one equation explains everything" site. The landing page surprised me. The subtitle "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" and the line "We proposed this. We tested it. It did not hold as stated." are the most honest opening I've seen on a site like this. The plain-language gloss under the equation ("crowd a system enough and its parts start acting as one — this says how fast that switch flips") worked for me. So did the marching band vs. plaza crowd picture. The scoreboard (0 confirmed, 6 refutations) is clear even though I don't know what "BTFR" or "Cassini/SPARC squeeze" mean. I did need to scroll a lot before I understood that the headline equation is the thing that *failed*.

I clicked the Beginner Path (6 steps, "~40 min", "no physics background needed").

**Step 1, /why-synchronism.** The opening paragraph is great: "Physics has a fragmentation problem…" and "What changes if this works?" Then the page keeps going for roughly 4,000+ words. I ran into "dimensional ansatz", "Hamiltonian", "compander family (short for compressor–expander…)", "Bose–Einstein condensates", "175 SPARC + 14,435 ALFALFA–SDSS", and a superconductor temperature "6.5× too high". Most of the jargon does get a gloss in parentheses, which I appreciated, but the sheer amount wore me out. This is step 1 of 6 for beginners and it felt like a textbook appendix. I skimmed the bottom half.

**Step 2, /first-encounter.** This is the page that works best for me: short parts A–G, the band analogy, and "An atom doesn't 'feel' the Andromeda galaxy." Part C clearly tells me the γ formula failed ("the sign came out backwards"). Then Part D confused me. Its whole text is: "The quantum-classical boundary. This is where phase transitions happen… 1,703 chemical phenomena cluster here. Not by design — by observation." It has no caveat. Part C *just told me* the rule for working out γ failed, so how did anyone put 1,703 chemistry things at γ ≈ 1? Part D reads like a win, sitting between two admissions of failure, and I couldn't tell whether to believe it. Part G's list of failures (melting points 53% off, superconductors 6.5× off) was clear.

**Interactive tools.** The /interactive-tools index suggests "Coherence Explorer → Galaxy Plotter → Honest Assessment" for beginners, which was helpful.

**Step 3, /core-idea.** The "dimmer switch" metaphor is nice. Then came a pile of names I've never heard: Ising model, Hill functions, Landau theory, critical exponents, logistic, sigmoid, Bell/CHSH. It seemed to repeat First Encounter at a harder level. I skimmed.

**Step 4, /what-synchronism-is-not.** The five plain-English bullets at the top are excellent and exactly my level: "Not proven… Not checked by science journals… Not a replacement for today's physics… Not compatible, as it stands, with a basic rule of relativity…". That relativity bullet actually explains the rule in parentheses. The detail cards below lost me (ΛCDM, EFE, Lorentz invariance). One sentence stuck out: "The honest-assessment page is the most rigorous self-audit on any physics-adjacent research site." Everywhere else the site is careful and humble, so this brag felt out of tone. I have no way of checking it, and it made me trust the page a little less.

**Step 5, /honest-assessment.** The learning-paths page told me to read only the "If you read nothing else" box, and that was good advice. The box ("The one equation did not hold as stated. Every decisive test it ran on real data, it lost — 6 refutations executed, 0 predictions confirmed.") and the Bottom Line paragraph are things I could repeat to a friend. The page says it is ~18,000 words, so I didn't try.

**Equation Anatomy** (labelled Beginner). The tanh explanation ("Like a dimmer switch") and the ln explanation ("A number 1,000× bigger comes out only ~7 units bigger") are the clearest math explanations on the site. I finally sort of get tanh. But the γ step says, as if it were a fact, "One particle: γ=2 (sharp). A million: γ=2×10⁻³ (flat)", and right beside it a red honesty note says the rule is sign-inverted against real physics. So I'm learning a rule and being told it's wrong in the same step. The note is honest, but it would be less confusing if the step said "the *original proposal* was…".

At the end I could tell a friend: "A guy and a bunch of AIs tried one S-curve equation for everything from atoms to galaxies. It didn't work, and the site is a record of how they found that out." I could not tell them what "coherence" physically *is*, or why a failed idea still has 70+ pages.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer**: loads (a static SVG preview is present; the sliders need JavaScript). Intuitive at first: two sliders, three presets (0.49 "SPARC fit", 0.5 "exact MOND", 2.0 "original"). The readouts C(ρcrit)=0.3272, C(10ρcrit)=0.8259 and C(100ρcrit)=0.9785 let me see the S-curve saturate. I didn't know why I'd pick one preset over another, and "SPARC fit" and "exact MOND" mean nothing to me.
- **Galaxy Curve Plotter**: worked, and was the best thing on the site for me. The dark-matter puzzle is explained plainly: "galaxies should rotate more slowly at their outer edges… They don't… Something invisible is adding gravity." Showing the theory's own violet curve hugging the too-low line ("max C on this disk: 0.001 — inert by construction") right next to the old hand-drawn amber curve ("It fits because it was drawn to fit") made the failure *visible*. That did more for me than any paragraph.
- **Equation Anatomy**: worked (a 6-step walkthrough, text only on step 1). It has the clearest tanh and ln explanations anywhere on the site. The γ step is confusing (see friction log).

### Unanswered Questions
1. What *is* coherence physically? Is it a thing you can measure with an instrument, or just a number the equation outputs?
2. If the γ rule failed, where did the "1,703 chemical phenomena cluster at γ ≈ 1" come from? Is that still true?
3. If every decisive test failed, why is the project still going? What is left that could still work? (The "ontology that is mostly untested" line hints at an answer, but I don't know what an ontology is here.)
4. Who is "dp", and why was no human physicist asked to look at it?
5. Why does the page call itself "the most rigorous self-audit" if nobody outside has checked it?

### Verdict
The honesty won me over. A site that leads with "we tested it and it lost", and whose best tool shows its own curve failing, is more interesting to me than one more "theory of everything". But the Beginner Path isn't really a beginner path: step 1 is a 4,000-word technical page. The good plain-language bits (the landing gloss, First Encounter, the "Not" bullets, the "If you read nothing else" box, the Galaxy Plotter) are scattered and buried under jargon. Put those together into a true 10-minute route and I'd share it. As it is, I'd probably bounce around step 3.

### Journey
**Landing.** The top nav has nine items: Start Here · Learning Paths · Tier 1: Existing Data · Test Roadmap · Tools · Glossary · Research Philosophy · For Researchers · Honest Assessment. Research Philosophy and For Researchers are both methodology pages, and Tier 1 is a sub-page of Test Roadmap, yet both sit at the same top level. The landing links its jargon to the glossary: SPARC, BTFR, RAR, ΔBIC and CHSH each go to `/glossary#…`, and I confirmed all seven landing anchors exist. That's good practice. Ω_m, DESI and ΛCDM are not linked on the landing. MRH is expanded on first use ("MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon) — the bubble of nearest neighbors…"). There is a badge-legend pointer near the top: "Badges used below: Reparametrization · Speculative · Untested · Failed … ('Validated' is deprecated; 'Withdrawn' is an operational state, not a badge.)" That line pre-empts a question I was about to write down.

**Nav label ≠ URL ≠ page title.** "Start Here" goes to `/why-synchronism`, whose heading reads "Start Here: Why Synchronism?". "Test Roadmap" goes to `/test-catalog`, whose title is "Test Roadmap". Tier 1's text refers to "the 24-test catalog". "Tools" goes to `/interactive-tools`. The "Crossover Regime Visualizer" lives at `/phase-boundary-visualizer`, and that page does say so ("the URL keeps that name"). Keeping old URLs stable is defensible, but "roadmap" and "catalog" name the same page in different places.

**Breadcrumbs** appear on every inner page I checked, and they're rendered accessibly (`aria-label="Breadcrumb"`). But the middle level is never a link, apart from "Interactive Tools" on tool pages. The breadcrumbs also use section names the nav doesn't have: "Predictions & Experiments" (Tier 1, Test Roadmap) and "Methodology" (For Researchers, Research Philosophy). And "Start Here" means two different things. In the nav it's a link to one page. In breadcrumbs it's a section containing Learning Paths, Key Claims, Honest Assessment, Glossary and First Encounter. So "Home / Start Here / Glossary" looks like it should lead back to the Start Here page, but that crumb isn't clickable.

**Badge taxonomy.** The canonical definitions on /honest-assessment are good documentation. They give a two-part contract (a formal status word, then a free-text descriptor after the dash). They have a paragraph titled "Three negative-outcome words, one relationship" that relates Failed, refutation and "Refuted by Execution". They say plainly "Not every defined tag has a current instance". The /test-catalog page repeats the full legend inline, which helps. The glossary summarises both badge families at the top. Two problems:
1. **"Withdrawn" has two different definitions.** Glossary: "Withdrawn means the claim was retracted before any test." Honest Assessment: "Framework disowned the test — not from data refutation, but from internal contradiction, unmotivated amplitude, or supersession." One says a *claim* was withdrawn *before any test*. The other says a *test* was disowned *for stated reasons*. The only instance, TEST-04 (BAO), can't satisfy both at once.
2. **"Artifact Lesson" is still called a level on the tool pages.** The Tools index says, with a fix note dated 2026-09-18: "Artifact Lesson is not a level — it is an operational state… It now sits in its own Status slot." But /gamma-calculator and /phase-boundary-visualizer both still say, verbatim, "Tool type: Model Explainer · Artifact Lesson (the Interactive Tools index grouping **and level tag** …)". The γ Calculator line even carries a note, "Added 2026-09-15: the index and this page showed different words with no key between them". The index was fixed three days later and the pages drifted apart again.

**Counts.** The footer on every page reads "6 refutations executed (5 on external data + 1 Bell/CHSH construction check) from 5 roots…". The first line of Tier 1 reads "Executed — 5: 4 Failed, kill fired … + 1 underpowered as registered (TEST-04a)". Tier 1 then reconciles the two in "Tracing the 6" (the 6 adds an unnumbered RAR-shape test and the non-Tier-1 Bell check), and I could follow it. But "executed" counts one set in the header and a different set in the footer on the same page, and a scanner will read that as 5 vs 6. The glossary says "93 terms", and I count 93 anchors, so that number is right. "26 tests (24 registry + 2 added later)" matches between /test-catalog and /learning-paths.

**Learning Paths.** This is now honest about its own coverage: "none of the eight paths currently include an interactive tool" and "the difficulty paths reach 10 pages no topic track does". The Beginner per-step times (8+10+8+5+4, plus the glossary as reference) add up to the advertised ~40 min. That's the best-maintained page I saw.

**Key Claims, claim 1.** The badge reads "Speculative untestable as stated — ontological reframe". A few lines later the body says: "These are testable engineering claims, not philosophy:". The first item in that list is badged "Reparametrization — Post-hoc Fit". So the heading, the lead sentence and the first example all describe the claim's testability differently. The same section also says "Every QM interpretation — Copenhagen, Many-Worlds, QBism, relational — is an epicycle patching the same privileged-frame error", which is much stronger than the site's usual voice. It's a Speculative claim, so the badge covers it, but the prose doesn't sound like it knows that.

**"Phase transition" vs "crossover."** The visualizer was renamed because "C(ρ) has no phase transition, only a smooth crossover". The γ Calculator's regime readout says "C(ρ) has no phase transition here: tanh is smooth, so this is a crossover". First Encounter Part D still says "The quantum-classical boundary. This is where phase transitions happen… 1,703 chemical phenomena cluster here. Not by design — by observation." The γ Calculator says of the same band: "Fitted chemistry γ values cluster near this band… neither placement follows from the equation." That caveat has reached the tool but not the beginner page. Pass 1 flagged Part D from the reader's side; this is the same defect as a terminology split.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (/gamma-calculator): works in the static render (2 inputs, preset buttons, a live SVG curve and a preset table). The default N_corr = 4 gives γ = 1.0000 and C(ρ_crit) = 0.60. I checked the arithmetic (tanh(ln 2) = 0.6) and the BCS preset (2/√10⁷ = 6.32×10⁻⁴). The copy is exceptionally clear about why a known-wrong formula is still interactive: "click 'Ideal gas', then 'BCS superconductor,' and notice the far more collective system gets the far flatter curve". That is a model of how to document an artifact. Problems: the stale "level tag" line (above), and the page is long. The tool widget comes after ~3 caveats and a multi-paragraph finding about galaxy parameters that its own revision note says "is a finding about the framework's parameters rather than a caveat on using the tool".
- **Chemistry Correlation Explorer** (/chemistry-correlation-explorer): works. It has 23 rows, filter buttons with counts (Neutral 4 · Coherence helps 12 · Incoherence helps 4 · Barrier-dominated 3, which sum to 23), and sort by r. The caveat layering is thorough. It has a "Read before sorting: the r column is not evidence" banner directly above the table, which is exactly the right place. "Regime" is defined on the page, with a note that labels "were assigned after the fits". But the page admits "Two readings are both live and neither is stated on this page", meaning it doesn't say how γ was assigned per material. That is a documentation gap the page names but doesn't close. And the Related Concepts footer undoes the caveats (friction log).
- **Crossover Regime Visualizer** (/phase-boundary-visualizer): page structure only. It has the same stale Artifact Lesson line, and the rename is explained in-page.

### Unanswered Questions
1. Which page owns the definition of each term? The site has a canonical badge reference, but no canonical source for tool-type lines, Related-Concepts blurbs or footer copy, and those are exactly the places that drifted.
2. What are "Predictions & Experiments" and "Methodology" as sections? Is there, or should there be, an index page for each?
3. For the chemistry table: was per-material γ computed from 2/√N_corr or fit freely? The page says this is "an open bookkeeping question". It seems answerable from the archive rather than an open research question.
4. Is there an automated check for the stale wordings the revision notes keep recording? Several notes say a count or term "drifted". A string-level lint across all pages would have caught both the Artifact Lesson and the Withdrawn splits.

### Verdict
This site documents its own errors better than most sites document their successes. It has dated revision notes, a two-part badge contract, reconciled counts, and a Learning Paths page that audits its own coverage. The recurring defect is structural, not careless: shared vocabulary (tool-type lines, badge definitions, related-link blurbs, "phase transition" vs "crossover") is hand-copied per page, so each fix lands on one page and leaves its siblings stale. I'd prioritise generating those strings from one source over any individual wording fix.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, /parameter-derivations, /key-claims, /coherence-function, /mond-unification, /honest-assessment, /for-researchers, /tools, /galaxy-plotter, /coherence-explorer)

### Journey
A colleague sent the link, so I skipped the onboarding and went straight to **Parameter Derivations**. I expected to be the person who points out that tanh(½ ln(1+x)) is just an interpolating function. The page already says it: "At γ = ½, C(x) = tanh(½ ln(1+x)) is identically MOND's simple interpolating function μ_simple". I checked it: tanh(½ ln y) = (y−1)/(y+1), so C = x/(x+2), which is μ_simple(x/2), i.e. a₀ → 2a₀. The page also retires the Landau and Ising framings for the right reasons: no self-consistency loop, no non-analyticity, no critical exponents. It correctly states that γ = 2/√N_corr is sign-inverted against mean-field intuition, where larger correlated systems give sharper transitions, not flatter ones. The ρ_crit audit's "must fall as V⁻² to track a₀" also checks out: with M ~ V⁴/(Ga₀) and R ~ V²/a₀, you get ρ ~ a₀²/(GV²).

Arithmetic I checked by hand:
- a₀ = cH₀/2π at H₀ = 67.4 gives 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² ✓
- Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) gives 0.2485 kg/m² = 119 M☉/pc² ✓
- Milgrom's Σ_M at a₀ = 1.2 gives 137 ✓
- Offset (1.20−1.04)/√(0.02²+0.24²) = 0.66σ ✓
- Inverted H₀ = 2πa₀/c = 77.6 ± 15.5 ✓
- BTFR gap 0.40/√(0.10²+0.07²) = 3.3σ ✓
- TEST-10: 1/(1−0.927) = 13.7 ✓
- MW knee 0.029·229² = 1.52×10³ M☉/pc³ ✓
- DDO 154 knee 0.029·47² = 64 M☉/pc³ ✓
- 47/3300 = 1.4% ✓

My first WebFetch summary reported a "0.5% match" to Freeman's 124. The raw text says the reverse: "Quoting agreement to 0.5% (or 4%) against a quantity known to a factor of two is precision theater". That was a summariser artifact, not a site claim. Good.

**Coherence Function** and **MOND Unification** raised the question I actually care about: *what is the argument of C when you fit galaxies?* The coherence-function page answers it in a box dated 2026-09-08. The SPARC fit solves g_bar = g_obs·tanh(γ ln(1+g_obs/a₀′)), which is μ-form on g_obs. It also notes that the literal reading g_obs = g_bar/C(g_bar) gives g_obs → g_bar + 2a₀′, a constant additive floor that "could never reach 0.1437 dex". That is exactly the derivation I would have done: on g_bar the division wiring is *not* MOND, and deep-regime curves would rise as V ∝ √r. Further down the same page, a sentence dated 2026-08-04 still says γ = 0.489 and the form-selection table were "computed with the argument g_bar, not ρ". That sentence contradicts the verified box above it (see friction log).

**Galaxy Plotter** and **Honest Assessment** complete the picture of three C functions: the density-keyed headline C(ρ) (inert at calibration, max C ~10⁻³ on discs), the acceleration-keyed μ-form used for γ = 0.489 and ΔBIC +184, and C_a = Ω_m + (1−Ω_m)·x/(1+x) with x = (g_bar/a₀)^{1/φ}, which is what TEST-09/10 actually evaluate. The Honest Assessment root accounting holds up: 6 executed runs on 5 roots, 2 of them framework-specific (the asserted B_max and the γ = 2 pin). It is internally consistent with the landing page's "5 roots (2 framework-specific)".

My main scientific reservation isn't an error on the site. It is a framing issue the site half-states. **The two framework-specific kills (TEST-09/10) refute C_a, a floored μ_simple-type function with a φ exponent. Neither is the headline tanh-log equation.** The headline density law is refuted separately, by being inert and by the ΔBIC +2843 head-to-head. So a reader who reads "the equation failed TEST-09" is reading a failure of a different function. The plotter's box says this plainly. The Key Claims / Honest Assessment tables don't label the function at the point of the kill.

### Journey
I came in expecting to write "reparametrized MOND with an AI press release". The landing beat me to it: 0 confirmed, 6 refutations, "It did not hold as stated". So I went looking for the places where a self-critical site is most likely to go wrong: the registration logic, the cosmology sector (which gets far less scrutiny than galaxies on sites like this), the methodology claims, and the quantum-foundations framing.

**Does anything differ from MOND + ΛCDM?** No. The Test Roadmap says so plainly: "Of these 24 proposed tests, 0 selected Synchronism over MOND+EFE+ΛCDM, and none still unrun can. 2 executed tests did discriminate, and both selected MOND." I checked the two discriminating kills. TEST-09: 0.406/0.124 = 3.27σ ✓. TEST-10: 106/153 = 69% of discs exceed B_max = 3.17, and NGC 2915 needs B ≥ 1/(1−0.927) = 13.7 ✓. The page is honest that both kills share one root (the underived 1/Ω_m ceiling) and that the verdict is convention-dependent: under Ω_m/Ω_b ≈ 6.4 the median passes and only the tail fails. That is the right way to report a kill that depends on a parameter you never derived. TEST-02 (wide binaries) is correctly called "self-eliminating": a 0.05–0.4% signal sits far below Gaia DR3 reach, so the framework has nothing to say about the Chae vs Banik dispute. TEST-25 (Cassini/SPARC) correctly says the RAR-preferred MOND interpolating functions fail Cassini by the same margin, so that "refutation" is inherited rather than discriminating.

**Cosmology sector.** This is the most interesting thing I found, and the site handles it correctly. The dark-energy family is ρ_DE = ρ_m(1−C)/C. At γ = ½, C = x/(x+2), so (1−C)/C = 2/x and ρ_DE = 2ρ_crit, a constant. That is exactly Λ, and I confirmed it numerically at x = 0.1, 1, 10 and 100. The DR2 BAO + Planck + Dovekie SN fit lands at γ = 0.487, with Δχ² = −0.3 relative to ΛCDM, and the site gives w₀ = −0.992, w_a = +0.015. So SPARC (γ = 0.489) and DESI (0.487) both land at γ ≈ ½. A less careful site would sell that as "one γ unifies galaxies and cosmology". This one says the opposite (Consciousness Demo): γ = ½ is "the point where each sector reduces to MOND or Λ". The coincidence is the identity point of each family, not a unification. TEST-26 is also honest: "a DR3 result that robustly requires a w = −1 crossing kills ΛCDM equally", so it discriminates against w₀w_a, not against ΛCDM. What's missing is the comparison a DESI person will reach for first. The family's best member gains Δχ² = −0.3 over ΛCDM. The w₀w_a gain on comparable combinations is roughly an order of magnitude or more (DR2 reports 2.8–4.2σ depending on the SN sample; I don't know the Dovekie-recalibrated number). So "nests ΛCDM" also means "cannot capture the DESI signal at all". That is a sharper statement than the site currently makes.

**DESI growth (TEST-04a): the one place the site is soft on itself.** The registered criterion is "fσ₈(z=0.51) > 0.46 (rules out Synchronism at >3σ)". DESI DR1 LRG1 gives 0.474×1.16 ± 0.474×0.13 = 0.550 ± 0.062 ✓ (arXiv:2411.12021). The site explains that 0.46 was the prediction (0.418) plus 3σ_forecast, with σ_forecast = 0.014 (I reproduced that value), and that DR1's σ came out 4.4× larger. So it reads the criterion as "must exceed 0.46 at 3σ" and calls the test underpowered. That reading is defensible for DR1: the prediction is only 2.13σ from the data either way. **But the DR2 pre-commitment keeps the compound reading.** Branch (B), "fσ₈ > 0.46 at ≥3σ", needs obs > 0.46 + 3σ. At σ = 0.03–0.04 that means obs ≳ 0.55–0.58, which puts ΛCDM (0.474) itself ~2.5–2.7σ low. So the registered kill can only fire if DESI *also* disfavours ΛCDM. The clean prospective statistic is (obs − 0.418)/σ > 3. At σ = 0.04 the DR1 central value (0.550) would already clear that bar (0.538). As written, branch (C) ("survives as underpowered") is the near-certain outcome. That is the same structural defect that got TEST-04 withdrawn (a threshold far below achievable precision), repeated one step removed. The σ₈-amplitude check (0.841 ± 0.034 vs 0.76, 2.38σ ✓) is the better-powered statistic, and the site already has it, labelled post-hoc.

**A2ACW: what's actually new here.** The For Researchers control arm is the most original thing on the site. The authors built a known-answer test for their own AI audit instrument. The positive arm is 3 reparametrizations (Eddington's 137, tired light, a Tully–Fisher "derivation" that assumes MOND). The negative arm is 6 canonical discoveries (Dirac, Bell, BCS, Higgs, Hawking, Noether). The result is sensitivity 1.0, specificity 0/6, Youden's J = 0. The conclusion is correct and important: any "0 survivors" count that rests on A2ACW audit rather than on data "carr[ies] no information about whether the seed framework contains novelty". Very few AI-for-science groups publish a result that undercuts their own filter. But the passage has three defects:
1. "An instrument that misclassifies every known **positive**…" should say *negative*. Positives are the reparametrizations, and the instrument caught all of them (sensitivity 1.0). What it misclassified was every canonical discovery (the negatives).
2. "Combined sensitivity … is 6/6" is drawn from a 3-item arm. It pools two rules (literal and steelmanned) over the same 3 items, and those are not independent trials. The page's own Clopper–Pearson bound (sensitivity ≥ 0.29) is the 3/3 bound: 0.025^(1/3) = 0.292 ✓.
3. The page gives two different J intervals: "J ∈ [−0.46, +0.46]" and "J between −0.71 and +0.46". I recomputed the second: 0.292 − 1 + 0 = −0.71, and 1 + 0.459 − 1 = +0.46 ✓. The symmetric one is wrong.

One consequence the landing page doesn't draw: its third scoreboard number ("0 of 9 'Validated' claims surviving audit") is, by the site's own control arm, partly instrument-limited. Some of those demotions rest on explicit algebra; the μ_simple identity at γ = ½ is one Pass 3 checked. Others rest on LLM prior-art mapping. The scoreboard should say which is which. As it stands, the landing puts an uninformative-instrument count next to the data refutations.

**Quantum foundations.** All three "differs from standard QM" claims are correctly badged Reparametrization: Palma–Suominen–Ekert 1996, waveguide-QED cos², and dynamical decoupling (Viola–Lloyd, CPMG, Uhrig). The Bell page's triptych (local 2 < Born 2√2 < PR box 4) is correct. It honestly says "hidden-communication substrates were not built and remain untested", so the framework has no construction that reaches Tsirelson. Two issues a foundations referee would raise:
- **"Every QM interpretation … is an epicycle patching the same privileged-frame error."** In physics, "privileged frame" means a preferred reference frame. This framework is the one with an absolute-time substrate: the For Researchers page's own Lorentz-violation no-go puts it 16–28 orders of magnitude from SME bounds (Collins et al. 2004, cited correctly). Copenhagen, Everett, QBism and relational QM carry no preferred frame. The site means observer privilege (the "Copernican argument"), but the term collides with its own biggest liability.
- **Missing no-go constraints.** "Superposition = rapid temporal scanning through phase modes" is a macrorealist hidden-variable picture: at each instant the system is in one mode. That is exactly what Leggett–Garg violations (superconducting qubits, NMR, neutrons) constrain. Kochen–Specker contextuality and PBR (2012) also apply. None is mentioned. They would be the natural kill tests for claim 1, which is currently badged "untestable as stated" because nobody has set it against these theorems.

**Density-keyed no-go.** The For Researchers page now cites the prior art (McGaugh 2004; Lelli, McGaugh & Schombert 2016; Lelli et al. 2017) and claims only "the quantified local-density instance". That is the right scope. It's citable as a short note, not as a discovery.
