## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary

### Journey
Came from Reddit. The headline — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — hooked me, and the subtitle "An Open Research Notebook — Not a Theory of Everything" plus "it did not hold as stated" was a genuine surprise. I've never seen a physics site lead with its own failure. That earned trust within 10 seconds. But the first screen also throws "0 confirmed predictions" and "6 refutations executed" at me as headline stats. I didn't know what a "refutation executed" was, and it read like a scoreboard for a game I don't know the rules of.

I wasn't sure where to start. The nav says "Start Here" and points to /why-synchronism, but the big button says "Start the 10-minute intro" and points to /first-encounter. A third button says "Beginner Path →". I clicked Start Here first. "Physics has a fragmentation problem" is a great opening line. The "crowd to marching band" image clicked for me immediately. Then the page turned into kill criteria, null models, SPARC, ALFALFA–SDSS, Bell/CHSH and YBCO, and I started skimming.

/first-encounter was the best page for me. It's broken into short sections with a progress bar and Continue buttons. "Coherence is how collectively a group of things behaves" is the clearest sentence on the site, and the tanh "dimmer switch" analogy worked. I lost the thread in Sections C–E, which cover γ = 2/√N_corr failing, γ fitted to ≈0.5 "which is MOND's own formula", and a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π) being a "dimensional coincidence, off by about 13%". I don't know what "fitted vs predicted" means in practice, or why matching MOND is bad news rather than good news. The section labels read like dev notes to me, e.g. "γ status: formula failed · γ = 2 loses to MOND". Section G ("Where It Fails": melting points 53% off, superconductors 6.5× wrong) was oddly satisfying. Concrete numbers of wrongness are easy to understand.

/interactive-tools helpfully says "Best first tool: Coherence Explorer", which is exactly the guidance I wanted. But 3 of the 7 tools are labelled "Artifact Lesson", meaning they demo something that failed. As a newcomer I didn't know whether to bother with those.

/honest-assessment has an "If you read nothing else" box, which is good. The sentence "It lost every test it executed" is blunt and clear. Then the page just keeps going. The fetch told me it was over 150,000 characters, about a short novel. I bailed after the summary. "Two of the six share one root, and the count is under review" made me less sure what the headline "6" means.

/key-claims was easy to scan: three questions, two marked Speculative and one marked Failed. "Is Dark Matter Incomplete Decoherence?" is the claim that sounds coolest, and it's the one marked Failed. That's honest, but it left me wondering what the site still thinks is promising.

The glossary saved me a few times; the MRH entry ("event horizon for influence") was great. The ΔBIC entry, "The BIC difference between two models", is circular for me because I don't know what BIC is.

### Journey
I started at the landing page and mapped the navigation. The top nav is a flat list of 9 items, all resolving, and the hero has three CTAs. Pass 1 already flagged the "Start Here" (/why-synchronism) vs "Start the 10-minute intro" (/first-encounter) split. /learning-paths settles it in favour of /why-synchronism (Beginner step 1), with /first-encounter as step 2. The architecture is coherent; the landing page's hero button just contradicts it. The fetch also surfaced a stray "IntroductionExplore All" text run on the landing page with no link targets. The landing page uses five badges (Reparametrization, Speculative, Untested, Failed, Audited-Negative) and gives no legend, only a link to `/honest-assessment#validation-badge-definitions`. That anchor exists and is labelled "canonical". The legend there is the best single artifact on the site. It has two named badge families (MRH-relationship, Descriptive), a deprecated family with migration guidance, and then a separate list of "operational states (not badges)" and "status lifecycle verbs". The trouble is that it lists about **9 badges + 9 operational states + 14 lifecycle verbs + tool-card labels**. On the live pages these all appear in the same visual position (the status slot under a heading), so the "not a badge" distinction is invisible at the point of use. "Kill Criterion Triggered" is defined **twice** in that legend: once as an operational state and once as a lifecycle verb, with different wording.

The vocabulary keeps growing past the legend. On /honest-assessment itself: "Asserted, Not Derived", "Non-discriminating" and "Fork" are used in the status position with no definition. On /tier-1-existing: "RE-ADJUDICATED", "Registered Test Never Run" (the legend says "NEVER RUN AS REGISTERED"), "Convention-Dependent", "PROPOSED, NOT REGISTERED" and "Closed". On /test-catalog: "Executed and Failed" and "unrunnable as stated". The heaviest overload is "Failed". The legend defines it as "Prediction contradicted by data", but TEST-04a is labelled "Failed — underpowered as registered (not counted as a refutation)" on /test-catalog and "Underpowered as Registered" (no Failed) on /tier-1-existing. Those are two pages giving two labels for one test, and one of them contradicts the legend definition.

The glossary is well built: 107 entries (the header count matches), alphabetized, Greek after Latin, letter-jump anchors and a breadcrumb. Problems: (a) a circular pair, where MRH is defined via "presence" and Presence is defined as elements "within a Markov Relevancy Horizon"; (b) the **BAO entry says "Synchronism's TEST-04 predicts" a shift, while the Withdrawn entry (and /tier-1-existing) says TEST-04 is withdrawn**, so the glossary contradicts itself; (c) entries lean on repo filenames a reader can't see ("STATUS.md", "PREDICTIONS.md"); (d) ALFALFA and SDSS have no standalone entries, only a combined "ALFALFA-SDSS". There's also a naming hazard: the badge **"Active-MRH"** reuses the acronym of the core concept MRH (Markov Relevancy Horizon). A reader seeing "Active-MRH" on a card will reasonably think it's a claim *about* the horizon, not a research-focus status.

Test numbering is described as "one flat namespace, TEST-01 … TEST-26", and /test-catalog's arithmetic checks out (10+4+7+3 = 24, plus TEST-25/26 = 26). But the namespace carries suffixes (03s, 04a), unnumbered items (RAR shape, CLUSTER-SCALE), and an admitted unresolved collision: the research repo files a run as "TEST-08", which is the site's Freeman-law card, and it's "routed to the operator". /test-catalog doesn't give a status for most of its own tests and defers to the tier pages. A roadmap page that can't tell me the status of TEST-06 isn't doing its job.

There's also a breadcrumb/nav label mismatch. /tier-1-existing's breadcrumb is "Home › Predictions & Experiments › Tier 1", and "Predictions & Experiments" links to /test-catalog, which the nav and H1 call "Test Roadmap". Other breadcrumbs work (Home / Interactive Tools / Equation Anatomy, Home / Core Theory / MRH, Home / Methodology / For Researchers). In the fetched markup the separator renders as a list item ("1. Home 2. / 3. …"), which screen readers will announce as a crumb. That's minor, but it's an accessibility smell: use CSS separators or `aria-hidden`.

Tool names don't match their slugs: "Crossover Regime Visualizer" lives at /phase-boundary-visualizer, "Equation Anatomy" at /equation-walkthrough, and "Decisive Tests: Status (none active)" at /top-5-tests. That's tolerable if the renames are documented, but the slugs leak old framing into every shared link.

The taxonomy for "Artifact Lesson" differs by page. /interactive-tools calls it "an operational state". The glossary calls it "a tool label rather than a validation badge". The honest-assessment legend files it under operational states but defines it as "Marks an interactive tool, not a prediction". /tier-1-existing doesn't define it. That's three category assignments for one label.

**The leak of internal process into reader text is the issue I'd prioritise.** I verified these by grepping the raw HTML, not just the fetch summary:
- /for-researchers: "Whether they stay in the count of 6 is pending dp." / "It is unrun (routed to dp since 2026-09-10)." "dp" is never introduced on the page. A reader can't know it's a person, let alone the project lead.

Counts consistency (headline: 0 confirmed / 6 refutations from 5 roots / 26 tests / 3,308 sessions):
- **0 confirmed**: consistent everywhere I looked.
- **6 refutations**: consistently "6" but qualified differently per page: "6 from 5 roots", "independent count is at most 5", "the split to quote is 2 + 1 + 1 + 1", "≤5", "about 4 of them are effectively independent" (/tier-1-existing), "6, plus one excluded reading pending a counting decision", "whether [TEST-09/10] stay in the count of 6 is pending dp". That's six different qualifiers on one headline number. /test-catalog adds an "Executed and Failed" set of **four** (TEST-09, -10, -25, -03s). A reader comparing pages will wonder whether that 4 is the 6.
- **/for-researchers, same paragraph**: "(N=6 audits, …)" and "9 claims were audited … 0 survived". Verified in the raw HTML. These may be different units (6 audit runs vs 9 claims), but the paragraph doesn't say so, and the landing page says "0 of 9".

That's three session numbers with no "as of" date beside the headline one. I'm flagging this as needing a grep. It may be a correct historical snapshot, but "1,703 phenomena" vs "1,703 phenomenon types … not a count of distinct phenomena" vs "1,703 fitted γ values" on the same page is a real definitional drift.

### Journey
A colleague sent the link with "interesting AI project". I expected a crank ToE and was braced for a mean-field tanh dressed up as a derivation. The landing page disarmed that in one line: "The one equation did not hold as stated", with 0 confirmed / 6 refutations. So I went straight to /parameter-derivations to see whether the site's self-criticism is itself correct.

**Checked by hand, all correct:**
- tanh(γ ln(1+x)) = [(1+x)^{2γ}−1]/[(1+x)^{2γ}+1]. At γ=½ this is x/(x+2) = μ_simple(x/2), so a₀_MOND = 2a₀′. The page says outright "the Ising tanh is derived; this tanh is chosen", which is exactly the objection I came to make.
- /galaxy-rotation's claim that keying the γ=½ compander on g_bar "gives a constant offset rather than MOND": g_obs = g_bar(x+2)/x = g_bar + 2a₀. That's correct, and a nice, non-obvious point.
- ΔBIC +7.1 with Δχ² ≈ −0.84: ln(2807) − 0.84 ≈ 7.10, which matches the 2,807-point count quoted on TEST-25. Internally consistent.
- a₀ = cH₀/2π = 1.04×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² at H₀=67.4 (13% under 1.2). Σ₀ = a₀/(2πG) = 119 M☉/pc². Both reproduced.
- ρ_crit normalisation: A = 4π/(β_J² G R₀²) with R₀=8 kpc and β_J=1 gives 4.6×10⁻⁵ M☉ pc⁻³ (km/s)⁻². The units work and I reproduce the "~600× off".
- /dark-energy: H² = 8πGρ_m/(3C) at γ=½ gives 1/C = 1 + 2ρ_c/ρ_m, so ρ_DE = 2ρ_c is constant. That's exactly ΛCDM, as claimed. ### Journey

**Galaxies.** The γ ≈ 0.49 acceleration-keyed fit is μ_simple, which the site says itself. The density-keyed C_ρ is the only form that makes a genuinely different prediction: environment dependence, and density-dependent wide-binary boosts of ~0.05–0.4% where MOND predicts ~18%. That form is dead three times over: r² = 0.0001 against Cosmicflows-4 (N = 141), ΔBIC +2843 on SPARC, and the lunar-laser-ranging exclusion the TEST-02 card cites. /cdm-discrimination frames its lever sizes correctly (MOND+EFE ~0.09 dex vs C_ρ ≤ 2×10⁻³ dex). It doesn't notice that the same null environment pipeline, re-keyed on external *acceleration* instead of ambient density, would be a direct test of the MOND EFE (the Chae+2020 signal). That would be a genuine contribution the project could make with code it already has.

**Solar System (TEST-25).** "Failed — inherited from MOND", and I agree with the verdict: Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024 get 8.7σ. But the card puts its own "+17.95σ in-house" beside a sentence saying the in-house intersection is empty *only* if the 2,807 points are treated as independent, and that scored per galaxy the retained interval is γ = 0.3–1.7. I also endorse Pass 3's channel objection. Cassini constrains the EFE-induced quadrupole, not the Saturn-orbit tail exponent.

**Dark energy.** This is the best page on the site. It names the model as a Cardassian-class modified Friedmann equation (Freese & Lewis 2002), cites the fluctuation/ISW problems (Koivisto+2005, Sandvik+2004), and derives the sign lock sign(w₀+1) = sign(wₐ). **Quantum.** /measurement-without-observers is honest. It calls itself an interpretive overlay with no rate equation and no differing prediction, and it checks its one candidate rate against matter stability: Planck-scale diffusion heats hydrogen at 2×10⁻⁴ eV/s, 15–30 orders past bound-state limits (Pearle & Squires 1994). That's the right bound to use, and better than starting from interferometry. There are still no CSL-style (λ, r_C) numbers to place on the standard exclusion plot.

**Bell/CHSH (refutation #6).** /for-researchers finally gives the S values: local 1.98, nonlocal grid 2.00, saturation-gated substrate 1.85, and global clock 2.67 "by also signaling". A signalling model can reach any S up to 4, so that arm shows nothing. A "nonlocal" grid landing exactly on the local bound looks like an effectively local construction. No error bars or sample counts are given, and the original bet still isn't quoted verbatim anywhere I looked. Pass 3 is right that this belongs in a separate "construction checks" column. The landing page now *does* split it ("5 on external data, 1 Bell/CHSH construction check"), which is good. The other surfaces should follow.

**What /for-researchers offers as citable.** The candidates are:
- The local-density no-go for algebraic coupling. It's scoped properly, attributes the general obstruction to Milgrom's non-locality theorem, and openly cites Burrage, Copeland & Millington 2017 as an existence-proof counterexample ("A no-go dies to one existence proof of the thing it forbids", which is a good line). It's modest but legitimate.
- The dim-4 LIV naturalness problem, correctly attributed to Collins–Perez–Sudarsky–Urrutia–Vucetich 2004, so not new.
- The GC window. It uses Baumgardt & Hilker Jacobi radii computed from Webb+2013 eq. 8 under a *Newtonian* Galactic potential, while the page admits the field-equation EFE "has not been computed on this data", and that MOND survives these clusters "*because of* the EFE". A window that excludes a knee using Newtonian tidal radii, while the comparison theory survives only through physics not computed here, is asymmetric. The page's "cite it as an L2 window, not as an exclusion" is the right caveat.

**A2ACW.** /research-philosophy is unusually candid. Specificity is 0/6 on the in-distribution canonical arm (sensitivity 3/3), and PPV is 0/9 (95% upper bound ~28%). With specificity 0/6, the filter passed every reparametrization it was shown. The "1.4% internal-consistency survival" therefore carries essentially no information about novelty, and the page half-says this ("not a calibrated reparametrization detector"). The decisive experiment is the out-of-distribution, post-cutoff, citation-stripped positive control, and it has never been run. That is the one experiment here whose outcome a researcher outside the project would want to know.
