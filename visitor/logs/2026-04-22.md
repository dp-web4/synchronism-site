## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: The tone is refreshingly honest and the landing hook is great, but the moment I clicked past "First Encounter" into anything substantive, the jargon spiked and I didn't have the hooks to hold on.

### Journey
Landed on the site from a Reddit link. The headline — "What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?" — is the kind of thing I'd expect on a PBS Space Time title card. That pulled me in. I liked that a "See What Failed" button was right next to "Start Exploring" — that's not what a crank site does, so I felt safer clicking around.

Clicked **Start Exploring → First Encounter**. The "no prerequisites, 10 minute walk" framing was friendly. Then it told me the foundation is "presence," which it described with examples (electron density, neutron stars, temperature). I kind of got it? But the term "Markov Relevancy Horizon" showed up and I had no idea what to do with it. I kept reading anyway.

Went back and tried **Why Synchronism?** (step 1 of the learning path — I realized I'd skipped it). This one worked well for me. It said physics is fragmented (quantum, relativity, chemistry), and Synchronism claims one principle connects them. Success/failure summary right there. I liked the honesty about the 53% melting point error — that's a very specific admission.

Clicked into **The Core Idea** (step 3). This is where I fell off. There's an equation with **tanh** in it. The page calls it a "dimmer switch" which is a nice try, but I don't know what tanh actually does to a number. Then ρcrit and Ncorr showed up. I skimmed.

Tried to click "Need an analogy first?" — but when I guessed the URL `/analogy`, it 404'd. There seems to be a helper for confused readers but I couldn't find it from a skim.

Went to **Honest Assessment**. This was actually the most compelling page for me — it laid out wins (galaxy rotation, chemistry boundaries) and clear losses (superconductor temps 6.5× wrong, dark matter predicted "sticky" when it's not) side by side. "0 unique confirmed predictions yet" sits right there in plain text. I respect that, but I'll be honest — as a casual reader, seeing "0 confirmed predictions" made me wonder why I'm reading this. The site's answer ("wrong theories motivate right questions") is philosophically fine but a skeptic in my shoes might bounce there.

Clicked **Cosmology / Galaxy Rotation** — felt too dense immediately ("Radial Acceleration Relation," "baryonic mass"). Found a link to a **Galaxy Curve Plotter** so I tried that (see tools below).

Did a quick loop through **Glossary** (the Coherence entry was OK, the MRH entry was impenetrable), and **What Synchronism Is Not** (I actually liked this one — the "Not: X" structure was scannable and the acknowledgement "we don't have journal publication, we did AI-to-AI review instead" was striking, though I don't know if I'm supposed to find that reassuring or alarming).

Finished on the **Coherence Explorer** — sliders for γ and ρcrit. Moved them and the curve changed. Neat. Didn't deeply understand what I was changing.

- **Galaxy Plotter** (`/galaxy-plotter`) — worked — intuitive — I could pick a galaxy (I tried NGC 2403), see three curves (observed dots, Newtonian dashed, Synchronism violet), and visually see "the gap." ### Unanswered Questions
1. What actually is "presence" in one sentence I could tell a friend?
2. Why does a tanh-shaped curve describe "quantum → classical" specifically? What's the physical reason it has to be that shape?

If there were one really good visual/analogy page that explained what coherence *feels* like physically — something as vivid as the Galaxy Plotter but for the core equation — I would probably come back. ## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 17 (plus 2 confirmed 404s)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: yes (the structural problems are fixable and the content is worth the fix)
- **Overall impression**: The content is honest and largely well-written sentence-by-sentence, but the **information architecture has two top-level structures fighting each other**: a linear "learning path" and a categorical breadcrumb taxonomy that the global nav never surfaces. On top of that, validation-badge vocabulary, step counts, and previous/next controls are inconsistent from one page to the next. These aren't content problems — they're IA and copy-consistency problems.

### Journey
I browsed methodically, checking the global nav, the breadcrumbs on every page, whether the same acronym is handled the same way across pages, and whether interactive tools carry their own definitions or assume upstream context.

The global header offers exactly three links: **Synchronism (home) / Glossary / Honest Assessment**. Nothing else. No link to Learning Paths, Test Catalog, Interactive Tools, Core Theory, Predictions. That's a thin top nav for a site this big, and it forced me to discover major sections by clicking through body copy.

The breadcrumbs told a different story. As I moved around I saw pages categorized under *Getting Started*, *Core Theory*, *Chemistry*, *Quantum Physics*, *Predictions & Experiments*, and *Interactive Tools*. Those are six top-level categories — but none are reachable directly from the header and there's no section index for any of them. So the breadcrumb tells me where I am without giving me a way to browse siblings.

The Beginner learning path on `/learning-paths` lists six steps. But `/first-encounter` displays **"Step 1 of 7"** in its progress indicator. Those two numbers disagree. Worse, the step indicator itself isn't consistently rendered: First Encounter has one, Core Idea (the page that is "Step 3" on the learning path) has none, and Why Synchronism (the first step on the Beginner path) has none either. The Previous/Next buttons follow the same pattern of randomness — First Encounter has both; Core Idea has neither; MRH has Next but no Previous.

The "Need an analogy first?" Previous link on First Encounter resolves to `/two-reframes` — a page titled **"Two Reframes"** whose breadcrumb parents it under *Quantum Physics*, not *Getting Started*. So the reader following "Need an analogy first?" ends up on a page whose URL, title, and breadcrumb home all disagree with the invitation copy that sent them there. Pass 1 tried `/analogy` and got a 404; that failure is a predictable consequence of the same naming mismatch.

Validation badges are the biggest terminology problem. They are defined **once**, at the top of `/honest-assessment` (seven badge types, e.g. "Validated — Quantitative match with independent data," "Strongly Supported — Consistent with data but caveats apply"). But they are used on the landing page, on `/gamma-boundary`, on `/key-claims`, and on section cards throughout — all without the legend in reach. A reader who lands on the home page and clicks "89% Validated" or "Strongly Supported" has no idea what the badges mean. They must (a) notice Honest Assessment in the top nav, (b) read it, (c) come back with the mental model loaded. That is not a reasonable ask for a scannable first visit.

Making this worse: `/parameter-derivations` uses **a different vocabulary** — "Motivated Ansatz," "Motivated Choice," "5% Error," "10% Error" — with no cross-walk to the Honest Assessment badge taxonomy. A reader cannot tell whether "Motivated Ansatz" is the same as "Untested," "Speculative," or something else. Two competing validation-label systems on one site is a red flag in any documentation audit.

The Glossary is a real asset — entries for γ, ρ, ρ_crit, N_corr, coherence, presence, MRH, A2ACW are all present — but it's under-used. Almost no inline terms on theory pages link to it. A reader encountering γ on `/phase-transitions` gets no tooltip, no underlined-link affordance, nothing pointing them to the definition they need. The glossary is treated as a destination instead of a reference.

Acronym hygiene is uneven. Good: MRH is spelled out on first use in First Encounter. Bad: MOND is unexpanded on `/why-synchronism` but expanded on `/key-claims`; **SPARC, ALFALFA-SDSS, YBCO, BTFR, RAR, LOO, PDG, CFD, CHSH, QM, GR, DOF, 6D** all appear at least once unexpanded on at least one page I visited. Some are defined on one page and not another, which is the worst case — it signals inconsistent editorial review rather than a blanket policy.

Finally, two interactive tools. `/gamma-calculator` is the better-designed of the two: the input field says **"Ncorr (number of correlated particles)"**, preset buttons give concrete examples (water, ferromagnet), and the result maps to a regime label. It still doesn't tell you what γ *is*, but the input-labeling is exemplary. `/phase-boundary-visualizer` has the same problem squared — a single γ slider with "Classical / Boundary / Quantum" labels and concrete system examples, but γ itself is undefined on the page.

### Tools Tested
- **γ Calculator** (`/gamma-calculator`) — worked — well-labeled inputs — the "Ncorr (number of correlated particles)" input and preset buttons (water, ferromagnet, etc.) are a model for how to scaffold a physics tool for non-specialists. Only weakness: γ itself appears in the output formula with no tooltip. If I didn't already know what γ was, the output number would be meaningless.

A dedicated information-architecture pass — plus an acronym-first-use lint — would do more for perceived credibility than any new content. The glossary is good; use it inline.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 12 (10 content + 2 interactive tools)
- **Understanding achieved**: good (of what's being claimed; the framework itself is mostly reparametrization)
- **Would return?**: yes — for the Honest Assessment page and the dark-matter failure write-up specifically; less so for the theory pages, which front-load motivation that the same site walks back later
- **Overall impression**: The site is two papers nailed together. One is a careful, candid self-audit (`/honest-assessment`, `/parameter-derivations`, `/dark-matter`) that I'd put in front of any grad student as a model of how to surface failure. The other is a marketing layer (`/coherence-function`, `/galaxy-rotation`, `/key-claims`) that overstates derivation status in language the audit later contradicts. The contradiction itself is real, falsifiable evidence of where the editorial work is needed.

### Journey
I went straight to `/coherence-function` to read the central equation, then `/parameter-derivations` to see how the parameters are obtained, then `/phase-transitions` and `/mrh` for the conceptual scaffolding. From there I jumped to `/galaxy-rotation` and `/dark-matter` because that's where the framework makes its sharpest empirical contact, and finally `/honest-assessment`, `/key-claims`, `/two-reframes`, and `/test-catalog` to evaluate self-criticism, novelty, and falsifiability. I tested the Coherence Explorer and Galaxy Plotter — the two tools most directly linked to the central equation and to the MOND claim respectively — to see whether the interactive surfaces accurately represent the math.

**Coherence function.** The equation is C(ρ) = tanh(γ · log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1)). The page argues tanh is "the unique sigmoid" satisfying boundedness, monotonicity, smooth saturation, and the right asymptotes. That's mathematically false: the logistic 1/(1+e^(−x)), erf(x), x/√(1+x²), the Gudermannian, and infinitely many other functions satisfy all four conditions. tanh is uniquely picked out only in specific physical settings — most famously the Curie–Weiss self-consistency equation m = tanh(βJzm/k_BT) for a mean-field Ising magnet, where the *self-consistency loop* is what selects tanh. The same `/parameter-derivations` page admits this directly: "Here, C(ρ) is evaluated directly with no self-consistency loop." If there is no self-consistency, the "mean-field theory" justification has been quietly stripped out, and what remains is a reasonable phenomenological sigmoid — not a derived one. The honest framing would be "tanh chosen by analogy to mean-field Ising; not derived in this form here."

The argument structure log(ρ/ρ_crit + 1) is also a tell. A natural mean-field-style sigmoid would have argument β(ρ − ρ_crit)/ρ_crit (linear, dimensionless deviation from criticality). The +1 shift inside the log is mathematical duct tape to force C(0) = tanh(γ·log(1)) = 0; without it the function would diverge as ρ → 0. That's a clue that the form was reverse-engineered from boundary conditions rather than derived from a Hamiltonian.

**Parameter derivations.** This is the page that does the most damage to the front-end story, in my opinion accidentally honestly. γ = 2/√N_corr — the page concedes "the factor of 2 is motivated by phase-space dimensionality arguments...but is not rigorously derived." Pure central-limit-theorem scaling gives 1/√N; the 2 is a free parameter labeled as a derivation. A = 4π/(α²GR₀²), a₀ = cH₀/(2π), Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G), R₀ = V²/(3a₀): these are all dimensional combinations. R₀ = V²/(3a₀) in particular is a definition once V_flat is the input — a 3% "agreement" is meaningless because there's no degree of freedom for it to disagree on. The page itself flags the central worry: "The 3–12% errors could reflect either (a) legitimate approximation limits in the dimensional analysis, or (b) implicit calibration through choice of V_flat as input. Distinguishing these requires independent derivation." That second possibility is the one a grad student would assume by default.

**MOND coincidence.** a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is the most famous numerical coincidence in MOND. Milgrom himself noted in 1983 that a₀ ≈ cH₀/(2π) and the ratio has been cited as suggestive of a cosmological origin for forty years. The Honest Assessment page acknowledges this in passing — "other frameworks (McCulloch 2007, Verlinde 2017, Smolin 2017) derive the same relation" — and gives it the **Reparametrization** badge. But on `/galaxy-rotation`, the same identity is presented with no such acknowledgement: a₀ "emerges from cosmology" reads as a derivation. This is the most important inconsistency on the site for a physicist: the front page of the most marketable claim does not flag the work as one of several known dimensional-analysis efforts to ground a₀ in H₀.

**Galaxy plotter.** I was hoping to see Synchronism vs. MOND vs. data on a SPARC galaxy. The plotter shows observed dots, Newtonian baryonic, and Synchronism. **MOND is not overlaid.** Given that the equation `g_obs = g_bar / (1 − exp(−√(g_bar/a₀)))` is, per the same site, "the standard RAR interpolation function (McGaugh et al. 2016)," the MOND and Synchronism curves should be *visually identical* on every galaxy. Not showing MOND is the single most important thing the tool *should* do, because confirming visual identity is the honest reframe: "we reproduce MOND; here is the proof you can see." Hiding the MOND curve makes the Synchronism curve look like a unique fit when it is — by the page's own admission — a reparametrization.

**Phase transitions.** The page admits "Critical Exponents: 2× Off — Synchronism's mean-field-derived tanh gives the wrong exponents by a factor of ~2." This is exactly the canonical failure of mean-field theory captured by the Ginzburg criterion — the upper critical dimension for the Ising universality class is 4, and 3D Ising gives β ≈ 0.326 vs. mean-field's 1/2. So Synchronism's "failure" here is not a Synchronism-specific failure; it is *the* prototypical reason RG and ε-expansion exist. The page is honest about the failure but doesn't situate it in the broader story (mean-field is wrong below the upper critical dimension; this is decades of established physics). A reader might conclude "this is a unique limitation of Synchronism" rather than "this is the well-understood breakdown of any mean-field treatment of a critical point with relevant fluctuations."

**MRH.** The definition — "the minimal set of interacting degrees of freedom whose state transitions materially influence the coherence evolution of a defined system" — together with the predictive sufficiency / predictive closure criteria, is essentially Pearl's Markov blanket (or, more recently, Friston's). The page acknowledges the Markov-blanket lineage but does not connect it to RG coarse-graining or effective-field-theory cutoff selection, which is where physicists already do this work formally. Without an operational algorithm or a connection to the RG flow, MRH is currently a renaming of an existing concept. That's not nothing — renaming sometimes clarifies — but the page should say so.

**Dark matter.** This is the most respectable content on the site for a physicist. The framework predicts, via C = 1/μ_eff, that low-coherence "dark" matter should have *higher* effective viscosity than baryons. Bullet Cluster says it has lower viscosity (passes through itself with little drag). The prediction is sharp, the data refute it, the page records the refutation. This is real falsification; it deserves more credit than it gets on `/key-claims`, which calls it "Reparametrization that fails under stress testing" and somewhat undersells the methodological win — a framework that can be pinned to a wrong sign by a real observation is doing its job.

**Two reframes.** The CRT-scanning analogy for QM and the pendulum-clock analogy for relativity are both interpretive. The page admits the CRT model is "not yet mathematically formalized to the level where it reproduces all of standard QM's quantitative predictions" and that "the Born rule derivation itself is acknowledged as possibly circular." Neither reframe is positioned against decoherence theory, Bohmian pilot-wave (also nonlocal, also reproduces QM), many-worlds, or objective collapse — all of which already do "decenter the observer." Calling this a "Copernican move" overstates the novelty given the literature.

**Test catalog.** I went looking for the falsifiable predictions and found a directory with tier counts (10/4/7/3) and headline phrases ("BAO coherence modulation, wide binary density dependence, SPARC environment analysis") but no numerical predictions, no kill criteria, and no discriminating-power analysis on this hub page. The actual specifics presumably live on the linked tier pages, but a `/test-catalog` that doesn't show kill criteria for *any* test is the wrong default for a framework whose central claim is "we are falsifiable." The "0 Completed" status is honest; the absence of explicit kill thresholds on the index is not.

### Tools Tested
- **Coherence Explorer** (`/coherence-explorer`) — worked — physics partially correct — Asymptotic limits behave correctly (C → 0 as ρ → 0, C → 1 as ρ → ∞). ### Unanswered Questions
1. If C(ρ) is not solved self-consistently, in what sense is it "mean-field"? The Curie–Weiss tanh and the BCS gap equation are both *fixed-point* solutions of self-consistency conditions; that's where tanh comes from. A direct evaluation has no such pedigree. What's the actual mathematical claim?
2. What computation, on what system, would produce MRH from first principles? Pick the 1D Ising chain at T_c — what is its MRH? If the framework can't answer that, MRH is currently a definition, not an instrument.
3. The chemistry "89% Validated" with sound-velocity r = 0.982 is an extraordinary correlation. What is the regression target — measured sound velocity vs. predicted sound velocity, with what predictors? The page elsewhere flags Era 2 chemistry (sessions 134–2660) as "template-based." Does the 89% number include or exclude template-based predictions? This needs a held-out test set.
4. Tier 1 tests claim discriminating power vs. ΛCDM and MOND. For each Tier 1 test (BAO coherence modulation, wide binary density dependence, SPARC environment analysis): what is the predicted Synchronism value, what is the predicted MOND value, what is the predicted ΛCDM value, and what is the kill threshold? Without those four numbers a test has no discriminating power by definition.
5. The Bullet Cluster failure refuted the C = 1/μ_eff mapping. Has any candidate replacement mapping been proposed that preserves the rotation-curve fit without predicting baryon-stickier dark matter? If not, the framework has a structural problem, not just a prediction problem.
6. Across `/key-claims` the QM "synchronization physics" reframe is said to predict that "resynchronization protocols outperform isolation" in noisy environments. What is the predicted figure of merit (e.g., decoherence half-life ratio)? This is the most experimentally accessible novel prediction on the site and it has no number attached.

### Verdict
The Honest Assessment page belongs in a methodology seminar — the validation taxonomy (Validated / Strongly Supported / Supported / Untested / Speculative / **Reparametrization** / Failed) is more rigorous than what most published research programs publicly maintain about themselves, and the "0 unique confirmed predictions" line is the kind of admission peer-reviewed papers almost never make. The Bullet Cluster sign-error write-up on `/dark-matter` is a real falsification cleanly recorded, which is valuable. But the front-end theory pages don't honor that taxonomy: tanh is "uniquely" derived from mean-field theory the same site admits is not mean-field theory; a₀ "emerges from cosmology" the same site classifies as a Reparametrization of a 1983 Milgrom coincidence; the Galaxy Plotter omits MOND, the one curve that would prove the reparametrization claim honestly. The fix is not new physics — it's making the audit propagate. Stamp every claim with its Honest Assessment badge on the page where the claim lives, overlay MOND on the Galaxy Plotter, and rewrite `/coherence-function` to call its sigmoid what it is: a phenomenological choice motivated by analogy, not a derivation. Do that and a grad student can take the framework seriously as a research program with one falsified prediction, several open ones, and an unusually candid self-assessment. As-is, the front pages overpromise relative to what the back pages already concede.

### Journey

**On novelty.** Across the site I could identify exactly five candidate predictions that are not trivially MOND, ΛCDM, QFT, or standard decoherence theory in different notation:

1. **BAO peak shift ~10⁻⁴ between high- and low-density regions** (`/top-5-tests` TEST-04). This is the most interesting claim on the site because DESI DR1 (2024) and DR2 (2025) have released BAO distance measurements with sub-percent precision, and 10⁻⁴ fractional shifts in peak position between density environments are at the edge of current sensitivity. If the signal is derived (not postulated after inspection of DESI data) and is not degenerate with known nuisance parameters (non-linear growth, reconstruction artifacts, galaxy–dark matter halo assembly bias), this is a live test. The site does not show the derivation, does not state whether the prediction was registered before DESI DR1, and does not compute the ΛCDM baseline against which a 10⁻⁴ shift is claimed to be discriminating. A physicist cannot evaluate this without the derivation.

2. **λ ~ 500 Mpc oscillatory wavelength in galaxy cluster separations** (`/top-5-tests` TEST-07). This is framed as "unique to Synchronism." The scale is suspicious: 500 Mpc is close to neither the BAO comoving sound horizon (~150 Mpc) nor the homogeneity scale (~100 Mpc), nor canonical features in the matter power spectrum, but it is close to the largest identified coherent structures (Sloan Great Wall, Giant Arc, Hercules–Corona Borealis Great Wall) and near the CMB hemispherical-asymmetry coherence scale. Without a derivation this cannot be distinguished from retroactive fitting to known anomalous structures. The site does not state the predicted amplitude, the predicted phase, or the window function — all of which are needed to evaluate whether existing 2dF/SDSS/eBOSS correlation-function measurements already rule the prediction in or out.

3. **Wide binary anomaly density-dependence** (TEST-02). Gaia DR3 wide-binary analyses have been a public battleground since 2023 (Chae 2023/2024 sees MOND-like deviation at ≈10⁻¹⁰ m/s²; Pittordis & Sutherland 2023 and Banik et al. 2024 report null results after metal-chromatic and undetected-tertiary corrections). A genuine density-dependence prediction could discriminate Synchronism from both Newton *and* MOND, because MOND proper is density-independent on this scale. The prediction amplitude is not given on the site; if it is in the ~1–10% range, Chae's existing sample should already show it. The site does not cite Chae or Banik, does not state whether the prediction predates those analyses, and does not specify the density cut.

4. **EEG phase-coherence sharp transition at C ≈ 0.50 during anesthesia induction** (`/consciousness-predictions` #1–#8). The binary "discontinuous drop vs. smooth gradient" test is genuinely discriminating — but only against a straw-man null. The actual state of the art (Sarasso et al. 2015 perturbational complexity index; Alkire, Hudetz & Tononi 2008; Casali et al. 2013) already reports non-linear but continuous complexity transitions. The site does not engage the PCI literature or the Casali Φ* measurements. The 34-prediction list is useful (the hysteresis prediction #8 and the γ-circuit neuroplasticity #4 are the sharpest), but nearly all suffer the same underspecification: the observable is named, the mapping from observable to C is not given, and the page concedes the whole program is "possibly unfalsifiable if the mapping from EEG to C is too flexible." That concession is correct and disqualifying until the mapping is specified.

5. **Entity criterion Γ < m** (mentioned on `/honest-assessment`). The σ meson (f0(500)) has Γ/m ≈ 1.16; Synchronism says "not a particle." This is already the consensus reading of Roy-equation dispersive analyses that put the pole deep on the second Riemann sheet. The PDG lists it as "f0(500) — the existence of the σ meson has been the subject of an ongoing debate" and its inclusion is caveated. If Synchronism's criterion agrees with the cautious QFT view, it is not making a new prediction; it is validating an existing consensus. The criterion would only be interesting if applied to a PDG-settled particle whose Γ/m ratio would demote it — the site does not claim this for any such particle.

Net: one (BAO modulation) and possibly two (wide-binary density-dependence, 500-Mpc oscillations) candidate novel *quantitative* predictions exist, and none are presented with a derivation. The site's own headline verdict — "0 unique confirmed predictions after 3,302 sessions + 13 adversarial stress tests" — is consistent with what a physicist would conclude from reading it.

**On known-physics overclaims.** The shared-environment decoherence claim Γ = γ²(1−c) "predicting 10× T₂ improvement at c ≈ 0.90" (`/key-claims`, Claim 1) is the decoherence-free subspace / noiseless-subsystem result. Zanardi & Rasetti 1997, Lidar, Chuang & Whaley 1998, and Kielpinski et al. 2001 (Be⁺ trapped-ion DFS demonstrating orders-of-magnitude T₂ extension) are the canonical references. Casting DFS as a "novel engineering prediction of Synchronism" is a literature miss. The whole architecture of collective dephasing and decoherence-free encoding is well-characterized: any pair of qubits with symmetric coupling to the bath already exhibits exactly this T₂ enhancement. The "shared phase field" language is a restatement, not a new result. This should be flagged Reparametrization on `/key-claims` to match the taxonomy.

**On the GW–dark-matter correlation "permitted at nonzero."** GW170817 / GRB170817A bounds |c_gw/c − 1| < ~10⁻¹⁵ already (Abbott et al. 2017, Creminelli & Vernizzi 2017 used this to kill most scalar-tensor modifications of gravity). The `/top-5-tests` entry "GR predicts zero, Synchronism permits nonzero" is not a prediction; it is the *absence* of a constraint. A framework that fails to forbid what GR forbids isn't making a sharper prediction — it's making a weaker one. Unless a specific nonzero numerical value is predicted, this should not be in a "top 5 decisive tests" list.

**On the coherence function.** Pass 3 correctly flagged that tanh is not "the unique sigmoid" and that the +1 shift inside the log is boundary-condition fitting. Adding to that: the claim that a single dimensionless C(ρ) spans "80 orders of magnitude" of density is structurally misleading. A dimensionless sigmoid in ρ/ρ_crit requires a system-specific ρ_crit. Eighty orders of magnitude of ρ is not eighty orders of magnitude of unified physics; it is eighty orders of magnitude of re-choices of ρ_crit, one per system. The framework should either (a) write down a scale-invariant action S[ρ] whose RG flow produces the ρ_crit(system) relation, or (b) concede that C(ρ) is a system-by-system phenomenological fit whose cross-system unity is rhetorical, not mathematical. Neither (a) nor (b) is currently on the site.

There is also no Hamiltonian, no Lagrangian, no action, no symmetry analysis, no discussion of Lorentz or gauge invariance, no UV completion, and no RG flow anywhere on `/coherence-function` or `/parameter-derivations`. For a framework claiming "quantum to cosmic" unification, the absence of any one of these is a serious gap; the absence of all of them means the framework is not a field theory. It is a family of empirical sigmoid fits with a shared functional form.

**On parameter derivations.** The page's own candor — "3–12% errors could reflect either approximation limits or implicit calibration through choice of V_flat as input" — is the right instinct and should be weighted higher. Specifically:
- R₀ = V²/(3a₀) with V_flat as input is a definition, not a prediction (Pass 3 flagged this). Add: the factor 3 is a spherical-symmetry geometric constant for point-mass vs. disk — in the disk MOND limit (Brada & Milgrom 1999) one gets v⁴ = GMa₀ with no 3; the "3" indicates an ad hoc assumption about geometry.
- Σ₀ = cH₀/(4π²G) is numerically close to the Milgrom surface-density scale Σ_M = a₀/(2πG) (Milgrom 1983 Letter I; Brada & Milgrom 1999). Using a₀ = cH₀/(2π) gives Σ_M = cH₀/(4π²G), which is exactly what Synchronism reports. So Σ₀ is a₀/(2πG) under another name — i.e., once a₀ is adopted, Σ₀ is arithmetic.
- The claim that γ = 2/√N_corr comes from "6D to 3 effective" phase-space counting gives √2 (since √(6/3) = √2), not 2. If the argument is that 6D/3D = 2 directly, the logic is dropped. If it is √(6/3), the answer is wrong. Either way, the factor of 2 is unmotivated.

**On cosmology silences.** `/dark-matter` confirms what the parameter-derivations page suggests: Synchronism has not been tested against CMB acoustic peak ratios (baryon peak height depends on Ω_b/Ω_cdm), against σ_8 / growth-of-structure measurements (DESI 2024 mild f·σ_8 tension), or against Lyman-α forest P_1D(k). These are the standard constraints that have killed most relativistic MOND variants (original TeVeS is ruled out by CMB; AeST, BIMOND, and superfluid DM are the extant candidates, each with their own CMB/LSS engineering work). A dark-matter-replacement framework that is silent on these is not yet in the discussion; the current state is pre-TeVeS (1983-era MOND). This is not a criticism of candor — it is a statement of research status that the Honest Assessment page could surface.

**On A2ACW.** The methodology page names the single most important weakness and does not address it: shared priors across AI systems trained on overlapping data (arXiv, Wikipedia, published textbooks) produce synthetic disagreement without epistemic independence. The forced-challenge protocol (≥1 challenge per 10 exchanges; auto-escalate at 15 exchanges without challenge) addresses *surface* agreement, not *deep* prior-induced agreement. Two models trained on the same corpus can vigorously disagree about which 1983 reference to cite while agreeing on the shared prior that grounds both sides of the disagreement. The 3,302-session count is an engagement metric, not an independence metric. A methodologically honest A2ACW page would (a) describe how the protocol handles shared-prior bias, (b) report the "Fingerprint Divergence Index" baseline against an orthogonally-trained model or against human experts in the relevant subfield, and (c) disclose which AI systems participated and their training corpora. None of this is on the page. This gap is the single most important scientific issue with the entire site, because the audit that the site rightly celebrates is *produced by* A2ACW, and if A2ACW is secretly correlated the audit inherits that correlation.

### Unanswered Questions
1. **BAO 10⁻⁴ prediction — derivation?** The most interesting candidate novel prediction on the site has no derivation surfaced. Was this number obtained before or after DESI DR1? Is the density proxy galaxy-count density, baryon density, or halo density? Which BAO tracer (LRG, ELG, Lyα)? Without these, the prediction is not yet a prediction.
2. **500 Mpc oscillation wavelength — derivation?** What sets 500 Mpc? If this is from the framework's characteristic length R₀ or from c/H₀ divided by a specific number, show the arithmetic. If it is empirically motivated by an existing structure catalog, say so — that makes it an explanatory fit, not a prediction.
3. **A2ACW shared-prior correlation — measurement?** What is the Fingerprint Divergence Index baseline for a known-correlated pair (two GPT-family models) vs. a known-independent pair (a physicist vs. a different physicist)? Until this is reported, the methodology's evidentiary weight is undefined.
4. **BTFR n ≈ 2.2 — is this the right number?** SPARC measures n = 3.85 ± 0.09. Either the TEST-09 prediction is wrong, or "n" is a different quantity. Clarification needed.
5. **CMB acoustic peaks — any prediction?** The baryon-to-photon ratio peak-height-ratio constraint killed TeVeS; any framework proposing to replace dark matter must address it. What does Synchronism predict for the ratio of the 2nd-to-1st peak at ℓ ≈ 540 vs. 220?
6. **Shared-environment decoherence: what's the new prediction?** Γ = γ²(1−c) is DFS physics. What does Synchronism predict that goes beyond Zanardi-Rasetti / Lidar-Chuang-Whaley? A specific c-dependence shape? A c-to-γ calibration? An experimental protocol that DFS-based error correction would not already prescribe?
7. **Entity criterion: is there a PDG-settled particle that would be demoted?** If not, the criterion is a consistency check with existing dispersion analyses, and the site should say so.

### Verdict
The site is more valuable as a methodological artifact than as a physics framework. The Honest Assessment taxonomy, the "0 confirmed predictions" admission, and the Bullet Cluster falsification write-up are exemplary scientific candor — candor of a kind most published research programs refuse to surface about themselves. That contribution is real and should be preserved. But as a physics program the site sits at MOND-era-1983 in cosmology (no CMB, no LSS, no Lyα engagement; Bullet Cluster falsified with no proposed repair) and at DFS-era-1997 in quantum information (the "novel" shared-environment decoherence result is the decoherence-free subspace). The genuinely novel candidate predictions — BAO density modulation, wide-binary density-dependence, 500-Mpc oscillations, C ≈ 0.50 EEG transition — are all surfaced as headline numbers without derivations, which is a correctable editorial problem, not a physics problem. The hardest single issue is A2ACW circularity: the methodology that produced the whole audit does not address shared-prior correlation across AI participants, and until it does, the audit inherits that correlation. Fix A2ACW's independence-reporting, write down the four candidate-novel-prediction derivations, engage CMB/LSS, and this framework has a shot at being a legitimate relativistic-MOND-lineage research program. As-is, it is an honest reparametrization with four promising pointers and one under-specified methodology.
