## Pass 1: Casual Science Enthusiast

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, start-here, what-this-is-not, coherence-explorer, two-reframes, honest-assessment, consciousness, learning-paths, galaxy-rotation, galaxy-curve-plotter)
- **Understanding achieved**: partial
- **Would return?**: maybe
- **Overall impression**: A genuinely interesting question and refreshingly honest, but it hands a casual reader an intimidating equation before it hands them a reason to care, and the "nothing worked yet" honesty arrives before I understand what "it" even is.

### Journey
I clicked through from Reddit. The opening line — *"What if one equation described reality from quantum to cosmic?"* — is a great hook. That's exactly the kind of big-swing idea I watch YouTube videos about. But the very next thing on the page is **C(ρ) = tanh(γ · ln(ρ/ρcrit + 1))**. I have no idea what tanh, ρ, or γ are, and seeing the equation that fast made me feel like I'd walked into the wrong room. The marching-band vs. wandering-crowd analogy right next to it rescued it a little — *that* I get.

I clicked "Start Here," which was the friendliest-sounding button. It was better: crowds, marching bands, neutron stars, and a plain definition of "coherence" as "how collectively a group of things behaves." Then it hit me with "Markov Relevancy Horizon" and "minimal set of degrees of freedom," and I skimmed. The "bubble of nearest neighbors that matter" version was fine; the formal version lost me.

Curiosity pulled me to "What this is not." This is where I got confused as a casual reader. The landing page sold me a revolutionary single equation; this page calmly told me nothing new has actually been confirmed and most of it is repackaged known physics. I respect the honesty a lot — but I genuinely wondered *why am I reading this then?* The transparency is admirable but it landed before I'd been given a reason to be invested.

I tried the **Coherence Explorer** because sliders are fun. I could see it had a γ slider and a ρcrit slider and an S-curve, and numbers like 0.8824. But I didn't know what 0.8824 *means* for me — coherence of 0.88 of what? It needed a one-liner like "this galaxy/material/whatever would behave 88% 'marching band.'"

"Two Reframes" sounded like the simple-version page. It wasn't — CRT scanning, pendulum clocks, Bell's theorem, Tsirelson's bound. The objects are familiar but the discussion assumed real physics.

The **Consciousness** page was the most exciting to me (this is the stuff pop-science thrives on) — "your experience IS the pattern." Cool idea! But then it admitted two of its own variables can't actually be measured, which deflated it.

Finally I found **Learning Paths**, which has a clearly-labeled **Beginner** track ("No physics background needed"). *This* is what I wanted from the start. I wish the landing page had pointed me here before the equation. I also poked the **Galaxy Curve Plotter**, which was the most intuitive thing on the whole site — pick a galaxy, see colored lines, with a record-player analogy. That one actually taught me something.

## Pass 2: Technical Writer

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 10 (landing, glossary, honest-assessment, interactive-tools, gamma-calculator, equation-walkthrough, first-encounter, phase-boundary-visualizer, why-synchronism, key-claims)

### Journey
I audited methodically: top nav, then the canonical reference pages (Glossary, Honest Assessment), then the tools, then the "Getting Started" funnel. The skeleton is well-built. Every page I touched carried a working breadcrumb (`Home / Getting Started / X` or `Home / Interactive Tools / X`), and the nav menu is stable across pages. The Glossary is the standout artifact: all six acronyms I spot-checked (MRH, MOND, RAR, BAO, DESI, SPARC) are defined there, and it carries *three* explicit "⚠ Physicist note" warnings that the framework's "coherence" inverts the standard quantum usage — "C=0 does not mean 'incoherent' in the CM sense; it means sparsely interacting." That is exactly the kind of disambiguation a documentation reviewer wants to see.

The problem is consistency *across* surfaces. The Glossary, landing page, γ Calculator, and Phase Boundary Visualizer all use the corrected framing: **0 = sparse/independent, 1 = dense/collective**, with superconductors (BCS/BEC) sitting at *low* C / low γ (the "collective" end by N_corr). But the **Equation Walkthrough** tool still defines the same variable as *"Coherence — a number from 0 (quantum, uncorrelated) to 1 (classical, collective)"* — the old quantum/classical framing the Glossary explicitly warns against. And the **First Encounter** page (a Getting Started page, the #2 funnel step) uses *"superconductors"* as its **"extremely high coherence"** exemplar — which directly contradicts the Glossary's statement that BEC/BCS superconductors map to **low C** in this framework. So the single most error-prone term on the whole site is defined one way in the reference material and the opposite way on the page most likely to be a reader's first technical contact. For a term the site itself flags three times as a known trap, that's the highest-value fix available.

On badges: the Honest Assessment page is the canonical legend and it's good — it defines 10 badges across an MRH-relationship family (active-mrh, parallel-paths, sidelined, superseded, audited-negative) and a descriptive family (untested, speculative, reparametrization, failed), and explicitly marks "Validated"/"Strongly Supported" as **deprecated**. But the **landing page's** own badge legend ("Badges used below") lists **"Validated"** (deprecated) and **"Withdrawn"** (not defined anywhere I could find — the canonical legend uses "Failed," not "Withdrawn"). So the front door advertises a badge taxonomy that disagrees with its own canonical legend one click away. Separately, every in-content badge I actually saw on Key Claims used the *descriptive* set (untested / speculative / failed) — I didn't encounter the MRH-relationship badges in use on any content page, so that richer family is defined but (at least on these pages) unexercised.

Acronym discipline is "defined in the Glossary, not on first inline use." DESI, BIC, SPARC, MOND, RAR, BAO, YBCO, ALFALFA-SDSS all appear on the landing and Why pages without an inline expansion or hover. That's tolerable because the Glossary is thorough, but it leans on the reader to leave the page.

## Pass 3: Graduate Physics Student

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 9 (landing, equation-walkthrough, parameter-derivations, galaxy-rotation, honest-assessment, coherence-explorer, two-reframes, consciousness, gamma-calculator)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid
- **Would return?**: yes
- **Overall impression**: The canonical reference pages (honest-assessment, parameter-derivations, the RAR verdict) are genuinely rigorous self-audits I'd trust — but two front-facing surfaces still teach a *physically wrong* coherence labeling, the galaxy page overclaims a₀ as "derived" in direct contradiction with the canonical pages, and the Coherence Explorer markets a saturation curve as a sigmoid "transition" with a steepness claim that doesn't survive differentiation.

### Journey
I went straight for the math. The headline C(ρ) = tanh(γ·ln(ρ/ρcrit+1)) is, to a condensed-matter eye, a **compander/saturation curve**, and to the site's credit the landing page now says exactly that — "sigmoid/compander family (μ-law, Hill, logistic)," with "no self-consistency loop, no free energy, no critical exponents." That is the correct category. It is *not* Landau theory, and I was glad to see mean-field framing explicitly rejected. So I was surprised to land on **/parameter-derivations** and read, for the tanh choice, "a phenomenological choice from the **Landau-universality family**." That's the prestige vocabulary the landing page just disowned — and it's a category error in the strict sense: Landau universality is about scaling near a critical point with a diverging correlation length and nontrivial critical exponents, none of which this object has (the page itself says so two parameters up). The two pages disagree about what kind of mathematical object the central equation is.

**The derivations themselves are handled honestly.** γ=2/√N_corr is badged "Motivated Ansatz" and the page volunteers its own fatal objection: the 1/√N scaling is borrowed from CLT, but CLT requires *independent* variables while N_corr counts *correlated* degrees of freedom — so the scaling is imported into precisely the regime where its justification fails. The "factor 2 from 6D→3 phase space" is hand-waving, but it's flagged as such. I'll take an ansatz that admits it's an ansatz over a dressed-up one any day. Parameter A (≈0.029 via a Jeans criterion) is the one quantity with a real physical path, and it's correctly hedged as "still calibrated to observational anchors."

Then the **a₀ contradiction**. On /parameter-derivations and /honest-assessment, a₀ = cH₀/(2π) is correctly called what it is: "dimensional bookkeeping," "noted since Milgrom (1983)," reproduced by McCulloch/Verlinde/Smolin — a reparametrization, not a derivation. But /galaxy-rotation states flatly: **"a₀ is derived, not fitted — it emerges from cosmology as cH₀/(2π)."** Those cannot both be the site's position. Numerically cH₀/(2π) ≈ 1.08×10⁻¹⁰ m/s² against Milgrom's 1.20×10⁻¹⁰ (10% low), and the 2π is unmotivated — Verlinde gets a₀ ~ cH₀ with a *different* O(1) prefactor; you can hit the observed value by choosing the factor. This is the 40-year-old a₀~cH₀ coincidence, and the canonical pages say so. The galaxy page's "derived, not fitted" is the one genuine overclaim I found, and it's on the site's headline physics result.

**The RAR verdict, by contrast, is exemplary.** SPARC, 2807 points: McGaugh MOND RMS 0.1437; γ=2 compander RMS 0.1485 at **ΔBIC=+184** (refuted); free-γ collapses to γ≈0.49 with RMS *identical* to McGaugh at ΔBIC=+7 (pure parameter penalty, zero fit improvement). That's the correct reading — when your free parameter relaxes to reproduce the function you were trying to beat, you've reparametrized it. The "γmax ≈ 3.17 but SPARC shows ⟨γ⟩=10.82, 579 galaxies exceed it" failure on honest-assessment is a clean falsification, stated as one.

**Quantum (two-reframes):** appropriately humble. No Born rule, no Tsirelson derivation — the page explicitly flags the circularity ("deriving E(a,b)=−cos(a−b) requires the measurement probabilities to follow the Born rule"). The "one pattern probed at two locations" is a nonlocal-realist gloss that makes no new prediction; calling the quantum results reparametrizations is correct.

**Consciousness:** C=f(γ,D,S) with D and S admitted to be operationally undefined → correctly badged *Speculative* ("cannot yet be tested, not just hasn't been"). Good. I'll note that "8-way convergence to C≈0.50" should raise an eyebrow: 0.50 is the **midpoint of the [0,1] output range**, the most natural attractor for any convergence-counting exercise, so convergence *to that particular number* is weak evidence. Credit where due: the page no longer makes any steepest-slope-at-0.50 argument, which would have been false.

### The two tools I checked against the math
I verified both arithmetically rather than just clicking.

- **γ Calculator** — *worked, exact.* N_corr=4 → γ=1.000, N_corr=100 → γ=0.200, ideal gas → 2.000, BCS(10⁷) → 6.3×10⁻⁴, BEC(10⁶) → 2.0×10⁻³. All consistent with γ=2/√N_corr, and the presets correctly put BCS/BEC at the collective (low-γ) end with an honest "these N_corr are estimates, not measured pair counts" caveat. No complaints — this tool is internally sound.

- **Coherence Explorer** — *worked, but markets the curve as something it isn't.* C(ρcrit)=0.8824, C(10ρcrit)=0.9999 — arithmetic correct (tanh(2·ln2)=0.8824 ✓), and it correctly states C=0.50 sits at ρ≈0.32ρcrit and calls ρcrit a "saturation knee, not a critical point." But two framings don't survive differentiation. (1) It's sold as an **S-curve / "transition."** Because the argument ln(ρ/ρcrit+1) is ≥0 for every physical density, you only ever see the **concave upper half** of tanh — there is no interior inflection point. The maximum slope on a *linear* ρ axis is at ρ=0 (C=0); on a *log* axis it's at ρ≈0.4ρcrit, **C≈0.59** — never at the midpoint and never at ρcrit. (2) The tool asserts **"at γ≈1 the transition is steepest — this is where chemistry and biology happen."** That conflates a regime label with a derivative. Slope-at-origin on a linear axis is exactly γ (steepest at γ=2, the slider's max); the peak slope on a log axis actually *increases as γ falls* (I get peak≈0.19 at γ=2, ≈0.32 at γ=1, ≈0.50 at γ=0.5). So γ≈1 is an extremum in neither direction — it's the chemistry/biology *regime boundary*, which is a fine thing to say, but it isn't "steepest."

## Pass 4: Leading-Edge Researcher

### Summary
- **Pages visited**: 8 (landing, key-claims, test-catalog, research-philosophy, galaxy-rotation, honest-assessment, parameter-derivations, two-reframes) + 2 tools (galaxy-curve-plotter, coherence-explorer)
- **Understanding achieved**: solid (I can locate every novelty claim and its disposition)
- **Would return?**: yes — but to cite the *methodology* result, not the physics
- **Overall impression**: The honest verdict ("0 confirmed novel predictions, 1 refuted") is accurate and, unusually, *under-stated* — the genuinely citable contribution here is a meta-result about the ceiling of AI-to-AI adversarial collaboration, not anything in modified gravity; but the front-line physics page still advertises a discrimination avenue (the External Field Effect) that the project's own research loop closed on 2026-06-03, so the lobby is now one step behind the back room on the single most important "is there anything new?" question.

### Journey
I came in as a referee would: skip the analogies, find the novelty claims, check them against DESI 2024, Gaia DR3 wide binaries, EFT-of-LSS, and the decoherence literature, and decide whether *any* prediction is distinguishable from MOND+ΛCDM. The site makes this easy — `/key-claims`, `/test-catalog`, and `/honest-assessment` are a self-audit, not a sales deck, and the scoreboard ("Confirmed novel predictions: 0; Prospective pre-registered predictions tested: 0; External refutations: 1") is exactly what I'd write in a report. So my job became verification: is the "0" honest, or is it false modesty hiding a real result, or false modesty hiding a *worse* result?

**Does anything differ from MOND + ΛCDM?** I traced every candidate to ground:

1. *Environment-dependent RAR scatter* (the one claim explicitly marketed as "Synchronism predicts this; standard MOND does not"). Refuted: R²=0.14 on 14,585 ALFALFA-SDSS galaxies, p=5×10⁻⁶ but a 4% effect — i.e. statistically resolvable, physically negligible, and the page correctly notes "standard MOND plus M/L corrections already explains essentially all of the RAR variance." A p-value driven to 5σ by sample size on a 14%-of-variance term is not a discriminating prediction; it's a detection of nothing of consequence. The site says as much. Correct call.

2. *RAR transition shape* — the only genuinely non-degenerate galaxy discriminator. Closed by execution: γ=2 compander ΔBIC=+184 on 2807 SPARC points; free-γ relaxes to 0.49 and reproduces McGaugh's RMS to four digits at ΔBIC=+7 (pure parameter penalty). This is the textbook signature of a reparametrization — when your free parameter relaxes to *become* the function you set out to beat, you have re-derived it. Clean kill, honestly stated.

3. *DESI fσ₈* — I checked this against my own reading of the DESI DR1 full-shape results. The site's current statement (LRG1 fσ₈/fid = 1.16±0.13, *above* ΛCDM; Synchronism's 12% suppression therefore disfavored at ~2.4σ, wrong sign) is correct. This matters because it's a *directional* failure: the framework predicts growth suppression and DESI sees growth *enhancement* in that bin. A wrong-sign result is worth more than a null — it rules out the suppression-of-growth mechanism class, not just this parameterization. The honest-assessment page makes that generalization; good.

4. *Clusters* — structurally dead, and for the right reason. The "wrong-variable" diagnosis ("C(ρ) is a function of local density ρ, but the RAR it mimics is a function of g_bar, a non-local enclosed-mass acceleration") is the sharpest single sentence on the site. It's not a calibration miss; it's a statement that a density-local function *cannot* reproduce an acceleration-space relation across differing mass geometries. That's a real impossibility argument, and it generalizes beyond Synchronism.

**Then the one that's stale.** On `/galaxy-rotation`, the External Field Effect is presented as an *open* avenue: "MOND with the EFE also predicts environment-dependent wide-binary dynamics… *the quantitative divergence between the two predictions has not yet been computed*… This prevents using wide binaries to discriminate Synchronism from MOND+EFE." As a researcher hunting for the live discrimination test, this is the most important sentence on the page — it says "here is where the action still is." But it is no longer true. The divergence *was* computed (per the project's own 2026-06-03 closure): a bounded C(a) is the only form whose EFE is distinct from MOND (TDG σ ≈ 14.5 vs 41), but that same boundedness caps the acceleration boost at ≈3.17 while ~42% of RAR points demand boosts above that (up to ~34×), giving RMS 0.224 vs McGaugh's 0.146 — and the boost ceiling trades RAR fit against EFE-distinctness monotonically with opposite signs, so *no* ceiling fits the RAR and stays distinct from MOND simultaneously. That closes the last open discriminator. The galaxy page still tells a visiting researcher to go look there. This is a propagation lag, not a physics error — but it's the highest-value propagation lag on the site, because "EFE not yet computed" is precisely the thread a referee would pull, and it now leads to a result the page doesn't know about.

**So what's actually new?** Nothing in the physics — and I'm satisfied the "0" is honest, not hiding anything. But there *is* a genuinely novel, citable result here, and it's epistemological: `/research-philosophy` documents a measured ceiling on AI-to-AI adversarial collaboration — 3,308 sessions, 47 internal-consistency survivors (1.4%), **0 surviving domain-expert audit**, every survivor resolving to a reparametrization or null. Paired with the correct diagnosis of *why* ("two models trained on the same corpus share blind spots; the protocol cannot detect errors systematic across the training corpus"), this is a real contribution to the AI-for-science literature — arguably the only original finding the project has produced. It's a negative result about a method, and it's well-supported by the framework's own corpus serving as the experimental substrate. The site files it modestly under methodology; I'd argue it's the headline.

### A2ACW soundness assessment (referee view)
As a falsifiability/self-consistency filter, A2ACW is sound and — importantly — the site does *not* overclaim it as a discovery engine ("a falsifiability and self-consistency filter, not a discovery method"). That self-characterization is correct and matches the evidence: it caught the Γ<m criterion as standard Breit-Wigner narrow-width (1936), the a₀~cH₀ coincidence as 40-year-old dimensional bookkeeping, and six "superconductor predictions" as standard condensed matter in different notation. What it cannot do — generate out-of-distribution novelty, or catch errors shared across the training corpus — is exactly what a referee would predict from first principles, and the site states it. The only caveat I'd add: a defender/challenger pair sharing weights is closer to a *single* model arguing with itself than to genuine adversarial collaboration, so "0 survivors" is partly a statement about correlated reviewers, not just about the framework. The site's "shared blind spot" section already implies this; it could say it more directly.
